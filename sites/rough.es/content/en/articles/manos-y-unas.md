Hands talk about us more than we think. They are seen when greeting, signing, gesturing in a meeting or pouring a drink, and unkempt nails or cracked hands can spoil the impression that a good suit leaves. Taking care of them does not require time or sophisticated products: a few minutes a week and some daily habits are enough. And, in the case of nails, doing it well also has a health dimension, because an incorrect cut or a poorly treated cuticle can end in an infection or an ingrown toenail.

In this guide you will see how to wash and moisturize your hands, how to protect them at work, in winter and in the sun, what tools you need for your nails, how to cut and file them correctly, what to do with cuticles and hangnails, how to care for your feet with the same principles, what to take into account in a professional manicure and what changes in your nails deserve a medical consultation.

## Wash and moisturize

- **Wash your hands with mild soap** and warm water; Very hot water and aggressive soaps dry out the skin.
- **Dry them well,** also between your fingers.
- **Apply hand cream** after washing them, especially if you wash them often, and at night before going to sleep.
- **Choose textures according to need:** light for the day, that do not leave your hands greasy; denser for the night or for very dry skin.
- **If you use hydroalcoholic gel frequently,** it compensates with more hydration.

## Protect them

| Situation | Protection |
|---|---|
| Cleaning and chemicals | gloves, because detergents and solvents damage the skin and nails |
| Garden, DIY and manual work | suitable work gloves |
| cold and wind | gloves and more nutritious cream |
| Sun | sunscreen on the back of the hands, which receives a lot of radiation and soon shows spots |
| Sport | gloves in disciplines that cause calluses and blisters, such as rowing or weights |

## Hardness and cracks

Hard skin appears in areas of friction and pressure. They are softened with a pumice stone or a specific file after the shower, when the skin is soft, without trying to remove them completely at once. Cracks in the fingers, common in winter and in those who work with water, improve with repair creams and protecting the hands. If the cracks bleed, do not heal within weeks, or appear blistered, itchy, or peeling, see a doctor: they may be due to eczema or contact dermatitis that needs treatment.

## nail tools

| Tool | What is it for? |
|---|---|
| Nail clippers | cut nails quickly and straight |
| nail scissors | shape with more control |
| Glass or cardboard file | smooth the edge and correct the shape |
| Orange wood stick | gently push cuticles |
| nail brush | clean under nails |
| Cuticle oil or cream | keep them flexible and hydrated |

Clean tools regularly and do not share them.

## Cut and file

1. **Cut after showering** when nails are softer.
2. **On hands,** cut leaving a slightly rounded edge that follows the shape of the finger, with a short, uniform length.
3. **On the feet,** cut straight, without rounding the corners or cutting too short, to prevent ingrown toenails.
4. **File in one direction only,** from the side towards the center, so as not to weaken the edge.
5. **Clean under the nails** with the brush.
6. **Frequency:** Fingernails grow faster than toenails; every week or every two, depending on your rhythm.

## Cuticles and hangnails

The cuticle protects the base of the nail against infections. That is why it is not advisable to cut it:

- **Push it gently** with an orange stick after the shower, without forcing.
- **Moisturize** with oil or cream.
- **Cut the hangnails,** those skins that rise next to the nail, with clean scissors, without pulling on them, because pulling opens wounds that can become infected.
- **If the skin around the nail becomes red, swollen or painful,** consult your pharmacy or doctor.

## Strong nails: what helps and what doesn't

Brittle nails or nails that split in layers are usually due to everyday causes: wetting and drying your hands many times a day, contact with detergents, dryness of the environment or using your nails as a tool to open cans or peel off labels. What helps the most is the simplest: gloves for scrubbing and cleaning, moisturizing nails and cuticles, and filing them instead of cutting them when they are fragile.

Be wary of products that promise steel nails in a few days. Supplements only make sense if there is a diagnosed deficiency, and some, such as biotin in high doses, can alter the results of certain blood tests: if you take them, tell your doctor before any tests.

## Hands that work

If you work with your hands, in the workshop, on the construction site, in the field or in the kitchen, care is even more important. Barrier creams applied before starting reduce skin contact with dirt and some products; Washing well when finished and applying a repair cream at night prevents many cracks. Take off your rings before working with tools or machinery, as we explain in the guide on [men's rings and jewelry](/es/anillos-y-joyas-masculinas/). And if you have any cut, clean it, cover it and keep your tetanus vaccination up to date, as recommended by health services.

## Nail biting

Nail biting damages the nail and the skin around it and facilitates infections. Keeping them short and filed, without edges that invite biting, helps; Also identify the times when you do it, such as stress or boredom, and keep your hands busy. There are bitter-tasting glazes that remind us of the habit. If you find it impossible to stop, talk to your doctor.

## The feet, with the same principles

- **Dry them well between your fingers,** where humidity favors fungus.
- **Alternate shoes** and let them dry between uses, as we explain in the guide on [caring for leather](/es/cuidar-el-cuero/).
- **Wear natural fiber socks** and change them daily.
- **Cut toenails straight.**
- **Go to the podiatrist** if you have ingrown toenails, painful calluses or changes in your nails. If you have diabetes, foot care requires special attention and periodic check-ups with health professionals.

## Professional manicure

More and more barbershops and beauty centers offer men's manicures: cleaning, cutting, filing, cuticle care and hydration, without polish or with a matte finish. It's a good way to learn how your nails should look. When choosing a facility, look at hygiene: metal tools should be sterilized between clients, and files and sticks should be single-use or personal. If something doesn't convince you, don't hesitate to ask.

## Signs that should be consulted

| Signal | Why consult |
|---|---|
| Thickened, yellowed, or crumbling nails | may indicate a fungal infection, which needs diagnosis and treatment |
| A new dark line along the nail | should be evaluated by a dermatologist |
| Nails that come off the bed | They may be due to trauma, infections or other causes |
| Unexplained changes in shape or color | They may reflect skin or general health problems |
| Pain, redness, or pus around the nail | infection that may require treatment |

Do not try to solve with cosmetic products what needs a diagnosis.

## A realistic routine

| Frequency | Care |
|---|---|
| every day | wash with mild soap, dry well and apply cream |
| Every week | cut and file fingernails, care for cuticles |
| Every two or three weeks | cut toenails, check calluses |
| Depending on the season | gloves in winter, sunscreen in summer |

## Frequent errors

- **Cut the cuticles.**
- **Tear off the hangnails.**
- **Round your toenails** or cut them too short.
- **Wash your hands with very hot water** and do not moisturize them.
- **Ignore changes** in color or shape in the nails.
- **Share nail clippers** and files.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should men use hand cream?

Yes. The skin on the hands is the same in men and women, and it dries out with water, soap, cold and the sun.

### How do you cut toenails properly?

Straight, without rounding the corners or cutting them too short, to prevent them from becoming ingrown.

### Can cuticles be cut?

It is not recommended: they protect the base of the nail. Just push them gently and moisturize them.

### What do I do if I have an ingrown toenail?

If it hurts, turns red or oozes, see a podiatrist or doctor. Don't try to cut it deep yourself.

## In summary

Caring for hands and nails is simple: wash your hands with mild soap, dry them well and moisturize them daily, and protect them with gloves and sunscreen when necessary. Cut fingernails with a slightly rounded edge and toenails straight, file in one direction and do not cut cuticles or remove hangnails. Apply the same principles to your feet and go to the podiatrist if you have problems. A professional manicure requires hygiene, and consult your doctor if you notice thickened nails, new dark lines, or signs of infection.
