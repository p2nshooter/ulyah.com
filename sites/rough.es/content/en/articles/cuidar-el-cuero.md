Leather is a living material in the most practical sense of the word: it absorbs and loses moisture, dries out, cracks if not nourished, and deforms if not stored properly. But it also responds wonderfully to care. Well-maintained good leather shoes improve over the years, develop a patina that no new product can imitate, and last for decades. Identical shoes, neglected, can be ruined in a couple of seasons.

In this guide you will see what you need to care for leather, the basic routine for smooth leather shoes and boots, how to get shine without damaging the leather, how to treat suede, nubuck, patent leather and oiled leather, what to do with wet footwear, how to remove salt and mold stains, and how to care for belts, bags, wallets and jackets. If you are thinking about buying footwear, start with the guide on [leather boots](/es/botas-de-cuero/).

## What you need

| Utensil or product | What is it for? |
|---|---|
| Cedar wood lasts | They maintain their shape and absorb moisture from the inside |
| shoehorn | avoid crushing the heel counter when putting on shoes |
| Horsehair brush | removes dust and shines |
| cotton cloths | apply products and polish |
| Leather cleaner | removes dirt and product residue |
| Nourishing cream | hydrates the skin and replenishes color |
| Wax or bitumen | protects the surface and adds shine |
| Brush and rubber for suede | cleans and lifts the hair of suede and nubuck |
| Waterproofing protector | reduces water and stain absorption |

Choose skin-colored or colorless products, and always test any new product on an inconspicuous area.

## The daily routine: what matters most

The most effective care is also the simplest:

1. **Use a shoehorn** always.
2. **Place cedar shoe trees** as soon as you take off your shoes, when they are still warm and moist inside.
3. **Let each pair rest** at least a day between uses, so the leather and lining can dry.
4. **Brush off dust** before storing.

With just this, the life of shoes is significantly extended.

## Cleanse, nourish and protect smooth skin

Every few weeks, depending on usage:

1. **Remove the laces** and remove the dust with the horsehair brush.
2. **Clean the surface** with a slightly damp cloth or a specific cleaner, without soaking the skin.
3. **Let dry** at room temperature.
4. **Apply nourishing cream** in thin layers with a cloth, with circular movements, insisting on the bending areas.
5. **Let it penetrate** a few minutes and brush vigorously.
6. **Apply wax or polish** if you want more protection and shine, especially on the toe and heel.
7. **Polish** with a clean cloth or brush.
8. **Go over the edges** of the sole with a specific product if they need it.

Less is more: several thin layers work better than a thick one, which leaves residue and weighs down the skin.

## The mirror shine

The mirror shine is achieved in the rigid areas of the shoe, the toe and the counter, never in the areas that bend, because the wax would crack. Very thin layers of hard wax are applied with a cotton cloth and a few drops of water, in small circles, allowing each layer to set before the next. It requires patience, but the result transforms dress shoes.

## Suede and nubuck

- **Brush with the specific brush** to remove dust and lift hair.
- **Erase dried stains** with a suede eraser.
- **Apply waterproofing protector** before first use and from time to time.
- **To lift flattened hair,** a little steam from a distance and subsequent brushing help.
- **Do not use creams or waxes,** which weigh down the hair and darken the skin.

## Patent leather and oiled leather

- **Patent leather:** clean it with a soft cloth and a specific product; Avoid bending it in intense cold, which encourages cracks, and store it without it rubbing against other leathers, because it can transfer color.
- **Oiled leather:** it is nourished with specific fats or oils for this type of leather, which maintain its appearance and water resistance. The light marks that appear when folding it are normal and part of its character.

## wet shoes

1. **Remove excess water** with a cloth.
2. **Remove the insoles** if they are removable.
3. **Fill the inside with newspaper** and change it when it is damp.
4. **Allow to dry at room temperature,** away from radiators, stoves and dryers, which harden and crack the leather.
5. **When they are dry,** place the lasts and nourish the skin.

## Salt and mold stains

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| White circles of salt or water | Clean with a cloth moistened with a mild mixture of water and white vinegar, let dry and nourish |
| Mold from wet storage | remove it outdoors with a brush, clean with a cloth moistened in a mixture of water and alcohol tested before in a hidden area, dry and nourish |
| Grease stains | some powdered absorbents help if applied early; on valuable pieces, consult a professional |

Mold is common in damp homes and coastal areas. The best prevention is to store the leather clean, nourished, in a ventilated place and in cloth bags, never plastic.

## Belts, bags, wallets and jackets

- **Belts:** alternate their use, nourish them from time to time and store them rolled or hanging, without bending them abruptly.
- **Bags and handbags:** do not overfill them, clean them with a cloth and store them stuffed with paper and inside cloth bags.
- **Pocket wallets:** avoid filling them so much that they become deformed; nourish them occasionally.
- **Leather jackets:** Hang them on wide hangers, keep them away from the sun and heat sources, let them dry naturally if they get wet, and use a specialized leather dry cleaner for major stains.

## Patina: aging well

Patina is the change in appearance that leather acquires with use: the color darkens or becomes more nuanced, shine appears in the areas of friction and the surface gains depth. It is produced by exposure to light, contact with the skin and natural oils, small scratches that soften with care and the passage of time. It is especially visible in vegetable-tanned and aniline-finished leathers, which show the natural surface of the leather.

A good patina is not created in an afternoon: it is the result of years of use and regular care. That is why it is advisable not to go overboard with products that cover the surface or with dyes that uniform the color. Nourishing, protecting and letting leather live is the best way for it to age with dignity.

## Laces, insoles and smells

- **Laces:** change them as soon as they become frayed; Waxed cotton ones last longer and look cleaner on dress shoes.
- **Insoles:** the leather ones absorb moisture and can be replaced when they wear out.
- **Odors:** airing the shoes, using cedar shoe trees and alternating pairs is the most effective. A little baking soda inside overnight, carefully removed afterwards, helps in specific cases.
- **Socks:** Those made of natural fibers, such as cotton or wool, reduce humidity and, with it, odors and wear on the lining.

## Frequent errors

- **Dry wet shoes next to a radiator.**
- **Store the leather in plastic bags** or in damp places.
- **Apply too much product** at once.
- **Use creams or waxes on suede** and nubuck.
- **Put on shoes without a shoehorn** and crush the heel.
- **Wear the same pair every day.**

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I put cream on my shoes?

Depends on use and climate. As a guide, every few weeks in frequently used shoes, or when the skin looks dry or dull.

### Are plastic lasts useful?

They retain their shape somewhat, but do not absorb moisture. Cedar ones are much better for leather.

### Can I wash leather shoes in the washing machine?

No. Abundant water, detergent and spinning damage the leather, adhesives and structure of the shoe.

### What is the difference between cream and wax?

The cream nourishes and moisturizes the skin; The wax creates a protective layer on the surface and adds shine. The ideal is to use both.

## In summary

Taking care of leather is more a matter of perseverance than expensive products: shoehorn, cedar lasts, rest between uses and brushing are the gestures that extend the life of the footwear the most. Every few weeks, clean, nourish with cream in thin layers and protect with wax, reserving the mirror shine for the toe and heel. Treat suede and nubuck with a brush, rubber and protector, never with creams. Dry wet footwear away from heat, remove salt and mold with gentle mixtures, and store purses, belts, and jackets clean, nourished, and in cloth bags.
