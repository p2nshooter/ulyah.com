If you look inside a gold ring purchased in Spain with a magnifying glass, it is normal to find several tiny marks: a number like 750, a small symbol and some letters or a drawing. They are not decorations. They are the contrasts, the marks that identify who manufactured or imported the piece and what proportion of precious metal it contains. Knowing how to read them is one of the most useful tools to buy, sell or inherit jewelry judiciously, because they turn a statement from the seller into verifiable data.

In this guide you will see what the grade of a precious metal is, what the most common numbers mean, what Spanish regulations generally require, how the old marks of Spanish silverware are read, what acronyms such as GP, GF or vermeil indicate that appear on imported pieces, how the grade is checked when there are no marks and what precautions to take. It is not legal advice: if you have specific doubts, consult the consumer administration or a testing laboratory.

## What is the law of a metal

The grade is the proportion of precious metal contained in an alloy, expressed in thousandths. A 750 grade gold contains 750 parts of fine gold per thousand; the rest are other metals, such as silver or copper, which give it hardness and color.

| Brand | metal | Usual equivalence |
|---|---|---|
| 999 | gold, silver or platinum | practically pure |
| 916 | gold | 22 carats |
| 750 | gold | 18 carats, the traditional law of Spanish jewelry |
| 585 | gold | 14 carats |
| 375 | gold | 9 carats |
| 925 | silver | sterling or sterling silver |
| 800 | silver | lower grade, common in antique pieces |
| 950 | platinum | common law of platinum in jewelry |

Karats are another way of expressing the same thing: pure gold has 24 carats, so 18 carats are equivalent to 18/24 parts of gold, that is, 750 thousandths.

## What Spanish regulations require

Spain regulates objects made of precious metals through a 1985 law and its implementing regulations. Broadly speaking, to market gold, silver and platinum objects, the regulations require two types of marks:

| Brand | What does it indicate? |
|---|---|
| Origin Identification Contrast | identifies the responsible manufacturer or importer, registered in an official registry |
| Warranty contrast | guarantees the law of metal; It is applied by official or authorized contrast laboratories |

The regulations also contemplate exceptions, for example for some very light objects or for specific cases established by the regulations. In practice, it is reasonable to expect that a new precious metal jewel purchased in a Spanish store will have its hallmarks, and ask if it does not have them.

In addition, the law regulates pieces that are not solid: plated or plated objects must not bear marks that could be confused with those made of solid precious metal, and their description must make it clear what they are.

## Colored gold: same law, different alloy

The grade number indicates how much gold is in the alloy, but not its color. A white gold ring, a rose gold ring and a yellow gold ring can all be 750 thousandths: what changes is the proportion and type of the other metals.

| Color | How to obtain | Observations |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow | gold with silver and copper in balanced proportions | the classic color |
| Pink or red | more copper in the alloy | more intense the more copper |
| White | gold with palladium or nickel, and often a rhodium plating | The bathroom wears out with use and can be renewed |
| Green | gold with more silver | uncommon |

White gold deserves a warning. Some alloys contain nickel, a metal that causes contact allergies in many people. European regulations limit the amount of nickel that can be released by objects that are in prolonged contact with the skin, but if you have an allergy, it is advisable to ask about the composition or choose alloys with palladium. Furthermore, the rhodium plating that gives the brilliant white disappears little by little and reveals the slightly yellowish tone of the alloy; Renewing it from time to time is normal maintenance.

## How to read the markings on a jewel

1. **Look in protected areas:** the inside of ring rings, the clasps of necklaces and bracelets, the hooks of earrings or the back of pendants.
2. **Use a 10x magnifying glass** and good light, because the marks are tiny.
3. **Identifies the grade number** and the metal to which it corresponds.
4. **Look for the manufacturer's brand,** which is usually a logo or letters.
5. **Look for the laboratory mark,** if there is one.
6. **Please note wear:** On tightly sized or heavily worn rings, the markings may have partially faded.

## Old brands of Spanish silverware

Historical Spanish silversmithing generally used a three-mark system, which allows specialists to date and locate many pieces:

| Brand | What did it indicate? |
|---|---|
| Locality mark | the city where the piece was marked, often with a heraldic symbol |
| Contrast mark or marker | the official in charge of checking the law, sometimes with a year |
| silversmith's mark | the author of the piece, with his name or initials |

Interpreting those brands requires specialized catalogs and experience, because they changed over time and between cities. If you have antique silver with marks, a specialist or auction house can help you identify it, as we explain in the guide on [jewelry auctions](/es/subastas-de-joyas/).

## Acronyms that do not indicate solid metal

On imported or antique pieces, acronyms often appear that can be confusing:

| Acronym or term | What does it mean |
|---|---|
| GP | gold plating by bath; very thin layer |
| HGE | slightly thicker gold plating, but still a plating |
| GF | rolled gold: a layer of gold mechanically bonded to another metal, thicker than a plating |
| RGP | laminated plating with a thinner layer of gold than GF |
| Vermeil | silver with gold plating |
| EP or EPNS | silver plating on non-precious metal |

Sometimes these acronyms accompany a number, such as "18K GP", which does not mean that the piece is made of 18 karat gold, but that the plating is. It is a frequent source of misunderstandings when inheriting or selling jewelry.

## How the law is proven without marks

| Method | How it works | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| touchstone | The piece is rubbed on a dark stone and acids of different strengths are applied to the mark. | It leaves a small streak and is not suitable for dipped pieces, which give misleading results. |
| X-ray fluorescence | a device analyzes the composition of the surface without damaging it | it measures above all the surface; a bath can falsify the result |
| laboratory test | analysis of a small sample | the most accurate, but requires taking material |

Gold buying businesses usually use one of the first two methods. If you sell a piece, ask that the test be done in front of you and that the result be explained to you, as we tell you in the guide on [selling gold and jewelry](/es/vender-oro-y-joyas/).

## Precautions when purchasing

- **Request that the grade and metal be listed on the invoice,** along with the weight if relevant.
- **Check the contrasts with a magnifying glass** before paying, especially with second-hand pieces.
- **Be wary of brands that seem recent on old pieces,** or imprecise engravings.
- **Do not confuse the color with the grade:** white, rose or yellow gold can have the same grade with different colors.
- **Remember that brands can be counterfeited:** in important purchases, an independent analysis is the best guarantee.

The guarantees and rights you have as a consumer are explained in the guide on [buying jewelry in Spain](/es/comprar-joyas-en-espana/).

## Frequent errors

- **Interpret “18K GP” as solid gold.**
- **Think that the number indicates carats** when it indicates thousandths, or the other way around.
- **Assume that an unmarked piece** is not precious metal.
- **Rely on a surface test** on parts that may be dipped.
- **Ignore the manufacturer's brand,** which also gives information.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does 750 on a ring mean?

That the alloy contains 750 thousandths of fine gold: it is 18 carat gold, the traditional law of Spanish jewelry.

### Is sterling silver pure silver?

No. Sterling silver is marked 925 and contains 925 thousandths of silver; the rest is usually copper, which hardens it.

### Is a jewel without marks fake?

Not necessarily. Many antique, foreign or repaired pieces lack or have lost markings. To know, you have to analyze them.

### What is vermeil?

Silver with gold plating. It has the color of gold, but its material value is that of silver plus a thin layer of gold.

Antique silverware used locality, hallmarks, and silversmith's marks. Acronyms like GP, GF or vermeil indicate plating or plating, not solid metal.
