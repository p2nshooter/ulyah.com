A perfume is the last thing you put on and often the first thing others notice. In Spain, furthermore, cologne has been part of daily life since childhood: squirting fresh cologne after a shower is a deep-rooted custom in many families. Choosing your own fragrance, however, can be disconcerting. Perfume stores offer hundreds of bottles, the names of the concentrations are confusing and what smells wonderful on a strip of paper can be very different on the skin an hour later.

In this guide you will see the difference between a cologne, an eau de toilette and an eau de parfum, how a perfume is structured into top, heart and base notes, what the major olfactory families are, how to try and choose without saturating yourself, how to apply it and in what quantity, how to preserve it so that it does not spoil, what precautions to take if you have sensitive skin and how to avoid fakes.

## The concentrations

The difference between some types and others is, above all, the proportion of aromatic essences dissolved in alcohol. The figures vary according to each house, so it is advisable to take them as a guide.

| Denomination | Approximate concentration of essences | Usual duration on the skin |
|---|---|---|
| cologne water | low | short; fresh and light |
| Eau de toilette | moderate | average; the most common in men's perfumery |
| Eau de parfum | high | long; more intense and involving |
| Parfum or extract | very high | very long; used in small quantities |

More concentration does not necessarily mean better perfume: it means more intensity and duration. A well-constructed eau de toilette can be more pleasant in summer than a dense extract.

## Top, heart and base notes

A perfume evolves on the skin as its components evaporate:

| Phase | When is perceived | Examples of notes |
|---|---|---|
| Exit | the first minutes | citrus fruits such as bergamot or lemon, aromatic herbs, fresh spices |
| heart | from a few minutes to several hours | flowers, spices, lavender, green notes |
| Background | the following hours | woods, amber, musks, vanilla, leather, resins |

That is why you should never decide a purchase based on the first impression: the exit does not last long, and what you will carry most of the day is the heart and substance.

## The olfactory families

| Family | Traits | When it works |
|---|---|---|
| Citrus | bergamot, lemon, orange, grapefruit; immediate freshness | summer day after shower |
| aromatic | lavender, rosemary, sage and Mediterranean herbs | almost always; very classic in men's perfumery |
| Fern or fougère | lavender, herbal notes and warm base | a barbershop classic |
| Woody | cedar, vetiver, sandalwood | office, autumn, all year |
| Cyprus | contrast of fresh citrus and mossy backgrounds | elegant and sober |
| Oriental or amber | spices, resins, vanilla, amber | winter, night |
| Leather | notes of tanned leather, sometimes smoky | autumn and winter, marked personality |
| Aquatic | marine and clean feeling | summer, daily use |

The aromatic herbs of the Mediterranean bush, such as rosemary, thyme or lavender, appear in many classic fragrances and are especially fresh in the Spanish climate.

## The colony, a very Spanish custom

Eau de cologne was born at the beginning of the 18th century in the German city of Cologne, by perfumers of Italian origin, as a light mixture of citrus and aromatic essences in alcohol. Its freshness conquered Europe, and in Spain it took root strongly: lavender, lemon or herbal colognes, in large bottles to be used generously after a shower, are part of the domestic landscape of several generations, and there is even a long tradition of gentle colognes for babies and children.

This custom explains why many Spanish men prefer fresh and clean fragrances for everyday use, and reserve the most intense perfumes for the evening or special occasions. A good classic cologne is still an excellent option for the hot months.

## Perfume and seasons

| Station | Families that usually work |
|---|---|
| summer | citrus, aromatic, aquatic and light colognes, reapplied if necessary |
| spring and autumn | aromatic, soft woody, chypres |
| winter | intense woody, oriental, amber and leather |

Some people prefer a single signature fragrance that identifies them all year round; others enjoy a small olfactory wardrobe that changes with the season and occasion. Both options are valid.

## How to try and choose

1. **Start with strips of paper** to discard what you don't like, without weighing down the skin.
2. **Skin test only two or three** fragrances maximum at each visit, one on each wrist or forearm.
3. **Wait:** leave the store, go about your day and smell your skin after half an hour and a few hours later.
4. **Ask for samples** to try the perfume for several days in real situations.
5. **Keep the weather in mind:** in the heat, fragrances project more; a very intense one can be heavy in August.
6. **Think about use:** one for day and work, another for night or winter, is a good starting point.

Each skin's chemistry influences how a perfume is perceived, and what looks great on a friend may not work for you.

## How to apply it

- **On clean and hydrated skin,** where the perfume lasts longer.
- **In warm areas** such as the neck, chest or wrists.
- **No rubbing:** it is not necessary, and can alter the first notes.
- **In moderation:** one or two sprays are usually enough in an eau de toilette; less in high concentrations.
- **On clothing, be careful:** some fragrances stain delicate or light fabrics; Try first in a hidden area.

The rule of courtesy is simple: perfume should be perceived when someone approaches, not from across the room. In the office, on public transport, at the gym, in health centers or at meals and tastings, moderation is even more important.

## Combine with other products

Deodorant, aftershave, shampoo, and cream also smell. If their aromas are intense, they can clash with your perfume. Choose unscented products or products from the same olfactory line, and let the fragrance be the protagonist. ## Preserve the perfume

- **Away from light,** especially direct sun.
- **Away from heat and temperature changes:** the bathroom is not the best place.
- **With the cap tightly closed.**
- **In its box,** if you want to protect it even more.

Well preserved, a perfume lasts for years. If you notice that the output smells sour or has changed color a lot, it has probably degraded.

## Sensitive skin and allergies

- **Read the label:** European regulations require certain potentially allergenic substances to be indicated when they exceed certain concentrations.
- **Do not use perfume on irritated,** freshly shaved or wounded skin.
- **Be careful with the sun:** some citrus essences can cause spots on skin exposed to intense sun; Avoid perfuming the areas you are going to tan.
- **If itching, redness or eczema appears,** stop using the product and consult a doctor or pharmacist.

## Avoid fakes

Imitations of brand name perfumes circulate in flea markets, social networks and unreliable online stores. In addition to not smelling like the original, they may contain uncontrolled ingredients that irritate the skin.

- **Purchase at authorized perfumeries and stores.**
- **Be wary of prices much lower than usual** on new fragrances.
- **Check the packaging:** Sloppy finishes, typos, or a batch code that doesn't match between the box and the bottle are red flags.

## Frequent errors

- **Buy based on first impression** of the outlet.
- **Trying too many fragrances** at once.
- **Apply excessively.**
- **Store the perfume in the bathroom** or in the sun.
- **Mix aromas** of many different products.
- **Buy brand name perfumes** at impossible prices.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between eau de toilette and eau de parfum?

Eau de parfum has a higher concentration of essences, so it is usually more intense and long-lasting.

### Why does my perfume last a short time?

It may be due to concentration, skin type, heat, or olfactory family: citrus fragrances are more volatile. Applying it to hydrated skin helps.

### How many fragrances do I need?

There is no number. Many people manage with a cool one for the day and a warmer one for the night or winter.

### Can clothes be scented?

Yes, be careful: some fragrances stain delicate fabrics. Test first in an area that cannot be seen.

## In summary

A perfume is distinguished by its concentration, from light cologne to intense extract, and evolves on the skin from top notes to heart and base notes. Knowing the olfactory families, citrus, aromatic, fern, woody, chypre, oriental, leather or aquatic, helps to orient yourself. Try a few fragrances at a time, on your skin, and wait hours before deciding. Apply it sparingly to clean and hydrated skin, keep it away from light and heat, take care of sensitive skin and the sun, and buy from authorized stores to avoid counterfeits.
