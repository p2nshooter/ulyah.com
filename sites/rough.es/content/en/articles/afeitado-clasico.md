For decades, men's shaving became a formality: a few seconds with a cartridge razor and spray foam, in a hurry before leaving the house. In recent years, many men have rediscovered classic shaving, with a safety razor, brush and soap, and some even with a razor. It's not nostalgia: done well, the classic shave can be closer, irritate the skin less and turn a routine gesture into a few minutes of calm at the start of the day. Traditional barbershops, which have reopened in many Spanish cities, have contributed to this renaissance.

In this guide you will see what tools exist and what advantages each one has, how to prepare the skin and beard, how to make a good lather with a brush, the step-by-step technique with safety razors, what care to apply afterwards, how to avoid irritation, cuts and ingrown hairs, and what hygiene rules should be respected. If you have persistent skin problems, consult a dermatologist: this guide is informative and is not a substitute for medical opinion.

## The tools

| Tool | Advantages | To take into account |
|---|---|---|
| safety razor | a single double-edged blade, good sharpness, less pulling; economical replacement blades | requires learning the angle and not tightening |
| Barber razor | maximum rush and traditional ritual | long learning curve; requires settling and sharpening the edge |
| Interchangeable blade knife | like the barber, but with disposable blades | equally demanding in technique |
| Cartridge razor | easy and fast | several leaves can irritate sensitive skin; expensive spare parts |
| electric machine | fast, without foam and with little risk of cuts | minor rush; may irritate sensitive skin |

To start with classic shaving, the safety razor is the most reasonable option: those who are patient in the first weeks learn quickly.

## Prepare skin and beard

The secret to a good shave is before touching the blade:

1. **Soften your beard:** Shave after showering or apply a warm, wet towel to your face for a couple of minutes. Hydrated hair is cut much more easily.
2. **Wash your face** with warm water to remove oil and dirt.
3. **Pre-shave oil, if you use it:** a few drops can help with sensitive skin, although they are not essential.

## foam with brush

| Element | Options |
|---|---|
| brush | natural badger or boar hair, or synthetic fibers that retain water and dry quickly |
| shaving soap | hard tablet that gives a dense foam |
| shaving cream | easier to ride for beginners |

How to mount the foam:

1. **Moisten the brush** in warm water and wring it out slightly.
2. **Load the brush** by rotating it over the soap, or take a small amount of cream.
3. **Mount the foam** in a bowl or directly on the face with circular movements, adding water little by little.
4. **Look for a dense, shiny foam,** like whipped cream, without large bubbles.
5. **Apply it** with circular movements, which lift the hair, and finish with brush strokes in a single direction.

## The technique, step by step

1. **Know your beard:** observe which direction the hair grows in each area; It usually changes on the neck and under the jaw.
2. **Find the angle:** Place the head of the clipper on your cheek with the handle perpendicular to your face and lower it little by little until the blade begins to cut, around thirty degrees.
3. **Do not apply pressure:** the weight of the clipper is sufficient.
4. **Short strokes:** of a few centimeters, tightening the skin with the other hand.
5. **First pass in favor of the hair,** in the direction in which it grows.
6. **Rinse the blade** frequently.
7. **Second pass, if you need it,** with new foam, perpendicular to the growth.
8. **Count it only if your skin tolerates it:** it gives the maximum closeness, but it is the pass that irritates the most.

With the razor, the principles are the same, with a lower angle and a technique that should be learned with help or in a barbershop.

## The difficult areas

- **Upper lip:** Tense the skin by bringing your lip over your teeth and make very short strokes; The nose gets in the way, so use the part of the blade closest to the tip of the razor.
- **Chin:** It is curved and usually has dense hair. Work in small strokes, changing the angle of your hand instead of applying pressure.
- **Neck and under the jaw:** Hair usually grows in swirls and in different directions. Check each area and extreme softness on the nut.
- **Sideburns:** Mark your line with the edge of the blade, looking straight at the mirror so that they are symmetrical.

## Sensitive skin and hard beards

If your skin is sensitive, reduce the number of strokes, prefer unscented soaps and creams, avoid lotions with alcohol, and test any new product on a small area before using it on your entire face. Some people tolerate alternate-day shaving better than daily shaving, because the skin has time to recover.

If your beard is hard or very dense, spend more time preparing: a long shower or two applications of a hot towel, a well-hydrated foam and a new blade make all the difference. In these cases, rushing to shave is the most common cause of irritation and cuts. If every shave is a problem, an electric shaver or a well-groomed short beard may be better options for your skin.

## after shave

- **Rinse with cold water** to remove soap residue and close the sensation of heat.
- **Alum stone:** passed on damp skin, it has an astringent effect and helps with small cuts. If it stings a lot, it indicates that you have irritated the skin.
- **Balm or Lotion:** Alcohol-free balms are gentler for sensitive skin; Lotions with alcohol refresh, but can be drying.
- **Hydration:** a simple cream completes the care.

## Irritation, cuts and ingrown hairs

| Problem | Usual cause | To do |
|---|---|---|
| Irritation | pressure, worn blade, too many passes, against the grain | less pressure, new leaves, fewer passes |
| Courteous | pressure, excessive angle or lateral movements | gentle pressure on the cut and alum or a hemostatic pencil |
| ingrown hairs | curly hair that re-enters the skin, very close shave | shave in favor of the hair, exfoliate gently and don't rush so much |
| Pimples after shaving | irritation or infection of the follicles | tool hygiene; If they persist or hurt, consult a doctor |

If pimples, redness or lesions do not improve or become infected, do not try to solve it with more products: consult a dermatologist, because some beard skin conditions need treatment.

## Hygiene

- **Do not share blades or clippers:** they can transmit infections through small cuts.
- **Change the blade frequently** as soon as you notice pulling.
- **Dry and store the razor** in a ventilated place.
- **Rinse the brush** and let it dry with the hair down or in the air, never in a damp closed case.
- **Dispose of used leaves** in a closed container so that no one gets cut.
- **At the barbershop,** check that they use a new blade for each client and that they disinfect the tools.

## Shaving and barbershop

A razor shave in a barbershop is a good way to discover the classic ritual and learn by observing: hot towels, foam, razor, balm and massage. Many barbers give helpful advice on the direction of beard growth and the right products for each skin type. If you prefer to grow a beard, consult the guide on [beard care](/es/cuidado-de-la-barba/).

## Frequent errors

- **Shave dry** or with unsoftened beard.
- **Squeeze the razor** against the skin.
- **Use dull blades** to save.
- **Go against the grain from the first pass.**
- **Share blades.**
- **Ignore persistent irritations.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the safety razor irritate less than cartridges?

Many people do, because it cuts with a single blade and without pressure. But it depends on the technique and each skin.

### How often do I change the blade?

When you start to notice pulling or irritation. It depends on the beard and the frequency of shaving.

### Is the brush necessary?

It is not essential, but it helps to lift the hair, distribute the foam well and gently exfoliate the skin.

### What do I do if I always get pimples when shaving?

Review your technique, change the blade more often, reduce the strokes and, if the pimples persist, consult a dermatologist.

## In summary

Classic shaving with a safety razor, brush, and soap can give an excellent close with less irritation. The key is in the preparation: beard softened with hot water and a dense foam. Then, an angle of about thirty degrees, without pressure, short strokes and first in favor of the hair, leaving the against the grain only for skin that tolerates it. Finish with cold water, alum and a mild balm. Change the blade often, don't share blades, and consult a dermatologist if irritation or pimples persist.
