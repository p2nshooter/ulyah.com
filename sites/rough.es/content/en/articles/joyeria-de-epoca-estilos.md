An antique jewel is a document of its time. The way diamonds were cut, the metals available, the workshop techniques, clothing fashion and even historical events left their mark on each brooch, ring or earring. Therefore, whoever learns to read these traces can place a piece in its era quite accurately, distinguish an original from a reproduction and better value what they buy, what they inherit or what they intend to sell.

In this guide you will see what terms such as antique, vintage or "style" mean, what are the great jewelry styles of the last two and a half centuries and how to recognize them, what technical clues help date a piece, what alterations and reproductions should be detected, what to check before buying and how to preserve an antique piece of jewelry without erasing its history.

## Old, vintage and "in style"

| Term | Common use in commerce |
|---|---|
| Ancient | pieces that are more than a hundred years old, approximately |
| Vintage | pieces several decades old, without reaching that threshold |
| vintage | manufactured in the period whose style shows |
| Style or "type" | recently manufactured imitating an old style |

An “art deco style” piece may be beautiful and legitimate, but it does not have the value of a piece of jewelry made in the 1920s. The description should make it clear.

## The great styles

| Style | Approximate dates | Characteristic features |
|---|---|---|
| Georgian | until the 1830s | handwork, settings closed at the back, rose-cut diamonds and antique cuts, metal sheets under the stones |
| Victorian | 1837 to 1901 | romanticism, snakes and hearts, mourning jewelry with jet, garnets, small pearls, lots of gold |
| Modernism or art nouveau | around 1890 to 1910 | organic shapes, women, dragonflies and flowers, translucent and openwork enamels, opals and moonstones |
| Edwardian or belle époque | around 1901 to 1915 | platinum, diamonds, garlands and bows, lace look, pearls, fine grained edges |
| art deco | around 1920 to 1935 | geometry, color contrasts, platinum, baguette and calibrated cuts, onyx, coral, jade and emerald |
| Vintage | around 1935 to 1950 | rose and yellow gold, large volumes, mechanical loops and shapes, large colored stones |
| mid century | 1950s and 1960s | textured gold, naturalistic shapes, cocktail rings |

The dates are indicative: the styles overlap, change depending on the country and coexist with local fashions.

## The Spanish case

In Spain, mid-19th century jewelry is often associated with the reign of Elizabeth II, and Elizabethan jewelry is used to refer to gold earrings, brooches and accessories, often with pearls, seed pearls and colored stones. ## Clues to date a piece

| Track | What does it indicate? |
|---|---|
| Diamond cut | pink and antique carvings on pieces from before the 20th century; modern bright in latest pieces |
| Closed or open setting | The closed back settings are typical of antique pieces; the open ones let in more light |
| metal | Platinum became widespread in jewelry at the beginning of the 20th century; white gold became popular later |
| Closures and systems | the safety clasps on brooches, screw and clip earrings correspond to different periods |
| Construction | Handmade pieces vs. cast or stamped pieces |
| Contrasts | locality, hallmark and silversmith marks on antique pieces; modern brands in recent |
| Wear | Natural wear is concentrated in areas of friction; artificial aging is usually uniform |

No clue is enough on its own. Dating a jewel is adding coherent clues, and in valuable pieces the opinion of a specialist is advisable.

## Ancient diamond cuts

The cut is one of the most reliable clues, because each era cut diamonds with the tools and tastes of their time.

| Size | Traits | Usual time |
|---|---|---|
| Pink | flat base and triangular faceted dome, without pavilion | very common until the 19th century |
| Old mine | cushion outline, high crown, small table and large collete | 18th and 19th centuries |
| ancient european | round outline, high crown, small table and open collete | late 19th century and first decades of the 20th |
| Bright modern | proportions calculated to maximize shine, tiny or non-existent collete | prevails throughout the 20th century |

The open collar of the ancient carvings is visible across the table as a small dark circle in the center of the stone, a very useful hallmark. In 1919, engineer Marcel Tolkowsky published a study on the ideal proportions of the brilliant that had a decisive influence on modern carving.

## Materials that reveal an era

Some materials only exist from a certain date, and their presence helps rule out impossible attributions:

- **Synthetic rubies and sapphires:** They have been manufactured on a commercial scale since the beginning of the 20th century, and appear in many pieces in the following decades.
- **Cultured pearls:** became widespread from the 1920s onwards; a string of perfectly round cultured pearls does not fit into an 18th century piece.
- **Antique plastics:** Bakelite and celluloid are typical of interwar jewelry.
- **Platinum:** Its use in jewelry extends to the beginning of the 20th century.
- **Imitation glass or rhinestones:** It has been used for centuries, also in quality pieces, and does not always indicate a lesser jewel.

## Alterations and reproductions

- **Reproductions:** new pieces with antique style, sometimes artificially aged.
- **Married pieces:** jewelry formed with parts of different pieces.
- **Transformations:** brooches converted into pendants, redone earrings or widened rings.
- **Replaced stones:** lost stones replaced by others of a different era or material.
- **Claws redone:** repairs necessary, but must be declared.
- **Old Cut Cultured Diamonds:** Today, old style cut lab diamonds are manufactured and can be set into antique or antique style pieces.

Many of these interventions are legitimate if declared. The problem arises when they are hidden and the piece is charged as an intact original.

## What to check before buying

1. **The written description:** era, materials, stones, known alterations.
2. **Condition:** claws, settings, clasps, enamels, pearls and possible repairs.
3. **The stones:** in diamonds of a certain size, a report confirming that they are natural; in colored stones, indicating treatments.
4. **Wearability:** Adjusting the size of an antique ring may damage decorations or settings.
5. **The origin of the seller:** experienced antique dealers, specialized jewelers and auction houses.
6. **The return policy,** in case a specialist contradicts the description.

If you buy at auction, read the guide on [jewelry auctions](/es/subastas-de-joyas/).

## Preserve an old jewel

- **Respect its history:** avoid excessive polishing that erases details and marks.
- **Check the settings** with a jeweler specialized in antique pieces.
- **Keep the original parts** that are replaced, such as stones or closures.
- **Restring pearl necklaces** regularly.
- **Store it separately,** protected from shocks and moisture.
- **Documents its history:** who took it, where it came from, what repairs it has had.

## What gives value to a period piece

| Factor | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Manufacturing quality | excellent pieces from any era are valued more |
| Condition and originality | intact and unaltered pieces are rarer |
| Signature or author | a recognized firm can multiply the value |
| Materials | metal, stones and their quality |
| Representative style | pieces that summarize their era well |
| Possibility of use | wearable pieces are more in demand |

## Frequent errors

- **Confusing "old style" with real age.**
- **Do not ask about transformations** and replaced stones.
- **Over polishing** old pieces.
- **Adjust sizes** without assessing the risk for the piece.
- **Appraise antique jewelry** by its metal only.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know if a jewel is really old?

Adding clues: stone size, type of setting, metal, clasps, construction, contrasts and wear. For valuable pieces, consult a specialist.

### Is an old jewel worth more than a new one?

Depends. Some antique pieces are worth much more due to their age, quality or signature; others are worth little more than their materials.

### Can an old ring be resized?

Often yes, but with risks for the decoration, settings or enamels. Do it with a jeweler with experience in antique pieces.

### What is a married piece?

A jewel formed by joining parts of different pieces. It is not always a deception, but it must be declared.

## In summary

Period jewelry reflects its time: the handwork and antique carvings of the Georgian period, Victorian romanticism and mourning, Spanish Elizabethan jewelry, the organic shapes and enamels of modernism, Edwardian platinum and lace, the geometry of Art Deco and the volumes of retro style. To date a piece, combine clues from cut, setting, metal, clasps, construction, and contrasts. Monitor reproductions, matching pieces, transformations and replaced stones, require a written description and preserve antique jewelry respecting its history.
