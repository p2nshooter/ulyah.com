Few situations raise as many questions as an invitation with a dress code. What exactly does "dark suit" mean? Can you wear a morning suit to an afternoon wedding? Is a tie necessary in an interview for a technology company? What does one wear to go to the Fair? Getting it right is not about looking more elegant than anyone else, but about respecting the occasion and those who organize it, and feeling comfortable knowing that you are appropriately dressed.

In this guide you will see what each dress code means, from the most formal to the most informal, how to apply them in the most common situations in Spain, such as day and night weddings, communions, funerals, interviews, fairs or dinners, and what details, from the tie to the socks, make the difference. If you're building your wardrobe from scratch, start with the guide on the [essential men's wardrobe](/es/armario-masculino-esencial/).

## The codes, from most to least formal

| Code | What does it imply? | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Tailcoat | jacket short in front and with skirts in the back, white vest and bow tie, stiff-fronted shirt | galas of maximum solemnity, increasingly rare |
| morning coat | jacket with rounded tails, gray striped pants, vest and tie | daytime ceremonies, especially morning weddings |
| Tuxedo | jacket with satin lapels, trousers with braid, black bow tie | evening events, galas, formal dinners |
| dark suit | navy blue or charcoal gray suit, light shirt and tie | afternoon weddings, official events, formal dinners |
| Cocktail or semi-formal | suit or jacket with dress pants; tie can be optional | celebrations, inaugurations, dinners |
| Casual grooming | blazer or cardigan with chinos or dark jeans, without a tie | meetings, current offices, informal dinners |
| Casual | neat clothes, no jacket | friends meetings, leisure |

In Spanish invitations it is common to find the formula "etiquette" to indicate a tuxedo at evening events, although it is not always used precisely. When in doubt, ask the hosts: it is much better than being wrong.

## Weddings

### by day

In Spain, the morning suit is the traditional garment for tomorrow's weddings, especially for the groom, the best man and his closest relatives. Guests can wear it if the invitation indicates it or if it is customary in the family; If not, a dark suit is always correct. The classic rule says that the morning coat is a day garment and should not be worn after sunset.

### Afternoon and night

For afternoon weddings, a dark suit is the safest option. If the invitation indicates a tuxedo, a tuxedo is worn. At summer weddings, especially on the coast or in the countryside, hosts sometimes ask for a lighter style: light, linen or cotton suits, without this meaning neglecting the ensemble.

### What a guest should not do

- **Dress white** from head to toe, a color that is associated with the bride.
- **Compete with the groom** in formality or in attracting attention.
- **Take off your jacket** before the groom or the hosts do so at the banquet.

## Communions and baptisms

They are daytime family celebrations, with a more relaxed tone than a wedding. A light or dark suit, or a blazer with dress pants, are appropriate. The tie depends on the family environment: in religious ceremonies it is usually appreciated.

## Funerals

Sobriety is the norm: dark suit, white shirt, black or dark tie and black shoes. If you don't have a suit, a dark blazer with gray or black pants is a worthy alternative. Avoid bright colors, prints and ostentatious accessories.

## Job interviews

The general criterion is to go one point more formal than the usual clothing in the company. For traditional sectors, such as banking, law or administration, the navy or gray suit with a tie continues to be the reference. For technological or creative companies, a blazer with dress pants or chinos and a shirt without a tie is usually appropriate. Find out about the company beforehand: wearing a suit to an interview where everyone dresses informally is not a serious mistake either, but getting it right conveys that you have done your homework.

## The office

Many Spanish offices have adopted the informal outfit: blazer or cardigan, shirt with or without a tie, chinos or wool trousers and leather shoes or clean leather sneakers in more relaxed environments. Friday is usually even more informal. Observe how the people around you dress more responsibly: it is the best guide.

## Fairs and festivals

At the April Fair in Seville and other Andalusian fairs, many men wear a suit and tie, sometimes in brighter colors than in an office, and those who ride horses wear the short suit, the traditional country attire with a short jacket, high-waisted pants and boots. Respecting these customs is part of enjoying the party. In other regional festivals, informing yourself about local traditions avoids clashing.

## Dinners and outings

- **Dinner at a standard restaurant:** American jacket, dress shirt and pants or chinos; The tie is optional.
- **Casual dinner with friends:** dark jeans, knit shirt or polo, and leather shoes or sneakers.
- **First date:** something that you like and that feels good to you, a point more careful than your daily life.

## Dress warmly

A good part of the Spanish celebrations are concentrated between May and September, with temperatures that put any outfit to the test. Some keys:

- **Cool fabrics:** linen, light cotton or cool open-weave wools, which breathe much better than synthetic blends.
- **Jackets with little lining:** unlined or half-lined jackets are lighter and cooler.
- **Light colors for daytime:** beige, light gray or medium blue, as long as the code allows it.
- **Thin cotton shirts** with long sleeves even in summer if you are wearing a jacket.
- **Loafers or light shoes** with leather soles.
- **Never shorts** at events with any degree of formality.

## If you don't know what to wear

1. **Ask the hosts:** It is the simplest and no one will take it the wrong way.
2. **Please note the location, time and season.**
3. **When in doubt, a more formal point:** it is always easier to take off your tie or jacket than to get one at the last minute.
4. **Have a versatile outfit prepared:** A well-fitted navy blue suit, white shirt, sober tie and black shoes solve the vast majority of situations.

## The details that make the difference

| Detail | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Blazer buttons | In the two-button version, only the top one is fastened; never the one below; unbuttons when sitting |
| tie | the tip should reach approximately the belt buckle |
| pocket square | complements the tie without being identical to it |
| Socks | long, so as not to show the leg when sitting, and in color according to the pants |
| Belt and shoes | of the same skin tone |
| Suspenders | with morning suit and tuxedo, instead of a belt |
| Clock | sober and fine with a suit; bulky dive watch doesn't fit with tuxedo |

## Rent or buy

The morning suit, tuxedo and tailcoat can be rented from specialized establishments, a good option if they are going to be worn once or twice. If you have several weddings or galas a year, buying a good quality one and adjusting it with a tailor pays off and will always look better. In any case, try it ahead of time, with the shoes and shirt you will wear, and leave room for adjustments.

## Frequent errors

- **Ignore the invitation code.**
- **Wear a morning coat to an evening wedding.**
- **Fasten the last button** of the jacket.
- **Short socks** with suit.
- **Mix black shoes** with brown belt.
- **Going too casual** to an interview or a funeral.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does “dark suit” mean on an invitation?

A navy blue or charcoal gray suit with a light shirt and tie. It's not a tuxedo.

### Can a guest wear a morning coat?

Yes, if the invitation indicates it or if it is customary in the family and the wedding is during the day. If not, a dark suit is the most appropriate.

### Is a tie mandatory at a wedding?

In most weddings, yes, unless the bride and groom indicate otherwise or it is a very clear informal style.

### What shoes do you wear with a tuxedo?

Black lace-up shoes, highly polished or patent leather.

## In summary

The dress codes range from the tailcoat and morning coat, of maximum formality, to the evening tuxedo, the dark suit, the cocktail, the casually groomed and the informal. In Spain, the morning suit is a tradition for daytime weddings and the dark suit is the safe option for almost any ceremony. At funerals, sobriety; in interviews, a more formal point than the company; at fairs, respect for local customs. Pay attention to the details: the bottom button not fastened, the tie at the height of the belt, long socks and a belt that matches the shoes. When in doubt, ask the hosts.
