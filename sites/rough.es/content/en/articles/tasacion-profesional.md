Almost everyone has some piece of jewelry whose value they don't know: an inherited ring, a watch that belonged to their father, a necklace given as a gift decades ago, or a batch of antique pieces at the bottom of a drawer. As long as no one needs to know how much they are worth, the doubt doesn't matter. But there comes a time when it does matter: when taking out insurance, when distributing an inheritance, in a divorce, when thinking about selling or when an insurer asks to justify what has been lost. Then something more than an opinion is needed: a professional appraisal.

This guide explains when it is best to order an appraisal, who can do it and how to choose it, what happens during the visit, what documentation you should bring, how special cases such as antique watches and jewelery are valued, how to read the report and how to spot unreliable valuations. If you are interested in understanding what a laboratory report identifies, start with the guide on [basic gemological instruments](/es/instrumentos-gemologicos-basicos/).

## When you need an appraisal

| Situation | What is the appraisal for? | Usual value type |
|---|---|---|
| Insurance | set the insured capital and justify a claim | replacement value |
| Inheritance or distribution | distribute equitably and declare assets | market value |
| Divorce or property settlement | agree on the distribution between the parties | market value |
| Sale | know a reasonable reference price | market or liquidation value |
| Guaranteed loan | grant a loan on the jewel | prudent value, close to that of metal |
| Litigation | resolve a dispute before a judge | depending on what is discussed |

In inheritances, in addition, the value of the jewelry can have tax consequences. If there are pieces of a certain value, consult with a tax advisor how they should be declared.

## Who can appraise

There is no single path to the profession of jewelry appraiser, so it is worth looking at three things:

- **Accredited gemological training:** recognized gemological studies, which allow stones and treatments to be identified.
- **Market experience:** know the real buying and selling prices of similar pieces.
- **Independence:** that the appraiser has no interest in buying or selling the piece to you.

For watches, signed vintage pieces, or special collections, look for someone with specific experience in that field. And if the appraisal is going to be used in a trial, it is common to use an expert with experience in court reports.

## How to charge

A professional appraiser charges a fixed rate, per piece or per work time, agreed upon before starting. Be wary of anyone who charges a percentage of the appraised value: they are interested in inflating it. Always ask for a quote and ask what it includes: number of pieces, tests, photographs, and report delivery time.

## What to bring to the appointment

| Document or object | Why does it help? |
|---|---|
| Purchase invoices | original date, price and description |
| Laboratory reports | stone identity and treatments |
| Previous appraisals | allow us to compare the evolution of the value |
| Cases, boxes and documents | in watches and branded pieces, influence the value |
| History of the piece | origin, repairs or transformations |
| Personal identification | to record who commissioned the appraisal |

Wear your jewelry clean, but without having subjected it to aggressive cleaning, and report any repairs you know of.

## What happens during the appraisal

1. **Reception and inventory:** each piece is registered and photographed.
2. **Gentle cleaning,** if necessary to observe it well.
3. **Metal examination:** contrasts, color, construction and, if necessary, proof of fineness.
4. **Identification of stones:** with a magnifying glass, refractometer, polariscope and other instruments, or from existing reports.
5. **Measurement and weight estimation:** Set stones cannot be weighed, so their weight is estimated from their measurements.
6. **Quality evaluation:** color, purity, cut and condition of the stones; manufacturing quality.
7. **Condition:** wear, repairs, loose or damaged stones.
8. **Market research:** comparison with sales of similar pieces.
9. **Report writing.**

Some pieces require testing that the appraiser cannot do, such as determining whether a diamond is natural or cultured or whether a ruby is filled with glass. In that case, the correct thing to do is to indicate this and recommend sending the stone to a laboratory.

## How to read the report

A good appraisal report should allow a third party, such as an insurer or a judge, to know exactly what item was appraised and why it is worth what it says. Check that it includes:

- **The purpose and type of value** that it indicates.
- **A complete description:** metal, grade, weight, measurements, stones with their identification and estimated quality, marks and signatures.
- **The limitations:** for example, that the weight of the stones is estimated or that certain treatments have not been ruled out.
- **Photographs.**
- **The date, the appraiser's identification and his signature.**

If the report only says "gold ring with stone" and a number, it is not of much use.

## Special cases

### Watches

A watch is influenced by the brand, model, reference, serial number, condition, originality of the parts, service history and the presence of the original box and documents. A watch with replaced parts or a restored dial can be worth significantly less than an original one. We explain it in the guide on [mechanical watches](/es/relojes-mecanicos/).

### antique jewelry

Antique pieces may be worth more for their era, their style, their author or their provenance than for their materials. An appraiser without experience in antique jewelry may undervalue it, appraising it only for the metal and stones. For these pieces, look for someone who knows the antique and auction market; More information in the guide about [period jewelry and styles](/es/joyeria-de-epoca-estilos/).

### Legacy Lots

When it comes to many pieces, what is practical is a quick first review to separate the jewelry and pieces of little value, and a detailed appraisal only of the important ones.

## Appraisal and insurance

When the appraisal is commissioned for insurance, it is a good idea to speak to the insurer or broker first: many policies require that jewelry over a certain value be declared individually, with its appraisal and photographs, and set security requirements such as a safe.

Two concepts of Spanish insurance law deserve attention. The first is the so-called proportional rule: if the insured capital is lower than the real value of the insured, the compensation can be reduced in the same proportion, even for partial damages. The second is the possibility of agreeing in the policy on the value of the insured assets, something that, with a solid appraisal behind it, reduces discussions in the event of an accident.

Also keep in mind that many insurers prefer to replace the jewelry through their suppliers instead of paying the amount in money. Read the condition calmly and ask what would happen to a unique or antique piece that cannot be replaced with another like it.

## Unreliable reviews

| Signal | Why worry |
|---|---|
| Appraisal made by the person who sells you the jewel | conflict of interest; sometimes it is inflated to give the impression of a good purchase |
| Figures much higher than the price paid | They can be a sales technique |
| Report without detailed description | does not serve as evidence |
| Appraiser who charges a percentage | incentive to inflate the value |
| Assessment by phone or photo | Without a physical exam, there is no appraisal. |

## How often to update it

Prices of gold, gems, and labor change, sometimes rapidly. For insured pieces, it is a good idea to review the appraisal every few years or when there are significant changes in the gold market. A very old appraisal can leave you underinsured.

## Frequent errors

- **Use seller appraisal** as an independent reference.
- **Confuse the insurance value** with what they will pay you when you sell.
- **Do not bring invoices or reports** to the appointment.
- **Appraise antique jewelry** with someone who only values ​​metal.
- **Do not update** appraisals of insured parts.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does an appraisal take?

It depends on the number of pieces and their complexity. A simple piece can be appraised in one appointment; Large batches or parts that require laboratory work require more time.

### Why is the gold buyer offering me less than the appraisal?

Because the insurance appraisal indicates the cost of replacing the piece, while a gold buyer pays, at most, a value close to that of the metal.

### Is an appraisal done in another country worth it?

It can serve as a reference, but if it is going to be used before an insurer or in a procedure in Spain, it is advisable to confirm that they accept it or order a new one.

### Is a laboratory certificate the same as an appraisal?

No. The lab report identifies the stone and its treatments; Appraisal assigns a value to the entire piece of jewelry for a specific purpose.

## In summary

A professional appraisal is necessary to insure jewelry, distribute inheritances, liquidate assets, sell with reference or resolve disputes, and each purpose requires a type of value. Choose an appraiser with gemological training, market experience and independence, who charges a fixed fee. Bring invoices, reports, previous appraisals and documents, and expect a full examination of the metal and stones. The report must describe the piece in detail, indicate its purpose, its limitations and be signed. Be wary of seller appraisals and inflated numbers, and update appraisals on insured parts every few years.
