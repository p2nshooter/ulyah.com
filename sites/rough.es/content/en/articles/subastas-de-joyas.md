Auctions are one of the most interesting places to buy jewelry: vintage pieces that are no longer made, jewelry signed by great houses, important stones with lab reports and, from time to time, real bargains. They are also a good channel to sell pieces with value beyond metal. But they have their own rules, a particular vocabulary and costs that are not always seen in the hammer figure. Those who bid without knowing them may end up paying much more than they thought, or buying a piece with defects that they will not be able to return.

In this guide you will see how a jewelry auction works in Spain, what the estimate, the starting price and the reserve price mean, how much a lot really costs when commission and taxes are added, how to examine the pieces before bidding, what forms of bidding exist, what happens after you are awarded a lot, what peculiarities antique pieces have and how to sell your jewelry at auction.

## How an auction works

Auction houses receive pieces from individuals and dealers, study them, photograph them and group them in a catalog that they publish in advance, increasingly online. Each piece or group of pieces is a lot, with a number, a description, photographs and reference figures. Before the auction there are usually exhibition days when you can see the lots in person.

On the day of the auction, the auctioneer presents the lots one by one and the attendees, in the room, by telephone, in writing or via the Internet, bid with fixed increments. The lot is awarded to the highest bid when the hammer falls, provided the established minimum is reached.

## The basic vocabulary

| Term | What does it mean |
|---|---|
| Lot | piece or set of pieces that are auctioned together |
| Estimation | indicative range of what the house believes the lot will achieve |
| Starting price | figure from which bidding begins |
| Reserve price | confidential minimum agreed with the seller, below which it will not be sold |
| auction price | figure to which the lot is awarded when the hammer falls |
| Buyer Commission | percentage that is added to the auction price |
| Deserted or retired lot | lot that is not sold or that is withdrawn before the auction |

The estimate is not an appraisal or a guarantee of value: it is a commercial reference that the house sets to attract bids and guide the seller.

## How much does a lot really cost?

The auction price is only part of the cost. Before bidding, read the general conditions of the house and add:

1. **The auction price.**
2. **The buyer's commission,** a percentage of the auction that varies depending on the house.
3. **VAT,** which is applied differently depending on the tax regime of the lot: some lots are sold under the special regime for used goods and antiques, and others under the general regime; The conditions of sale indicate this.
4. **Shipping and insurance costs,** if you do not pick up the piece in person.
5. **Possible storage costs** if you take too long to pick it up.
6. **Later adjustments,** such as changing the size of a ring or checking settings.

Always do the full accounting and set your maximum bid based on the total cost, not the hammer price.

## Examine before bidding

The pieces are generally sold in the condition in which they are found, and in public auctions the fourteen-day right of withdrawal that exists in other remote purchases does not apply. That is why the prior examination is essential:

- **Visit the exhibition** and examine the pieces with a magnifying glass: contrasts, settings, claws, wear, repairs.
- **Ask for a condition report,** a document in which the house describes the conservation of the lot in more detail.
- **Request additional photographs** of areas not seen in the catalog.
- **Check the lab reports** mentioned, their date and their lab, and verify their number when possible.
- **Ask about the treatments** of the stones if the catalog does not mention them.
- **Check the size** of rings and the length of necklaces and bracelets.

## How to research the price

Before setting your limit, look for references. Many houses publish the results of their previous auctions, and comparing similar lots will give you a much more realistic idea than the catalog estimate. When comparing, keep in mind:

- **That they are truly comparable pieces:** same type of jewel, metal, era, signature and quality of the stones.
- **The state of conservation:** a restored piece or with replaced stones is worth less.
- **If the published result includes the commission** or is only the auction price.
- **Documentation:** A recent lab report or a known provenance raises the price.
- **The date:** Gold prices and fashions change, and a result from years ago may not work.

If you can't find clear references, a consultation with an independent appraiser before the auction can avoid an expensive mistake.

## Ways to bid

| Shape | How it works | To take into account |
|---|---|---|
| In room | bidding is done by lifting a numbered pallet | requires prior registration |
| By phone | a house employee calls you during the auction and bids for you | It is requested in advance, normally for lots of a certain amount |
| in writing | You leave an order with your maximum bid and the house bids for you up to that limit | avoid getting carried away by emotion |
| online | You bid through the house's website or a platform | Check if the platform adds its own commission |

Written bidding is a good tool for beginners: it forces you to decide the limit calmly, before the auction begins.

## After award

- **You will receive the invoice** with the auction, commission and taxes.
- **Pay within the deadline** set in the conditions; Delay may result in penalties.
- **Pick up or request delivery** as soon as possible.
- **Please check the piece upon receipt** and immediately report any discrepancies with the description.
- **Save documentation:** invoice, status report and laboratory reports.

## Old pieces: export and right of first refusal

Jewelry and antique objects can be part of the historical heritage. Spanish legislation requires authorization to export certain cultural goods, particularly those more than one hundred years old, and the State can exercise a right of first refusal at auctions to acquire pieces of interest for public collections at the auction price. If you buy an antique piece and you live outside of Spain or plan to take it out of the country, check the requirements with the auction house first.

## Sell at auction

1. **Ask for an appraisal** from one or more houses: they usually do it free of charge for pieces of interest.
2. **Sign a consignment contract** that includes the estimate, reserve price, seller's commission, photography, insurance or reporting costs, and deadlines.
3. **Provide all documentation:** invoices, reports, boxes and provenance.
4. **Wait for the auction,** which may take several months depending on the house's calendar.
5. **Collect after the buyer pays,** within the agreed period.
6. **If the lot does not sell,** agree whether it will be re-auctioned with another reserve or returned.

The auction is especially suitable for signed, antique pieces, with important stones or with interesting provenance. For common jewelry, it may not compensate compared to other channels, as we explain in the guide on [selling gold and jewelry](/es/vender-oro-y-joyas/).

## Frequent errors

- **Bid without adding commission and VAT.**
- **Do not examine the lot** or ask for a status report.
- **Let yourself be carried away by the emotion** of the room.
- **Confusing the estimate with the actual value.**
- **Buy important stones without a laboratory report**.
- **Sell without reading the consignment contract.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I return a lot purchased at auction?

In general, not because of simple regret: the right of withdrawal does not apply in public auctions. Yes, you can complain if the lot does not correspond to the description, according to the conditions of the house and the law.

### What is buyer's commission?

A percentage that the house adds to the auction price and charges the buyer, plus the corresponding taxes.

### Are auctions cheaper than stores?

Sometimes, but not always. There are lots that are awarded below their market value and others that far exceed their estimate. Always compare the total cost.

### How long does it take for an auction sale to collect?

It depends on the house and the contract. Typically collected a few weeks after the auction, once the buyer has paid.

## In summary

At a jewelry auction, lots are presented in a catalog with an indicative estimate, a starting price and often a confidential reserve price. The actual cost includes the auction, the buyer's commission, VAT according to the lot regime and shipping costs. Parts are sold as is and there is no right of withdrawal, so examine them, ask for condition reports and check lab reports. Bid with a limit set in advance, preferably in writing. Old pieces may require an export permit. To sell, sign a clear consignment contract.
