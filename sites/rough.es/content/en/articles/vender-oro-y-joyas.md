In almost all Spanish cities there are shop windows with “buy gold” signs and prices per gram that change every day. For many families, selling jewelry that is no longer worn is a legitimate way to obtain liquidity: broken chains, mismatched earrings, rings that no longer work, or heirlooms that no one will take. But selling well requires knowing what is being sold, how the price is calculated and what alternatives exist, because the difference between the first offer and the best can be considerable, and because some pieces are worth much more whole than cast.

In this guide you will see how what they pay you for gold is calculated, what tests are carried out and how to monitor them, what documentation they will ask for and why, which pieces deserve another sales channel, how to compare offers, what tax implications the sale may have and what the most common mistakes are.

## Before selling: know what you have

1. **Separate the pieces:** gold, silver, costume jewelry and dubious pieces.
2. **Look for contrasts:** numbers like 750, 585 or 925 indicate the law; Acronyms like GP or vermeil indicate bathrooms. We explain it in the guide on [contrasts and guarantee marks](/es/contrastes-y-marcas-de-garantia/).
3. **Weigh the pieces at home** with a precision kitchen scale if you have one; will give you a reference.
4. **Identify special pieces:** signed, antique jewelry, with important stones or with sentimental value for other family members.
5. **Gather documentation:** invoices, stone reports, boxes and watch documents.

## How the price of gold is calculated

Gold buyers generally pay for the fine gold contained in the piece:

| Factor | What does it mean |
|---|---|
| Weight | the total weight of the piece in grams |
| Law | the proportion of fine gold; an 18 carat piece contains 750 thousandths |
| Quote | the price of gold in international markets, which changes daily |
| Buyer margin | the difference that the buyer keeps to cover costs, refining and profit |

An example without market figures helps to understand it. If a chain weighs 10 grams and is made of 18-karat gold, it contains 7.5 grams of fine gold, because 750 thousandths of 10 grams is 7.5. The buyer will multiply those 7.5 grams by the price he pays that day per gram of fine gold, which will always be lower than the international price. If the same chain were 14 karat, it would contain about 5.85 grams of fine gold and the supply would be proportionately less. With this simple account you can check if an offer is consistent with the grade and the weight of your piece.

Many establishments advertise a price per gram of “18 carat” or “24 carat”. Make sure what price they are applying to you and that it corresponds to the actual grade of your piece. Small stones, on the other hand, usually pay little or nothing, and diamonds and important gems require a separate valuation.

## Testing: what is done and how to monitor it

| Test | How it works | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Weighing | precision scale | that the scale is visible, verified and zeroed before weighing |
| Touchstone and acids | The piece is scratched on a stone and acids are applied | have it done in front of you and the result explained to you |
| X-ray fluorescence | a device analyzes the surface without damaging it | useful, but can be fooled by thick baths |
| Cutting or filing | an area is filed to check the interior | only with your permission, and knowing that it damages the piece |

Demand that everything be done in plain sight and don't leave your jewelry out of reach. If something doesn't convince you, pick up your pieces and leave.

## Why do they ask for your ID?

Establishments that buy and sell gold and second-hand items are required to identify who is selling and record the transactions, in part to make it difficult to sell stolen items. That is why they will ask you for an identity document and make you sign a receipt. It is not a personal mistrust, but a legal obligation, and a business that does not ask you for anything should make you suspicious.

Furthermore, Spanish regulations limit cash payments when one of the parties acts as a businessman or professional, so that, from a thousand euros, payment will normally be made by transfer or other means that leaves a trace.

## Where to sell

| Channel | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Buying and selling gold | speed and immediate payment | pay for the metal, not the design or age |
| Jewelry store that buys used pieces | You can value beautiful or brand-name pieces for resale | not all buy, and apply their margin |
| Antique jewelry dealers | They value era, style and signature | only for pieces with interest |
| Auction houses | access to many buyers for important parts | fees, deadlines and uncertainty; see the guide on [jewelry auctions](/es/subastas-de-joyas/) |
| Sale between individuals | possible best price | security and fraud risks |

## Parts that should not be melted

- **Signed jewelry** by renowned houses or designers.
- **Antique pieces** with manufacturing quality, period style or techniques such as enamel or filigree.
- **Watches,** whose value depends on the brand, model and condition, not the weight of the box.
- **Jewelry with important stones,** that deserve a report and their own evaluation.
- **Pieces with sentimental value for other family members:** before selling, ask.

To identify these pieces, the guide on [period jewelry and styles](/es/joyeria-de-epoca-estilos/) can help you.

## How to compare offers

1. **Ask for at least three offers** in different establishments.
2. **Ask for the breakdown:** weight, grade, price per gram applied and discounts.
3. **Compare the same day,** because the price of gold changes.
4. **Don't be pressured** with “only valid now” offers.
5. **Be wary of maximum price advertisements** that are then not applied to your piece for unclear reasons.

## Sell silver

Silver fetches a much lower price per gram than gold, and many buyers offer modest amounts for it. Before selling inherited cutlery, trays or silver pieces, keep three things in mind:

- **Not everything silver is silver:** Silver metal has only one plating and has almost no value as a metal.
- **The law matters:** 925 thousandths silver contains more precious metal than 800 silver, common in antique pieces.
- **Weight is deceiving:** the handles of many silver knives are hollow and filled with other materials, and their blades are made of steel, so not all of their weight is silver.

Antique silverware pieces with legible marks, good condition and period design may interest antique dealers and auction houses above their weight value.

## Sell gold and Treasury

When an individual sells a piece of jewelry, a capital gain or loss may be generated in income tax: the difference between what is obtained from the sale and what it cost to acquire it. In inherited pieces, the acquisition value is usually the one declared in the inheritance. If you sell pieces of a certain value, save proof of the sale and consult with a tax advisor or the official information of the Tax Agency how to declare it.

## Security

- **Do not advertise on networks** that you have gold at home or that you are going to sell it.
- **Take only the pieces you are going to sell** and transport them discreetly.
- **Be wary of home buyers** or street buyers.
- **In sales between individuals,** stay in public places and avoid cash in large quantities.

## Frequent errors

- **Sell at first offer** without comparing.
- **Cast old or signed pieces** that were worth more whole.
- **Do not check to which law** the price per gram applies.
- **Leave jewelry out of sight** during fittings.
- **Sell family pieces** without consulting the other heirs.
- **Forget the possible tax implications.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Why am I paid so much less than what the jewel cost?

Because the buyer pays for the fine gold it contains, less his margin, and not for the design, workmanship or brand, which form a large part of the price of a new piece of jewelry.

### Do they pay me for the stones?

Small stones usually pay little or nothing. Important diamonds and gems should be valued separately, preferably with a report.

### Is it mandatory to give your DNI to sell gold?

Yes. Buying and selling establishments are required to identify the seller and register the transaction.

### Do I have to declare the sale of a jewel?

It can generate a capital gain or loss in income tax. Consult with an advisor or the Tax Agency depending on your case.

Before selling gold or jewelry, identify what you have, check the contrasts and separate the special pieces.
