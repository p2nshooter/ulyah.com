Enamel is literally glass cast onto metal. A very fine powder of colored glass is placed on a piece of gold, silver or copper and baked until it melts and adheres to it, forming a shiny, hard surface with colors that do not fade over the centuries. Romanesque reliquaries, Renaissance jewels, painted miniatures of court portraits, Russian imperial eggs and some of the most beautiful pieces of modernism were made with this technique, including those that came out of the Barcelona workshops at the beginning of the 20th century.

In this guide you will see what vitreous enamel is and how it is worked, what the classic techniques are and how they are recognized, what place enamel occupies in the history of art in Spain, why it is not the same as the resin used today in much jewelry, what to check when buying an enameled piece and how to care for it so that it does not chip or crack.

## What is vitreous enamel

Vitreous enamel is a low-melting point glass, ground into powder, to which metallic oxides are added to give it color. It can be opaque, translucent or opalescent. When baked at high temperatures, it melts onto the metal and forms a glassy layer attached to it.

| Feature | vitreous enamel |
|---|---|
| material | colored glass with metal oxides |
| Support | copper, fine silver, high-grade gold and some steels |
| Cooking | oven at very high temperatures, for a few minutes per layer |
| Hardness | high: resists scratching better than resin |
| Fragility | Like all glass, it chips when hit and cracks if the metal is bent. |
| Color | stable over time and light |

## How to work

1. **Prepare the metal:** it is cleaned, degreased and stripped until no trace of rust or grease remains.
2. **Prepare the glaze:** the glass is ground, washed several times to remove the finest particles, which cloud the color, and kept moist.
3. **Apply the counterglaze:** In many pieces, the back is also enameled to balance the stresses and prevent the front layer from cracking when it cools.
4. **Loading the polish:** Using a spatula or a very fine brush, the polisher places the wet powder in each area.
5. **Dry and bake:** The piece is dried and placed in the oven until the glaze melts and becomes shiny.
6. **Repeat:** several layers and firing are applied until the desired color and thickness are achieved.
7. **Even out and polish:** the surface is sanded with abrasive stones to level it and the shine is restored with a final firing or polishing.

Each color behaves differently in the oven, and some require specific temperatures or times. That is why enamelling is a craft that is learned with years of practice.

## The classic techniques

| Technique | How to do it | How to recognize |
|---|---|---|
| Alveolate or partitioned | thin metal threads, welded or fixed on the base, form cells that are filled with enamel | thin metallic lines separating colors |
| excavated | the cells are emptied into the metal itself and filled with enamel | enamel areas surrounded by thick metal, at the same height |
| Embossed enamel | translucent enamel on an engraved or chiseled relief | the color is more intense where the relief is deeper |
| Draft | translucent enamel in bottomless cells, like a miniature stained glass window | light passes through the piece when viewed against the light |
| painted enamel | Scenes or portraits are painted with very fine enamels, in successive layers | miniature painting look with vitreous shine |
| Enamel on guilloché | translucent enamel over a mechanical line engraving | geometric drawings that are seen through color |

Openwork is one of the most difficult techniques: the glaze must hold itself inside each cell, without any background to hold it. That is why openwork pieces are especially fragile and appreciated.

## Enamel in the history of Spain

On the peninsula, enamel played a prominent role in Romanesque art. Works such as the enameled front of the sanctuary of San Miguel de Aralar, in Navarra, or the urn of Santo Domingo de Silos, preserved in Burgos, show the technique of excavation on gilded copper, with blue, green and white figures and backgrounds of a vividness that surprises after more than eight centuries.

Centuries later, Catalan modernism gave one of the great chapters of enamel in jewelry. The Barcelona jeweler Lluís Masriera, trained in translucent enamel techniques, created at the beginning of the 20th century pendants, brooches and combs with nymphs, dragonflies and flowers in openwork and translucent enamel that are today sought after at auctions around the world. His work is related to that of the great jewelers of European art nouveau and demonstrates that Spanish jewelry was at the height of the avant-garde of its time. ## Vitreous enamel and resin

Much of what is sold as "enamel" in fashion jewelry and costume jewelry today is actually colored resin, sometimes called cold enamel. It is applied without an oven and hardens at room temperature.

| Appearance | vitreous enamel | Resin |
|---|---|---|
| material | molten glass | synthetic polymer |
| Process | high temperature baking | hardened without oven |
| Hardness | high | softer, scratches easily |
| Color Durability | very high | may yellow or lose shine over the years |
| Touch | cold, like glass | more tempered, like plastic |
| Repair | complex: requires rebaking | simpler, but the result is noticeable |

Resin is not a fraud if sold as such, and allows for affordable colors and prices. What is not correct is to present it as vitreous enamel, which requires much more work and lasts much longer.

## What to check when purchasing an enameled piece

- **Ask if it is vitreous enamel or resin,** and ask that it appear on the invoice.
- **Look for chips and cracks** with a magnifying glass, especially around edges and corners.
- **Check the surface:** pores, bubbles or sunken areas indicate poor cooking.
- **Look at the back:** Many quality pieces have counter-enamel.
- **For signed antique pieces,** ask for documentation or the opinion of a specialist.
- **Check the contrast of the metal,** as we explain in the guide on [contrasts and guarantee marks] (/es/contrastes-y-marcas-de-garantia/).

## Enamel care

- **Avoid bumps:** the enamel is glass and chips.
- **Do not bend the piece:** If the metal warps, the enamel will crack.
- **No ultrasound or steam,** which can cause cracks.
- **Avoid chemicals and acidic cleaners,** which can dull some polishes.
- **Clean it with a soft cloth** or with warm water and neutral soap, and dry it well.
- **Store it separately,** protected from friction with stones and metals.
- **For repairs,** go to an enameler: re-baking a piece with stones or solders is delicate.

## Enamel on other objects

Vitreous enamel is not limited to jewelry. It is used in high-quality watch faces, whose kiln-fired enamel retains color and shine for generations, in medals, decorations and insignia, and in art objects such as boxes and miniatures. In watchmaking, enamel dials are highly appreciated, as we discussed in the guide on [mechanical watches](/es/relojes-mecanicos/).

## Frequent errors

- **Confuse resin with vitreous enamel.**
- **Wear enameled rings** in activities with blows.
- **Clean enamelled parts with ultrasound.**
- **Buy old pieces** without checking for cracks and restorations.
- **Try to repair the enamel** with glues or paints.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does enamel wear off with use?

Vitreous enamel is hard and resists rubbing well, but it can chip or crack if the metal is bent.

### How do I tell enamel from resin?

Vitreous enamel is cooler to the touch, harder, and usually has a deeper shine. If in doubt, ask the seller and ask them to indicate it in writing.

### Can chipped enamel be repaired?

Yes, but it is a specialized job. Oven repair is the most durable; Cold repairs with resin are simpler, but they are noticeable and age worse.

### What is openwork enamel?

A technique in which translucent enamel fills bottomless cells, so that light passes through it like a stained glass window.

## In summary

Vitreous enamel is colored glass fused on metal at high temperature, hard and color stable, but fragile when hit and flexed. Its classic techniques are honeycombing, excavation, enamel on relief, openwork, painting and enamel on guilloché. In Spain it shines in Romanesque works such as the front of San Miguel de Aralar and in the modernist jewelry of Lluís Masriera. It is not the same as resin, softer and less durable. When purchasing, ask about the material, look for chips and cracks, and check the contrast; To take care of it, avoid shocks, ultrasounds and chemicals.
