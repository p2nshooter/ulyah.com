On the British imperial crown, right in front, shines a large red stone known for centuries as the Black Prince's ruby. It's not a ruby: it's a spinel. And its history goes through 14th century Castile, through the hands of Pedro I and through a battle fought in Rioja lands. The anecdote well summarizes the destiny of this gem for centuries: admired, set in the most important jewels of Europe and Asia, but almost always with a name that was not its own.

Today spinel has regained its identity and is one of the most appreciated gems by connoisseurs: hard, shiny, with intense colors and, unlike almost all colored gems, often without any treatment. In this guide you will see what it is, why it was confused with ruby, how they are distinguished, what colors and origins exist, why synthetic spinel is so common as an imitation and what to check before buying one.

## What is spinel

Spinel is an oxide of magnesium and aluminum. It crystallizes in the cubic system and forms very characteristic octahedrons, sometimes united in a twin so typical that in mineralogy it is known as "spinel twin."

| Property | Spinel |
|---|---|
| Composition | magnesium aluminum oxide |
| crystalline system | cubic |
| usual way | octahedra |
| Hardness on the Mohs scale | 8 |
| Tenacity | good, no marked exfoliation |
| Refraction | simple: it does not bend the light or show pleochroism |
| Brightness | intense vitreous |
| Color agents | chrome in reds and pinks; iron and cobalt in blues and violets |

## The ruby that was not ruby

Until chemistry and crystallography allowed them to be separated using scientific criteria, red spinels and rubies were considered the same stone. Old inventories speak of "balaje" rubies to refer to spinels with pink or violet tones from Badakhshan, a mountainous region between present-day Afghanistan and Tajikistan. The word balaje still appears in the Spanish dictionary.

The story of the Black Prince ruby, according to the most widespread story, begins in the Nasrid kingdom of Granada. After the death of Sultan Abu Said in Seville in 1362, the great stone passed into the hands of Peter I of Castile. A few years later, in the midst of the Castilian civil war, Pedro received the help of Prince Edward of Woodstock, the Black Prince, who supported him in the battle of Nájera in 1367. The stone was part of the payment. It has since remained in the English crown jewels, where it is preserved today.

There are other large historic spinels in treasures from Russia, Iran or India, many barely carved, polished to their natural form and engraved with the names of their successive owners. They were all rubies until they weren't.

## How to distinguish it from ruby

| Feature | Spinel | ruby |
|---|---|---|
| Species | magnesium aluminum oxide | corundum |
| Refraction | simple | double |
| Pleochroism | absent | present: two shades of red |
| Hardness | 8 | 9 |
| Typical inclusions | small octahedral crystals, sometimes in rows | rutile silk, footprints and crystals |
| Treatments | uncommon | very frequent |

At first glance they are difficult to separate; With basic instruments, such as a polariscope or a dichroscope, a gemologist can distinguish them in a few minutes. We explain it in the guide on [basic gemological instruments](/es/instrumentos-gemologicos-basicos/).

| Region | General features |

| Tanzania | Very vivid pink and red spinels, among the most sought after in recent decades |
| Sri Lanka | wide variety of colors, including blues and violets |
| Vietnam | pinks, reds, violets and some cobalt blues |
| Tajikistan and Afghanistan | the historic region of the balajes |
| Madagascar | varied colors |

As with ruby and sapphire, geographical origin can only be determined by a specialized laboratory, and not always with certainty.

## An almost always untreated gem

One of the great virtues of spinel is that, in most cases, it is sold as it comes out of the earth, carved and polished but without color treatments. Heating does not usually improve it and, when applied, laboratories can detect it with spectroscopic techniques. Crack fillings in low quality material are also known.

That doesn't mean you can assume a spinel is untreated: for valuable stones, ask for the lab report to confirm this.

## synthetic spinel

Synthetic spinel has been manufactured for more than a century, mainly by flame fusion, and is cheap. It is widely used as an imitation of other gems: aquamarines, blue zircons, peridots or colored sapphires. Many costume jewelry birthstones are actually synthetic spinels.

| Hint | What does it suggest? |
|---|---|
| Completely clean stone, very uniform color | possible synthetic, to be confirmed |
| gas bubbles | synthetic by flame fusion |
| Visible voltages with polariscope | characteristic of many synthetic spinels by flame fusion |
| Very low price for a rare color | reason to request a report |

There are also red synthetic spinels obtained by flux, which are much more difficult to identify. In important pieces, the laboratory is essential.

## size and size

Spinel has a practical advantage for the lapidary: because its refraction is simple and does not show pleochroism, the color is the same in all directions, and the stone can be oriented to make the most of the weight of the rough without worrying about whether the color changes depending on the axis. In ruby ​​and sapphire, however, a poor orientation can give a browner or duller tone.

The most common shapes are the cushion and the oval, often with cuts that retain weight, and in the markets of origin you still see many stones with somewhat irregular native cuts that a good lapidary can improve. Spinels with intense colors and good sizes are rare: it is common to find small and medium-sized stones, and from certain sizes onwards the price rises significantly. ## How to choose a spinel

1. **Prioritize color:** intense and clean, without excessive brownish or gray tones, unless you are looking for precisely those tones.
2. **Value the brightness:** a good cut takes advantage of the intense luminosity of spinel.
3. **Look at the inclusions:** Many spinels are quite clean; small octahedral inclusions are normal.
4. **Ask for a laboratory report** on stones of a certain value, to confirm that it is natural and untreated.
5. **Ask about the origin,** knowing that only a laboratory can issue a reliable opinion.

## Spinel in jewelry

With a hardness of 8 and good toughness, spinel is suitable for rings for daily use, always with a setting that protects the edges. It is an interesting alternative to ruby ​​and sapphire for those looking for intense color and an untreated stone, and it also has a history that few materials can match. We explain it in the guide on [men's rings and jewelry](/es/anillos-y-joyas-masculinas/), where gray or black spinel fits especially well.

## Spinel care

- **Cleaning:** warm water, neutral soap and a soft brush.
- **Ultrasounds:** generally safe on stones without fractures or fillings; Consult the jeweler if you have questions.
- **Blows:** avoid sharp blows on the edges.
- **Storage:** separately, to prevent it from scratching or being scratched by other gems.

## Frequent errors

- **Consider spinel a second-rate stone** compared to ruby.
- **Take a perfect and cheap spinel as natural** without report.
- **Buy jewelry birthstones** thinking that they are the gem they represent.
- **Assume it is never treated.**
- **Trust prestigious origins** without laboratory support.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is spinel a gemstone?

Yes. Although the traditional classification reserved that term for diamond, ruby, sapphire and emerald, spinel is a top-level gem for hardness, brilliance and rarity in its best qualities.

### Why was he so confused with ruby?

Because red spinels often appear in the same deposits as rubies and look very similar. Only with scientific mineralogy could they be safely separated.

### Is the Black Prince ruby ​​really a spinel?

Yes. It is a large red spinel that, according to the most widespread story, passed from Granada to Pedro I of Castile and from him to the Black Prince, and which today is part of the British imperial crown.

### Are spinels usually treated?

In general, no; It is one of its advantages. Even so, in valuable stones it is advisable to confirm it with a laboratory report.

## In summary

Spinel is a magnesium and aluminum oxide with a hardness of 8, simple refraction and intense shine, which for centuries was confused with ruby. The famous Black Prince ruby, which according to tradition passed through the hands of Peter I of Castile, is actually a spinel. It comes in reds, pinks, oranges, violets, blues and grays, and is often sold untreated, a rare advantage among colored gemstones. Synthetic spinel, on the other hand, is cheap and widely used as an imitation. To buy safely, prioritize color and brilliance, and ask for a laboratory report on valuable stones.
