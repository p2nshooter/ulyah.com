Every New Year's Eve, millions of people in Spain look at the same clock: that of the Real Casa de Correos, in Madrid's Puerta del Sol. It was built and given as a gift in the 19th century by José Rodríguez de Losada, a clockmaker from León who made his career in London, and today it still marks the chimes with a mechanism of gears, weights and escapement. A mechanical wristwatch works, in essence, with the same principles, reduced to a few centimeters: a spring that stores energy, gears that distribute it and a small balance that oscillates with astonishing regularity.

In the age of telephones that tell the exact time, the mechanical watch is no longer a necessity, but a choice: a well-made object that can last a lifetime and be passed on to the next generation. In this guide you will see how it works, how it is different from a quartz watch, what accuracy to expect, what terms like rubies, power reserve or water resistance mean, how to choose the size, materials and style, and how to care for it.

## How a mechanical watch works

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| Mainspring and barrel | a coiled spring inside a drum stores energy |
| gear train | transmits energy and reduces speed to the needles |
| Escape | releases energy in regular impulses |
| Flywheel and spiral | a wheel that oscillates back and forth, attached to a very fine spring, sets the rhythm |
| Rubies | small synthetic ruby bearings reduce friction at pivot points |

The balance wheel is the heart of the watch: it oscillates several times per second, and each oscillation advances the gear train one exact step. The characteristic ticking sound is the sound of the exhaust. Rubies are not an ornament or an indicator of value in themselves: they are synthetic corundum, very hard and polished, which reduces wear. We talk about this material in the guide on [ruby](/es/rubi/).

## Manual, automatic and quartz

| Type | How you receive energy | Traits |
|---|---|---|
| Hand winding mechanic | turning the crown every day | daily contact with the clock; often finer movements |
| auto mechanic | an oscillating mass, the rotor, is wound by moving the wrist | practical; If you don't get along, you end up stopping. |
| Quartz | a battery powers a quartz crystal that vibrates at a constant frequency | much more precise and economical; needs battery change |

A quartz watch is objectively more precise. Whoever chooses a mechanical one does so for the craftsmanship, durability and the pleasure of carrying a small living mechanism.

## What precision can be expected?

A mechanical watch advances or loses a few seconds a day, and the figure varies depending on the position in which it is left, the temperature, the winding level and the condition of the mechanism. Chronometer-certified watches have passed official accuracy tests with strict margins. If your watch suddenly starts to fast forward a lot, it may be magnetized: magnets from speakers, cell phone cases, tablets or bag closures can affect the hairspring. A watchmaker can demagnetize it in minutes.

## Complications

In watchmaking, any function other than time is called a complication:

| Complication | What does it do |
|---|---|
| Date | indicates the day of the month |
| Chronograph | time intervals with buttons |
| Second time zone | shows the time of another place |
| power reserve | indicates how much energy is left |
| Moon phases | represents the phase of the Moon |
| Annual or perpetual calendar | automatically adjusts the length of the months |
| Minute repeater | tells the time with sounds when activating a mechanism |

Each complication adds parts, complexity and maintenance cost. To start, a simple three-hand watch, with or without a date, is the most practical option.

## Water resistance

The water resistance indicated in meters or bars corresponds to a laboratory pressure test, not the depth to which the watch can be submerged in practice.

| Indication | Fair use |
|---|---|
| Water resistant without further figures or 30 meters | splashes and rain; not for bathing |
| 50 meters | washing hands and heavy rain; swim with caution |
| 100 meters | swimming and bathing |
| Certified diving watch | diving, according to the standard that meets |

Gaskets age: if you swim with your watch, it is a good idea to check its tightness periodically. And never operate the crown or pushers underwater, unless the manufacturer allows it.

## Choose size and style

- **The size:** the important thing is not only the diameter, but the distance between the ends of the handles. The handles should not protrude from the wrist.
- **Thickness:** A dress watch should fit under the cuff of your shirt.
- **The style:** dress, diving, pilot, field or chronograph. For a single watch, a sober one with a light or dark dial and interchangeable strap is the most versatile.
- **Consistency:** with formal clothing, a thin leather strap and tones that accompany the shoes and the belt; with casual clothes, more freedom. We explain it in the guide on [dressing according to the occasion](/es/vestir-segun-la-ocasion/).

## Straps: change character

A new strap can transform a watch. Most are secured with spring pins that a watchmaker changes in minutes, and many modern straps have a quick-change system that allows you to do this at home.

- **Leather:** elegant and versatile; It fits the wrist, but suffers from water and sweat.
- **Metallic armband:** resistant and comfortable in all seasons; A watchmaker adjusts the links to the size of the wrist.
- **Fabric:** Light, fresh and easy to wash, ideal for summer.
- **Rubber:** perfect for water and sports.

In the hot summers of much of Spain, alternating the leather strap with a fabric or rubber one greatly extends its life.

## Watchmaking in Spain

Spain has a less well-known watchmaking tradition than Switzerland, but with notable chapters. In the 18th century, the Crown promoted a royal watch factory in Madrid, within the royal manufactures program of the Enlightenment, and the National Heritage collections, in the Royal Palace of Madrid, preserve an extraordinary collection of watches from the great European houses. In Jerez de la Frontera, a museum dedicated to clocks brings together ancient pieces of great value, and the Puerta del Sol clock continues to remind us every year of the talent of José Rodríguez de Losada. Visiting these collections is a good way to understand the evolution of technology before choosing your own watch.

## Buy wisely

1. **In official distributors,** with manufacturer's warranty.
2. **Second-hand,** ask for the box and documents, service history and photographs of the serial number; Check that the parts are original.
3. **Be wary of prices that are too low** on highly sought-after models: fakes are abundant.
4. **Do not buy thinking about the investment:** the prices of some models rise and fall with fashions that no one controls.

## Care and reviews

- **Wind at the same time** every day on manual watches, until you feel resistance, without forcing.
- **Do not change the date at dawn** on watches that do not allow it: in many, between night and early hours, the date mechanism is engaged and can be damaged.
- **Screw down the crown** if it is a screw-down one.
- **Avoid shocks, magnets and sudden changes in temperature.**
- **Clean the box** with a soft cloth; leather straps, away from water and excessive sweat.
- **Periodic review:** Manufacturers recommend intervals of several years; The watchmaker dismantles, cleans, lubricates and adjusts the mechanism.

## Frequent errors

- **Bathing with a 30 meter clock.**
- **Change the early morning date.**
- **Leave the watch next to magnets.**
- **Wait for the clock to stop** to review it.
- **Buy second hand** without documentation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does an automatic watch need a battery?

No. It is charged with the movement of the wrist. If you're not wearing it, it stops and you just need to wind it or move it to get it working again.

### Why is my mechanical watch fast?

It is normal for it to vary a few seconds a day. If you suddenly overtake too much, it may be magnetized or need servicing.

### How often should a mechanical watch be serviced?

Every few years, depending on manufacturer's recommendations and usage. A timely review prevents further wear and tear.

### Can I swim with my watch?

Only if it has adequate resistance, such as 100 meters or a diving watch, and if its seals are checked.

## In summary

A mechanical watch works with a mainspring that stores energy, a train of gears, an escapement and a balance that oscillates regularly, on synthetic ruby bearings. It can be manual or automatic, and is less precise than a quartz one, but it lasts a lifetime with care. Water resistance indicates a pressure test, not a depth. Choose the size by the distance between handles, prefer a sober model to start, buy from official distributors or with documentation, avoid magnets and knocks, do not change the date at dawn and review it every few years.
