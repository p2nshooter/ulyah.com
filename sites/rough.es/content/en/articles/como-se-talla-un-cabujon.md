The cabochon is the oldest and, for many, the most honest form of carving: a smooth, polished dome that shows the stone as it is, without facets to multiply the light. For centuries it was the usual gem cut in Europe, and still today it is the best choice for agates, jaspers, turquoise, opals, moonstones and all star or cat's eye gems. It is also, almost always, the gateway to lapidary: with modest equipment and patience, anyone can learn to turn a piece of stone into a piece ready to be set.

In this guide you will see what exactly a cabochon is and what shapes exist, what stones are cut like this, what equipment is needed, the complete step-by-step process, the most frequent problems and how to solve them, the particularities of some delicate stones and the safety precautions, which in lapidary are not optional.

## What is a cabochon

A cabochon is a stone with a domed, polished top, without facets, and a generally flat base. The word comes from Old French and is related to the term that designated the head, due to its rounded shape.

| Type | Features | Regular use |
|---|---|---|
| simple cabochon | polished dome and flat base | the most common in jewelry |
| tall cabochon | very high dome | stones with a star or cat's eye, to concentrate the effect |
| low cabochon | low dome | agates and stones with a drawing, to show the surface |
| double cabochon | bulging on both sides | transparent stones or pendants that can be seen from both sides |
| hollow cabochon | base cast in concave shape | very dark stones, so that they let in more light |
| Curved table | upper dome with faceted pavilion | combines shine and softness |

The most common shapes are oval, circle, drop, navette and free shapes. For commercial jewelry, calibrated measurements are used, such as 10 × 8, 14 × 10 or 18 × 13 millimeters, which fit into standard settings.

## What stones are cut into cabochons

- **Opaque or translucent stones:** agate, jasper, malachite, lapis lazuli, turquoise, sodalite, jade.
- **Stones with optical phenomena:** star, cat's eye, moonstone adularescence, opal color play.
- **Stones with many inclusions:** that would lose their attractiveness if they were faceted.
- **Soft or fragile stones:** because a dome resists wear better than the edges of the facets.

## Basic equipment

| Element | What is it for? |
|---|---|
| Diamond Edge Disc Saw | cut sheets and trim the contour, always refrigerated |
| cabochon machine | progressive grain diamond wheels, from coarse to very fine, with continuous water |
| dops | wooden or metal rods to which the stone is glued to hold it |
| dop wax or adhesive | fix the stone to dop |
| Aluminum or brass template and punch | mark the outline on the stone |
| Polisher | felt or leather disc with diamond paste or polishing oxides |
| Protective glasses and apron | protection against splinters and splashes |
| Magnifying glass | check stripes between each grain |

## The step by step process

1. **Choose and orient the material.** Wet the stone to see its pattern and its cracks how they will look polished. Decide which area will be the dome, avoiding fractures that cross the piece.
2. **Cut a sheet.** Using the saw, obtain a sheet of suitable thickness, usually a few millimeters more than the height of the cabochon.
3. **Mark the contour.** Place the template over the chosen area and draw the contour with the metal punch, which leaves a line that resists water.
4. **Trim.** With the saw, remove excess material with straight cuts around the contour, without reaching the line.
5. **Profile.** On the thicker wheel, roughen the contour to the marked line, keeping the sides perpendicular to the base.
6. **Bevel the base.** Make a small chamfer on the edge of the base. Prevents splintering and makes setting easier.
7. **Mount on the dop.** Heat the wax, place the stone centered on the dop with the base facing down and let it cool completely.
8. **Form the dome.** Start by working an inclined strip around the entire edge and then gradually move up towards the center, turning the stone continuously so as not to create flat areas.
9. **Sand.** Go through each grit in the series, from coarsest to finest. Wash the stone and your hands between each grain and check with a magnifying glass that there are no streaks left from the previous grain.
10. **Polish.** On the felt or leather disc, with the appropriate polish for the material, until a uniform shine is achieved.
11. **Disassemble.** Separate the stone from the dop with cold or with the appropriate solvent for the wax, depending on the type used.
12. **Check and refinish the base.** Check the symmetry, gloss and bevel, and smooth the base if it will be visible.

## Common problems

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Flat areas on the dome | do not rotate the stone continuously | go back to a coarser grain and round |
| Stripes that don't go away | skip grains or remains of the previous grain | go back to the point where they appeared and wash well |
| Undermined | softer areas that wear out before the rest | fine grain, little pressure and wide movements |
| Edge chipping | missing bevel or bumps against the wheel | bevel the base and work slowly |
| Heat cracks | dry work or excessive pressure | plenty of water and light pressure |
| Off-center dome | badly mounted stone in the dop | check centering before starting |

## Stones that require special care

- **Opal:** very sensitive to heat; Always work with water and minimum pressure. Color play depends on orientation.
- **Turquoise and variscite:** often porous and stabilized; Heat and some products can alter them. Variscite has its own guide: [Gavà variscite](/es/variscita-de-gava/).
- **Malachite:** its dust contains copper and should not be breathed; only wet.
- **Stones with a star or cat's eye:** orientation is everything. In the cat's eye, the fibers should be parallel to the base; In the star, the base must be perpendicular to the axis of the crystal. A higher dome concentrates the effect.
- **Azabache:** soft, it is worked almost by hand with files and fine abrasives, as we explain in the guide on [Asturias jet] (/es/azabache-de-asturias/).

## Safety in the workshop

- **Always wet:** Dust from stones with silica, such as quartz, agate or jasper, can cause silicosis, a serious and irreversible lung disease. In Spain, numerous cases have been recorded among workers who dry cut quartz agglomerate countertops. Never work these materials dry.
- **Water and electricity:** the machines must be connected to an installation with differential and with the cables away from water.
- **Eyes protected:** Safety glasses at all times, also when polishing.
- **Nothing that can get caught:** Hair up, no pendants, loose sleeves or gloves near the wheels.
- **Cleaning the workshop:** with water or a suitable vacuum cleaner, never dry sweeping the accumulated dust.

## How long does it take to learn

The first cabochons usually come out with flat areas, stripes and asymmetries, and this is normal. What helps the most is to practice with cheap and grateful materials, such as agate or jasper, before moving on to expensive or delicate stones. A course in a workshop or in a school of applied arts greatly accelerates learning, because it corrects posture and pressure vices from the beginning that are later difficult to lose.

When you master the cabochon, the natural next step is faceting, which we explain in the guide on [machine faceting and diagrams](/es/facetado-maquina-y-diagramas/).

## Frequent errors

- **Work dry** to go faster.
- **Skip sanding grains.**
- **Do not orient** stones with a star or cat's eye.
- **Tighten too much,** which heats up and cracks the stone.
- **Forget the base bezel.**
- **Start with expensive** or delicate stones.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which stone is best for learning?

Agate and jasper: They are cheap, resistant and show scratches well, which helps you learn how to sand correctly.

### Can you carve a cabochon without a machine?

Yes, with files, water sandpaper on a flat surface and a lot of patience, especially on soft stones. It's slow, but it helps you understand the process.

### Why does my cabochon have flat areas?

Because the stone has not been turned continuously on the wheel. You have to go back to a coarser grain and round the dome with constant movements.

### How tall should the dome be?

It depends on the stone and the use: low to show drawings, high to concentrate stars and cat eyes, and medium for most jewelry.

## In summary

The cabochon is a smooth and polished dome cut, ideal for opaque, translucent stones, with inclusions or with a star or cat's eye. The material is chosen and oriented, a sheet is cut, the contour is marked and trimmed, the base is profiled, the base is beveled, it is mounted on the dop, the dome is formed by rotating without stopping, it is sanded with progressive grains and polished. The most common problems, such as flat spots, scratches or undercuts, are corrected by patiently going back. Always work wet, with glasses and a safe electrical installation, and learn first with agates and jaspers.
