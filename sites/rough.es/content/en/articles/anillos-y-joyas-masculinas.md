For a long time, men's jewelry in Spain was limited to a few pieces with a very specific meaning: the wedding ring, the signet ring with the initials or the family crest, the cufflinks for big occasions, the baptism medal and the watch. Today the range is broader, and many men wear rings with stones, bracelets or fine chains with total naturalness. What has not changed is the golden rule: men's jewelry works best when it is few, of quality and with meaning.

In this guide you will see the most common types of rings and their tradition, which metals to choose according to use and budget, which stones look good on a men's ring, how to get the size right, how to wear cufflinks, barrettes, chains and bracelets, how to combine the pieces with each other and with the watch, and how to care for them, including a safety warning that you should know.

## Rings and their tradition

| Ring | Tradition and use |
|---|---|
| Alliance | symbol of marriage; In much of Spain it is traditionally worn on the ring finger of the right hand, although in some areas, such as Catalonia, the left is common. |
| signet ring | with engraved initials or shield; was born to seal documents with sealing wax and is usually worn on the little finger |
| Ring with stone | onyx, lapis lazuli, gray spinel, sapphire or garnet, in sober settings |
| Smooth or textured band | no special meaning; the easiest way to wear a ring |
| Author's ring | designer pieces, often with textures or artisanal techniques |

The signet ring deserves a mention: traditionally, the engraving was made in negative so that, when pressed on the sealing wax, the drawing would appear correctly. Today many are recorded in positive, only as decoration. If you order one with a family crest, it is advisable that the heraldry be correct and documented.

## Metals

| metal | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| 18 karat gold | classic, can be adjusted and repaired | scratches with use; white needs rhodium plating |
| Platinum | dense, resistant and naturally white | more expensive; it gets scratched, although hardly losing metal |
| Sterling silver | affordable and versatile | soft and darkens over time |
| Titanium | light and resistant | difficult or impossible to adjust size |
| stainless steel | economical and resistant | difficult to adjust |
| tungsten | very scratch resistant | cannot be adjusted; It is fragile under strong impacts and can break rather than bend |
| Palladium | white and light | less widespread; not all workshops work on it |

For a wedding ring or a signet that you want to wear for a lifetime, gold or platinum are the most sensible options, because they allow size adjustments, engravings and repairs. How to read the metal law is explained in the guide on [hallmarks and guarantee marks](/es/contrastes-y-marcas-de-garantia/).

## Stones for a male ring

- **Black onyx:** sober, classic in signets and evening rings.
- **Lapis lazuli:** deep blue with golden sparkles.
- **Gray or black spinel:** hard, shiny and discreet; We explain it in the guide on [spinel](/es/espinela/).
- **Blue sapphire:** very resistant for daily use.
- **Maroon:** dark red, elegant and affordable; see the guide on [garnets](/es/granates/).
- **Small diamonds:** set flush with the metal, without edges to catch.

For everyday rings, choose hard stones and protective settings, such as bezel or flush settings, which protect the stone from impacts.

## Get the size right

1. **Measure yourself at a jewelry store,** with measuring rings the same width as the ring you are going to buy.
2. **Remember that wide rings** usually require a half size larger.
3. **Measure in mid-afternoon** and with normal temperature: the cold reduces the fingers and the heat swells them.
4. **Check that it passes the knuckle** without forcing, but that it does not rotate or fall with your hand facing downwards.
5. **Ask beforehand if it can be adjusted:** Titanium, steel, tungsten and rings with stones around the entire hoop have very limited or impossible adjustments.

## Choose the alliance

- **Profile:** the half-round, domed on the outside, is the most classic; The flat one is more contemporary.
- **Comfort interior:** A slightly rounded interior makes the ring go in and out more easily and is less noticeable on the finger.
- **Width:** medium-width wedding rings tend to work better on male hands; The very fine ones can become deformed over the years.
- **Thickness and weight:** A wedding ring with enough metal withstands knocks and size adjustments better.
- **Interior engraving:** the date and names are tradition; ask that it be done before delivery and check the spelling.
- **Deadlines:** Order the wedding rings with enough time before the wedding, to be able to correct the size if necessary.

## Order a signet ring

The seal can be made of solid metal or have an engraved hard stone, such as carnelian, onyx or lapis lazuli, cut into a hollow. When ordering, you decide the shape of the surface, the weight of the metal and the type of engraving: hand-made engraving has a depth and sharpness that mechanical or laser engraving rarely matches. If you will carry a shield, document its design well and, if in doubt, consult a heraldry specialist. And if you really want to use it to seal, remember that the engraving must be done in negative.

## Cufflinks, barrettes and pins

- **Twins:** They are worn with double cuff or French cuff shirts, the one that folds over itself, or with mixed cuff shirts prepared for them. For the day, simple metal designs; for the night and ceremonies, dark stones or mother-of-pearl. Damascene or charro button cufflinks are an elegant way to wear Spanish craftsmanship; See the guides on the [Toledo and Éibar damascene](/es/damasquinado-de-toledo-y-eibar/) and the [Spanish filigree](/es/filigrana-espanola/).
- **Tie bar:** It should be slightly narrower than the tie and placed approximately at chest height, between the third and fourth buttons of the shirt.
- **Lapel pin:** on the left lapel, discreet and meaningful.

## Chains, medals and bracelets

- **Thin chains:** gold or silver, often with the baptism medal or a cross, tucked inside the shirt or visible with casual clothing.
- **Bracelets:** made of leather, metal or beads, on the wrist opposite the watch or next to it if they are thin and do not hit it.
- **Earrings:** If you wear them, small and quality ones are better.

## Combine the pieces

- **Few pieces:** a watch, a wedding ring or a seal and, at most, one other piece.
- **Consistent metals:** It is not mandatory, but the same tone of metal between the watch, rings, cufflinks and belt buckle gives a more polished appearance.
- **Proportion:** Large hands accept wider rings; thin hands, narrower pieces.
- **Occasion:** the more formal the occasion, the more sober the jewelry. We explain it in the guide on [dressing according to the occasion](/es/vestir-segun-la-ocasion/).

## Care and safety

- **Take off your rings** when playing sports, working with tools or manipulating machinery. A snagged ring can cause serious finger injuries.
- **If you injure your hand,** remove the rings as soon as possible, before the finger swells. In emergencies they can cut gold or silver rings; those made of tungsten or ceramic require other methods.
- **Cleaning:** warm water, neutral soap and a soft brush; dry well.
- **Inspection:** the settings, once a year at the jeweler.
- **Storage:** separately, so they don't scratch each other.
- **Hands also count:** well-kept nails and hydrated skin look as good as the best ring; We explain it in the guide on [hands and nails] (/es/manos-y-unas/).

## Frequent errors

- **Accumulate too many pieces** at once.
- **Buying a titanium or tungsten ring** without knowing that it cannot be adjusted.
- **Wear rings** at the gym or at manual work.
- **Choose soft stones** for an everyday wear ring.
- **Use cufflinks** with shirts without a proper cuff.

## Frequently asked questions

### In which hand is the alliance carried in Spain?

Traditionally, on the ring finger of the right hand in much of the country, although in some areas, such as Catalonia, it is common to wear it on the left. Today it is a personal choice.

### Which finger is the signet ring worn on?

The most common is the little finger, generally on the non-dominant hand.

### Can a man wear multiple rings?

Yes, but in moderation: two or three well-chosen rings, on different hands or on separate fingers, usually work better than many together.

### What metal is best for a men's wedding ring?

Gold and platinum, because they can be adjusted, engraved and repaired for a lifetime.

## In summary

Men's jewelry works best with a few quality and meaningful pieces: the wedding ring, which in much of Spain is worn on the right hand; the seal, traditionally on the little finger; a ring with a hard stone, cufflinks for double cuff shirts and a discreet chain or bracelet. For pieces that last a lifetime, choose gold or platinum, which allow adjustments and repairs; titanium and tungsten cannot be adjusted. Get measured at a jewelry store with rings of the same width, coordinate metals, remove rings for craft work, and check settings every year.
