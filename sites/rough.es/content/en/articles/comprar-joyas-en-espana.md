Buying an important piece of jewelry in Spain is, almost always, a decision that combines emotion and money: an engagement ring, an anniversary gift, a watch to celebrate an achievement or a piece that you want to keep for a lifetime. Precisely for this reason it is advisable to do it with a cool head. Jewelry is a difficult product to value for an untrained buyer: the metal cannot be seen inside, the stones may be treated or synthetic, and the price difference between two seemingly identical pieces can be enormous.

The good news is that the Spanish buyer has effective tools: a hallmark system for precious metals, a broad legal guarantee and clear information obligations on the part of the seller. In this guide you will see where to buy, what to ask, what should appear on the invoice, how to check the contrasts, how to pay safely, what guarantee you have and when you can return a piece, with a review of the most common errors.

## Where to buy

| Type of establishment | Advantages | To take into account |
|---|---|---|
| traditional jewelry | advice, after-sales service and long-lasting relationship | Prices vary greatly between stores. |
| Jeweler's workshop | custom pieces and direct dealings with the manufacturer | manufacturing deadlines and written budget |
| Department stores and chains | clear return policies and well-known brands | less customization and variable advice |
| Online stores | simple comparison and right of withdrawal | you have to verify the seller; We explain it in the guide on [buy jewelry online](/es/comprar-joyas-online/) |
| Second hand, antique shops and auctions | unique and antique pieces | review the status and ask for a detailed description; see the guide on [jewelry auctions](/es/subastas-de-joyas/) |
| Markets and street sales | low prices | difficult to claim; inadvisable for valuable pieces |

In all cases, the important thing is to be able to identify the seller: name or company name, address and tax information. Without that, any claim will be very complicated.

## Questions to ask

1. **What metal is it and what grade?** For example, 18K gold, sterling silver or platinum.
2. **How ​​much does it weigh?** For solid gold pieces, the weight is a useful reference for comparison.
3. **What stones do you have?** Species, size or approximate weight and quality.
4. **Are they natural, synthetic or imitation?** The answer should be clear.
5. **Are they treated?** Heating, fillers or dyes must be declared.
6. **Do you have a laboratory report?** For stones of a certain value, it is recommended.
7. **Who made it?** Handmade, cast, branded, imported.
8. **What warranty and after-sales service does the store offer?** Size adjustments, cleaning, setting checks.
9. **Can I return or exchange it?** In a physical store, only if the business offers it.

A serious salesperson answers all of these questions without getting embarrassed and is willing to put them in writing.

## What should appear on the invoice

The invoice or detailed receipt is your main proof of what you purchased. Ask that it include:

| Data | Description example |
|---|---|
| Seller ID | name or company name, address and tax identification number |
| Date | of the purchase |
| Part Description | 18K yellow gold ring (750) |
| Metal weight | when relevant |
| stones | a heated natural sapphire, oval cut, with its approximate weight |
| Treatments or synthetic character | expressly indicated |
| Reports | lab report number, if any |
| Price | final price, taxes included |
| Conditions | agreed delivery times or adjustments, if any |

A vague description, such as "ring with stone", leaves you without arguments if you later discover that the stone was synthetic or the metal of lower grade.

## Words that should be understood

| Term | What does it mean |
|---|---|
| Brilliant | In commerce, it refers to a brilliant-cut diamond; applied to other stones without more precision, is misleading |
| Zircon | synthetic imitation diamond, low value; It is not the same as zircon, a natural gem |
| Moissanite | very bright, synthetic silicon carbide used as an alternative to diamond |
| Laboratory or cultured | synthetic stone: same substance as natural stone, but manufactured; must always be indicated |
| Plated or dipped | layer of precious metal on another metal; It's not solid metal. |
| natural stone | stone formed in nature, which may be treated; ask about treatments |

If a seller uses these terms ambiguously, ask them to clarify in writing what they mean.

## Check the contrasts

Gold, silver and platinum jewelry sold in Spain must generally bear marks that identify the manufacturer or importer and guarantee the quality of the metal. Ask for a magnifying glass and check them before paying: they are usually inside the ring, on the closures or on the back. How to read them is explained in the guide on [contrasts and guarantee marks](/es/contrastes-y-marcas-de-garantia/).

## The price: what it includes

In a piece of jewelry you pay much more than the metal and stones: design, labor, brand, trade costs, insurance, warranty and after-sales service. That is why it does not make sense to compare the price of a jewel with the value of its metal by weight. What does make sense is:

- **Compare equivalent parts** in several stores.
- **Ask what part of the price corresponds to the brand** when there is one.
- **Be wary of permanent spectacular discounts,** which can hide inflated initial prices.
- **Think about use:** A piece to wear every day needs a sturdy metal and setting, even if it costs a little more.

## How to pay safely

- **Use means that leave a trace,** such as card or transfer, and save the receipts.
- **Remember that the law limits cash payments** when one of the parties acts as a professional, from certain amounts.
- **In custom orders,** agree in writing on the down payment, the deadline, the final price and what happens if you don't like the result.
- **Be wary of those who demand urgent payments,** in cash or by methods that are difficult to trace.

## Warranty and returns

When buying from a professional, the law protects you if the jewelry does not comply with what was contracted: if it has a defect, if it is not of the indicated metal or grade, or if the stone is not what was described. For products purchased since 2022, the legal guarantee is three years from delivery, and for second-hand products a shorter period can be agreed upon, with a legal minimum.

Another thing is to repent. In a physical store, there is no general right to return a piece of jewelry that you simply no longer like: it depends on the store's policy, which should be known and requested in writing before paying. In remote purchases, such as those on the internet, there is a right of withdrawal of fourteen calendar days, with some exceptions, such as personalized pieces. All details are explained in the guide on [warranties, returns and claims](/es/garantias-devoluciones-y-reclamaciones/).

## Custom and personalized jewelry

Ordering a unique piece is a magnificent experience, but it is best to leave everything in writing:

1. **Approved Design:** A signed or confirmed sketch or 3D model.
2. **Materials:** metal, sterling, stones and treatments.
3. **Closed price or method of calculation,** especially if the price of gold may vary during manufacturing.
4. **Delivery time.**
5. **What happens if the result does not match** what was agreed.

Remember that personalized pieces, such as engraved or custom-made rings, are normally excluded from the right of withdrawal for remote purchases.

## After purchasing

- **Keep the invoice and reports** in a safe place, with a digital copy.
- **Photograph the piece** from various angles.
- **Consider insuring it** if it is of value, as we explain in the guide on [professional appraisal](/es/tasacion-profesional/).
- **Check the settings** periodically at the jewelry store itself.

## Frequent errors

- **Buy without detailed invoice.**
- **Assume that jewelry purchased in a store can be returned**.
- **Do not ask about treatments** or the synthetic nature of the stones.
- **Order custom pieces** without a quote and written conditions.
- **Pay large amounts in cash** without proof.
- **Compare the price of a piece of jewelry** with the value of its metal by weight.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I return jewelry purchased in a physical store?

Only if the store allows it in its commercial policy or if the jewelry has a defect or does not comply with what was agreed. Repentance does not give the right to a return in a physical store.

### How long does the warranty last for a piece of jewelry?

For new products purchased from a professional since 2022, the legal guarantee is three years from delivery. In second hand a shorter term can be agreed upon, with the minimum established by law.

### Is it mandatory for the invoice to indicate if a stone is synthetic?

The seller must truthfully report the essential characteristics of the product. Always ask that the natural or synthetic nature and treatments appear in writing.

### What do I do if I suspect that the metal is not of the indicated grade?

Save the invoice, request an analysis at a trusted laboratory or workshop and, if confirmed, complain to the seller in writing.

## In summary

To buy jewelry in Spain safely, choose an identifiable seller, ask specific questions about metal, grade, weight, stones, treatments and reports, and ask for everything to appear on a detailed invoice. Review the contrasts with a magnifying glass, pay with means that leave a trace and leave any custom order in writing. Remember that the legal guarantee for new products is three years from 2022, that in a physical store there is no general right to return due to regret and that in remote purchases there are fourteen days of withdrawal, with exceptions such as personalized pieces.
