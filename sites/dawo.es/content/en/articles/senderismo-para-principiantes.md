Spain is one of the countries with the most mountains in Europe. From the Pyrenees to the Sierra Nevada, passing through the Picos de Europa, the Central System, the mountains of Andalusia or the volcanoes of the Canary Islands, there are routes for all levels just a few hours from almost any city. In addition, there is an extensive network of marked trails that allow you to walk safely and discover incredible landscapes.

Hiking is one of the most complete and accessible physical activities: it improves physical fitness, adapts to any age and combines exercise with nature. But the mountains also demand respect and preparation. This article explains how to plan your first route so that it is a pleasant and safe experience.

## Choose the right route

The most common mistake made by beginners is choosing a route that is too long or with too much elevation. To start, look at these data:

- **Distance:** between 6 and 12 kilometers is a good reference for the first outings.
- **Accumulated positive gradient:** is the sum of everything climbed along the route. To start, less than 500 meters.
- **Type of route:** circular, linear or round trip. Round trips allow you to turn around at any time.
- **Technical difficulty:** At first avoid routes with exposed steps, climbs or very rocky terrain.
- **Estimated time:** many guides indicate the duration. Always calculate something more if it is your first time.

A rough rule that many hikers use to calculate time is to add one hour for every four or five kilometers on the flat and an additional hour for every three or four hundred meters of ascent, plus stops. It is just an orientation; With experience you will know your own rhythm.

## Trail signs

In Spain, many approved trails follow a system of markings painted on rocks, trees or posts:

- **GR (grand tour):** white and red markings. Long routes, lasting several days, that can be traveled in stages.
- **PR (small route):** white and yellow marks. Routes of between 10 and 50 kilometers, usually lasting one day.
- **SL (local trail):** white and green markings. Short routes, less than 10 kilometers, ideal to start.

At intersections, a cross-shaped marking indicates "wrong path," and corner-shaped markings indicate a change of direction. Even so, the marks can be erased or missing, so it is advisable to also carry a map or an application with the route track.

## What to carry in your backpack

For a one-day route, a backpack of between 20 and 30 liters is enough. The basics:

- **Water:** at least one and a half or two liters, more if it is hot or the route is long. Don't rely on sources you don't know.
- **Food:** sandwiches, fruit, nuts, bars.
- **Outerwear:** a light warm layer and a raincoat, even in summer. In the mountains, the temperature drops with altitude and the weather can change quickly.
- **Sun protection:** cap, sunglasses and cream.
- **Mobile phone with a charged battery** and, if possible, an external battery.
- **Map or application with the route downloaded**, as there may be no coverage.
- **Basic first aid kit:** plasters, blister dressings, a bandage, antiseptic and personal medications.
- **Headlamp**, in case the route becomes longer.
- **Whistle**, useful in case of emergency.

## Footwear and clothing

- **Footwear:** hiking boots or shoes with a sole that grips well. For simple trail routes, trail shoes may be enough; For rocky terrain or with a heavy backpack, a mid-cut boot protects the ankle better. Never wear your shoes on a long route.
- **Technical socks**, which reduce friction and humidity.
- **Layered clothing:** a breathable t-shirt, a warm mid-layer, and an outer layer against wind and rain. This way you can adapt to changes in temperature.

## How to walk in the mountains

### On the climbs

- **Short steps and constant pace.** It is better to climb slowly without stopping than fast with many stops.
- **Breathe calmly.** If you choke, slow down.
- **Lean your body forward slightly.**
- **Use canes** if you have them: they distribute the effort with your arms.

### On the downhills

Downhills are more demanding on the knees and quads than you might think, and are where many sprains occur:

- **Short, controlled steps**, with knees slightly bent.
- **Watch where you step**, especially on loose rocky terrain.
- **Poles** reduce the load on the knees on long descents.
- **Don't run** if you don't have experience.

## Taking care of your feet: blisters

Blisters are the most common cause of a good ride ending badly. They appear due to friction and humidity, especially with new shoes, cotton socks or feet that move inside the boot on descents. To prevent them, wear broken-in shoes and technical socks, tie the laces well before a long descent and, if you have prone areas, protect them before going out with a suitable dressing or tape. The key is to act early: as soon as you notice a “hot spot,” stop, take off your boot, and cover the area. An annoyance taken care of in minute one does not become an open blister halfway through.

## summer and winter

- **In summer**, leave at dawn, avoid the middle of the day, choose shady routes or next to water and carry more water than you think is necessary. Heat stroke is a real risk in the southern and inland mountain ranges.
- **In winter**, the days are short and roads can be icy in shady areas. Calculate the route to finish with plenty of light and avoid routes with snow if you do not have the training and specific material, such as crampons and ice axes, and experience in using them.

## The security

- **Check the weather forecast** before leaving and avoid the mountains in the forecast for storms, snow or extreme heat.
- **Notify someone** of your route and expected return time.
- **Don't go alone** on your first outings.
- **Calculate your return time** with margin and leave early, especially in winter, when the days are short.
- **Turn around if necessary.** If the weather worsens, you feel very tired or the route is more difficult than anticipated, turning back is not a failure, it is a good decision.
- **In case of emergency, call 112.** Knowing your coordinates or the point on the route where you are makes rescue easier.

## Respect for the environment

- Do not leave the marked trails to avoid erosion.
- Take all your trash, including fruit peels, with you.
- Do not light fires outside authorized areas and respect prohibitions during fire risk seasons.
- Respect wildlife, livestock and property. Close the gates that you find closed.
- Find out about the rules of the protected natural areas you visit.

## Prepare physically

You don't have to be in exceptional shape to hike, but some preparation goes a long way:

- Walk daily, including hills or stairs.
- Strength exercises for legs: squats, lunges and climbing on a step.
- Exercises with slow descent, which prepare the quadriceps for descents and reduce soreness.
- Progressive outings: start with short routes and gradually increase the distance and elevation gain.

## Your first route, step by step

1. **One week before:** Choose a short, well-marked route, preferably a local trail or a short, well-known route. Check its distance, gradient and estimated time, and download the map or track.
2. **Two or three days before:** Check the weather forecast and prepare your backpack. Check your shoes and socks.
3. **The night before:** Leave everything ready, charge your phone and notify someone of the route and return time.
4. **The day of the route:** leave early, have a good breakfast and start at a leisurely pace. Make short stops for drinks and snacks every hour or so.
5. **At the top or at the point of interest:** enjoy, take photos and calculate the return. If you are short on time or strength, return the same way.
6. **When you return:** stretch gently, hydrate yourself and write down how you felt. This information will help you choose the next route.

## Frequent errors

1. **Choosing an overly ambitious route.**
2. **Carry little water.**
3. **Leave late** and let it get dark.
4. **Do not wear warm clothing or a raincoat.**
5. **Brand new footwear.**
6. **Depend only on the mobile phone without a backup battery.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need boots or do I need sneakers?

For simple routes on paths in good condition, trail or hiking shoes are usually sufficient. For rocky terrain, long hikes with a heavy backpack, or if you're prone to spraining your ankle, a mid-cut boot offers more protection.

### Are canes necessary?

They are not essential, but they help a lot on climbs and, above all, on long descents. Many people with knee discomfort notice a big difference.

### Can I go with children?

Yes, choosing short routes, with little gradient and with points of interest: a river, a viewpoint, a forest. Allow more time and bring enough water and food.

## In summary

A short and well-chosen route, a backpack with the essentials, good shoes, information about the weather and someone who knows where you are: with that, your first mountain route can be the beginning of a lifelong hobby. Start small, enjoy the landscape and remember that the summit is optional; come home, no.
