Paddle tennis has become one of the most practiced sports in Spain. Just walk through any neighborhood to find crowded slopes first thing in the morning, at lunchtime and at night. Its success has a simple explanation: it is easy to start, it is played in pairs, it is fun from the first day and it allows people of different levels to share the game.

Just because it's easy to start doesn't mean it's easy to master. The walls, pair placement and variety of shots mean there is always something to learn. This article brings together what you need to know for your first matches: what the court is like, how it is served, how the walls work, how points are counted and which shots should be learned first.

## The track

The paddle tennis court is a rectangle 20 meters long by 10 meters wide, divided into two fields by a net. The net measures 88 centimeters high in the center and slightly more at the ends.

What distinguishes padel is that the court is closed. At the bottom of each field there is a wall, usually made of glass, and on the sides there are glass walls in the part closest to the bottom and metal mesh in the rest. These walls are part of the game: the ball can bounce off them and remain in play.

On the ground there are two important lines on each court: the **service line**, parallel to the net, and the **center line**, which divides the service area into two squares.

## The basic material

- **Paddle:** unstrung, with a perforated surface. To begin with, a round paddle of medium or soft hardness facilitates control. In another article we explain in detail how to choose it.
- **Balls:** similar to tennis balls, although with a little less pressure.
- **Specific padel shoes:** with a sole that grips well on artificial grass with sand without blocking during turns. Running with running shoes on a paddle tennis court is one of the typical causes of ankle sprains.

## The serve

The paddle serve is very different from that of tennis and much simpler:

1. The serving player stands behind the service line, between the center line and the side wall.
2. Dribble the ball on the ground behind that line.
3. Hit the ball **at waist height or below**.
4. The ball must go over the net and bounce in the opposite service box, **diagonally**.

There are two opportunities to serve. If the ball hits the net and enters the correct box, the serve is repeated. If after bouncing in the correct box the ball hits the metal mesh, the serve is a foul; If it hits the glass, it is valid.

The first serve of each game is made from the right side, and the sides are alternated at each point.

### The rest

The player receiving the serve **must let the ball bounce** before returning it. You can hit it after the bounce or wait for it to bounce off the bottom or side glass.

## How walls work

This is the part that baffles beginners the most:

- The ball must first bounce on the ground in the opponent's field. If it directly hits an opponent's wall without first bouncing on its floor, the point goes to the other team.
- After the bounce on the ground, the ball may bounce off any wall on that field and the player may hit it as long as it has not bounced twice on the ground.
- A player can hit the ball against his own walls to return it to the opponent's court. This shot is called **counterwall** and is a defensive resource when the ball is very close to the glass.
- You can volley, that is, hit the ball before it bounces, at any time except during the rest of the serve.

Learning to "wait" for the ball after it bounces off the back glass is one of the keys to padel. Beginners usually try to hit it before the wall, in a hurry, when letting it come would give them much more time.

## The score

Padel is counted the same as tennis:

- **Points:** 15, 30, 40 and game. If both teams reach 40, they are called "equals." In the traditional system you have to win two points in a row to win the game. In many competitions it is played with **golden point**: in equals, the next point decides the game.
- **Games:** The team that reaches six games first with a difference of two wins the set. If they reach six of a kind, a tie-break is played.
- **Sets:** Matches are usually played in the best of three sets.

In matches between friends it is common to agree on the format before starting, especially if the court is reserved for a limited time.

## The basic blows

### Forehand and backhand

They are the groundstrokes, after the bounce. Unlike tennis, in padel, precision is sought more than power. The movement is shorter and the blade remains in front of the body. A good reference for the beginner: prepare the racket early, flex your legs and guide the ball towards the goal.

### The volley

It is done near the net, before the ball bounces. The movement is short, almost a block, with a firm wrist. The net is the area from which most points are won, so mastering the volley is essential.

### The globe

It is a high shot that goes over opponents when they are at the net, forcing them to retreat. It is probably the most important shot in amateur padel: it allows you to regain position on the net and upset the opposing pair.

### The tray

It is an overhead shot, with a slice effect, used to respond to a lob without losing position on the net. He does not seek to win the point, but to maintain control. It has its own article in this section.

### The finishing

Powerful overhead shot to finish the point. Beginners tend to spike all high balls, but many times a layup is safer.

## Pair placement

In padel, position on the court matters as much as technique:

- **The net is the attacking position.** The pair that dominates the net usually wins the point.
- **The bottom line is the defense position.** From there you seek to return the ball safely and, when possible, play a lob to go up to the net.
- **The couple moves together**, as if they were linked by an imaginary rope: if one goes up, the other goes up; If one retreats, so does the other.
- **The center of the court is a shared area.** It is advisable to agree who takes the balls that go in the middle. A common custom is for the player who has the right hand in the center to play them.

Communicating with short phrases ("mine", "yours", "up", "back") avoids many mistakes.

## Typical beginner mistakes

1. **Hitting too hard.** The uncontrolled power bounces off the glass and gives the ball to the opponent.
2. **Don't let the ball leave the wall.** There is more time than it seems.
3. **Stay in the middle of the court**, in the so-called "no man's zone", where the balls reach your feet.
4. **Forget the balloon.** It is the best resource to get out of defensive situations.
5. **Do not warm up.** Changes of direction and overhead strikes require prepared shoulders, knees and ankles.

## Warming up before playing

A ten-minute warm-up reduces the risk of common padel injuries, such as calf, ankle, shoulder and elbow injuries:

- Mobility of ankles, knees, hips and shoulders.
- Smooth lateral movements and crossed steps.
- Progressive rallies from the bottom, soft volleys and some overhead shots.

## Courtesy rules on the court

- Opponents are greeted before and after the game.
- When a ball from another court enters yours, play stops and is returned.
- If there are doubts about whether a ball was good or not, in friendly matches the point is usually awarded to the opponent or repeated.
- At the end of the reserved time, the court is promptly left free.

## Find partners and get started without spending a lot

To play paddle tennis you need four people, and that is sometimes the most difficult thing. Many clubs organize tiered open games, internal leagues and group classes that allow you to meet other players, and there are booking apps that show matches that are missing a player. The cost of the court is divided among the four, so padel is affordable. For the first weeks you do not need an expensive racket: a starter racket, round and soft to the touch, is the best choice, as we explain in the guide for [choosing padel racket](/articulos/elegir-pala-de-padel/). Once you have mastered the basic shots, the next step will be to learn the [tray and viper](/articulos/bandeja-y-vibora/).

## How to progress

The quickest way to improve is to combine matches with some classes. A good monitor corrects in just a few sessions errors that can take months to resolve on their own, especially in the placement and use of walls. It also helps to play with people of a slightly higher level than you.

Paddle tennis is a social sport, and that is part of its charm. Enjoy the games, laugh at the mistakes and remember that all the players, including those who make perfect layups today, started chasing balls that came off the glass where they were not expecting.
