In many Spanish cities, the bicycle has gone in a few years from a rarity to an everyday way of getting around. Cycle lanes have multiplied, public bike-share schemes have spread and more and more people are discovering that cycling to work is a way of exercising without finding extra time, saving money and avoiding traffic jams.

Getting around the city by bike has many benefits, but it also means knowing the rules, taking a few precautions and building up confidence little by little. This article is for anyone who wants to start using a bike for their daily journeys.

## The benefits

- **Exercise built into your day:** a twenty-minute bike journey each way adds up to a good part of the recommended weekly physical activity.
- **Predictable journey times:** over urban distances of up to five or six kilometres, a bike is usually as fast as a car in the rush hour, or faster.
- **Savings:** no fuel, no parking and simple maintenance.
- **Wellbeing:** many people arrive at work more alert and in a better mood.
- **The city:** less noise, less pollution and less space taken up.

## Choosing a bike

For the city, what matters most is comfort and practicality:

- **City or town bike:** upright riding position, mudguards, a rear rack and often hub gears, which need little maintenance. It is the most comfortable option for daily journeys.
- **Hybrid bike:** somewhat lighter and faster, with an intermediate position.
- **Folding bike:** ideal if you combine the bike with the train, underground or bus, or if you have little space at home.
- **Electric bike:** with pedal assistance, it lets you cover longer or hillier distances without arriving in a sweat. In the European Union, pedal-assisted bikes have assistance limited to 25 km/h and you must pedal for the motor to work.

Make sure the size is right: with the saddle at the correct height, your leg should be almost straight, with the knee slightly bent, when the pedal is at its lowest point.

## The basic equipment

- **Lights:** white at the front and red at the back. They are compulsory at night and in poor visibility.
- **A bell.**
- **Reflective clothing or items**, compulsory at night on roads outside towns and highly advisable in the city.
- **A helmet:** in Spain it is compulsory on roads outside towns, and for under-sixteens on any road. In town, national rules do not generally require one for adults, although some local by-laws and specific situations do; it is advisable protection in any case.
- **A good-quality lock.**
- **Mudguards and a rear rack**, very practical for everyday use.

Check the current rules and your local council's by-laws, because they can vary.

## Basic traffic rules

A bicycle is a vehicle and must follow the traffic rules:

- **Obey traffic lights and signs.**
- **Use the cycle lane where there is one**, or the road, in the right-hand lane, unless you need to turn left. In many cities you may ride in the middle of the lane when it is necessary for safety, especially on single-lane streets.
- **Do not ride on pavements** unless a sign or the local by-law expressly allows it, and always give way to pedestrians.
- **Signal your manoeuvres** with your arm before turning or changing lanes.
- **Do not use earphones or your phone** while riding.
- **Do not ride under the influence of alcohol**: drink-driving limits also apply to cyclists.

## Riding safely on the road

For many beginners, sharing the street with cars and buses is the most daunting part. Some keys:

### Be visible and predictable

- Ride in a straight line, without weaving in and out of parked cars.
- Keep about a metre away from parked cars to avoid doors that open suddenly, one of the most common accidents.
- Signal in good time.
- Make eye contact with drivers at junctions.

### Watch out for blind spots

Large vehicles, such as buses and lorries, have wide blind spots. Do not position yourself on their right at a junction, especially if they may turn that way. It is better to wait behind.

### Roundabouts

On roundabouts, ride in the middle of the lane so that drivers see you and do not try to overtake you on the roundabout itself. Signal your exit.

## Choosing your route

The shortest route by car is not always the best by bike. Look for:

- **Cycle lanes and quiet streets**, even if they mean a small detour.
- **Streets with a 30 km/h limit**, more and more common in Spanish cities.
- **Parks and pedestrian areas** where bikes are allowed.

Many map apps offer routes designed for cycling. Before you ride to work for the first time, try the route out calmly at the weekend.

## Parking and protecting your bike

Theft is one of urban cyclists' main worries:

- **Use a sturdy lock**, preferably a D-lock.
- **Lock the frame and one wheel to something fixed**, such as a bike stand.
- **Avoid leaving it in the street overnight** if you can.
- **Note down the frame number** and take photos of the bike. In some cities there are bike registers that make it easier to get a stolen bike back.

## Carrying loads

- **Panniers on the rear rack** or a front basket are much more comfortable than a rucksack, especially on long journeys or in the heat.
- **For the shopping**, large panniers or a trailer let you carry quite a lot of weight.
- **With children**, there are approved child seats, trailers and cargo bikes. Find out properly about the rules and about safety.

## Basic maintenance

- **Check your tyre pressure** every week or two.
- **Check your brakes** regularly.
- **Keep the chain clean and lubricated.**
- **Take the bike for a service** at least once a year.

Learning to fix a puncture will give you a lot of peace of mind.

## Fixing a puncture

With a spare inner tube or a patch kit, two tyre levers and a small pump, a puncture can be sorted out in a quarter of an hour:

1. **Take the wheel off.** If it has a quick release, open it; if it has nuts, loosen them. For the rear wheel, first shift the chain onto the smallest sprocket.
2. **Lever the tyre off on one side** with the tyre levers and take out the inner tube.
3. **Find the cause:** run your fingers carefully around the inside of the tyre to find the glass, thorn or stone that pierced it, and remove it. If you do not, you will get another puncture straight away.
4. **Change the tube or put on a patch**, after sanding and cleaning the area around the hole.
5. **Put it back together**, easing the tyre on with your hands without pinching the tube, and pump it up to the pressure shown on the side of the tyre.

Practising it once at home, without any hurry, takes away much of the fear of your first puncture in the street.

## The first few weeks

There is no need to start by cycling every day. A sensible progression is to try the route at the weekend, use the bike two days in the first week and add days as you feel comfortable. Get the bike ready the night before, with the lights charged and the tyres checked: the fewer obstacles there are in the morning, the easier it will be to make it a habit.

## Heat and rain

- **In the heat**, leave in good time so that you can go more slowly and not arrive soaked. Carrying a spare T-shirt or using an electric bike helps.
- **In the rain**, mudguards, a waterproof jacket and a longer braking distance. Be careful with painted road markings and manhole covers, which are very slippery when wet.

## Combining the bike with public transport

In many Spanish cities you can combine the bike with commuter trains, the underground or the bus, with rules that vary from one transport company to another: times when bikes are allowed on board, specific carriages, or the requirement that it be a folding bike in the rush hour. Checking these rules opens up many possibilities: cycling to the station, doing a long stretch by train and finishing the journey by bike.

Public bike-share schemes, found in many cities, are another way to start without having to buy a bike or worry about theft or parking. Many now include electric bikes.

## Common mistakes

1. **Riding too close to parked cars.**
2. **Riding on the pavement** where it is not allowed.
3. **Not using lights** at dusk.
4. **Passing large vehicles on the right** at junctions.
5. **Using a cheap lock.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Is cycling in the city dangerous?

Like any way of getting around, it carries risks, but they are greatly reduced by following the rules, choosing your routes well and riding visibly and predictably. What is more, the health benefits of daily exercise are considerable.

### What distance is reasonable?

For a beginner, journeys of up to five kilometres are very comfortable. With an electric bike, many people cover ten kilometres or more without any trouble.

### Will I arrive at work sweaty?

It depends on the distance, the pace and the heat. Pedalling calmly, wearing breathable clothes and, if possible, bringing a change of clothes deals with most situations.

## In short

Getting around the city by bike is one of the simplest ways of building exercise into daily life. A comfortable bike, lights, a good lock, knowing the rules and riding visibly and predictably are the basis for getting started. Try a short journey at the weekend and discover what your city has to offer on two wheels.
