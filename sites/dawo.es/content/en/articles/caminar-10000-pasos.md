Smartwatches, activity trackers and phones have made popular a figure that many people chase every day: 10,000 steps. For some it is a motivating goal; for others, an unreachable target that makes them feel that if they do not get there, it is not worth bothering. But where does that number come from? Do you really need to walk that much to notice the benefits?

Walking is the most accessible form of physical activity and one of the most underrated. It needs no equipment, it can be done at any age and it fits into everyday life. This article reviews what research says about daily steps and suggests simple ways to walk more.

## Where 10,000 steps comes from

The 10,000-step figure was not born in a laboratory. It is usually traced back to a marketing campaign for a pedometer in Japan in the 1960s, whose name referred to that number. It was a round number and easy to remember, and it became a popular reference long before there were any studies to back it up.

That does not mean that walking 10,000 steps is a bad idea, just that the figure is not a magic threshold.

## What the studies say

In recent years several large studies have been published linking the number of daily steps with health. Some general conclusions:

- **More steps are linked to lower mortality**, compared with very sedentary people.
- **The benefits appear well before 10,000 steps.** Going from very few steps a day to a few thousand more is already linked to significant improvements.
- **The benefit tends to level off beyond a certain point.** A large pooled analysis of studies published in 2022 found that the reduction in the risk of death levelled off at around 6,000 to 8,000 steps a day in people over sixty, and at 8,000 to 10,000 in younger adults.
- **Every increase counts**: even modest rises in daily steps are linked to benefits.

These studies are observational, which means they show associations and do not on their own prove cause and effect, but the pattern is consistent with what we know about physical activity.

## Intensity matters too

Not all steps are equal. Walking briskly, at a pace that speeds up your breathing but still lets you talk, brings extra cardiovascular benefits. Some studies suggest that cadence — the number of steps per minute — also matters: spells of fast walking are linked to benefits beyond the total number of steps.

A practical guide: walking at a pace at which you notice your breathing quicken slightly counts as moderate physical activity. The World Health Organization's recommendation of 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week can be met by walking briskly for about thirty minutes on most days.

## How many steps you take now

Before setting yourself a target, it is worth knowing where you are starting from. Carry your phone or wear a watch for a normal week without changing your habits, and work out your daily average. Many people with desk jobs find that they walk between 3,000 and 5,000 steps a day.

From there, a reasonable goal is to **add 1,000 to 2,000 steps a day** to your average, keep that up for a few weeks and then go up again if you feel like it.

## How to add more steps without changing your life

### When getting around

- Get off the bus or underground one stop early.
- Park further from where you are going.
- Walk to nearby errands.
- Take the stairs instead of the lift or the escalator.

### At work

- Get up every hour and walk for a few minutes.
- Take phone calls while walking.
- Suggest walking meetings for short conversations.
- Go and talk to a colleague instead of messaging them.

### In your free time

- Go for a walk after lunch or dinner. A walk after meals also helps to control blood sugar.
- Meet friends for a walk instead of sitting down for a drink.
- Explore your town or city on foot at the weekend.
- Walk the dog a little further.

## Walking as training

If you want your walk to be more than a way of adding steps:

- **Brisk walking:** 30 minutes at a lively pace, three to five days a week.
- **Walking intervals:** alternate 3 minutes of fast walking with 2 minutes at a comfortable pace, for 30 minutes.
- **Hills and stairs:** they raise the intensity without any need to run.
- **Nordic walking:** walking with purpose-made poles also works the arms and trunk, and it is very popular among middle-aged and older people.

## Walking and health

Research has linked regular walking with:

- Better cardiovascular health and blood pressure.
- Better blood sugar control.
- Better mood and fewer symptoms of anxiety and depression.
- Keeping your mobility and independence as you age.
- Better sleep quality.

For many people who do no exercise at all, walking is the simplest way into a more active life.

## What walking does not replace

Walking is excellent, but it does not cover every need. To maintain strength, muscle mass and bone density, it is a good idea to add strength exercises at least two days a week. And to improve your cardiovascular fitness beyond a certain point, it helps to include some rather more intense activities.

## A four-week plan

For someone starting from around 4,000 or 5,000 steps a day:

| Week | Daily target | How to get there |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Your average + 1,000 | A 10-minute walk after lunch or dinner |
| 2 | Your average + 2,000 | The 10-minute walk plus getting off public transport one stop early |
| 3 | Your average + 3,000 | Two walks of 10 to 15 minutes, and the stairs whenever you can |
| 4 | Your average + 3,000, with 20 min of brisk walking | One of the walks at a lively pace |

If you fall short one day, it does not matter: what counts is the weekly average. And if a week feels hard, repeat it before moving up.

## Walking in the heat, the cold or the rain

- **In the heat**, walk first thing in the morning or at dusk, look for shade, and take water and a cap. On days with a high-temperature warning, shopping centres or museums can be a good place to add steps.
- **In the cold**, dress in layers, protect your hands and head, and start at a gentle pace to warm up.
- **In the rain**, a good waterproof and shoes that do not slip. If you do not feel like going out, the stairs at home or at work, or simply walking up and down the corridor during a phone call, count too.

## Walking with company

One of the best allies for walking more is company. Arranging a regular walk several days a week with a friend, your partner or a group of neighbours turns exercise into a social moment and makes it harder to skip. Many town councils and health centres in Spain run healthy-walking groups, often free of charge, which are a good way in for people who are starting out.

## Counting steps sensibly

Phones, watches and wristbands do not count in exactly the same way: it depends on where you wear them, how you swing your arms and each device's algorithm, and two devices can differ by hundreds or even thousands of steps by the end of the day. That is why there is little point in comparing yourself with other people. What is useful is comparing yourself with yourself, with the same device, always worn in the same way. If you leave your phone on the table while you move around the house or the office, those steps will not be recorded: bear that in mind before a low figure discourages you.

## If you are overweight or walking is uncomfortable

For anyone starting from very little activity, it is better to begin with short walks on flat ground and repeat them several times a day than to take a single long walk. Comfortable shoes with good support make a big difference. If pain appears in your feet, knees or hips and does not ease after a few days of relative rest, see your doctor or a physiotherapist. In the meantime, walking in water in a swimming pool or pedalling on an exercise bike lets you stay active with much less load on your joints.

## Common mistakes

1. **Getting obsessed with the figure** and feeling bad about not reaching 10,000.
2. **Trying to jump from 3,000 to 12,000 steps in one go**, risking aches in your feet and legs.
3. **Wearing unsuitable shoes** for walking a lot.
4. **Counting only the steps** and forgetting about intensity.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do steps at home count?

Yes. Every step adds up, although steps taken on continuous walks at a good pace bring more cardiovascular benefits.

### Is it better to walk a lot in one go or to spread it out?

Both are beneficial. Spreading your steps across the day helps to break up periods of sitting, which also have negative effects.

### What shoes do I need for walking?

Comfortable shoes with good support and some cushioning. You do not need special footwear for walks around town.

## In short

10,000 steps is a useful goal, but not a magic threshold. The benefits start well before that, every extra step counts and intensity matters too. Know your starting point, add steps gradually, include some stretches of brisk walking and combine walking with strength exercises. It is one of the simplest investments in your health there is.
