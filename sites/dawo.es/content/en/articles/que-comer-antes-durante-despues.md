Sports nutrition is full of myths, special products and strict rules that seem taken from the professional world. For the amateur athlete who trains three or four times a week, the reality is much simpler: a varied and balanced diet covers most of their needs, and a few adjustments around training help them perform better and recover.

This article explains what to eat before, during and after exercise, with practical examples adapted to the usual schedules in Spain. It is not a substitute for the advice of a dietitian-nutritionist, especially if you have a medical condition, specific weight goals, or train at a very high level.

## The base: what you eat every day

Before thinking about the banana before running or the shake after the gym, it is worth keeping in mind that what most influences performance and health is the day-to-day diet. A diet based on:

- **Vegetables and vegetables** in abundance.
- **Varied fruits**.
- **Legumes**, several times a week.
- **Cereals**, preferably whole grains: bread, rice, pasta, oatmeal.
- **Quality proteins**: fish, eggs, legumes, dairy products, meat, nuts.
- **Olive oil** as main fat.
- **Water** as a regular drink.

This pattern, very similar to the traditional Mediterranean diet, is an excellent base for any athlete.

## Before training

The objective of the pre-meal is to arrive at training with sufficient energy and without digestive discomfort.

### When to eat

- **Full meal:** between two and four hours before exercise.
- **Light snack:** between thirty minutes and an hour before, if it has been a long time since the last meal.

### What to eat

Before training, it is advisable to prioritize foods rich in **carbohydrates**, easy to digest, with moderate amounts of protein and low in fat and fiber, which are digested more slowly.

Examples of a previous meal, two or three hours before:

- Pasta or rice with vegetables and some chicken or egg.
- Toast with tomato and oil, and yogurt.
- A not very abundant dish of lentils, if they suit you.

Examples of snacks, half an hour to an hour before:

- A banana.
- A toast with jam or honey.
- A yogurt with a little fruit.
- A small handful of nuts and a piece of fruit, if that suits you.

### Train on an empty stomach

Many people train early in the morning without having breakfast. For light or moderate sessions of less than an hour, it is well tolerated by most healthy people. For intense or long sessions, eating something light beforehand usually improves performance. If you notice dizziness, weakness or lack of energy, have a small snack before training. People with diabetes or other diseases should consult their doctor.

## during training

### Sessions of less than an hour

In most cases, you don't need to eat anything during exercise. It is enough to drink water according to thirst, especially if it is hot.

### Sessions of more than an hour to an hour and a half

In prolonged efforts, such as a long run, a bike ride lasting several hours or a mountain route, providing carbohydrates during exercise helps maintain energy. Some options:

- Fruit: banana, dates, raisins.
- Small quince or honey sandwiches.
- Sports drinks.
- Specific gels or bars, if they suit you.

A common guideline in sports literature is to provide between 30 and 60 grams of carbohydrates per hour in efforts of between one and three hours, starting early and in small doses. Practice in training what you are going to do in a competition: the stomach also trains.

### Hydration

Drink according to thirst and adapt the amount to the heat and duration. During long efforts with a lot of sweat, drinks with salts help replace lost sodium. You can read more in our article about [hydration for athletes](/articulos/hidratacion-para-deportistas/).

## after training

Recovery has three goals: replenish energy, repair muscles, and rehydrate.

### Proteins

Proteins provide the amino acids necessary to repair and build muscle. One serving of protein-rich foods at the post-workout meal is sufficient for most people. Some examples:

- A couple of eggs.
- A can of tuna or sardines.
- A plate of legumes.
- A yogurt or a glass of milk.
- A piece of fish or meat.

More important than the exact moment is the total amount of protein throughout the day, well distributed over several meals.

### carbohydrates

After long or intense workouts, carbohydrates help replenish muscle glycogen. If you are going to train again in less than 24 hours, this replenishment is especially important. Bread, rice, pasta, potatoes, legumes and fruit are good options.

### The famous "metabolic window"

For years it was said that you had to eat within thirty minutes of exercising so as not to "lose" the benefits. Today we know that this window is much wider than previously believed. For the amateur athlete, it is enough to eat a full meal in the following hours. Only in cases of very close training or prolonged fasting does it make more sense to eat early.

## Practical examples according to the schedule

### You train first thing in the morning

- Before, if you need it: a banana or toast with honey.
- After: full breakfast, for example toast with tomato and oil, scrambled eggs, fruit and a coffee with milk.

### You train at noon

- Normal breakfast and, if there is a lot of time until training, a mid-morning snack.
- After: midday meal, with legumes, rice or pasta, vegetables and a source of protein.

### You train in the evening

- Snack one or two hours before: yogurt with fruit, small sandwich or fruit and nuts.
- After: full but not excessively heavy dinner, such as fish with potatoes and salad or an omelet with bread and vegetables.

## Depending on the type of training

The general guidelines are adjusted a bit depending on what you train:

- **Long endurance** (running, cycling, hiking): carbohydrates are the main fuel. On long session days, it is advisable that they be present at all meals, and during efforts of more than an hour or an hour and a half, also during exercise.
- **Strength** (gym, home training): Protein spread throughout the day is especially important for recovery and muscle gain. It is not necessary to eat right before, but you do need to avoid training while very hungry.
- **Intermittent sports** (football, paddle tennis, tennis): they combine both demands. A meal rich in carbohydrates two or three hours before the game and good hydration make the difference in the second half.

## On-the-go snack ideas

Having simple options on hand prevents you from arriving at training without energy or resorting to ultra-processed products:

- Banana, tangerines, apple.
- A small sandwich of bread with tomato and fresh cheese.
- Natural yogurt with a handful of oats.
- A handful of nuts and some raisins or dates.
- Rice pancakes with a little peanut butter.
- A glass of milk or enriched vegetable drink with a piece of bread.

## The eve of a race or an important match

The day before a competition is not the time to do experiments. Some simple guidelines:

- **Make a familiar dinner**, with a good portion of carbohydrates: pasta, rice or potatoes with some protein and cooked vegetables.
- **Moderate fiber and fat** if you have a sensitive stomach, to avoid discomfort the next day.
- **Avoid alcohol**, which worsens rest and hydration.
- **Drink normally** throughout the day, without forcing large amounts of water at night.
- **Leave breakfast prepared** that you have already tried before training.

There is no need to "carbohydrate load" for several days for a game or a race of less than an hour and a half: just eat well and rest.

## Frequent errors

1. **Train on a full stomach** after a heavy meal.
2. **Try new foods on race or game day.**
3. **Abuse bars and gels** in short sessions that do not need them.
4. **Skipping meals** to "compensate" for exercise, which often results in poorer performance and more hunger later.
5. **Neglect vegetables and fruit** by focusing only on proteins and carbohydrates.

## Supplements

Most amateur athletes do not need supplements if their diet is adequate. Some, such as caffeine or creatine, have evidence of effectiveness in specific contexts, but should be used with knowledge and, preferably, with the advice of a professional. Be wary of products that promise spectacular results.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need protein shakes?

It is not essential. They can be a practical option if you struggle to meet your protein needs through food, but foods cover those needs perfectly.

### Is it bad to eat right before training?

A large meal just before can cause digestive discomfort. A light snack is usually well tolerated.

### Should I eat less if I want to lose weight and train?

To lose weight you need an energy deficit, but if it is too large, performance and recovery suffer. A dietitian-nutritionist can help you adjust it.

## In summary

A varied Mediterranean-based diet, a meal rich in carbohydrates two or three hours before training, water during exercise, some extra energy only during long efforts and a complete meal with proteins and carbohydrates afterwards: nothing more is needed to perform and recover well as an amateur athlete.
