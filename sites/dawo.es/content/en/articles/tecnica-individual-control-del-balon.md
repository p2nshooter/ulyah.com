In amateur football you train little and play a lot. Most adult players limit themselves to the weekly match, and those who have training with their team usually dedicate it to physical preparation or tactical aspects. What almost no one works on after adolescence is individual technique: control, driving, passing and hitting.

And yet, it is what is most noticeable in a game. A good first check gives you time to think; Poor control forces you to run after the ball. Individual technique can be improved at any age and, best of all, it can be trained alone, with a ball, a wall and a few cones. This article proposes a practical routine to do so.

## Why the technique continues to improve in adulthood

There is the idea that technique is learned as a child and that as an adult it cannot be changed. It is true that childhood is a privileged stage for learning motor skills, but the adult brain is still capable of learning new gestures and refining the ones it already knows. The key is **deliberate practice**: repetitions with attention, with a specific objective and gradually increasing the difficulty.

Ten or fifteen minutes a day, or two half-hour sessions per week, are enough to notice improvements in a few weeks.

## The material

- A ball the size you use in your games.
- A smooth wall: that of a fronton, a park wall or an empty court.
- Four to six cones or, failing that, bottles or backpacks.
- Sneakers or boots suitable for the surface.

## Ball warm-up

Before starting, spend five minutes mobility of your ankles, knees and hips, and driving the ball gently through space, alternating both feet. Gentle touches with the interior, exterior and plant help to "awaken" sensitivity.

## Block 1: control

The first control is the most important technical gesture. Your objective is not only to stop the ball, but to leave it in the best position for the next action.

### Exercise 1: control against the wall

1. Stand about five meters from the wall.
2. Pass the ball with the inside of your right foot and control it with the inside of your right foot when it returns.
3. Repeat ten times with each foot, then alternate: step with one foot and control with the other.

### Exercise 2: oriented control

Oriented control consists of receiving the ball by already taking it towards the space you want to go to.

1. Pass the ball against the wall.
2. Upon receiving it, control it with the inside of your foot to the side, leaving it one step away.
3. Alternate sides: one control to the right, one to the left.
4. Progress by using the outside of your foot.

### Exercise 3: control of high balls

Throw the ball up with your hands or hit it against the wall to make it come back high, and control it with your thigh, chest or instep, trying to get it to land dead on the ground near you. Start with gentle throws.

## Block 2: driving

Driving is moving forward with the ball under control. A good driver looks more at the field than at the ball.

### Exercise 4: slalom between cones

1. Place five or six cones in a line, one and a half meters apart.
2. Go through the slalom driving with the inside and outside of the same foot.
3. Come back with the other foot.
4. Progress by reducing the distance between cones and increasing speed.

### Exercise 5: driving with your head up

Drive in a straight line for twenty meters, trying to look straight ahead most of the time, with brief glances at the ball. If you are with someone, ask them to hold up a number of fingers that you have to say out loud while driving.

### Exercise 6: changes of direction

Drive towards a cone, brake with the brakes and exit in another direction. Practice different changes: with the inside, with the outside, stepping and dragging the ball backwards.

## Block 3: the pass

### Exercise 7: passes to the wall with both feet

1. From about eight meters, pass the ball against the wall with the inside of your foot.
2. Always try to hit the same point on the wall. You can mark it with chalk.
3. Make series of twenty passes with each foot.
4. Progress by playing one touch: return the ball without controlling it.

### Exercise 8: Pass to first touch with spin

Pass against the wall, let the ball return, control it with one foot pointing it to one side and pass with the other foot. This exercise combines control and passing and improves coordination between both legs.

## Block 4: the hit

### Exercise 9: hitting with instep

From about fifteen meters, hit the ball against the wall with your instep, trying to make it go low and hard. Look at three details: the supporting foot next to the ball, the body slightly above the ball, and the ankle firm at impact.

### Exercise 10: precision

Mark two or three areas on the wall and try to hit them with different types of hits: inside, instep and outside.

## Block 5: dribbling

### Exercise 11: feints in front of a cone

Set up a cone as if it were a defender. Drive towards it and practice simple dribbles: a feint with your body, a change of direction with the outside, a bicycle or a "cut" with the inside. The important thing is speed after the dribble.

### Exercise 12: protection of the ball

With a partner, practice protecting the ball with your body for ten seconds, with your back to him, using your sole and inside to move the ball away from your foot.

## A twenty minute routine

| Block | Exercises | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | Mobility and smooth driving | 4 min |
| Control | Exercises 1 and 2 | 5 min |
| Driving | Exercises 4 and 6 | 4 min |
| Pass | Exercise 7 | 4 min |
| Hitting or dribbling | Alternate exercises 9 and 11 depending on the day | 3 min |

Repeat this routine two or three times a week, and every two weeks increase the difficulty: more speed, less space, less touches.

## If you have a partner

With another person, the exercises gain realism, because the ball no longer always returns the same:

- **Two-touch pass and control**, ten or twelve meters away: control oriented to one side and pass with the opposite leg.
- **Moving wall:** you advance in parallel and return the ball to each other with the first touch, as in a match wall.
- **Aerial balls:** one throws with the hand at different heights and the other controls with the thigh, chest or instep and returns with the foot.
- **One against one in a small space:** five meters wide, a cone as a goal on each side; The one who defends tries to steal and the one who attacks, protect and turn.

Switch roles often and keep track of who gets the most clean actions: a little competition makes practice more intense.

## Measure your progress

Choose two or three simple tests and repeat them every two weeks under the same conditions: how many passes to the wall you make in one minute with each foot from the same distance, how long it takes you to complete the slalom there and back, or your record for consecutive touches without the ball falling. Write down the results. Seeing your numbers improve is a great motivation to keep practicing on the days when you feel like it less.

## The least able leg

Most players have one dominant leg and another that they hardly use. In amateur football, being able to pass and control with both legs gives a huge advantage. Dedicate at least a third of each session to the less capable leg, even if the exercises go worse. It's normal to feel clumsy at first; With a few weeks of practice, you will notice the difference.

## Frequent errors

1. **Always practice what already works well.** The improvement is in what it costs.
2. **Going too fast too soon.** Precision first, speed second.
3. **Practice without attention.** A hundred distracted touches are worth less than twenty well done ones.
4. **Forget the warm-up.** Strong cold hits are a common cause of injuries to the adductors and quadriceps.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it useful to touch the ball?

Yes. Keeping the ball in the air with your feet, thighs and head improves sensitivity and coordination. It is not a skill that is used like this in games, but it helps to control difficult balls better.

### Can I practice at home?

In a small space you can practice touches with the sole, driving in a small space and control with a softer ball. Be careful with fragile objects and neighbors.

### How long will it take me to notice improvements?

It depends on the frequency and your starting point, but many players notice more confident control after three or four weeks of regular practice.

## In summary

Individual technique is the great forgotten territory of amateur football. With a ball, a wall and twenty minutes, two or three times a week, any player can improve their control, passing and confidence with the ball. And that confidence is noticeable in every game.
