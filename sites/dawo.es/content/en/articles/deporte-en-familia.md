Between work, school, after-school activities and housework, finding time for sport is hard. Finding time to do it as a family can seem impossible. Yet moving together is one of the best investments a family can make: children pick up healthy habits by copying adults, adults add physical activity without taking time away from the family, and everyone shares moments that are remembered.

The World Health Organization's recommendations for children and adolescents aged 5 to 17 call for an average of at least sixty minutes a day of moderate or vigorous physical activity. Many Spanish children fall short of that figure. Sport as a family is a simple way of getting closer to it.

This article suggests ideas for activities adapted to different ages and tips for turning them into a habit.

## Why moving as a family works

- **Example:** children with active parents are more likely to be active. What they see done weighs more than what they are told.
- **Fun:** when exercise is a shared game, children do not experience it as an obligation.
- **Quality time:** walking, cycling or playing together creates space to talk and share without screens.
- **Everyone's health:** adults benefit too, and it is easier to keep it up when the activity is part of family life.

## Activities by age

### With babies and children up to 3

- **Walks** with the pushchair or a baby carrier, at a good pace for the adult.
- **Free play on the floor** and in playgrounds: crawling, climbing, rolling.
- **Baby swimming**, at pools that offer water activities for families.
- **Copycat games**: jumping like frogs, walking like bears, stretching like cats.

### With children from 3 to 6

- **Balance bikes or bikes with stabilisers**, and outings where the adult walks or cycles alongside.
- **Ball games**: throwing, catching, kicking.
- **Short nature walks**, with appealing goals: a river, a swing, a viewpoint.
- **Dancing at home**, to music they like.
- **Obstacle courses** with cushions, chairs and ropes.

### With children from 6 to 12

- **Bike rides** along cycle lanes, greenways or through parks.
- **Hiking** on suitable routes of a few kilometres, with stops to explore.
- **Swimming** in a pool or at safe, lifeguarded beaches.
- **Racket sports**: badminton or padel on adapted courts.
- **Roller skates or a scooter.**
- **Football, basketball or volleyball matches in the park**, parents against children or mixed teams.
- **Traditional games**: tag, hide-and-seek, skipping, hopscotch.

### With teenagers

- **Shared challenges**: training together for a 5-kilometre fun run, a walking route over several days or a kayak trip down a river.
- **Activities that are new to everyone**: indoor climbing, surfing, skiing, canoeing.
- **Training together** at the gym or at home, if the teenager is interested.
- **Letting the teenager choose** some activities: involving them in the decision increases their motivation.

## Greenways: an extraordinary resource

Spain has an extensive network of greenways (*vías verdes*), old railway lines converted into paths for walkers and cyclists. They usually have gentle gradients, are away from traffic and cross beautiful countryside, with tunnels and viaducts that children love. They are ideal for family bike rides. Before you go, check the state of the route and plan distances suited to the children's ages.

## How to make it a habit

1. **Set specific times**: the Sunday morning walk, the Saturday bike ride, Friday evening dancing.
2. **Start small**: twenty or thirty minutes is enough at first.
3. **Build movement into your routine**: walking or cycling to school whenever possible.
4. **Look for activities everyone enjoys**, and take turns so that each member of the family gets to choose now and then.
5. **Celebrate achievements**: the first time your child cycles without stabilisers or completes a route.
6. **Have the gear ready**: bikes checked, a rucksack with water and a snack, suitable clothing.

## Adapting the effort

- **Children are not small adults.** Their attention span and stamina are different. Activities need variety, frequent breaks and an element of play.
- **Plan routes around the youngest**, not the adult.
- **Always take water and food**, and plan rest stops.
- **Protect them from the sun**: a cap, sun cream and avoiding the hottest hours.
- **Safety on the bike**: helmets for the children — compulsory for under-sixteens in Spain — and choosing safe routes.

## When nobody feels like it

There will be days when no one wants to. Some strategies:

- **Suggest rather than impose**: "Shall we go to the park and try out the kite?" works better than "we have to exercise".
- **Add an incentive**: a special snack at the end of the route, a treasure hunt, photos for an album.
- **Invite friends**: children are very motivated by the company of other children.
- **Be flexible**: if it does not happen one day, it does not matter; what counts is consistency over the weeks.

## Benefits for children

Regular physical activity in childhood is linked to:

- Better fitness and cardiovascular health.
- Development of coordination and motor skills.
- Stronger bones and muscles.
- Better sleep.
- Better mood and stress management.
- Better concentration and school performance, in some studies.
- Social skills, cooperation and respect for rules.

## An example of an active weekend

To turn ideas into plans, here is an example weekend with children aged between six and ten:

- **Saturday morning:** an hour's bike ride along a cycle lane or a greenway, with a stop for a snack in a park. Approximate distance: eight to twelve kilometres, depending on the children's age and experience.
- **Saturday afternoon:** free time at home, with a spell of play in the park as evening falls.
- **Sunday morning:** an hour-and-a-half nature walk with a treasure hunt: find a pine cone, a feather, a heart-shaped leaf, a river.
- **Sunday afternoon:** dancing at home or a game of badminton in the courtyard or the park.

There is no need to do everything every week. As long as there are one or two shared activities, the habit builds.

## Walking or cycling to school

The school run is a daily chance to move that many families miss. Walking or cycling, when distance and safety allow, adds physical activity without finding extra time and helps children arrive more awake. Some cities have "safe routes to school" schemes and groups of families who walk children to school, a kind of walking bus that picks pupils up along the way. If there is not one in your area, it might be a good idea to suggest it to the school's parents' association.

## Moving as a family without spending much

Family sport does not have to be expensive. Parks, beaches, country paths and greenways are free, and many town councils organise outdoor activities, guided walks or sports days for families free of charge or at very low prices. Municipal sports centres often offer family passes for the pool, and clubs hold open days when children can try a sport before signing up. Children's bikes, skates and helmets are soon outgrown, so buying them second-hand or passing them on between families is very sensible, as long as they are in good condition.

## When someone in the family has a disability

Many activities can be adapted so that the whole family can take part: accessible routes, adapted bikes or tandems, swimming, ball games with modified rules. There are adapted-sport clubs and federations in every autonomous community in Spain that advise families and organise inclusive activities. Talking to the health professional or physiotherapist who works with the child helps you choose the right activity.

## Common mistakes

1. **Planning activities that are too long** for the youngest.
2. **Turning play into competition** when the children just want to have fun.
3. **Constantly correcting technique**: in the family, fun comes first.
4. **Forgetting sun protection and water.**
5. **Letting screens fill every free moment.**

## Frequently asked questions

### What if we parents are not fit?

Even better: sport as a family is a chance for adults to start moving. Activities can be adapted to everyone's level.

### How much activity does a child need?

The WHO recommends that children and adolescents aged 5 to 17 get an average of at least sixty minutes a day of moderate or vigorous activity, including activities that strengthen muscles and bones on several days a week.

### What do I do if my child does not like sport?

Try different activities, without pressure: dancing, skating, climbing, swimming, walks with a dog. Often the problem is not movement itself, but a particular activity that does not motivate them.

## In short

Moving as a family does not need big plans or expensive equipment: a walk, a bike ride, a game in the park or dancing at home is enough. What matters is consistency and fun. Children learn what they see, and an active family sows healthy habits that last a lifetime.
