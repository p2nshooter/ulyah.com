In amateur football there is a phrase that is repeated in every locker room: "in the second half we couldn't take it anymore." It is the logical consequence of playing a very demanding sport without adequate physical preparation. But it is also common to see players who go out for a forty-minute run at a constant pace during the week and still drown in games.

The explanation is that football is not a continuous career. It is a succession of efforts of very variable intensity: walking, jogging, running, sprinting, braking, changing direction, jumping and starting again. The resistance required is **intermittent**. This article explains how to train it effectively with the little time that an amateur player has.

## What is the effort like in a match?

During a match, a player constantly alternates intensities. Most of the time he moves around walking or jogging, but the decisive moments, a pressure, a break, a defensive recovery, are usually short and very intense efforts, often repeated with little rest between them.

This implies two different capabilities:

1. **A solid aerobic base**, which allows you to recover quickly between intense efforts and maintain activity throughout the game.
2. **The ability to repeat intense efforts**, sprint, brake and sprint again without performance dropping too much.

Running long and slow trains the first, but not the second. That is why it is necessary to combine both types of work.

## The aerobic base

The aerobic base is the foundation. A player with good aerobic capacity recovers sooner between plays and arrives with more energy at the end of the game.

To build it:

- **Continuous easy running**, 25 to 40 minutes, at a conversational pace.
- **Fartlek**, continuous running with free changes of pace: for example, for 30 minutes, alternate 2 easy minutes with 1 slightly faster minute.
- **Other aerobic activities** such as cycling or swimming, useful for adding volume without as much impact.

One or two weekly sessions of this type are enough for most amateur players, especially in preseason.

## The intervals

Intervals consist of alternating periods of high intensity with periods of recovery. They are very effective in improving maximum aerobic capacity and are more similar to the actual effort of soccer.

Some proposals:

- **Long intervals:** 4 repetitions of 4 minutes at a challenging pace, with 3 minutes of easy jogging in between. It is a very studied and effective format to improve resistance.
- **Short intervals:** 2 or 3 sets of 8 to 10 repetitions of 15 seconds fast and 15 seconds easy, with 3 minutes of rest between sets.

The intervals are demanding. One session per week is sufficient at first, and never the day before a match.

## The ability to repeat sprints

This work specifically prepares for the decisive moments of the match:

- **Repeated sprints:** 2 or 3 sets of 6 sprints of 20 to 30 meters, with about 20 or 25 seconds of walking or jogging recovery between each one, and 3 or 4 minutes between sets.
- **Sprints with change of direction:** round trips of 10 or 15 meters, braking and turning.

Quality is what is important: each sprint must be done at high speed. If the speed drops a lot, the series is over.

## Small games: resistance with a ball

One of the most effective ways to work on specific resistance is to play with the ball in small spaces: 3 on 3 or 4 on 4 matches in a small space, for 3 or 4 minutes, with 2 or 3 minutes of rest between them. The intensity is very high, accelerations and braking are repeated as in the real match and, in addition, technique and decision-making are worked on.

For an amateur team that trains once or twice a week, small games allow physical preparation and football to be combined in the same exercise.

## Strength, basis of everything

Without strength, resistance cannot be fully expressed. Strong legs accelerate better, brake with more control and fatigue less. Additionally, strength reduces the risk of injury. Two weekly strength sessions, with squats, lunges, deadlifts, hamstring and core work, perfectly complement the resistance work.

## A typical weekly plan

For an amateur player who plays the game on the weekend:

| Day | Content |
|---|---|
| Monday | Rest or gentle activity (walking, quiet cycling) |
| Tuesday | Strength (30-40 min) + easy running (20 min) |
| Wednesday | Intervals or reduced games |
| Thursday | Rest |
| Friday | Light strength + short sprints and ball technique |
| Saturday | Very gentle rest or activation |
| Sunday | Party |

If you only have two days during the week, combine strength with easy running on one and intervals with some sprints on another.

## Preseason and season

### Preseason

It's time to build the foundation. For four to six weeks before the start of the competition, you can dedicate more time to aerobic running and strength training, and progressively introduce intervals and sprints.

### Season

During the season, the objective is to maintain what has been built without accumulating fatigue for the games. Less volume, well-dosed intensity and sufficient rest.

## Play again after months without doing so

The first weeks after a long break are the ones with the highest risk of injury, especially to the hamstrings in sprints. Before the first full match, give yourself three or four weeks of preparation: easy running and strength in the first two, then adding intervals and, only at the end, sprints at maximum speed. It includes specific exercises for the back of the thigh, such as the Nordic hamstring exercise in its most assisted version, which is part of proven prevention programs such as the FIFA 11+ warm-up. The first games, it is better to play half a half or a while and increase the minutes little by little.

## If you only have twenty minutes

A short and well-planned session keeps you in shape during difficult weeks:

1. **Eight minute warm-up:** gentle jogging, mobility and two progressive accelerations.
2. **Six repetitions of 30 seconds fast and 90 seconds of easy jogging.**
3. **Four minutes back to calm.**

It doesn't replace a full week of training, but it prevents you from arriving at Sunday's game without having done anything during the week.

## Signs of overload

Some signs indicate that you are training more than you can recover:

- Persistent fatigue.
- Resting heart rate higher than usual in the morning.
- Worse performance in matches despite training more.
- Muscle discomfort that does not go away.
- Difficulty sleeping.

If they appear, reduce the load for a week. More is not always better.

## Food and hydration

Stamina is also built at the table. A diet with sufficient carbohydrates, especially on days of intense training and before games, provides the necessary energy. Proteins help muscle recovery. And good hydration, especially on hot days, prevents performance from dropping in the second half.

## An example of a complete session

To be more specific, this is what a specific resistance session of about fifty minutes could be like for an amateur player:

1. **Warm-up (12 minutes):** gentle jogging, mobility, light strength and balance exercises, and two progressive accelerations.
2. **Interval block (16 minutes):** 4 repetitions of 2 minutes at a demanding pace, with 2 minutes of easy jogging between them.
3. **Recovery (4 minutes):** very easy walking and jogging.
4. **Repeated sprints (8 minutes):** 2 sets of 5 20-meter sprints, with 25 seconds of recovery between sprints and 3 minutes between sets.
5. **Return to calm (8 minutes):** gentle jogging and mobility.

If you train with the team, the interval block can be replaced by shortened 4-on-4 games of 3 minutes with 2 minutes rest.

## How to measure your progress

Measuring helps you know if the plan is working. Some simple tests that you can repeat every four to six weeks:

- **Twelve-minute continuous running test:** Run the maximum possible distance in twelve minutes on a measured track or circuit. If the distance increases, your aerobic endurance has improved.
- **Repeated Sprints:** Time six 30-meter sprints with 20 seconds of recovery. Look at the difference between the fastest and the slowest: if it is reduced, your ability to repeat efforts improves.
- **Feelings in the matches:** Do you arrive with energy at the end? Do you recover faster after intense effort? It is often the most useful indicator.

Do the tests in similar conditions, rested and well warmed up, so that the results are comparable.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I improve my stamina just by playing matches?

Playing helps, but a single weekly game is usually not enough to improve significantly, and it also increases the risk of injury if there is no preparation during the week.

### How long does it take to notice improvements?

With two or three well-planned weekly sessions, many players notice differences in four to six weeks.

### Is running on hills useful?

Yes, hills are a good way to work on strength and endurance at the same time. It is advisable to introduce them progressively, because they put a lot of strain on the calves and the Achilles tendon.

## In summary

The soccer player's resistance is intermittent: it combines a good aerobic base with the ability to repeat intense efforts. Easy running, intervals, repeated sprints, small games and strength, well combined throughout the week, are the recipe to reach the eighty minute with legs to win the match.
