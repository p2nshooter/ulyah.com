Not having time to go to the gym, not wanting to pay a membership fee or simply preferring the comfort of home are very common reasons for not doing strength exercise. The good news is that your own body weight is enough to train fully, especially when you are starting out or want to stay in shape.

This article suggests a full-body routine that can be done in a space of two metres by two, without any equipment, in about thirty minutes. It includes three levels of difficulty so that anyone can adapt it and keep progressing for months.

## Before you start

- **Space:** find a clear area, with no furniture with sharp corners nearby. A mat or a rug helps for the floor exercises.
- **Footwear:** you can train barefoot or in flat-soled trainers. Avoid socks on a smooth floor, which slip.
- **Health:** if you have an injury or an illness, or have not exercised for a long time, consult a professional before you start.

## The warm-up (5 minutes)

1. **Marching on the spot** with high knees, 1 minute.
2. **Arm circles** forwards and backwards, 30 seconds.
3. **Hip circles**, 30 seconds.
4. **Gentle squats** without going all the way down, 10 repetitions.
5. **Alternating reverse lunges** without much depth, 10 repetitions.
6. **Cat-camel** on all fours, alternating a rounded and an arched back, 10 repetitions.
7. **Jumping jacks or their no-jump version**, 1 minute.

## The main routine

The routine is organised as a circuit of six exercises. Do each exercise for the number of repetitions shown and move on to the next one with a short rest of 15 to 30 seconds. At the end of the circuit, rest for one to two minutes and repeat. Do two to four rounds depending on your level.

### 1. Squat

- **Level 1:** squat to a chair, sitting down and standing up. 10 to 12 repetitions.
- **Level 2:** full squat without support. 12 to 15 repetitions.
- **Level 3:** squat with a three-second pause at the bottom, or Bulgarian split squat with your back foot resting on a sofa. 10 to 12 repetitions per leg.

### 2. Press-ups

- **Level 1:** press-ups with your hands against a wall or a kitchen worktop. 10 to 12 repetitions.
- **Level 2:** press-ups on your knees or with your hands on the seat of a stable chair. 8 to 12 repetitions.
- **Level 3:** full press-ups, lowering slowly over three seconds. 8 to 15 repetitions.

### 3. Glute bridge

- **Level 1:** bridge with both feet on the floor, lying on your back. 12 to 15 repetitions.
- **Level 2:** bridge with a two-second pause at the top. 12 to 15 repetitions.
- **Level 3:** single-leg bridge. 8 to 12 repetitions per leg.

### 4. Rows without equipment

Pulling is the hardest thing to train without equipment, but there are options:

- **Level 1:** "towel row": hold a towel by its ends, stand on the middle with one foot or loop it around a sturdy door frame, and pull while leaning back, keeping the tension. You can also do "supermans" lying face down, lifting your arms and legs. 12 to 15 repetitions.
- **Level 2:** inverted row under a sturdy table: lie underneath, hold the edge and pull your chest towards the table with your knees bent. First check that the table will take your weight without moving. 6 to 10 repetitions.
- **Level 3:** inverted row with your legs straight. 8 to 12 repetitions.

### 5. Lunges

- **Level 1:** static lunge, with one foot in front and the other behind, going up and down without moving them. You can steady yourself against a wall. 8 to 10 per leg.
- **Level 2:** alternating reverse lunge. 10 to 12 per leg.
- **Level 3:** reverse lunge with a knee drive as you come up, or a gentle jumping lunge if your knees tolerate it. 10 per leg.

### 6. Trunk

- **Level 1:** plank on your knees, 20 to 30 seconds, and dead bug lying on your back, moving the opposite arm and leg, 8 per side.
- **Level 2:** full plank, 30 to 45 seconds, and side plank on your knees, 20 seconds per side.
- **Level 3:** plank with alternating shoulder taps, 10 per side, and full side plank, 30 seconds per side.

## A conditioning finisher (optional, 5 minutes)

If you want to add a cardiovascular element, finish with a five-minute block alternating 30 seconds of work and 30 of rest:

- Mountain climbers (knees to chest in a plank position), in a slow version for level 1.
- Fast squats.
- Jumping jacks or quick side steps.

If you live in a flat, choose the no-jump versions out of consideration for your neighbours and your knees.

## The cool-down (3 minutes)

A few minutes of calm breathing and gentle stretches for the quadriceps, hamstrings, glutes, chest and back help to round off the session.

## How many times a week

- **Beginners:** two sessions a week, on non-consecutive days.
- **Intermediate level:** three sessions a week.

On the days in between you can walk, cycle or do another gentle aerobic activity.

## How to progress without equipment

With no weights to add, progress comes in other ways:

1. **More repetitions**, up to the top of the range.
2. **More rounds of the circuit.**
3. **Less rest** between exercises.
4. **More time under tension:** slow lowering over three or four seconds.
5. **Pauses** at the hardest part of the movement.
6. **Single-leg or single-arm variations**, which double the load on each side.
7. **Moving up a level** when you complete every set with good technique.

A practical rule: when you can do the maximum number of repetitions of an exercise in every round with good technique, move on to the next level's variation.

## An eight-week plan

To make the progression clear, you can follow this outline with two or three sessions a week:

| Weeks | Rounds of the circuit | Rest between exercises | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | 2 | 30 seconds | Learn the technique at the most comfortable level |
| 3–4 | 3 | 30 seconds | Complete every repetition in the range |
| 5–6 | 3 | 20 seconds | Move up a level in one or two exercises |
| 7–8 | 3–4 | 20 seconds | Consolidate the new level and add slow lowering |

Note down in a notebook or on your phone which variation you do of each exercise and how many repetitions you complete. Seeing your progress week by week is one of the best motivators.

## The fifteen-minute version

On days when time is short, a short session is much better than none. One option:

1. Two minutes of warm-up: marching on the spot, arm circles and ten gentle squats.
2. Three rounds of this mini-circuit, with 20 seconds' rest between exercises: squat, press-ups, glute bridge and plank.
3. One minute of breathing and gentle stretching.

It is a cut-down version, but it keeps the habit going and still works the large muscle groups. Many people find that once they start, they end up doing a bit more.

## Training at home with children or little space

If you share your home with children, turning the session into a game can work: let them count the repetitions, do their own version of the exercises or join in with the conditioning part. In very small spaces, prioritise exercises done on the spot, such as squats, static lunges, press-ups, bridges and planks, and save the ones that need you to move around for another time.

## Training without disturbing the neighbours

In a flat, jumps and thuds on the floor can be heard more than you might think. If you have neighbours below, replace the high-impact exercises with quiet versions that keep the intensity:

| Instead of… | Try… |
|---|---|
| Jumping jacks | quick side steps while opening and closing your arms |
| Jump squats | a squat followed by an explosive calf raise |
| Full burpees | no-jump burpees, walking your feet back and forward |
| Running on the spot | fast marching with high knees |

A thick mat muffles the noise and protects your knees in lunges and planks. And if you train first thing in the morning or at night, save the noisiest exercises for another time.

## Common mistakes

- **Going too fast.** Speed reduces control and effectiveness. Better slow and well done.
- **Losing alignment in press-ups and planks**, letting your hips sag.
- **Letting your knees cave inwards** in squats and lunges.
- **Forgetting about pulling.** Many home programmes focus on pushing and legs and neglect the back.
- **Trying to do everything on day one.** Excessive soreness is discouraging.

## When to add equipment

If after a few months you want to keep progressing, some simple equipment multiplies your options: a resistance band, a set of adjustable dumbbells or a pull-up bar for the door frame. With that you can train at home for years.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it enough to build real strength?

For beginners and for staying in shape, yes. As you advance, you will need harder variations or some equipment to keep progressing.

### Can I do it every day?

It is not ideal. Muscles need rest between sessions. If you want to move every day, alternate the routine with walking or mobility work.

### How long will it take to notice changes?

With two or three sessions a week, most people find that the exercises feel easier within three or four weeks.

## In short

Six exercises, thirty minutes and no equipment at all. With consistency and progression, your living room can become a perfectly good place to build strength, improve your fitness and feel better day to day.
