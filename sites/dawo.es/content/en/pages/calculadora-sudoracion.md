There is no amount of water that suits everyone: sweating varies a lot with the heat, the intensity, your clothing and the person. The simplest way to find out yours is the weighing test explained in our guide to [hydration for athletes](/articulos/hidratacion-para-deportistas/), and this calculator does the sums.

## The test

1. Weigh yourself before exercise, with no or minimal clothing, after going to the toilet.
2. Train, for example for an hour, and note what you drink.
3. Weigh yourself afterwards, after drying off and without the wet clothes.
4. **Sweat = weight lost + what you drank − urine, if you went to the toilet during exercise.**

For example, if you lose half a kilo and drank half a litre, you sweated about a litre in that hour. It is the example the calculator opens with.

## How to read the result

| Weight lost | What it means |
|---|---|
| less than 2 % of body weight | what you drank was about right for that session |
| 2 % or more | drink a little more in similar conditions |
| you gained weight | you drank more than you sweated |

It has been found that dehydration of around 2 % of body weight can start to affect performance in endurance efforts, especially in the heat. Drinking far more than you lose in very long efforts is not good either: it can dilute the sodium in your blood (hyponatraemia). That is why the guide is thirst and what you know of your sweat rate, not a fixed amount.

## Repeat the test

Do the test in summer and in winter, on an easy run and in a hard session. With two or three results you will know how much to drink per hour in each situation and whether a drink with salts suits you in long efforts. Our guide to [running in the heat](/articulos/correr-con-calor/) explains how to adapt timing and pace in summer.
