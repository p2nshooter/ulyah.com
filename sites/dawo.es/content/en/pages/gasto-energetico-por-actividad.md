Does an hour of padel use more than an hour on the bike? How much energy does a strength session take? This calculator compares activities with a simple method widely used in research: METs.

## What a MET is

One MET is the energy the body uses at rest, sitting down. An activity of 6 METs uses six times as much. With your weight, the sum is straightforward:

**Energy (kcal) ≈ MET × weight (kg) × hours**

The MET values come from the Compendium of Physical Activities, a reference collection of measurements in adults.

## Reference values

| Activity | MET | kcal in 30 min at 70 kg |
|---|---|---|
| Brisk walking (about 5.5 km/h) | 4.3 | about 150 |
| Easy running (8 km/h) | 8.3 | about 290 |
| Running (about 10 km/h) | 9.8 | about 340 |
| Cycling in town or for leisure (16–19 km/h) | 6.8 | about 240 |
| Front crawl at a moderate pace | 5.8 | about 200 |
| Padel or doubles tennis | 6.0 | about 210 |
| Casual football | 7.0 | about 245 |
| Weight training, moderate effort | 3.5 | about 120 |

At 70 kg, half an hour of easy running uses about 290 kcal: it is the example the calculator opens with.

## What it is for and what it is not for

It is useful for comparing activities and for understanding that exercise uses less energy than people usually think: half an hour of running is roughly a sandwich. It is no good for counting calories precisely: technique, terrain, intensity and fitness change the real figure, sometimes by a fifth or more, and watches and wristbands estimate in a similar way.

For health, what counts most is moving regularly: the World Health Organization's recommendations call for 150 to 300 minutes a week of moderate activity, or 75 to 150 of vigorous activity, plus strength on two days a week.
