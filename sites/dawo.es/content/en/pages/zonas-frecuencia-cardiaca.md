Training by heart rate helps make easy days genuinely easy and hard days genuinely hard. Heart-rate zones divide effort into five bands, from recovery to maximum. This calculator works them out from your maximum heart rate and, if you know it, your resting heart rate.

## Maximum heart rate

It is best to measure it: with a chest-strap monitor, at the end of an exercise test or of some very demanding hill repetitions, as long as you are healthy and used to training. If you do not know it, the calculator estimates it with Tanaka's formula, **208 − 0.7 × age**, which fits better than the classic 220 − age. Bear in mind that any formula can be 10 or 15 beats out either way.

## With or without a resting heart rate

- **Without a resting heart rate**, each zone is a percentage of the maximum.
- **With a resting heart rate** (take it in bed on waking, over several days), the calculator uses the heart-rate reserve, the Karvonen method: **resting + (maximum − resting) × percentage**. It fits each person better.

## The five zones

| Zone | % of reserve or of maximum | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Very easy | 50–60 % | recovery, warm-up, walking |
| 2 · Easy | 60–70 % | conversational running, base endurance |
| 3 · Moderate | 70–80 % | steady pace, faster breathing |
| 4 · Hard | 80–90 % | intervals, race pace over 5 to 10 km |
| 5 · Maximum | 90–100 % | short efforts, only a few minutes in total |

For a 40-year-old with no resting data, the estimated maximum is 180 beats, and zone 2 lies between 108 and 126. These are the values the calculator opens with.

## How to spread your training

Most of the week, around eight sessions in ten, is best done in zones 1 and 2: that is where endurance is built with little risk of injury. Zones 4 and 5 are kept for one or two quality sessions. ## Precautions

Heart rate rises with heat, dehydration, tiredness, coffee and nerves, so the same pace can give different readings on different days. If you have a heart condition or take medicines that change your pulse, such as beta blockers, ask your doctor before training by heart rate and do not do maximum tests without supervision.
