A budget is a plan for where your money goes before the month starts. The planner above takes your take-home pay, the costs you list and the type of each one, and shows what is left and how your plan compares with a common rule of thumb. A second part works out monthly amounts for sinking funds.

## How to fill it in

1. **Take-home pay.** Use what reaches your account after tax and deductions, not your salary before tax. If your income varies, use a cautious figure: a low month from the last year.
2. **Costs.** List what you pay each month. Turn yearly bills into monthly amounts by dividing by 12.
3. **Needs, wants or saving.** Needs are what you must pay to live and work: housing, energy, food, transport, minimum debt payments. Wants are the rest of your spending. Savings include an emergency fund and sinking funds.

## The 50/30/20 reference

The planner shows the 50/30/20 split — half of take-home pay for needs, 30 % for wants, 20 % for savings — next to your own figures. It is a rule of thumb, and the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau notes that not everyone can follow it. Where rents are high or income is low, needs alone can take more than half. The point is to see where your money goes, not to pass a test.

| Take-home pay | Needs (50 %) | Wants (30 %) | Savings (20 %) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,500 | 750 | 450 | 300 |
| 2,500 | 1,250 | 750 | 500 |
| 4,000 | 2,000 | 1,200 | 800 |

## Sinking funds

A sinking fund is money you put aside each month for a cost you know is coming: car insurance, a holiday, school uniforms, a new phone. Divide what you need by the months until it is due, minus anything already set aside. Six hundred due in ten months is sixty a month — much easier than six hundred at once.

## When the numbers do not add up

If costs are higher than income, start with the wants, then look at the biggest needs one by one: energy, phone and insurance are often cheaper after a comparison. If you are behind on bills or debts, contact the lender early and talk to a free, independent debt-advice service.
