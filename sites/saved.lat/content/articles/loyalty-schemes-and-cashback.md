Almost every shop, supermarket, café and app now offers some kind of reward: points on a loyalty card, a percentage back through a cashback site, a voucher for your next visit, an alert when the price of something you want drops. Used well, these tools can take a real slice off household spending. Used carelessly, they do the opposite: they encourage extra purchases, tie you to one shop even when another is cheaper and collect a great deal of information about your habits.

This guide explains how the main types of reward and price tool work, what you give in exchange for them, how to use them so that they genuinely save money and the warning signs that a scheme is costing you more than it gives back.

## Why shops offer rewards

Rewards are a business tool. Shops offer them because they expect to gain more than they give away, usually through:

- **loyalty:** you return to the same shop rather than comparing prices elsewhere;
- **bigger baskets:** offers that encourage you to buy more, or to buy items you would not otherwise choose;
- **data:** detailed information about what you buy, when and how often, used for marketing and pricing.

None of this makes rewards bad. It simply means that the best way to benefit is to stay in control: use the rewards on what you were going to buy anyway, at a price that is already good.

## Loyalty cards and points schemes

### How they work

You register, usually with your name and email, and show a card or app at the checkout. You collect points or receive personal offers, which you can turn into money off, vouchers or other rewards.

### Using them well

- **Join the schemes of shops you already use regularly.** Collecting points at a shop you rarely visit adds little.
- **Check the value of a point.** Work out how much you need to spend to earn a reward worth a given amount. This tells you the real percentage you get back.
- **Use personalised offers selectively.** They are often based on your past purchases. Take the ones for items you buy anyway; ignore the rest.
- **Watch expiry dates.** Points and vouchers often expire. A reminder in your calendar helps.
- **Compare member prices with other shops.** A "member price" is not automatically the lowest price available. Our guide to [unit pricing](/guides/unit-pricing-at-the-supermarket/) helps you compare.

### Watch out for

- prices that are only good **with** the card, while non-members pay noticeably more;
- offers designed to make you spend a certain amount to unlock a reward;
- schemes that encourage you to buy everything in one place even when other shops are cheaper for some items.

## Cashback sites and apps

### How they work

Cashback sites and apps earn a commission from shops when you click through and buy. They pass part of that commission back to you, usually after a waiting period.

### Using them well

- **Start from a price you have already checked.** Find the best price first, then see whether cashback is available on it.
- **Read the conditions.** Cashback may not apply to some products, to purchases with vouchers or to returned items.
- **Expect delays.** Cashback is often "pending" for weeks while the shop confirms that the item was not returned.
- **Track your claims.** Keep a note of purchases and expected cashback, and follow up if it does not arrive.
- **Withdraw regularly.** Do not let a large balance sit with a cashback site for a long time.

### Watch out for

- cashback that makes an **overpriced item** look like a bargain;
- browser extensions or apps that ask for **broad access to your data**;
- sites that promise unusually high rates without a clear explanation.

## Credit card rewards

Some credit cards offer points, air miles or cashback. They can be useful **only** if you pay the full balance every month. Interest charges on an unpaid balance quickly exceed any reward. Our guide to [understanding interest on debt](/guides/understanding-interest-on-debt/) explains why. Never spend more, or borrow, to earn rewards.

## Vouchers and discount codes

- **Search for a code before you pay**, but do not let the search delay a purchase you have already judged worth making.
- **Use codes from reliable sources**: the shop's own newsletter, official apps or well-known voucher sites.
- **Unsubscribe from newsletters** that only tempt you into extra purchases. You can always sign up again when you need something.
- **Beware of "spend more to save" codes**, such as a discount on orders above a certain amount, if it means adding items you do not need.

## Price trackers and alerts

Price trackers follow the price of a product over time and send an alert when it drops.

### Using them well

- **Track items you have already decided to buy**, especially larger purchases.
- **Look at the price history.** It shows whether a "sale" price is really low or simply back to normal after a temporary increase.
- **Set a target price** you are happy to pay, and buy when it is reached.
- **Combine tracking with seasonal timing.** Our guide to [when to buy](/guides/when-to-buy-seasonal-sales/) explains which months tend to offer the best prices for different items.

### Watch out for

- tracking so many products that alerts turn into **temptations**;
- waiting too long for the perfect price on something you genuinely need now.

## Your data: the hidden price

Loyalty schemes, cashback sites and shopping apps collect information about you. Before joining:

- **read the privacy policy**, at least the parts about how your data is used and shared;
- **use a separate email address** for shopping accounts, to keep your main inbox clear and limit spam;
- **turn off unnecessary permissions** in apps, such as location tracking or contacts;
- **use strong, unique passwords**, and enable two-step verification where available;
- **close accounts you no longer use**.

Be careful with messages that claim to be from a loyalty scheme and ask you to log in or confirm details. Phishing messages often copy well-known brands. Our guide to [avoiding online shopping scams](/guides/avoiding-online-shopping-scams/) explains how to spot them.

## A simple system that keeps you in control

1. **Choose a few schemes** at the shops you use most, and ignore the rest.
2. **Keep cards in one app or wallet**, so you are not tempted to join new schemes at every till.
3. **Check prices before rewards**: find the best price, then add rewards on top.
4. **Set a reminder** once a month to use points and vouchers before they expire.
5. **Track cashback** in a simple list.
6. **Review once a year**: which schemes actually saved you money, and which just filled your inbox?

## Household strategies

Rewards often work better when the household coordinates:

- **Share a single loyalty account** at the main supermarket, where the scheme allows it, so that everyone's purchases count towards the same rewards rather than being split across several accounts that never reach a useful total.
- **Agree on the main shops.** If the household uses one supermarket for the weekly shop and another for a few cheaper items, register only with those two.
- **Teach children how rewards work.** Many apps and games use points, streaks and badges in the same way shops do. Explaining why a shop gives "free" points is a useful money lesson.
- **Keep a running total.** Write down the value of rewards you actually redeem in a year. It gives you an honest picture of what the schemes are worth, and makes it easier to drop those that are not.
- **Value your time.** Chasing small rewards across many apps, codes and sites takes time and attention. If a scheme saves very little for a lot of effort, it is fine to let it go.

## Is it worth the effort?

For many households, a couple of well-chosen loyalty schemes and occasional cashback on larger purchases give a modest but real saving. The bigger wins usually come from basic habits: planning meals, comparing unit prices, buying second-hand and cutting unused subscriptions. Our guides to [the subscription audit](/guides/subscription-audit/) and [breaking the impulse buying habit](/guides/impulse-buying-habits/) cover two of the most effective.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do loyalty cards really save money?

They can, on purchases you would make anyway at that shop. They stop saving money when they keep you away from cheaper shops or tempt you into extra purchases.

### Is cashback safe?

Established cashback sites are widely used, but read the terms, protect your account and treat cashback as a bonus, not a reason to buy.

### Should I stack rewards, vouchers and cashback?

Where the rules allow, combining them can increase the saving, but check the conditions: some vouchers cancel cashback.

You will find more ways to get real value for your money in the [Smart Shopping](/topics/smart-shopping/) section.

## The bottom line

Loyalty cards, cashback, vouchers and price trackers exist because shops expect to gain from them, through your loyalty, bigger baskets and your data. You benefit by staying in control: join only the schemes of shops you already use, check the real value of points, start from the best price before adding rewards, read the conditions, protect your data and review once a year which schemes actually saved money. Never spend more, or borrow, just to earn a reward.
