Every hot shower, every sink of washing-up and every warm wash in the machine uses energy to heat water. In many homes, heating water is one of the largest items on the energy bill after space heating, and unlike heating it does not stop in summer. Yet most households never think about it, because hot water simply arrives when the tap is turned. Small changes in habits, a little insulation and the right settings on the water heater can bring that cost down noticeably without anyone having to put up with cold showers.

This guide explains how water is heated in different types of home, where the biggest savings usually are, how to insulate tanks and pipes, how to use timers and thermostats safely and how to judge whether a new shower head or appliance is worth buying.

## How your home heats water

Before changing anything, find out which system you have. The main types are:

| System | How it works | Where savings come from |
|---|---|---|
| Combination boiler | heats water instantly when you open a hot tap; no tank | using less hot water; sensible boiler settings |
| Boiler with a hot water cylinder | heats a tank of water stored for later use | insulation, timers, thermostat settings, using less |
| Electric immersion heater | an electric element heats water in a cylinder | timers, insulation, using cheaper times if your tariff allows |
| Electric shower | heats water as it passes through the shower unit | shorter showers |
| Instantaneous water heaters | gas or electric units that heat water on demand | using less hot water |
| Heat pump or solar water heating | uses renewable heat to warm a tank | correct settings and timing |

If you are not sure, look at the boiler or tank, check the manual, or ask the person who services your heating.

## The biggest saving: using less hot water

### Showers

- **Shorten your shower.** Even a minute or two less each day adds up over a year for each person in the household. A timer or a favourite song helps.
- **Choose a shower over a bath** most of the time; a deep bath usually uses more hot water than a short shower.
- **Turn the water off** while you shampoo or soap up, if your shower makes it easy.

### Washing up and the kitchen

- **Wash up in a bowl** rather than under a running hot tap.
- **Run the dishwasher only when full**, and use an eco programme if it has one.
- **Fill the kettle only with what you need**, as our guide to [energy-efficient cooking](/guides/energy-efficient-cooking/) explains.

### Laundry

Most of the energy a washing machine uses goes into heating water. Washing at lower temperatures, with full loads, makes a big difference. Our guide to [doing laundry for less](/guides/laundry-costs/) explains which items still need a hot wash.

### Taps

- **Fix dripping hot taps.** A drip may look small, but it wastes both water and the energy used to heat it. Our guide to [fixing a dripping tap](/guides/fix-a-dripping-tap/) shows how.
- **Use the cold tap** when you do not need hot water, for example for rinsing or brushing teeth. With some systems, turning the mixer to the hot side starts the boiler even for a few seconds.

## Shower heads and flow

**Water-saving shower heads** mix air into the water or restrict the flow, so they use less water while still feeling strong. Using less hot water means heating less. But check compatibility before buying:

- **Electric showers** usually need their own matching shower head; fitting the wrong type may be unsafe or affect the warranty.
- **Low-pressure systems**, common in some older homes, may feel weak with a low-flow head.
- **Combi boilers** need a minimum flow to fire; very low-flow heads may not work well with some models.

The manufacturer of your boiler or shower can tell you what is suitable. Some water companies offer water-saving devices at low cost or free; it is worth checking.

## Tanks and pipes: keep the heat in

If you have a hot water cylinder:

- **Insulate the cylinder.** Many modern tanks have factory-fitted foam insulation. Older ones may need a jacket. A well-insulated tank keeps water hot for much longer.
- **Insulate the pipes**, especially the first part of the pipe leaving the tank and any pipes running through cold spaces such as lofts or under floors. Foam pipe lagging is cheap and easy to fit.
- **Check for leaks** around the tank and valves.

Insulating pipes also helps protect them from freezing in cold weather.

## Timers and thermostats

### Timers

If your water is heated in a tank, a **timer** lets you heat it only when it will be used, for example before morning showers and evening washing-up, rather than all day. If you have a time-of-use electricity tariff, you may be able to heat water with an immersion heater at the cheaper times. Our guide to [reading your energy bill](/guides/reading-your-energy-bill/) explains how tariffs work.

Avoid leaving an immersion heater switched on permanently unless it has its own thermostat and timer and is designed to be used that way.

### Thermostats and safety

It is tempting to turn the hot water thermostat right down to save energy. Be careful: **water stored at too low a temperature can allow bacteria such as Legionella to grow**. Health and safety guidance in many countries recommends storing hot water at a temperature high enough to control these bacteria, and some systems run a periodic high-temperature cycle for this purpose. Follow the manufacturer's instructions and your local guidance, and ask a qualified heating engineer if you are unsure.

At the tap, very hot water can scald, especially children and older people. Some homes have **thermostatic mixing valves** that blend hot and cold water to a safe temperature at baths and basins.

For combi boilers, the hot water temperature setting affects comfort and efficiency. Your heating engineer can advise on a sensible setting for your model.

## Servicing and maintenance

- **Have your boiler serviced** as recommended by the manufacturer. A well-maintained boiler works more efficiently and more safely.
- **Descale** showers, taps and kettles in hard-water areas; limescale reduces efficiency.
- **Bleed and balance** the heating system as part of regular maintenance; our guide to [staying warm for less](/guides/lower-heating-bills-in-winter/) explains how this affects heating costs.

Gas appliances must be installed and repaired only by qualified, registered engineers. Never attempt gas work yourself.

## Should you replace your system?

Replacing a working boiler or water heater purely to save energy is a big expense and rarely pays back quickly. It makes more sense to consider a more efficient system when:

- the current one is old, unreliable or expensive to repair;
- you are renovating anyway;
- grants or support schemes are available in your area for efficient heating or renewable systems.

Get several quotes, ask about running costs and maintenance, and check what support exists before deciding. Our guide to [cost per use](/guides/cost-per-use/) can help you think about the long-term value of a big purchase.

## A quick hot-water checklist

1. Find out what type of system you have.
2. Shorten showers and choose them over baths most days.
3. Wash up in a bowl and run full dishwasher and washing machine loads.
4. Fix dripping hot taps.
5. Insulate the cylinder and the pipes.
6. Set a timer so stored water is heated only when needed.
7. Keep stored water at a safe temperature, following guidance.
8. Check whether a water-saving shower head is compatible with your system.
9. Service the boiler regularly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it cheaper to leave the hot water on all the time?

For a cylinder, it is usually more efficient to heat water when you need it with a timer, as long as the tank is well insulated. For combi boilers, water is heated only when you open a tap, so there is nothing to leave on.

### Do short showers really make a difference?

Yes. Hot water used every day by every member of the household adds up over a year, so small reductions in shower time have a noticeable effect.

### Can I fit a water-saving shower head myself?

Usually yes, on a standard mixer shower, by unscrewing the old head from the hose. For electric showers or anything that needs wiring or plumbing work, use a qualified professional.

## The bottom line

Heating water is a year-round cost, and the most effective way to cut it is simply to use less hot water: shorter showers, washing up in a bowl, full loads in the dishwasher and washing machine, and fixing dripping hot taps. If your water is stored in a tank, insulate the cylinder and pipes and use a timer, but keep stored water hot enough to control bacteria, following your local guidance. Check compatibility before fitting a water-saving shower head, keep the boiler serviced, and only replace a working system when it is old, unreliable or when support makes the upgrade worthwhile.
