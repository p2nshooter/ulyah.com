Keeping children entertained can be expensive. Soft play centres, cinema trips, theme parks, holiday clubs and organised activities add up quickly, especially during school holidays. Yet some of the experiences children remember most fondly cost very little: building a den, a picnic in the park, a trip to the library, baking together or a muddy walk in the woods.

This guide collects practical ideas for free and low-cost activities, at home and further afield, for different ages and seasons. The goal is not to avoid paid outings altogether, but to have plenty of good options when money is tight or when you simply want to spend time together.

## Why low-cost activities work so well

- **Children value time and attention** more than expensive venues.
- **Open-ended play** — with simple materials, nature and imagination — supports creativity, problem-solving and independence.
- **Outdoor activities** are good for physical health and mood.
- **Low-cost days out** mean you can do more of them.
- **Planning together** gives children a sense of ownership and something to look forward to.

## At home

### Creative play

- **Junk modelling.** Save cardboard boxes, tubes, egg cartons and bottle tops for building rockets, castles, robots and marble runs.
- **Drawing and painting.** Large sheets of paper (or the back of old wallpaper) taped to the floor make for ambitious artwork.
- **Home-made play dough.** Flour, salt, water, a little oil and food colouring make an inexpensive dough; follow a trusted recipe and supervise young children, as salt dough should not be eaten.
- **Collage.** Old magazines, catalogues, wrapping paper and leaves can be cut and glued into pictures.
- **Puppet shows.** Make puppets from socks or paper bags, and use a table turned on its side as a stage.

### Imaginative play

- **Den building.** Blankets, chairs, cushions and clothes pegs make indoor dens; branches and tarps work outdoors.
- **Dressing up.** Old clothes, scarves, hats and jewellery from charity shops make a great dressing-up box.
- **Shops and cafés.** Use empty packaging, play money and a notepad for taking orders.
- **Treasure hunts.** Hide objects with clues to read or pictures to follow.

### Cooking and baking

- **Bake simple recipes** such as biscuits, bread or pizza, which teach measuring, following instructions and patience.
- **Make a picnic** together, even if it is eaten on the living room floor.
- **Grow cress or herbs** on a windowsill (see our windowsill herb guide).

### Games and learning

- **Board games and card games**, many available cheaply second-hand or borrowed from libraries.
- **Science experiments** with household items: vinegar and bicarbonate of soda volcanoes, floating and sinking tests, growing crystals from salt or sugar solutions.
- **Reading together**, with books from the library.
- **Music and dancing**: home-made shakers from rice in sealed containers, or a living room disco.
- **Family film night** with home-made popcorn instead of a cinema trip.

## Outdoors

### Parks and playgrounds

- **Explore different playgrounds** in your area or neighbouring towns.
- **Take a ball, frisbee or kite** to the park.
- **Organise races, obstacle courses and relay games.**
- **Feed ducks responsibly** with suitable foods such as oats or defrosted frozen peas rather than bread, if local guidance allows.

### Nature activities

- **Nature walks and scavenger hunts.** Make a list of things to find: a feather, a pine cone, a heart-shaped stone, five different leaves.
- **Bug hunts.** Look under logs and stones (and gently replace them).
- **Rock pooling** at the seaside, following safety advice about tides and returning creatures to where they were found.
- **Puddle jumping and mud play** with waterproofs and wellies.
- **Stargazing** on clear nights, away from bright lights.
- **Bird watching** from a window or garden; many organisations run free bird-counting events.
- **Tree climbing** on suitable, safe trees under supervision.
- **Blackberry and apple picking** where foraging is permitted and safe, making sure everyone knows what is safe to eat.

### Walks and adventures

- **Woodland walks and nature trails**, many with free activity sheets.
- **Geocaching**, a treasure-hunting game using a phone or GPS device to find hidden containers.
- **Bike rides** on traffic-free paths.
- **A "yes day" walk** where children choose which direction to go at each junction (within safe limits).
- **Beach days** with buckets, spades and a picnic.

### Gardening

- **Plant seeds** in pots or a small patch.
- **Build a bug hotel** from sticks, pine cones and bamboo canes.
- **Make a mini pond** in a washing-up bowl to attract wildlife, ensuring it is safe for young children.

## In the community

Many communities offer free or low-cost activities, especially during school holidays.

- **Libraries.** As well as books, many libraries run free storytimes, rhyme sessions, craft activities, reading challenges, coding clubs and homework help.
- **Museums and galleries.** Many offer free entry, at least to permanent collections, and run free family activity days and trails.
- **Community centres** often host low-cost toddler groups, holiday clubs and sports sessions.
- **Free festivals and events.** Look out for local fairs, parades, open days, outdoor concerts and cultural celebrations.
- **Sports taster sessions.** Clubs and leisure centres sometimes offer free trials, and some areas run free or subsidised holiday sports programmes.
- **Places of worship and charities** often run family events and holiday activities open to everyone.
- **Farms and allotments** sometimes have open days.

Check local council websites, library noticeboards, community social media groups and school newsletters for upcoming events.

## Activities by season

### Spring

- Plant seeds and watch them grow.
- Look for frogspawn and spring flowers.
- Go on a bug hunt.
- Fly kites on windy days.

### Summer

- Picnics in the park.
- Paddling pools and water play in the garden.
- Beach and riverside trips (following water safety advice).
- Outdoor cinema or concerts in the park, if free.
- Library summer reading challenges.

### Autumn

- Collect conkers, leaves and pine cones for crafts.
- Go blackberry picking where safe and allowed.
- Jump in leaf piles.
- Make bird feeders for winter.

### Winter

- Build snowmen and have snowball fights, if it snows.
- Make paper snowflakes and decorations.
- Visit free light displays and festive markets.
- Have board game afternoons and indoor dens.

## Activities by age

### Toddlers and preschoolers

- Sensory play with water, sand, rice or pasta (supervised).
- Library rhyme times.
- Playgrounds and toddler groups.
- Simple baking and messy painting.

### Primary-age children

- Den building, treasure hunts and scavenger hunts.
- Bike rides and nature trails.
- Science experiments and crafts.
- Museum trails and library challenges.

### Older children and teenagers

- Geocaching and longer hikes.
- Learning new skills through free online tutorials: drawing, coding, cooking or music.
- Volunteering together for local causes.
- Planning and cooking a family meal.
- Photography walks with a phone camera.

Involve older children in choosing and planning activities; they are more likely to enjoy them.

## Planning a budget day out

When you do go further afield:

- **Take a packed lunch, snacks and drinks.** Food and drink at attractions is often expensive.
- **Look for discounts**: family tickets, off-peak prices, online booking discounts and annual passes if you will visit several times.
- **Check free days.** Some attractions have free or reduced-price days.
- **Use travel discounts**: family railcards, children's fares and group tickets can reduce transport costs.
- **Avoid the gift shop**, or set a small budget in advance.
- **Bring reusable water bottles.**
- **Check membership schemes** for heritage sites, zoos or nature reserves; for regular visitors they can save money.

## Holiday planning on a budget

School holidays can be the most expensive time for families. A simple plan helps:

- **Make a list of ideas** with your children before the holidays begin.
- **Mix free and paid activities** across the week.
- **Swap childcare and outings with friends**, taking turns to look after each other's children.
- **Look for holiday programmes** run by councils, schools and charities; some are free or subsidised for families on low incomes.
- **Plan quiet days** at home too; children benefit from downtime.

## Rainy-day rescue list

Bad weather is when entertainment costs creep up, because indoor attractions feel like the only option. Keep a list of rainy-day ideas on the fridge so you are not caught out:

- **Indoor obstacle course** using cushions, chairs and blankets.
- **Build a cardboard city** for toy cars or figures.
- **Indoor camping**, with a tent or den, torches and a picnic tea.
- **Bake and decorate** biscuits or buns.
- **Make a family newspaper or comic**, with interviews, drawings and stories.
- **Put on a show** — a play, talent show or puppet performance — and invite relatives by video call.
- **Sort and photograph** old toys to sell or donate, which can even raise a little money.
- **Visit a free museum, library or gallery.**
- **Puddle walk** in waterproofs, followed by hot chocolate at home.

## The power of routine treats

Small, regular traditions often matter more to children than big, occasional outings. A Friday film night, a Saturday morning pancake breakfast, a monthly library visit or a Sunday walk to the same favourite spot gives children something to look forward to and costs very little. Over the years, these rituals become some of the happiest family memories.

## Keeping it safe

- **Supervise water play** closely, and follow local safety advice at beaches, rivers and lakes.
- **Use sun protection** in summer and warm, waterproof clothing in winter.
- **Teach children what not to touch or eat** in nature.
- **Check activities are suitable** for your children's ages and abilities.
- **Take a basic first-aid kit** on outings.

## The bottom line

The best activities for children are often the simplest: time together, outdoor adventures, creativity and play. With a supply of cardboard, a library card, a local park and a packed lunch, you can fill weekends and holidays with memorable experiences that cost very little. Keep a list of ideas, check what your community offers, and save paid outings for special occasions — your budget and your children will both benefit.
