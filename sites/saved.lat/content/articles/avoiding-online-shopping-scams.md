Shopping online is convenient and often cheaper than buying in store, but it has also become one of the most common targets for fraud. Fake shops advertise designer goods at impossible prices, phishing messages pretend to be from well-known retailers or delivery companies, and marketplace sellers take payment for items that never existed. Scams are increasingly polished and can fool careful, experienced shoppers.

The good news is that most scams follow recognisable patterns, and a few habits greatly reduce your risk. This guide explains the common tricks, how to check a shop before buying, which payment methods protect you, and what to do if something goes wrong.

## Common online shopping scams

### Fake online shops

Fraudsters create websites that look like genuine shops, often selling popular products — trainers, designer bags, electronics, cosmetics or furniture — at large discounts. The goods never arrive, or cheap counterfeits arrive instead. Fake shops are often promoted through social media adverts and may copy the branding of real retailers.

### Phishing messages

Phishing emails, text messages and social media messages pretend to be from retailers, banks, delivery firms or payment services. They might claim:

- there is a problem with your order or account;
- a parcel is waiting and needs a small redelivery fee;
- you have won a prize or a gift card;
- a payment has failed and you need to update your details.

The links lead to fake websites designed to steal passwords, card details or personal information.

### Marketplace and classified ad scams

On online marketplaces, scammers may:

- advertise items they do not have and ask for payment by bank transfer;
- ask you to pay outside the platform, avoiding buyer protection;
- send fake payment confirmation emails when you are the seller;
- overpay and ask for a refund of the difference, then reverse the original payment;
- send you a link to a fake "secure payment" or courier page.

### Delivery scams

Messages claiming to be from couriers ask you to pay a small fee or confirm your address via a link. The aim is usually to collect card details. Some scams also involve parcels sent to your address that the fraudster then tries to collect.

### Fake reviews and social media shops

Glowing reviews can be fabricated, and social media shops may appear and disappear quickly. Influencer-style promotions of "limited-time" bargains can be part of the same tactics.

### Subscription traps

"Free" samples or trials that require card details may sign you up for expensive recurring payments hidden in the small print.

## Warning signs of a fake shop

Look out for:

- **Prices that are too good to be true**, particularly on high-demand or premium goods.
- **A recently created website.** Domain lookup tools can show when a domain was registered; a very new site selling luxury goods at deep discounts is a red flag.
- **Poor spelling, grammar and design inconsistencies.**
- **Missing or vague contact details.** No physical address, no phone number, or only a contact form.
- **A web address that imitates a known brand**, with extra words, hyphens or unusual domain endings.
- **Unusual payment requests**, such as bank transfer only, cryptocurrency, gift cards or money transfer services.
- **No clear returns policy**, terms or privacy policy, or ones copied from elsewhere.
- **Reviews that are all extremely positive** or appear only on the shop's own site.
- **Pressure tactics** such as countdown timers and claims of low stock.
- **Social media profiles with few followers** or recently created accounts.

## How to check a shop before buying

1. **Search for the shop's name** together with words like "reviews", "scam" or "complaints" to see what others say.
2. **Check independent review sites**, bearing in mind that reviews can be manipulated.
3. **Look for full contact details**, and check that addresses and phone numbers are genuine.
4. **Check the web address carefully.** Type the address of known retailers directly rather than clicking adverts or links.
5. **Look at the payment options.** Reputable shops offer card payments and established payment services.
6. **Read the terms and returns policy.**
7. **Compare the price** with established retailers. A huge discount on a product that is discounted nowhere else is suspicious.

Remember that a padlock symbol in the browser only means the connection is encrypted. It does not prove the shop is genuine; many scam sites use encryption too.

## Safer ways to pay

How you pay can make a big difference to whether you can get your money back.

- **Credit cards** often offer the strongest protection. In many countries, card schemes and consumer credit laws allow you to dispute charges for goods that do not arrive or are not as described.
- **Debit cards** may offer chargeback protection through the card scheme, though rules and timescales vary.
- **Established payment services** with buyer protection can help, provided you pay through the proper process for goods and services rather than as a payment to friends and family.
- **Marketplace checkout systems** usually include protection when used correctly.

Be very cautious with:

- **Bank transfers** to people or businesses you do not know. These are hard to reverse.
- **Gift cards, vouchers and money transfer services** as payment for goods. Legitimate shops rarely ask for these.
- **Cryptocurrency**, which is usually irreversible.

## Protecting your accounts

- **Use strong, unique passwords** for shopping and payment accounts. A password manager makes this practical.
- **Turn on two-step verification** wherever possible.
- **Never share one-time passcodes** sent by your bank or a retailer. Legitimate organisations will not ask for them.
- **Keep devices and apps updated** to fix security weaknesses.
- **Avoid shopping on public Wi-Fi** for sensitive transactions, or use mobile data instead.
- **Check statements regularly** to spot unfamiliar payments quickly.
- **Be careful about saving card details** on websites you rarely use.

## Spotting phishing messages

Before clicking any link in a message:

- **Check the sender's address or number.** Look carefully for small differences from the genuine organisation.
- **Look for urgency or threats**, such as accounts being closed or parcels being returned.
- **Hover over links** on a computer to see where they lead, but avoid clicking if unsure.
- **Go directly to the official website or app** by typing the address yourself, and check your account or order there.
- **Be suspicious of unexpected attachments.**

Many countries run official services for reporting phishing emails and text messages; reporting helps take fake sites down.

## Safe marketplace habits

- **Inspect items in person** before paying when buying locally.
- **Keep communication and payment on the platform.**
- **Do not send deposits** to hold items.
- **As a seller, wait for payments to clear** in your own account — not just a confirmation email — before sending goods.
- **Never refund "overpayments"**; contact the platform instead.
- **Check profiles, ratings and account age.**

## Counterfeit goods

Counterfeit products are not just a bad deal; some can be dangerous. Fake chargers, electrical goods, cosmetics, toys and medicines may not meet safety standards. Buy these items from reputable retailers and be wary of steep discounts on branded goods.

## Helping older relatives and children

Scammers often target people who are less familiar with online shopping, as well as children using games and app stores. A few simple steps protect the whole family:

- **Talk about common scams** openly, so relatives feel comfortable asking before they pay.
- **Agree on a "check with someone" rule** for unexpected messages about parcels, prizes or account problems.
- **Set spending controls** on children's devices, game consoles and app stores, and require a password for purchases.
- **Help set up two-step verification** and a password manager on relatives' devices.
- **Bookmark trusted shops** in the browser so they are easy to reach without searching.

## Ticket and holiday booking scams

Concert, sports and festival tickets and holiday rentals are popular with fraudsters because demand is high and buyers feel rushed. Buy tickets from the official seller or an authorised resale platform, be wary of social media posts offering sold-out tickets, and book holiday accommodation through established platforms rather than paying a "host" directly by bank transfer. If a rental listing looks unusually cheap for its location, search for the photos online to check they have not been copied from another listing.

## What to do if you have been scammed

Act quickly:

1. **Contact your bank or card provider immediately.** Explain what happened, ask them to stop or reverse the payment if possible, and ask about chargeback or dispute options.
2. **Change passwords** for any accounts you think may be compromised, starting with email, banking and shopping accounts. Turn on two-step verification.
3. **Report the scam** to the platform or website involved and to the official fraud reporting body in your country.
4. **Keep evidence**: screenshots of the website or listing, messages, emails, receipts and payment details.
5. **Watch for follow-up scams.** Fraudsters sometimes contact victims pretending to help recover lost money for a fee. Genuine recovery services will not ask for upfront payments.
6. **Check your credit reports** if you shared personal information, and consider any identity protection services offered in your country.

Being scammed is not a sign of foolishness; scams are designed by professionals to be convincing. Reporting helps protect others.

## A pre-purchase checklist

1. Is the price realistic compared with other retailers?
2. Have I typed the web address myself or used a trusted app?
3. Does the shop have full contact details and a clear returns policy?
4. Have I searched for independent reviews and complaints?
5. Am I paying by a protected method such as a credit card?
6. Am I being pressured to act quickly?
7. Would I be comfortable if this money were lost?

## The bottom line

Online shopping scams succeed by exploiting trust, urgency and the appeal of a bargain. Slow down, check unfamiliar shops carefully, type addresses yourself rather than clicking links, pay with methods that offer protection, and never share passcodes. If something does go wrong, contact your bank straight away and report it. These habits let you enjoy the convenience and savings of online shopping with much less risk.
