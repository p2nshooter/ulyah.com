Travel is one of the things people most want to spend money on, and one of the easiest places to overspend. Flights, accommodation, meals out, attractions, transport, travel money fees and souvenirs all add up, and the costs often grow during the trip itself. Yet with some planning, flexibility and a few habits, it is possible to take memorable trips for considerably less.

This guide explains how to plan a holiday on a budget, from choosing when and where to go, to booking transport and accommodation, to managing money while you are away.

## Start with a budget

Before looking at destinations, decide how much you can afford to spend in total. Break it down into categories:

- Transport to and from your destination.
- Accommodation.
- Food and drink.
- Local transport.
- Activities and attractions.
- Travel insurance.
- Visas, vaccinations and documents, if needed.
- Spending money and souvenirs.
- A contingency for unexpected costs.

Saving for the trip in advance with a sinking fund, rather than paying on credit, avoids interest and makes the holiday more relaxing (see our sinking funds guide).

## Choose the right time

Timing has a huge effect on price.

- **Avoid peak periods** where possible. School holidays, public holidays and major events drive prices up.
- **Travel in the shoulder season**, just before or after peak season, for lower prices, fewer crowds and often pleasant weather.
- **Be flexible with dates.** Flying or staying mid-week is often cheaper than weekends. Many booking sites show prices across a range of dates.
- **Book early for peak times.** If you must travel during school holidays, booking well in advance often secures better prices and choice.
- **Consider last-minute deals** if you are flexible, although these are less predictable.

Families with school-age children may have little choice about dates; in that case, booking early and choosing destinations carefully become even more important.

## Choose your destination wisely

- **Consider the daily cost of living** at your destination. Food, transport and accommodation vary greatly between countries and cities.
- **Look at less famous alternatives.** A smaller town or a less popular region can offer similar experiences at lower prices.
- **Explore closer to home.** Domestic trips avoid flights and currency costs, and many people overlook attractive places in their own country.
- **Check exchange rates**, which can make some destinations more affordable.

## Transport

### Flights

- **Compare prices** across several airlines and comparison sites.
- **Set price alerts** for routes you are interested in.
- **Check nearby airports**, which may offer cheaper flights.
- **Read the baggage rules carefully.** Low-cost airlines often charge extra for checked bags, cabin bags above a small size, seat selection and printing boarding passes. Travel light to avoid fees.
- **Check total costs**, including getting to and from airports.

### Trains and coaches

- **Book train tickets in advance** where operators release cheaper fares early.
- **Use railcards and passes** if eligible, such as family, youth or senior railcards.
- **Consider coaches**, which are often the cheapest way to travel between cities.
- **Night trains and coaches** can save the cost of a night's accommodation.

### Driving

- **Share costs** with travel companions.
- **Plan fuel stops** and compare prices.
- **Check tolls, parking and congestion charges** at your destination.
- **Consider car hire carefully**: compare insurance options and check fuel policies, deposit requirements and charges for extra drivers.

## Accommodation

- **Compare different types**: hotels, guesthouses, hostels (many offer private rooms), holiday rentals, campsites and caravan parks.
- **Self-catering** accommodation can save a lot on food, especially for families.
- **Stay slightly outside the centre**, where prices are lower, if public transport is good.
- **Check what is included**: breakfast, parking, Wi-Fi and cleaning fees.
- **Read recent reviews** to avoid unpleasant surprises.
- **Consider house-swapping** or home-sitting schemes.
- **Book directly** with the accommodation sometimes; they may offer a better rate or perks.
- **Check cancellation policies** before booking.

Be cautious of rental listings that seem too cheap or that ask for payment outside a booking platform; holiday rental scams are common (see our guide to avoiding online scams).

## Package holidays versus independent travel

**Package holidays** that combine flights and accommodation can sometimes be cheaper than booking separately, especially for popular resort destinations. In some regions, packages also come with financial protection if the travel company fails. **Independent travel** offers more flexibility and can be cheaper for city breaks and less touristy destinations. Compare both before booking.

## Food and drink

Food can be one of the biggest holiday expenses.

- **Eat a big breakfast**, particularly if it is included in your accommodation.
- **Have your main meal at lunchtime**, when many restaurants offer cheaper set menus.
- **Shop at local markets and supermarkets** for picnic lunches and snacks.
- **Self-cater some meals** if you have a kitchen.
- **Avoid restaurants in the most touristy areas**, which often charge more.
- **Carry a reusable water bottle**, where tap water is safe to drink.
- **Look for local street food** from busy, reputable stalls.

## Activities and attractions

- **Research free attractions**: parks, beaches, museums with free entry, walking tours that run on tips, markets, festivals and viewpoints.
- **Use city passes** only if you will visit enough attractions to make them worthwhile.
- **Book attractions online** in advance, which can be cheaper and avoids queues.
- **Look for discounts** for children, students, seniors and families.
- **Walk or cycle** to explore; it is free and often the best way to see a place.

## Money abroad

Currency exchange and card fees can quietly add to holiday costs.

- **Use a card with low or no foreign transaction fees.** Many banks and providers offer travel-friendly debit or credit cards.
- **Always pay in the local currency.** When a card machine or cash machine offers to charge you in your home currency, choose the local currency instead; the conversion offered at the till is often at a poor rate.
- **Avoid exchanging money at airports**, where rates are usually worse.
- **Check cash machine fees.** Withdraw larger amounts less often to reduce fixed fees, while being careful about carrying large sums.
- **Tell your bank** you are travelling, if required, so payments are not blocked.
- **Carry a backup card** stored separately.

## Travel insurance

Travel insurance is essential for most trips, particularly abroad. It can cover medical emergencies, cancellations, lost luggage and other problems.

- **Buy insurance when you book**, so you are covered if you need to cancel.
- **Compare policies**, checking medical cover limits, cancellation cover, excesses and exclusions.
- **Declare pre-existing medical conditions**; failing to do so can invalidate a claim.
- **Consider an annual policy** if you travel more than once a year.
- **Check existing cover**, as some bank accounts and credit cards include travel insurance.
- **Carry any health cards** your country provides for medical treatment abroad, where they apply, but do not rely on them in place of insurance.

## Packing to save money

- **Pack light** to avoid baggage fees.
- **Bring essentials** such as sunscreen, medicines and toiletries, which can be more expensive at tourist destinations and airports.
- **Pack snacks** for travel days.
- **Bring adapters and chargers** to avoid buying replacements.

## Staying connected

Mobile roaming charges can be expensive in some destinations. Check your mobile plan's roaming terms before travelling, consider a local SIM card or an eSIM for longer trips, and use Wi-Fi where available. Download maps and translation tools for offline use.

## Travelling as a family

- **Look for family rooms** or apartments rather than multiple hotel rooms.
- **Choose accommodation with a kitchen.**
- **Plan a mix of paid and free activities.**
- **Bring entertainment** for travel days.
- **Check child discounts** for transport and attractions.

## Tracking spending while you travel

It is easy to lose track of money on holiday, especially when paying in an unfamiliar currency. A few simple habits keep spending in check:

- **Set a daily budget** for food, local transport and activities, based on your overall plan.
- **Record spending each evening** in a notes app or a simple travel budgeting app.
- **Convert prices mentally** using a rough rule — for example, dividing or multiplying by a round number — so you can judge costs quickly.
- **Use a separate card or account** for holiday spending, loaded with your holiday budget, so you can see at a glance how much is left.
- **Keep some contingency money** untouched for genuine emergencies.

## Make the most of short breaks

A long holiday is not the only way to travel. Short breaks — a weekend in a nearby city, a few nights camping, or a day trip by train — can give a real sense of getting away for much less money. Travelling off-peak, booking transport early and choosing self-catering accommodation make short breaks particularly good value, and spreading several small trips across the year can feel as refreshing as one expensive holiday.

## Avoiding hidden costs

- **Resort fees and city taxes** may be charged on arrival.
- **Airport transfers** can be expensive; research public transport options.
- **Tipping customs** vary between countries; check what is expected.
- **Excursions sold at resorts** are often cheaper booked independently.
- **Minibars and room service** are best avoided.

## A budget travel checklist

1. Set a total budget and save in advance.
2. Travel outside peak periods where possible.
3. Compare destinations by daily cost.
4. Compare transport options and baggage fees.
5. Choose accommodation with self-catering if possible.
6. Plan free activities and picnic lunches.
7. Use a low-fee card and pay in local currency.
8. Buy travel insurance when you book.
9. Pack light and bring essentials.
10. Keep a contingency fund.

## The bottom line

Budget travel is less about sacrifice and more about smart choices. Travelling outside peak times, being flexible, comparing transport and accommodation, eating like a local, enjoying free attractions and avoiding fees can make a holiday far more affordable. With a clear budget and some planning, you can enjoy the experience fully — and come home without a pile of bills.
