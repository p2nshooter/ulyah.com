Gift-giving can put real pressure on a household budget. Birthdays, weddings, festivals, teachers' end-of-year presents, new babies and thank-you gifts come round throughout the year, and the expectation to spend can grow every time. Yet the gifts people treasure most are often not the most expensive; they are the ones that show thought, effort and an understanding of what the recipient enjoys.

This guide offers ideas for gifts that cost little or nothing, ways to plan gift spending, and tactful approaches to reducing gift expectations with family and friends.

## Plan your gift budget

Gifts are easier to afford when they are planned.

- **List the occasions** you usually buy gifts for over a year: birthdays, festivals, anniversaries, teachers, and celebrations.
- **Set a budget** for each person or occasion.
- **Save monthly** in a gift sinking fund, so December or a busy birthday month does not strain your finances (see our sinking funds guide).
- **Buy throughout the year** when you see something suitable at a good price, and store gifts in one place — with a list so you remember what you have.
- **Keep a note of ideas** when friends mention things they would like.

## Handmade gifts

Handmade gifts can be very personal and often cost little in materials.

### Food and drink gifts

- **Home-baked biscuits, brownies or bread**, packed in a jar or tin.
- **Jams, chutneys and preserves**, made from seasonal or foraged fruit (follow safe preserving guidance).
- **Flavoured salts or sugars**, such as herb salt or vanilla sugar.
- **Hot chocolate kits** in a jar, layered with cocoa, sugar and marshmallows.
- **Granola** in a decorative jar.
- **Home-made fudge or truffles.**

Label ingredients clearly, especially common allergens, and include a "best before" date.

### Crafts

- **Knitted or crocheted items** such as scarves, hats or blankets.
- **Sewn gifts**: tote bags, cushion covers or fabric gift wraps made from scraps.
- **Candles** made from wax and reusable jars, following safety instructions.
- **Decorated plant pots** with a cutting or seedling grown from your own plants.
- **Photo albums or scrapbooks** filled with shared memories.
- **Framed artwork** by children, which grandparents often love.
- **Personalised cards** made by hand.

### Gifts from the garden

- **Cuttings or divisions** of houseplants and garden plants, potted up.
- **Seed packets** saved from your own garden, with growing instructions.
- **Bunches of flowers or herbs** from the garden.

## Experience gifts

Experiences create memories without adding clutter.

- **A day out together**: a walk, a picnic, a visit to a free museum or a trip to the beach.
- **A home-cooked meal** for a friend or family member.
- **A film night** with snacks and their favourite film.
- **Teaching a skill**: cooking, baking, knitting, gardening, photography or a language.
- **Tickets to a local event**, such as a community theatre production or a concert, which can be inexpensive.
- **A "tour" of your town**, visiting favourite places.

## Gifts of time and help

Some of the most valued gifts are practical help.

- **Babysitting vouchers** for parents of young children.
- **Gardening, cleaning or DIY help** for older relatives or busy friends.
- **Cooking freezer meals** for a new parent or someone recovering from illness.
- **Pet-sitting or dog walking.**
- **Tech help**: setting up a new phone, organising photos or teaching video calling.
- **A lift** to appointments or events.

Make a simple voucher or card describing the offer, and be sure to follow through.

## Personal and thoughtful gifts

- **A letter** telling someone what they mean to you.
- **A memory jar** filled with notes of shared memories or reasons you appreciate someone.
- **A playlist** of songs that mean something to both of you.
- **A recipe book** of family recipes, handwritten or printed.
- **A family tree** researched using free resources.
- **A "book I loved" gift**: a second-hand copy of a favourite book with a note explaining why.

## Inexpensive bought gifts

When buying gifts, small budgets can still go a long way:

- **Second-hand books, records and games**, often in excellent condition at charity shops and online marketplaces.
- **Good-quality basics**: a nice pen, a notebook, a mug with a favourite tea or coffee.
- **Plants**: small houseplants are inexpensive and long-lasting.
- **Bundles**: combine several small items around a theme — a pamper kit, a baking kit, a gardening kit.
- **Seasonal sales**: buy gifts in sales throughout the year (see our seasonal sales guide).
- **Gift wrapping**: reuse paper, use brown paper with string, or wrap in fabric.

## Gifts for children

- **Second-hand toys and books** in good condition.
- **Craft kits** made up from supplies you already have.
- **Experiences**: a trip to the park, a baking afternoon or a camping night in the garden.
- **Books** from charity shops or book swaps.
- **A "treasure box"** of dressing-up clothes, old jewellery, hats and scarves.
- **A home-made voucher** for a special activity with a parent or grandparent.

Check toys meet safety standards and are suitable for the child's age.

## Gifts for teachers, colleagues and neighbours

- **A handwritten card** from a child, which teachers often value most.
- **A small plant** or home-made treat.
- **A class collection** where parents contribute a small amount to one gift.
- **A kind message** to a colleague's manager praising their work.

## Reducing gift expectations

Many people would welcome spending less on gifts but feel awkward suggesting it. Some approaches:

- **Suggest a "Secret Santa"** or gift exchange, where each adult buys one gift for one person, with a spending limit.
- **Agree on a spending limit** for all gifts.
- **Focus on children**: adults agree not to exchange gifts with each other, but buy for the children.
- **Suggest experiences** instead of presents: a family meal or day out together.
- **Agree to give to charity** instead of exchanging gifts.
- **Raise the idea early**, well before the occasion, so nobody has already bought presents.

Most people are relieved when someone suggests spending less. Frame it positively — as a way to focus on time together.

## Gifts for specific occasions

### New babies

New parents often receive many baby clothes in small sizes that are quickly outgrown. More useful, low-cost gifts include:

- **Home-cooked freezer meals** for the first weeks.
- **An offer to hold the baby** while a parent sleeps or showers.
- **Larger-size clothes** for when the baby grows.
- **A book** for the baby's first library.
- **Help with practical tasks**, such as shopping, laundry or walking the dog.

### Weddings

- **Contribute to a shared gift** or a couple's chosen fund, if they have one.
- **Offer a skill**, such as photography, baking, flower arranging or calligraphy, if the couple would welcome it and you are confident.
- **A framed photograph** of the couple, or a handwritten card with good wishes and advice.

### Thank-you gifts

- **A sincere handwritten note**, often valued more than any present.
- **Home-baked treats.**
- **A small plant** or cutting.
- **Returning a favour** with help or time.

### Older relatives

Older people often have most of the things they need and may prefer not to accumulate more. Consider:

- **Regular visits or phone calls**, perhaps on an agreed day each week.
- **Printed photographs** of grandchildren and family events, which can be put in an album or frame.
- **Help with tasks** such as gardening, shopping or technology.
- **An outing** to a favourite place.
- **Consumables** they enjoy, such as good tea, coffee, biscuits or toiletries.

### Friends far away

For friends who live far away, a video call with a shared activity — watching a film together, cooking the same recipe, or playing an online game — can be a warm, free gift. A parcel of small local treats from your area, or a letter and photos sent by post, also shows care.

## Make a year-round gift cupboard

Many people save money by keeping a small "gift cupboard" or box: books, candles, notebooks, toiletries, children's toys and cards bought in sales or on offer throughout the year, ready for unexpected birthdays and occasions. Keep a list of what is in it and who each item might suit, and check items have not passed their use-by dates.

## Wrapping for less

- **Reuse gift bags, boxes and tissue paper.**
- **Use brown paper** decorated with stamps, drawings or greenery.
- **Wrap in fabric**, such as a scarf or tea towel, which becomes part of the gift.
- **Use newspaper or old maps** for a creative look.
- **Skip the wrapping** for experiences and vouchers, using a card instead.

## Gifts that keep giving

Some low-cost gifts continue to bring pleasure long after the occasion:

- **A sourdough starter** shared from your own, with instructions for keeping it alive.
- **A cutting from a houseplant** that will grow for years.
- **A library of family recipes** that can be added to over time.
- **An introduction to a free hobby**, such as birdwatching with a printed guide to local birds, or geocaching with a first route planned.
- **A subscription you already pay for** that allows sharing within the rules, such as a family plan.

## Avoiding gift guilt

It is easy to feel that a small gift reflects badly on you. Remember:

- **The thought really does count.**
- **Many people appreciate** practical, personal or handmade gifts more than expensive ones.
- **Staying within your budget** is a responsible choice.
- **Debt from gift-giving** can take months to repay and causes stress.

## The bottom line

Thoughtful gifts do not need to be expensive. Handmade food and crafts, experiences, offers of time and help, personal mementos and well-chosen second-hand finds can be more meaningful than costly presents. Plan your gift budget, save throughout the year, and talk openly with family and friends about expectations — and gift-giving can become a pleasure rather than a financial strain.
