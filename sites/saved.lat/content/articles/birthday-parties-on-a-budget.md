Birthday parties can easily become one of the biggest one-off expenses in a family's year. Venue hire, entertainers, catering, themed decorations, a professional cake and party bags for a whole class can add up to a startling amount — often for a party that lasts only two hours. Many parents feel pressure to match the parties their children attend, even when money is tight.

The good news is that a birthday does not need to be expensive to be special. Children tend to remember games, friends and feeling celebrated far more than the price of the venue. This guide explains how to plan a fun party on a budget, for children or adults, with ideas for venues, food, decorations, games and cakes.

## Start with a budget and a plan

### Set a budget

Decide how much you can comfortably spend before planning anything else. Break it down into categories:

- Venue.
- Food and drink.
- Cake.
- Decorations.
- Activities or entertainment.
- Party bags or favours.
- Invitations.

Having a figure for each category stops costs creeping up. If you know a birthday is coming, consider saving a small amount each month in a sinking fund (see our sinking funds guide).

### Decide on the size

The guest list has the biggest impact on cost. Options include:

- **A small party** with a few close friends, which allows more personal activities.
- **The age-plus-one rule**: invite as many children as the birthday child's age plus one, a popular way of keeping numbers manageable for young children.
- **A whole-class party**, which some schools encourage for inclusivity but which is expensive. Some families share a joint party with classmates whose birthdays fall close together.

### Consider alternatives to a party

Some children are happy, or even happier, with:

- **A special day out** with one or two friends.
- **A family celebration** at home with a favourite meal.
- **A sleepover** with a small group.
- **An experience gift** instead of a party.

Ask your child what they would like; the answer may surprise you.

## Choosing a venue

### At home

A party at home is usually the cheapest option. To make it work:

- **Clear the space** and put away valuables and breakables.
- **Limit numbers** to what your home can comfortably hold.
- **Plan activities in advance** so children are busy.
- **Use the garden** in good weather.

### Free or low-cost venues

- **Parks and playgrounds** for a picnic party in summer (check whether permission is needed for larger gatherings, and have a backup plan for rain).
- **Community halls**, church halls and village halls are often much cheaper to hire than commercial venues.
- **Local sports clubs** sometimes offer affordable party packages.
- **Beaches and woodlands** for adventurous outdoor parties, following safety guidance.

### Commercial venues

If you choose a soft play centre, bowling alley or activity venue:

- **Compare packages** and check exactly what is included.
- **Ask about off-peak times**, which can be cheaper.
- **Check whether you can bring your own cake** and food.
- **Ask about minimum numbers** and extra charges per child.

## Invitations

- **Send digital invitations** by message or email, which are free and easy to track.
- **Make handmade invitations** with your child as a craft activity.
- **Print simple invitations** at home if needed.

Include clear details: time, place, what to bring, any dietary questions and how to reply.

## Food and drink

Food is often a large part of the cost. Keep it simple:

- **Plan a party time** that avoids a full meal, for example mid-afternoon, so snacks are enough.
- **Make food at home**: sandwiches, wraps, mini pizzas, sausage rolls, vegetable sticks with dips, fruit skewers, popcorn and home-made biscuits.
- **Buy in bulk** and compare unit prices (see our unit-pricing guide).
- **Use supermarket own-brand products.**
- **Offer water and diluted squash** rather than expensive soft drinks.
- **Ask about allergies and dietary needs** in advance, and label foods clearly.

For adult parties, a potluck — where guests bring a dish to share — spreads the cost and adds variety.

## The cake

- **Bake your own.** A simple sponge or tray bake decorated with icing, sweets or fruit can look wonderful. Many children love helping.
- **Decorate a shop-bought cake.** Plain supermarket cakes can be personalised with candles, toppers, sprinkles or small toys.
- **Make cupcakes** instead of one large cake; they are easy to share and need no cutting.
- **Use a theme simply**: a number-shaped cake can be made by cutting a rectangular sponge into shape.

## Decorations

- **Choose a colour scheme** rather than an expensive branded theme.
- **Make decorations**: paper chains, bunting from fabric scraps or wrapping paper, and paper pompoms.
- **Reuse decorations**: keep bunting, banners and tablecloths for future parties.
- **Borrow** from friends and family.
- **Balloons** add impact for little cost, but follow safety advice for young children, who can choke on uninflated or burst balloons, and dispose of them responsibly.
- **Avoid single-use items** where possible; reusable plates and cups save money over several parties.

## Games and activities

Traditional party games are free and still hugely popular:

- **Pass the parcel**, with a small prize in the middle (or a sweet in each layer).
- **Musical statues, musical chairs and musical bumps.**
- **Treasure hunts** with clues around the house or garden.
- **Sleeping lions**, a useful calm-down game.
- **Simon says.**
- **Egg-and-spoon and sack races** in the garden or park.
- **Pin the tail on the donkey**, or a home-made version based on the party theme.
- **Limbo** with a broom handle.

### Activity stations

- **Craft table**: decorate biscuits, make crowns, or colour masks.
- **Dressing up**: a box of costumes and props.
- **Photo corner**: a backdrop and props for pictures.
- **Mini disco** with a playlist and dimmed lights.

### For older children and teenagers

- **Film night** with home-made popcorn.
- **Pizza-making party** with ready-made bases and toppings.
- **Sports tournament** in the park.
- **Games night** with board games or video games.
- **Karaoke** with a free online karaoke playlist.

### Entertainers

Professional entertainers can be expensive. Alternatives include:

- **A talented friend or relative** who can do magic tricks, face painting or music.
- **Older children or teenagers** who can run games for a small fee or as a favour.
- **Simple face painting** at home with skin-safe face paints.

## Party bags and favours

Party bags are often filled with small plastic items that are quickly forgotten. Cheaper and more thoughtful alternatives include:

- **A slice of birthday cake** wrapped in a napkin.
- **A single, useful item**, such as a book (second-hand books in good condition can be very cheap), a packet of seeds, crayons or a bubble mix.
- **Something made at the party**, like decorated biscuits or a craft.
- **No party bags at all**, which many parents welcome.

If you do give party bags, buy items in bulk and avoid choking hazards for young children.

## Gifts

Large parties can lead to an overwhelming pile of presents. Some families suggest:

- **No gifts**, or
- **A "fiver party"**, where guests contribute a small fixed amount towards one larger gift, or
- **Book swaps** or donations to a charity chosen by the birthday child.

Be sensitive to other families' circumstances; a simple card is always enough.

## Adult birthday parties on a budget

Many of the same ideas apply to adult celebrations:

- **Host at home** or in a park.
- **Organise a potluck** or a barbecue where everyone brings something.
- **Ask guests to bring their own drinks.**
- **Choose a free activity**, such as a picnic, hike or games night.
- **Book a restaurant with a set menu**, or meet for dessert or drinks instead of a full meal.

## Party themes that cost almost nothing

A theme gives a party focus without requiring branded merchandise. Ideas that rely on imagination and household items include:

- **Pirates**: treasure hunt with a hand-drawn map, eye patches cut from card, and a "treasure chest" made from a shoebox.
- **Superheroes**: capes made from old pillowcases or fabric scraps, and an obstacle course "training camp".
- **Science lab**: simple safe experiments, such as fizzing volcanoes and colour-changing red cabbage water, with adult supervision.
- **Teddy bears' picnic**: guests bring a favourite soft toy to a picnic in the park.
- **Sports day**: classic races, medals made from card and ribbon, and team colours.
- **Art studio**: an easel or wall of paper, aprons made from old shirts, and an exhibition at the end.

## Timing and helpers

Short parties are cheaper and often more enjoyable. For young children, around one and a half to two hours is usually plenty. Plan a simple running order — arrival activity, games, food, cake, a calm final game — and recruit a few adult helpers so you can enjoy the party too. Ask helpers to take photographs, keep an eye on younger guests and help with tidying up.

## Avoiding the comparison trap

It is natural to feel pressure when other parties seem bigger and more expensive. Remember:

- **Children rarely remember** the cost of a party.
- **Simple parties can be more relaxed** and fun for everyone.
- **Your budget reflects your priorities**, and protecting your household finances is a good example to set.

## A party planning checklist

1. Set a budget and decide on the guest list.
2. Choose a venue: home, park, community hall or commercial venue.
3. Send digital or handmade invitations.
4. Plan simple, home-made food and a cake.
5. Make or reuse decorations.
6. Prepare a list of games and activities.
7. Decide on party bags or simple favours.
8. Ask about allergies and dietary needs.
9. Recruit helpers.
10. Enjoy the day.

## The bottom line

A birthday party does not need a big budget to be memorable. With a clear plan, a sensible guest list, home-made food, classic games and simple decorations, you can create a celebration that children and adults will love — at a fraction of the cost of commercial packages. The most important ingredients are friends, laughter and making the birthday person feel special.
