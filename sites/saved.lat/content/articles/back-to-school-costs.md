The start of a new school year is exciting for children and expensive for parents. Uniforms, school shoes, coats, sports kit, bags, stationery, lunch boxes, calculators, laptops or tablets, and contributions to trips and activities can all fall due at once, often after an expensive summer holiday. For families with several children, the total can be daunting.

With some planning, much of this cost can be spread out or reduced. This guide explains how to prepare a back-to-school budget, where to find second-hand and discounted items, what support may be available, and how to keep costs down through the year.

## Make a list and a budget

Start by working out what each child actually needs.

1. **Check the school's requirements.** Look at the uniform policy, the equipment list and any letters about trips, books or technology. Some items must carry the school logo; others can be bought from any shop.
2. **Check what you already have.** Try on last year's uniform, shoes and sports kit; check bags, coats and stationery from previous years.
3. **List what is genuinely needed**, separating essentials from nice-to-haves.
4. **Estimate costs** and set a budget.
5. **Start saving early**, ideally with a sinking fund through the year (see our sinking funds guide).

## Uniforms

### Buy logo items only where required

Many schools require only a few logo items, such as a jumper or blazer, while plain items such as trousers, skirts, shirts and polo shirts can be bought from supermarkets and high-street shops, which are often much cheaper. In some countries, guidance or rules encourage schools to keep uniform costs affordable, for example by limiting branded items.

### Second-hand uniforms

- **School uniform swaps and sales.** Many schools and parent associations run second-hand uniform shops or swaps, often very cheaply or free.
- **Community groups and online marketplaces** where parents pass on outgrown uniforms.
- **Charity shops**, particularly near schools.

### Buy for growth — sensibly

Buying slightly larger sizes can extend wear, but very oversized clothes look uncomfortable and may not last as long. Adjustable waistbands, hems that can be let down and sleeves that can be turned up help.

### Make uniforms last

- **Label everything** with the child's name; lost uniform is a common cost.
- **Wash at lower temperatures** and follow care labels (see our laundry guide).
- **Use stain removal quickly.**
- **Mend small tears and loose buttons** promptly (see our mending guide).
- **Have spares** of everyday items so you are not forced to wash every night.

## School shoes

Shoes are often one of the most expensive items, and children's feet grow quickly.

- **Get feet measured** if possible, as poorly fitting shoes can be uncomfortable.
- **Compare prices** across shops; many supermarkets and high-street retailers sell durable school shoes at lower prices.
- **Look for sales** before and just after the school term starts.
- **Choose durable materials** and good construction, which may last longer.
- **Look after them**: polishing leather shoes and using a shoe horn extends their life.
- **Consider a second pair** of cheaper shoes for weekends, so school shoes are not worn out at play.

Second-hand shoes are sometimes discouraged because they mould to the previous wearer's feet, but nearly new shoes outgrown quickly may be fine; use your judgement.

## Coats, bags and sports kit

- **Coats**: buy out of season at the end of winter, or second-hand.
- **Bags**: a sturdy, well-made bag may last several years; avoid buying a new one each year if the old one is still in good condition.
- **Sports kit**: check whether plain items can replace logo items, and look for second-hand kit.
- **Water bottles and lunch boxes**: choose durable, reusable options, and label them.

## Stationery

- **Check the school's list**; avoid buying extras that are not needed.
- **Reuse** last year's pencil cases, rulers, calculators and geometry sets.
- **Buy basics in bulk** — pens, pencils, glue sticks, exercise books — and share them between siblings.
- **Compare supermarket and discount shop prices** with specialist stationers.
- **Avoid character-branded items**, which often cost more.
- **Ask whether the school provides some equipment**, such as exercise books.

### Calculators

Scientific calculators can be expensive. Check exactly which model the school recommends, buy second-hand from older students, and label it clearly.

## Technology

Some schools require or recommend laptops, tablets or other devices.

- **Check exactly what is needed** and whether the school has a device scheme.
- **Ask about school-provided devices or loans**, which some schools and local authorities offer, especially for families on low incomes.
- **Consider refurbished devices** from reputable sellers, which often come with warranties.
- **Avoid paying for features you do not need**; basic models are often sufficient for schoolwork.
- **Protect devices** with a sturdy case.
- **Check insurance**; your home contents policy might cover devices outside the home.

## Lunches and snacks

- **Check eligibility for free school meals** or subsidised meals, which may be available depending on age, income or local policy.
- **Compare packed lunch costs** with school dinners; for some families, one is clearly cheaper.
- **Prepare packed lunches in batches**, such as making and freezing sandwiches or muffins.
- **Use reusable containers** rather than single-use packaging.
- **Buy snacks in larger packs** and portion them at home.

See our meal planning guide for more ideas.

## Transport

- **Check eligibility for free or discounted school transport.**
- **Use student travel passes** where available.
- **Walk or cycle** when safe, which is free and healthy.
- **Share lifts** with other families.

## Trips, activities and extras

Schools may ask for contributions towards trips, music lessons, clubs and special events.

- **Ask about payment plans** for expensive trips.
- **Ask about financial support**: many schools have funds to help families who would otherwise struggle.
- **Plan for extras** in your budget, such as non-uniform days, charity events and school photos.
- **Prioritise** activities that matter most to your child.

## Support schemes

Depending on where you live, help may include:

- **School uniform grants** or vouchers.
- **Free school meals.**
- **Transport assistance.**
- **Funds for trips and activities.**
- **Device loan schemes.**

Check your local authority, the school and official government websites. Schools are often discreet about financial help, so do not hesitate to ask.

## Timing your shopping

- **Shop early** for the best selection and to avoid last-minute stress.
- **Watch for back-to-school sales** in late summer.
- **Shop after term starts** for remaining items, when prices may fall.
- **Buy uniform and shoes in stages**: essentials before term, others once you know what is really needed.

## Keeping costs down through the year

Back-to-school spending is not the only school expense. Costs continue through the year, and planning for them reduces surprises:

- **Keep a school calendar** of non-uniform days, charity events, trips, exams, school photos, performances and holiday clubs, so you can budget ahead.
- **Set up a small monthly saving** for school extras, alongside your back-to-school fund.
- **Check uniform condition** at half-term so replacements can be bought in sales rather than urgently.
- **Organise a lost-property check** regularly; named items are often found and returned.
- **Share costs with other parents**, such as costumes for performances or materials for projects.
- **Borrow rather than buy** for one-off needs, such as a costume for book day or equipment for a single school project.

## Homework and learning at home

Children do not need expensive resources to learn at home. Libraries offer books, study spaces, computers and often free homework clubs. Many reputable educational websites and apps are free, and schools often provide access to online learning platforms. A quiet, well-lit place to work, a basic stationery kit and a regular routine matter far more than costly extras. If your child needs extra support, ask the school first; many offer help sessions, mentoring or study groups at no charge.

## Teaching children about money

Back-to-school shopping is a good opportunity to involve children in money decisions. Older children can help compare prices, stay within a budget and decide between options. Explaining why some items are bought second-hand or from cheaper shops helps children understand value and planning.

## Passing items down

In families with more than one child, buying with the next child in mind can save a great deal. Choose neutral colours for coats, bags and lunch boxes so they can be passed on, store outgrown uniforms clean and labelled by size, and keep a list of what you have in storage so you do not buy duplicates. Friends and relatives with older children are often happy to pass on uniforms and equipment too.

## A back-to-school checklist

1. Read the school's uniform and equipment requirements.
2. Check what you already have.
3. List essentials and set a budget.
4. Buy logo items only where required.
5. Use uniform swaps and second-hand sources.
6. Compare prices for shoes and plain uniform.
7. Reuse stationery and buy basics in bulk.
8. Check for device schemes and refurbished options.
9. Ask about free school meals, grants and support.
10. Label everything.

## The bottom line

Back-to-school costs can be significant, but they do not need to overwhelm a household budget. Plan early, buy only what is required, use second-hand uniforms and plain items, look after clothes and shoes so they last, and ask about the support available. With a list, a budget and a few smart choices, children can start the school year well equipped — and parents can start it without financial stress.
