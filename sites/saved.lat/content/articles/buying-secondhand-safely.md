Buying second-hand is one of the most reliable ways to cut household spending. Furniture, books, tools, children's clothes, sports equipment, kitchenware and many electronics can be bought for a fraction of their new price, often in excellent condition. It also keeps usable goods in circulation instead of in landfill.

But second-hand shopping has its own risks: items that are worn out or unsafe, sellers who are not honest, and scams that target online buyers. A few habits — knowing what to check, which items to avoid, and how to pay safely — let you enjoy the bargains while avoiding the pitfalls.

## Where to buy second-hand

Each source has its own strengths:

- **Charity shops and thrift stores.** Prices are low and purchases support good causes. Stock changes constantly, so visiting regularly pays off.
- **Online marketplaces and classified sites.** The widest choice, with the chance to search for exactly what you want and compare prices. They also carry the most scam risk.
- **Specialist second-hand shops.** Shops selling used furniture, instruments, cameras, books or children's goods often check items and may offer a short guarantee.
- **Refurbished sellers.** Phones, laptops, tablets and appliances refurbished by manufacturers or reputable retailers are tested, cleaned and usually come with a warranty.
- **Car-boot sales, flea markets and yard sales.** Good for bargains and haggling, with no guarantees.
- **Community groups and free-sharing networks.** Neighbours often give away items they no longer need.
- **Auctions.** Online and local auctions can offer good prices on furniture and household goods, but read the terms and fees carefully.

## Decide what you need first

Second-hand bargains can tempt you into buying things you do not need. Before shopping:

- **Make a list** of what you are looking for.
- **Know your measurements** for furniture and appliances, including doorways and stairs.
- **Check the new price** so you know what a fair second-hand price is. A used item is not a bargain if a similar new one is on sale for little more.
- **Set a budget** and stick to it.

## What to check before you buy

### General checks

- **Condition.** Look closely in good light for damage, wear, stains, missing parts and repairs.
- **Function.** Test anything with moving parts, switches or mechanisms.
- **Completeness.** Are all parts, accessories, cables and instructions included? Missing chargers, lids and fittings can be expensive to replace.
- **Cleanliness and smell.** Some odours, such as smoke and damp, are hard to remove.
- **Model information.** Note brand and model numbers so you can research reviews, parts availability and recalls.

### Furniture

- **Push and pull** on the frame to check for wobbles and loose joints.
- **Open drawers and doors.**
- **Look for woodworm**: small round holes and fine dust.
- **Check upholstered furniture** for fire-safety labels where these are legally required, and for stains, bed bugs and broken springs. Inspect seams and folds carefully.

### Electronics

- **See it working.** Ask to see the item powered on, connected and running.
- **Check batteries.** Phone, laptop and tablet batteries degrade with age; many devices show battery health in their settings.
- **Check accounts and locks.** Phones, tablets and computers should be reset to factory settings and removed from the seller's account. A device still linked to someone else's account may be unusable.
- **Check that it is not reported stolen** where your country offers a way to check phone identity numbers (IMEI) or serial numbers.
- **Check software support.** Older devices may no longer receive security updates, which matters for phones, tablets, laptops and routers.

### Appliances

- **Ask to see it running** if possible.
- **Check the age** from the model or serial number, and whether spare parts are still available.
- **Look at energy ratings** for fridges, freezers and washing machines; an old, inefficient appliance may cost more to run than a bargain price saves.
- **Look for recalls.** Many governments and manufacturers publish recall lists searchable by model number.

### Clothing and shoes

- **Check seams, zips and buttons.**
- **Look for stains, pilling and holes** in good light.
- **Try on** where possible; sizes vary between brands and eras.
- **Wash or clean** everything before wearing.

## What to buy second-hand — and what to buy new

### Usually great second-hand

- Solid wood furniture, bookcases and tables.
- Books, music, films and games.
- Hand tools and garden tools.
- Kitchenware: pans, plates, glasses, cutlery and utensils.
- Sports equipment such as weights, tennis rackets and bicycles (checked carefully).
- Children's clothes, toys in good condition and books.
- Musical instruments.
- Picture frames, mirrors, lamps (checked for safe wiring) and decorative items.

### Think carefully or buy new

Some items are better bought new for safety or hygiene reasons, or need extra checks:

- **Child car seats.** Safety organisations generally advise against buying used car seats unless you know their full history, because a seat involved in a crash may be damaged invisibly, and standards and expiry dates change.
- **Cycle and motorcycle helmets.** A helmet that has taken an impact may no longer protect you, even if it looks fine.
- **Mattresses.** Hygiene and fire-safety concerns make used mattresses risky; if you do buy one, check labels and condition very carefully.
- **Cots and baby equipment.** Check that they meet current safety standards and have not been recalled. Buy a new cot mattress.
- **Electric heaters, electric blankets and chargers.** Unknown history and damaged wiring are fire risks; inspect carefully or buy new.
- **Cosmetics and personal care items.**

## Paying and collecting safely

### Online marketplace safety

Online marketplaces are where most second-hand scams happen. Protect yourself by:

- **Inspecting before paying** whenever possible. Collect in person and see the item working.
- **Using the platform's own payment system** where available, which may offer buyer protection. Do not move the conversation or payment off-platform when the seller suggests it.
- **Avoiding bank transfers to strangers** for items you have not seen. Bank transfers usually offer little protection if the item never arrives.
- **Never paying a "deposit"** to hold an item you have not seen.
- **Being wary of prices that are too good to be true**, especially for phones, games consoles, cameras, concert tickets and pets.
- **Checking seller profiles**, ratings and how long they have been active.
- **Being suspicious of pressure.** Scammers often claim many other buyers are interested, or that they are abroad and will ship the item.
- **Not sharing personal information** such as bank details, verification codes or identity documents with buyers or sellers.

For more on spotting fraud, see our guide to avoiding online shopping scams.

### Meeting sellers

- **Meet in a public, well-lit place** for smaller items, or bring a friend when collecting from someone's home.
- **Tell someone** where you are going.
- **Bring the right tools** to test items — batteries, an extension lead, or a phone charger.
- **Count cash discreetly**, or use a safe electronic payment method after inspection.

## Negotiating politely

Haggling is normal at car-boot sales, markets and on many online marketplaces.

- **Research prices first** so your offer is reasonable.
- **Point out genuine issues** such as missing parts or wear.
- **Offer a fair price** rather than an insulting one; sellers are more likely to agree.
- **Bundle items** — buying several things from one seller often earns a discount.
- **Be ready to walk away.** There will always be another bargain.

## Your consumer rights

Rights when buying second-hand vary by country and by seller:

- **Buying from a business**, including a second-hand shop or refurbished seller, usually gives you consumer rights similar to buying new, adjusted for the item's age and condition.
- **Buying from a private individual** often gives fewer rights; the item may need only to be as described. This is why inspection before payment is important.
- **Online purchases from businesses** may include a cooling-off period for returns in some regions.

Keep receipts, screenshots of listings and messages in case of a dispute.

## Cleaning and preparing second-hand items

- **Wash textiles** at the highest temperature the care label allows.
- **Clean hard surfaces** with warm soapy water.
- **Inspect upholstered items** for pests before bringing them inside.
- **Have electrical items checked** if you are unsure about their safety.
- **Register second-hand appliances** with the manufacturer where possible so you receive recall notices.

## Timing your second-hand shopping

A little timing can improve both choice and price:

- **Shop early at car-boot sales and markets** for the best selection, or late in the day when sellers would rather discount than pack items away.
- **Watch for seasonal clear-outs.** Spring cleaning, the end of the school year and house moves bring a wave of furniture, toys and children's clothes onto the market.
- **Buy out of season.** Garden furniture is cheaper in autumn, winter coats in late spring, and bikes are often easier to find in late summer and autumn.
- **Check university towns** at the end of the academic year, when students sell desks, lamps and kitchen equipment before moving out.
- **Set saved searches and alerts** on online marketplaces so you hear about new listings quickly; good-value items often sell within hours.

## Keep a second-hand wish list

A running list of things you need — with measurements, preferred colours, a target price and the new price for comparison — keeps second-hand shopping focused. When you spot a bargain, you will know immediately whether it is something you genuinely want and whether it fits, and you will be far less likely to bring home something just because it was cheap.

## Selling your own items

Second-hand shopping works both ways. Selling things you no longer need funds your next purchase and clears space. Take clear photos in natural light, describe items honestly, including flaws, and follow the same safety advice when meeting buyers.

## The bottom line

Second-hand shopping can save a great deal of money when done carefully. Know what you need and what it costs new, inspect and test items before paying, avoid used safety equipment such as car seats and helmets, and pay in ways that protect you. With a little care, you can furnish and equip a home for much less — and enjoy the hunt along the way.
