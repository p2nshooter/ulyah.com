Shopping online is convenient: around the clock, without having to look for a parking space and with a huge selection. At the same time, online trading is a popular area for fraudsters. Fake shops lure people with incredibly low prices, disappear after payment and never deliver. There are also many scams on classified ad platforms and social networks. The good news: With a few checks before purchasing and the correct payment method, you are well protected. And if something does go wrong, you have strong rights in the EU. This guide explains what you should pay attention to.

## Fake shops: How the scam works

A fake shop is a website that looks like a real online shop but never delivers. Such shops often advertise popular products at very low prices: branded shoes, electronics, tools, garden furniture or firewood and pellets in winter. The sites are professionally designed, sometimes even copies of real shops. In the end, only **advance payment via bank transfer** is accepted and the money is gone.

Fake shops are found via search engines, advertisements on social networks and price comparisons. They often only exist for a few weeks before reappearing under a new name.

## Ten warning signs for a dubious shop

1. **The price is far too low.** If a product costs 300 euros everywhere and 89 euros here, that's not a bargain, but a warning signal.
2. **In the end, only advance payment remains.** Many fake shops initially show several payment methods, but only bank transfer works when ordering.
3. **The imprint is missing or strange.** Real shops in Germany must have a complete imprint with address, authorized representatives and contact details. Missing information, an address abroad with no connection to the shop or just a contact form are suspicious.
4. **The internet address does not match.** A shop for garden furniture at an address like `schuhe-billig-24.shop` or an address that is similar to a well-known shop with small differences.
5. **Fake quality seals.** Seals such as “Trusted Shops” or “TÜV” are sometimes just images without a link. Click on it: A real seal leads to the certificate on the seal provider's website.
6. **No or only perfect reviews.** Search outside the store for experiences. If they are missing completely or there are lots of warnings, stay away.
7. **Incorrect texts, missing general terms and conditions or cancellation instructions.** Legal texts are often poorly copied or missing.
8. **Print by countdown counter** and notices such as “Only 2 pieces left” for a large number of products at the same time.
9. **The bank details do not match.** A shop based in Germany, but an account abroad or in the name of a private individual.
10. **No customer account, no order confirmation**, or the confirmation comes from a freemail address.

## Check stores before purchasing

- **Fake shop finder from the consumer advice center:** There is a free tool on verbraucherzentrale.de that automatically checks Internet addresses for typical characteristics of fake shops. Enter the address and you will receive an estimate.
- **Warning lists:** The consumer advice centers and police publish warnings about known fake shops.
- **Search Engine:** Enter the store name with terms like “experiences” or “scam”.
- **Review portals:** Pay attention to many different reviews over a longer period of time.
- **Age of the domain:** Fake shops are often only a few weeks old. This can be checked using so-called Whois queries, but this takes some practice.

## The right payment method: your most important protection

The choice of payment method determines whether you will get your money back in an emergency.

| Payment method | Protection for you | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Purchase on account** | very high | You only pay when the goods are there. |
| **Direct debit** | high | You can return direct debits for up to eight weeks without giving a reason. |
| **PayPal with buyer protection** | high | Only for “Goods and Services”, not for “Send Money to Friends”. |
| **Credit Card** | high | In the event of fraud or non-delivery, you can request a chargeback through the bank. |
| **Instalment purchase / Klarna & Co.** | medium to high | Depending on the provider with buyer protection; pay attention to interest and costs. |
| **Advance payment by bank transfer** | very low | A transfer can hardly be retrieved. Only from well-known, reputable shops. |
| **Voucher cards, crypto, cash transfer** | none | Anyone who has to pay like this almost always has to deal with fraudsters. |

**Rule of thumb:** For unknown shops, only pay with invoice, direct debit, PayPal buyer protection or credit card. If a shop only offers payment in advance, buy somewhere else.

### Pay securely with your card

When paying with a credit card or debit card online, you usually also have to confirm the payment in your bank's app. This is called “strong customer authentication” and is mandatory in the EU. Always check **amount and recipient** in the confirmation. If it says a different name or a much higher amount, cancel.

## Classifieds and marketplaces

You usually buy from private individuals on platforms such as classifieds, marketplaces in social networks or Vinted. There are special stitches here:

- **The Overpaying Buyer:** A supposed buyer sends a link to a “secure payment” where you are asked to enter your card details “to receive the money”. This is fraud. You never have to enter card details to receive money.
- **The shipping trick:** A seller wants to negotiate outside the platform via WhatsApp and prepayment via bank transfer or “PayPal to friends”. This means you lose all protection.
- **Fake Payment Confirmations:** A buyer sends a screenshot of a supposed payment and asks you to ship immediately. Always check your account to see whether the money has actually been received.

If possible, use the platform's **payment function**, which often offers its own buyer and seller protection. For larger amounts, the safest option is **handover on site** with cash or immediate transfer at the meeting, in a busy place and preferably in pairs.

## Your rights when purchasing online

### 14 day right of withdrawal

If you order from a retailer online, you can usually cancel the purchase **within 14 days** of receiving the goods **without giving reasons**. Important:

- The revocation must be **clearly explained**, for example by email or via the shop's form. Simply returning the goods is not always enough.
- The retailer must refund you the purchase price and original shipping costs. He only has to pay the **return shipping costs** if he has offered to do so or has not informed you correctly about it.
- There are exceptions, for example, for sealed hygiene products after opening, custom-made goods, quickly perishable food or unsealed software.
- There is no right of withdrawal for purchases from **private individuals**.

### Two year warranty

A **statutory warranty of two years** applies to new goods from the dealer. If the goods are defective, the dealer can repair or replace them. If this doesn't work, you can withdraw from the contract or reduce the price. In the first year it is assumed that the defect was already present when purchased.

A **guarantee**, on the other hand, is a voluntary commitment from the manufacturer and is in addition to the warranty.

## If something goes wrong

### The goods don't come

1. Check the shipment tracking and ask the shop.
2. Give the retailer a **deadline for delivery** in writing, approximately ten to fourteen days.
3. If nothing happens, withdraw from the contract and demand your money back.
4. For PayPal or credit card: Open a **dispute** or request a **chargeback**.

### You fell for a fake shop

1. **Inform bank immediately** and attempt to recall the transfer. For direct debit: return. For credit card: request a chargeback.
2. **Report** to the police, also via the online watchdog. Take screenshots of the shop, order confirmation and proof of payment with you.
3. **Report the shop** to the consumer advice center.
4. **Change password** if you have created a customer account with a password that you also use elsewhere.

## General tips for safe shopping

- **Type in the shop address yourself** or access it via a bookmark instead of clicking on links in advertising or emails.
- **A separate password for each shop**, preferably from the password manager.
- Use **Guest Order** if you only purchase something once. Then less data is stored.
- **Do not permanently save payment data** in shops that you rarely use.
- **Check account statements regularly** to quickly notice any unusual debits.
- **Be suspicious of package notifications via SMS**. The best way to track shipments is in the parcel service’s app.

## Subscription traps and hidden costs

Not all problems when buying online come from fraudsters. Sometimes they are serious-looking offers with hidden conditions:

- **Trial subscriptions** that automatically convert to a paid subscription after 14 or 30 days. Note the end of the probationary period on your calendar and cancel in good time if you do not want to continue.
- **Cancellation button:** Since 2022, providers in Germany with whom you can conclude a contract online must also offer a clearly visible **cancellation button**. So you no longer have to look for a postal address.
- **Pre-selected additional services** such as insurance or premium shipping. Check the shopping cart and summary before submitting.
- **Customs and taxes** for orders from countries outside the EU. For several years now, import sales tax has been charged on almost all shipments from outside the country, and parcel service fees are often added. A low price can quickly become more expensive.
- **Very long delivery times** for marketplaces with dealers from the Far East. If a product doesn't arrive for another six weeks, the cancellation period isn't a problem, but returning it can be expensive and complicated.

Before buying on large marketplaces, read whether you are buying from the marketplace itself or from a third party and where they are located. This determines how easy it is to return and guarantee in an emergency.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are shops with a .de address automatically reputable?**
No. .de addresses are also used for fake shops, and sometimes they are even expired addresses of formerly reputable shops.

**Does the lock icon in the browser help?**
It just shows that the connection is encrypted. Fake shops also have encrypted pages.

**How do I recognize purchased reviews?**
Many reviews in a short time, similar wording, only five-star reviews without details or reviews that don't fit the product at all are suspicious.

## Conclusion

Shopping safely online means above all: Be suspicious of prices that are cheap, check the imprint and reviews and test unknown shops using the consumer advice center's fake shop finder. Only pay with new dealers by invoice, direct debit, PayPal buyer protection or credit card, never by advance payment or voucher card. And if something does go wrong, act quickly: call the bank, file a report and use your rights of cancellation and warranty.
