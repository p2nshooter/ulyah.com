The cell phone falls into the water, is stolen on the train or simply doesn't start one morning. It's always annoying, but it only gets bad when photos of children, pictures from the wedding or the last voice messages of a loved one disappear with the device. A smartphone can be replaced, but memories cannot. The good news: Setting up a reliable backup only takes half an hour and then runs itself. This guide will show you what options there are and how to combine them sensibly.

## What a backup actually is

A backup is a **copy** of your data in a different location than the original. This sounds obvious, but is often confused. If your photos are only in the cloud and you have deleted them on your phone, the cloud is no longer a backup but the only storage location. If something goes wrong there, for example because your account is blocked or you accidentally delete an album, the pictures are gone.

Equally important: **Synchronization is not a complete backup.** If you delete a photo on your phone, a synchronized cloud will usually delete it too, although often only after waiting in the trash. A real backup, on the other hand, also retains data that you accidentally deleted.

## The 3-2-1 rule simply explained

Experts recommend the so-called 3-2-1 rule. It sounds technical, but is easy to implement:

- **3 copies** of your important data: the original on your phone and two backups.
- **2 different storage types**, for example cloud and hard drive.
- **1 copy away from home** so that a fire, water damage or break-in doesn't destroy everything at once. The cloud fulfills this point automatically.

For most households this means: **Automatic cloud backup plus occasional copy to an external hard drive.** That's all you need.

## What should be secured

Not all data is equally important. Focus on what cannot be recovered:

1. **Photos and videos** – usually the most valuable.
2. **Chats and voice messages** from messengers.
3. **Contacts and Calendar** – most people have them in their Google account or iCloud anyway.
4. **Documents**, such as scans of contracts, ID cards or prescriptions.
5. **Credentials** in a password manager and **Recovery codes** for two-factor logins.
6. **Notes** and voice recordings.

You don't have to back up the apps themselves; they can be reinstalled at any time. Only the data in it is important.

## Way 1: System backup in the cloud

### iPhone with iCloud

The iPhone automatically backs up when you turn on **iCloud backup**:

1. Open *Settings > [your name] > iCloud > iCloud Backup.*
2. Enable “Back up this iPhone.”
3. The backup runs daily when the iPhone is locked, connected to power and on WiFi.

Below the switch you can see when the last backup was created. If there is a date that is weeks ago, something is wrong and the memory is often full.

iCloud only offers 5 GB for free, which is almost never enough for backups and photos. For most people, a 50GB or 200GB subscription is necessary; With family sharing, storage can be shared.

### Android with Google

Back up Android devices via Google account:

1. Open *Settings > Google > Backup* or *Settings > System > Backup.*
2. Enable Google One Backup.
3. Tap “Backup Now” to start the first backup.

App data, call history, contacts, settings and SMS are backed up. Photos and videos run separately via Google Photos. Google offers 15GB for free, shared between Gmail, Google Drive and Google Photos. Samsung devices also have their own backup via the Samsung account.

## Way 2: Photos automatically to the cloud

There are separate services for photos that automatically upload every new image:

- **iCloud Photos** on iPhone: *Settings > [your name] > iCloud > Photos > Sync this iPhone.*
- **Google Photos** on Android and iPhone: Tap your profile picture in the app, then activate *Photos Settings > Backup*.
- Other providers include OneDrive from Microsoft, Dropbox, Amazon Photos (for Prime members) or European services such as pCloud or your mail provider's storage.

When using Google Photos, pay attention to the **backup quality**: “Original quality” saves the photos unchanged, “Save storage space” compresses them slightly. For souvenir photos, original quality is the better choice if there is enough space.

It is also important that **videos** are backed up and that backup via mobile data is allowed if necessary if you are rarely on WiFi. Check the app regularly to see if “Backup Complete” is displayed.

## Way 3: Backup to computer or hard drive

A local copy independent of any cloud service is the best addition. It costs a one-time fee for a hard drive and protects you if your account is blocked or the service is discontinued.

### Photos from iPhone to computer

- **Mac:** Connect your iPhone and open the Photos app. Select the iPhone in the sidebar and click Import.
- **Windows:** Connect iPhone, unlock it, and tap Trust. Then open the Windows Photos app and choose Import. Alternatively, you can find the images in Explorer under “Apple iPhone > Internal Storage > DCIM”.

You can create a complete iPhone backup including settings and messages on Mac in Finder and on Windows with the “Apple Devices” app. Enable “Encrypt local backup” so that passwords and health data are also secured.

### Photos from Android phone to computer

Connect the smartphone via USB, pull down the notification bar and select “File Transfer”. On the computer, the device appears like a drive. The photos are in the “DCIM > Camera” folder, screenshots are usually under “Pictures > Screenshots”, WhatsApp images are under “Android > media > com.whatsapp”. Copy the folders to the hard drive.

### The external hard drive

For photos, an external hard drive or SSD with 1 to 2 TB is sufficient. SSDs are more robust and faster, hard drives are cheaper per gigabyte. A few tips:

- Do not store the hard drive **directly next to the computer** so that it is not lost in the event of water damage or theft.
- Arrange the photos in **folders by year and month**, something like “2026 > 2026-10”. This way you can find them even without special software.
- Renew the copy **every one to three months** and put an appointment in your calendar.
- Hard drives don't last forever. After about five years, it's worth getting a new one and copying everything onto it.

## Way 4: Secure Messenger Chats

Chats are not always fully captured by the system backup. The major messengers therefore have their own backups:

| Messenger | Where the fuse is | Setting |
|---|---|---|
| Whatsapp | Google Drive (Android) or iCloud (iPhone) | *Settings > Chats > Chat Backup* |
| signal | locally on the device or encrypted by the provider, depending on the version | *Settings > Backups* or *Settings > Chats* |
| Telegram | Chats are in the Telegram cloud, except secret chats | no setting necessary |
| Threema | Threema Safe for contacts and settings, data backup for chats | *Settings > Backup* |

With WhatsApp you can **end-to-end encrypt** the backup with your own password or a 64-digit key. Then no one but you can read the backup. But keep your password or key safe, because without them the backup cannot be restored.

## Test recovery

A backup that cannot be restored is worthless. At least once a year you should check whether everything is working:

1. **Cloud Photos:** Open Google Photos or iCloud.com in the browser on your computer and see if the pictures from the last few weeks are there.
2. **Hard Drive:** Randomly open some photos and videos from the hard drive.
3. **System backup:** Check the date of the last backup in the settings.
4. **Messenger:** Check when the last chat backup was created and how large it is.

The best test is the next device change: If you set up a new smartphone from the backup, you can see immediately whether everything is there.

## Typical backup errors

- **Cloud storage is full.** Then the backup stops, often without any noticeable warning. Check the free memory regularly.
- **The backup only runs on WiFi, but you're rarely on one.** Then hundreds of photos pile up on the device.
- **Photos are deleted from your phone after uploading, and the cloud is the only copy.** Then be sure to add a local backup.
- **The password for the cloud account is forgotten** or the two-factor login is stuck on the lost phone. Provide a recovery email, phone number and print out the recovery codes.
- **Family photos are only on one device.** Create a shared album or save the photos of all family members on a shared hard drive.

## A simple plan for every household

1. **Today:** Turn on automatic photo backup in iCloud or Google Photos and check if there is enough storage.
2. **Today:** Enable system backup and set up chat backup in Messenger.
3. **This week:** Get an external hard drive and copy all the photos onto it once.
4. **Every three months:** Copy new photos to the hard drive.
5. **Once a year:** Open cloud and disk samples and check recovery codes.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the cloud secure enough for private photos?**
The major providers protect their data centers very well. The greater risk is usually a weak password. Therefore, protect your cloud account with a long, unique password and two-factor login.

**How much memory do I need?**
Look in your photo app to see how big your media library is and plan about half of that extra for the next few years.

## Conclusion

A good backup is invisible until you need it. With automatic cloud backup for photos and system data, your own chat backup and a regular copy to an external hard drive, you can meet the 3-2-1 rule without much effort. It is crucial that you check whether the backup really works. Then a lost or broken cell phone is just a nuisance and no longer a catastrophe.
