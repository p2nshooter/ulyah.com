“Storage almost full” – this message often appears at the most inopportune moment: when taking photos at a birthday party, downloading a ticket or when an important update is pending. But a full smartphone is not just annoying. If less than about ten percent of the memory is free, many devices become noticeably slower, updates fail and some apps crash. In this guide, you'll find out what's taking up the space, how to clean up safely, and how to prevent it from happening again in three months.

## Step 1: See what's taking up the space

Before you randomly delete, get an overview. Both systems show you exactly which areas need the most space.

- **iPhone:** *Settings > General > iPhone Storage.* At the top you'll see a colored bar with Apps, Photos, Media, Messages and System. Below, the iPhone lists all the apps by size and often makes suggestions straight away, such as “Move unused apps”.
- **Android:** *Settings > Storage* or *Settings > Battery and device maintenance > Storage*, depending on the manufacturer. Many devices also have the “Files” app from Google with a “Cleanup” section.

There are usually three things that fill the memory: **photos and videos**, **messenger data** and **individual large apps** such as games, streaming apps with downloads or navigation apps with offline maps. The system memory, which often appears large in the overview, can hardly be influenced.

## Step 2: Photos and videos – backup first, then clean up

Photos and videos are the biggest chunk for most people. A minute of video in 4K resolution can take up several hundred megabytes, and a long video from a school concert can quickly take up several gigabytes.

### Back up first

Never delete photos until you are sure there is a copy. You have several options for this:

1. **Cloud storage** such as iCloud Photos or Google Photos upload images automatically. Before deleting, check the app to make sure the sync is complete.
2. **Computer:** Connect the smartphone via cable and copy the folders with photos and videos to the hard drive. On the iPhone you can do this via the Photos app on the Mac or the Photos app on Windows.
3. **External hard drive or USB stick with USB-C:** Many smartphones can copy files directly to a stick.

It's safest to have your most important photos in at least two places, such as the cloud and a hard drive at home. We explain how to set this up in our instructions for backing up photos.

### Use the optimization functions

If you use a cloud service anyway, the smartphone can keep the originals there and only save smaller versions locally:

- **iPhone:** *Settings > Photos > Optimize iPhone Storage.* The full resolution is not loaded until you open an image.
- **Google Photos:** The Free Up Storage feature only deletes photos on your device that are already backed up.

Note: You need enough cloud storage for this. The free quotas fill up quickly; a subscription is usually required for larger media libraries.

### Clear out carefully

A lot of space can be gained even without the cloud:

- **Screenshots:** Many people have hundreds of them that no one needs anymore. Photo apps usually have their own album for this.
- **Burst photos and duplicates:** The iPhone displays duplicate photos under *Albums > Duplicates* and can merge them. On Android, the “Files” app helps you find similar images.
- **Large Videos:** Sort by size or view the Videos album. Often it is enough to move a long video to the computer.
- **Empty “Recently Deleted”:** Depending on the system, deleted photos remain in the trash for 30 to 60 days and continue to take up space. Only when you empty this folder will the memory be free.

## Step 3: Clean up Messenger

WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram and other messengers often automatically save photos, videos, voice messages and documents you receive to the device. In active family or club groups, several gigabytes can quickly be accumulated.

### Whatsapp

1. Open *Settings > Storage and data > Manage storage.*
2. Above you can see large files and files that have been forwarded multiple times. Below are your chats, sorted by size.
3. Tap a chat and select specific videos or files to delete.

Under *Settings > Storage and data > Automatic media download* you can specify that videos and documents are no longer downloaded automatically. Photos via Wi-Fi are usually fine, but you're better off only loading videos when necessary.

On the iPhone, you can also prevent WhatsApp images from being saved twice: Switch off the “Save to Photos” option under *Chats* in WhatsApp if you don’t need the images in the media library.

### Other messengers and SMS

On Signal, you can find storage management under *Settings > Data and storage > Manage storage*. There you can also set old messages to be automatically deleted after a certain period of time. On the iPhone, SMS and iMessages can be automatically removed after 30 days or a year under *Settings > Messages > Keep Messages*. Check beforehand whether there are messages in it that you want to keep.

## Step 4: Apps and app data

### Remove or outsource rarely used apps

Go through the app list in Storage View and ask yourself for each large app: When was the last time I opened it? Games you haven't played in months and apps for one-off occasions like a festival or trip can go.

The iPhone offers an interim solution: **Outsource the app.** The app itself is deleted, but your data and settings are retained. If you reinstall the app later, everything will be as before. The iPhone can do this automatically under *Settings > App Store > Outsource unused apps*. Many Android devices also automatically archive rarely used apps if desired.

### Downloads in streaming apps

Music, podcasts, series and audio books that you have downloaded for on the go often take up a lot of space. Open Spotify, Netflix, Audible or your streaming service's app and delete old downloads. Many apps have a setting that automatically removes finished episodes.

### Clear cache

Many apps create a buffer, the cache, so that they can display images and content more quickly. For browsers and social networks, it can grow to several hundred megabytes.

- **Android:** *Settings > Apps*, select app, then *Storage > Clear cache.* Do **don't** select “Clear data” or “Clear storage,” otherwise you will completely reset the app and have to log in again.
- **iPhone:** There is no general cache function here. Some apps have their own option in their settings. For Safari, go to *Settings > Apps > Safari > Clear history and website data*. Otherwise, the only solution is to delete the app and reinstall it.

The cache fills up again over time. Clearing the cache is therefore more of an emergency solution when things have to be done quickly than a permanent strategy.

## Step 5: Files, Downloads and Offline Maps

The “Downloads” folder contains PDFs, tickets, invoices and installation files that you once needed. On Android, you can find it in the Files app or My Documents; on iPhone, you can find it in the Files app under On My iPhone, or in iCloud Drive.

**Offline maps** in Google Maps or other navigation apps can also be several gigabytes in size. Delete maps of regions you are no longer traveling to. And if you have loaded language packs for translator apps, it's worth taking a look to see if you still need them.

## What you shouldn't do

- **Don't install "cleaner" apps with panic messages.** Apps that advertise red warnings like "Your phone is 87% full of trash" often display ads, collect data, and sometimes do more harm than good. The built-in tools of Android and iOS are completely sufficient.
- **Do not delete system files.** If you don't know exactly what a folder contains, leave it alone.
- **Don't reset to factory settings just to make room.** This only makes sense if the device has serious problems, and then only after a full backup.

## Expand storage: memory card and cloud

Some Android smartphones still have a slot for a microSD card. You can store photos, videos and music on it. Memory cards are less suitable for apps because they are slower than the internal memory. When buying, look for a class U3 or A1/A2 card from a well-known manufacturer.

iPhones and many newer Android devices do not have a card slot. This is where the cloud or a USB-C stick stays for occasional backups. The next time you buy a device, it is worth considering whether the slightly larger storage variant is cheaper than cloud storage that has been paid for for years.

| Memory size | For whom it is enough |
|---|---|
| 64GB | Only just for very economical use with cloud photos |
| 128GB | Most people who clean up regularly or have photos in the cloud |
| 256GB | A lot of photography and filming, games, offline music |
| 512GB and more | Anyone who films a lot in 4K or uses their smartphone as their main camera |

## This way the memory remains free

A few habits will help so that you don't end up in front of a full device every few months:

1. **Set up automatic photo backup** and use the optimization function.
2. **Limit automatic downloads in messengers**, especially for videos.
3. **Once a month** set aside five minutes to review your storage: delete screenshots, empty the Recently Deleted folder, and remove old downloads.
4. **Adjust video resolution:** For everyday videos, 1080p with 30 frames per second is often sufficient. You only need 4K with 60 images for special recordings. You can find the setting in the camera settings.
5. **Regularly move out** or delete old apps.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is the memory full again even though I just deleted?**
Most of the time, the files are still in the trash, for example under “Recently Deleted” in the Photos app or in the trash in the Files app. Only when you empty it will the space become free.

**What is “Other” or “System Data”?**
This includes caches, logs, updates and temporary files. The range often fluctuates and shrinks on its own after a reboot or system update.

**Will my chats be deleted if I clear WhatsApp's cache?**
On Android, Clear Cache doesn't delete chats. “Clear Storage” or “Clear Data”, on the other hand, removes all local data. Therefore, back up your chats beforehand using WhatsApp’s backup function.

## Conclusion

A full cell phone memory can almost always be solved without losing data if you proceed in the right order: first check what is taking up space, then back up photos, clean up messengers and downloads and move out rarely used apps. With automatic photo backup, limited Messenger downloads and five minutes of care per month, your smartphone stays fast and ready for the next photo.
