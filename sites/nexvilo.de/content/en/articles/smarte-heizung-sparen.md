Heating is the largest energy consumer in most households, far ahead of lights, refrigerators or televisions. At the same time, a lot of heat is wasted: the heating is on in the office when everyone is out, the bedroom is warm during the day even though no one is in it, and when airing, the radiator heats up against the open window. This is exactly where **smart radiator thermostats** come into play. They heat according to a schedule, detect open windows and can be controlled from the sofa or while on the move. This guide explains how they work, what they realistically achieve and how to use them correctly.

## How a smart thermostat works

A conventional thermostat on the radiator has a dial with levels from 1 to 5. There is a sensor in it that expands when it gets warm and closes the valve. This works, but it doesn't know anything about times, open windows or your absence.

A **smart thermostat** replaces this head. It has a small motor that opens and closes the valve, a temperature sensor and a radio connection. This means it can:

- **heat according to schedule**, for example warm in the morning and evening, cooler at night and during the day,
- **detect open windows**, either by a sudden drop in temperature or via a window contact, and then close the valve,
- **controlled via app and voice**,
- **react to presence**, for example lowering when the last smartphone leaves the apartment,
- **Show consumption and temperature history** so you can see when and where you heat.

## How much you can save with it

Manufacturers often advertise savings of up to 30 percent. In practice, the savings depend heavily on how you have heated up until now:

- Anyone who consistently turns down the thermostat when they leave the house saves little extra.
- Anyone who has previously left the heating running evenly all day can save significantly, especially in rooms that are only used intermittently.

As a rule of thumb: **Each degree less in room temperature saves about six percent of heating energy.** A schedule that only warms rooms when they are in use makes a big difference. Consumer advocates cite realistic savings of around 8 to 15 percent, depending on the building, heating and usage behavior.

With a thermostat price of 30 to 70 euros per radiator, the devices often pay for themselves after one to three heating periods.

## Which temperatures make sense

Smart thermostats only help if the temperatures are selected sensibly. Recommendations from energy consultants and consumer advice centers:

| space | Recommended temperature |
|---|---|
| Living room, study | 20°C |
| Children's room | 20-22°C |
| Kitchen | 18°C |
| bedroom | 16-18°C |
| Bathroom | 22-23°C when in use |
| hallway | 15-18°C |
| Night setback | around 16-18°C |
| Absence during the day | around 16-17°C |

**Important:** Rooms should not cool down below **16 °C** for a long time. Otherwise, the risk of moisture and **mold** on cold walls increases, and reheating them takes a lot of energy. This is particularly true for old buildings and rental apartments with external walls.

## The systems at a glance

Smart thermostats are available from many manufacturers. More important than the brand is the question of how they connect to your home:

- **FRITZ!Box thermostats (FRITZ!DECT):** If you have a FRITZ!Box, the thermostats connect directly to the router. No additional control center required.
- **Matter and Thread Thermostats:** Work with Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa and SmartThings when a suitable control panel is present. Examples are devices from Eve, Tado or Aqara.
- **Zigbee thermostats:** Work with a Zigbee central unit, such as Philips Hue, IKEA, Homey or Home Assistant.
- **Systems with their own control center:** Homematic IP or Bosch Smart Home have their own base stations that work very reliably and often locally.
- **Wi-Fi and Bluetooth thermostats:** Simple, but often with limitations in range or cloud dependency.

When purchasing, pay attention to whether functions such as presence detection or weather data require a **subscription**. Some manufacturers offer basic functions free of charge and additional functions for a fee.

## Assembly: completed in just a few minutes

Installation is usually easy and unproblematic for tenants because no water has to be drained. The valve remains closed, only the thermostat head is replaced.

1. **Turn the old thermostat to level 5**, so open it completely. Then it fits loosely.
2. **Loosen the union nut** or open the clip that secures the old thermostat and pull it off.
3. **Check valve type.** Most valves in Germany have a thread **M30 × 1.5**. For other valves, such as Danfoss, Heimeier with a different connection or older manufacturers, **adapters** are usually included or are available cheaply.
4. **Put on the smart thermostat** and screw it tight, only hand tight.
5. **Insert batteries.** The thermostat travels the valve path and calibrates itself.
6. **Pair in the app** and assign to the correct room.

**For renters:** Keep the old thermostat and put it back in when you move out. Replacing the thermostat head is usually permitted without the landlord's consent because nothing is changed in the heating system.

## Set up: Schedules that fit your everyday life

The best technology is useless if the schedule doesn't fit. This is how you do it:

1. **Write down your typical daily routine:** When do you get up, when do you leave the house, when do you come back, when do you go to sleep? Distinguish between working days and weekends.
2. **Create a schedule for each room.** The bathroom becomes warm in the morning and evening, the living room from late afternoon onwards, and the bedroom usually stays cool.
3. **Plan ahead.** Depending on the heating system, a room needs 30 to 60 minutes to get from 17 to 20 degrees. Many thermostats learn this themselves and start on time.
4. **Activate window open detection** and test.
5. **Readjust after a week.** Is it too cold in the morning? Does the heating run when no one is there?

### Attendance and geofencing

Many systems can use the location function of the smartphone to detect whether someone is at home. When all residents leave the apartment, the thermostats lower and when the first cell phone approaches, heating resumes. This is convenient for people with irregular schedules. Please note data protection: The app needs access to your location, and all residents should agree.

## Common mistakes that wipe out savings

- **Constantly turning it up:** If you turn the thermostat to 28 degrees when it's cold, you won't get warm any faster. The radiator just heats for longer and overheats the room.
- **Leave doors open:** If the cool bedroom is open to the warm living room, the living room cools down and the humidity in the bedroom increases.
- **Furniture and curtains in front of the radiator:** They block the heat and interfere with the temperature sensor. Many thermostats then allow an external room sensor.
- **Tilt ventilation:** A permanently tilted window cools the wall and wastes energy. Better: ventilate for five to ten minutes three to four times a day with the windows wide open.
- **Too deep subsidence in the old building:** Below 16 degrees there is a risk of mold.
- **Empty batteries:** Replace them before the heating season begins and use the app's warning.

## Interaction with central heating

Smart radiator thermostats regulate how much warm water flows into each radiator. In most cases, the heating system itself, i.e. gas boiler, oil heater or heat pump, is not directly controlled. For even more savings, it's worth taking a look at the system:

- **Hydraulic balancing:** A specialist company adjusts the heating so that all radiators are supplied evenly. This saves energy and prevents some rooms from being too warm and others too cold.
- **Flow temperature:** Many systems run with a flow temperature that is too high. A lowering by a specialist company can save a lot.
- **Heat pumps** work most efficiently with low, consistent temperatures. Strong reductions and rapid heating up make less sense here. Talk to your installer before combining smart thermostats with a heat pump.

## Step by step through the first winter

In order for the thermostats to be effective, it is worth having a small plan for the first heating period:

1. **In the fall:** Install thermostats, check batteries, create rooms in the app and set up schedules based on your everyday life. Write down the gas, oil or district heating meter reading so you can compare later.
2. **In the first two weeks:** Observe whether the rooms are warm at the right time. Adjust the lead times and check whether the open window detection reacts reliably.
3. **In midwinter:** Pay attention to humidity in cool rooms, especially in the bedroom. A small hygrometer or a smart humidity sensor shows whether the humidity rises above 60 percent. Then ventilate more frequently or increase the temperature slightly.
4. **In spring:** Compare consumption with the previous year, ideally adjusted for particularly cold or mild winters. Your heating bill or your provider’s app can help with this.
5. **In Summer:** Many thermostats have a summer mode that leaves the valve open or moves it once a week to keep it from sticking. Activate it when the heating is off.

With this process, after a year you will see in black and white how much the smart control brings to you.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do the batteries last long?**
Usually one or two heating periods. Many thermostats use AA batteries. Batteries do not work on all models because their voltage is lower.

**Can I also operate the thermostats without the app?**
Yes, almost all of them have a dial or buttons directly on the device. This is important for guests and family members without an app.

**Do the thermostats work if there is an internet outage?**
Schedules continue to run locally on most systems. Only the control while on the go is not possible.

**Is it worth it with underfloor heating?**
There are separate smart room thermostats for underfloor heating that are located on the distributor or on the wall. Because underfloor heating is slow, schedules there are less effective than with radiators.

## Conclusion

Smart radiator thermostats are one of the most sensible ways to start a smart home because they bring comfort and measurably save energy. What is crucial is a schedule that fits your everyday life, sensible temperatures of around 20 degrees in the living area and not below 16 degrees in cool rooms, window open detection and correct ventilation. Installation takes a few minutes, is also permitted in rented apartments, and after one to three winters the devices have usually paid for themselves.
