Making transfers on the sofa, checking your account balance during your lunch break, paying a bill directly using a photo: online banking has long been a given for most people in Germany. Bank procedures are much more secure today than they were a few years ago. Nevertheless, fraudsters still manage to withdraw money from other people's accounts. In most cases, they do not crack the bank's technology, but rather use clever tricks to get customers to reveal access data or release payments themselves. If you know the most important tricks and follow a few basic rules, you can protect your account very well. This guide shows how online banking works today, what you should pay attention to and what to do in an emergency.

## How online banking is secured today

Since the European Payment Services Directive PSD2, so-called strong customer authentication has applied to online banking. This means: Two independent features from different categories are required for registration and payments. On the one hand, this is something that only you know, such as your PIN or password. On the other hand, something that only you have, such as your smartphone with the TAN app or a TAN generator. Alternatively, a feature such as your fingerprint or face can also be used.

The idea behind it: Even if a fraudster steals your password, he cannot approve a transfer without the second feature. That's exactly why criminals today mainly try to get you to give permission yourself. Anyone who has understood this has already taken a big step further.

## The TAN procedures at a glance

A TAN is a transaction number that you use to confirm an order. Today, banks use different procedures for this.

| --- | --- | --- |
| App approval (e.g. pushTAN, SecureGo plus, BestSign) | The bank's own app shows the order and you confirm it with your PIN or fingerprint | very common and convenient, safe with protected smartphone |
| photoTAN oder QR-TAN | You scan a colored graphic or a QR code with an app or reader | secure, displays the order details before release |
| chipTAN mit Lesegerät | The bank card is inserted into a small TAN generator, which generates the TAN | very safe because it is independent of the smartphone, but a little more complicated |
| SMS-TAN | Die TAN kommt per SMS | is considered outdated and many banks have abolished it or are restricting it |

No matter which method you use, one rule always applies: **Read what you confirm carefully before each release.** The app or reader displays the recipient IBAN and the amount. If this information does not match what you want to do, stop immediately. Many fraud cases occur because people confirm a release without reading what they are actually releasing.

## The most important basic rules

- **Do not pass on any data:** Your bank will never ask for a PIN, password or TAN via email, SMS, messenger or telephone. Anyone who asks is a fraud.
- **Type in yourself:** Always access your bank's page yourself by entering the address or using a saved bookmark. Never click on links in messages that claim to come from the bank.
- **Official apps:** Only download banking and TAN apps from the official App Store or Google Play Store and check whether the provider is really your bank.
- **Keep devices up to date:** Install updates for operating systems, browsers and apps promptly.
- **Own devices:** If possible, only use online banking on your own devices, not on other people's or public computers.
- **Log out:** Actively log out after banking instead of simply closing the window.
- **Set limits:** Set a daily limit for transfers in online banking that suits your everyday life. You can activate larger amounts at short notice if necessary.
- **Activate notifications:** Many banks report account transactions via push notifications. This way you notice unusual debits immediately.

## Recognize scams

### Phishing via email and SMS

The most well-known scam is fake news that supposedly comes from your bank. They warn of an account suspension, an alleged security check, a new procedure or a suspicious payment and encourage you to quickly click on a link. The link leads to a deceptively real-looking page that asks for access data. Typical warning signs include time pressure, an impersonal salutation, unusual sender addresses and links to addresses that only sound similar to your bank's. If in doubt: delete the message and open online banking via your own app or a bookmark. If there really is a problem, you'll see it there.

### Fake bank employees on the phone

Fraudsters are increasingly calling directly and pretending to be bank employees. Sometimes the bank's real number even appears on the display, as phone numbers can technically be forged. The callers report allegedly suspicious debits and offer to “protect” the account. To do this, you should confirm approval in the app or provide a TAN. What you're actually doing is releasing a payment to the scammers or setting up a new device for your access.

Don't let yourself be put under pressure. Hang up if someone asks you for login details or pressures you to release them. Then call your bank yourself using the number on your card or on the official website. A real bank representative will never ask you to release anything over the phone.

### Fake apps and ads

Scammers sometimes place advertisements on search engines that lead to fake banking sites. Others distribute fake apps or ask you to install remote maintenance software, supposedly for technical support. With such software, perpetrators can remotely control your smartphone or computer. Never install an app at the direction of a caller or access the bank site through ads in search results.

### Classifieds and real-time transfers

When buying or selling via classifieds portals, fraudsters try to process payments outside of the secure payment function or lure you to fake payment sites that ask for card details. Real-time transfers are particularly tricky because the money reaches the recipient within seconds and can no longer be retrieved. Since October 2025, banks in the euro area have been checking whether the recipient name entered matches the IBAN when making transfers. If your bank shows you a message saying that your name and account do not match, you should not make the payment until you have verified the information using another method.

## Secure your device and banking app

Because the smartphone today is often both a banking device and a TAN generator, it deserves special protection.

- Secure your smartphone with a PIN of at least six digits or with fingerprint and facial recognition.
- Use your own password for the banking app that you don't use anywhere else. A password manager helps with this.
- Avoid unlocking the system by root or jailbreak, as this will undermine important protection mechanisms. Many banking apps refuse to work on such devices anyway.
- Activate the device location and wipe function so that you can react quickly if it is lost.
- It is better to use mobile data on public WiFi. The connection to the bank is encrypted, but additional risks lurk in foreign networks.

## If something did happen

If you suspect that fraudsters have accessed your data or that you have provided incorrect authorization, every minute counts.

1. **Block access:** Call your bank immediately or use the central blocking emergency number 116 116, which blocks cards and online banking for many banks. The number is available 24/7.
2. **Check payments:** Let the bank tell you which transactions can still be recovered. Normal transfers can sometimes be stopped if they have not yet been carried out.
3. **Make a report:** Make a report to the police, in many federal states also online via the internet watchdog. The notification is often a prerequisite for the bank to consider a refund.
4. **Change passwords:** Change your online banking and email account credentials, especially if you entered them somewhere.
5. **Check device:** If you have installed a third-party app or remote maintenance software, remove it and, if in doubt, have the device reset.

The following generally applies to liability: If you make a payment that you did not authorize, the bank usually has to refund the amount. However, if you have acted with gross negligence, such as passing on a TAN to a stranger over the phone or confirming a release without reading the order displayed, the bank can refuse the refund. Whether there is gross negligence depends on the individual case. In the event of a dispute, the consumer advice centers or the arbitration boards of the banking associations can help.

## Retrieve direct debits

Direct debits can also be misused, for example if fraudsters use your IBAN for an order. You can return a direct debit that you issued yourself within eight weeks without giving a reason. For a direct debit that you never issued, you have up to 13 months. It is therefore worth checking your account statements regularly, at least once a month.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is online banking safer in the browser or in the app?**
Both are safe if you use the official app or the address you type in yourself and your device is up to date. The app has the advantage that you don't accidentally end up on a fake site.

**Should I use the banking app and TAN app on the same smartphone?**
Most banks allow this and the procedures are designed to accommodate this. If you want to be particularly careful, use a separate device for approvals, such as a chipTAN generator.

**What do I do if I clicked on a phishing link?**
If you haven't entered any data, nothing usually happened. Once you have entered your login details, change your password immediately and inform your bank so that they can monitor or block your account.

**Can I save my banking password in the browser?**
A password manager with a strong main password is better. Passwords stored in the browser are convenient, but less secure when other people have access to the device.

**How do I recognize a real bank app?**
In the App Store, check the name of the provider, the number of reviews and the link on the official website of your bank. Many banks link their apps directly on their website.

## Conclusion

Online banking is safe when you stay in control. Strong customer authentication protects your account well as long as you do not give out any data and only grant approval for orders that you have initiated yourself. Before each confirmation, read carefully what is displayed, only open your bank via the app or bookmarks and simply hang up on calls with time pressure. Set daily limits, activate notifications and keep your devices up to date. If something does happen, block it immediately via your bank or the blocking emergency number 116 116 and report it. This way your money stays where it belongs.
