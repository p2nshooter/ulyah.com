Unboxing a new smartphone is a nice moment until the setup begins. Suddenly the device asks for accounts, passwords, privacy settings and whether you want to transfer data from the old device. Anyone who hastily clicks through here will later be annoyed by missing photos, lost chats or dozens of notifications. With these instructions you can set up your new smartphone in peace and quiet, whether Android or iPhone, and make the settings that will help you most in everyday life.

## Preparation: What you should prepare before switching on

A good setup begins before the new device is even turned on. Prepare the following:

- **The old smartphone**, fully charged and with a current backup.
- **Your access data**: the password for your Apple ID or Google account, for your WiFi and for important apps such as online banking. If you use a password manager, you have most of your passwords there.
- **Your mobile provider's SIM card or eSIM information**. With an eSIM, depending on the provider, you need a QR code or the provider's app.
- **A charging cable** and a nearby socket. The data migration may take a while.
- **Time:** Plan on one to two hours, or more if there are a lot of photos.

### Prepare the old device

1. Install the latest updates on the old smartphone.
2. Create a current backup: on the iPhone via iCloud or the computer, on Android via the Google Account under *Settings > System > Backup* or the manufacturer's backup function.
3. Secure Messenger chats separately if necessary. WhatsApp has its own backup under *Settings > Chats > Chat Backup*.
4. Check whether your two-factor login authentication app can be transferred. This is the point where things most often go wrong. More on that in a moment.

## Step 1: Turn on, voice and Wi-Fi

Press and hold the side button until the logo appears. The setup wizard first asks for language and region and then for Wi-Fi. Connect to your home network so that the data transfer does not run over your mobile data volume.

Now insert the SIM card or set up the eSIM if the assistant asks for it. For many devices, you can transfer the eSIM directly from the old phone if both are from the same manufacturer and your provider supports this.

## Step 2: Transfer data from the old device

The most important part of the setup is the data move. There are three typical ways.

### From iPhone to new iPhone

The easiest way is the **quick start function**: place the old iPhone next to the new one. A message “Configure new iPhone” appears on the old one. Confirm it, use the old device to scan the animation on the new one and enter the code from the old iPhone. You can then choose whether the data is transferred directly from device to device or from iCloud backup. Direct transmission is usually faster and more complete.

### From Android to the new Android device

When setting up, most Android devices offer to copy data from the old device, via cable or wirelessly. Samsung uses “Smart Switch” for this, Google uses its own assistant. Using a cable is faster and more reliable. Apps, contacts, photos, messages, call lists and many settings are usually transferred.

### Switch between Android and iPhone

Switching between systems now also works well: from Android to iPhone with the “Transfer to iOS” app, from iPhone to Android with the Google Assistant or the “Switch to Android” app. Purchased apps cannot be transferred; you simply reinstall most free apps. When switching from iPhone, remember to disable iMessage and FaceTime on the old device.

### Start over instead of transferring

Sometimes it makes sense to start over, for example if your old device was full of apps that you no longer need. Then you set up the new smartphone as a new device and just log in with your account. Contacts, calendars, cloud photos, and passwords will still come back because they're stored in your account. You reinstall apps specifically.

## Step 3: Account, lock and biometrics

### Log in with your account

On iPhone you sign in with your **Apple ID**, on Android with your **Google account**. Some manufacturers such as Samsung also offer their own account. This is optional; it is useful for manufacturer-specific functions such as Samsung's device search, but not absolutely necessary.

### Set up a secure screen lock

Under no circumstances should you skip this step. Screen lock protects your data if the device is lost and is a prerequisite for encryption.

- Choose a **PIN with at least six digits** or a password. Avoid birth dates, 123456 or patterns that are easy to guess.
- Additionally set up **fingerprint or facial recognition**. This makes it easy to unlock and you only need the PIN occasionally.
- Do not write the PIN on the cover or on a piece of paper in your wallet.

## Step 4: Install updates

New devices often already have an update when you unpack them because time has passed since they were manufactured. Check immediately after setup:

- **iPhone:** *Settings > General > Software update.* Enable “Automatic Updates” there too.
- **Android:** *Settings > System > Software update* or *Settings > Software update*, depending on the manufacturer. Additionally, there are Google Play system updates under *Settings > Security and privacy*.

Then update all apps in the App Store or Google Play Store.

## Step 5: Turn on backup

So that you don't lose anything if it is lost or broken, the new device should be backed up automatically right from the start:

- **iPhone:** *Settings > [your name] > iCloud > iCloud Backup.* The backup runs at night when the device is charging and on WiFi.
- **Android:** *Settings > Google > Backup* or *Settings > System > Backup*. Turn on Google One Backup or equivalent.
- **Photos:** Turn on iCloud Photos or Google Photos. Check whether your free storage space is sufficient.

## Step 6: Enable device discovery

If your phone is lost or stolen, you can use Device Finder to locate, lock, or erase it.

- **iPhone:** *Settings > [your name] > Where is? > Find My iPhone.* Also enable Find My Network and Send Last Location.
- **Android:** *Settings > Google > All Services > Find My Device* or *Settings > Security > Find My Device*.

Try the function out on your computer while you have the device in your hand so that you know how it works in an emergency.

## Step 7: Two-factor apps and banking

This is the trickiest part of the move. If you sign in to email, social media, or online banking with an additional code from an app, that app will need to move to the new device.

- **Google Authenticator** can export accounts to a new device via QR code using the Transfer Accounts feature or sync them with Google Account.
- **Microsoft Authenticator** will restore accounts from cloud backup if you have previously enabled it.
- **Password manager with built-in code function** synchronize the codes automatically.
- **Banking apps** and **TAN apps** such as pushTAN, SecureGo plus or the direct bank apps usually have to be re-registered. Depending on the bank, you will need an activation letter, a code by post or a confirmation in online banking.

**Don't delete the old device until all registrations and TAN apps are working on the new one.** Otherwise you may find yourself locked out of important accounts.

## Step 8: Useful settings for everyday life

Now is a good time to adapt the device to your needs:

1. **Notifications:** Go through the list of apps and turn off notifications for anything that isn't important. Games, shopping apps and news portals are otherwise constantly reporting.
2. **Focus or Quiet Times:** Set up a nighttime sleep schedule where only calls from favorites come through.
3. **Location sharing:** Under Privacy, check which apps are allowed to use your location and set it to “When using the app”.
4. **Emergency Passport and Emergency Contacts:** On iPhone in the Health app, on Android in *Settings > Security and Emergency*. This allows helpers to see important information in an emergency without unlocking the phone.
5. **Font size and display:** You can enlarge the font and symbols under *Display* or *Accessibility*.
6. **Default apps:** Determine which browser, mail app and navigation app you want to use.
7. **Charging Optimization:** Turn on optimized charging or charging limitation to ensure the battery lasts for a long time.

## Step 9: Pass on the old smartphone safely

Once everything is working on the new device, you can sell, give away, or recycle the old one. Before you do this you have to reset it cleanly:

1. Sign out of your account: on iPhone under *Settings > [your name] > Sign Out*, this will also display Find My? disabled. On Android, remove the Google account under *Settings > Accounts*.
2. Reset the device to factory settings: on iPhone under *Settings > General > Transfer/Reset iPhone > Erase all content and settings*, on Android under *Settings > System > Reset options*.
3. Remove SIM card and memory card.

Without deregistering, the device remains tied to your account and is unusable for the next owner.

## First day checklist

- Old device backed up and updated
- Data transferred and photos checked
- Screen lock with secure PIN and biometrics
- Updates installed, automatic updates on
- Backup and photo backup active
- Device search switched on and tested
- Two-factor apps and banking work on the new device
- Notifications cleaned up
- Old device deregistered and reset

## Conclusion

If you take the time to set up a new smartphone, you will have a device that is secure, has inherited everything important from the old one and doesn't constantly bother you with unnecessary messages. The key points are complete data migration, secure locking, automatic backups and careful transfer of two-factor and banking apps. You only reset the old device when everything really works on the new one.
