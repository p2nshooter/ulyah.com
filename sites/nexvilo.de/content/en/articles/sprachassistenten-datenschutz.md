“Alexa, what will the weather be like tomorrow?”, “Hey Siri, set a timer for ten minutes,” “Hey Google, turn off the light in the living room”: voice assistants have become a given in many households. They are found in smart speakers, smartphones, televisions, cars and headphones. They are practical, especially when your hands are full or someone has difficulty using the screen. At the same time, there is a device with a microphone in the living room waiting for a word. What is it really listening to? What is saved? And how can you reap the benefits without giving too much away? This guide explains it clearly and shows the most important settings.

## How a voice assistant works

A voice assistant consists of two parts:

1. **The device with microphone** constantly listens for a **wake word**, such as “Alexa”, “Hey Siri” or “Hey Google”. This recognition runs on the device itself. According to the manufacturers, nothing is saved or transmitted unless the word is mentioned.
2. **After the activation word**, the device records your request and in many cases sends it to the manufacturer's servers. There the language is converted into text, understood and answered. For some systems and requests, part of the processing now happens directly on the device.

A light ring or icon indicates that the assistant is listening. For example, on Amazon devices a blue ring lights up, on Google devices dots light up, and on Siri an animation appears on the display.

## What is saved

Depending on the provider and settings, the following are stored:

- **Voice recordings** of your requests,
- the **texts** generated from it,
- **Metadata** such as time, device and location,
- **Interactions** with connected services, such as music, shopping lists or smart home devices.

This data is used to answer queries, improve the assistant and, in some cases, to personalize content and advertising. In the past it was known that all major providers also had people listen to excerpts from recordings in order to improve speech recognition. Since then, all major providers have been able to switch this off.

### Accidental activations

A known problem is **false activations**: The assistant thinks it has heard its activation word, for example a similar-sounding word on TV or in a conversation, and records a few seconds. You can find such recordings in the history and delete them.

## The most important settings on Amazon Alexa

Open the **Alexa app** and go to *More > Alexa Privacy* or *Settings > Alexa Privacy*.

- **View and delete voice recordings:** Under “Check voice recording history” you can delete individual or all recordings.
- **Automatic Delete:** Set recordings to be automatically deleted after 3 or 18 months, or not saved at all. Note that Amazon has now largely moved request processing to its cloud and some local processing options have been eliminated. Check the current settings in the app.
- **Help with improvement:** Turn off allowing voice recordings to be used to improve services.
- **Delete by Voice:** Enable Delete by Voice, then you can say, “Alexa, delete what I said today.”
- **Voice Purchasing:** Under *Settings > Account Settings > Voice Purchasing* you can turn off voice purchases completely or protect them with a **confirmation code**. This is particularly important if there are children living in the household.

## The most important settings on Google Assistant

You can find the settings in your **Google account** at myaccount.google.com > *Data and privacy* or in the **Google Home app**.

- **Web and app activities:** Here you determine whether recordings are saved. Under “Include voice and audio activities” you decide whether audio recordings are saved. Without this option, Google will only save the text of your queries if the activity is active overall.
- **Automatic deletion:** Choose whether activities are automatically deleted after 3, 18 or 36 months.
- **My Activities:** You can view and delete individual requests at myactivity.google.com.
- **Delete by voice:** “Hey Google, delete what I just said” or “Delete everything from this week.”
- **Voice Match:** This allows the assistant to recognize who is speaking and only gives personal results such as appointments to the right person.

Google is gradually replacing the Assistant with the AI ​​assistant Gemini. You can then find the data protection settings under “Gemini Apps Activities”.

## The most important settings for Apple Siri

Apple processes many Siri requests directly on the device and associates requests with a random identifier rather than your Apple ID.

- **Siri Improvement:** You can turn off “Improve Siri and Dictation” under *Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements*. Then no recordings will be saved for enhancement.
- **Clear Siri history:** You can delete the history under *Settings > Apple Intelligence & Siri > Siri & Dictation History* (name depends on iOS version).
- **Siri when device is locked:** You can set whether Siri is also available on the lock screen under *Settings > Apple Intelligence & Siri*. If not, no one can access Siri without unlocking it.
- **HomePod:** In the Home app, you can set for each HomePod whether personal requests such as messages and reminders are allowed and for which people.

## Turn off the microphone

All smart speakers have a **microphone mute button**. When it is pressed, a red ring or a red light usually lights up. According to the manufacturer, the microphone is then disconnected from the hardware and the assistant no longer responds to the activation word.

This makes sense, for example:

- during confidential conversations, for example about health, money or work,
- when there are guests who don't know that an assistant is listening,
- at night in the bedroom,
- if you only use the device to listen to music.

Devices with a **camera**, such as the Echo Show or Nest Hub Max, also have a slider or button to cover or turn off the camera.

## The right location

Where the loudspeaker is located influences privacy and false triggering:

- **Not right next to the TV** so that voices from movies don't trigger it.
- **Not in the bedroom or bathroom** if you want to be undisturbed there. A smartphone is often enough for an alarm clock and music.
- **Away from the front door or an open window** so that no one can give commands from outside, such as "Alexa, open the door" if a smart lock is connected.
- **In children's rooms only with child profile** and purchases switched off.

## Security in smart home commands

If the voice assistant controls smart devices, you should secure sensitive functions:

- **Door locks and garage doors** can only be opened with a **voice PIN**. All major systems offer this.
- **Do not allow alarm systems** to be deactivated by voice.
- **Do not activate cameras** by voice without everyone in the household knowing.

## Family, children and guests

### Children

Children love voice assistants. So that you can handle it safely:

- **Disable purchases via voice** or protect them with a code.
- Use **children's profiles**, such as “Amazon Kids” or children's profiles on Google with Family Link. They filter content and block certain functions.
- **Talk to the children**: A voice assistant is neither a friend nor a keeper of secrets. What you say to him can be saved.

### Roommates and guests

Other people in the household or visitors are also recorded if they use the assistant or accidentally trigger it. Alert guests that an assistant is in the room and, if in doubt, mute the microphone. This is not only polite, but also corresponds to the basic idea of ​​data protection.

## Do you even need a smart speaker?

Many of the functions of a voice assistant are also available on the smartphone that you already have. A smart speaker is particularly worthwhile if you:

- want to hear music and radio in the room,
- you want to control the smart home by voice, for example with your hands full in the kitchen,
- you need a matter and thread controller, which many speakers come with,
- You want to make operation easier for someone in the household, such as a person with visual impairment or limited mobility.

If you don't want a speaker, Matter devices can also be controlled via Apple TV, Google TV devices or independent control centers such as Home Assistant. Home Assistant even offers its own voice control, which can run completely locally without the cloud, but requires some training.

## Checklist: Set up a voice assistant in a data protection-friendly manner

1. Turn off recordings for human enhancement.
2. Set automatic deletion of recordings to the shortest possible time.
3. Delete old recordings completely.
4. Switch off purchases by voice or secure them with a code.
5. Set up a voice PIN for door locks and alarm systems.
6. Do not place speakers next to the television or on the front door.
7. Know the microphone button and use it if necessary.
8. Set up child profiles and voice recognition.
9. Inform guests.
10. Install updates and protect the manufacturer account with two-factor login.

## Language assistants as help in everyday life

Despite all caution, one should not forget how helpful voice assistants can be. People with visual impairments can have messages read aloud, older people can call relatives using voice commands without having to look for a number, and timers can be set in the kitchen without touching their cell phones with wet hands. If you want to take advantage of these advantages, you don't have to forego privacy: with the settings above, the assistant remains a useful tool that only listens when you want it to.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Alexa hear everything I say?**
According to the manufacturer, the device only waits locally for the activation word and only then transmits. False triggering occurs. As you progress, you can check what was recorded.

**Can I avoid recordings completely?**
At Apple and Google you can largely switch off the storage of audio recordings. At Amazon, the options depend on the current settings and devices. The big assistants usually don't work without transmission.

**What happens to my data if I sell the device?**
Factory reset the device and remove it from your account. Then delete the history in the app.

## Conclusion

Voice assistants are practical helpers, but they work with a microphone and often with the manufacturer's cloud. You stay in control with just a few settings: turn off enhancement programs, have recordings automatically deleted, secure purchases, protect sensitive commands with a PIN and mute the microphone during confidential conversations. A well-thought-out location and open words to family and guests do the rest. This means the assistant remains a helper and does not become a silent listener.
