A camera on the front door shows who is ringing the doorbell, even if you are not at home. A camera in the living room reveals whether the dog is lying on the sofa again. A camera in the garden should deter burglars. Smart cameras and video doorbells have become inexpensive and can be set up in minutes. But hardly any other smart home topic is legally as sensitive. Anyone who films incorrectly violates the rights of neighbors, passers-by or roommates and risks warnings, injunctions or fines. There is also the question of IT security: a poorly secured camera can become a window into your home. This guide explains the most important rules and shows you how to use cameras responsibly.

**Important note:** This article provides a general overview of the legal situation in Germany and does not replace legal advice. Courts decide differently in individual cases.

## The basic rule: your property yes, someone else's no

You are generally allowed to film in your own house, in your own apartment and on your own property. But as soon as the camera captures areas that don't belong exclusively to you, things get difficult:

- **Public space:** Sidewalk, street, public parking spaces. Private individuals are generally not allowed to film these areas permanently.
- **Neighboring properties:** Neighbors' gardens, driveways or windows are off-limits.
- **Common areas:** Hallway, stairwell, common courtyard or underground car park in apartment buildings. Here you usually need the consent of all those affected, and in the case of homeowners' associations, a resolution.

As soon as a camera captures public or foreign areas, the **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** applies. Then you need a legal reason, you have to inform about the surveillance and delete recordings quickly. This can hardly be implemented cleanly for private individuals.

### Dummy and swiveling cameras can also cause trouble

Courts have repeatedly ruled that even the **surveillance pressure** can be a violation of the law. If a neighbor has serious concerns about being filmed, perhaps by a camera panning onto their property or by a deceptively realistic dummy, they may be able to request that it be removed.

## This is how you align an outdoor camera correctly

1. **Only record your own property.** Align the camera so that neither the sidewalk nor the neighboring property are in the picture.
2. **Use private zones.** Many cameras have a function that allows you to **black** areas of the image, such as the sidewalk at the edge of the image. These areas are then neither recorded nor displayed.
3. **Limit motion detection.** Set only motion in certain zones to trigger recording.
4. **No sound recording** if not necessary. Secretly recording conversations is a punishable offense in Germany. Therefore, switch off the microphone on outdoor cameras.
5. **Hang up a sign** if visitors, suppliers or tradesmen can come into the picture, for example on the front door.
6. **Save recordings for a short time**, if possible only a few days, and have them deleted automatically.

## Video doorbells: the most common dispute

Video doorbells are particularly popular, but also particularly controversial because they often cover the area in front of the front door and thus the sidewalk or hallway.

### In a single-family home

A doorbell that only shows a brief image when it rings and captures the area directly in front of your door is usually unproblematic. It becomes critical with **continuous recording**, **wide angle** across the sidewalk and **motion detection** that records passers-by. Use private zones and turn off motion recording for public areas.

### In rented apartments and apartment buildings

In an apartment building, a video doorbell almost always records the **common hallway** or the stairwell. The following applies here:

- As a tenant, you usually need the **consent of the landlord** and usually also of the other residents who use the hallway.
- For condominiums, a **resolution from the owners' association** is required.
- Courts have often banned permanent cameras in hallways. A bell that only briefly transmits an image after ringing and does not save anything is more likely to be tolerated.

A privacy-friendly alternative is a **digital door viewer** that only shows an image when you look or the doorbell rings, without recording.

## Cameras in your own home

There are also rules indoors as soon as other people are affected.

### Roommates, partners and family

Anyone who lives with others is not allowed to secretly film them. All residents must be informed about the camera and agree. Cameras in other people's **bathrooms or bedrooms** are an absolute taboo and can be punishable by law. For young people, privacy is also part of it; a camera in a teenager's bedroom is not a good idea.

### Babysitters, cleaners and carers

Anyone who employs people to work in their home is not allowed to secretly monitor them. A camera filming a cleaner or a babysitter is only permitted with **open information and consent**, and even then only within narrow limits. Secret recordings violate personal rights. So talk about it openly and turn off cameras when someone works for you.

### guests

You should also inform visitors. One rule is simple: When guests are there, the indoor cameras go into **private mode**. Many systems can do this automatically when your smartphone is at home.

### Pets and babies

The dog camera in the living room or the baby monitor with video are not a problem as long as only residents who know about it are affected. When it comes to baby monitors with WiFi, it is particularly important to secure them well, as they are in the children's room.

## IT security: So that no one else is watching

A camera is a device with internet access that transmits images from your home. Poorly secured cameras are repeatedly hacked and their images appear on the Internet. How to protect yourself:

- **Choose well-known manufacturers** who regularly provide updates and have a clear data protection declaration.
- **Install updates** for the camera and the app. Enable automatic updates if possible.
- **Change default passwords immediately** and use your own, long password for the manufacturer account.
- **Enable two-factor authentication** for the manufacturer's account.
- Turn on **end-to-end encryption** if the camera offers it. Then the manufacturer cannot see your videos either.
- **Prefer local storage** on a memory card or base station rather than storing everything in the cloud.
- **Place cameras in your own network** or a separate network and avoid port sharing in the router.
- **Cover or turn off camera** when you are home. Some models have a mechanical shutter.

## Cloud or local?

Many cameras offer cloud storage as a subscription. This is practical because the recordings are retained even if a burglar takes the camera. At the same time, pictures from your home are stored on third-party servers.

| Location | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Memory card in the camera | no costs, no cloud | if the camera is stolen |
| Base station or network storage at home | local, well protected | Purchase, some technical knowledge |
| Manufacturer's cloud | convenient, theft-proof | Subscription costs, data from the provider |

If you use the cloud, choose providers with end-to-end encryption and server location in the EU, and set a short storage period.

## Do you even need a camera?

Before you buy a camera, ask yourself what your goal is. There are often alternatives that intrude less on privacy:

- **Burglary protection:** Good locks, window locks and motion detectors with lights are often more of a deterrent than a camera. The police offer free advice on burglary protection.
- **Know who is ringing:** An intercom or digital door viewer without recording is often enough.
- **Package acceptance:** A parcel box or a parking permit when delivering a parcel is more privacy-friendly than a camera.
- **Check windows and doors:** Door and window contacts report whether everything is closed without filming.

## Set up the camera correctly: step by step

Once you've decided on a camera, this order will help you get everything right from the start:

1. **Plan location:** Before mounting, think about which area the camera should show and which not. With the camera app open, hold your smartphone at the planned location and check the image section.
2. **Create an account securely:** Create the manufacturer account with your own email address or alias, a long password from the password manager and two-factor login.
3. **Update firmware:** Install all available updates immediately after setup.
4. **Set privacy zones and motion zones:** Black out everything that isn't part of your property and limit motion detection to the areas that interest you.
5. **Turn sound off** if you don't really need it.
6. **Set storage duration:** As short as possible, often 48 to 72 hours is enough.
7. **Use schedules:** Only switch on indoor cameras when no one is at home, preferably automatically via the presence of smartphones.
8. **Check access:** Who other than you has access to the app? Only share access with people who really need it and remove former roommates or partners.
9. **Attach a notice** if visitors or suppliers come into the detection area.
10. **Check regularly:** Has the camera shifted due to wind or work? Does it still only cover your property?

Also keep a printout or screenshot of your settings. If there is a dispute with neighbors, you can show that you have set up privacy zones and limited the image section to your property.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I hang a dummy camera?**
Dummy devices can also cause trouble if they appear to be aimed at neighboring properties or the sidewalk and thus create surveillance pressure.

**Can I give footage of a burglar to the police?**
Yes, you can hand over recordings that show a crime to the police. However, do not publish it yourself on social networks, as this can violate the rights of the person shown and disrupt the investigation.

**What can I do if my neighbor is filming me?**
First, speak to him kindly. If that doesn't help, you can contact your state's data protection supervisory authority or seek legal advice.

## Conclusion

Cameras and video doorbells can bring security and convenience when properly aligned and well protected. Only film your own property, use private areas, avoid sound recordings and permanent recordings in the apartment building and inform everyone who is affected: roommates, guests, babysitters and cleaning staff. Protect the camera with updates, a strong password, two-factor login and, if possible, local or encrypted storage. And before you buy, ask yourself whether an alternative without a camera might help just as well.
