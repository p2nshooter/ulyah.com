For many young people, an AI assistant has long been as commonplace as a smartphone. Homework, presentations, vocabulary, math: the temptation is to just let the AI ​​do it. Teachers and parents are unsettled; some schools ban AI, others specifically promote it. Artificial intelligence can be an excellent learning companion if you use it correctly: patient, available around the clock and ready to explain the same thing ten times differently. These instructions are aimed at students as well as parents. It shows how AI helps with learning, where the limits are and which rules are important.

## Learn or copy: the crucial difference

An AI can write you an essay about the French Revolution in seconds. The problem: You didn't learn anything afterwards. In the next class test, in your Abitur or in your training you will be sitting there without AI. Additionally, copied AI text often stands out because it sounds different than you, contains errors you can't explain, or doesn't fit the task.

The difference lies in the question you ask:

| Write off | Learn |
|---|---|
| “Write me a discussion about the cell phone ban.” | “Help me collect arguments for and against a cell phone ban and explain to me how a discussion is structured.” |
| “Solve problem 4.” | “Tell me how to approach such a task without telling me the result.” |
| “Translate this text into English.” | "I translated the text. Show me my mistakes and explain them." |
| “Summarize the book.” | “Ask me questions about the book so I can see what I’ve understood.” |

## This is how the AI becomes a tutor

### Let it be explained

If you didn't understand something in class, you can have the explanation given as often and as differently as you need:

> Explain the Pythagorean theorem to me like I was in 8th grade. Use an example from everyday life. Then give me an easy practice task.

If the explanation doesn't help: "Explain it another way, with a drawing in words" or "I don't understand why you square."

### Let queries be made

The AI can query vocabulary, formulas or historical data:

> Ask me 10 English vocabulary words about the environment, one at a time. After each answer, tell me if it was correct.

### Improve your own texts

Instead of having a text written, write yourself and get feedback:

> Here is my introduction to a discussion. Give me three concrete tips on how I can improve it, but don't rewrite it for me.

This is how you learn how a good text works, and your work remains your work.

### Calculate step by step

The Socratic approach helps with math, physics or chemistry:

> I have this equation: 3x + 7 = 22. Guide me to the solution with questions, but don't tell me the result.

Attention: AI assistants occasionally miscalculate. Check results by substitution or using the calculator.

### Plan for exams

> I'm writing a biology paper on cells in two weeks. Make me a study schedule of 20 minutes a day and tell me where to start.

### Prepare presentations

For presentations, AI can help to structure a topic, collect questions and explain technical terms. However, you should check the information itself in textbooks, encyclopedias and reputable websites, because AI assistants sometimes invent facts and sources. Find out more in our guide to AI errors.

## What schools allow

The rules are not uniform in Germany. The Conference of Ministers of Education has made recommendations, but the individual federal states and schools decide for themselves. The following often applies:

- **AI as an aid to learning** at home is usually permitted or even desired.
- **For homework that is graded**, the teacher determines whether and how AI may be used.
- **In specialist papers, presentations and projects**, AI use often has to be stated, for example in a statement at the end.
- **AI is of course prohibited in class tests and exams** unless it is expressly permitted.

**Rule of thumb:** Ask your teacher before using AI for a graded assignment and be honest about using it. Anyone who submits AI texts as their own achievement is deceptive. This can be treated like a cheat sheet.

## Why teachers often recognize copied AI text

AI recognition programs are not reliable. However, teachers often recognize AI texts in other ways:

- The text doesn't sound like you, but rather smooth, general and without any thoughts of your own.
- It contains words or facts that were not used in class.
- It only roughly answers the task.
- If asked, you can't explain what you wrote.

Many teachers now make conscious use of verbal questions in particular. Anyone who has learned for themselves passes this test.

## Data protection and age

According to their terms of use, most AI assistants can only be used **from the age of 13** and with parental consent, some only from the age of 18. There are separate, protected learning opportunities for children, some of which are provided by schools or federal states, for example via school platforms with data protection-compliant AI access. Ask at school if there is such a thing.

The following applies to everyone:

- **Do not enter any personal information**: no full name, no address, no school name, no photos of you or classmates.
- **Don't tell secrets and problems** using other people's names.
- **Your own account** with a secure password and, if possible, without advertising and training with your data.

## For parents: accompany instead of prohibit

A complete ban is difficult to enforce and deprives children of the opportunity to learn how to behave responsibly. Accompaniment is better:

1. **Try it out together:** Sit down together and test what the AI can do and where it makes mistakes. Have them explain something your child knows well and find the mistakes together.
2. **Agree on rules:** What can AI be used for and what not? For example: Explaining yes, writing homework no.
3. **Ask about learning success:** “Can you explain to me what you learned today?” is more effective than any control of the course.
4. **Watch out for companion apps:** Some apps simulate friendships or relationships with AI characters. They can be emotionally addictive. Talk to your child about how an AI is not a real friend.
5. **Be a role model:** When parents themselves approach AI critically, check sources and talk openly about mistakes, children learn best.

## AI competence as a future capability

Whether in training, studies or work: dealing with AI is becoming a basic skill, similar to working with search engines or word processing. Anyone who learns to ask good questions, examine answers critically and use AI as a tool rather than a replacement for their own thinking will be well prepared. At the same time, basic knowledge remains indispensable, because only those who understand something can recognize when the AI ​​is wrong.

## Five learning prompts to try out

You can copy and adapt these templates directly:

1. **Check comprehension:** "I think I understand [topic]. Ask me three questions from easy to difficult and then tell me what I'm missing."
2. **Find errors:** "Here is my solution to a math problem. Just tell me if it's correct, and if not, which step the error is in, but not what the correct answer is."
3. **From big to small:** “Explain [topic] to me in one sentence, then in three sentences, then in a paragraph.”
4. **Mnemonic:** “Help me remember the order of the planets or the states and their capitals with a mnemonic.”
5. **Practice presentation:** "I'm giving a presentation on [topic] tomorrow. Ask me the questions my teacher might ask afterwards."

Try out which type of learning helps you the most. Many people quickly notice that they retain more from the AI's questions than from ready-made summaries.

## Study time and breaks

Even with AI, learning requires repetition and breaks. Short learning units of 20 to 30 minutes with small breaks are usually more effective than cramming for hours in the evening before work. AI can help you make such a plan. You have to comply with it yourself.

## When the AI reveals too much

Sometimes the AI will reveal the complete solution despite your request. Then it helps not to simply accept the result, but to cover it up and calculate or write the task again yourself. Only compare afterwards. You can also tell the AI ​​at the beginning of the conversation: "You are my learning coach. Never give me the finished solution, only tips and questions." Many assistants then stick to this throughout the entire conversation.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it cheating if I let the AI explain something to me?**
No. Having something explained to you is learning, just like with tutoring or an explanatory video. Cheating is passing off someone else's work as your own.

**Which AI is best for students?**
To get started, the free version of the well-known assistants is sufficient, provided the minimum age is reached. Many federal states and schools offer their own data protection-friendly solutions.

**What do I do if the AI says something different than my textbook?**
If in doubt, the textbook is right, or ask your teacher. AI assistants can make mistakes and textbooks are checked.

**Can I use AI for application letters?**
Yes, to help with structure and formulations. However, the cover letter should contain your own experiences and language, otherwise it will seem interchangeable.

## Conclusion

AI can be an excellent learning companion: it explains patiently, asks questions, gives feedback on your own texts and helps with planning. On the other hand, if you let them complete the tasks, you won't learn anything and risk getting in trouble, because copied AI text is often noticeable. Ask your teacher before graded assignments, be honest about AI usage, check facts, and do not enter any personal information. Parents help the most when they try things out together with their children, agree on rules and ask about what they have learned.
