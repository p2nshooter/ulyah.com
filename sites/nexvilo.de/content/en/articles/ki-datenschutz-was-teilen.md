AI assistants are surprisingly good listeners. You tell them about the dispute with the landlord, upload the doctor's note, insert the payslip or ask for help with a sensitive email to the boss. What's easy to forget is that everything you type or upload first ends up on a company's servers. There it can be saved, evaluated and, depending on the settings, even used to train future models. This guide explains what happens to your entries, which data you should never share, how to choose privacy-friendly settings and how you can still use AI for sensitive tasks.

## What happens to your entries

When you ask an AI assistant a question, your text, along with uploaded files, images or voice recordings, is transmitted to the provider's servers. There the model generates the answer. What happens to the data afterwards depends on the provider, your account and your settings:

- **Conversation History:** Most services save your conversations so you can revisit them later.
- **Training:** Many free and private accounts allow conversations to be used to improve future models unless you turn this off.
- **Human review:** Selected conversations can be read by employees or service providers, for example for quality control or to detect misuse.
- **Safety Retention:** Even if you delete conversations, some providers will retain them for a period of time, such as 30 days, to check for abuse.
- **Location:** Many providers process data in the USA. Companies often have contracts with data processing in the EU.

This doesn't mean that someone is specifically reading your conversations. What it does mean is that you shouldn't treat it like a soliloquy.

## What you should never type

This data generally does not belong in an AI chat:

| Data type | Why not |
|---|---|
| Passwords, PINs, TANs, recovery codes | Anyone who knows them can get access to your accounts |
| Account numbers, credit card details | Abuse for payments and fraud |
| ID card, passport and tax numbers | Identity theft |
| Health data with names | Particularly worthy of protection can be assigned to you |
| Confidential company information | Violation of employment contract and trade secrets |
| Other people's data | You didn't agree |
| Intimate photos and private pictures of others | Personal rights, risk of abuse |

Rule of thumb: **Don't include anything you wouldn't write on a postcard.**

## The right settings

With the big providers you can control some things yourself. The menus change regularly, but the options are similar:

### Switch off training

- **ChatGPT:** In the settings under *Data Controls* you will find an option like “Improve the model for everyone”. Turn it off if you don't want your conversations to be used for training. There are also **temporary chats** that do not appear in the history and are not used for training.
- **Google Gemini:** Under *Activities in Gemini apps* you can specify whether conversations are saved and choose an automatic deletion period.
- **Microsoft Copilot:** You can opt out of training with your conversations in your Microsoft account privacy settings.
- **Other providers** such as Claude or Le Chat offer similar switches in their privacy or account settings.

### Clean up history

Regularly delete conversations that you no longer need, especially those with personal content. Many services have a function to delete all conversations at once.

### Check memory functions

Some assistants remember information about you beyond individual conversations, such as your job, family or preferences. This is convenient, but it means that a profile is created. In the settings you can see what is saved, delete individual entries or switch off the function.

### Secure your account

Your AI account may contain very personal conversations. Protect it with your own long password and two-factor login, just like your email inbox.

## Use sensitive documents safely: black out instead of uploading

You often want help with sensitive texts: understanding a letter from the health insurance company, having an employment contract explained, formulating a complaint to the landlord. This also saves data:

1. **Copy only the necessary part.** Instead of the entire document, the paragraph in question is often sufficient.
2. **Replace personal.** Replace names with [name], addresses with [address], contract and insurance numbers with [number], monetary amounts with approximate values ​​if necessary.
3. **Check images.** If you upload a photo of a letter, black out the header, address field and barcodes beforehand, for example using the marking function on your smartphone.
4. **Context rather than details.** “My landlord demands an additional payment of around 600 euros for heating costs” is enough for a good answer, the exact billing with all the names is not.

This means you can get the same help without transmitting any identifiable data.

## Photos and voice recordings

Many assistants can analyze images and process speech. Remember:

- **Photos contain more than you think:** Other people's faces, house numbers, license plates, documents in the background and sometimes location information in the image data.
- **Voice mode:** When you speak to the assistant, audio recordings are transmitted. Pay attention to whether other people in the room are being heard and spoken to.
- **Photos of children** generally do not belong in AI services, not even for image editing.

## AI at work: Be careful with company data

Anyone who uses AI at work should know the employer's rules. Many companies only allow certain, contractually secured AI services or prohibit entering customer data, internal numbers and source code into public assistants. A violation can have consequences under labor law and violate the GDPR. If in doubt, ask the IT department or the data protection officer. Find out more in our guide to AI at work.

## The alternative: AI on your own device

There are now AI models that run **locally** on your own computer or smartphone. Your data does not leave the device.

- **On smartphone:** Newer iPhones and Android devices perform some AI tasks directly on the device, such as notification summaries, text correction, or image editing. Which functions run locally and which go to the cloud can be found in the manufacturer's data protection information.
- **On the computer:** Programs like **LM Studio**, **Ollama** or **GPT4All** make it possible to download open language models and use them without an internet connection. The computer needs enough RAM for this, ideally 16 GB or more, and the models are usually a little less powerful than the large cloud assistants.

For very sensitive tasks, such as merging private documents, local AI can be a good solution.

## What you can pay attention to when choosing a provider

- **Headquarters and data processing:** Does the provider process data in the EU? Is there a clear data protection declaration in German?
- **Training:** Are conversations used for training by default and can this be switched off?
- **Deletion periods:** How long are conversations stored?
- **Business versions:** There are often business versions for work where data is not used for training and is contractually protected.
- **Reputable App:** Download only the official app. Fake AI apps collect data or sell expensive subscriptions.

## Children and young people

Many AI services can only be used by those aged 13 and over with parental consent, and some only by 18. Talk to children about not entering names, addresses, schools, photos or secrets in AI chats, and that a chatbot is not a friend, even if it sounds friendly. Caution is particularly advised when it comes to companion apps that simulate a friendship or relationship.

## An example: Understanding the letter from the health insurance company in a data-efficient manner

Let's assume you've received a notice from your health insurance company and don't understand why an application for cost coverage was rejected. This is how you do it:

1. Read the letter once and mark the section with the reason.
2. Just type this section or copy it from the PDF, without the letterhead, name, insurance number and address.
3. Replace the name of your health insurance company with “my health insurance company” and the name of your doctor with “my doctor.”
4. Ask the AI: "Explain to me in simple words what this reason means, what options I have and by when I have to react. Tell me where I can get independent advice."
5. Check the deadlines mentioned in the original letter and, if necessary, get advice from the Independent Patient Advice Center or the consumer advice center.

The AI ​​has everything it needs for a good explanation, but nothing that can be used to assign the letter to you.

## Also check extensions and apps

In addition to the large assistants, there are countless browser extensions, keyboard apps and additional programs with AI functions that, for example, rephrase text or summarize websites. Some of them read everything you type or see on a page, even in online banking. Only install such helpers from well-known providers, check their permissions and remove what you no longer use.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I request my data from the provider or have it deleted?**
Yes. According to the GDPR you have a right to information and deletion. Most providers have forms or functions for this in the account settings.

**Is it dangerous to discuss personal problems with an AI?**
Many people use AI to organize thoughts. That can help. Just keep in mind that the conversations can be saved and don't give out any identifying details. If you are experiencing serious psychological distress, a person is the better person to contact, such as the telephone counseling service on 0800 111 0 111 or 0800 111 0 222.

**Are paid subscriptions more privacy-friendly?**
Not automatically. The settings and conditions are crucial. Business versions usually offer more protection than private subscriptions.

## Conclusion

What you tell an AI assistant is saved and, depending on your setting, can be used for training or read by humans. Therefore, never enter passwords, account details, ID numbers, health information including names, company secrets or other people's data. Turn off training, delete history regularly, check memory function and protect your account. For sensitive documents: black out instead of uploading, or use local AI on your own device. This way you can get the help of AI without giving up your privacy.
