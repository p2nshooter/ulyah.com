Whether in the office, in the doctor's office, in a craft business or in a club: artificial intelligence has arrived in everyday work. Many people already secretly use AI assistants for emails or summaries, others are unsure whether they are even allowed to do so. Used correctly, AI can take away tedious routine work and free up time for the tasks where people are irreplaceable. If used incorrectly, there is a risk of embarrassing mistakes, data protection violations and trouble with the employer. This guide shows what AI is suitable for in your job, how you can get good results and what rules you should know.

## What AI is suitable for in everyday work

### Draft and revise texts

AI saves the most time when writing:

- **Emails:** a first draft for a cancellation, an appointment confirmation, a query to the supplier or a response to a complaint.
- **Rephrase:** shorten a text that is too long, make a tone that is too direct more friendly, translate technical language in a way that is understandable for customers.
- **Proofreading:** Check spelling, grammar and punctuation.
- **Templates:** recurring texts such as job advertisements, product descriptions or notices.

### Summarize and structure

- **Summarize long documents** into bullet points, such as guidelines, reports or tenders.
- **Order notes:** Turn unsorted keywords into structured minutes after a meeting.
- **Extract task lists** from an email history.

### Collect and prepare ideas

- Outline for a presentation, questions for an employee interview, ideas for a social media post, arguments for and against a decision.
- Preparing for a customer meeting: “What objections might a customer have to a maintenance subscription and how can I address them?”

### Tables and formulas

AI assistants can explain and suggest formulas for Excel or Google Sheets, such as “How do I average only values over 100 in Excel?” They also help with reformatting lists. Always check formulas with an example before relying on them.

### Translate

AI assistants are very helpful for understanding foreign language emails or simple translations into other languages. However, contracts, product safety information or legally relevant texts should be translated or at least checked by experts.

### In craft and in practice

AI also helps outside the office: formulating offer texts, answering customer inquiries, writing an understandable explanation for customers as to why a repair is necessary, or creating a checklist for a workflow. Voice input on the smartphone makes this possible even while on the construction site.

## What AI is not suitable for

- **Final decisions** about people, such as applications, terminations, or creditworthiness. Strict legal rules also apply here.
- **Technical information without verification**, such as legal, tax or medical information provided to customers.
- **Numbers and calculations** that matter, without counter-calculation.
- **Confidential content** in public AI services without employer approval.
- **Texts that are considered your own professional work**, such as reports, without transparent review by you.

## Good results: the workflow

A proven process for professional tasks:

1. **Describe the task clearly:** Who is the recipient, what is the goal, what tone, what length?
2. **Provide context, without sensitive data:** "A customer complains that the delivery was two weeks late. We had delivery problems with the manufacturer. We are offering a 10 percent discount on the next order."
3. **Check draft:** Are all the facts correct? Does the tone suit your company?
4. **Repair:** “Shorter”, “less formal”, “without empty phrases”.
5. **Finish it yourself:** You are responsible for what you send. Read the text completely again before sending.

## Typical mistakes in everyday working life

- **Invented facts:** An AI can invent paragraphs, product data or dates. Check any information that you did not provide yourself.
- **Forgot placeholder:** The draft still contains “[customer name]” or “Dear”. Embarrassing, but common.
- **Generic tone:** AI texts often sound interchangeable, with phrases like “I hope this email reaches you well”. Cross them out and write as you normally write.
- **Too long texts:** AI tends to give detailed answers. Please expressly ask for brevity.
- **Blind trust in formulas:** An incorrect Excel formula can falsify an entire calculation.

## The rules: what is allowed?

### Employers and internal guidelines

Many companies now have **AI policies**. They determine which services may be used, which data is taboo and whether AI content must be labeled. Often there is a released enterprise version, such as Microsoft 365 Copilot or a business version of another assistant, where data is contractually protected and is not used for training.

If there is no policy, ask before entering company information into an AI service. The secret use of private AI accounts for official tasks, often called “shadow AI”, can violate employment contracts and data protection.

### General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)

Personal data of customers, patients, members or colleagues may not be easily transferred to an AI provider. This requires a legal basis and usually an order processing contract with the provider. In everyday life, this means: names, addresses, health data and other personal information remain outside or are replaced with placeholders.

### The EU AI regulation

The EU AI Regulation (AI Act) has been in force gradually since 2024. It has little direct impact on most everyday office applications, but a few points are important:

- Since February 2025, companies that use AI must ensure that their employees have **sufficient AI skills**, for example through training.
- Certain practices are prohibited, such as emotion recognition in the workplace for surveillance purposes.
- AI systems in sensitive areas such as personnel selection are considered **high risk** and are subject to strict requirements.
- AI-generated content must be labeled in certain cases, such as deepfakes.

### Copyright and responsibility

Texts and images generated by an AI are generally not protected by copyright in Germany because they are not human creations. At the same time, an AI can reproduce foreign texts very similarly without being noticed. You or your company are responsible for published content, for example on the company website. Therefore, check advertising texts and images in particular to see whether they could infringe the rights of third parties. More about images in our guide to AI images and rights.

### Transparency towards customers

Whether you need to mark AI support depends on the individual case. But honesty is always a good idea: When a chatbot responds on your website, customers should know that they are talking to a machine. The EU AI regulation prescribes this for such systems.

## An entry-level plan for teams

If you want to introduce AI in your team or company, you can do this:

1. **Clarify what for:** Which tasks take a lot of time and are well suited? Mostly it is texts, summaries and research.
2. **Select service:** It is best to have a business version with a data protection agreement and data processing in the EU.
3. **Write down rules:** Which dates are taboo? Who checks results? How is it marked?
4. **Schools:** A short introduction to good questions, typical mistakes and data protection also fulfills the requirement for AI competence.
5. **Start small:** Start with one or two use cases, gain experience, then expand.
6. **Check regularly:** What's working and what's not? Are mistakes made?

## Three examples from practice

**The doctor's practice:** A practice manager has a shared AI service write a friendly notice about changed office hours and a template for appointment reminders. She never enters patient names or diagnoses. She adapts the texts to the tone of the practice and has them approved by the doctor.

**The craft business:** A master electrician dictates keywords for an order into his smartphone on the construction site. In the office, the AI ​​turns it into an easy-to-read offer text. He enters prices, quantities and technical information from his calculations and checks them, because he is liable for them.

**The club:** The secretary of a sports club has the minutes of the annual general meeting structured from her notes and an invitation to the summer party formulated. Member lists with names and addresses remain outside the AI. Before sending, the board proofreads.

In all three cases, the AI ​​takes over the formulation and ordering, while specialist knowledge, numbers and responsibility remain with humans. It is precisely this division of labor that makes working at work safe and useful.

## Save time without losing quality

Anyone who regularly uses AI at work should occasionally take stock. Are you really saving time, or are you spending it correcting bad drafts? It has proven useful to create a collection of good prompts for recurring tasks, such as appointment confirmations, cancellations or minutes, and share them with colleagues. This gradually creates a consistent tone and no one has to think about how to ask each time. At the same time, it is worthwhile to consciously complete tasks without AI that require experience and sensitivity, such as a difficult conversation with a customer or feedback to a trainee.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can my employer prohibit me from using AI?**
Yes, the employer can specify which tools can be used for work. In companies with a works council, they often have a say in the introduction of AI systems.

**Do I have to indicate that a text was written with AI?**
Usually not in normal business transactions, provided you have checked and taken responsibility for the text. However, science, journalism and certain publications often have their own labeling rules.

**Will AI replace my job?**
AI is changing many activities, especially routine tasks like writing and sorting. Experience, specialist knowledge, judgment and dealing with people remain in demand. Those who learn to use AI sensibly are more likely to gain an advantage.

## Conclusion

At work, AI saves a lot of time, especially when drafting and revising texts, summarizing and structuring notes. Human review remains indispensable for decisions about people, technical information and important figures. Follow your employer's policies, avoid entering personal or sensitive information into public services, and check every text before it leaves your home. Then AI becomes a reliable tool and not a risk.
