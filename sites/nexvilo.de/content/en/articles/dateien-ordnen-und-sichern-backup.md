The desktop is full of icons, the downloads folder contains 800 files with names like “Scan_0047.pdf” and “Document(3).docx,” and last year’s tax documents are somewhere between vacation photos and applications. That's how it is for many people. Organizing your computer not only saves time when searching, it is also the basis for a good backup: If you can't find it, you often don't save it. This guide will show you a simple folder structure that works well in everyday life, rules for file names and three ways to reliably back up your files under Windows.

## Why organizing your PC is worth it

Good order has three advantages:

1. **You find things faster.** Instead of searching, you know where something is.
2. **Backups become easier.** If all important files are in a few folders, you only need to back up those.
3. **Others find their way around.** In an emergency, such as if you are sick or someone needs to settle your estate, relatives can find important documents.

You don't have to clean everything up at once. All you need to do is create a structure and store new files correctly from now on. You can sort old files gradually.

## Step 1: The basic structure

Windows already comes with a few folders: **Documents, Pictures, Music, Videos, Downloads** and **Desktop**. Use them instead of creating your own folders directly on the C: drive. They are quickly accessible in Explorer, and many backup programs back them up automatically.

Within **Documents**, a structure according to areas of life has proven useful. For example:

- **01 Personal** – copies of ID, certificates, certificates, CV
- **02 Finance** – bank statements, taxes, insurance, contracts
- **03 Housing** – rental agreement, additional costs, tradesmen, guarantee documents
- **04 Health** – findings, vaccinations, prescriptions
- **05 Work** or **School**
- **06 Hobbies and Projects**
- **99 Archive** - Completed items you want to keep

The numbers in front of the folder names ensure that the order always remains the same. You only create subfolders where they are necessary, for example under “Finance” for each tax year: “Tax 2025”, “Tax 2026”.

**Rule of thumb:** No more than three to four levels deep. If you have to click through ten subfolders, you will eventually put files back on the desktop.

### Organize photos

For photos, the easiest way is to structure them by year and month: **Images > 2026 > 2026-10 autumn holidays Baltic Sea**. This keeps the folders sorted chronologically, and you can also name them with a keyword. Photo programs such as the Windows app “Photos” still display the images as a timeline.

## Step 2: File names you understand

A good file name reveals what's in the file without you having to open it. This pattern has proven itself:

**Date – Sender or Topic – Content**

For example:

- `2026-10-12 Stadtwerke Nebenkostenabrechnung 2025.pdf`
- `2026-09-30 Krankenkasse Bescheid Zuzahlungsbefreiung.pdf`
- `2026-03-01 Lebenslauf Bewerbung Bibliothek.docx`

A few rules:

- **Date in YYYY-MM-DD format** at the beginning. Then Explorer automatically sorts files chronologically.
- **No special characters** like / \ : * ? " < > |, which Windows does not allow. Umlauts are fine.
- **No words like “new,” “final,” or “final2.”** Instead, use a date or version number like “v2.”
- **Short but clear.** Better “rental agreement at Hauptstrasse 12” than “contract”.

## Step 3: The Downloads Folder and Desktop

The download folder is like a mailbox: everything arrives there, but nothing should stay there permanently. Get into the habit of emptying it once a week: move important things to the right place and delete the rest.

Windows can help you with this: Under *Settings > System > Storage > Storage Optimization* you can set files in the Downloads folder that have not been opened for more than 30 or 60 days to be automatically deleted. Only activate this if you stick to the weekly routine.

The **Desktop** is suitable for things you are currently working on. What is done goes into the filing cabinet. A tidy desktop doesn't make the computer faster, but it makes you more relaxed.

## Step 4: Digitize paper

Many documents are still on paper. You can quickly scan them with your smartphone:

- The **Google Drive app** and the **Microsoft Lens** or **Adobe Scan** apps automatically crop documents and save them as PDF.
- On **iPhone**, you can select “Scan Documents” in the Notes app or Files app.

Immediately name scans according to the scheme above and place them in the appropriate tray. For important originals such as documents, certificates and contracts, keep the paper additionally. Private individuals usually do not have to keep tax-related documents for as long as companies, but it still makes sense to keep receipts for a few years.

## Step 5: Set up a backup

Order alone does not protect against hard drive damage, theft or blackmail Trojans. For this you need a backup. On Windows there are three proven ways that you can combine.

### Way A: File History to an external hard drive

**File History** is built into Windows. He regularly backs up the Documents, Pictures, Music, Videos and Desktop folders to an external hard drive and keeps older versions. If you accidentally overwrite a file, you can restore an earlier version.

1. Connect an external hard drive.
2. Open **Control Panel** (via search in the Start menu) and select *System and Security > File History*.
3. Select the hard drive and click **Power On**.
4. Under *Advanced Settings* you can specify how often backups are made, approximately every hour, and how long versions are retained.

To restore a file, right-click the folder in Explorer and select *Properties > Previous Versions* or open “Restore Personal Files” in File History.

### Way B: Cloud storage with OneDrive or other services

**OneDrive** is built into Windows 11. With **Folder Backup** you can automatically sync your desktop, documents and pictures to the cloud: *OneDrive icon in the taskbar > Settings > Sync and backup > Manage backup.*

Advantages: The files are backed up off-site and available on any device. OneDrive keeps deleted files in the Trash for 30 days and saves older versions of Office documents. With a Microsoft 365 subscription you also get protection against blackmail Trojans, with which you can reset the entire OneDrive folder to an earlier point in time.

Note: There is only 5 GB available for free. For larger amounts of data you need a subscription. Alternatives are Google Drive, Dropbox or European providers.

**Important:** A sync is not a full backup. If you delete a file, it will also be deleted in the cloud, permanently after the trash deadline has expired. That's why you should combine the cloud with local backup.

### Way C: Windows Backup and System Image

The **Windows Backup** app backs up settings, installed apps, passwords and selected folders in your Microsoft account. This makes setting up a new PC easier, but does not replace backing up your files on your own hardware.

A **system image**, i.e. a complete copy of the entire drive, is particularly interesting for advanced users who want to be able to work again quickly in the event of a hard drive defect. There are programs from hard drive manufacturers or specialized backup software for this.

## The 3-2-1 rule for your PC

As with smartphones, the 3-2-1 rule also applies to computers: **three copies** of your data on **two different storage devices**, **one of which is away from home**. For the household this specifically means:

| Copy | Where | Like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Original on PC | Documents, Pictures, etc. folders |
| 2 | External hard drive at home | File History |
| 3 | Cloud | OneDrive or another service |

### Protection against blackmail Trojans

Blackmail Trojans encrypt all files they reach, including on connected hard drives. Therefore:

- **Disconnect the backup hard drive** from the computer after the backup if you don't need it to be connected all the time.
- Use **versioning** in the cloud to go back to a clean version in an emergency.
- Keep Windows and programs up to date and do not open unknown attachments.

## Step 6: Test recovery

Try at least once to see if you can restore a file from the backup. For example, delete an unimportant test file and restore it via File History or the OneDrive Trash. This way, in an emergency, you know how to do it and have the certainty that the backup will work.

## A routine that lasts

- **Daily:** Name and store new files correctly.
- **Weekly:** Clean up download folder and desktop.
- **Monthly:** Check whether File History and Cloud Backup are running. Connect external hard drive if it is not permanently connected.
- **Annually:** Move completed folders to archive, test recovery, check hard drive for errors.

## Find files if something is missing

Even with the best organization, you'll occasionally misplace a file. Windows helps you search:

- **Search in Explorer:** Open the folder you are looking in, such as “Documents”, and type the search term into the search field at the top right. Windows searches file names and, for many formats, also the content.
- **Filter by date:** In Explorer you can sort by “Date Modified” via *View > Details*. Here's how to find the file you edited last week.
- **Recently Used:** The Explorer home page and Office programs show the most recently opened files.
- **Search index:** Under *Settings > Privacy and Security > Search Windows* you can specify that Windows also index other folders, for example on a second hard drive.

If you find yourself searching in the same place over and over again, that's a sign that your folder structure there needs to be adjusted.

## Shared storage for the family

In many households, one person manages the documents for everyone. So that someone still has access in the event of illness or vacation, you can create a shared folder in the cloud and share it with your partner or adult children. Place a brief overview of which contracts, insurance policies and accounts there are and where the documents are located. You should only store sensitive documents such as copies of ID cards in the cloud in a particularly protected area, such as OneDrive's “Personal Vault,” which requires an additional login.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I store files on C: or on a second partition?**
For most people, the user folders on C: are sufficient. A second partition does not protect against a hard drive failure, a real backup does.

**How big should the backup hard drive be?**
At least twice as large as the amount of data you want to back up so that older versions also have space.

## Conclusion

A simple folder structure according to areas of life, file names with a date at the beginning and a weekly emptied download folder are enough to keep your PC permanently organized. Combined with file version history on an external hard drive and cloud backup, you meet the 3-2-1 rule and are well protected against hard drive defects, theft and accidental deletion. Test the recovery once and you'll know that your files are really safe.
