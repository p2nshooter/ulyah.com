A unicorn on a combine harvester in the style of an old oil painting, a logo for the bowling club, an image for the invitation to the summer party: with AI image generators, what would previously have required a graphic designer or hours of image editing can be created in seconds. Programs like the image functions of ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Microsoft Designer and Copilot, Adobe Firefly, Midjourney or Stable Diffusion make this possible for everyone. But questions quickly arise: Can I use the image on my website? Who does it belong to? Can I have myself or other people portrayed? This guide explains how AI images are created, how you can get better results and what you should legally consider.

## How AI image generators work

AI image generators were trained with a large number of images and their descriptions. They learned which image features belong to which words: what a “sunset” looks like, what “watercolor” means, what a “golden retriever” looks like. Many modern generators work according to the **diffusion principle**: They start with random image noise and, step by step, form an image that matches your description.

The result is a new image, not a collage of existing photos. However, because the models have learned from existing works, they can imitate styles and, in rare cases, recreate motifs very similarly.

## Good pictures describe: the building blocks

As with text assistants, the result depends heavily on your description. These building blocks have proven themselves:

1. **Motive:** What should be seen? “A red lighthouse on a cliff”
2. **Settings and details:** “during a storm, waves crashing against the rocks, seagulls in the sky”
3. **Style:** "Watercolor", "Photography", "Pencil Drawing", "Flat Illustration", "3D Rendering"
4. **Light and mood:** “in the evening light”, “dark”, “friendly and bright”
5. **Image detail and perspective:** “bird’s eye view”, “close-up”, “wide angle”
6. **Format:** “square”, “landscape 16:9” for presentations, “portrait” for cell phone backgrounds

A complete example:

> Flat friendly illustration of a community garden in spring, raised beds with vegetables, a watering can, birds, lots of greenery, bright colors, landscape format, no text.

### Tips for better results

- **Text in image** Not all generators work. It's better to add fonts later using a graphics program.
- **Hands and faces** are better, but check details carefully.
- **Create variants** and choose the best one.
- **Repair** with targeted changes: “same image, but without seagulls” or “warmer colors”.
- **Edit instead of recreate:** Many programs can swap parts of an image, expand the background or remove objects.

## What AI images are useful for in everyday life

- **Invitations and cards** for birthdays, weddings, celebrations
- **Presentations** for school, university and work
- **Club posters, flyers and notices**
- **Illustrations for blogs and newsletters**
- **Creative hobbies**, such as templates for painting or ideas for tattoos and interior design
- **Image editing**: Remove backgrounds, restore photos, remove distracting objects

## The legal side

**Note:** This section provides a general overview of the legal situation in Germany and does not replace legal advice. The case law on AI is still evolving.

### Who owns an AI image?

According to German copyright law, only a person's **personal intellectual creation** is protected. An image that an AI creates based on a short description is usually not considered such a creation. That means:

- You usually have **no copyright** to a purely AI-generated image.
- Others are often allowed to use your AI image without you being able to ban it.
- If you do significant further editing of an AI image yourself, protection may arise for your own editing.

What you are allowed to do with an image is also regulated by the respective provider's **Terms of Use**. Many allow private and commercial use, some only with a paid subscription. Read the terms before using AI images for your business or club.

### Rights of others: authors, trademarks, persons

Even if the AI image itself is not protected, it may violate the rights of others:

- **Copyright:** If an AI image closely replicates an existing work, such as a well-known comic book character, a famous photo, or a painting by a living artist, this may be a copyright infringement. Imitating a style is generally permitted, but recreating specific works is not.
- **Trademarks:** Company logos, product designs and well-known characters may not simply be used in advertising or on products, even as AI replicas.
- **Personal rights:** Images that show or recognizably depict real people may generally not be published without their consent. This applies to celebrities as well as to neighbors or colleagues.

### Pictures of you and your family

Many apps offer the ability to turn your own photos into AI portraits, avatars or funny montages. Consider:

- You upload **biometric data**, i.e. your face, to a provider. Check how long he stores the photos and whether he uses them for training.
- **You should not upload photos of children** to such services.
- **Photos of other people** only with their permission.

### Deepfakes and deception

Images that show real people in fictional situations can cause harm as deepfakes. Fake intimate images, defamation and fraud are punishable offenses. Even harmless joke images can violate personal rights if they are published. Find out more in our guide to detecting deepfakes.

## Label AI images

Honesty protects against misunderstandings. Whether you need to label AI images depends on the individual case:

- The **EU AI Regulation** requires that deepfakes, i.e. realistic-looking representations of real people, places or events, be recognizable as artificially created. This obligation takes effect gradually.
- **Social networks** such as Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and YouTube require labeling of realistic AI content and automatically label many of them.
- Many generators embed **invisible watermarks** or **source information** (content credentials according to C2PA).

Regardless of the duty, a quick note like “Illustration: created with AI” is a good habit, especially with realistic-looking images, in club magazines, on websites, and in presentations.

## Fairness towards creative people

AI image generators were largely trained with works by photographers, illustrators and artists, often without their consent. Many creative people view this critically. If you need an image for an important purpose, such as a logo, a book cover, or an advertising campaign, it's worth considering whether a human designer might be a better choice. He can understand your wishes, create a clearly protected work and grant you the rights to it. Some providers, such as Adobe Firefly, advertise that they only train with licensed images.

## Pre-publication checklist

1. Do the generator terms of use allow my purpose?
2. Does the image closely resemble a well-known work, figure or logo?
3. Are real, recognizable people visible and do I have their consent?
4. Could the picture deceive someone? Then label.
5. Have I checked details like hands, text and background?

## An example: The poster for the club festival

Suppose your sports club celebrates its 50th anniversary in June and you are supposed to design the poster. This is how you do it:

1. **Create image:** “Happy, flat illustration of a summer party on a sports field, bunting, barbecue, children playing football, sunshine, portrait format, no text, no recognizable faces.”
2. **Compare variants:** Have three or four versions shown to you and choose the one that best suits the club.
3. **Check details:** Are the proportions correct, are there strange hands, signs with fantasy fonts or logos that are reminiscent of real brands?
4. **Set your own text:** Add the title, date, time and location using a graphics program or template so that everything is spelled correctly.
5. **Use club logo:** The real logo of the club is added as a separate file, not as an AI replica.
6. **Mark:** A small note “Illustration created with AI” in the margin doesn’t hurt.
7. **Check Terms of Use:** Is use on posters and social media allowed with your account?

In this way, an attractive poster can be created in half an hour without violating the rights of others.

## Data protection for image apps

Apps that turn selfies into fantasy portraits are often free and spread quickly. Before uploading, read who is behind the app, where the photos are processed and whether they will be deleted. Reputable providers explain this clearly. If this information is missing, it's better to ignore the app.

## Edit photos with AI

In addition to creating new images, smartphones and image programs offer more and more AI tools for editing: removing distracting people in the background, replacing the sky, sharpening a blurry snapshot or coloring an old family photo. This is wonderful for private memories. However, if you use edited photos as evidence, for example when selling a used car, with an insurance company or in an apartment ad, you are not allowed to change anything that would gloss over the condition. That would be deception.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use an AI logo for my company?**
It's possible, but risky: you usually don't have a copyright on it, so it's difficult to protect it against imitators, and it could be similar to existing logos. For an important company logo, it is advisable to have a designer and trademark research.

**May I create an AI image in the style of a specific painter?**
Imitating a style is generally permitted. However, concrete works may not be recreated, and with living artists, sensitivity is required.

**Are AI images free?**
Many generators offer a limited number of free images, more are available with a subscription. Pay attention to the usage rights of the free version.

## Conclusion

AI image generators make it easy to create illustrations for invitations, presentations and club posters. Good results come from clear descriptions of subject, style, light and format. Legally, you usually have no copyright to pure AI images, the provider's terms of use decide what you are allowed to do, and other people's rights to works, brands and your own image remain. Tag realistic AI images, don't upload photos of children or others without permission, and rely on human designers for important projects.
