The grandchildren wave from across town, the friend shows off her new garden, the team discusses the week, and the family doctor takes time for a short conversation without anyone having to go into the waiting room: video telephony brings people together who would otherwise only talk on the phone or rarely see each other. What was complicated a few years ago now works with almost every smartphone, tablet or laptop. Nevertheless, there are always hurdles. Which app should you use? Why is the image jerky? Why does the other person only hear a noise? These instructions explain step by step how video calls are successful, how the picture and sound can be improved and how you can make it easy for older relatives to get started.

## What you need

For a video call you basically only need three things: a device with a camera, microphone and speaker, an internet connection and an app that both parties use.

- **Smartphone:** Ideal for spontaneous calls on the go. The front camera is good enough in almost all devices today.
- **Tablet:** The larger screen is particularly comfortable for longer conversations and for people who have poor eyesight. With a stand, no one has to hold the device.
- **Laptop or computer:** Well suited for professional conversations, video conferences with many people and when documents need to be shown.
- **Internet:** A stable WiFi is best. Mobile data is also sufficient when on the move if the reception is good.

Headphones with a microphone are not a must, but they often significantly improve the sound quality. Even the simple headphones that come with many smartphones or are available as wireless versions prevent echoes and feedback.

## Choose the right app

The most important rule is: use the app that the people you are talking to already have. The best technology is of no use if the other side has to install and understand it first. The following overview shows the most common options.

| app | Suitable for | Special features |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whatsapp | family and friends | very common, group calls with up to 32 people, end-to-end encrypted |
| FaceTime | Apple users among themselves | Built directly into the iPhone, iPad and Mac, Android and Windows users can participate via a link in the browser |
| signal | Anyone who attaches particular importance to data protection | End-to-end encrypted, collects very little data |
| Google Meet | Meeting via link | runs in the browser without installation, often preinstalled on Android |
| Zoom and Microsoft Teams | Job, clubs, courses and larger groups | Invitation via link, many functions for conferences |

Anyone who previously used Skype will have to switch. Microsoft discontinued Skype in May 2025 and recommends the free version of Microsoft Teams instead. Contacts and chat histories could be transferred there.

## Step by step: the first video call

### With WhatsApp

1. Open WhatsApp and tap the chat with the person you want to call.
2. Tap the camera icon in the top right. The call starts immediately.
3. The first time, the smartphone asks whether WhatsApp can access the camera and microphone. Confirm both.
4. During the conversation you can use the icons below to change the camera, mute the microphone or hang up.

To make a group call, open a group chat and tap the camera icon there, or you can add additional participants during an ongoing call.

### With FaceTime

1. Open the FaceTime app on your iPhone, iPad, or Mac.
2. Tap New FaceTime and enter the person's name, phone number, or email address.
3. Tap the green button with the camera icon.

If you want to invite someone without an Apple device, tap “Create link” and send the link, for example via message. The other person opens it in the browser, enters their name and then you let them into the conversation.

### Via link with Zoom, Teams or Meet

For video conferences, you usually receive an invitation with a link. Clicking on it opens the conference in the browser or app. Often a preview will appear first, allowing you to check the camera and microphone before joining. It's worth opening the link a few minutes before the start. This leaves time if the app needs to be installed or authorization needs to be granted.

## Look good and sound good

With just a few simple steps, a video call becomes much more pleasant, for you and for the person you're talking to.

- **Light from the front:** Sit so that the light, such as from a window, falls on your face. A bright window at your back turns you into a dark shadow.
- **Camera at eye level:** Place your smartphone, tablet or laptop so that the camera is approximately at eye level. A stack of books works wonders. This way no one looks at your face from below.
- **Support the device:** A stand or holder prevents blurry images and is easier on your arms.
- **Quiet environment:** Close windows facing the street, turn off the radio and TV and choose a room with little reverberation.
- **Mute:** In larger groups, mute your microphone when you are not speaking. This means that background noise doesn't bother anyone.
- **Background:** Many apps can blur or replace the background. This is practical if the apartment is not tidy at the moment.
- **Look into the camera:** When you speak, occasionally look directly into the camera instead of at the picture of the person you are speaking to. Then it seems as if you are looking the person in the eyes.

## Set up video calls for older relatives

For many older people, a video call with family is the best reason to use a smartphone or tablet. In order for it to really work, relatives should prepare the device well.

A tablet with a large screen and a sturdy stand is often the best choice because no one has to hold the device and faces can be seen large. Put only the most important apps on the home screen, especially the video calling app. Set the font and volume to large and create family members as favorites.

It helps a lot if the older person only has to answer calls instead of calling themselves. Agree on fixed times, such as Sundays after coffee, so that the device is charged and within reach. The iPhone and iPad also have a setting in the operating aids that automatically accepts calls after a few seconds. This can be a great relief for people with limited mobility, but should only be activated with their consent.

Practice your first call together, preferably on site. A small note next to the device with the sentence “Press the green button to answer” provides security if no one is sitting next to you later.

## Data usage and connection

Video telephony requires significantly more data than a normal telephone call. Depending on the quality, a video call roughly consumes several hundred megabytes per hour. If you often make video calls while on the go, you should have sufficient data volume or, if possible, make calls via WLAN. Many apps offer an option in the settings to use less data on the mobile network, for example WhatsApp under “Storage and data”.

If the picture jerks or the sound breaks off, it is usually due to the connection. Move closer to the Wi-Fi router, close other apps that are loading data, and ask other people in the household not to stream large videos during the conversation. If that doesn't help, turn off the camera briefly. The sound then usually continues to run more stable.

## Security and privacy

Video calls on WhatsApp, FaceTime and Signal are end-to-end encrypted. This means that only the other party can see and hear the image and sound, not the provider. When using video conferences via link, you should also pay attention to a few points.

- Share invitation links only with the people you really want to attend and not publicly on social networks.
- Use a waiting room or a password at your own conferences so that no one barges in without being asked.
- Before the conversation, check what can be seen in the background, such as letters, photos or notes with personal information.
- Only record conversations if everyone involved agrees.
- Be suspicious if strangers contact you via video call. Fraudsters are increasingly using video calls for blackmail or to fake emergencies.

## Video consultation with the doctor

Many doctors' practices and psychotherapists now offer video consultations, for example for diagnostic reviews, follow-up appointments or consultations. The practice will send you a link to a certified video service. For those with statutory health insurance, the health insurance company will cover the costs as for a normal appointment. The same tips apply to the video consultation as to any other video call: quiet room, good light, stable connection. Also have your insurance card, medication list and questions ready.

## Common problems and quick solutions

- **The other person doesn't see me:** Check whether the camera is turned on in the app and whether the app has access to the camera in the device's settings.
- **The other person can't hear me:** Check the microphone icon in the app. If headphones are connected, the sound may come through their microphone.
- **There is an echo:** Most of the time the sound from a participant's speaker comes back into the microphone. A pair of headphones or a lower volume helps.
- **The picture is dark:** More light from the front, window not in the back.
- **The call doesn't arrive:** Check that both sides are connected to the internet and that the app is updated.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does video calling cost anything?**
The common apps are free. Only the internet connection costs apply. There are usually no additional costs for Wi-Fi at home.

**Can I video call someone on Android from an iPhone?**
Yes, for example with WhatsApp, Signal or Google Meet. FaceTime also works via a link that the Android person opens in the browser.

**How many people can participate at the same time?**
With WhatsApp there are up to 32 people. Services like Zoom, Teams or Meet are suitable for much larger groups.

**Does video calling also work on the TV?**
Some smart TVs and streaming devices support video calls with an additional camera. It is often easier to transfer the image from the smartphone or tablet to the television.

**What do I do if the image freezes?**
Wait a few seconds, the connection often recovers on its own. If that doesn't help, turn the camera off and on again or end the call and restart it.

## Conclusion

Video calling is now easier than ever. The key is to choose an app that everyone involved already uses and to prepare the device well: camera at eye level, light from the front, quiet surroundings and a stable internet connection. With a tablet on a stand, large symbols and fixed call times, older relatives can also get started. If you also pay attention to data consumption and privacy, you can enjoy the advantages in a relaxed manner: See people who are far away and be a little closer to them.
