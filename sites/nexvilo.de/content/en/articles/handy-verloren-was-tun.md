I reach into my jacket pocket and there's nothing there. A lost or stolen smartphone immediately triggers panic in most people because it contains photos, messages, banking apps, emails and often access to almost all online accounts. Precisely because there is so much at stake, it helps to proceed calmly and in the right order. This guide will show you what you should do in the first minutes, the first hour, and the days after. It's best to read them before anything happens and prepare yourself with the tips at the end.

## First take a deep breath and think

Many cell phones are not stolen at all, but are left in the car, in the café or between the sofa cushions. Before you lock everything, take two minutes:

- Where was the last time you had the device in your hand?
- Call you from another phone. Maybe the doorbell rings nearby or an honest finder picks up.
- Ask at the store, restaurant or public transport company whose bus or train you were last on.

If that doesn't work, it's time to locate it.

## Step 1: Locate the smartphone

If device search was turned on, you can find your smartphone from any computer, tablet or other phone.

### iPhone

1. Open **icloud.com/find** in your browser or the Find My app. on another Apple device.
2. Sign in with your Apple ID. You usually don't need a two-factor code to search for devices, so you can get in without your phone.
3. Select your iPhone. The map shows the current or last known location.

Even if iPhone is offline or turned off, it can still be found for a while using the Find My network of other nearby Apple devices.

### Android

1. Open **google.com/android/find** in your browser or the Find My Device app on another Android device.
2. Sign in with the Google account set up on the lost phone.
3. The map shows the location. Android devices can now also be found via a network of other Android devices when they are offline.

Samsung users can also use **SmartThings Find** or the Samsung device search.

### What you can do remotely

| Function | When makes sense |
|---|---|
| **Play sound** | The device is nearby, for example in the apartment or in the car. |
| **Mark as lost / Back up device** | The device is somewhere else. It will be blocked and you can display a message with a callback number. Apple Pay and Google Wallet will be disabled. |
| **Delete device** | You don't expect to get it back or it contains very sensitive data. |

**Important:** Don't delete the device too quickly. After deleting it, you can no longer locate it on many devices. Mark it as lost first. It can still be deleted, even later.

**Never go alone to a location where you suspect thieves.** Instead, give the location to the police.

## Step 2: Block the SIM card

With your SIM card, a thief can make expensive calls, but above all, receive SMS messages that are sent for registrations and confirmations. So call your mobile phone provider and have the SIM card blocked. You can find the number on the provider's website or on your contract. Many providers also offer the blocking in the customer account online.

You keep your phone number: You get a new SIM card or eSIM with the same number, usually within a few days or immediately in the shop.

If you have an **eSIM**, the same applies: have it blocked by the provider and transferred to a new device.

## Step 3: Protect banking and payment functions

Modern banking apps are well protected, but you shouldn't take any risks.

- If you are afraid that someone knows your unlocking PIN or if you have bank cards in your phone case: **Call the blocking emergency number 116 116.** It is available around the clock and blocks bank cards, credit cards and, at many banks, online banking access. From abroad you can reach him on +49 116 116.
- Contact your bank if a TAN app was installed on your cell phone. The bank can unregister the device.
- **Apple Pay** and **Google Wallet** will be blocked when marked as lost. You can also remove the stored cards in your Apple or Google account.

In the next few days, check your bank statements carefully for any unusual debits.

## Step 4: Change the most important passwords

If the phone was stolen, and especially if the screen lock was weak or missing, you should change the most important passwords. Start with the accounts you use to get to other accounts:

1. **Apple ID or Google Account** - this can be used to control other devices and services.
2. **Email inbox** – almost all services use this to reset passwords.
3. **Messengers** like WhatsApp, Signal or Telegram – set it up on a new device, then the session on the old device will end. Enable two-step verification with a PIN.
4. **Social networks, online shops and payment services** such as PayPal.

In the security settings of many accounts, you can also see which devices are logged in and **log out** the lost device. On Google you can find this under “Security > Your Devices”, on Apple under “Devices” in the Apple ID settings.

## Step 5: Report to the police

In the event of theft, you should report it, either at the police station or, in many federal states, online via the police's **online watch**. To display you need:

- the device's **IMEI number** (a 15-digit identification number),
- brand, model and color,
- place and time of loss,
- If necessary, the last location from the device search.

You can find the IMEI on the original packaging, on the invoice, in the contract or in your Apple or Google account in the device overview. You also need the notification for insurance.

If you just lost your cell phone, ask your city's lost and found office. Many cities have an online lost property office, and public transport companies maintain their own lost property offices.

## Step 6: Insurance and contract

- **Cell phone insurance:** If you have it, report the damage within the deadline, which is usually a few days. Read the conditions beforehand: Many insurance companies only pay in the event of theft under certain circumstances, such as robbery or burglary, but not in the event of simple loss.
- **Home contents or travel insurance:** Some contracts cover smartphones in the event of burglary.
- **Paying in installments with the provider:** If you pay off the device via your mobile phone contract, the installments will continue even if the device is gone.

## Step 7: Set up new device and get data back

If you have a replacement device, set it up with your account and import the latest backup. Photos from iCloud or Google Photos, contacts, calendars and many app data come back this way. You can restore Messenger chats using the respective chat backup.

The biggest hurdle is often **two-factor logins**, where the codes were generated on the lost cell phone. Help here:

- **Recovery codes** that you received during setup and hopefully printed out,
- an **authentication app with cloud backup** that can be restored to the new device,
- the respective service's **account recovery process**, which may take a few days.

With banking and TAN apps, you usually have to re-register the new device through the bank.

## Checklist: Cell phone away

1. Call and ask about places you've been
2. Open device search and check location
3. Mark as lost and leave a message with a callback number
4. Block SIM card with provider
5. If you suspect: Block emergency call 116 116, inform the bank
6. Change passwords for Apple ID/Google, email and messenger
7. Deregister old device from all accounts
8. Report it or ask the lost property office
9. Inform insurance
10. Set up a new device and import a backup

## This way you are prepared

Most steps are much easier if you have done a few things beforehand:

- **Turn on device search** and try it out on the computer.
- **Use secure screen lock** with at least six-digit PIN.
- **Enable Anti-Theft**: Stolen Device Protection on iPhone, Anti-Theft on Android.
- **Set up automatic backups** for system, photos and chats.
- **Note down the IMEI** and keep it in a safe place along with your mobile phone provider's hotline, such as in your password manager or on paper in your important documents folder.
- **Print recovery codes** for important accounts.
- **An emergency card** in your wallet: name, a contact number and the number 116 116.
- **Set a lock screen message** such as "Found? Please call:..." with a family member's number. On many Android devices this can be done under *Lock screen > Add text*; The emergency pass helps with the iPhone.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a thief just reset my phone and resell it?**
On iPhones and Android devices with device discovery turned on, an activation lock prevents this. After the reset, the device asks for the password of the original account. Without this password, the device is largely worthless to thieves, which makes theft less attractive.

**What if the phone is turned off?**
Then the device search usually shows the last known location. Newer iPhones and Android devices can still be found via the network of other devices for a while, even when they are switched off. Get notified as soon as the device is back online.

**Should I threaten the thieves via the lock screen message?**
No. A friendly message with a callback number increases the chance that an honest finder will get in touch. Everything else is a matter for the police.

**Do I have to inform my contacts?**
If messenger or email accounts were open, a quick warning to family and friends makes sense. Scammers like to use stolen accounts to solicit money in the victim's name.

## Conclusion

If your smartphone disappears, the order counts: first locate it and mark it as lost, then block the SIM card, protect banking and change the most important passwords. This is followed by notification, insurance and setting up a new device. Anyone who has previously set up device searches, theft protection, backups and recovery codes will survive the loss with a shock, but without lasting damage.
