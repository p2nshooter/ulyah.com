Anyone looking for a new smartphone is faced with a wall of numbers: 200 megapixels, 120 Hertz, 8 GB RAM, 5,000 mAh, octa-core. Dealers and manufacturers outdo each other with data sheets that often mean little in everyday life. At the same time, the information that is most important to you is often missing from advertising: how long the device will receive updates. This buying guide will help you separate the important features from the unimportant ones and find a smartphone that fits your everyday life without paying for features you never use.

## Step 1: Clarify what you use the smartphone for

Before you compare data sheets, answer a few questions:

- **What do you do most?** Phone calls and messages, social networks, photos, navigation, games, work?
- **How ​​long do you want to use it?** Two years, four years or as long as possible?
- **How ​​important is the camera?** Do you take photos occasionally or is your phone your only camera?
- **What's your budget?** Set a limit before you go to the store.
- **What devices do you already have?** A Mac, a smartwatch or a tablet influence the choice of system.

For most people, an upper middle class device between 350 and 600 euros offers almost everything you need in everyday life. Top models are particularly worthwhile for very ambitious photographers, frequent gamers or those who simply want the best.

## Step 2: Updates – the most important information

A smartphone without security updates becomes a risk over time: gaps are no longer closed, and some apps such as banking apps no longer work at some point. That's why the **update period** is the first number you should look for.

- Experience has shown that Apple provides iPhones with updates for five to seven years.
- Google and Samsung promise up to seven years for many current models.
- Other manufacturers often offer three to five years, and sometimes less for cheaper models.

An ecodesign regulation has also been in force in the EU since June 2025: Manufacturers of new smartphones must provide operating system updates for at least five years after a model ends sales and offer spare parts for many years. This improves the situation, but does not replace looking at the concrete commitment.

**How ​​to check:** On the manufacturer's website, search for "update policy" or "security updates" and the model name. Calculate from the release date, not the purchase date. A model that has already been on the market for two years has correspondingly less remaining life.

## Step 3: Battery and Charging

The battery capacity is given in milliampere hours (mAh). More is generally good, but not everything: an efficient processor and an economical display often bring more than a large battery. For a day of intensive use, around 4,500 to 5,000 mAh is usual for Android devices; iPhones get by with a little less thanks to efficient coordination.

When testing, pay attention to the **measured running time** in everyday life, not just the number in the data sheet. Further points:

- **Charging speed:** 25 to 45 watts are completely sufficient in everyday life. Very high values ​​over 100 watts are a nice extra, but not a must.
- **Power supply:** Many manufacturers no longer include one. If you don't have a suitable USB-C power supply, include it in the price.
- **Wireless charging:** handy if you want a charging station on your bedside table. This is often missing from cheap devices.
- **Charging Limit:** A feature that stops charging at 80 percent significantly extends battery life.

## Step 4: Storage and RAM

### Internal memory

You shouldn't skimp on storage space. Apps, photos and videos are getting bigger and a full phone becomes slow.

| Storage | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| 64GB | Too scarce today, only for very basic use |
| 128GB | Minimum for most people |
| 256GB | Recommended if you take a lot of photos or want to use the device for a long time |
| 512GB and more | for 4K videos, many games or offline media |

Check if the device has a **microSD slot**. This is still the case with some Android models and enables inexpensive extensions for photos and music. iPhones don't have one.

### Main memory (RAM)

The RAM determines how many apps run smoothly at the same time. For Android devices, 6 to 8 GB is a good value today, 12 GB and more are primarily needed by gamers or those who want to use functions with artificial intelligence on the device. For iPhones, the RAM specification hardly plays a role in the purchase because Apple does not advertise it and the system works very efficiently.

## Step 5: Display

The display is what you look at all day. Three points are important:

- **Technology:** OLED screens offer strong colors, true black and save power in dark mode. LCDs are cheaper and can now be found primarily in entry-level devices.
- **Brightness:** If you often use your cell phone outside, a high peak brightness is important so that you can see something even in the sun.
- **Refresh rate:** 90 or 120 Hertz makes scrolling smoother. Once you're used to it, you rarely want to go back. But it is not a must.

When it comes to **size**: Large displays are comfortable for videos and reading, but cumbersome in your pocket and with one hand. Hold devices in your hand in the store before you decide. Compact models have become rare, but they still exist.

## Step 6: Camera – the megapixel trap

The megapixel count says little about the image quality. A 50 megapixel sensor from a good manufacturer often delivers better photos than 200 megapixels from a cheap device. More important are:

- **Size of the main sensor** and light intensity of the lens, which make the difference, especially in low light,
- **Image processing** by the manufacturer,
- an **optical image stabilizer** (OIS) to prevent blurry photos and videos,
- a real **telephoto lens** for zoom, if that's important to you,
- a usable **ultra wide angle camera** for landscapes and interiors.

Additional “macro” or “depth” cameras with 2 megapixels, as are often installed in inexpensive devices, are mostly marketing and are hardly usable. Compare sample photos in independent testing, especially nighttime and indoor shots.

## Step 7: Robustness, repair and equipment

- **Water Protection:** IP67 or IP68 certification protects against rain, dust and a short immersion. It is rarer on cheaper devices.
- **Frame and Glass:** Metal frames and tempered glass are sturdier than plastic. A cover and a protective film are still useful.
- **Repairability:** Since June 2025, every new smartphone in the EU has had an **energy label** with information on battery life, robustness in the event of drops and a repairability index from A to E. Pay attention, it helps when comparing.
- **Features:** NFC for contactless payment, 5G, good vibration, stereo speakers and a reliable fingerprint sensor or facial recognition belong in almost every price range today. A headphone jack has become rare; If you need it, check specifically.
- **Dual SIM and eSIM:** practical for work and private use or for travel.

## Step 8: Where and how to buy

### With or without a contract?

A smartphone as part of a mobile phone contract seems cheap because you only make a small one-off payment. But do the math: monthly basic fee times term plus one-off payment, minus the price of a comparable tariff without a device. It is often cheaper to buy the smartphone separately and choose a cheap tariff without the device.

### New, last year's model or completely overhauled?

- **Previous year's models** are often 20 to 40 percent cheaper and hardly worse.
- **Refurbished devices** from reputable dealers have been tested, have a new or tested battery and a warranty. This protects the environment and your wallet.
- **Private purchase:** cheapest, but without warranty. Check the condition, battery and, above all, that the device is no longer linked to the previous owner's account.

### Rights when purchasing

When purchasing from an online shop, you usually have a right of withdrawal within 14 days in the EU. The statutory warranty is two years. Save the invoice as you will also need it for warranty and insurance.

## Information you can safely ignore

- **The number of processor cores.** “Octa-core” says nothing about actual performance.
- **Megapixels over 50.** See above.
- **“AI camera” and “AI functions”** as advertising terms without a concrete explanation.
- **Benchmark records.** Even mid-range processors are fast enough for everyday life, photos and social networks.
- **Included apps and games.** They are often advertising and cost storage space.

## The checklist for the store

1. How many years of updates are there from today?
2. At least 128 GB, better 256 GB of storage?
3. How long does the battery last according to independent tests?
4. Does the device fit comfortably in your hand and fits in your pocket?
5. How are photos in low light?
6. Is there water protection, NFC and a good unlocking method?
7. What does the EU energy label say about batteries and repairability?
8. Is a power supply included or do I have to buy one?
9. Is the overall price cheaper without a contract?

## Frequently asked questions

**When is the best time to buy?**
Shortly after a new model is introduced, the prices of its predecessors usually drop significantly. Even a few months after their market launch, the prices of new Android devices often fall noticeably.

**Do I need 5G?**
Almost all new smartphones support 5G. It is especially important for you if your tariff includes 5G and the network is well developed in your locations. It makes sense for a longer service life.

**Is a device with a pen or foldable display worth it?**
For people who do a lot of note-taking, drawing or working on a large screen, yes. Foldable devices are expensive and more sensitive; for most people, a classic smartphone is the better choice.

## Conclusion

The best smartphone is not the one with the biggest numbers, but the one that gets long updates, has enough memory, gets through your day with the battery and fits well in your hand. If you first pay attention to the update period, memory and battery and ignore advertising terms such as megapixel records, you will find a device in the upper middle class that will work reliably for many years. A previous year's model or a refurbished device may be the wisest choice.
