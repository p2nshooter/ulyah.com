Hardly any technology question is discussed as passionately as this one: Android or iPhone? In many families there are staunch supporters of both camps, and anyone who buys new gets a different recommendation from each. The honest answer is unspectacular: Both systems are now sophisticated, safe and suitable for everyday use. Which one suits you better depends less on the technology than on your habits, your budget and the devices you already own. This decision-making aid goes through the most important points soberly.

## The fundamental difference

The **iPhone** comes exclusively from Apple. Hardware, operating system (iOS) and app store come from a single source. This ensures a very consistent experience: Every iPhone operates the same, updates arrive for all models on the same day, and accessories usually fit without any guesswork.

**Android** is an operating system from Google that many manufacturers use: Samsung, Google itself with the Pixel devices, Xiaomi, Motorola, OnePlus, Nothing, Fairphone and many others. Each manufacturer adapts the interface, includes its own apps and decides for itself how long it will provide updates. This means there is a huge selection, from very affordable entry-level devices to top models, but also larger differences between the devices.

## Price: selection against value retention

On Android you can find smartphones in every price range. Solid devices with a good screen and a decent camera are available for just 200 to 300 euros. New iPhones start significantly higher, older models and the cheaper variants are usually in the mid to upper price range.

On the other hand, iPhones lose value more slowly on the used market. If you sell your device after three years, you will often get a decent amount back for an iPhone. The price of many Android devices falls more quickly, which in turn makes good used purchases possible.

**So don't just calculate the purchase price**, but also how long you want to use the device and what it will ultimately earn you.

## Updates and security

Updates are one of the most important points because a smartphone without security updates becomes vulnerable over time.

- **iPhone:** Experience shows that Apple provides its devices with system updates for five to seven years and sometimes even longer with security updates. All supported models receive updates at the same time.
- **Android:** This depends heavily on the manufacturer and model. Google and Samsung now promise up to seven years of updates for their current top and many mid-range models. For cheap devices from other manufacturers it is sometimes only two to four years. Since mid-2025, an EU regulation has also required manufacturers of new smartphones to provide security updates for several years after sales end.

**Tip:** Before buying an Android device, check the manufacturer's website to see when the model will receive updates. In the end, a cheap device that is only supported for another year is not a good choice.

When it comes to malware, both systems are well protected as long as you install apps from the official stores. Android allows apps to be installed from other sources, which means more freedom but also more risk. Alternative app marketplaces are now also possible in the EU on the iPhone, but they play a small role in everyday life.

## Operation and adjustment

The iPhone is considered particularly easy to use. The interface is clear, settings are in the same place on all devices, and many processes work without thinking. A lot can now be customized, such as the lock screen, widgets and the arrangement of apps, but Apple sets limits.

Android offers more freedom: different start screens, own standard apps for almost everything, deeper access to settings and files. Those who like to try things out appreciate this. Those who simply want a phone that works often don't need this freedom. Especially with manufacturers like Samsung or Xiaomi, the menus are more extensive and sometimes more complex than on an iPhone.

For older people or beginners, there are simplified modes on both sides, such as “Easy Mode” on Samsung or “Assisted Operation” on the iPhone. Find out more in our guide to smartphones for seniors.

## The Ecosystem: What you already own

An often underestimated point is which devices you already use.

- **Do you have a Mac, an iPad or an Apple Watch?** Then an iPhone shows its strengths: photos, messages, passwords and notes synchronize automatically, you can answer calls on the Mac, and AirDrop sends files in seconds.
- **Do you work a lot with Windows and Google services?** Android connects well to Windows via the “Smartphone Link” app, and Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Photos and Google Drive are deeply anchored in the system. Many of these services are also available for the iPhone.
- **Smartwatch:** The Apple Watch only works with the iPhone. There are watches from Samsung, Google and others for Android, which in turn do not work with the iPhone or only work to a limited extent.

You can change at any time, but it takes time: purchased apps cannot be transferred and some accessories no longer fit. We describe below how to proceed with the change.

## Data protection

Both systems have significantly improved their data protection in recent years. You can fine-tune app access to location, camera, microphone, contacts, and photos, and see which app accessed what last.

Apple emphasizes data protection heavily in advertising and makes its money primarily from devices and services. Google earns a large part of its revenue from advertising and uses data for this, but also offers extensive settings, for example to switch off personalized advertising or deactivate location history. On both systems, you ultimately decide which apps you install and what rights you give them. This makes a bigger difference in everyday life than the choice of system.

## Camera, battery and performance

When it comes to top models, both camps are at a very high level. There are differences in style: iPhones deliver more natural colors and very good videos, some Android cameras have stronger zoom lenses or more setting options. In the mid-range, the range for Android is wider, from excellent to mediocre.

The battery life depends heavily on the individual model. Many Android devices have larger batteries and charge faster, but iPhones compensate for this with efficient coordination. A look at independent test reports on the specific model is more meaningful than a comparison of the systems.

## Repair and sustainability

If you want to use a smartphone for a long time, you should pay attention to repairability. Batteries, displays and backs will need to be replaced at some point. Apple offers repairs through its own service and authorized partners and also provides spare parts for workshops for many models. When it comes to Android, the manufacturers differ significantly: the Fairphone is particularly easy to repair, while spare parts for other brands are more difficult to obtain. The EU now stipulates that spare parts must be available for new models for many years.

## family and children

If several family members use a smartphone, a shared system can make life easier. Apple's Family Sharing or Google Family Link can be used to manage children's accounts, share purchases, set screen times and share locations. But mixed families are no problem: WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram and other messengers work across platforms, and photo albums can also be exchanged via Google Photos or shared links.

## Five questions for your decision

1. **How much do you want to spend?** Under 300 euros, Android is almost always the better choice. Both are eligible.
2. **Which devices do you already use?** An existing Mac or an Apple Watch speaks for the iPhone, a Windows PC and many Google services are more likely for Android.
3. **How ​​long do you want to use the device?** Then pay particular attention to the update promise of the model.
4. **Do you want to customize or just use?** Hobbyists feel more comfortable with Android, those who want to set as few settings as possible often with iPhone.
5. **Who can help you with questions?** If children, grandchildren or friends help you with technology, it may make sense to use the same system as them.

## If you want to change

Changing is easier than before:

- **From Android to iPhone:** Apple offers the Transfer to iOS app. It transfers contacts, messages, photos, calendars and accounts when setting up the new iPhone.
- **From iPhone to Android:** When setting up an Android device, Google provides a wizard that transfers data via cable or via the Switch to Android app. Important: Deactivate iMessage in the iPhone settings beforehand, otherwise text messages from other iPhone users will be lost for a while.
- **WhatsApp chats** can be transferred between both systems and the app guides you through the process.
- **You have to check purchased apps and subscriptions**: Apps have to be purchased again on the new system, subscriptions such as music or video streaming usually continue via the provider's account.

## New, last year's model or used?

Regardless of the system, you don't always have to buy the latest model. The previous year's model is often significantly cheaper and hardly worse, and it usually gets updates for many years to come. Used or refurbished devices from reputable dealers are also a good choice if you keep a few things in mind:

- **Check update period:** A three-year-old device should receive at least two to three years of security updates.
- **Inquire about battery status:** For iPhones it is in the settings, for Android devices you can ask the dealer. Below 80 percent you should consider replacing the battery.
- **Warranty:** Refurbished devices from dealers have a guarantee of at least one year in the EU, often more. This is usually not the case when buying privately.
- **Exclude account lockout:** Show that the device has been factory reset and from Find My? or the previous owner's Google account is logged out. Otherwise you can't use it.

So you can often get a better device for the same money, whether it's an apple or a green robot.

## Conclusion

There is no objectively better system, just the one that is more suitable for you. The iPhone impresses with its easy operation, long updates and high resale value, Android with a large selection, affordable entry-level models and more freedom. What matters is your budget, the devices you already own and a reliable update promise. Once you have clarified these three points, you will make a decision that you will be happy with for a few years.
