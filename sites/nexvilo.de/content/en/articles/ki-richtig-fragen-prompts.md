Two people seemingly ask an AI assistant the same question. One gets a general, meaningless answer, the other gets a precise text that she can use almost unchanged. The difference is rarely due to the AI, but rather the way the question was asked. The input to an AI is called a **prompt**, and good prompts are not a secret science. With a few simple rules you will get significantly better results. This guide explains the basics, shows typical mistakes and gives you templates for everyday tasks that you can try out straight away.

## Why the question is so important

An AI assistant doesn't know anything about you unless you tell it. He doesn't know your age, your job, where you live, or the purpose of your question. If you just type “Write a letter to my landlord,” the AI ​​has to guess: Is it about a repair, a rent increase, or a termination? Should the tone be friendly or firm? The more she has to guess, the more general the answer becomes.

Think of the AI ​​as a very well-read but clueless new colleague. The more clearly you explain the task to him, the better the result will be.

## The five building blocks of a good prompt

### 1. Context: Who are you, what is it about?

Briefly describe the situation. For example:

> I live in a three-room apartment in Cologne for rent. The heating in the bedroom has been cold for three weeks. I told the landlord by phone two weeks ago and nothing happened.

### 2. Goal: What should the result be at the end?

Say exactly what you need: a letter, a summary, a list, an explanation, a decision-making aid.

> Write me a written report of the defect, which I can send by email or registered mail.

### 3. Role and tone: How should it sound?

You can give the AI a role or describe the tone you want:

> The tone should be matter-of-fact and polite, but firm. Set a deadline of 14 days.

### 4. Format: What should the answer look like?

Specify the length, structure or shape:

> Maximum one page. With subject line, date, salutation and greeting. Leave placeholders for name and address in square brackets.

### 5. Examples or material

If you want a specific style, give an example. If it's a text, include it, of course without any personal information.

Put together, this creates a prompt that almost always leads to a usable result.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

| Error | Better |
|---|---|
| “Write something about health.” | “Explain to a 70-year-old in simple terms why exercise is important in old age, in five paragraphs.” |
| Ten tasks in one message | One task at a time, check the results, then move on |
| Simply accept the answer | Read, check, have it improved |
| Send personal information | Omit names, addresses, account numbers or replace them with placeholders |
| If the answer is wrong, start over | Say what doesn't fit in the same conversation |

## Asking is half the battle

Many people give up after the first answer if it doesn't fit. The real strength of the assistants lies in the **conversation**. They remember what was said before. Use this:

- “Make it shorter and less formal.”
- “Replace the second paragraph, it sounds too threatening.”
- “Explain that again like I’m twelve years old.”
- “Give me three options to choose from.”
- “What information are you missing to make this better?”

The last question is particularly helpful: the AI ​​often tells you what context it needs.

## Making the AI think

For tasks that have several steps, such as a decision or an invoice, it helps to explicitly ask the AI to proceed step by step:

> I'm thinking about whether a Germany ticket is worth it for me. I take the bus to work three times a week (single journey 3.20 euros) and sometimes take the regional train at the weekend. Calculate step by step and show me the bill.

This way you can see how the AI ​​comes to its conclusion and identify errors. Nevertheless, check the calculations yourself, as language models occasionally miscalculate.

## Facts and sources

When it comes to facts, please express caution:

> Explain to me how the cancellation period works when purchasing online in Germany. If you're unsure about something, say so. Tell me official sources where I can read this.

Many assistants can now search the Internet and link sources. Open the links and check whether they really say what the AI ​​claims. Sometimes sources are misattributed or even invented.

## Templates for typical everyday tasks

You can copy and adapt the following templates. Replace the parts in square brackets with your information.

### Write a letter or an email

> Write a [polite/firm] email to [recipient, e.g. B. my health insurance]. It's about [concern]. What is important to me is: [points]. Length: maximum [number] of sentences. Leave placeholders for personal information.

### Understanding a difficult text

> Here is a passage from a letter [e.g. B. my insurance]. Explain to me in simple language what it says, what I have to do and by when. At the end, list questions I should ask. [Insert text without personal information]

### A weekly meal plan

> Create a meal plan for [number] people for Monday through Friday. We eat [vegetarian / everything / without pork]. In the evening we only have [minutes] minutes at most. Budget: about [amount] euros per week. Finally, give a shopping list sorted by supermarket department.

### Prepare a decision

> I'm faced with a decision between [Option A] and [Option B]. My situation: [context]. [Criteria] are important to me. Compare the advantages and disadvantages in a table and then ask me three questions to help me make a decision.

### Learn something

> Explain [topic] to me as if I had no prior knowledge. Start with an everyday example. Then ask me three questions to check if I understand and wait for my answers.

### Plan a trip

> Plan a three-day trip to [location] for [people, e.g. B. two adults and a child of 8 years] in [month]. We travel by [train/car], like [interests] and don't want to spend more than [hours] hours per day. Provide a daily plan with alternatives in case of rain.

### Improve your own text

> Here is my text. Improve spelling and grammar, but change my style as little as possible. Highlight the most important changes and briefly explain why. [text]

## What you shouldn't enter

As helpful as the assistants are, what you enter can be saved by the provider and used for training depending on your settings. Therefore, never enter:

- Passwords, PINs, TANs or access codes,
- full account numbers, credit card details or ID numbers,
- health data together with your name,
- confidential information from your employer,
- personal data of other people without their consent.

Replace such information with placeholders such as [name] or [customer number]. The result is just as good.

## Prepare prompts for older relatives and children

If you want to show parents, grandparents or children how to use AI, ready-made templates on a piece of paper or as a note on your smartphone will help. For example:

- “Explain this letter to me in simple words: …”
- “Help me write a friendly message to my neighbor because…”
- “What does the word…?”

This makes the AI ​​a practical helper without you having to think long and hard about how to ask.

## From simple to good prompt: an example

A comparison shows how much a few additional details help. First version:

> Write a birthday card.

The result is usually a general text that could suit everyone. Second version:

> Write a short, heartfelt text for a birthday card to my neighbor Ms. [Name], who is turning 80. We have known each other for 20 years, she waters my flowers on vacation and bakes cookies for the children in the house every year. The text should be personal, not cheesy and no more than four sentences long.

Now the AI ​​has everything it needs: occasion, relationship, shared memories, tone and length. The result sounds like you and not like a greeting card from the supermarket. If you don't like a wording, just say: "Please make the second sentence simpler."

## Save your own templates

Many assistants allow you to store **permanent instructions**, for example under “Personalization”, “Custom Instructions” or “Reminders”. For example, you can enter: “Answer in German, in simple language, without technical terms, and cite an official source for facts.” Then you don't have to repeat this every time. But think carefully about what personal information you store there, because it will be included in every conversation.

For tasks that you do regularly, it's also worth making a note with your best prompts. So you can copy them, adapt them and use them again and again.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to write “please” and “thank you”?**
It's not necessary, it doesn't hurt either. More important than politeness is clear information about the goal, context and format.

**Are there “magic” formulations?**
No. There are many alleged secret tricks circulating on the Internet. What reliably helps is what also works with people: say clearly what you want and ask.

**Do the templates work with all assistants?**
Yes, the basic rules apply equally to ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, Claude, Le Chat and others.

## Conclusion

Good answers from an AI start with a good question: provide context, state the goal, set the tone and format and, if necessary, provide an example or the text. Then make improvements during the conversation instead of starting over, and always check the facts yourself. With the templates in this guide, you can get started straight away with letters, meal plans, decisions, travel or studying. And most importantly: personal and confidential data stays outside.
