Many millions of computers in Germany still run Windows 10. The system is popular, familiar and often runs better on older devices than its successor. But Microsoft ended regular support for Windows 10 on October 14, 2025. Anyone who signed up for the free program with extended security updates in the European Economic Area received protection for another year. This program for private users ended in October 2026. Since then, most private Windows 10 computers have no longer received security updates. This guide explains what this means and which paths you can take now.

## What “end of support” means

Windows 10 will continue to work after support ends. It doesn't turn off and your files stay where they are. But:

- **There are no more security updates.** Newly discovered vulnerabilities will no longer be closed. Attackers know this and are specifically looking for such computers.
- **Programs are gradually discontinuing support.** Browsers, office programs, banking software and virus scanners will continue to support Windows 10 for a while, but not indefinitely.
- **New devices and drivers** will eventually no longer be offered for Windows 10.

The risk increases with each month. A computer that is only used occasionally for documents without the Internet is less at risk than one that you use every day to read emails and do online banking. You should no longer use an unprotected system for online banking and purchases.

## Option 1: Switch to Windows 11 for free

If your computer meets the requirements, the **free upgrade to Windows 11** is the easiest way. Your files, programs and most settings are retained.

### Check whether your PC is compatible

1. Open *Settings > Update and Security > Windows Update.* If your PC is compatible, Windows will often show a message there that the upgrade to Windows 11 is available.
2. Alternatively, download the **PC Integrity Check** app from the Microsoft website. She checks all the requirements and tells you what might be wrong.

The main requirements of Windows 11 are:

| requirement | What that means |
|---|---|
| processor | Intel from around the 8th generation (from 2017/2018), AMD Ryzen from the 2000 series, or a supported ARM processor |
| RAM | at least 4 GB, 8 GB makes sense |
| Storage | at least 64 GB |
| TPM 2.0 | a security chip that most computers have from around 2016, but often has to be switched on in the BIOS |
| Secure Boot | a boot protection function that must be activated in the BIOS or UEFI |

### If only TPM or Secure Boot is missing

Many computers have TPM 2.0 and Secure Boot, but the features are turned off. They can be activated in the **BIOS or UEFI**, which you can usually access when starting the computer using keys such as **Del**, **F2** or **F10**. TPM is often called “fTPM” (AMD) or “PTT” (Intel). If you don't trust yourself to do this, a specialist workshop or a technology-savvy friend can help. Only change what you understand in the BIOS.

### Perform the upgrade

1. **Back up your files** to an external hard drive or to the cloud.
2. Write down important access data and license keys.
3. Start the upgrade via Windows Update or the **Windows 11 Installation Wizard** from the Microsoft website.
4. Allow one to two hours and leave the laptop plugged in.

After upgrading, you can return to Windows 10 within ten days if something important doesn't work. However, this only helps in the short term.

## Option 2: Windows 11 on unsupported hardware

There are instructions that can be used to install Windows 11 on older computers that do not meet the requirements. Microsoft advises against this and reserves the right not to provide such devices with updates. In practice, many of these installations are running, but large feature updates can cause problems and there is no guarantee for the future.

**Our assessment:** An option for hobbyists with a backup, but not so much for people who simply need a reliable computer.

## Option 3: Switch to Linux

If your computer is too old for Windows 11 but otherwise works well, **Linux** is a serious alternative. Linux is free, is updated regularly and runs quickly even on older devices.

Particularly suitable for beginners are:

- **Linux Mint** with an interface reminiscent of Windows, with a start menu and taskbar.
- **Ubuntu** with a large community and lots of instructions.
- **Zorin OS**, which is deliberately designed for those switching from Windows.

### Which works fine on Linux

- Surfing with Firefox or Chrome
- Emails with Thunderbird or in the browser
- Office work with LibreOffice, which opens Word, Excel and PowerPoint files
- Photos, music and videos
- Video conferences in the browser
- Online banking in the browser

### Where things can get difficult

- **Special programs** for tax, accounting or work are often only available for Windows. You can do the tax return via **ELSTER** in the browser.
- **Microsoft Office** doesn't run directly, but the web version in the browser does.
- **Some printers and scanners** require additional drivers. Most current devices work without any problems.
- **Games** now run surprisingly well thanks to the Steam platform, but not all of them.

### Try Linux without changing anything

Download the file of the desired Linux version and write it to a USB stick using a program like **balenaEtcher** or **Rufus**. Start the computer from this stick. You can then try out Linux without changing anything on the hard drive. If you like it, install it permanently. Back up all files beforehand.

Many cities have initiatives, repair cafés or adult education courses that help with the change.

## Option 4: A new or refurbished device

If your computer is older than seven or eight years, runs slowly and does not support Windows 11, a new device may be the best solution. Consider:

- **Refurbished business laptops** with Windows 11 are often cheaper than new entry-level devices and are more robust.
- **Chromebooks** are a cheap and secure alternative for everyone who primarily uses the Internet.
- **A Mac** makes sense if you already use an iPhone and are willing to spend more.

Do not dispose of the old device in the household waste. Once the data has been securely deleted, it can be donated, sold or taken to a recycling center. Maybe a club or school would be happy to have a Linux computer.

## Option 5: Continue using Windows 10, with caution

Some people don't want or can't replace their Windows 10 computer right away. If you continue to use it anyway, reduce the risk:

1. **No more online banking and no shopping** on this computer. Use your smartphone or another current device to do this.
2. **Keep your browser up to date** as long as updates for Windows 10 are available. The browser is the most important gateway.
3. According to Microsoft, **Microsoft Defender** will continue to receive virus signatures for a while, but this does not replace system security updates.
4. **Do not open unknown attachments** and do not install programs from dubious sources.
5. **Back up regularly** so that a ransom Trojan attack doesn't destroy everything.
6. **Use without Internet** if the computer is only needed for certain tasks such as playing music or editing old documents.

Companies can still purchase paid extended updates. The program has ended for private users.

## Decision support

| Your computer | Our recommendation |
|---|---|
| Meets Windows 11 requirements | Upgrade to Windows 11 for free |
| Almost fulfills them, TPM or Secure Boot are just switched off | Activate in BIOS, then upgrade |
| Too old for Windows 11, but still in good shape | Try Linux Mint or Zorin OS |
| Old, slow and in need of repair | Buy new or refurbished device |
| Only used offline for individual tasks | Continue to use, but disconnected from the internet |

## Before every change: back up your data

No matter which route you choose: **Back up your data first.** Copy the Documents, Pictures, Music, Videos and Desktop folders to an external hard drive. Export bookmarks and saved passwords from the browser or sync them to your account. Write down license keys for programs that you need to reinstall and export emails if you use a local mail program such as Outlook or Thunderbird.

## Help for older relatives

Parents and grandparents in particular often still have Windows 10 computers running that are rarely used. It's worth spending an afternoon together here: Check together whether the upgrade is possible, save the photos and documents and calmly explain the changes. Windows 11 looks different, the start menu is in the middle and some settings have been moved. You can reorient the taskbar to the left in *Settings > Personalization > Taskbar > Taskbar Behavior* to make everything feel more familiar.

If the computer is too old, a tablet or Chromebook may be a better solution, especially if it will only be used for email, video calls with family, and reading the newspaper. Such devices update themselves automatically and are less vulnerable to malware. It is important that you accompany the changeover and leave short written instructions for the most important processes.

## How to recognize fake “support” calls

The end of support is a welcome opportunity for scammers. They call, pretend to be Microsoft employees and claim that the computer is unsafe or infected. They then ask to install a remote maintenance program and demand money for an alleged repair or update. **Microsoft never calls unsolicited.** In such a case, hang up, do not install anything and do not give out any login details or payment information.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does it cost anything to upgrade to Windows 11?**
No, it is free for computers with a valid Windows 10 license.

**Do my programs run on Windows 11?**
Almost all programs that run under Windows 10 also work under Windows 11. For very old or special software, it's worth taking a look at the manufacturer's website.

**Will my computer be slower with Windows 11?**
On computers that meet the requirements, Windows 11 usually runs just as quickly as Windows 10. However, with only 4 GB of RAM, things get tough.

**What happens to my data when I upgrade?**
During a normal upgrade, files and programs are retained. Nevertheless, you should make a backup beforehand.

## Conclusion

Windows 10 is finally without security updates for private users. If you want to continue surfing safely, reading emails and carrying out banking transactions, you should act now. The easiest way is to upgrade to Windows 11 for free if your computer supports it. For older but still good devices, Linux is a free and safe alternative. And if your computer is on its last legs anyway, it's worth taking a look at refurbished devices. The same thing is important in all ways: back up the data first.
