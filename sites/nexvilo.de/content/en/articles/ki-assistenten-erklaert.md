A few years ago it still sounded like science fiction: you type a question into a text field and a program answers in complete sentences, writes a letter to the property manager, explains the utility bill or suggests a recipe for the leftovers in the fridge. Today millions of people in Germany use such **AI assistants**, such as ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot, Claude, Le Chat from Mistral or Meta AI. For others, the whole thing seems scary or overrated. This guide explains without technical jargon how these programs work, what they are really useful for in everyday life and where you should be careful.

## What an AI assistant actually is

Behind the well-known assistants are so-called **large language models**, in English “Large Language Models” or LLM for short. A language model is a computer program that has been trained on huge amounts of text: books, websites, articles, program code, and much more. It learned which words and sentence parts typically follow one another.

When you ask a question, the model calculates, word by word, which continuation is most likely to match your question. That sounds simple, but it leads to astonishing results: Because the model has seen so many texts, it can explain connections, summarize texts, translate languages ​​and even draw logical conclusions.

What's important is what a language model **isn't**:

- It's **not a search engine** that checks what's true. Some assistants can also search the Internet, but the actual model generates text from probabilities.
- It is **not a being with feelings or a will of its own**, even if it is expressed in a friendly and understanding way.
- It is **not an expert** that you can blindly rely on. It can sound very convincing and still be wrong.

## What AI assistants really help with in everyday life

### Write and improve texts

That is the greatest strength. An assistant can:

- write a **draft**, such as a termination, a complaint, an invitation or a greeting card,
- **improve** your own text, shorten it, make it friendlier or more formal,
- **check spelling and grammar**,
- Translate a text into **simple language**, for example for older relatives or children.

You remain the author: read the suggestion, adapt it and only adopt what is correct.

### Explain and summarize

Long letters from authorities, insurance companies or landlords are often difficult to understand. An assistant can explain what a term means, summarize the main points of a text, or suggest questions to ask the sender. Pay attention to data protection: Black out names, addresses and contract numbers before uploading such documents. Find out more in our guide to data protection in AI.

### Collect ideas and plan

An assistant is a patient interlocutor for brainstorming: ideas for a child's birthday party, a weekly meal plan, a packing list for a vacation, a training plan for starting running. The suggestions are a starting point that you adapt to your situation.

### Translate

AI assistants often translate more naturally than traditional translation programs and can also explain why something is translated that way. However, they do not replace a certified translation for important documents such as contracts or certificates.

### Learn

Whether it's a new language, math problem or tax terms: an assistant can explain things step by step, give examples and quiz you. This is particularly helpful for students if they use the AI ​​to understand and not to copy.

### Working with images and language

Many assistants now understand **photos**: you take a photo of a plant, a label or an error message, and the AI describes what it sees. With **Voice Mode** you can converse as if you were on the phone. This is particularly practical for people with visual impairments or when cooking with full hands.

## Where the limits are

### Made up facts

Language models can invent things that sound plausible but are wrong: numbers, laws, quotes, sources, even court rulings or book titles. Experts call these **hallucinations**. The more specific and topical a question, the greater the risk. Therefore, always check important information in reliable sources. We explain this in detail in our guide to AI errors.

### Outdated knowledge

A model only knows what was in its training data. It doesn't know about recent events, current prices or changes in the law unless it can search on the Internet. Even then you should look at the linked sources yourself.

### No advice in individual cases

When it comes to health, law, taxes and money, an AI can explain how something fundamentally works. But she doesn't know your situation and takes no responsibility. For diagnoses, legal questions and financial decisions, you need experts: doctors, lawyers, tax advisors or consumer advice centers.

### Calculation and logic

Although models are improving, they sometimes miscalculate or draw incorrect conclusions, especially in multi-stage calculations. For tax returns, budget books or loan comparisons, you're better off using a calculator or a table and only using the AI ​​to explain.

### Bias

Because the models have learned from human texts, they can adopt prejudices, for example about gender, origin or professions. Be critical if answers seem one-sided.

## Free or with subscription?

Most assistants come in a **free version** with limitations: less powerful models, limited messages per day, or fewer features. **Subscriptions** usually cost around 20 euros per month and offer more powerful models, more usage and additional functions such as image editing or long documents.

The free version is almost always sufficient for occasional everyday use. A subscription is worth it if you use the AI ​​every day for work, study or larger projects. Try several free offers before committing, and pay attention to how to cancel the subscription.

Be careful with **fake apps**: There are imitators in app stores with similar names and logos that sell expensive subscriptions or collect data. Only download assistants via the provider's official website or check exactly who the developer is in the store.

## This is how you get started safely

1. **Choose an assistant** and download the official app or open the website.
2. **Create an account** with your own secure password and activate two-factor login.
3. **Check your privacy settings**: Many services allow you to turn off your conversations from being used for training and clear your history.
4. **Start with simple tasks**: rephrase a text, suggest a recipe, explain a term.
5. **Do not enter sensitive information**: no passwords, no account numbers, no health information with names.
6. **Check the answers** before using them.

## How to get better answers

The quality of the answer depends largely on your question. A few basic rules:

- **Provide context:** "I'm 68 and want to wish my grandson a happy 16th birthday" produces better results than "Write a happy birthday."
- **State the goal and form:** “Summarize this letter in five bullet points in simple language.”
- **Ask:** If an answer doesn't fit, say what you would like different. The assistant remembers the course of the conversation.
- **Ask for sources** when it comes to facts and check them.

You can find detailed tips in our guide to asking the AI ​​correctly.

## AI in everyday life: stay realistic

There is a lot of excitement and a lot of fear around artificial intelligence. Neither helps much. Realistically speaking, AI assistants are useful tools, similar to spell checkers, calculators or search engines: they save time, help formulate and explain things and make some things more accessible. But they don't think for you, they don't know everything and they don't bear any responsibility.

If you keep this in mind, you can benefit from the assistants without being fooled by them.

## A little test to get you started

Anyone trying out an AI assistant for the first time learns the most when they test it on things they know well. Three suggestions:

1. **Ask about your hobby.** If you're familiar with gardening, football or cooking, you'll quickly notice where the AI gets it right and where it gets inaccurate.
2. **Have your own text improved.** Take an email that you have to write anyway and compare your original with the proposal.
3. **Ask a question about where you live.** Local details such as opening times, bus routes or events show the importance of checking information.

This will help you develop a feeling for when you can trust the AI ​​and when you can't. This instinct is more important than any technical knowledge when dealing with AI.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is anyone reading my conversations with the AI?**
Many providers allow conversations to be saved and used to improve models, sometimes even checked by humans. This can usually be restricted in the settings. So don't enter anything that you don't want anyone to see.

**Can AI take over my computer?**
A normal chat assistant can only generate text, images or files that you give it. Newer “agents” can act for you in the browser, such as filling out forms. You should only use such functions consciously and carefully.

**Is AI use allowed in school?**
Schools and teachers regulate this differently. Ask before using AI for homework or presentations. More about this in our instructions for students.

## Conclusion

AI assistants like ChatGPT, Gemini or Copilot are language models that have learned to formulate likely answers from huge amounts of text. They help with writing, explaining, translating, planning and learning, but they can invent facts, have outdated knowledge and are not a substitute for specialist advice. If you start with simple tasks, do not enter sensitive data and check important information, you will have a helpful companion in your everyday digital life with an AI assistant.
