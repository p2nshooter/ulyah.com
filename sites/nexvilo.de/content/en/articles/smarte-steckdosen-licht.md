Smart sockets and smart lamps are the first step into a smart home for many people, and for good reason. They are inexpensive, can be set up in just a few minutes and immediately bring a noticeable benefit: You can see how much electricity the old refrigerator really uses, the television is no longer on standby at night, and the light in the hallway dims itself in the evening. This guide will show you what these little helpers can do, how to use them sensibly and what you need to pay attention to when buying and when it comes to safety.

## Smart sockets: what they can do

You plug a smart socket, often also called an adapter plug, into a normal socket and the device into the adapter plug. You can then switch the power on and off via an app, by voice or automatically.

Typical functions:

- **Switching via app**, even on the go
- **Schedules**, such as “off at 11pm every night”
- **Random mode**, which switches lights on and off irregularly during the holiday to make the apartment appear occupied
- **Consumption measurement** in watts and kilowatt hours, with cost calculation for many models
- **Automations**, such as “if the washing machine uses less than 5 watts, send me a message that it is ready”
- **Child lock** and key lock on some models

## Find power guzzlers with the measuring function

The **consumption measurement** is the most useful function for many. Plug in a device for a week and see how much it uses. Particularly revealing are:

- **Old Refrigerators and Freezers:** A 15 year old refrigerator can use many times more than a new appliance. The measurement over a week shows whether an exchange is worthwhile.
- **Consumer electronics in standby:** Television, receiver, soundbar, game console and router together can draw continuous power in standby.
- **Computer with accessories:** Monitor, printer, speakers and chargers often run 24/7.
- **Aquariums, terrariums, pond pumps** that are constantly in operation.
- **Tumble dryer and washing machine:** The measurement shows how much a program really costs and whether the Eco program is worth it.

### This is how you calculate the costs

Most apps calculate the costs themselves when you enter your electricity price. A simple calculation helps to check:

> **Power in watts × hours per day × 365 ÷ 1,000 = kilowatt hours per year**
> Kilowatt hours × electricity price = costs per year

Example: An entertainment corner draws 15 watts in standby, around the clock. That's 15 × 24 × 365 ÷ 1,000 = 131 kilowatt hours per year. At an electricity price of 35 cents per kilowatt hour, that's around **46 euros per year** just for standby. A smart socket that switches this off at night and during the day quickly paid for itself. Note that the socket itself also requires some power, usually less than one watt.

## Switch off standby specifically

With a smart socket, you can disconnect entire groups of devices from the network at a time-controlled manner. The following have proven themselves:

- **TV and Entertainment:** Turn off at midnight, turn on in the afternoon. Use a **power strip** behind the smart socket to switch all devices at once.
- **Workplace:** Monitor, printer and speakers off in the evening.
- **Coffee machine:** Let it preheat in the morning, turn it off at lunchtime.

**Don't turn off** devices that require power to function properly: refrigerators and freezers, routers (otherwise the smart home will stop working), recorders with scheduled recordings, and some televisions and consoles that install updates at night. For ink printers, frequent complete shutdowns can result in additional cleaning procedures that consume ink. Check the instructions to see how your printer reacts.

## Security: what a smart socket can withstand

Smart sockets have a maximum load capacity, usually **16 amps** or around **3,680 watts**, some cheaper models less. Pay attention to the following points:

- **No continuous load at the limit:** Fan heaters, kettles, ovens or air conditioning units draw a lot of electricity. Check whether the socket is approved for this and it is better not to switch on such devices automatically when no one is at home.
- **Never switch on heaters unattended via the app:** A fan heater that starts unnoticed next to a curtain is a fire hazard.
- **Note the test mark:** Buy devices with CE marking and preferably with a test mark such as GS or VDE from well-known manufacturers.
- **Do not plug in one behind the other:** Several adapter plugs or multiple sockets in a row are dangerous.
- **Outdoor area:** Only use sockets outside with sufficient protection class, at least IP44.

## Smart light: more than just on and off

Smart lamps and light sources can be controlled via app, voice, switch or automatically. Depending on the model you can:

- **dimming**, without a special dimmer on the wall,
- change the **light color** from warm white to daylight white,
- Set **any color** for colored lamps,
- **Save scenes**, such as “Reading,” “Watching TV,” or “Dinner,”
- switch the light according to **schedule** or **sunset**,
- Connect with **motion detectors**, for example in the hallway or basement.

### Which lamp is suitable?

| Art | Suitable for | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Smart bulb (E27, E14, GU10) | existing lamps | Easiest to get started |
| Smart lamp complete | new lights, floor lamps | Often with better light quality |
| LED strips | indirect light, shelves, television | Pay attention to the power supply and length |
| Smart light switch | permanently installed ceiling lights | Installation only by a qualified electrician |
| Smart adapter plug | Floor lamps with normal light sources | Only on/off, no dimming |

When it comes to bulbs, pay attention to the **base** (E27 is the large standard base, E14 the small one, GU10 for spots), the **brightness in lumens** (an old 60-watt light bulb corresponds to about 800 lumens) and the **color rendering** (CRI 90 or higher ensures natural colors).

### The wall switch problem

Smart lights need electricity to be accessible. If someone turns off the normal light switch, the lamp is no longer accessible to the app. Solutions:

1. **Radio switches** that you stick next to or on the existing switch. You control the smart lamp directly.
2. **Switch Covers** that fix the old switch in the on position while providing a smart button.
3. **Smart flush-mounted switches** instead of smart bulbs, installed by a qualified electrician.
4. **Inform family**: A small note next to the counter also helps guests.

## Useful automation

Here are a few ideas that have proven themselves in everyday life:

- **Hallway lights dimmed at sunset**, switched off at 11 p.m.
- **Night light:** Motion detector in the hallway turns on a dim, warm light at night so no one stumbles without bright light waking everyone up.
- **Wake up light:** The bedroom light slowly gets brighter in the morning, like a sunrise.
- **Absence:** When everyone leaves the house, all lights turn off and standby devices turn off.
- **Holiday Mode:** In the evening, lamps in different rooms change at random times.
- **Washing machine ready:** If the consumption of the socket falls below a certain value, a message comes to the cell phone.

Start with one or two automations and expand as you go. Too many rules at once can quickly lead to confusion when lights seem to turn on at random.

## Which system?

Smart sockets and lamps are available for all systems:

- **Matter devices** work with Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa and SmartThings and are the most future-proof choice.
- **Philips Hue** and **IKEA** rely on Zigbee with their own bridge, which now also makes their devices available as Matter devices.
- **FRITZ!DECT sockets** connect directly to the FRITZ!Box and offer very precise consumption measurement.
- **Cheap Wi-Fi devices** are easy to set up, but often depend on the manufacturer's cloud.

The app for your system is enough to get started. If you want to combine many devices from different manufacturers, you can later switch to a central unit like Home Assistant.

## Data protection

A socket also collects data: the consumption history shows when you cook, watch TV or sleep. Prefer devices that work **locally**, use a separate password for each manufacturer account and install updates. Cheap devices from unknown manufacturers without updates are best placed in the **guest WiFi**, separate from your computers.

## An example from everyday life

How much can be found in a typical household? An example of a family in a four-room apartment who measured with two smart sockets for a week:

- The **entertainment corner** with television, receiver, soundbar and games console drew a total of around 18 watts in standby. After setting up a shutdown between midnight and 4 p.m., standby hours dropped by two-thirds.
- The **freezer in the basement**, a good 15 years old, consumed significantly more than a new appliance with efficiency class D. The family replaced it because buying a new one would pay off after a few years.
- The **workstation in the children's room** with monitor, speakers and chargers ran around the clock. Now it goes off at 10pm and comes back on at 2pm.
- The **washing machine** will now send a message when it is finished. This doesn't save any electricity, but the laundry no longer stays damp in the drum for hours.

Such measurements are not science, but they quickly show where action is worthwhile. It is important to measure for at least a week because many devices behave differently depending on the day.

## Light for the elderly and children

Smart light can be particularly helpful for older people and children. A motion detector on the bed that illuminates the way to the toilet with dim light at night reduces the risk of falls. For children, a night light that slowly dims at a fixed time can make it easier for children to fall asleep. It is important that everything can be operated without an app.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do smart sockets themselves use a lot of electricity?**
Good models need less than one watt. This is significantly less than the standby consumption you save.

**How accurate is the consumption measurement?**
Accurate enough for everyday use. For very low consumption levels of less than one watt, some models are inaccurate.

**Can I screw smart lamps into any light?**
In almost every one with a matching base. It can get warm in closed lights and metal screens can weaken the radio signal.

## Conclusion

Smart sockets with measuring functions help to find power guzzlers and to specifically switch off standby consumption, which often quickly saves more than the devices cost. Smart lamps bring convenience with dimming, light colors, scenes and automations such as night or wake-up lights. Pay attention to the maximum load capacity of sockets and never switch on heating devices unattended. Solve the wall switch problem with radio switches and rely on Matter devices from well-known manufacturers for the future.
