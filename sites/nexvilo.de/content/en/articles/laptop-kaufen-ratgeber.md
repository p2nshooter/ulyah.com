A laptop is the most important work device for many people: for homework and applications, for the home office, for photos, tax returns and video calls with the family. The selection is correspondingly large and the data sheets are correspondingly confusing. In the electronics market there are dozens of devices next to each other that at first glance look the same but cost between 300 and 3,000 euros. This guide explains the most important components in understandable language, shows typical pitfalls and gives you recommendations for different needs.

## First the question: What do you need the laptop for?

The right choice depends primarily on what you do with the device. Four groups can be roughly distinguished:

1. **Everyday life and surfing:** Emails, internet, streaming videos, online banking, occasionally a letter. A solid entry-level or mid-range device is sufficient here.
2. **School, study and office:** Word processing, spreadsheets, presentations, video conferences, many browser tabs. A good display, a comfortable keyboard, long battery life and low weight are important.
3. **Creative work:** Photo editing, video editing, music production. What counts here is a powerful processor, plenty of RAM and a true-color display.
4. **Gaming:** Current games require their own graphics card, which makes laptops heavy, noisy and expensive.

Also make a note of whether you take the laptop with you a lot. A device that travels to university or the office every day should be as light as possible and not need a power outlet for a long time.

## The operating system: Windows, macOS or ChromeOS

### Windows

Windows is the most common system. There are devices in every price range from many manufacturers, and almost every software runs on them. If you need specific programs for school or work, Windows is often the safest choice. Make sure Windows 11 is preinstalled.

### macOS

Apple's MacBooks are more expensive, but offer very long battery life, high-quality workmanship, an excellent display and long updates. They are particularly useful if you already use an iPhone. Before doing so, check whether all the programs you need are available on the Mac.

### ChromeOS

Chromebooks run on a slim system from Google that relies primarily on the browser and online services. They are cheap, fast, safe and are suitable for students, older people or as a second device. However, classic Windows programs do not run on it. Pay attention to the automatic update expiration date that Google provides for each model.

## Processor: the heart of the laptop

The processor (CPU) determines how quickly the laptop calculates. For Windows laptops, the processors come primarily from **Intel** (Core Ultra, Core i3/i5/i7) and **AMD** (Ryzen 3/5/7/9), and now also from **Qualcomm** (Snapdragon X). MacBooks have Apple M-series chips.

A simple rule of thumb:

- **Entry level (Intel Core i3, Core 3, AMD Ryzen 3):** sufficient for everyday life and simple office work.
- **Mid-range (Intel Core i5, Core Ultra 5, AMD Ryzen 5, Snapdragon X):** the best choice for most people, including for studying and working from home.
- **Upper class (Intel Core i7/Ultra 7, AMD Ryzen 7, Apple M-Chips Pro/Max):** for photo and video editing or demanding software.

Pay attention to the **generation**: A current Ryzen 5 is faster than a Core i7 that is several years old. You can recognize the generation by the model number; A look at an independent test or a benchmark list helps with comparison.

Laptops with **Snapdragon processors** have a very long battery life. Almost all programs now run on it, but there may still be limitations with some older special programs, games or printer drivers. Check before purchasing.

## RAM and SSD

### Main memory (RAM)

The RAM is the desktop of the laptop: the larger it is, the more programs and browser tabs can be open at the same time without it jerking.

- **8 GB:** Lower limit for Windows 11. Sufficient for everyday life and the office, but with little reserves.
- **16 GB:** the recommendation for most. This means you will still be well positioned in a few years.
- **32 GB and more:** for video editing, large photo collections or virtual machines.

In many modern laptops, the RAM is **soldered in** and cannot be upgraded. So it's better to buy a little more.

### Storage (SSD)

Stop buying a laptop with a classic hard drive (HDD). A **SSD** is many times faster. The following applies to the size:

- **256 GB:** close, only if you store a lot of things in the cloud.
- **512GB:** good standard size.
- **1 TB:** if you want to have a lot of photos, videos or games on the device.

## Display: what you look at all day long

The display is one of the most important and often underestimated points. A bad display also makes a fast laptop uncomfortable.

- **Resolution:** At least **Full HD (1920 × 1080)**. At 14 inches and larger, higher resolutions such as 2560 × 1600 are pleasantly sharp.
- **Surface:** A **matte** display is less reflective and is suitable for bright rooms and outdoors. Glossy displays have stronger colors, but are more reflective.
- **Brightness:** At least 300 nits for indoor use, 400 nits and more if you often work near the window or outside. Cheap devices often have dark displays with 250 nits, which are only comfortable in dimly lit rooms.
- **Panel Type:** IPS and OLED displays offer good viewing angles and colors. TN panels, which are found on very cheap devices, look pale from the side.
- **Size:** 13 to 14 inches is ideal for carrying around, 15 to 16 inches is more comfortable for the desk and with a number pad on the keyboard.

## Battery, weight and workmanship

Manufacturer information about battery life is usually optimistic. Rely on tests with realistic scenarios such as Wi-Fi surfing or video playback. In the test, a working day without a power outlet should be at least eight to ten hours.

When it comes to **weight**, the following applies: If you weigh less than 1.4 kg, you'll be happy to carry a laptop with you every day; if it's over 2 kg, it's more of a desk device. A sturdy aluminum housing is more robust than plastic and can withstand daily transport better.

## Keyboard, touchpad and webcam

If you write a lot, you should try the **keyboard**: Does it have a clear pressure point, is it illuminated, are special keys such as Del and Home useful? A large, precise **touchpad** eliminates the need for a mouse on the go.

A **webcam with full HD resolution** and good microphones are important for video conferences. Many devices now have a slider that you can use to cover the camera mechanically. An infrared camera or a fingerprint sensor make it easy to log in with Windows Hello.

## Connections

Modern, thin laptops often only have a few ports. Check what you need:

- **USB-C with Power Delivery and DisplayPort** so you can charge via a cable and connect a monitor. **Thunderbolt** or **USB4** allow for fast docks and external displays.
- **USB-A** for older USB sticks, mice and printers.
- **HDMI** for televisions and projectors.
- **Card reader** for photos from the camera.
- **Headphone jack** if you use wired headsets.

Missing ports can be supplemented with a USB-C adapter, but that's another part that can be forgotten.

## Typical pitfalls when buying a laptop

- **Too little RAM:** 4 GB is not enough for Windows 11. Such devices are cheap, but a pain in everyday life.
- **Too small storage:** Laptops with 64 or 128 GB eMMC storage fill up quickly with Windows updates.
- **Dark display:** Savings are often made here on very cheap devices.
- **Pre-installed Software:** Many laptops come with trial versions of virus scanners and Office. Uninstall what you don't need.
- **Without operating system:** Some offers are cheaper because Windows is not installed. The license costs extra.

## New, last year's model or used business device?

An often overlooked alternative is **refurbished business laptops**. Devices such as the ThinkPad series from Lenovo, Latitude from Dell or EliteBook from HP are retired by companies after two to three years, professionally refurbished and sold with a warranty. They are sturdy, easy to repair, and have excellent keyboards. Make sure that Windows 11 is officially supported, i.e. at least an 8th generation Intel processor or an AMD Ryzen 2000 series is installed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need Microsoft Office?**
For many, the free web version of Office, Google Docs or LibreOffice is sufficient. Licenses are often available from the educational institution for school and studies.

**Do I need additional antivirus protection?**
For private users, the built-in Microsoft Defender in Windows 11 is sufficient, provided updates are installed.

**How long does a laptop last?**
A good device lasts five to seven years. The battery wears out after three to four years, but can be replaced in many models.

## Conclusion

For most people, a laptop with a current mid-range processor, 16 GB of RAM, a 512 GB SSD and a bright, matte Full HD display is the best choice. Before you buy, clarify what you need it for, how often you will take it with you and which programs need to be running. Pay attention to the display and battery in independent tests, not just the data sheet. And also consider refurbished business devices, they often offer more quality for the money than new entry-level devices.
