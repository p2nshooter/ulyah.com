The lights come on when you get home, the heater warms up the bathroom before you get up, and a message on your cell phone says that the washing machine is ready. A smart home sounds like comfort and energy saving, but also about expensive technology, complicated apps and the worry that a corporation is listening in in the living room. The truth lies in between. A connected home can really help in everyday life if you build it up gradually and with a plan. This guide will show you what makes sense to start with, what technology is behind it, how much it costs and what typical mistakes you should avoid.

## What a smart home actually is

A smart home is a home in which devices are networked with each other and can be controlled via app, voice or automatically. These include, for example:

- **Light:** smart lamps, bulbs and light switches
- **Sockets:** switchable adapter plugs with consumption measurement
- **Heating:** smart radiator thermostats and room thermostats
- **Security:** Door and window contacts, smoke detectors, cameras, doorbells, water detectors
- **Household appliances:** Robot vacuum cleaners, washing machines, refrigerators with app
- **Entertainment:** Speakers, TVs, streaming devices
- **Shading:** Roller shutter and blind controls

The core is **automations**: rules like “When the last cell phone leaves the house, turn down the heating and turn off the lights” or “If the window is open, turn off the radiators in the room”. Only through such rules can individual devices become smart homes.

## First think: What problem do you want to solve?

The most common mistake is buying equipment because it's on sale and then looking for a purpose. It's better to start with a specific wish:

- “I often forget to turn off the lights.”
- “The heating is running even though no one is at home.”
- “I want to know how much electricity the old refrigerator uses.”
- “I want to see if I closed the balcony door.”
- “My mother lives alone and I want to notice when something is wrong.”

There are simple solutions for each of these needs that you can set up in an afternoon. You don't need a big system for this.

## The best first devices

These devices are particularly suitable for beginners because they are inexpensive, easy to set up and quickly provide benefits:

| device | Benefit | Rough price |

| Smart LED lamp | Light via app, schedule, dimming, light color | 10–40 € |

## The technology: radio standards simply explained

Smart devices talk to each other using different radio standards. This is the part that most confuses beginners. Here are the most important ones:

### WiFi

Many inexpensive devices connect directly to your WiFi. This is convenient because you don't need a central office. Disadvantages: Every device puts a strain on the WLAN, many only work via the manufacturer's cloud, and with many devices the network becomes confusing.

### Zigbee

Zigbee is an economical wireless standard that Philips Hue, IKEA and many others use. The devices form their own network in which mains-operated devices such as lamps pass on the signals. You need a **central** (bridge or hub) for this. Battery-operated sensors often last one to two years.

### Thread and Matter

**Thread** is a modern, economical wireless standard, and **Matter** is a common language agreed upon by Apple, Google, Amazon, Samsung and many manufacturers. Matter devices work with all major systems and are designed to end the standards mess. More on this in our own guide to Matter.

### DECT ULE

Anyone who has a **FRITZ!Box** can connect smart sockets, radiator thermostats and buttons from AVM via DECT, the same radio as cordless telephones. The FRITZ!Box itself is the control center; an additional bridge is not necessary.

### Bluetooth

Some inexpensive devices use Bluetooth. The range is limited and control on the go is usually only possible via an additional control center.

## App, control center or voice assistant?

There are three typical ways to control a smart home:

1. **Manufacturer apps:** Each device has its own app. That's enough to start with a single manufacturer. With several brands it quickly becomes confusing.
2. **Platforms from major providers:** **Apple Home**, **Google Home**, **Amazon Alexa** and **Samsung SmartThings** bundle devices from different manufacturers in one app and enable automation. For Matter devices you often need a speaker, a display, an Apple TV or another device as a **control center** (Matter controller and thread border router).
3. **Independent control centers:** Systems like **Home Assistant**, **Homey** or the **Homematic IP** control centers often work locally without a cloud and connect almost everything with each other. Home Assistant is free and very powerful, but requires some training.

**For beginners:** Use the platform that fits your smartphone, Apple Home for iPhone households and Google Home for Android, and purchase Matter-compatible devices if possible. Then you remain flexible.

## Rent or own?

### In the rented apartment

Tenants should rely on devices that require **no modifications** and can be taken with them when moving out:

- Smart lamps and light sources
- Adapter plug instead of permanently installed flush-mounted switch
- Radiator thermostats that replace the old thermostat and can be exchanged back later. Keep the original.
- Battery-operated sensors and radio buttons that are glued instead of screwed
- Smart doorbells that do not change the existing bell system, or an attachment solution for the door viewer

Anything that intervenes in the **electrical installation**, i.e. flush-mounted actuators, light switches or roller shutter motors, may only be installed by a qualified electrician and, in the case of rented apartments, requires the consent of the landlord.

### In your own home

Anyone building or renovating can plan for a smart home right from the start: empty pipes and network cables in every room, deep flush-mounted sockets for smart switches, power connections for cameras and roller shutters. It is worth seeking advice from an electrician who is familiar with smart homes. Wired systems like KNX are very reliable, but more expensive.

## Privacy and security

Smart devices collect data: when you are home, when you sleep, how much electricity you use. A few rules help you stay in control:

- **Buy devices from well-known manufacturers** that regularly provide updates and have a data protection declaration in German.
- **Prefer local control** where possible. Matter, Zigbee with its own control center and Home Assistant also work without the Internet.
- **Set up a guest WiFi** for smart devices that only need the cloud. This keeps them separate from your computers.
- **Change default passwords** and use a unique password with two-factor authentication for each manufacturer account.
- **Install updates** for devices and apps.
- **Think about roommates and guests**: Cameras and microphones affect other people too. Talk about it before you put it up.

Since August 2025, the EU has also had stricter security requirements for new radio devices that are connected to the Internet, such as no longer having uniform standard passwords. Further obligations such as security updates over the entire service life will be introduced gradually by the end of 2027 with the Cyber ​​Resilience Act.

## How much does a smart home cost?

You can get started with just a few euros. Rough examples:

- **Mini entry:** two smart sockets and a lamp, around 50 euros.
- **Heating in a 3-room apartment:** five radiator thermostats, two window contacts, possibly a central unit, around 250 to 450 euros.
- **Security:** networked smoke detectors in all bedrooms and hallways, water detectors in the kitchen and bathroom, around 150 to 300 euros.

There may also be costs for a central office or subscriptions, for example for cloud storage of camera recordings. Before purchasing, check whether a device can be fully used without a subscription.

## Typical mistakes when getting started

1. **Too many systems at once:** Three apps from three manufacturers that don't talk to each other. Better to rely on a platform and matter.
2. **Cheap no-name devices:** They often don't get any updates, only work via an unknown cloud and disappear from the market after a short time.
3. **Want to automate everything:** Automations that constantly trigger incorrectly are annoying. Start with a few, clear rules.
4. **Forget the light switch:** If a smart lamp is turned off at the wall switch, it is no longer accessible. Don't tape the switch, use a smart radio switch or explain it to everyone in the household.
5. **No alternative in the event of a failure:** Everything should work without the internet and without an app, at least light and heating by hand.

## A plan for the first few weeks

1. **Week 1:** Choose a problem, decide on a platform, buy two devices and set up.
2. **Week 2:** Create a simple automation, such as setting a schedule for the floor lamp or turning off the TV overnight.
3. **Week 3:** Check what works and what bothers you. Adjust.
4. **Afterwards:** Expand step by step, for example with radiator thermostats before winter.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will my smart home work if the internet goes down?**
That depends on the devices. Local systems with Matter, Zigbee central or FRITZ!Box continue to work, pure cloud devices do not. Lamps and thermostats can usually always be operated directly.

**What happens if the manufacturer goes bankrupt?**
Pure cloud devices can then become unusable. Matter devices and local systems are less affected.

**Do I need a smart speaker?**
No. Voice control is convenient, but not a must. For Matter and Thread, however, a loudspeaker can serve as a central station; alternatively, there are Apple TV, routers or your own hubs.

## Conclusion

A smart home is worthwhile if it solves a specific problem. Start small with smart sockets, lamps or radiator thermostats, choose a platform that fits your smartphone and, if possible, buy Matter-compatible devices from well-known manufacturers. Pay attention to updates, separate networks and local control to keep control of your data. With a little patience, you can grow a home that makes your everyday life easier instead of having to worry about technology.
