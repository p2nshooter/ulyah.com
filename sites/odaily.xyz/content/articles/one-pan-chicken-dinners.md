Chicken is the backbone of weeknight cooking in many homes. It is affordable, widely available and goes with almost any flavor, from lemon and herbs to soy and ginger or tomatoes and olives. But chicken also has a reputation for being dry, bland or tricky to cook safely. The solution is a simple, flexible method: sear the chicken in a hot skillet until golden, let it finish cooking gently, then use the same pan to make a quick sauce from the flavorful browned bits left behind. Once you learn this technique, you can make dozens of different dinners with one pan and about thirty minutes, and only one pan to wash afterward.

## Choosing the right cut

Different parts of the chicken cook differently, and the right choice makes weeknight cooking easier.

- **Boneless, skinless thighs:** The most forgiving cut. Thighs contain more fat and connective tissue, so they stay juicy even if cooked a little longer. They are often cheaper than breasts and full of flavor.
- **Boneless, skinless breasts:** Lean and mild, they cook quickly but dry out easily when overcooked. They work best when pounded to an even thickness or sliced into cutlets.
- **Chicken cutlets:** Thin slices of breast that cook in just a few minutes per side. You can buy them ready-made or make them by slicing a breast horizontally.
- **Bone-in, skin-on thighs:** The most flavorful option, with crisp skin and juicy meat, but they take longer, around 30 to 40 minutes, usually finished in the oven.
- **Chicken tenders:** Small strips of meat from underneath the breast, quick to cook and popular with children.

For a fast dinner, boneless thighs or cutlets are the most reliable choices.

## Preparing chicken for the pan

### Even thickness

A chicken breast is thick at one end and thin at the other. If you cook it as it is, the thin end dries out long before the thick end is done. To fix this, place the breast between two sheets of plastic wrap or parchment and pound the thick part gently with a rolling pin or the bottom of a heavy pan until it is about 3/4 inch (2 cm) thick all over. Alternatively, slice it horizontally into two thinner cutlets.

### Dry the surface

Pat the chicken dry with paper towels. A dry surface browns; a wet one steams. This one small step makes a big difference to color and flavor.

### Season ahead if you can

Salt the chicken on both sides. If you have time, salt it 30 minutes to a few hours ahead and keep it uncovered or loosely covered in the refrigerator. This is called dry brining. The salt seasons the meat more deeply and helps it hold on to its juices. Even 15 minutes makes a difference. Add pepper and any dry spices just before cooking.

### Handle raw chicken safely

Raw chicken can carry bacteria such as *Salmonella* and *Campylobacter*. Do not rinse raw chicken in the sink; washing does not remove bacteria and instead spreads droplets onto nearby surfaces. Use a separate cutting board or plate, wash your hands with soap after handling raw chicken, and clean any surfaces it touched.

## The basic method: sear, cook, sauce

### Step 1: Sear the chicken

Heat a large skillet over medium-high heat. Stainless steel or cast iron produces the best browning and the most flavorful browned bits for the sauce. Add one to two tablespoons of oil. When the oil shimmers, add the chicken pieces with space between them.

Now leave them alone. It is tempting to move the chicken, but it needs time to form a crust. When it is ready, it will release from the pan on its own. This usually takes about four to six minutes for boneless thighs and breasts. Flip and cook the second side.

### Step 2: Cook through

The only reliable way to know when chicken is done is to check its internal temperature with an instant-read thermometer. Insert the thermometer into the thickest part of the meat. The USDA recommends cooking all poultry to **165 °F (74 °C)**.

- **Cutlets** usually cook through during searing, about two to three minutes per side.
- **Boneless breasts and thighs** of normal thickness need about five to seven minutes per side over medium heat. If the outside is browning too quickly before the inside is done, lower the heat and cover the pan for a few minutes.
- **Bone-in pieces** are best seared skin-side down until crisp and then finished in a 400 °F (200 °C) oven.

Thighs are pleasant and tender even when cooked beyond 165 °F, up to about 175 to 185 °F (80 to 85 °C), because their connective tissue softens. Breasts become dry quickly above 165 °F, so take them off promptly.

### Step 3: Rest

Transfer the chicken to a plate and let it rest for five minutes while you make the sauce. Resting lets the juices settle back into the meat, so they stay inside when you cut it instead of running onto the board.

### Step 4: Make the pan sauce

Look at the bottom of the pan. Those golden-brown bits stuck to it are called fond, and they are concentrated flavor. A pan sauce dissolves them into a quick, glossy sauce.

1. **Pour off excess fat**, leaving about a tablespoon in the pan. Lower the heat to medium.
2. **Add aromatics** such as a sliced shallot, a little garlic or ginger, and cook for 30 seconds to a minute until fragrant.
3. **Deglaze** by adding about 1/2 to 3/4 cup (120 to 180 ml) of liquid: chicken stock, a splash of wine, citrus juice or even water. Scrape the bottom of the pan with a wooden spoon to loosen the browned bits.
4. **Reduce** by letting the liquid simmer for two to four minutes until it thickens slightly and the flavor concentrates.
5. **Finish** by turning off the heat and stirring in a knob of cold butter, a spoonful of mustard, cream or fresh herbs. Taste and adjust the seasoning with salt, pepper and a little lemon juice.
6. **Return the chicken** and any juices from the plate to the pan, spoon the sauce over it and serve.

## Five weeknight variations

Once you know the method, you can change the flavors endlessly. Here are five ideas to rotate through the week.

### Lemon, garlic and herb

Sear the chicken, then make a sauce with shallot, garlic, chicken stock and lemon juice. Finish with butter and plenty of chopped parsley. Serve with rice, potatoes or crusty bread.

### Creamy mustard and mushroom

After searing, cook sliced mushrooms in the pan until golden. Add garlic, then deglaze with stock. Stir in a spoonful of Dijon mustard and a splash of cream, and simmer until thick. Sprinkle with thyme. This is excellent with mashed potatoes or egg noodles.

### Honey, soy and ginger

Sear boneless thighs. In the pan, cook grated ginger and garlic, then add a mixture of soy sauce, honey, a little rice vinegar and water. Simmer until sticky, then toss the chicken in the glaze. Garnish with scallions and sesame seeds and serve with rice and steamed greens.

### Tomato, olive and caper

After searing, cook sliced onion and garlic, then add a can of chopped tomatoes, a handful of olives and a spoonful of capers. Simmer for ten minutes, return the chicken and finish cooking it in the sauce. Add basil at the end. Serve with pasta or polenta.

### Smoky paprika and peppers

Season the chicken with smoked paprika and cumin before searing. Then sauté sliced bell peppers and onions in the pan until soft and slightly charred. Add garlic and a splash of stock, scrape up the browned bits and return the chicken. Serve with tortillas, rice or a green salad.

## Making a complete meal in one pan

To make dinner even easier, cook vegetables in the same pan.

- **Quick vegetables** such as spinach, zucchini, cherry tomatoes, snap peas or green beans can be cooked in the pan after the chicken and before making the sauce.
- **Slower vegetables** like potatoes or carrots can be cut small and browned first, then pushed to the side or removed while the chicken cooks. Alternatively, finish the whole skillet in the oven.
- **Starches** such as canned beans, cooked rice or gnocchi can be warmed in the sauce at the end.

Serve with a simple salad or bread, and you have a complete meal.

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Pale chicken:** The pan was not hot enough or the chicken was wet. Preheat the pan well and pat the meat dry.
- **Dry chicken breast:** It was cooked too long or was uneven in thickness. Pound it evenly, use a thermometer and take it off at 165 °F (74 °C).
- **Chicken sticks to the pan:** It was moved too early. Wait until it releases naturally.
- **Burnt fond:** The heat was too high. If the bottom of the pan is black rather than brown, the sauce will taste bitter. Wipe out the pan and make the sauce with fresh oil.
- **Thin, watery sauce:** It needs more reduction. Let it simmer a few minutes longer, or finish it with a little butter to give it body.

## Storing leftovers

Cooked chicken should be refrigerated within two hours of cooking, in a shallow, covered container. It keeps for three to four days. Reheat leftovers until hot throughout, to 165 °F (74 °C). Leftover chicken is also excellent cold in sandwiches, wraps and salads, or chopped into fried rice and pasta.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it safe if my chicken is still slightly pink?**
Color is not a reliable sign of doneness. Chicken cooked to 165 °F (74 °C) is safe even if it is slightly pink near the bone, and undercooked chicken can look white. Always use a thermometer.

**Can I use frozen chicken?**
It is best to thaw chicken in the refrigerator overnight before cooking. Frozen chicken does not brown well and cooks unevenly. If you are in a hurry, seal it in a bag and thaw it in cold water, changing the water every 30 minutes.

**What can I use instead of wine for deglazing?**
Chicken or vegetable stock works perfectly. Add a little lemon juice or vinegar at the end for brightness.

**Why do my thighs taste better than breasts?**
Thighs have more fat and connective tissue, which keeps them moist and adds flavor. They are more forgiving if cooked a little longer.

**How do I keep the first batch warm while I cook the second?**
Place the cooked chicken on a plate in an oven set to about 200 °F (95 °C), loosely covered with foil.

## The bottom line

One-pan chicken dinners follow a simple pattern: dry and season the chicken, sear it in a hot skillet without moving it, cook it to 165 °F (74 °C), let it rest and build a quick pan sauce from the browned bits. With thighs or evenly pounded breasts and a thermometer, you will get juicy chicken every time. Change the aromatics, liquid and finishing touch to create endless variations, add vegetables to the same pan, and enjoy a complete, flavorful dinner in about thirty minutes with only one pan to wash.
