Muffins and quick breads are the friendliest kind of baking. There is no yeast to wake up, no dough to knead and no waiting for anything to rise. Mix two bowls of ingredients together, pour the batter into a tin and, less than an hour later, the kitchen smells of warm banana bread or blueberry muffins. They are perfect for breakfast, lunchboxes and afternoon snacks, and they are an excellent way to use up overripe fruit.

Yet quick breads have their own pitfalls: muffins that come out tough and full of tunnels, loaves that sink in the middle, fruit that sinks to the bottom, tops that stay flat. This guide explains the classic muffin method and why it works, the most common mistakes and how to avoid them, how to get domed muffin tops, how to add fruit, nuts and other mix-ins, how to tell when a quick bread is done, and how to store and freeze your baking.

## What makes a quick bread "quick"

Quick breads rise with **chemical leaveners**, baking powder and baking soda, rather than yeast. When these leaveners meet liquid and heat, they release carbon dioxide gas, which makes the batter rise in the oven. Because no yeast is involved, there is no waiting time for dough to rise. Our guide to [baking soda vs. baking powder](/kitchen/baking-soda-vs-baking-powder/) explains the difference between the two and when each is used.

Quick breads include muffins, banana bread and other loaf cakes, cornbread, soda bread, scones and some pancakes. Our guide to [biscuits and scones](/kitchen/biscuits-and-scones/) covers a related method using cold butter.

## The muffin method

Most muffins and many quick breads use the same simple technique, often called the **muffin method**:

1. **Mix the dry ingredients** in one bowl: flour, sugar, baking powder or soda, salt and any spices.
2. **Mix the wet ingredients** in another bowl: eggs, milk or yogurt, melted butter or oil, and vanilla or mashed fruit.
3. **Pour the wet into the dry** and stir gently, just until combined. A few streaks of flour and a lumpy batter are perfectly fine.
4. **Fold in mix-ins** such as fruit, nuts or chocolate chips with a few strokes.
5. **Fill the tin** and bake.

### Why gentle mixing matters

Flour contains proteins that form gluten when they are mixed with liquid. A little gluten gives structure, but too much makes muffins tough, rubbery and full of long tunnels. That is why the muffin method stops mixing as soon as the dry ingredients are moistened. Our guide to [gluten explained](/kitchen/gluten-explained/) explains this in more detail.

A useful rule: **stir fewer times than you think you need**, about ten to fifteen gentle folds, scraping the bottom of the bowl.

## Measuring and ingredients

- **Measure flour correctly.** Too much flour makes dry, dense muffins. Weighing ingredients is the most accurate method; if you use cups, spoon flour into the cup and level it rather than scooping from the bag. See our guide to [measuring ingredients for baking](/kitchen/measuring-ingredients-for-baking/).
- **Check your leaveners.** Baking powder and soda lose strength over time. Test baking powder by stirring a little into hot water; it should bubble vigorously.
- **Use room-temperature ingredients** where possible, especially eggs and dairy, which mix more evenly.
- **Melted butter or oil?** Butter gives flavor; oil gives a moister crumb that stays soft longer. Many recipes use a mix.
- **Acidic ingredients** such as yogurt, buttermilk or sour cream make muffins tender and work with baking soda.

## Getting a domed muffin top

Bakery muffins often have tall, domed tops. A few techniques help at home:

- **Fill the cups generously**, almost to the top, so the batter has somewhere to go.
- **Rest the batter** for fifteen to thirty minutes before baking, or even overnight in the refrigerator for some recipes, which lets the flour hydrate and thickens the batter.
- **Start with a hot oven.** Some bakers start at a higher temperature for the first few minutes to give a quick burst of rise, then lower it to finish baking. Follow your recipe or experiment carefully.
- **Use a thick batter**: a batter that is too runny spreads rather than rising.

Our guide to [oven temperature and baking](/kitchen/oven-temperature-and-baking/) explains why an accurate oven matters.

## Adding fruit, nuts and other mix-ins

### Fruit

- **Fresh berries:** fold them in gently at the end. Tossing them in a spoonful of the flour mixture first helps keep them suspended.
- **Frozen berries:** add them straight from frozen to prevent the color bleeding through the batter; they may need a few extra minutes of baking.
- **Banana:** use very ripe, spotty bananas for the most flavor and sweetness.
- **Apples and pears:** dice them small or grate them.
- **Dried fruit:** soak in hot water or juice for a few minutes if very dry, then drain.

### Nuts and seeds

Toast nuts lightly in a dry pan or the oven for more flavor. Chop them roughly and fold them in at the end.

### Chocolate and other additions

Chocolate chips, chopped chocolate, citrus zest, spices, oats and seeds all work well. Keep the total amount of mix-ins reasonable, so the batter can still hold them.

### Savory muffins

Muffins do not have to be sweet. Cheese, herbs, cooked vegetables, sweetcorn and ham make excellent savory muffins for lunches and picnics.

## Loaves: banana bread and other quick breads

Quick bread loaves use the same method but bake longer at a moderate temperature.

- **Use the right tin size**; too small a tin can cause overflow and an undercooked center.
- **Line the tin** with parchment for easy removal.
- **Bake in the center of the oven.**
- **Tent with foil** if the top browns too quickly before the center is done.
- **A crack along the top** is normal for many loaves and shows that the batter rose well.

## Testing for doneness

- **Toothpick or skewer test:** insert it into the center; it should come out clean or with a few moist crumbs, not wet batter.
- **Spring-back test:** press the top gently; it should spring back.
- **Color:** the top should be golden brown.
- **Internal temperature:** some bakers use a thermometer, following the temperature in their recipe.

Loaves take much longer than muffins. Start checking a little before the time in the recipe, because ovens vary.

## Common problems and fixes

| Problem | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Tough, rubbery muffins with tunnels | overmixing | stir gently, just until combined |
| Dry, dense crumb | too much flour, overbaking | measure carefully; check earlier |
| Flat tops | batter too thin, old leavener, cups underfilled | thicker batter, fresh leavener, fill cups higher |
| Sunken center | underbaking, too much leavener, opening the oven early | bake fully, measure leavener, keep the door closed |
| Fruit sank to the bottom | batter too thin, fruit too heavy | toss fruit in flour; use a thicker batter |
| Bitter or soapy taste | too much baking soda | measure accurately |

## Muffin tins and liners

Paper liners make cleanup easy and help muffins stay moist, but muffins baked directly in a greased tin often develop crisper, more golden edges. If you use liners, fill them generously; if you grease the tin, use butter or oil on the bottom and sides of each cup. Fill any empty cups with a little water when baking a partial batch, which helps the tin heat evenly.

## Cooling and storing

- **Cool muffins** in the tin for a few minutes, then move them to a wire rack, so the bottoms do not become soggy.
- **Cool loaves** in the tin for about ten minutes before turning them out.
- **Store** at room temperature in an airtight container for a day or two, placing a paper towel underneath to absorb moisture.
- **Freeze** muffins and sliced loaves, wrapped well, for longer storage. Thaw at room temperature or warm briefly in the oven.

Our guide to [cooling, storing and freezing baked goods](/kitchen/storing-and-freezing-baked-goods/) has more detail.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I reduce the sugar in muffin recipes?

You can usually reduce it a little, but sugar also affects moisture, tenderness and browning. Reduce gradually and expect a slightly different texture.

### Can I make muffin batter the night before?

Many muffin batters can be refrigerated overnight, which can even improve the dome. Recipes with baking soda and acidic ingredients may lose some lift, so test with your favorite recipe.

### How do I turn a muffin recipe into a loaf?

Bake it in a lined loaf tin at a slightly lower temperature for much longer, checking with a skewer, and tent with foil if the top browns too quickly.

## The bottom line

Muffins and quick breads rise with baking powder and soda, so they need no kneading or rising time. Mix dry and wet ingredients separately, combine them gently with just a few strokes, fold in mix-ins and bake in a properly preheated oven. Measure flour carefully, keep leaveners fresh, fill muffin cups generously and rest the batter for taller tops. Test with a skewer, cool on a rack and freeze extras. With these habits, every batch will be tender, moist and well risen.
