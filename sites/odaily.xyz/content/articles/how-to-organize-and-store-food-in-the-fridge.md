The refrigerator is the hardest-working appliance in most kitchens, yet few of us think much about how we use it. Groceries go wherever there is space, leftovers get pushed to the back, and the vegetable drawer becomes a place where good intentions go to wilt. But how and where food is stored in the fridge makes a big difference. The right temperature slows the growth of bacteria, the right shelf prevents raw meat from contaminating other food, and the right drawer keeps vegetables crisp for longer. This guide explains how your refrigerator works, where to store different foods and how to keep everything fresh, safe and easy to find.

## Get the temperature right

The most important factor in refrigerator food safety is temperature. Bacteria that cause foodborne illness grow fastest between 40 °F and 140 °F (4 °C and 60 °C), a range known as the danger zone. Keeping your fridge at **40 °F (4 °C) or below** slows their growth significantly. Many experts recommend aiming for 37 °F (3 °C), a little below the limit, to provide a safety margin. The freezer should be at **0 °F (−18 °C)**.

Many refrigerators have dials marked with numbers rather than actual temperatures, and built-in displays are not always accurate. An inexpensive appliance thermometer, placed on a middle shelf, tells you the real temperature. Check it regularly, especially after a power outage or in hot weather.

## Understanding the zones in your fridge

Temperatures vary inside a refrigerator. Cold air sinks, the back is usually colder than the front, and the door is the warmest part because it is exposed to room air every time it opens. Using these zones wisely helps food last longer.

| Zone | Typical temperature | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Door | warmest | condiments, juices, butter, jams, pickles |
| Upper shelves | moderate and steady | leftovers, ready-to-eat foods, drinks, herbs |
| Lower shelves | coldest | raw meat, poultry and fish, dairy, eggs |
| Crisper drawers | humid, slightly cooler | vegetables and fruit |

Refrigerator layouts differ, so check your own fridge with a thermometer to find its coldest and warmest areas.

## Where to store each type of food

### Raw meat, poultry and seafood: bottom shelf

Store raw meat, poultry and fish on the bottom shelf, in their packaging and on a plate or tray or in a sealed container. This is the coldest part of the fridge and prevents juices from dripping onto other foods, which could spread bacteria such as *Salmonella* and *E. coli*. Some refrigerators have a dedicated meat drawer, which works well.

Cook or freeze raw poultry, ground meat and seafood within one to two days, and steaks, chops and roasts within three to five days.

### Dairy and eggs: lower or middle shelves

Milk, yogurt, cream and cheese stay freshest on a cold shelf, not in the door. The same goes for eggs. Many refrigerators have an egg rack in the door, but the temperature there fluctuates. Store eggs in their original carton on a shelf instead. The carton protects them from absorbing odors and shows the date.

### Leftovers and ready-to-eat foods: upper and middle shelves

Leftovers, cooked foods, deli meats, prepared salads and anything that does not need cooking should go on upper or middle shelves, above raw meat. Store them in shallow, airtight containers and label them with the date. Clear containers help you see what is inside so nothing gets forgotten.

### Fruits and vegetables: crisper drawers

Crisper drawers are designed to control humidity. Many have adjustable vents.

- **High-humidity drawer (vent closed):** For produce that wilts easily, such as leafy greens, herbs, broccoli, carrots, cucumbers, peppers and zucchini.
- **Low-humidity drawer (vent open):** For produce that tends to rot, especially fruits that release ethylene gas, such as apples, pears, stone fruits, kiwis and avocados once ripe.

Ethylene is a natural ripening gas. Ethylene-producing fruits can make nearby vegetables spoil faster, which is why it is helpful to store them separately.

### Condiments and drinks: the door

The door is fine for foods that contain preservatives like salt, sugar or vinegar and are less sensitive to temperature changes: ketchup, mustard, mayonnaise, soy sauce, jams, pickles, salad dressings and bottled drinks. Butter is often stored in a covered compartment in the door.

## What does not belong in the fridge

Some foods keep better at room temperature, and refrigeration can change their texture or flavor:

- **Tomatoes:** Cold temperatures can dull their flavor and make them mealy. Keep them on the counter until ripe and eat them soon. Very ripe tomatoes can go in the fridge for a day or two to prevent spoilage, then come back to room temperature before eating.
- **Bananas:** The skin turns black in the fridge, though the inside is usually fine.
- **Onions and garlic:** Store them in a cool, dark, well-ventilated place. Cut onions should be refrigerated in a sealed container.
- **Potatoes and sweet potatoes:** Store them in a cool, dark, well-ventilated place away from onions.
- **Bread:** It goes stale faster in the fridge. Keep it at room temperature for a few days or freeze it for longer storage.
- **Whole melons, avocados and stone fruit before they are ripe:** Ripen them on the counter, then refrigerate once ripe to slow further ripening.
- **Honey:** It crystallizes in the fridge and keeps indefinitely at room temperature.

## Cooling hot food safely

It is a common myth that hot food should cool completely on the counter before going into the fridge. In fact, food should be refrigerated within two hours of cooking, or within one hour if the room is above 90 °F (32 °C). To cool large amounts quickly:

- Divide food into small, shallow containers.
- Cut large roasts into smaller pieces.
- Stir soups and stews in a sink of ice water before refrigerating.
- Leave space around containers so cold air can circulate.

Modern refrigerators can handle warm food, but putting a very large pot of hot food in the fridge can raise the temperature of everything around it.

## Do not overcrowd

Refrigerators need air circulation to keep food evenly cold. Packing every shelf tightly blocks the flow of cold air and creates warm spots. Leave some space between items and avoid blocking the vents, usually at the back of the fridge. On the other hand, a nearly empty fridge cycles more often, so a moderately full fridge is ideal.

## Keep it clean

A clean fridge is a safer fridge.

- **Wipe up spills immediately**, especially juices from raw meat.
- **Clean shelves and drawers regularly**, every few weeks, with warm, soapy water. Rinse and dry them well.
- **Check for expired and spoiled food** every week, ideally before your main grocery shop.
- **Control odors** with an open box of baking soda, replaced every few months.
- **Wash produce just before eating**, not before storing, because extra moisture encourages mold and spoilage. Leafy greens can be washed, dried thoroughly and stored wrapped in a towel.

## Organization habits that reduce waste

- **First in, first out:** Place new groceries behind older ones so you use older food first.
- **Create an "eat first" bin:** A small container on a visible shelf for foods that need to be used soon.
- **Label leftovers** with the contents and date.
- **Use clear containers** so you can see what is inside.
- **Plan a weekly leftovers meal** to use up odds and ends before they spoil.

## Power outages

During a power outage, keep the refrigerator and freezer doors closed as much as possible. A closed refrigerator keeps food cold for about four hours. A full freezer holds its temperature for about 48 hours, a half-full freezer for about 24 hours. After the power returns, check the temperature. Discard perishable foods such as meat, poultry, seafood, milk, eggs and leftovers that have been above 40 °F (4 °C) for more than two hours.

## Frequently asked questions

**What temperature should my refrigerator be?**
At or below 40 °F (4 °C), ideally around 37 °F (3 °C). The freezer should be at 0 °F (−18 °C).

**Why should raw meat go on the bottom shelf?**
It is the coldest area, and storing meat there prevents juices from dripping onto foods that will not be cooked.

**Is it safe to put hot food in the fridge?**
Yes. Refrigerate food within two hours. Dividing it into shallow containers helps it cool quickly and safely.

**Should eggs be stored in the door?**
It is better to store eggs in their carton on a shelf, where the temperature is more stable.

**How often should I clean my refrigerator?**
Wipe spills immediately, check for old food weekly and give shelves and drawers a thorough clean every few weeks.

## The bottom line

A well-organized refrigerator keeps food safe, fresh and easy to use. Keep it at 40 °F (4 °C) or below and check the temperature with a thermometer. Store raw meat on the bottom shelf, dairy and eggs on cold shelves, leftovers and ready-to-eat foods higher up, produce in the crisper drawers and condiments in the door. Keep some foods out of the fridge, cool cooked food quickly, avoid overcrowding and clean regularly. These simple habits will help your food last longer, reduce waste and protect your family's health.
