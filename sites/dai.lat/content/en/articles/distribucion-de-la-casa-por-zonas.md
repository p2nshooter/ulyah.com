There are big houses that feel uncomfortable and small houses that work wonderfully. The difference is usually in the distribution: in how the rooms are related to each other, where you enter, what is close to what and how many meters are lost in hallways that serve no purpose. A good layout makes daily life flow without anyone noticing. A bad one forces you to cross the bedroom to get to the bathroom, to receive visitors next to the hanging clothes or to listen to television from the bed.

Distributing well is not a matter of taste, but of understanding how a home is used. In this guide you will see how to organize the house into zones, what relationships should be respected, what guiding measures the environments have, how to reduce circulation and how to think about a home that can grow and adapt over the years, something very common in Latin America.

## The three zones of a house

Almost all homes can be organized into three large areas:

| Area | Typical environments | What do you need |
|---|---|---|
| Social | entrance, living room, dining room, open kitchen, terrace, grill area | light, spaciousness, relationship with the outside |
| Private | bedrooms, private bathrooms, study, dressing room | silence, privacy, ventilation |
| Service | kitchen, laundry, service yard, cellar, garage | direct access, ventilation, resistance to use |

The basic rule is that **each area has its own character** and that the circulations between them are clear. The ideal is that a visitor can enter, use a bathroom and reach the terrace without going through the bedrooms, and that someone returning from the market can reach the kitchen without going through the living room.

## A journey from public to private

A well-distributed house usually has a **gradient of privacy**: from the entrance door you first pass through the most public spaces and, at the end, you reach the most private ones.

1. **Entry or hall:** a small transition space to leave keys, coats or shoes and prevent the door from opening directly onto the living room.
2. **Social area:** living room, dining room and, if open, kitchen.
3. **Private area:** bedrooms and bathrooms, ideally grouped and somewhat separated.

In two-story houses, the usual solution is to leave the social area on the ground floor and the private area upstairs. In one-story houses, it is advisable to separate the areas with a short hallway, a patio or a change in level.

## Relationships that should be respected

- **Kitchen near the dining room** and with practical access to the service patio or laundry room.
- **Kitchen near the service entrance or garage,** to unload purchases without crossing the entire house.
- **Guest bathroom** accessible from the social area.
- **Bedrooms away from the street** and the meeting area, so that noise does not interrupt rest. More about noise in the [acoustic insulation at home] guide (/articulos/aislamiento-acustico-en-casa/).
- **Laundry next to an outdoor space** where you can hang clothes, such as a service patio, a roof terrace or a ventilated balcony.
- **Grouped wet areas:** kitchen, bathrooms and laundry close together, or stacked on different floors, reduce the cost of water and sewer installations.

## Guidance measures for the environments

Each country and municipality establishes minimum measurements in their construction regulations. These figures are only indicative for a comfortable home:

| Environment | Orientative surface | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Master bedroom | 10 to 14 m² | double bed with passage to both sides and closet |
| Secondary bedroom | 8 to 10 m² | single bed, desk and closet |
| Living room and dining room for four people | 18 to 25 m² | depending on whether the kitchen is integrated |
| Closed kitchen | 6 to 10 m² | depends on the distribution |
| full bathroom | 3 to 5 m² | see the [small bathroom] guide (/articulos/bano-pequeno-medidas-y-distribucion/) |
| Laundry | 3 to 5 m² | with space for laundry, washing machine and clothesline |

More important than the meters is the **shape**. A square bedroom of 10 square meters is usually better furnished than an elongated one of the same size with poorly located doors and windows.

## Corridors: the meters that are not inhabited

Hallways are necessary, but every meter of hallway is a meter that is not used for living. Some ideas to reduce them:

- **Group bedroom doors** around a small foyer instead of lining them up a long hallway.
- **Turn circulation into useful space,** for example with a slightly wider hallway that includes a bookcase, a desk or a storage unit.
- **It integrates circulations in open environments,** such as a room through which one passes to the terrace.

When the hallway is unavoidable, it should be at least 90 centimeters wide, and much more if it must allow the passage of a wheelchair.

## Doors, windows and furniture

A layout is tested by furnishing it. Before accepting a plan:

- **Draw the furniture to scale:** bed, closet, sofa, table and appliances.
- **Check the swing of the doors** so that they do not collide with furniture or other doors.
- **Leave free walls** to support the bed, sofa or kitchen furniture. A room with doors and windows on all its walls is difficult to furnish.
- **Locate the windows** thinking about light and cross ventilation, as we explain in the [cross ventilation] guide (/articulos/ventilacion-cruzada/).

## The orientation decides where each zone goes

Once the zones are defined, they are distributed according to the sun and the wind. In general terms, the social area takes advantage of the best orientation and views, the bedrooms look for the cooler or sunnier side depending on the climate, and the service area can occupy less favorable orientations, such as the west façade in hot climates. The guide on [home solar orientation](/articulos/orientacion-solar-de-la-casa/) explains how to decide.

## Very Latin American spaces

Many homes in the region include spaces that rarely appear in international magazines and that should be planned from the beginning:

- **Service patio or outdoor laundry room** with stone or cement laundry room, clothesline and access from the kitchen.
- **Livable roof,** very common in Mexico and other areas, used to hang clothes, store water tanks or water tanks and, sometimes, as a terrace.
- **Grill, barbecue or barbecue area,** central to the social life of Argentina, Uruguay or Chile.
- **Carport or garage** that often becomes a workshop, commercial premises or space for a future extension.
- **Water tanks and cisterns** that need space, access for cleaning and a correct technical location.

## A house that can grow

In Latin America it is very common to build in stages: first the ground floor, years later a second floor or one more room. A good initial distribution facilitates this growth:

- **Leave the location of the staircase** and its space planned.
- **Design the structure** from the beginning to support the future floor, with an engineer's calculation.
- **Provisions for water, drainage and electricity outlets** for the bathrooms and kitchen of the future.
- **Think flexible environments:** a studio that can be a bedroom or a ground floor bedroom for an elderly person.

The guide on [extending the house with a second floor](/articulos/ampliar-la-casa-segundo-piso/) explains the technical and legal aspects.

## How to plan step by step

1. **Make a list of needs:** how many people live, how they work, if they receive visitors, if they cook a lot, if they have pets or a vehicle.
2. **Draw a bubble diagram:** circles for each environment and lines for the relationships that should be close.
3. **Locate the areas on the land** according to orientation, access, views and noise.
4. **Go to a scale plan** with actual measurements and furniture.
5. **Simulates the daily routine:** get up, shower, have breakfast, go out, return with shopping, receive visitors.
6. **Consult an architect** for a new project or a major renovation. In addition to designing, know the local regulations and necessary permits.

## Frequent errors

- **Bedrooms that open directly to the living room** or kitchen.
- **A single bathroom** that can only be reached by crossing a bedroom.
- **Long, dark hallways** without any use.
- **Laundry room with no place to hang** or ventilation.
- **Doors that collide** with each other or with furniture.
- **Not anticipating growth** when you know the house will expand.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an open or closed kitchen better?

It depends on how you cook. The open kitchen integrates the family and gives space, but spreads odors and noise. If you cook a lot with frying, consider a semi-open kitchen or good extraction. We see it in the [kitchen layout](/articulos/distribucion-de-la-cocina/) guide.

### Where do I put my studio or home office?

In a quiet place, with good natural light and somewhat separated from the social area. If you receive clients, it should be close to the entrance so that they do not have to cross the house.

### Is it advisable to have a bedroom on the ground floor?

In two-story houses, it is a great advantage: it is suitable for visitors, for elderly people or for someone recovering from an injury.

## In summary

Organize the house into a social, private and service area, with a route that goes from public to private. Keep the kitchen close to the dining room and service access, offer a guest bathroom, keep the bedrooms away from noise and group wet areas. Reduce the hallways, check the layout by drawing the furniture to scale and distribute the areas according to the sun and wind. If the house will grow, plan the staircase, structure and facilities from the beginning.
