In many Latin American cities, houses grow as families grow. First the ground floor is built, we live in it for a few years and, when the money arrives or a child forms their own home, a second floor is built. The steel rods that protrude from the columns on so many rooftops, awaiting expansion, are part of the region's urban landscape.

Building upwards makes sense: the land is used without losing a patio or garden. But it is also one of the most delicate works that can be done in a home. A second floor adds a lot of weight, changes the behavior of the house during an earthquake and affects the installations, waterproofing and the relationship with the neighbors. In this guide you will see what to check before starting, what permits are usually necessary, how to plan the staircase and the facilities, what construction systems exist and what errors cost the most.

## First: does the house hold up?

The most important question is not what the new floor will be like, but whether the existing structure can support it. Many one-story houses were built without thought for an addition, or with columns, beams, and foundations sized only for a roof slab.

A **civil or structural engineer** must evaluate:

- **The foundation:** its size, depth and condition, and the type of soil.
- **Columns and beams:** their dimensions, the steel they contain and their condition.
- **The current slab:** it can function as a floor, with the weight of walls, furniture and people.
- **The walls:** if they are load-bearing, if they are confined and if they have cracks.
- **The seismic behavior** of the complete house with the new floor.

Sometimes the answer is that structure works. Other times it is necessary to **reinforce** columns, expand foundations or add new elements. And in some cases, it's safest to opt for a lightweight system or forego expansion.

## Rods waiting

Leaving rods protruding from the columns to continue building later is a widespread practice. It has a problem: steel exposed to the elements for years **rusts**, loses section and adhesion, and the rust can advance towards the interior of the column.

If your home has rods on standby:

- **Protect them** with an anti-corrosion coating, cement grout, or caps, if the expansion will take a while.
- **Ask the engineer to review them** before building. Heavily rusted rods may require cleaning, treatment or reinforcing.

## Permits and regulations

Expanding a home almost always requires a municipal permit or license. Although the names and procedures change depending on the country and city, they are usually revised:

- **The height and number of floors allowed** in your area.
- **The occupancy and construction coefficients,** which limit how much can be built on the land.
- **The setbacks or distances** to the boundaries and the street.
- **Windows facing neighbors:** Many regulations limit openings that look directly onto the adjacent land.
- **The structural calculation** signed by a professional.
- **In condominiums,** the internal rules and the approval of the assembly.

Building without a permit can bring fines, demolition orders and problems when selling, mortgaging or inheriting the home, in addition to leaving the family without technical support if something goes wrong.

## The ladder: deciding your place from the beginning

The staircase occupies space on both floors and defines how they relate. There are two great options:

- **Interior staircase:** integrates the house and protects from rain. Requires removing space from the ground floor.
- **Exterior staircase:** allows independent access, useful if the second floor will be for another family or for rent. It must be protected from rain and sun.

Guidance measures for a comfortable and safe staircase:

| Element | Indicative measure |
|---|---|
| Height of each step | 16 to 18cm |
| Imprint depth | 25 to 30cm |
| comfort rule | two heights plus a footprint between 60 and 64 cm |
| Width | at least 80 to 90 cm |
| Clear height over stairs | at least 2 m |
| handrails | about 90 cm, firm and continuous |

All steps must be the same height. A step different from the others is a common cause of falls.

## Facilities: water, drainage and electricity

- **Stacking bathrooms and kitchen:** Placing the wet areas of the second floor above those of the first facilitates drainage and reduces costs.
- **Check the water pressure:** on the second floor it may be insufficient, especially if the tank is at a low height. A pump or raising the tank may be required.
- **Tanks and water tanks:** water weighs a lot. A full tank of a thousand liters weighs about a ton. It must rest on calculated structural elements, not on any part of the slab.
- **Electricity:** A new apartment usually requires new circuits and sometimes increases the capacity of the connection. This must be done by a qualified electrician.

## The old roof becomes a floor

The slab that until now was the ceiling will become the floor. Suitable:

- **Check and repair** cracks and damaged areas.
- **Think about acoustic insulation** between floors, especially if different families will live there. We see it in the guide to [acoustic insulation at home](/articulos/aislamiento-acustico-en-casa/).
- **Waterproof the new roof well,** and protect it from the sun. The [roof thermal insulation](/articulos/aislamiento-termico-del-techo/) guide explains how.
- **Plan how the ground floor will be protected** from rain during the work.

## Construction systems: heavy or light

| System | Advantages | To take into account |
|---|---|---|
| Confined masonry and concrete slab | known system, good durability and mass | a lot of weight on the existing structure |
| Metal structure with light enclosures | less weight, speed | protection against corrosion and fire, thermal and acoustic insulation |
| Dry construction with steel or wood profiles | light, fast, less construction humidity | requires specialized design and labor |

When the existing foundations have little capacity, a lightweight system can make an expansion viable that would not be feasible with concrete. Whatever the system, it should be designed for the earthquakes in your area. The guide on [brick and block](/articulos/ladrillo-y-bloque-tipos-y-usos/) explains the principle of confined masonry.

## Budget and stages

An extension usually costs more than expected, especially if structural reinforcements appear that were not in the initial plan. Some recommendations:

- **Include in the budget** the structural evaluation, plans, permits and supervision, not just materials and labor.
- **Reserve a margin for unforeseen events,** because when opening existing walls and slabs surprises usually appear.
- **If you build in stages,** finish the structure and the new floor covering first to protect the house, and leave the finishes for later.
- **Do not sacrifice the structure** to save: finishes can be improved over time, a poorly made column cannot.

## The work in progress

1. **Hire with a detailed budget** and a phased schedule.
2. **Ensures technical supervision** during the work, especially in foundations, columns, beams and slabs.
3. **Protect the family:** Delimit the work area, take care of children and pets and plan where you will live while the noise and dust lasts.
4. **Notify the neighbors** and take care of your surrounding walls and roofs.
5. **Save plans, permits and invoices.** They will be valuable for maintenance and for any future procedures.

## Frequent errors

- **Build without engineer evaluation.**
- **Use the ceiling slab as a floor** without verifying its capacity.
- **Leaving standby rods exposed** for years without protection.
- **Locate water tanks** on any part of the slab.
- **Stairs with uneven steps** or without handrails.
- **Build without permission** and have problems when selling or inheriting.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I build the second floor with the same foundation?

Sometimes yes, but only an engineer can confirm this after evaluating the foundation, soil and structure. In many cases reinforcement is required.

### How long can I leave the rods on hold?

The ideal is to protect them from the beginning. If they will be exposed for a long time, apply an anti-corrosion treatment and have them inspected before continuing the work.

### Is an interior or exterior staircase more convenient?

If the second floor will be part of the same home, the interior floor is usually more comfortable. Whether it will be independent or for rent, the exterior facilitates privacy.

## In summary

Before building a second floor, have the structure evaluated by an engineer: foundations, columns, beams, slab and seismic behavior. Protect the waiting rods, process permits and respect heights, setbacks and windows towards neighbors. Locate the staircase from the beginning with equal steps and handrails, stack bathrooms and kitchens, support tanks on structural elements and consider lightweight systems if the foundations are limited. Supervises the work and saves all documentation.
