The mirror that's still foggy half an hour after a shower, the paint on the ceiling that's peeling, the black spots on the silicone gaskets, and that musty smell that won't go away: these are the signs of a bathroom that's failing to dry. In many homes in the region the bathroom is small, sometimes has no window and is used several times a day. Each hot shower releases a large amount of steam that, if not released, ends up condensing on walls, ceilings and furniture.

Mold does not appear due to lack of cleaning, but rather due to excess humidity. Therefore, cleaning without resolving the ventilation only gives a respite of a few weeks. In this guide you will see how mold forms, how to choose and install an extractor fan that really works, what options exist for bathrooms without windows, how to clean safely and what simple habits keep the bathroom dry.

## How mold gets

1. **The shower fills the air with steam.** Hot air can contain a lot of moisture.
2. **The steam touches cold surfaces**, such as a mirror, tile, ceiling or an exterior wall, and turns into droplets.
3. **If surfaces take hours to dry**, mold spores, which are everywhere, find water and soap scum to feed on.
4. **Black or green stains appear**, especially in corners, joints, silicones and on the ceiling above the shower.

The key, then, is for the bathroom to **dry quickly** after each use.

## Signs that your bathroom ventilates poorly

| Signal | What does it indicate? |
|---|---|
| The mirror remains foggy for a long time | the steam does not come out |
| Drops on the ceiling after shower | condensation due to lack of extraction |
| Ceiling paint that swells or peels | repeated humidity for months |
| Black dots in silicones and gaskets | persistent damp mold |
| Musty smell with the door closed | stale air and damp surfaces |
| Towels that never dry completely | high ambient humidity |

## With window: use it well

If the bathroom has a window, open it wide during and after the shower, at least 10 to 15 minutes, and leave the bathroom door closed while ventilating so that the steam does not spread throughout the house. A small, high window helps, but is often not enough on cold or windless days. In these cases, an extractor fan complements natural ventilation. The [cross ventilation](/articulos/ventilacion-cruzada/) guide explains how air currents work.

## Extractors: how to choose one that works

An extractor exhausts humid air to the outside. To be effective:

- **Sufficient flow:** As a reference, many guides indicate for a domestic bathroom an extractor of around 50 to 100 cubic meters per hour, depending on the size of the bathroom and the length of the duct. A large bathroom or one with a long duct needs more.
- **Location:** High up, near the shower and as far from the door as possible, so that air passes through the bathroom.
- **Exit to the outside:** the duct must end outside the home or in a ventilation duct of the building, never in the false ceiling or in the crawl space, where humidity would cause hidden mold.
- **Short duct with few curves:** Each elbow reduces the flow.
- **Non-return damper:** prevents cold air, odors or other contaminants from entering when the extractor is turned off.
- **Low noise:** A noisy exhaust fan ends up turned off.
- **Timer or humidity sensor:** Ideally, it should continue working 15 to 20 minutes after the shower, or turn on only when the humidity rises.

### The air has to enter from somewhere

An extractor fan can only remove air if new air enters. If the bathroom door closes tightly, the exhaust fan barely moves air. A gap of one or two centimeters under the door or a grille in the door allows makeup air to enter.

The electrical installation of the extractor, especially near the shower, must comply with local regulations and be carried out by qualified personnel.

## Bathrooms without windows

In many apartments the bathroom does not have a window and is ventilated through a **vertical duct** in the building or an extractor fan connected to it.

- **Keep the duct grilles clean** and do not cover them with furniture, pictures or tape.
- **Check if the air is really coming out:** Hold a sheet of thin paper close to the grill. If it doesn't move, the duct may be clogged or need an extractor.
- **Consult building management** before installing an exhaust fan in a shared duct - some systems have their own rules.
- **Leave the door open** after the shower if the bathroom does not have an extractor fan, so that the humidity is diluted in the rest of the home, and ventilate that area.

## Habits that keep the bathroom dry

- **Use a squeegee or squeegee** over the tiles and screen after the shower. Removing water from surfaces greatly reduces the moisture that evaporates.
- **Dry the silicone seals** in the shower with a cloth.
- **Take the towels out** to dry in a ventilated place or spread them well on separate towel racks.
- **Raise bathroom mats** to dry.
- **Slightly less hot and shorter showers** produce less steam.
- **Do not dry clothes in the bathroom** without ventilation.

## Clean Mold Safely

On small, smooth surfaces, such as gaskets, silicone or a corner of the ceiling:

1. **Ventilate** and protect yourself with gloves, mask and glasses.
2. **Use a mold-specific cleaner** and follow label directions.
3. **Never mix products.** Chlorine cleaners release toxic gases if combined with ammonia or acidic cleaners, such as anti-scale cleaners.
4. **Do not dry scrape mold,** because it spreads spores.
5. **Rinse and dry** the surface well.

**Silicones:** If mold penetrated the silicone, the stains return even if they are cleaned. It is best to remove the silicone, clean, let it dry and apply a new sanitary silicone with anti-mold protection.

**Ceilings:** After cleaning and resolving ventilation, a bathroom paint with an anti-mold additive helps stains take longer to return. More information in the [interior and exterior paints] guide (/articulos/pinturas-interior-y-exterior/).

If the mold covers a large surface, returns in a few days or there are people with asthma or allergies at home, it is advisable to ask for professional help and check for leaks, as we explain in the guide on [humidity in walls](/articulos/humedad-en-paredes-causas-y-soluciones/).

## Materials that help

Some finishing decisions make the bathroom dry faster and give mold less room to settle:

- **Ceramic or porcelain coatings** in wet areas, with well-filled joints.
- **Epoxy joints** in the shower, more resistant to water and stains than cement ones.
- **Washable paints for bathrooms** on ceilings and walls outside the splash zone.
- **Moisture resistant furniture** with sealed edges and legs that separate them from the floor.
- **Screen screens instead of curtains,** which dry faster and do not accumulate mold in the folds.

## When the problem is a leak

If the stains always appear in the same place, such as the base of the shower wall, the floor next to the shower tray, or the ceiling under another bathroom, there may be a leak and not just condensation. Open joints, lack of waterproofing or a leaking pipe keep the wall wet. The guide to [waterproofing your shower](/articulos/impermeabilizar-la-ducha/) explains how to properly protect it.

## Safety: gas and carbon monoxide

Gas water heaters, called calefón, terma or boiler depending on the country, should not be installed inside the bathroom. They need air for combustion and a gas outlet to the outside. Poorly located or poorly ventilated equipment can produce carbon monoxide, an odorless, colorless gas that can be deadly. More details in the guide on [extractor hood and gas in the kitchen](/articulos/campana-extractora-y-gas-en-la-cocina/).

## Frequent errors

- **Clean up mold without improving ventilation.**
- **Extractors that discharge into the false ceiling** or into the crawl space.
- **Doors that close tightly** without air entry.
- **Mix bleach cleaners** with acids or ammonia.
- **Cover the grilles** of the ventilation duct.
- **Wet towels and mats** that never dry out.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should the extractor run?

During the shower and about 15 to 20 minutes afterward, until the mirror is dry. A timer or humidity sensor does this automatically.

### Do plants help remove humidity from the bathroom?

Not significantly. Some plants tolerate humidity well, but they are not a substitute for ventilation.

### Does a dehumidifier work in the bathroom?

It can help in unventilated bathrooms, but it is a complementary solution. The effective thing is to extract the steam to the outside.

## In summary

Bathroom mold is combated with ventilation, not just cleaning. Open the window during and after the shower or install an extractor fan with sufficient flow, outlet to the outside, short duct, non-return damper and timer, and leave an air inlet under the door. In bathrooms without windows, take care of the building's ductwork. Dry surfaces, towels and mats, clean mold with protection and without mixing products, renew stained silicones and check for leaks. Never install a gas heater inside the bathroom.
