If the bedroom on the top floor is the warmest in your house, if the ceiling feels warm when you touch it at night, or if the cold comes down from above in winter, the problem is probably with the roof. The roof is the part of the house that receives the most sun, especially in the tropical zone, where the sun passes almost vertically for much of the year. An unprotected concrete slab or metal sheet can reach temperatures well above that of the air and continue to radiate heat inward for hours.

Protecting the roof is, in many cases, the most effective comfort improvement that can be made to a home. In this guide you will see how heat passes through the roof, what strategies exist, which one is best depending on the type of roof and the climate, and what care to take with humidity, fire and safety when working at height.

## How heat enters through the roof

The sun heats the outer surface of the roof. This heat passes through the material by conduction and, upon reaching the interior face, is transmitted to the room by radiation and convection. The hotter the outer surface gets and the easier it is to pass through the material, the more heat reaches the inside.

There are three ways to stop this process:

1. **Reflect** solar radiation before it heats the roof.
2. **Insulate** so that heat passes through the ceiling with more difficulty.
3. **Ventilate or shade** to dissipate heat before it reaches the room.

The best solutions usually combine two or three of these strategies.

## The most common roofs in the region

| Roof type | Unprotected behavior | Common solutions |
|---|---|---|
| concrete slab | accumulates heat and releases it at night | reflective coating, exterior insulation, shade |
| Metal sheet | It heats up very quickly, radiates a lot of heat and makes noise in the rain | insulation under the sheet, ventilated ceiling, insulated panel |
| Clay or ceramic tile | better than foil, especially if it is ventilated | ceiling insulation, crawl space ventilation |
| Fiber cement | it gets quite hot | bottom insulation, ventilated ceiling |
| Palm or plant materials | cool and ventilated | maintenance and protection against insects and fire |

**Be careful with old fiber cement:** many sheets manufactured decades ago contain asbestos. Do not cut, drill, sand or tear them: the released fibers are dangerous for your health. Its handling or removal must be done by specialized personnel, according to the regulations of your country.

## Strategy 1: reflect the sun

A light-colored roof or one with a **reflective coating** can greatly reduce surface temperatures. On flat slabs it is common to use white or reflective waterproofing materials that, in addition to protecting from water, return an important part of the radiation to the sky.

- **Advantages:** Economical, quick to apply, while improving waterproofing.
- **Limitations:** It loses effectiveness with dirt and aging, so it should be cleaned and renewed according to the manufacturer. In cold climates it can reduce heat gain that would be useful in winter.

## Strategy 2: isolate

Insulators contain air or other gases trapped in small cells or between fibers, making it difficult for heat to pass through.

| material | Features | Care |
|---|---|---|
| expanded polystyrene | light and economical, known by different names depending on the country | protect from sun and fire, use flame retardant versions |
| extruded polystyrene | more resistant to humidity and compression | suitable for inverted covers |
| polyurethane foam | projects and adapts to any shape | protect it from solar radiation, professional application |
| glass or rock wool | good for ceilings and crawl spaces | Protect it from water, use gloves and a mask when installing |
| Natural fibers and cellulose | option with lower environmental impact | protect from moisture and insects |

The effectiveness depends on the material and **thickness**. Each product indicates its thermal conductivity or thermal resistance. Some countries have envelope efficiency standards that set minimum levels for roofs according to the climatic zone, as is the case in Chile with its thermal regulations. Consult local regulations and technical sheets.

### Insulate outside or inside

- **On the outside, on the slab:** keeps the slab cool and protects the waterproofing from temperature changes. It requires that the insulation be protected from the sun, water and traffic, for example with a layer of mortar or tiles.
- **Inside, with a ceiling:** easier in a renovation, because it does not touch the waterproofing. The slab continues to heat up, but much less heat reaches the room.

## Strategy 3: radiant barriers

Aluminum sheets, alone or combined with bubbles or foams, reflect radiant heat. They work **only if there is an air chamber** in front of the shiny face. Glued directly to another material without air space, they lose much of their effect. They are especially useful under metal sheets, in ventilated ceilings.

## Strategy 4: double roof and ventilation

A vented plenum between the deck and ceiling allows hot air to escape before heating the room. Some keys:

- **Low air inlets** in the eaves and **high outlets** in the ridge or with wind extractors.
- **An insulated ceiling** below the chamber.
- **Protection against insects and birds** in the ventilation openings.

In tin roofs, an insulated ceiling and a ventilated soffit completely transform the comfort of the house. More details about roof types in the [roofs and roofs] guide (/articulos/techos-y-cubiertas/).

## Strategy 5: shade and green roofs

- **Shading the roof** with pergolas, separate light roofs or even well-installed solar panels reduces the radiation reaching the slab.
- **Green roofs:** a layer of substrate and plants protects the slab from the sun and cools it through evaporation. They require quality waterproofing and an anti-root barrier, drainage and, above all, verifying that the structure supports the weight of the wet substrate. This verification must be done by an engineer.

## Humidity and condensation

Poorly planned insulation can create moisture problems:

- **Leaks:** If the roof leaks, the water soaks into the insulation. Wet mineral wool loses much of its insulating capacity and can encourage mold. Resolve leaks first.
- **Condensation in cold climates:** Water vapor from the interior can condense inside the roof. In these cases, a vapor barrier is usually placed on the interior side, according to the manufacturer's instructions. In hot, humid climates, poorly placed vapor barriers can trap moisture, so get advice.

If you already have stains on your ceiling or walls, the guide on [damp in walls](/articulos/humedad-en-paredes-causas-y-soluciones/) helps identify the cause.

## Which solution to choose according to your situation

| Situation | First measure | Complete upgrade |
|---|---|---|
| Flat slab in hot weather | reflective coating | protected exterior insulation or shade on the roof |
| Flat slab in high altitude cold climate | check waterproofing | exterior insulation or insulated ceiling |
| Metal sheet | insulated ceiling | ventilated ceiling and radiant barrier with air chamber |
| Roof tile | ceiling insulation | ventilation in eaves and ridge |
| Top floor apartment | thermal curtains and night ventilation | manage a roof improvement with the administration |

In a top-floor apartment, the roof is usually a common asset of the building. Any intervention on the roof must be agreed with the administration or the board of owners, who can also take advantage of it to improve the waterproofing of all.

## Safety: fire and work at height

- **Fire:** some plastic insulators are combustible. Use flame retardant products and protect them with non-combustible materials indoors. Keep the electrical installations of the crawl space in good condition.
- **Height:** Working on ceilings is dangerous. Wear fall protection equipment, do not walk on fragile sheets, skylights or fiber cement, and for important jobs, hire experienced personnel.

## Frequent errors

- **Insulate a leaking roof** without first repairing the leak.
- **Paste a radiant barrier without an air chamber.**
- **Cut or drill old fiber cement** that may contain asbestos.
- **Leave plastic insulation exposed** to the sun or without protection against fire.
- **Completely close the ceiling** without ventilation.
- **Install a green roof** without verifying the structure.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is better on a flat slab: paint white or insulate?

Reflective coating is the fastest and most economical improvement. Insulation offers more stable protection, also in winter. Combining both gives the best results.

### Is a plaster ceiling useful without insulation?

It helps somewhat, because it creates an air chamber, but it improves a lot if you place insulation on the ceiling and ventilate the ceiling.

### How noticeable is the change?

In homes with unprotected slab or sheet roofs, the change is usually very noticeable, especially in rooms on the top floor and at night.

## In summary

The roof is the main source of heat in many homes in the region. Reflect the sun with clear coatings, insulate with the appropriate material and thickness, use radiant barriers only with an air chamber and ventilate the ceiling in sheet or tile roofs. Repair leaks before insulating, take care of condensation in cold climates, protect insulators from fire and never handle old fiber cement without specialists. Well resolved, the ceiling stops being a radiator and the house becomes much more habitable.
