Much more than the smell of food is generated in the kitchen. Every time you fry, boil or bake, the air is filled with water vapor, fat particles and, if the stove is gas, combustion gases. Without good ventilation, grease sticks to furniture and ceilings, humidity encourages mold, and odors spread throughout the house. And when gas is involved, ventilation stops being a matter of comfort and becomes a matter of safety.

In Latin America they cook a lot and with fire: fried foods, stews, tortillas on the comal, large pots for hours. Many homes use liquefied gas in cylinders or tanks, and others use natural gas through the network. In this guide you will see how to choose and size a hood, how to install it so that it really works, why the air needs to enter from somewhere and what gas safety rules should never be ignored.

## Extraction or recirculation

| Type | How it works | What eliminates | When to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extraction hood | It sucks in air and expels it outside through a duct. | grease, odors, steam, heat and combustion gases | whenever possible |
| Recirculation hood | filters the air and returns it to the kitchen | part of the grease and, with carbon filter, part of the odors | when there is no way to remove a duct |

**Recirculation does not remove water vapor, heat or combustion gases**. On a gas stove, a hood with an exit to the outside is clearly the best option.

## Sizing the hood

### Width

The hood should be **at least as wide as the stove or grill**, and ideally a little wider, to catch smoke escaping from the sides.

### Height

Each manufacturer indicates the distance between the hood and the stove. As a reference, it is usually around **65 to 75 centimeters on gas burners** and somewhat less on electric or induction hobs. A bell that is too high picks up poorly; Too low is annoying and can be a fire risk.

### Flow

The flow rate indicates how much air the hood moves, in cubic meters per hour. A practical way to estimate it is to find that the hood can renew the air in the kitchen about **ten to twelve times per hour**:

> Kitchen volume in m³ × 10 to 12 = indicative flow rate in m³/h

For example, a 3 by 3 meter kitchen with a height of 2.5 meters has about 22 cubic meters and would need around 220 to 270 cubic meters per hour. If you cook a lot with frying or the kitchen is open to the living room, more capacity is advisable. Keep in mind that the real flow rate drops with long pipelines and with many curves.

## A well-made pipeline

- **As short and straight as possible,** with few curves.
- **The diameter indicated by the manufacturer,** without reductions.
- **Rigid and smooth inside** when possible, because corrugated tubes slow down air and accumulate grease.
- **With non-return damper** so that cold air and odors do not enter when it is off.
- **With exit to the outside,** never to the false ceiling, to the crawl space or to a smoke duct of another gas appliance.

A hood connected to a heater or chimney duct can alter the escape of these gases and bring them into the house.

## The air has to enter

A powerful hood draws a lot of air. If the kitchen is closed, that air has to be replaced somewhere. When there is no air intake, the hood loses effectiveness and, what is more dangerous, it can **reverse the draft** of nearby gas heaters or stoves, introducing their combustion gases into the home.

Therefore:

- **Open a window slightly** while the bell works.
- **Do not cover permanent vents** which are required by many standards in environments with gas appliances.
- **Consult an installer** if you have a gas heater near the kitchen and a powerful hood.

## Hood maintenance

- **Metal grease filters:** Wash them frequently, for example once a month if you cook daily. Many can go in the dishwasher.
- **Charcoal filters** in recirculation hoods: These are replaced depending on the manufacturer, often every few months.
- **Accumulated grease is fuel.** A dirty hood can spread a pan fire.
- **Turn it on before you start cooking** and let it run for a few minutes when you're done.

## Gas safety

### Liquefied gas and natural gas

- **Liquefied petroleum gas,** from cylinders, cylinders or stationary tanks, is heavier than air: if it escapes, it accumulates near the floor.
- **Network natural gas** is lighter and tends to rise.

Both have an added odor to detect leaks.

### If you smell gas

1. **Do not turn on or off lights or electrical appliances,** or use matches or lighters.
2. **Close the gas or cylinder stopcock**.
3. **Open doors and windows.**
4. **Leave the house** and call the gas company or emergency services from outside.

### Check connections and hoses

- **Use certified hoses and regulators,** and respect their expiration date.
- **Check for leaks with soapy water** on the connections: if bubbles form, there is a leak. **Never use a flame** to look for leaks.
- **Keep the cylinders in a vertical position** in ventilated places, away from heat sources and never in basements.
- **Entrust fixed installations** to authorized installers, according to the regulations of your country.

### The flame says a lot

A **blue and stable** flame indicates good combustion. A yellow or orange flame with soot in the pots indicates poor combustion that produces more carbon monoxide. The device needs inspection and cleaning.

## Carbon monoxide: the invisible danger

Carbon monoxide is a colorless, odorless gas produced when combustion is incomplete. It can cause headache, dizziness, nausea, drowsiness, and, at high concentrations, death.

- **Install carbon monoxide detectors** if there are stoves, heaters or other gas appliances in your home.
- **Never use the oven or stove to heat the house.**
- **Gas water heaters,** such as the water heater, the water heater or the boiler, must have gas outlet to the outside and be in a ventilated place. Never inside a bathroom, as we remember in the [small bathroom](/articulos/bano-pequeno-medidas-y-distribucion/) guide.
- **If several people feel symptoms at once** at home, go outdoors and seek medical help.

## Fire in the kitchen

- **Never pour water on burning oil:** the water evaporates suddenly and projects the oil into flames.
- **Cover the pan or pot** with a lid or a metal tray and turn off the heat.
- **Have a kitchen-grade fire extinguisher on hand** and learn how to use it.
- **Do not leave pots with hot oil unattended.**

## What if I switch to electric or induction cooking?

Electric and induction hobs do not produce combustion gases, which improves indoor air quality. Induction is efficient and fast, but requires pots compatible with magnets and an electrical installation with sufficient capacity. Even so, the kitchen still requires extraction for steam, grease and odors. More ideas about consumption in the [energy saving at home](/articulos/ahorro-de-energia-en-casa/) guide.

## Frequent errors

- **Recirculation hoods** on gas stoves without other ventilation.
- **Ducts that end in the false ceiling.**
- **Powerful hoods without air inlet.**
- **Filters full of grease.**
- **Expired hoses** or uncertified regulators.
- **Look for leaks with fire.**

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I do if I can't take a duct outside?

A recirculation hood with filters in good condition helps with grease and odors, but it should be complemented with natural ventilation: open the window while cooking. If you cook with gas, consult an installer about the necessary ventilation.

### Can the hood go over a window?

It is not advisable to place the stove under a window, due to the risk of curtains and because the wind can blow out the flame. We see it in the [kitchen layout](/articulos/distribucion-de-la-cocina/) guide.

### Why is my hood making so much noise?

It may be due to a duct that is too narrow or with many curves, dirty filters, or always operating at maximum speed. A well-sized hood can work at medium speed with less noise and the same efficiency.

### How often should the gas installation be checked?

Follow local regulations and the instructions of the gas company. At a minimum, check hoses and connections regularly and have appliances inspected if the flame changes color or soot appears.

## In summary

Prefer an extraction hood with an outlet to the outside, at least as wide as the stove, installed at the indicated height and with a sufficient flow rate to renew the air in the kitchen about ten times per hour. Use a short, smooth, gated duct, never towards the false ceiling or a gas duct, and let in makeup air. Clean the filters frequently. With gas, use certified hoses and regulators, check for leaks with soapy water, act without sparks if you smell gas, monitor the color of the flame and install carbon monoxide detectors.
