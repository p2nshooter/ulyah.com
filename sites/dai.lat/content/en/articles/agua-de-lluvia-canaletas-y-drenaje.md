In much of Latin America it doesn't rain little by little: it rains in buckets. A tropical downpour or a summer storm can deliver in an hour what falls elsewhere in a week. If water from the roof falls directly next to the walls, if the patio does not have a slope or if the gutters are clogged, the result appears quickly: wet walls, raised floors, weakened foundations, flooded patios and, sometimes, water that enters under the doors.

Managing rainwater well is one of the cheapest ways to protect a home. It is not about large works, but about directing the water along the correct path: from the roof to the gutter, from the gutter to the downspout, and from the downspout to a place where it will not cause harm. In this guide you will see how to size and maintain gutters and downspouts, how to slope the land and patios, what to do on uneven terrain and how to prepare for heavy rains.

## The journey of water

Think of rainwater as a small river that must have a clear channel:

1. **The roof** collects water and carries it to its edges.
2. **The gutters** receive it along the eaves.
3. **The descents** lead her to the ground.
4. **Land drainage** takes it away from the foundation: to a stormwater network, to the street, to an infiltration well, to a rain garden or to a collection system.

If any of these steps fail, the water finds its own path, and it is almost always the worst.

## Gutters: size, slope and fixings

| Appearance | Usual recommendation |
|---|---|
| Size | proportional to the roof surface and the intensity of local rainfall |
| Pending | light and constant towards the descents, of a few millimeters per meter |
| Downhills | enough to not overload the gutter; often one every 10 to 12 meters of gutter |
| Supports | every few decimeters, firm, so that the gutter does not deform with the weight of the water |
| Protection | meshes or grates that retain leaves and trash |

The most common materials are PVC, galvanized or prepainted steel and aluminum. The important thing is that the joints are well sealed, that the gutter does not have sunken sections where the water stays still, and that its supports resist the weight of a full gutter.

A gutter with stagnant water not only overflows: it also breeds mosquitoes. Check your slope after installing it, spraying water with a hose.

## Downhills: let the water go far

The most common mistake is that the drain ends **close to the wall**, discharging the water right over the foundation. Some solutions:

- **Prolong the outlet** with an elbow and a horizontal section that carries the water at least a meter or more away from the house.
- **Place a splash pad** or open gutter to direct water to the drain.
- **Connect the downspout to a buried pipe** that takes water to the stormwater network, to an infiltration well or to a collection tank. The guide on [rainwater harvesting](/articulos/captacion-de-agua-de-lluvia/) explains how to use it.
- **Never discharge water from your roof** onto your neighbor's land or roof.

In many cities it is prohibited to connect rainwater to the wastewater network, because it saturates it during storms. Check local regulations.

## The slope of the land

The ground next to the house should **keep water away from the walls**. For reference, many guides recommend a slope of around 5 percent in the first few meters around the home. If the land slopes toward the house, water will accumulate against the foundation and end up leaking. The problems this causes are explained in the guide on [dampness in walls](/articulos/humedad-en-paredes-causas-y-soluciones/).

## Patios and paved surfaces

- **Slope of 1 to 2 percent** towards grates, gutters or towards the garden, never towards the door of the house.
- **Linear grilles** in front of doors and at the meeting between the patio and the house.
- **Sumps with a sandpit or trap,** that retain soil and leaves and prevent bad odors.
- **Permeable pavements,** such as paving stones with open joints or blocks with grass, which allow part of the water to infiltrate.

The guide on [non-slip outdoor flooring](/articulos/pisos-exteriores-antiderrapantes/) helps you choose surfaces that drain well and do not slip.

## Uneven terrain and retaining walls

Many homes in the region are built on slopes. There, water is a serious threat:

- **Retaining walls need drainage behind them:** a layer of gravel, a geotextile and, in many cases, a perforated tube that collects water, as well as **drainage holes** in the wall so that water pressure does not push it out.
- **Do not discharge water from the roof onto the slope** without control: it can erode it and encourage landslides.
- **Notice cracks in the ground, leaning walls or leaning trees:** these are signs to request a professional evaluation immediately.
- **Works on slopes** must be designed by engineers, with soil studies.

## Buried drains and infiltration wells

In land where water accumulates, a **buried drain**, a trench filled with gravel with a perforated tube wrapped in geotextile, captures subsoil water and directs it to a discharge point. An infiltration well receives water from the downspouts and lets it slowly seep into the ground, as long as the ground is permeable and the well is far from the foundation and drinking water wells. A **rain garden**, explained in the guide on [garden with native plants](/articulos/jardin-con-plantas-nativas/), serves a similar function with plants.

## Signs that the drain is failing

- **Water marks or mud splashes** on the lower part of the exterior walls.
- **Puddles that take hours to disappear** next to the house or in the patio.
- **Floors that sink or crack** near drop-offs.
- **Moisture or saltpeter** at the base of the interior walls after rains.
- **Gutters that leak at the joints** or overflow even though they are clean.
- **Dragged soil** that leaves furrows in the garden or covers the grates.

Each of these signs indicates that the water is not following its intended path. Correcting it in time avoids much more expensive repairs to walls, floors and foundations.

## Prepare for heavy rain

- **Non-return valves** in the home's drains prevent water from a saturated network from returning inward during a storm.
- **Raised thresholds** on doors that open to patios or the street.
- **Sandbags or barriers** for lower entrances if you live in a flood zone.
- **Appliances and electrical panels** away from floor level in areas at risk of flooding.
- **A family plan** that tells what to do and where to go if the water rises.

## Seasonal maintenance

| Moment | What to do |
|---|---|
| before the rains | clean gutters, screens, downspouts, grates and drains; review pending |
| during the season | check after strong storms, remove leaves and branches |
| after the rains | look for damp spots, sinkholes, erosion and cracks |
| Once a year | check supports, joints and condition of buried pipes |

On homes with trees nearby, gutters may need cleaning several times per season. Always work with a sturdy ladder, and for high ceilings, consider hiring someone with safety equipment. The [roofs and covers] guide (/articulos/techos-y-cubiertas/) includes recommendations for working at height.

## Frequent errors

- **Descents that discharge next to the foundation.**
- **Clogged gutters** or with sunken sections.
- **Patios with a slope towards the house.**
- **Retaining walls without drainage** or holes.
- **Discharge the water on the neighbor** or on a hillside.
- **Connect rainwater to wastewater network** where prohibited.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know if my gutters are too small?

If they overflow during heavy rains even when they are clean, they probably lack capacity or need more drops. It may also happen that the slope is insufficient.

### Can I let water fall into the garden without gutters?

In houses with wide eaves and a lot that directs water away from the walls, sometimes it works, especially with a strip of gravel where the water falls. In most cases, gutters better protect walls and foundations.

### What do I do if water enters under the patio door?

Check the slope of the patio, add a linear grate in front of the door and, if possible, raise the threshold slightly. Also check that the patio drain is not clogged.

More ideas in the section [Patios and exteriors](/temas/patios-y-exteriores/) and in the guide on [safe balconies and terraces](/articulos/balcones-y-terrazas-seguras/).

## In summary

It directs rainwater along a clear path: roof, gutter, downspout and drain away from the house. Size gutters for roof and local rainfall, give them a constant slope, protect them from leaves, and place enough downspouts that discharge away from the foundation. Slope the land and patios to direct water away from the walls, drain the retaining walls and do not discharge on neighbors or slopes. Prepare for storms with backflow valves and raised thresholds, and clean everything before each rainy season.
