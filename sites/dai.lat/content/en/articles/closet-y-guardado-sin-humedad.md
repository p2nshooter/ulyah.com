Opening the closet and finding white spots on a leather jacket, stains on a stored shirt, or that smell of confinement that sticks to all your clothes is more common than it seems. In humid climates such as those on the Caribbean coast, Guayaquil or the Amazon, and in cities with humid winters such as Lima, Bogotá or southern Chile, the closet is one of the places where mold first appears. And in any climate, moths can quietly ruin a wool sweater or alpaca blanket.

A good closet not only stores clothes: it protects them. In this guide you will see how to organize the space by areas and measurements, how to ventilate it to avoid humidity, how to prevent mold and moths, how to care for leather shoes and bags and how to store seasonal clothes so that they come out in good condition months later.

## Why does the closet accumulate moisture?

- **It is closed** and the air is hardly renewed.
- **Often against an exterior wall** or next to a bathroom, cooler or wetter surfaces.
- **We store clothes with some moisture,** recently ironed or poorly dried.
- **It is filled to capacity,** with no room for air to circulate.
- **In humid climates,** the ambient humidity is already high all year round.

If the problem is serious and stains appear on the wall behind the closet, the cause may be in the wall itself. The guide on [damp in walls](/articulos/humedad-en-paredes-causas-y-soluciones/) helps identify it.

## Ventilate: the first defense

- **Open the closet doors** for a while each day, especially on dry or sunny mornings.
- **Leave a few centimeters** between the furniture and the wall, especially if it is an exterior wall.
- **Prefers louvered doors** or slats in humid climates.
- **Do not fill it one hundred percent:** Tight clothing does not breathe and wrinkles.
- **Ventilate the room** regularly. The [cross ventilation](/articulos/ventilacion-cruzada/) guide explains how to do it right.

## Store only dry, clean clothing

- **Dry clothes completely** before putting them away. A barely damp garment can become moldy in a few days in a closed closet.
- **Let freshly ironed clothes cool** before putting them away.
- **Keep clean clothes.** Remains of sweat, food or perfume attract moths and promote odors.

## Moisture absorbers and dehumidifiers

- **Moisture absorbers** based on salts such as calcium chloride: they collect water from the air in a container. They are useful in small closets. The tank must be emptied or replaced and kept out of the reach of children and pets.
- **Silica gel bags:** are used for drawers, boxes and bags.
- **Electric dehumidifier:** in rooms with persistent humidity, a small one can make a difference, always emptying the tank regularly.

These solutions help, but are not a substitute for ventilation or correcting a leak.

## Organize by zones

A tidy closet is easier to ventilate and maintain. Organize it according to frequency of use:

- **Height of eyes and hands:** everyday clothes.
- **Above:** seasonal clothing, suitcases, blankets.
- **Below:** heavy shoes and boxes, without placing them directly on a floor that may become wet.

### Guidance measures

| Element | Indicative measure |
|---|---|
| Depth for hanging clothes | about 55 to 60 cm |
| Long garment bar | about 1.6 to 1.8 m from the floor |
| Double bar for short garments | approximately 1 m and 2 m |
| Shelves for folded clothes | 30 to 40 cm deep |
| Shoe racks | 30 to 35 cm deep |

A **double bar** for folded shirts, skirts and pants doubles the capacity in the same width.

### Hang or fold

- **Hanging:** shirts, blouses, dresses, jackets and dress pants.
- **Fold:** knitted sweaters, which become deformed when hanging, t-shirts, sportswear and jeans.
- **Equal and slim hangers** save space and give a neat image.

In small apartments, the ideas in the guide to [gain space in a small apartment](/articulos/departamento-pequeno-ganar-espacio/) also apply to the closet.

## Moths: how to prevent them

Clothes moths attack fibers of animal origin: wool, alpaca, cashmere, silk and feathers. Their larvae feed on the fibers and leave holes.

- **Keep wool garments clean:** Sweat and food stains attract them.
- **Use tightly closed bags or boxes** for seasonal clothing.
- **Check and air** stored items from time to time. Moths like darkness and stillness.
- **Vacuum the inside of the closet,** including the corners and corners.
- **Natural repellents** such as cedar wood or lavender may help somewhat, but they are not a substitute for cleaning and sealing.
- **If you detect larvae,** wash the garment according to its label or leave it in the freezer for a few days in a closed bag, a common practice to eliminate eggs and larvae.

## Leather, shoes and bags

Leather is especially sensitive to mold in humid climates.

- **Do not store them in plastic bags:** they trap moisture. Use cotton cloth bags.
- **Fill bags and shoes** with paper so they maintain their shape and absorb moisture.
- **Allow the shoes to air out** a day before storing them.
- **If mold appears,** clean the piece outdoors with a slightly damp cloth, let it dry in the shade and apply a leather care product.

## seasonal clothing

1. **Wash or clean** everything before storing it.
2. **Make sure it is completely dry.**
3. **Use breathable fabric boxes or bags** for cotton and linen, and clean airtight containers for wool and alpaca.
4. **Label each box** with its contents.
5. **Store in a dry place,** not in damp basements or next to damp walls.
6. **Check once or twice during the season.**

Seasonal blankets, curtains and rugs follow the same rules, as we explain in the [curtains, textiles and rugs](/articulos/cortinas-textiles-y-alfombras/) guide.

## The furniture also matters

- **Chipboard or MDF furniture with melamine** swells if it gets wet. Avoid placing them directly on floors that are cleaned with a lot of water.
- **Solid wood** breathes better, but it also needs ventilation. We see it in the guide on [wood at home] (/articulos/madera-en-casa-especies-y-cuidados/).
- **A bottom with small openings** or a separation from the wall improves air circulation.
- **An LED interior light** with sensor helps you see everything without leaving the doors open for too long. More ideas in the [house lighting](/articulos/iluminacion-de-la-casa/) guide.

## The entrance closet and the children's closet

**Next to the entrance,** a piece of furniture or a small closet for coats, umbrellas, backpacks and shoes prevents humidity from the street from entering the bedroom. Let umbrellas and raincoats dry open before storing them, and place a tray to catch water from wet shoes.

**In the children's room,** the closet must grow with them. Adjustable bars, low drawers and boxes with labels or drawings allow them to organize their own clothes. Clothes that no longer fit should be removed each season: a closet with fewer items ventilates better and stays in order with less effort.

## If it already smells damp

1. **Empty the closet** completely and leave the doors open for ventilation.
2. **Clean the interior** with a damp cloth and a mild cleaner, and let it dry completely.
3. **Wash the affected clothing** according to its label and dry it in the sun if the fabric allows it.
4. **Look for the cause:** filtration, condensation or lack of ventilation.
5. **Store again** with fewer clothes and more space between clothes.

## Frequent errors

- **Store damp** or freshly ironed clothes warm.
- **Fill the closet to the maximum** without space for air.
- **Plastic bags** for leather and clothing for occasional use.
- **Furniture attached** to cold exterior walls.
- **Dirty wool** stored for months.
- **Ignore the musty smell,** which is the first sign of mold.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it useful to put baking soda in the closet?

An open container of baking soda can help reduce mild odors, but it does not absorb moisture effectively. For this, specific absorbents or a dehumidifier are better.

### How often should you check the stored clothes?

Once or twice a season is enough. Take the opportunity to air out the clothes and check for mold or moths.

### Is an open closet or one with doors better?

An open closet ventilates better, but clothes accumulate more dust. In humid climates, louvered doors are a good balance.

## In summary

So that the closet does not accumulate humidity, ventilate it daily, separate it from the exterior walls, do not fill it to the maximum and store only clean and completely dry clothes. Organize it by areas of use with appropriate measurements, hang what wrinkles and fold knitted fabrics. Protect wool and alpaca from moths with cleaning and closed containers, store leather in cloth bags, and go through seasonal clothing. If a musty smell appears, empty, clean, dry and look for the cause.
