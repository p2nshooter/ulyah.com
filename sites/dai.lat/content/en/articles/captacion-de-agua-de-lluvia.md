In many Latin American cities, mains water is not as safe as it seems. There are neighborhoods with supply in shifts, increasingly longer drought seasons and unexpected outages. At the same time, in the rainy season, water falls in abundance on the roofs and goes directly to the drain, sometimes flooding the streets. Capturing part of that rain is one of the simplest ways to gain autonomy and reduce drinking water consumption.

A collection system does not have to be complex or expensive. In its simplest version it is a gutter, a filter and a tank. The most complete ones include first water separators, pumps and treatment. In this guide you will see how much water your roof can collect, what components you need, how to size the storage, what uses rainwater is suitable for and what care is essential so that it does not become a health problem.

## How much water can your roof collect?

The calculation is simple: **one millimeter of rain on one square meter is equivalent to one liter of water.**

> Liters per year ≈ roof area in m² × annual rainfall in mm × runoff coefficient

The **runoff coefficient** reflects that part of the water is lost through splashing, evaporation or absorption. For sheet, concrete or tile roofs it is usually estimated between 0.8 and 0.9.

| Roof | Annual rainfall | Approximate catchable water |
|---|---|---|
| 60 m² | 600mm | about 29,000 liters per year |
| 80 m² | 800mm | about 51,000 liters per year |
| 100 m² | 1500mm | about 120,000 liters per year |

The surface that counts is the **horizontal projection** of the roof, that is, what it would occupy seen from above, not the inclined surface. The annual rainfall in your city is published by the national meteorological services.

Keep in mind that in much of the region the rain is concentrated in some months. You can collect a lot of water in the rainy season and almost none in the dry season. This is why tank size is so important.

## The components of a system

1. **Collection surface:** the ceiling. It must be clean and in good condition. Avoid using water from roofs with old fiber cement sheets that may contain asbestos or with materials that release substances.
2. **Gutters and downspouts:** collect water and lead it to the system. They must be sloped and clean. We see it in the guide to [rainwater, gutters and drainage](/articulos/agua-de-lluvia-canaletas-y-drenaje/).
3. **Meshes or grates** in gutters and downspouts, to stop leaves and garbage.
4. **First water separator:** diverts the first rain, which carries dust, leaves, bird droppings and contaminants accumulated on the roof.
5. **Filter:** retains particles before the water reaches the tank.
6. **Tank or cistern:** stores water. It must be opaque, closed and with a secure lid.
7. **Overflow:** directs the excess to a garden, an infiltration pit or the drain.
8. **Pump and distribution:** if the water will be used in toilets, washing machines or pressure irrigation.

## The first water separator

It is one of the most important and most forgotten pieces. The first rain after several dry days washes the roof and carries away dirt. A simple separator is a vertical tube that is first filled with that dirty water; When it is full, the cleaner water passes into the tank. After each rain it is emptied through a tap or a lower hole.

For reference, many guides recommend diverting between half a liter and two liters for every square meter of roof, depending on how dirty it is and how much time has passed since the last rain.

## What size tank do I need?

It depends on three factors: how much it rains, how much water you will use, and how much space and budget you have.

- **For occasional watering and cleaning:** a tank of a few hundred to a couple of thousand liters already makes a difference.
- **For toilets and washing machines:** larger volumes are required to cover several weeks without rain.
- **To cover the entire dry season:** the volume can be very large and require buried cisterns.

A practical approach is to start with a modest tank and expand based on experience. Remember that water weighs a lot: a full thousand-liter tank weighs about a ton. If it is on a slab or a structure, it must be supported by calculated elements, as we explain in the guide on [expanding the house with a second floor](/articulos/ampliar-la-casa-segundo-piso/).

### A simple example

Imagine a family of four people who wants to use rainwater in the toilets. If each flush uses about 6 liters and each person uses the toilet about four times a day at home, consumption is around 96 liters per day, that is, about 2900 liters per month. A 2500 to 3000 liter tank would cover approximately one month without rain. With a roof of 80 square meters, a single 20 millimeter rain provides about 1,300 liters, almost half of that tank. Making this type of account with your own data helps you avoid buying a tank that is too small or one that will never be filled.

## What to use rainwater for

| Use | Is it suitable? | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Garden and plant watering | yes | with basic filter |
| Washing floors, patios and cars | yes | with basic filter |
| toilet unloading | yes | distribution system separated from the drinking network |
| washing machine | yes | with good filtration |
| Shower and personal hygiene | carefully | requires proper filtration and disinfection |
| Drink and cook | only with treatment | certified filtration and disinfection, following local regulations |

Rainwater can contain bacteria, dust, and other substances collected on the roof and gutters. **Do not drink it without treatment.** Drinking water systems require filters, disinfection and periodic checks, and some local authorities have specific rules.

**Never connect** the rain system directly to the home's drinking water network without adequate separation: rainwater could contaminate the network.

## Mosquitoes: the risk you cannot ignore

An open or poorly closed tank can become a breeding ground for mosquitoes that transmit dengue, Zika or chikungunya. To avoid it:

- **Airtight lid** on the tank.
- **Fine mesh** in all inlets, outlets and overflows.
- **Gutters without standing water:** A poorly leveled gutter also breeds mosquitoes.
- **Check frequently** that there are no larvae in the water.

## Maintenance

- **Before the rainy season:** clean the roof, gutters, meshes and the first water separator.
- **After each heavy rain:** empty the separator and check the filters.
- **Once a year:** Empty and clean the tank to remove sediment.
- **Always:** check that lids and meshes are in good condition.

## Gray water: the next step

In addition to rain, water from washing machines, showers and sinks, called gray water, can be reused for irrigation or toilets with proper filtration and storage systems. It is a more complex step, because this water contains soaps and organic remains that decompose quickly, and its reuse is regulated in many places. It is advisable to obtain advice before installing a system of this type.

## Programs and supports

In some cities in the region there are public programs that have installed collection systems in homes, especially in areas with supply problems. Check with your municipality or water authority if there are supports available or rules you must comply with.

## Frequent errors

- **Take the first rain directly to the tank.**
- **Transparent tanks** exposed to the sun, where algae grow.
- **Tanks without lid** or without mesh.
- **Connect rainwater to the drinking network.**
- **Drink untreated rainwater.**
- **Place heavy tanks** on uncalculated structures.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it worth it if it rains little in my city?

Even in dry locations, a medium-sized roof can collect thousands of liters a year. For irrigation and cleaning it can be very useful, although the system should be smaller and the storage used to the maximum in the rainy season.

### Can I use a common water tank or tank?

Yes, as long as it is opaque, has an airtight lid and is clean. For rainwater, a dedicated one is convenient, separate from the drinking water tank.

### Where should the tank be located?

In a firm place, in the shade if possible, near the roof drops and where you will use the water. If you raise it a little on a solid base, you can use gravity to water without a pump. Leave room to open the lid, clean it, and check the overflow.

### Is rainwater good for plants?

Yes. It is usually soft and chlorine-free, something that many plants appreciate. It is ideal for irrigation, as we explain in the guide on [garden with native plants](/articulos/jardin-con-plantas-nativas/).

## In summary

Each millimeter of rain on a square meter of roof is equivalent to one liter. Calculate your potential with the roof area, annual rainfall and a coefficient close to 0.8. A good system includes clean gutters, mesh, first water separator, filter, opaque and closed tank and overflow. Use the water for irrigation, cleaning, toilets and washing, and only for drinking with certified treatment. Protect the tank from mosquitoes, do not connect it to the drinking network and keep everything clean before each rainy season.
