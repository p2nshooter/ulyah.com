Apparent concrete, also called exposed concrete or exposed concrete, is concrete that is left exposed, without plaster or paint. For decades it was the trademark of modern Latin American architecture, especially in Brazil, and today it reappears in houses, apartments, cafes and offices throughout the region. Liked for its texture, its honesty and the feeling of solidity it conveys.

But the apparent concrete is not forgiving. A plastered wall hides imperfections; one seen shows them all: the formwork joints, the bubbles, the stains, the poorly vibrated gaps and every crack. Furthermore, although it may seem indestructible, it absorbs water, stains and can deteriorate if not cared for. In this guide you will see what a good finish depends on, what the most common defects are, how to protect and clean it and when a crack stops being aesthetic and becomes a matter for a professional.

## What does good apparent concrete depend on?

The final look is decided before and during casting, not after.

- **The formwork or formwork:** the concrete copies the surface of the mold. Wooden boards leave their grain marked, phenolic boards give a smooth surface and metal molds give a uniform texture. The joints between boards will also be visible, so they must be planned.
- **The release agent:** a product that is applied to the formwork so that the concrete does not adhere. If applied excessively or irregularly, it leaves stains.
- **The mixture:** an adequate and uniform dosage gives an even color. Changing suppliers or cement in the middle of the job can produce different tones.
- **Vibrating:** eliminates trapped air and makes the mixture fill the entire mold. Insufficient vibration leaves gaps; too much can separate the components.
- **Curing:** Concrete needs to be kept moist for the first few days to harden properly. Poor curing, especially in the sun and wind, favors cracks.

For all this, apparent concrete requires experienced labor and good technical supervision. It is not the place to improvise.

## Common defects and what they mean

| Defect | How it looks | Usual cause | Gravity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bubbles or surface pores | small holes on the surface | air trapped next to the mold | aesthetic |
| Crab pots or gravel nests | areas with exposed stones and holes | insufficient vibrating, grout leakage | can be structural if they are deep |
| Fine network cracks | very fine and superficial cracks | shrinkage due to drying or poor curing | usually aesthetic |
| Wide, diagonal, or growing cracks | marked cracks | settlements, overloads, structural problems | requires professional evaluation |
| rust stains | reddish or brown spots | wires or armor near the surface | may indicate corrosion of the steel |
| Efflorescence | white salt stains | moisture migrating to the surface | aesthetic, but indicates humidity |
| Green or black spots | mold, algae or lichen | persistent humidity and shade | aesthetics, it is advisable to treat |

**Rust stains** deserve special attention. They can come from a simple tie wire forgotten near the surface, but they can also indicate that the reinforcing steel has little coating and is corroding. Rusted steel increases in volume and can break the concrete that covers it. If cracks appear along the line of a rod, consult an engineer.

## Cracks: when to worry

Almost all concrete cracks to some degree because it shrinks as it dries. Fine, superficial, non-growing cracks are usually normal. Instead, consult a civil or structural engineer when:

- the fissure is wide or is noticeable when passing the nail easily,
- is diagonal, especially near corners of doors and windows,
- grows over time,
- shows rusty steel,
- appears after an earthquake,
- doors and windows begin to stick near the crack.

A simple way to monitor a crack is to mark its ends with pencil and the date, and photograph it next to a ruler. If it lengthens or widens in a few weeks, do not cover it: have it evaluated.

## Protection: water repellents and sealants

Concrete is porous. Absorbs water, grease, wine, coffee and dirt. Outdoors, rain carries dust and promotes mold. There are two large families of protection:

- **Penetrating water repellents** based on silanes or siloxanes. They penetrate the pores and repel water without forming a film. They maintain the natural appearance and allow the material to breathe. They are the usual choice for exterior walls.
- **Film-forming sealants,** acrylic, epoxy or polyurethane. They create a protective layer on the surface, resist stains better and make cleaning easier, but they change the appearance, can darken or brighten and require renewal. They are widely used in floors and roofs.

Before applying any product, test in an inconspicuous area, because some darken the concrete. The surface must be clean, dry and without open cracks. Follow the manufacturer's directions for concrete curing times before sealing.

## Cleaning

- **Regular cleaning:** water with a neutral detergent and a soft bristle brush or cloth. Rinse well.
- **Pressure washer:** useful outdoors, but at moderate pressure and without bringing the nozzle too close, so as not to erode the surface.
- **Grease stains:** mild degreasing detergents and patience. On sealed surfaces they are much easier to remove.
- **Mold and algae:** specific cleaners for facades following the instructions, rinse thoroughly and, above all, correct the humidity that causes them.
- **Avoid acids:** Concrete is alkaline and acids dissolve cement paste. Vinegar, muriatic acid, and acidic cleansers can leave marks and open pores. Use them only if a professional indicates it for a specific case.

## Concrete on floors and roofs

Polished concrete floors and concrete countertops are becoming more common. Some considerations:

- **Polished floors:** are durable and easy to clean, but can be slippery with water. Outdoors or in bathrooms, a more textured finish is advisable. We see it in the guide on [non-slip exterior floors](/articulos/pisos-exteriores-antiderrapantes/).
- **Microcement:** is a coating of a few millimeters based on cement and resins that imitates concrete. It is applied to other surfaces and has its own care.
- **Kitchen countertops in particular:** They are attractive but porous. They need a food grade seal and care with acids like lemon or vinegar.

## Heat and acoustics

Concrete has a large **thermal mass**: it accumulates heat and releases it slowly. On a roof slab exposed to the tropical sun, this means a hot interior well into the night. The solution is to insulate or shade the roof, as we explain in the [roof thermal insulation] guide (/articulos/aislamiento-termico-del-techo/).

Indoors, concrete surfaces are hard and reflect sound. An environment with concrete walls, ceiling and floor can have a lot of echo. Rugs, curtains, upholstered furniture, bookcases and absorbent panels help balance it out. More ideas in the guide on [acoustic insulation at home](/articulos/aislamiento-acustico-en-casa/).

## Cosmetic repairs

Bubbles, small detachments or beaten edges can be repaired with specific repair mortars, adjusting the color with tests. It is a delicate job: a repair of a different tone can be more visible than the original defect. For large or visible surfaces, it is advisable to hire personnel with experience in architectural concrete.

## How exposed concrete ages

Over the years, exposed concrete changes. The sun lightens some areas, the rain marks water paths under sills and cornices, and in humid places dark spots of microorganisms appear. Part of that aging is considered a patina that adds character. So that it is even and does not become deterioration, it helps to design the details well: drips under the edges of slabs and windows, finishes that keep water away from the walls and slopes that prevent puddles. Checking the façade once a year and renewing the protection when necessary greatly prolongs its useful life.

## Frequent errors

- **Think that exposed concrete does not need maintenance.**
- **Clean it with muriatic acid or vinegar.**
- **Covering growing cracks** instead of having them evaluated.
- **Ignore rust stains** that appear over time.
- **Seal without testing first** in an inconspicuous area.
- **Leaving a roof slab exposed to the sun** without insulation in hot climates.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is exposed concrete cheaper than plastering and painting?

Not necessarily. Plastering and painting are saved, but the formwork must be of better quality, the workmanship more careful and the supervision more strict. A bad result is difficult to correct.

### Can exposed concrete be painted?

Yes, with paints suitable for alkaline surfaces, but their natural appearance is lost. If you are looking to protect it without changing its appearance, a transparent water-repellent is a better option. We expand it in the guide to [interior and exterior paints] (/articulos/pinturas-interior-y-exterior/).

### How often is the sealant renewed?

It depends on the product, sun exposure and use. Facade water repellents usually last several years; high-traffic floor sealers, less so. A simple test is to add a few drops of water: if they stop forming drops and are absorbed, it is time to renew.

## In summary

Good apparent concrete depends on formwork, release agent, uniform mixing, correct vibrating and careful curing. Fine bubbles and cracks are usually cosmetic, but wide, diagonal or growing cracks and rust spots require an engineer's evaluation. Protect the concrete with a breathable water-repellent or sealer depending on use, clean it with water and neutral detergent and avoid acids. Insulate slabs exposed to the sun and balance the eco with textiles and furniture.
