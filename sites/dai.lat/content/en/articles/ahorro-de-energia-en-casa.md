The electricity and gas bill weighs more and more on the family budget. In many countries in the region, in addition, rates rise in blocks: the more consumed, the more expensive each kilowatt is. Therefore, reducing consumption not only lowers the bill, but sometimes avoids going to a higher rate. And every kilowatt that is not used is also a small help for the environment.

Saving energy does not mean living in the dark or being hot or cold. Most of the savings come from a few well-made decisions: an efficient and well-located refrigerator, hot water without waste, LED lighting, a house that does not overheat and appliances that are not left on unnecessarily. In this guide you will see how to understand your consumption, which devices use the most, which changes yield the most and what to take into account before investing in solar energy.

## First, understand your bill

Take your latest bills and look for:

- **The consumption in kilowatt hours (kWh)** of each period, for electricity, and in cubic meters or kilos, for gas.
- **If the rate changes by consumption blocks** or if there is a limit beyond which a subsidy is lost.
- **How ​​consumption varies between months:** Peaks usually coincide with heat, cold or the arrival of a new device.

Comparing consumption from year to year in the same month is the easiest way to know if your changes are working.

## How to calculate how much a device uses

The consumption of a device is calculated by multiplying its **power** by the **hours of use**:

> Power in watts × hours of use ÷ 1000 = kWh

For example, a 100-watt television turned on 5 hours a day consumes 0.5 kWh per day, about 15 kWh per month. For appliances that operate on cycles, such as a refrigerator or air conditioner, the calculation is more difficult: a **consumption meter** that plugs between the appliance and the outlet shows the actual consumption and is a very useful economic tool.

## Where does the energy go in a typical home?

| Use | Why does it weigh | What more help |
|---|---|---|
| Refrigerator | works 24 hours | efficient equipment, good location, door that closes well |
| hot water | heating water requires a lot of energy | shorter showers, good insulation, solar energy |
| Air conditioning | high power for many hours | shade, insulation, moderate temperature |
| Lighting | many hours of use | LED and take advantage of natural light |
| Kitchen | gas or electricity daily | lids, pressure cooker, suitable pot size |
| Washing and ironing | hot water and heat | full loads, cold water, sun drying |
| Devices on standby | small but constant consumption | power strips with switch, disconnect |

## The refrigerator

- **Place it away from the sun, stove and oven,** and leave space behind and on the sides for heat dissipation.
- **Adjust the temperature:** Around 3 to 5 degrees in the refrigerator and 18 degrees below zero in the freezer are usually adequate for preserving food.
- **Check the door weatherstripping:** Close the door on a sheet of paper; If it slides easily, the seal does not fit properly.
- **Defrosts** freezers that accumulate frost.
- **Do not open more than necessary** or overfill.
- **If it is very old,** it can consume much more than a new one. When purchasing, check the **energy efficiency label** that exists in many countries in the region.

## hot water

Heating water is one of the largest energy costs in many homes, whether with gas or electric heaters, or with the electric showers common in some countries.

- **Shorter showers:** is the simplest and most effective saving.
- **Low consumption showerheads** that reduce the flow without losing comfort.
- **Moderate temperature** in the heater, according to the manufacturer's instructions.
- **Insulates hot water pipes** that run through cold areas.
- **Heater maintenance:** A well-regulated gas appliance consumes less and is safer. We explain it in the guide on [extractor hood and gas in the kitchen](/articulos/campana-extractora-y-gas-en-la-cocina/).
- **Solar heaters:** In sunny regions, a solar heater can cover much of the year's hot water and pay for itself in a few years.

## Lighting

LED bulbs consume much less than incandescent bulbs and last for many years. Changing the most used light bulbs, such as those in the kitchen, living room and outside, is one of the investments that recovers the fastest. Take advantage of natural light and turn off the lights in empty spaces. The [house lighting](/articulos/iluminacion-de-la-casa/) guide explains how to choose LEDs.

## cold and heat

In hot climates, air conditioning can be the biggest consumer in the house. Before turning it on:

- **Shades windows and walls** and protects the roof. We see it in the guide for [cooling the house without air conditioning](/articulos/enfriar-la-casa-sin-aire-acondicionado/) and in the [thermal insulation of the roof](/articulos/aislamiento-termico-del-techo/).
- **Use fans,** which consume much less.
- **If you use air conditioning,** choose efficient equipment, set a moderate temperature of around 24 to 26 degrees, close doors and windows and clean the filters regularly.

In cold climates, savings begin by preventing heat from escaping, as we explain in the guide on the [house for high altitude cold climates](/articulos/casa-para-clima-frio-de-altura/).

## Kitchen

- **Cover the pots:** the water boils faster and less gas or electricity is used.
- **Use the pressure cooker** for legumes and long stews: it greatly reduces cooking time.
- **Adjust flame or plate** to the size of the pot.
- **Microwave** small portions instead of turning on the oven.
- **Turn off the oven a few minutes before** finishing: the accumulated heat continues cooking.

## Washing and ironing

- **Wash with full loads** and, whenever possible, with cold water.
- **Dry clothes in the sun and air:** it is free and helps eliminate odors.
- **Iron in batches,** taking advantage of the heat of the iron, and leave the garments that require less temperature for last.

## Devices on standby

Televisions, decoders, consoles, chargers and audio equipment can consume energy even if they are turned off. A **power strip with switch** allows you to cut off several devices at the same time when they are not in use. Unplug the chargers when you finish charging.

## A savings plan in four weeks

1. **Week One:** Write down your meter readings each day at the same time to know your base consumption.
2. **Week two:** measure the refrigerator, television and other frequently used appliances with a plug-in meter, and change the bulbs that spend the most hours on to LEDs.
3. **Week Three:** Adjust the temperature of the refrigerator and heater, check weather stripping, and shorten family showers.
4. **Week Four:** organize power strips with switches, adjust washing and ironing habits, and compare readings to the first week.

Making the changes in stages allows you to see which ones work best in your home and maintain them over time.

## solar energy at home

Photovoltaic solar panels are increasingly accessible, and several countries in the region allow energy that is not consumed to be injected into the grid and deducted from the bill. Before investing:

1. **Reduce consumption first:** the less you spend, the smaller and cheaper the system will be.
2. **Check the roof:** orientation, shadows from trees or buildings, state of waterproofing and capacity of the structure. We see it in the [roofs and covers] guide (/articulos/techos-y-cubiertas/).
3. **Check local regulations** regarding grid connection and metering.
4. **Ask for multiple quotes** from certified installers, with production estimates and written guarantees.

## Frequent errors

- **Refrigerator next to the stove** or in the sun.
- **Long showers** with very hot water.
- **Air conditioning at very low temperatures** with doors open.
- **Incandescent bulbs** in places with a lot of use.
- **Devices on standby** 24 hours a day.
- **Install solar panels** without first reducing consumption.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which change saves faster?

It depends on each house, but in general: change the most used light bulbs to LED, shorten the showers, adjust and position the refrigerator well, and use the air conditioning in moderation.

### Is it worth changing an old refrigerator that still works?

If it is very old, it can consume much more than a new efficient one. A consumption meter for a few days will tell you how much you spend and if the change is paid within a reasonable period of time.

### Does a solar heater work on cloudy days?

It works with less performance. That is why it is usually combined with a backup gas or electric heater for days without sun.

## In summary

To save energy, first understand your bill and measure how much your appliances use. Locate and adjust the refrigerator properly, shorten showers and consider a solar heater, switch to LEDs, use fans rather than air conditioning, and protect the house from the sun. In the kitchen, cover the pots and use the pressure cooker; in the wash, cold water and drying in the sun. Cut standby consumption and, if you think about solar panels, reduce consumption first and choose certified installers.
