The traffic that doesn't stop, the neighbor's music on a Friday night, the footsteps from upstairs, the dogs, the construction, the speakers on the street. In Latin American cities, noise is part of the landscape, and many homes, especially apartments, are built with thin walls, hollow doors and windows that do not close properly. The result is fatigue, poor sleep and arguments with neighbors.

Noise is not just a nuisance. The World Health Organization considers it a health risk factor, especially due to its effect on sleep. The good news is that there are improvements possible in almost any home, from sealing a crack to building a siding on the shared wall. In this guide you will see how sound travels, why isolating is not the same as absorbing, which improvements work best, and which are low-cost.

## Isolating is not the same as absorbing

This is the most common confusion:

- **Isolate** means **preventing sound from passing** from one space to another: from the street to the bedroom or from the apartment next to yours. It is achieved with mass, airtightness and separate layers.
- **Absorb** means **reduce the echo within a room,** so that voice and music do not bounce back. It is achieved with soft and porous materials: carpets, curtains, sofas, books, absorbent panels.

The foams and panels that are glued to the wall improve the echo of the room, but they hardly stop the noise from the neighbor. Other solutions are needed to isolate.

## How noise travels

| Noise type | Examples | How it is transmitted |
|---|---|---|
| Aerial | voices, traffic, music, television | through the air, passing through walls, windows and cracks |
| Of impact | footsteps, falling objects, furniture being dragged | blows that make the structure vibrate |
| On flanks | noise surrounding isolated wall | through slabs, columns, ducts and pipes |

Each type requires a different solution. Airborne noise is combated with mass and airtightness; the impact one, with layers that cushion the blow where it occurs; the flanks, taking care of the joints between elements.

## The three rules of isolation

1. **Mass:** The heavier a wall or window is, the more sound it stops. A solid brick wall insulates better than a hollow brick wall of the same thickness, as we explain in the guide on [brick and block](/articulos/ladrillo-y-bloque-tipos-y-usos/).
2. **Hermeticity:** sound passes through any crack, just like water. An excellent door with an open space underneath insulates very little.
3. **Desolidarization:** two layers separated by a chamber with absorbent material, and joined elastically, insulate much more than a single layer of the same total weight. It is the principle of the transdosados ​​and the double windows.

## Windows: the weakest point

In most houses, street noise enters through the windows.

- **Seals the frames well:** the cracks between the frame and the wall let a lot of noise through. They are filled with foam or acoustic sealant and finished.
- **Replace the worn weather stripping** on the leaves.
- **Prefers casement or casement windows** with good compression closure; Sliding doors usually let in more noise.
- **Acoustic laminated glass:** two sheets of glass joined by a special plastic layer that dampens vibration.
- **Double glass with sheets of different thickness:** insulates noise better than one with two identical glasses.
- **Double window:** a second window on the inside, separated several centimeters from the original, is one of the most effective solutions for bedrooms that face very noisy streets.

In cold climates, these improvements also reduce heat losses, as we explain in the guide on the [high altitude cold climate house](/articulos/casa-para-clima-frio-de-altura/).

## The doors

- **Solid doors** instead of hollow doors, especially at the entrance and in the bedrooms.
- **Perimeter weather stripping** on the frame.
- **Lower weather stripping,** ideally automatic, which lowers when the door is closed and seals the space with the floor.

## Walls shared with neighbors

If the noise comes through a wall:

- **Look for leaks first:** back-to-back electrical outlets or boxes, pipes that go through the wall, gaps where they meet the ceiling or floor. Sealing them with acoustic sealant can greatly improve the result.
- **An acoustic lining:** a new sheet of plasterboard on profiles or studs separated from the wall, with mineral wool in the chamber and elastic joints. It takes up a few centimeters, but significantly reduces airborne noise.
- **Heavy furniture such as full bookcases** against the shared wall helps somewhat, although it does not replace insulation.

## Noise from upstairs

Impact noise is the most difficult to resolve from below, because the vibration enters through the structure.

- **The most effective thing is to act above:** carpets, floating floors with a shock-absorbing base and felts under the legs of furniture.
- **From below,** a false ceiling with mineral wool, hung with elastic supports, improves airborne noise and somewhat impact noise.
- **Talk to your neighbors:** many times they don't know how much they can hear. A friendly conversation and a rug usually solve more than a piece of work.

If you are building a second floor, plan for acoustic insulation from the beginning, as we suggest in the guide on [expanding the house with a second floor](/articulos/ampliar-la-casa-segundo-piso/).

## Reduce the echo inside the house

Rooms with hard floors, smooth walls and little furniture sound "empty": voices bounce and everything is heard louder. To improve it:

- **Carpets and rugs,** especially in rooms with ceramic, concrete or wooden floors.
- **Fabric curtains** and textiles. More ideas in the [curtains, textiles and rugs](/articulos/cortinas-textiles-y-alfombras/) guide.
- **Upholstered sofas and armchairs.**
- **Full bookcases** and furniture with uneven surfaces.
- **Decorative absorbent panels** on ceilings or walls.

In spaces with a lot of exposed concrete, the echo is more noticeable, as we discussed in the [apparent concrete](/articulos/concreto-aparente-cuidados/) guide.

## The distribution also isolates

The cheapest way to sleep peacefully is to put the bedroom on the quietest side of the house: towards the patio or inside the lot and away from the street, the elevator or the living room. Closets, bathrooms, and hallways between the bedroom and the noise source serve as buffers. We see it in the guide on [house distribution by zones](/articulos/distribucion-de-la-casa-por-zonas/).

## Ventilate without letting in noise

Closing all the windows improves the silence, but the house needs air. Some alternatives:

- **Ventilate during quieter hours.**
- **Open the windows that face the interior** of the lot or the patio instead of those facing the street.
- **Acoustic aerators:** silencer grilles that allow minimal ventilation with less noise.

The [cross ventilation](/articulos/ventilacion-cruzada/) guide explains how to combine openings.

## Noises from your own house

Not all the noise comes from outside. Some internal sources can also be reduced:

- **Appliances:** washing machines, refrigerators and dishwashers on anti-vibration and well-leveled bases vibrate less.
- **Pipes:** water drops that pass through thin walls are heard in bedrooms. Covering them with insulating material and securing them with rubber clamps reduces noise.
- **Extractors and air conditioning:** Low noise models and elastic supports prevent vibration from passing into the structure.
- **Slamming doors:** Rubber buffers or stops on frames and furniture.

## Rules and coexistence

Many cities have noise regulations that set limits and rest times. If the noise from a neighbor or a premises is persistent, it is advisable to speak first, then turn to the building administration and, if there is no solution, to the competent municipal authority.

## Frequent errors

- **Confuse absorption with insulation** and stick foam for the neighbor's noise.
- **Ignore cracks** in doors, windows and outlets.
- **Hollow doors** in bedrooms.
- **Old sliding windows** on noisy streets.
- **Attack impact noise only from below.**
- **Bedrooms facing the street** when there is another option.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do egg cartons isolate noise?

No. They may reduce the echo inside the room a little, but they don't stop the noise coming from outside and they are also combustible.

### What improvement gives more results with less money?

Seal cracks in windows and doors, change weather stripping and put a bottom weather stripping on the door. They are economic improvements with a notable effect.

### Does white noise help you sleep?

A constant, soft noise, such as a fan or white noise machine, can mask intermittent noises and help some people sleep. It doesn't reduce the noise, but it makes it less annoying.

## In summary

To isolate is to prevent sound from passing through; to absorb is to reduce the echo. To insulate, use mass, airtightness and separate layers: seal cracks, improve windows with laminated glass, double glazing or double glazing, use solid doors with weather stripping and, in shared walls, consider acoustic backing. Impact noise is best resolved upstairs, with carpets and floating floors. Reduce the echo with textiles and furniture, place the bedrooms on the quiet side and ventilate during the quietest hours.
