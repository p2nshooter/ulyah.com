The patio is one of the great legacies of Latin American architecture. The colonial mansions of Oaxaca, Cartagena, Lima or Antigua Guatemala are organized around an open space with galleries, plants and sometimes a fountain. The chorizo ​​houses of Buenos Aires arrange their rooms along a side patio. And in many current homes, a small patio or light well is the only thing that allows air and clarity to enter the back rooms.

A well-thought-out patio is not just a pretty place. It is a climate regulator for the entire house: it ventilates, gives light, offers shade and creates a cooler corner on hot days. If poorly resolved, however, it can become a covered oven, a damp and dark well or a breeding ground for mosquitoes. In this guide you will see how the microclimate of a patio works, what proportions are best depending on your climate and how to choose shade, water, plants and floors.

## How the microclimate of a patio works

A patio acts on the temperature in several ways at the same time:

- **Shade:** the surrounding walls, galleries and trees prevent the sun from heating the ground for much of the day.
- **Accumulated fresh air:** At night, the coldest air tends to stay at the bottom of the patio. In the morning, that air can enter the rooms.
- **Chimney effect:** when the air inside heats up, it rises and leaves through the patio. That movement draws in cooler air from other openings in the house.
- **Evaporation:** water from a fountain, irrigation or plants absorbs heat when it evaporates. This effect is very noticeable in dry climates and less so in very humid climates, where the air is already almost saturated.
- **Thermal mass:** stone or ceramic floors in the shade stay cool and absorb some of the heat from the environment.

For these effects to work, rooms should have windows or doors facing the patio and, if possible, another opening on the opposite side. This is how an air current is formed. We explain it in the [cross ventilation](/articulos/ventilacion-cruzada/) guide.

## The proportion according to the climate

Not all patios work the same. The relationship between the width of the patio and the height of the walls that surround it completely changes its behavior.

| Courtyard type | Climate where it works best | why |
|---|---|---|
| Narrow and tall | hot and dry, hot and humid | receives little direct sun, retains fresh air and shade |
| Medium, with gallery | tempered | balances sun in winter and shade in summer |
| Wide and open | cold or high | lets the sun in to heat floors and walls |

In hot climates, a deep, shaded patio can be several degrees cooler than the street during the hottest hours. In cold high altitude climates, however, it is advisable for the midday sun to reach the ground and walls to warm them.

## Shade: trees, galleries and awnings

- **Trees:** They provide the most pleasant shade, because in addition to blocking the sun, they breathe and refresh the air. In small yards, choose appropriately sized, non-invasive root species, and keep them away from foundations and pipes.
- **Vines on pergolas:** They grow quickly and create filtered shade. The deciduous ones let the sun shine through in winter.
- **Galleries and eaves:** they protect the windows of the rooms and create a transition space between the inside and the outside.
- **Awnings and shade sails:** They are flexible and can be removed when you want sun.

A very common mistake is to **roof the patio with polycarbonate or glass** to protect it from rain. Without ventilation in the upper part, the patio becomes a greenhouse and stops cooling the house. If you need protection from water, prefer retractable awnings or partial covers with air vents. More ideas in the guide about [pergolas, awnings and shade](/articulos/pergolas-toldos-y-sombra/).

## Water: fountains, mirrors and irrigation

Water is a traditional element of the patio and can help refresh, but it requires care:

- **Prefers moving water.** A small fountain with a recirculating pump evaporates more and prevents stagnant water.
- **Avoid containers with still water.** Dishes under pots, buckets or fountains without circulation can become breeding grounds for mosquitoes, including those that transmit dengue, Zika or chikungunya in many regions.
- **Clean and renew the water frequently,** and empty and scrub the containers to remove eggs stuck to the walls.
- **In very humid climates,** the cooling effect of water is small. There they provide more shade and ventilation.

## Plants for the patio

Plants soften light, absorb noise, provide freshness and make the space more pleasant. Please choose according to the actual light of the patio:

- **Patios in shade almost all day:** ferns, araceae, calatheas, large foliage plants and undergrowth species.
- **Patios with morning sun:** many floral, aromatic and climbing plants.
- **Very sunny patios:** succulents, cacti, grasses and native species adapted to drought.

Plants native to your region usually require less water and care. We see it in the guide about [garden with native plants](/articulos/jardin-con-plantas-nativas/).

## Floors and drainage

The floor of a patio receives sun, rain, soil and irrigation water. It must be:

- **Non-slip when wet,** especially if there is a fountain, plants or frequent rain.
- **Weather resistant** and, in cold areas, frost resistant.
- **Easy to clean,** with joints that do not accumulate moss.

Equally important is the **drainage**. The floor should slope slightly, usually about 1 to 2 percent, toward a grate or gutter with sufficient outlet for heavy rain. A flooding patio damages interior walls, doors and floors. More information in the guides on [non-slip exterior floors](/articulos/pisos-exteriores-antiderrapantes/) and [rainwater, gutters and drainage](/articulos/agua-de-lluvia-canaletas-y-drenaje/).

## Walls and colors

The patio walls reflect light into the rooms. Light colors increase interior luminosity, something very useful in deep houses and narrow patios. In very sunny patios, however, pure white can be dazzling. Warm, soft tones, such as sand, light ocher or terracotta, reduce reflection without darkening.

In shady patios, moisture can stain the walls with mold and moss. Good exterior paint, waterproof baseboard, and efficient drainage help keep them healthy.

## The small patio or light well

In apartments and houses between party walls, the patio can be a space of a few square meters. Still, it is valuable:

- **Keep it clear.** Filling it with objects blocks light and air.
- **Use light colors** on the walls to multiply the light.
- **Add plants in height** in planters or wall supports.
- **Check the drain** regularly, because leaves and trash easily clog it.
- **If it is used to hang clothes,** ventilate it well so that humidity does not enter the rooms.

## Light and use at night

A patio is also enjoyed at night. Low, warm lighting, directed at plants and walls, creates atmosphere without attracting too many insects. Outdoor luminaires must be prepared for the elements and the electrical installation must comply with local regulations.

## Furniture and objects for the outdoors

Patio furniture receives sun, rain and humidity. Choose prepared materials: treated hardwoods, metal with outdoor paint, synthetic fibers or stone. Cushions and textiles should be dried quickly or stored when it rains, because in humid climates mold stains appear within a few days. Leave free space to circulate and do not cover grates or drains with pots or furniture. Fewer, well-chosen objects make a small patio feel larger and fresher.

## Frequent errors

- **Roof the patio completely** without ventilation in the upper part.
- **Leave standing water** in fountains, plates or containers.
- **Choose smooth floors** that are slippery in the rain.
- **Plant large trees** near foundations and pipes.
- **Do not provide drainage** for heavy rains.
- **Use the patio as a cellar** and block light and air.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a patio really cool the house?

Yes, when it has shade, ventilation and the rooms open towards it. In dry climates, evaporation of plants and water can significantly reduce temperatures. In humid climates, shade and airflow are the most important factors.

### Can I cover the patio with a skylight?

You can, but the patio will no longer function as a climate regulator if the cover does not have ventilation. If you need to protect yourself from the rain, choose a system that opens or leaves air vents at the top.

### What do I do if my patio is too dark?

Paint the walls light colors, choose shady plants, clear the space and use medium-toned floors that reflect some light. Also check if any element, such as a roof or added construction, is blocking the entry of the sky.

## In summary

A patio functions as the lung of the house: it ventilates, gives light and creates a cooler microclimate. It adapts its proportion to the climate, narrow and shaded in warm places and more open in cold climates. Provide shade with trees, vines, galleries or awnings, avoid roofing without ventilation and use moving water, never stagnant. Choose plants based on light, non-slip floors and good drainage. Thus, the patio becomes the most pleasant room in the house.
