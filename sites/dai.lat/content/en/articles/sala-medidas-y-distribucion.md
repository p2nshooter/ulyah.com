The living room is the social heart of the house. There you talk, watch television, receive your family at parties, read, children play and, in many homes, work with the computer on your knees. That is why it is also the environment where distribution errors are most noticeable: a sofa that blocks the way, a coffee table so far away that no one can reach their cup, a television that forces you to twist your neck, or an empty corner that no one uses.

Distributing a room well does not depend on the budget, but on understanding how it is used and respecting some basic measures. In this guide you will see how to define the use and the focal point, what distances work between furniture, where and at what height to place the television, how to solve small rooms and rooms integrated with the dining room and how to make the space comfortable for all ages.

## First: what do you use your living room for?

Before moving furniture, answer some questions:

- Is it primarily a place to **watch TV** or **talk**?
- How many people sit down daily and how many in a meeting?
- Are there children playing on the floor or pets climbing onto the couch?
- Is it also used for **working** or studying?
- Is it the obligatory passage to other rooms?

The answers define the distribution. A room designed for conversation groups the seats face to face; one designed for home cinema directs them towards the screen.

## The focal point

Every room needs a point where the view is organized: a window with a good view, a fireplace in cold climates, an important piece of furniture, a work of art or the television. Placing the seats in relation to that point gives order to the whole. If the television and the window compete, try not to place the television **in front of a bright window**, because the backlight tires the eyes, nor with the window behind the viewer if it produces reflections on the screen.

## Measures that work

| Element | Indicative measure |
|---|---|
| Distance between sofa and coffee table | 40 to 50cm |
| Coffee table height | similar to the height of the seat, about 40 to 45 cm |
| Length of the coffee table | about two-thirds the length of the sofa |
| Main circulation passage | 75 to 90cm |
| Secondary step | at least 60cm |
| Maximum distance between seats for conversation | about 2.5 to 3 m |
| side table | at the height of the arm of the sofa or slightly less |
| Seat height for seniors | about 45 cm, firm and with arms |

With these measures, anyone can reach the coffee table without getting up, you can move around without tripping over other people's knees, and the conversation flows without having to raise your voice.

## Usual distributions

- **Facing each other:** two sofas or a sofa and two armchairs facing each other, with the table in the center. Ideal for conversation.
- **In L:** a sofa and an armchair or a corner sofa. Works well with the TV on the open side.
- **U-shaped:** three sides with seats and the fourth towards the focal point. You need a large room.
- **Floating:** the furniture separated from the walls, grouped in the center. In large rooms it creates a more intimate atmosphere; In small rooms, however, it is advisable to lean the furniture against the walls.

A rug of the right size helps tie the whole thing together. We explain it in the [curtains, textiles and carpets] guide (/articulos/cortinas-textiles-y-alfombras/).

## The television: distance and height

**Height:** The center of the screen should be approximately eye level for a seated person, which is typically around 1 to 1.1 meters off the floor. Hanging the television too high, for example over a fireplace, forces you to raise your head and tires your neck.

**Distance:** depends on size and resolution. For reference, with high-resolution displays many people are comfortable at a distance between one and one and a half times the diagonal of the screen; with lower resolutions, somewhat further. The important thing is to be able to see the entire image without moving your head and without distinguishing the pixels.

**Safety:** fix the TV to the wall with a suitable support or anchor the furniture that supports it. In seismic areas and with small children, an unanchored television or tall piece of furniture can fall.

**Cables:** ducts, cable glands or a piece of furniture with a rear exit maintain order and prevent tripping hazards.

## small rooms

- **A two-seater sofa with thin arms** instead of a large one.
- **Furniture with legs** that show the floor.
- **Lightweight** or nestable coffee tables, which can be separated when there are visitors.
- **Pouf or stools** as extra seats that are stored under a console.
- **Tall, shallow shelves** instead of low, wide furniture.
- **A large mirror** that multiplies the light.

## Integrated living room and dining room

In many current homes, the living room and dining room share a single room. So that each area has its identity:

- **Define each area with a rug** and your own lighting: a hanging lamp over the table and floor or table lamps in the room.
- **Use the back of the sofa as a divider,** with a low console behind it.
- **Leave a clear passage** between both areas, of at least 75 to 90 centimeters.
- **Keep a common color palette** so everything looks like a set. The [interior colors](/articulos/colores-para-interiores/) guide helps you choose it.

## Try before you buy

Before buying a sofa or table, mark its outline on the floor with paper tape or actual size cardboard. Leave it like this for a few days and circulate around the room as you normally would. This way you will discover if the passage is narrow, if the door clashes or if the furniture dominates the space too much. It also measures doors, hallways, stairs and elevators: more than one sofa has been left at the entrance of the building because it did not fit through the door.

## Light in the room

The room needs several layers of light: soft general lighting, reading lamps next to the seats and some ambient lighting at night. Avoid depending on a single central focus. The [house lighting](/articulos/iluminacion-de-la-casa/) guide explains how to combine them.

## Comfort depending on the weather

- **In warm climates:** breathable fabrics such as cotton or linen, washable covers and cushions that can be aired. Leather and some synthetic fabrics can be hot.
- **In cold climates:** blankets, wool rugs and thick curtains that keep heat in at night.
- **In humid climates:** avoid the sofa being stuck to an exterior wall and ventilate the room frequently.

## Acoustics: less echo, better conversation

Rooms with hard floors, smooth walls, and little furniture produce an echo. A rug, curtains, cushions, a bookcase with books and some large plants absorb sound and make conversation more pleasant. If the problem is noise coming from outside, the [sound insulation at home](/articulos/aislamiento-acustico-en-casa/) guide offers solutions.

## Children, pets and seniors

- **Rounded corners** on coffee tables if there are small children.
- **Resistant and washable fabrics** if there are pets.
- **Firm seats, not too low and with arms** so that older people can get up easily.
- **Wide steps without cables** to avoid falls.

## Frequent errors

- **All the furniture stuck to the walls** in a large room, with a void in the center.
- **Coffee table too far away** or too high.
- **TV hung very high.**
- **Circulation paths that cross** the conversation area.
- **Small rugs** that do not connect the furniture.
- **A single central focus** as the only light.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much space do I need between the sofa and the TV?

It depends on the size and resolution of the screen. As a reference, for high resolution screens, between one and one and a half times the diagonal. The most important thing is that the entire image enters your field of vision without moving your head.

### Can the sofa go in the middle of the living room?

Yes, in large rooms or integrated with the dining room. Its backrest can be used to separate environments. Place a console or narrow table behind it to make the view clearer.

### What size sofa do I need?

Measure the wall or available space and subtract the circulation steps. A sofa that leaves at least 60 to 90 centimeters free on the sides for movement is usually a good reference.

## In summary

First define how the room is used and what its focal point is. Leave 40 to 50 centimeters between the sofa and the coffee table, steps of 75 to 90 centimeters and seats less than 3 meters apart for conversation. Place the television at eye level, at a distance proportional to its size and well anchored. In small rooms, choose light and multifunctional furniture; In integrated rooms, separate areas with carpets and light. Adapt fabrics to the climate, reduce the eco with textiles and think about the comfort of children, pets and the elderly.
