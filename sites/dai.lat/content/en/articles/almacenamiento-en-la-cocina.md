A kitchen can have new furniture and stone countertops and still be chaotic: pots that fall when you open the door, lids that never appear, spices repeated because you couldn't see what was already there, flour with weevils at the back of the cupboard. Almost always the problem is not a lack of space, but rather the way of storing.

Organizing the kitchen has an immediate effect on daily life: you cook faster, less food is wasted and the counter is free for work. In Latin American kitchens, where there is usually a generous pantry of grains, flours, canned goods and spices, and large pots that are frequently used, organization is even more important. In this guide you will see how to distribute things according to their use, how to take advantage of each type of furniture, how to store food so that it lasts and what to do with cleaning products.

## Three principles to save better

1. **Everything close to where it is used.** Pots next to the stove, dishes near the sink or drainer, knives and boards next to the preparation area.
2. **The most used, at the most comfortable height.** Everyday objects go between the hips and the shoulders. Above, the occasional. Below, the heavy.
3. **See to use.** What is not seen is forgotten. Drawers, clear containers and labels help keep everything in view.

The [kitchen layout](/articulos/distribucion-de-la-cocina/) guide explains how to organize work areas: storing, washing, preparing, cooking and serving.

## Low furniture: better drawers than doors

In base units, **deep drawers** are much more practical than doors with shelves. When you open a drawer you see everything from above and you don't have to kneel to search at the bottom.

- **Pots and pans:** in wide, deep drawers near the stove, with lids in a vertical organizer.
- **Plastic containers:** with lids paired or stored upright. Discard any that have lost their lids.
- **Utensils:** in the first drawer next to the preparation area, with dividers.
- **Knives:** on a block, magnet bar, or drawer organizer. Loose in a drawer they become damaged and dangerous.

### The corners

The corners of an L-shaped or U-shaped kitchen are difficult to reach. There are hardware that takes advantage of them: rotating trays, removable shelves or systems that take the content out. If you don't have them, use the corner for what you use the least.

### under the sink

It is a space exposed to leaks and humidity. Use trays or containers that contain possible drips, check the connections from time to time and leave access to the stopcocks clear.

## Tall furniture: take advantage of the height

- **Dishes, glasses and cups for daily use** on the lower part of the wall units, near the sink or drainer.
- **The occasional thing above:** party tableware, molds, large seasonal pots.
- **Furniture up to the ceiling:** prevent dust from accumulating on the furniture and gain space. Have a small, stable ladder to reach them.
- **Intermediate or lifting shelves** inside the furniture double the useful surface for cups or cans.

## The pantry

The Latin American pantry is usually abundant: rice, beans, lentils, flour, sugar, oil, pasta, canned goods, dried chilies and spices. So that nothing is lost:

- **Airtight** glass or plastic containers for grains and flour. They protect from humidity, insects and odors.
- **Labels with the content and date** of purchase or opening.
- **The new back, the old ahead,** to consume the oldest first.
- **A cool, dry, dark place** away from the stove, oven and the back of the refrigerator, which gives off heat.
- **Shallow shelves** if possible: on very deep shelves, the bottom is forgotten.

### Weevils and pantry moths

Weevils and small moths that appear in flour, rice and grains usually arrive already inside the packages. To prevent them:

- **Transfer products into airtight containers** as soon as you get home.
- **Freeze new flour or grains for a few days** if you tend to have pest problems.
- **Check opened products** regularly and discard affected ones.
- **Wipe shelves** with a damp cloth and allow to dry before storing again.

Against cockroaches and ants, the most effective thing is not to leave crumbs or food remains, keep containers closed and seal cracks and crevices in furniture and walls.

## The neat refrigerator

An organized refrigerator preserves food better and reduces waste:

| Area | What to save |
|---|---|
| Upper shelves | ready-to-eat foods, leftovers in closed containers |
| Middle shelves | dairy, eggs |
| Bottom shelf | raw meats and fish in containers, so that they do not drip on other foods |
| Drawers | vegetables and fruits |
| Door | sauces, condiments and drinks, which better tolerate temperature changes |

Don't overload it: cold air needs to circulate.

## Fruits and vegetables out of the refrigerator

Some foods are best kept at room temperature, in a cool, ventilated place:

- **Bananas, avocados and tomatoes** until they ripen.
- **Potatoes, onions and garlic** in a dark and dry place. Store potatoes and onions separately, because together they spoil faster.
- **Tropical fruits** that ripen better outside.

## Spices and condiments

Spices lose aroma with light, heat and humidity. Store them in tightly closed jars, away from the stove, in a drawer with dividers, or on a shallow shelf where the labels can be seen. Buy small quantities of those you use little.

## Small appliances

Leave only what you use daily on the cover, such as the blender, coffee maker, or rice cooker. The rest can be stored in a low or high cabinet depending on its weight. A clear countertop cleans better and makes the kitchen appear larger. We also see it in the guide to [gain space in a small apartment](/articulos/departamento-pequeno-ganar-espacio/).

## The water corner

In many houses in the region, drinking water arrives in jugs or drums, or passes through a filter. It is advisable to give it a fixed place:

- **A stable support or dispenser** at a comfortable height, so as not to lift the bottle every time.
- **Away from the sun and heat sources,** which alter the flavor and encourage the proliferation of algae in transparent containers.
- **Near the sink,** for easy cleaning of spills.
- **Space for spare jugs,** resting on the floor and not on high shelves, because they weigh a lot.

If you use a filter, change the cartridges according to the manufacturer's instructions and note the date of the last change.

## Five minutes of order a day

The kitchen is kept tidy with small habits: putting away utensils after cooking, cleaning the countertop before going to sleep, returning each product to its place when finished using it, and writing down on a list what we run out of. A small chalkboard or note on the refrigerator door prevents repeat purchases and forgetfulness.

## Cleaning products: always separate

- **Never store cleaning products** next to food or in food or drink containers. It is a common cause of poisoning, especially in children.
- **Store them in a tall piece of furniture or with a safety lock** if there are children at home.
- **Keep them in their original packaging,** with the label visible.

## A thorough cleaning every so often

Two or three times a year, empty cupboards and pantry, clean shelves, check expiration dates, donate what you will not use and get rid of broken or lidless containers. It's the ideal time to reorganize based on how you cook now.

## Frequent errors

- **Save the most used at the top** or at the bottom.
- **Grains and flours in their open bags,** exposed to humidity and insects.
- **The pantry next to the oven** or behind the refrigerator.
- **Plastic containers without lids** taking up space.
- **Cleaning products with food.**
- **Loose knives** in the drawers.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best way to store rice and beans?

In airtight containers, in a cool, dry and dark place. This way they are preserved for months without humidity or insects.

### How do I take advantage of a kitchen without a pantry?

Allocate one or two tall pieces of furniture to the pantry, use door organizers and intermediate shelves, and take advantage of the space above the refrigerator for what you use the least.

### Is it worth investing in special hardware?

In the corners and in the pot drawers, yes: they greatly improve access. In other areas, simple, well-organized drawers are usually sufficient.

## In summary

Store everything close to where you use it, the most used items at the most comfortable height and everything in sight. Prefer drawers in low furniture, take advantage of the height in tall furniture and solve the corners with hardware or little-used objects. Organize your pantry in labeled airtight containers, away from heat, and protect it from weevils and cockroaches. Organize the refrigerator by zones, store cleaning products separate and out of reach of children, and do a thorough cleaning two or three times a year.
