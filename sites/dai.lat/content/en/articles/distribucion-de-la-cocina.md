There are beautiful kitchens that are uncomfortable to cook in. To get from the refrigerator to the stove you have to go around an island, there is no space to rest the hot pot, the oven door collides with the dishwasher and the blender lives in a corner without a plug. On the other hand, there are simple kitchens, with common furniture, where everything is at hand and is cooked with pleasure every day.

The difference is in the **distribution**. In Latin America, the kitchen is not a decorative space: a lot of cooking is done, with large pots, fried foods, a comal, a pressure cooker and a daily blender. Good planning saves steps, avoids accidents and makes the space last for years without renovations. In this guide you will see the types of distribution, the basic measurements, how to organize the work areas and what details should be resolved before purchasing a single piece of furniture.

## The work triangle

The classic concept of kitchen planning unites three points: **refrigerator, sink and stove**. The sink has different names depending on the country, such as sink, dishwasher or basin, and the stove is also called kitchen or cooktop. What is important is the relationship between the three.

As guidance, many design guides recommend that:

- Each side of the triangle measures **between 1.2 and 2.7 meters** approximately,
- The sum of the three sides does not exceed **7.9 meters**,
- No tall furniture, island or circulation passage cuts through the triangle.

If the sides are too short, there is no surface to work with. If they are too long, cooking becomes a walk. Today, with more open kitchens and more appliances, many designers prefer to talk about **work zones**, but the triangle is still a quick and useful check.

## The five zones of a kitchen

Think about the natural journey of food:

1. **Save:** refrigerator, cupboard and pantry.
2. **Wash:** sink, drainer and trash.
3. **Prepare:** The longest countertop surface, ideally between the sink and stove.
4. **Cooking:** stove, oven, microwave and hood.
5. **Serve and clean:** dishes, cutlery, dishwasher if available.

If the zones follow this order, the work flows. For example: take the vegetables out of the refrigerator, wash them, chop them on the cover and take them to the pot without crossing the kitchen.

## Distribution types

| Distribution | Ideal for | Advantages | Points to take care of |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linear or wall | small kitchens, apartments | economical, takes up little space | little work surface, stretched triangle |
| Parallel or corridor | long and narrow spaces | very efficient, lots of cover | hallway of at least 90 to 120 cm |
| In L | medium and open kitchens | flexible, leaves room for table | corner corner difficult to take advantage of |
| U-shaped | medium or large kitchens | lots of cover and storage | can feel confined if she is small |
| With island or peninsula | large or integrated kitchens | extra work and meeting area | needs generous circulation space |

A **peninsula** is an island attached to a piece of furniture or wall at one end. It takes up less space than an island and works well in medium-sized kitchens.

## Basic measures that should be respected

The exact measurements depend on the furniture and the height of the person cooking, but these references are common:

| Element | Indicative measure |
|---|---|
| Deck height | 85 to 95cm |
| Deck depth | about 60cm |
| Distance between cover and tall furniture | about 50 to 65 cm |
| Depth of wall units | 30 to 35cm |
| work hallway | at least 90 cm, ideally 105 to 120 cm if two people are cooking |
| Passage around an island | at least 100 to 120 cm |
| Free cover on each side of the stove | at least 30 to 40 cm |
| Preparation surface | at least 60 to 90 cm continuous |

The height of the deck is the measurement that most influences daily comfort. A practical rule is that it is about 10 to 15 centimeters below the elbow of the person who cooks the most. If there are people of very different heights at home, an island or a section of deck at a different height can be a good solution.

## Details that make the difference

### The stove

- **Do not place under a window.** Curtains can catch fire and, on gas stoves, the wind can blow out the flame.
- **Leave cover on both sides** to support hot pots and so that the handles do not protrude into a hallway.
- **Plan the hood or extraction** at the same time as the stove. We explain it in the guide on [extractor hood and gas in the kitchen](/articulos/campana-extractora-y-gas-en-la-cocina/).

### The sink

- **Locate it near the window** if possible: natural light and views help in the most repeated task in the kitchen.
- **Choose the size according to your pots.** In many Latin American homes, large pots and comales are washed. A deep or double sink is more useful than a small, decorative one.
- **Leaves room for drainage** and for the trash and recycling bin under the cover.

### The refrigerator

- **Put it at one end** of the kitchen, so it can be opened without interrupting the person cooking.
- **Do not put it next to the oven or stove** without separation, because the heat forces it to work harder.
- **Respect the ventilation** indicated by the manufacturer on the sides and back.
- **Check which side you open** the door so it doesn't hit a wall.

### Plugs and electricity

The blender, coffee maker, microwave, rice cooker, and crockpot need accessible outlets. Plan for multiple outlets on the countertop, away from the sink, and task lighting under wall units. Electrical installations near water must comply with the regulations of your country and should be carried out by a qualified electrician.

## Latin American cuisine has its own needs

International kitchen design recommendations usually think about another type of kitchen. Before copying a photo, think about how you cook at home:

- **Fried foods, stews and tortillas** generate a lot of steam and fat. Ventilation is a priority.
- **Large pots and pressure cookers** need powerful burners and room to maneuver.
- **The water jug ​​or container** takes up space. Provide a fixed place, with a base or dispenser.
- **The blender and rice cooker** are used daily and deserve a place on the countertop, not on a high cabinet.
- **The pantry** is usually large, with grains, flours and preserves. We see it in the [kitchen storage](/articulos/almacenamiento-en-la-cocina/) guide.

## Open or closed kitchen

Kitchens integrated with the living room are popular because they give space and allow for conversation while cooking. They have a cost: the smell and grease reach the entire social area, and so does the noise. If you cook a lot with frying, consider a semi-open kitchen with a sliding or glass door, or invest in an efficient extractor with access to the outside.

## Step by step to plan

1. **Measures space** accurately, including windows, doors, columns, pipes, drains and gas outlet.
2. **Make a list of appliances** with their actual measurements.
3. **Draw a scale plan** on graph paper or with a simple app.
4. **Locate the fixed points first:** sink near the drain, stove near the gas and the hood outlet.
5. **Order the areas** save, wash, prepare, cook and serve.
6. **Check the triangle** and the circulation measurements.
7. **Simulate the routine:** imagine how you prepare your most common meal and check if the route makes sense.

Moving drains and gas pipes is possible, but it makes the work more expensive and must be done by qualified personnel. Whenever possible, it is advisable to respect the existing location of the water and gas points.

## Frequent errors

- **Think about design before use.** A beautiful kitchen that requires you to walk around becomes uncomfortable.
- **Leave the stove without a cover on the sides** or against a wall.
- **Wasted corners** without hardware that allows you to reach the bottom.
- **Narrow hallways** where the oven and dishwasher cannot be opened at the same time.
- **Few plugs** on the deck.
- **Forget ventilation** in an open kitchen.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much space do I need to put an island?

In addition to the island, you need at least 100 to 120 centimeters free around it to circulate and open doors. An island measuring 90 by 180 centimeters requires, in practice, a kitchen measuring several meters per side. In smaller kitchens, a peninsula is usually a better option.

### Where do I put the microwave?

At a height where you can see inside and safely remove hot containers, usually between cover and shoulder height. Avoid placing it too high, on the stove, if the user is short.

### L-shaped or U-shaped kitchen for a medium space?

The L leaves more free space and allows for a small table. The U offers more cover and storage, but needs at least eight feet wide to make the center aisle comfortable.

## In summary

A comfortable kitchen is planned according to the food journey: storing, washing, preparing, cooking and serving. The triangle between refrigerator, sink and stove should be compact but not tight, with sides between 1.2 and 2.7 meters. Respect circulation measures, leave a cover on both sides of the stove and a continuous preparation surface between the sink and the stove. Choose the layout according to the space, plan sockets, light and ventilation from the beginning and adapt everything to your actual way of cooking.
