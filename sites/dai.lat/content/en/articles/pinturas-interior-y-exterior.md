Painting is the most economical and most appreciated renovation of a house. With a few gallons of paint and a weekend you can completely change a room or renew a facade. But it is also one of the tasks where mistakes are repeated the most: paint that peels after a few months, moisture stains that reappear, enamels that do not dry, facades that lose color with the first summer.

Almost always the cause is not in the brand, but in choosing the wrong type of paint or skipping preparation. In this guide you will see what types of paint exist and what each one is for, how to prepare walls, wood and metal, how to calculate how much paint to buy, how to apply it and what to do with the most common problems.

## The most used types of paint

| Type | Base | Common uses | Comments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vinyl or latex | water | interior walls and ceilings | economical, quick drying, low odor |
| Acrylic | water | interior and exterior | more resistant and washable than basic vinyl |
| Elastomeric | water | facades, walls with fine cracks, some roofs | forms an elastic film that accompanies small movements |
| water enamel | water | doors, frames, furniture, primed metal | less odor, easier to clean |
| Synthetic or alkyd enamel | solvent | wood and metal, areas of high friction | very resistant, more odor and slow drying |
| lime paint | water and lime | adobe walls, stone, old houses | very breathable, matte and mineral appearance |
| Anti-mold paint | water | bathrooms, kitchens, wet areas | contains anti-fungal additives |
| Floor paint or epoxy | variable | garages, concrete floors, workshops | resists traffic and chemicals depending on the type |

Water-based paints dominate interiors today due to their low odor and lower content of **volatile organic compounds**, substances that evaporate when drying and that should be minimized inside the home.

## Interior: what paint in each place

- **Living rooms and bedrooms:** Good quality vinyl or acrylic, in matte or satin.
- **Kitchens and bathrooms:** washable acrylic in satin or semi-gloss, and anti-mold where there is humidity. More about this problem in the guide to [ventilation and mold in the bathroom](/articulos/ventilacion-y-moho-en-el-bano/).
- **Hallways and children's rooms:** washable paints that resist rubbing and cleaning.
- **Ceilings:** specific matte paint for ceilings, which hides imperfections.
- **Doors, frames and furniture:** water-based or synthetic enamel.

The choice of finish and color is explained in the [interior colors] guide (/articulos/colores-para-interiores/).

## Exterior: paints that withstand the weather

Facades suffer from sun, rain, dust and temperature changes. Choose:

- **Paints formulated for exteriors,** with resistance to ultraviolet radiation.
- **Elastomeric** if there are fine cracks or a lot of rain.
- **Light colors** in hot climates: they reflect more sun and the façade heats up less.
- **Lime or silicate mineral paints** on adobe, stone walls or old buildings that need to breathe. A waterproof plastic paint on an earthen wall can trap moisture, as we explain in the guide on [adobe and earthen construction](/articulos/adobe-y-construccion-con-tierra/).

Cheap exterior paints tend to **caking**: a white powder forms on the surface that stains your hand. It is a sign that the binder degraded in the sun.

## Preparation: seventy percent of the result

No paint adheres well to a dirty, wet or loose surface.

### Walls

1. **Resolve the humidity first.** If the wall has humidity or salt stains, the paint will swell. The guide on [damp in walls](/articulos/humedad-en-paredes-causas-y-soluciones/) helps find the cause.
2. **Remove loose paint** with a spatula and brush.
3. **Cleans** dust, grease and mold. Mold must be removed with a suitable product and the surface must be dried.
4. **Repair cracks and holes** with putty or patching paste, let dry and sand.
5. **Apply a sealer or primer,** especially on new walls, large repairs or highly absorbent surfaces.

**New plaster or concrete walls:** they need time to dry and lose alkalinity before painting. Respect the waiting time indicated by the manufacturer and use alkalinity-resistant sealants. More about concrete in the [apparent concrete](/articulos/concreto-aparente-cuidados/) guide.

### Wood

Sand to open the pore and remove the previous shine, wipe off any dust, and apply a primer or wood sealer before glazing. Outdoors, use products with sunscreen, as we explain in the guide on [wood at home](/articulos/madera-en-casa-especies-y-cuidados/).

### metal

Remove rust with a wire brush or sandpaper, clean the grease and apply an **anti-corrosion primer** before polishing. In coastal areas, preparation and protection must be more demanding due to salinity.

## How much paint to buy

1. **Calculate the surface:** length of each wall times its height, adding all the walls.
2. **Subtract doors and windows.**
3. **Multiply by the number of hands,** usually two.
4. **Divide by the yield** indicated on the packaging in square meters per liter.

For example, a room with 40 square meters of walls, with two coats and with a paint that yields 10 square meters per liter, needs about 8 liters. Since a gallon is about 3.8 liters, a little more than two gallons would be needed. Buy everything at once to avoid shade differences between batches and save a little for touch-ups.

## Apply well

- **Check the conditions:** Avoid painting exteriors under intense direct sun, with nearby rain or with temperatures outside the range indicated on the packaging.
- **Mix the paint well** before starting and from time to time during work.
- **First edges and corners** with a brush, then surfaces with a roller.
- **Respect the time between coats.** A second coat on a still soft first coat may wrinkle or peel off.
- **Remove the masking tape** before the paint completely hardens, for a clean edge.
- **Ventilate** during and after painting.

## Tools that make a difference

- **Rollers depending on the surface:** short hair for smooth and enamel walls, longer hair for rough or textured surfaces.
- **Good quality brushes** for edges and corners; The very cheap ones release hairs that get trapped in the paint.
- **Tray or bucket with grid** to discharge excess roller.
- **Masking tape** for painters, which is removed without removing the paint.
- **Plastics or cardboard** to protect floors and furniture.
- **Stable ladder** instead of chairs or drawers.

When finished, wash the tools according to the type of paint: with soap and water for water-based paint, and with the solvent indicated on the container for synthetic enamel. A good, well-washed roller is useful for many jobs.

## Common problems and their causes

| Problem | Usual cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Paint that swells or peels | humidity on the wall or poor adhesion | resolve moisture, scrape, seal and repaint |
| White saltpeter stains | salts carried by humidity | dry brush and remove the cause |
| Mold that reappears | humidity or lack of ventilation | ventilate, clean and use anti-mold |
| White dust on facades | sun faded paint | wash, seal and repaint with exterior paint |
| Cracks in the film | very thick layers or walls with cracks | sand, repair and apply thin coats |
| Non-drying nail polish | cold, humidity or very thick layer | wait, ventilate and apply thin coats |

## Security

- **Old homes:** Old paints may contain lead. Do not dry sand or burn them; Consult specialists if you suspect they contain lead.
- **Always ventilate,** especially with solvent-based enamels.
- **Wear a mask and glasses** when sanding and painting ceilings.
- **Do not pour leftover paint or solvents** into drains or onto the floor. Follow local disposal regulations.
- **Store cans** tightly closed, away from heat and children.

## Frequent errors

- **Paint over humidity** without resolving it.
- **Skip the sealant** on new or repaired walls.
- **Use interior paint on facades.**
- **Enamel on oxidized metal** without anticorrosive primer.
- **Plastic paint on adobe.**
- **Buy in parts** and get different shades.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many coats of paint do I need?

In general, two hands. For strong color changes, especially from dark to light, an extra coat or a pigmented sealer may be necessary.

### Can I paint over enamel with water-based paint?

Yes, but the surface must be sanded to remove the shine and, in many cases, a bonding primer applied. Without that preparation, new paint can peel off.

### Do thermal paints work on ceilings?

Clear or reflective coatings reduce roof surface temperatures and help keep your home cooler. We explain it in the guide on [roof thermal insulation](/articulos/aislamiento-termico-del-techo/).

## In summary

Choose the paint according to the surface and the location: vinyl or acrylic indoors, acrylic or elastomeric outdoors, enamel on wood and metal, lime on walls that must breathe, and anti-mold in humid areas. Prepare well: resolve moisture, remove looseness, clean, repair and seal. Calculate the quantity with the surface, the coats and the yield, buy everything from the same batch, apply in appropriate conditions and respect the times between coats. Ventilate, protect yourself and dispose of remains responsibly.
