In Cartagena, Veracruz, Mérida, Guayaquil, Panama, Manaus or Santo Domingo, the heat does not let up even at night. The temperature changes little between day and night, humidity is high almost all year round and rain, when it comes, falls heavily. In this climate, a house designed for another place, with small windows, sun-heavy walls and low ceilings, becomes a hot box where the air conditioning works tirelessly.

The traditional architecture of the humid tropics solved these problems centuries ago: elevated houses, large eaves, shady corridors, high ceilings and lattices through which the breeze flows. Many of those ideas are still the most effective. In this guide you will see the principles for designing or adapting a house in a hot and humid climate: how to provide shade, how to ventilate, what materials to use, how to manage rain and humidity and what precautions to take against mosquitoes and hurricanes.

## What makes hot and humid weather different?

| Feature | Consequence for the house |
|---|---|
| High temperatures all year round | the priority is to avoid gaining heat |
| Little difference between day and night | thermal mass helps little, nights are also hot |
| High humidity | sweat evaporates poorly, moving air is key |
| Heavy rains | roofs, gutters and drains must be generous |
| High and strong sun | the ceiling is the most exposed surface |
| insects | ventilation must coexist with mosquito nets |
| Hurricanes or storms in some areas | Roofs and openings must be anchored and protected |

Unlike dry climates, where thick walls keep in the cool at night, here the main strategy is to provide shade and move the air.

## 1. shadow in everything

- **Large eaves** that protect walls and windows from the sun and rain. In traditional Caribbean and tropical coastal architecture, deep eaves and corridors are the rule.
- **Corridors, galleries and porches** around the house: create cool outdoor spaces and shade rooms.
- **Trees and vegetation** on the east and west sides.
- **Lattices and blinds** that let air through but not the sun.

Orientation also helps: long facades facing north and south are easier to shade than those facing east and west, as we explain in the [house solar orientation](/articulos/orientacion-solar-de-la-casa/) guide.

## 2. A roof that does not radiate heat

In the tropics, the roof receives most of the sun. So that it does not heat up the rooms:

- **High ceilings** with more air volume between the deck and the people.
- **Ventilated air chamber** between the cover and the ceiling, with low inlets and high outlets for hot air.
- **Light colors or reflective coatings** on the cover.
- **Insulation** under the roof or above the ceiling.

The [roof thermal insulation](/articulos/aislamiento-termico-del-techo/) guide explains each option, and the [roofs and covers](/articulos/techos-y-cubiertas/) guide compares roof types.

## 3. moving air

In a humid climate, moving air is the most effective way to feel cool: it helps evaporate sweat. To achieve this:

- **Long and narrow plan,** with a single bay if possible, with rooms that have openings on opposite sides.
- **Large windows** with casements or lattices, that open almost completely.
- **High openings** and stiles over doors to allow hot air to escape.
- **Ceiling fans** in all rooms.
- **Doors and interior divisions** that do not cut off the power.

The [cross ventilation](/articulos/ventilacion-cruzada/) guide explains how to locate inlets and outlets.

## 4. Materials that do not keep heat

In climates with warm nights, heavy materials exposed to the sun accumulate heat during the day and release it at night, just when you want to sleep. Therefore:

- **Exposed walls should be shaded.** If they are block or brick, eaves and vegetation are essential.
- **Lightweight materials** such as treated wood, bamboo, or insulated panels cool quickly when the sun goes down.
- **Light exterior surfaces** heat up less.

Wood is a traditional material of the tropics, but it requires protection against termites, fungi and humidity, as we explain in the guide on [wood at home] (/articulos/madera-en-casa-especies-y-cuidados/).

## 5. Elevate the house

Many traditional tropical houses were built on stilts or with raised floors. It has several advantages:

- **Air circulates underneath** and cools the floor.
- **The house is protected** from flooding, soil moisture and some animals.
- **The breeze is better used,** which blows more strongly at a certain height.

In current constructions, a high plinth, a floor raised above the ground or an open ground floor fulfill a similar function.

## 6. Heavy rain

- **Roofs with sufficient slope** to evacuate water quickly.
- **Gutters and downspouts sized** for heavy downpours, with meshes against leaves.
- **Eaves that protect the walls** from the rain that falls at an angle.
- **Drainage around the house** and sloping exterior floors.

More details in the guide to [rainwater, gutters and drainage](/articulos/agua-de-lluvia-canaletas-y-drenaje/).

## 7. Moisture and mold

Constant humidity favors mold on walls, closets, clothes and furniture. To control it:

- **Ventilate every day**, also closed spaces such as closets and cellars.
- **Choose materials that dry quickly** and avoid coatings that trap moisture, such as some vinyl papers.
- **Separate furniture** from exterior walls.
- **If you use air conditioning,** it also reduces the humidity in the air while it operates. Keep doors and windows closed in that room and clean the filters.

The guide on [damp in walls](/articulos/humedad-en-paredes-causas-y-soluciones/) helps identify the origin of stains.

## 8. Mosquitoes

In many areas of the tropics, mosquitoes transmit dengue, Zika, chikungunya or malaria. A ventilated house must be protected:

- **Mosquito nets** on windows, doors and, if necessary, over the beds.
- **No stagnant water:** clean gutters, covered tanks, no containers that accumulate rain.
- **Fans** in the living and sleeping areas, which also make it difficult for mosquitoes to fly.

## 9. Hurricanes and storms

In the Caribbean, the Gulf of Mexico and some coasts of Central America, tropical storms and hurricanes are a seasonal threat.

- **Roofs well anchored** to the structure, with adequate fixings and ties.
- **Shutters or protections** for glass windows and doors.
- **Well designed eaves** that do not act as sails against the wind.
- **Compliance with local wind construction regulations**, which in many areas are specific.

The structural details must be defined by professionals who know the requirements of the area.

## Kitchen, bathrooms and laundry

In the tropics, the kitchen and laundry are important sources of heat and humidity. Traditionally the kitchen was separated from the house or located in an open corridor. Today it is advisable to have generous ventilation, a hood with access to the outside and, if possible, a high window. Clothes dry better on an outdoor clothesline under cover and in the breeze than inside the house. Bathrooms need extraction or windows that open completely so they dry quickly between showers.

## Adapt an existing house

If you already live in a hot house, these are the improvements with the most impact:

1. **Protect the roof** with light color, insulation or a ventilated double roof.
2. **Shade** windows and exposed walls with awnings, lattices, pergolas or trees. The guide to [pergolas, awnings and shade](/articulos/pergolas-toldos-y-sombra/) offers options.
3. **Improve ventilation** by opening openings, studs or grilles where possible.
4. **Install ceiling fans.**
5. **Place mosquito nets** to be able to leave the windows open.

More strategies in the guide to [cool the house without air conditioning](/articulos/enfriar-la-casa-sin-aire-acondicionado/).

## Frequent errors

- **Small and sliding windows** that provide little ventilation.
- **Low slab or sheet ceilings** without insulation.
- **Heavy walls in the sun** without protection.
- **Compact and deep houses,** without cross ventilation.
- **Small gutters** for tropical rains.
- **Ventilate without mosquito nets.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it advisable to build with concrete in the tropics?

It is possible, but it is advisable to shade the walls well, insulate and ventilate the roof and ensure good cross ventilation. Without these measures, the concrete accumulates heat and releases it at night.

### What ceiling height is recommended?

In hot climates, high ceilings, around three meters or more, give more air volume and draw heat away from people's roofs, especially if there are high openings for hot air to escape.

### Do light colors really help?

Yes. On ceilings and walls exposed to the sun, light colors reflect more radiation and heat up less.

## In summary

In the hot and humid climate, the house must avoid heat and move air. Use wide eaves, breezeways, trellises and vegetation for shading, high ceilings with a ventilated cavity, insulation and light colors, and an elongated floor plan with cross ventilation and ceiling fans. Prefer materials that do not accumulate heat or shade heavy ones well, raise the floor when possible, prepare the house for heavy rains, control humidity, protect yourself from mosquitoes and, in hurricane zones, anchor roofs well and protect openings.
