The roof is the part of the house that works the most and the one we look at the least. Withstands midday sun, tropical downpours, hail, wind and, in some regions, hurricanes or snow. When it works, no one thinks about it. When it fails, leaks, stains, mold, sagging ceilings, and rooms that become uninhabitable due to heat or cold appear.

In Latin America many types of roof coexist: the flat concrete slab used as a roof, the clay tile of colonial houses, the metal sheet that covers millions of homes, fiber cement, insulated panels and palm or thatch roofs. Each one has different advantages, limits and demands. In this guide you'll see how they compare, what elements make a roof leak-free, how to choose based on the climate, and how to maintain it safely.

## The most common roofs

| Type | Advantages | Weak points | Climate where it works best |
|---|---|---|---|
| flat concrete slab | resistant, allows roof and extensions | it gets very hot, it depends on the waterproofing | almost all, if isolated and protected |
| Clay or ceramic tile | durable, good ventilation under the tiles, traditional aesthetics | heavier structure, shingles that break or slide | temperate and rainy, also warm |
| Metal sheet | light, economical, quick to install | heat, noise with rain, corrosion near the sea | any climate with insulation and ceiling |
| Fiber cement | economical, less noisy than foil | fragile underfoot, old sheets may contain asbestos | temperate |
| Insulated sandwich panel | built-in insulation, fast | joints that must be sealed well, higher cost | warm and cold |
| Palm, straw or plant materials | cool and ventilated | frequent maintenance, risk of fire and insects | warm, traditional uses |

## The elements that make a roof not leak

### The slope

The water must come out of the roof quickly. Each material needs a **minimum slope** indicated by the manufacturer: shingles require steep slopes, sheets allow less slope depending on their profile and overlaps, and flat slabs need at least a slight slope, usually 1 to 2 percent, toward drains. An insufficient slope causes water to stagnate or the wind to push it under the pieces.

### Waterproofing and backing layers

- **On flat slabs,** waterproofing is the main barrier. There are asphalt layers, acrylic or elastomeric coatings and synthetic membranes, each with its useful life.
- **Under shingles and sheets,** a membrane or backing sheet acts as a second defense if a piece breaks or the wind pushes water.

### The meetings

Most leaks do not appear in the center of the roof, but in the **junctions**: where the roof touches a higher wall, around chimneys, ducts and skylights, in the valleys where two skirts meet and at the ridges. There, metal finishing pieces are placed, known as flashings or flashings, and seals that must be checked frequently.

### The fixings

- **Sheets:** are fixed on the crest of the wave, not in the valley through which the water runs, with screws and rubber washers that seal the perforation.
- **Tiles:** must be well supported and, in wind or earthquake zones, fixed according to the system.
- **Structure anchorage:** the wind not only pushes, it also **sucks** the roofs upward. In hurricane or strong wind areas, the connection of the roof with the walls and the structure must be calculated and executed with adequate ties.

### Gutters and downspouts

They collect water and move it away from the walls. They must have a slope, sufficient capacity for heavy rain and mesh against leaves. We see it in the guide to [rainwater, gutters and drainage](/articulos/agua-de-lluvia-canaletas-y-drenaje/).

## Heat and cold: the ceiling as a regulator

In the tropics, the roof is the main source of heat in the house; in cold climates, the main route of heat loss. In both cases the solution involves **insulation**, **ceiling ventilation** and, in hot climates, **light colors** or reflective coatings. All options are explained in the [roof thermal insulation] guide (/articulos/aislamiento-termico-del-techo/).

## Choose according to climate and location

- **Hot and humid climate:** High, ventilated ceilings, with wide eaves, insulated ceiling and generous gutters. We develop it in the guide on the [house for hot and humid climate](/articulos/casa-para-clima-calido-humedo/).
- **Cold high altitude climate:** priority to insulation and airtightness, as we explain in the guide on the [house for cold high altitude climate](/articulos/casa-para-clima-frio-de-altura/).
- **Very rainy areas:** greater slopes, eaves that protect the walls and oversized drains.
- **Costa:** materials and fixings resistant to corrosion due to salinity.
- **Seismic zones:** the lighter the roof, the lower the forces that the earthquake generates on the walls. A heavy tile roof on weak walls is a risk, especially in adobe houses, as we explain in the guide on [adobe and earth construction](/articulos/adobe-y-construccion-con-tierra/).
- **Hurricane Zones:** Calculated anchorages and compliance with local wind regulations.

## The structure that supports the roof

Sloped roofs are supported on a structure of beams, trusses or trusses and purlins, made of wood or steel. That structure must be sized for the weight of the covering material, rain, wind, and, where applicable, snow or volcanic ash. A wooden structure needs protection against humidity and termites, as we explain in the guide on [wood at home](/articulos/madera-en-casa-especies-y-cuidados/), and a steel structure needs protection against corrosion. Changing a light roof for a heavier one, for example a sheet for a tile, requires first checking that the structure and walls can support it.

## The flat slab as a roof

In many cities the flat slab is used to hang clothes, place water tanks or grow later. Some recommendations:

- **Support the tanks** on beams or structural elements, because water weighs a lot.
- **Protect the waterproofing** from traffic with appropriate flooring in areas of use.
- **Keep drains clean,** especially before the rainy season.
- **If you plan a second floor,** follow the recommendations in the guide on [expanding the house with a second floor](/articulos/ampliar-la-casa-segundo-piso/).

## Maintenance: one review per year

Before each rainy season, check or have checked:

1. **Broken, displaced or loose tiles.**
2. **Sheets with rust, loose screws or deteriorated washers.**
3. **Fissures, blisters or wear** in the waterproofing of slabs.
4. **Encounters with walls, chimneys and ducts.**
5. **Gutters and downspouts** clogged or deformed.
6. **Puddles** that form on the slab after it rains.
7. **Stains on ceiling** or interior walls under ceiling.

Waterproof tile coatings typically need renewal every few years, depending on the product and exposure. A timely repair is much cheaper than replacing a water-damaged ceiling or furniture.

## Roof Safety

- **Falls from roofs** are serious and frequent accidents. Use firm ladders, harness and lifeline when working at height.
- **Never walk on sheets of fiber cement, polycarbonate or skylights:** they can break under the weight of a person.
- **Do not touch electrical cables** near the ceiling.
- **Old fiber cement sheets** may contain asbestos: do not cut, drill or break them, and leave their removal to specialists.
- **For important jobs,** hire experienced personnel and safety equipment.

## Frequent errors

- **Insufficient slopes** for the chosen material.
- **Screws into valley of sheet** instead of ridge.
- **Encounters with walls without metal finishes** or seals.
- **Water tanks on any part** of the slab.
- **Expired waterproofing** that no one checks.
- **Uninsulated tin roofs** in hot climates.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which roof is cheaper in the long run?

It depends on the weather and maintenance. Foil is the cheapest to install, but may require insulation and replacement due to corrosion. Slab and tile cost more initially, but if well maintained they last for many decades.

### How do I find the source of a leak?

Water usually travels along the ceiling before dripping, so the interior stain may be far from the entrance. First check the joints, fixings and broken pieces above and upstream of the stain.

### Can I put solar panels on any roof?

Most roofs accept them, but the structure, orientation and waterproofing of the fixings must be checked. It is advisable to have it evaluated by a qualified installer.

## In summary

Each roof has advantages and demands: the slab is resistant but depends on its waterproofing, the tile is durable and ventilated but heavy, the sheet is light and economical but hot and noisy, and insulated panels solve comfort at a higher cost. Respect the minimum slope, take care of the joints and fixings, anchor the structure well against the wind, insulate and ventilate according to the climate and check the roof every year before the rains. Always work safely at height.
