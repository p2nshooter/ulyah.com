When the house heats up, the first impulse is to think about an air conditioning unit. It is an effective solution, but expensive to install and keep on, and not always possible in a rented apartment or in a home with limited electrical installation. Additionally, if the house receives a lot of sun and is poorly protected, the air conditioning will have to work much harder to compensate.

The good news is that much of the heat in a home can be avoided or expelled with simple strategies, many of them free. The traditional houses of Yucatán, the Caribbean or the Andean valleys did so without electricity. In this guide you will see where the heat comes from, which measures have the most effect and how the strategy changes between a dry climate, such as that of northern Mexico or the coast of Peru in summer, and a hot and humid climate, such as that of Guayaquil, Barranquilla or Mérida.

## Where does the heat come from?

Before acting, it is advisable to know where the heat comes in:

| Source | How to heat | What more help |
|---|---|---|
| Sun through the windows | passes through glass and heats floors and furniture | outside shadow |
| Roof | heats up in the sun and radiates heat downwards | light color, insulation, double roof |
| exposed walls | They accumulate heat and release it hours later | shadow, light color, vegetation |
| Hot air coming in | windows open during the hottest hours | ventilate in cool hours |
| internal heat | kitchen, spotlights, appliances, people | reduce and displace heat sources |

In most homes, **unshaded windows and the roof** are the two main sources. That's why they are the best place to start.

## 1. Stop the sun before it comes in

Once the sun passes through the glass, the heat is already inside. Interior curtains help with light, but stop only part of the heat. Outdoor shade is much more effective:

- **Exterior blinds or shutters**, which can be closed during sunny hours.
- **Awnings and eaves** over the windows that receive direct sun.
- **Vertical louvers and sunshades** on the east and west facades, where the sun reaches low.
- **Vegetation:** Trees, vines or large pots on the balcony.
- **Solar control films** on the glass, when there is no other option.

The west façade is the most critical in almost the entire continent, because it receives direct sun during the hottest hours. The guide on [house solar orientation](/articulos/orientacion-solar-de-la-casa/) explains how to identify each facade.

## 2. Protect the roof

In tropical climates, the roof is the surface that receives the most sun. An unprotected concrete slab or metal sheet can reach very high temperatures and heat the room below well into the night.

The most effective measures are:

- **Paint the roof white or with a reflective coating.** A white or reflective waterproofing reflects much of the solar radiation.
- **Insulate** with suitable materials above or below the cover.
- **Create a ventilated double roof** or an air chamber that allows heat to escape.
- **Shade the roof** with pergolas, separate light roofs or plants.

All of these options are explained in the [roof thermal insulation] guide (/articulos/aislamiento-termico-del-techo/).

## 3. Ventilate according to your climate

Ventilation is a powerful tool, but it is used differently depending on the type of climate.

**In dry climates with cool nights**, such as northern Mexico, the highlands, areas of Argentina or the inter-Andean valleys:

1. **Open everything at night and early in the morning**, when the air outside is cooler than the inside.
2. **Close windows and protections** when the outside temperature begins to rise.
3. **Let the walls and floors cool** overnight so they absorb heat the next day.

**In hot and humid climates**, such as the Caribbean, the tropical Pacific coast or the Amazon:

1. **Maintain a constant airflow**, because the nights are also warm.
2. **Look for openings on opposite sides** of the house to allow air to pass through the rooms.
3. **Use mosquito nets** on windows and beds so you can sleep with the windows open and protect yourself from mosquito-borne diseases.

The [cross ventilation](/articulos/ventilacion-cruzada/) guide explains how to locate the openings so that air can truly circulate.

## 4. Take advantage of the chimney effect

Hot air rises. If the house has openings high up, such as high windows, opening skylights, stairwells, or a patio, hot air escapes from the top and draws cooler air through the low openings. Roof wind extractors, very common in some regions, help remove hot air accumulated under the roof. A well-designed patio fulfills the same function, as explained in the guide on [interior patio and microclimate] (/articulos/patio-interior-microclima/).

## 5. Use fans wisely

The fans do not lower the air temperature, but they do lower the **thermal sensation**: the moving air evaporates sweat and you feel several degrees cooler. They consume much less energy than an air conditioner.

- **Ceiling fan:** distributes the air well throughout the room. In summer it should rotate in the direction that pushes the air downwards.
- **Standing or table fan:** direct it towards people, not the wall.
- **Fan in the window:** at night, in dry climates, it can bring in fresh air or expel hot air.
- **Turn it off when you leave:** Cools people, not the room.

## 6. Reduces the heat you produce inside

- **Change incandescent bulbs for LEDs.** Incandescents convert most of the energy into heat.
- **Cook during cool hours**, use a pressure cooker, cover the pots and turn on the hood or open the kitchen window.
- **Dry clothes outdoors** and not inside the house.
- **Disconnect devices** that you do not use and that remain hot while waiting.
- **Avoid the oven** on the hottest days.

## 7. Evaporative coolers: only in dry climates

Evaporative coolers, known in some regions as coolers, pass air through a wet panel. In dry climates they work very well and consume little energy. In humid climates, on the other hand, they hardly cool and increase the humidity of the environment, which makes the sensation heavier. Before buying one, check that the climate in your city is really dry in the hot season.

## 8. Light colors and vegetation around

Dark surfaces, such as concrete floors, asphalt, or dark walls, absorb heat and radiate it into the home. An environment with plants, grass or light floors heats up much less. A well-placed tree to the west can protect an entire facade in the afternoons. More ideas in the guide about [pergolas, awnings and shade](/articulos/pergolas-toldos-y-sombra/).

## 9. Sleep cooler

- **Choose the coolest bedroom** in the house, away from the west and, if possible, not right under the roof.
- **Use cotton or linen sheets**, which breathe better than synthetic ones.
- **Ventilate the bedroom** in the evening in dry climates and maintain a draft in humid climates.

## When is air conditioning appropriate?

In extreme heat waves, or if there are elderly people, babies, people with chronic illnesses or health problems at home, air conditioning may be necessary for health. If you use it, apply the previous strategies first: a shaded house with a protected roof needs smaller equipment and spends less. Set a moderate temperature, around 24 to 26 degrees, keep doors and windows closed while it works, and clean the filters regularly. More tips in the [energy saving at home](/articulos/ahorro-de-energia-en-casa/) guide.

## Frequent errors

- **Open the windows during the hottest hours** in dry climates.
- **Rely only on interior curtains** against the sun.
- **Forget the roof**, which in the tropics is the largest source of heat.
- **Use evaporative coolers in humid climates.**
- **Leave fans on in empty rooms.**
- **Cooking and drying clothes inside the house** in the heat.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many degrees can you lower it without air conditioning?

It depends on the climate and the house. By combining outdoor shade, a protected roof and good ventilation, many homes manage to keep the interior several degrees below the outside temperature during the hottest hours, especially in dry climates. The fans add a sensation of additional coolness.

### Does wetting the floor or hanging wet towels help?

In dry climates it can help a little, because evaporation absorbs heat. In humid climates it only increases humidity and the feeling of heaviness.

### Is it advisable to close everything during the day?

In dry climates, yes: closing windows and screens keeps hot air out. In humid climates, it is best to keep the air flowing with shaded windows.

## In summary

To cool your home without air conditioning, start by stopping the sun from entering with exterior shading and protecting the roof with light colors, insulation, or a ventilated flysheet. Ventilate at night and close during the day in dry climates, and maintain a constant draft in humid climates. Take advantage of the chimney effect, use fans directed at people and reduce the heat you produce inside. Reserve evaporative coolers for dry climates and use air conditioning sparingly when health dictates.
