Many of the world's most popular houseplants originate from Latin America. Adam's rib grows wild in the forests of southern Mexico and Central America; Anthuriums, philodendrons, bromeliads, peperomias and calatheas come from the tropical forests of the continent, and cacti are American. However, at home many of them suffer: they turn yellow, stretch out looking for light or rot due to excess watering.

The most common cause is only one: **the plant is not in the right place for it**. Each species needs a different amount of light, and the light inside a house is much less than it seems. In this guide you will see how to evaluate the real light in each corner, which plants are suitable for each light level, how to water them without drowning them, how to avoid pests and which plants are toxic to pets and children.

## The light inside the house is less than it seems

The human eye adapts so well that we do not perceive how much the light decreases when we move away from a window. A few meters from the glass, the light may be a small fraction of what is next to it. For a plant, that difference is enormous.

To evaluate the light in a corner:

- **Observe the shadows at noon.** If the hand casts a clear shadow, there is intense light; if the shadow is diffuse, medium light; If you can barely see it, there is little light.
- **Look how much sky you can see** from the place where the plant will be.
- **Please note the orientation** of the window. The guide on [solar orientation of the house](/articulos/orientacion-solar-de-la-casa/) explains which facade receives the most sun in each region.
- **Some light measurement apps** on the phone give a rough idea.

## Four light levels

| Level | Where is it located | What plants usually work |
|---|---|---|
| direct sun | next to windows that receive sun for several hours | cactus, succulents, aloe, aromatic herbs |
| Indirect bright light | near a bright window with no direct sun on the leaves | Adam's rib, anthuriums, calatheas, ficus, bromeliads, orchids |
| medium light | a few meters from the window or next to shaded windows | philodendrons, peperomias, ribbons, pothos |
| low light | hallways, corners away from windows | mother-in-law's tongue, zamioculca, aspidistra |

No plant lives in the dark. The so-called "low light" ones tolerate poor conditions, but grow very slowly. If a corner is really dark, a grow light can help.

## Plants for every corner

### By the sunny window

**Cacti and succulents** need several hours of sun and spaced watering. **Aromatic herbs** such as basil, rosemary or mint also appreciate the sun in the kitchen. Beware: glass can intensify the heat of the afternoon sun and burn delicate leaves.

### Bright light without direct sun

It is the ideal place for most tropical foliage plants: **Adam's rib, anthuriums, calatheas, bromeliads and orchids**. Colombia, for example, has one of the greatest diversities of orchids in the world, and many cultivated species come from its cloud forests. These plants also appreciate moderate environmental humidity.

### medium light

**Philodendrons, peperomias, ribbons and pothos** adapt well to light that reaches a few meters from a window. They are good options for shelves and shelves.

### low light

**Mother-in-law's tongue, zamioculca and aspidistra** tolerate corners with little light and spaced watering. They are very resistant to neglect, but not miraculous: with more light they grow better.

## Water well: the key to everything

Overwatering kills more houseplants than underwatering.

1. **Check the soil before watering:** insert a finger a few centimeters. If it's wet, wait.
2. **Water thoroughly** until water runs out of the drainage holes, then let the top layer dry.
3. **Empty the plate** of the pot after a few minutes. The accumulated water rots the roots and can also breed mosquitoes, something important in areas with dengue.
4. **Water less when it is cold** or there is less light, and more in warm and bright times.
5. **Use pots with drainage holes.** If the decorative pot does not have them, place a plastic pot inside that does.

## Ambient humidity

Tropical plants usually appreciate humidity. In humid coastal cities it is not usually a problem; In homes with dry or high altitude climates, the air may be too dry for some species.

- **Group several plants:** together they create a somewhat more humid microclimate.
- **Place the pot on a tray with stones and water,** without the base of the pot touching the water.
- **Keep them away from hot or cold air currents.**

## Substrate and pots

- **A loose and aerated substrate,** with bark, perlite or other materials, prevents the roots from suffocating.
- **Transplant** when the roots come out of the holes or the plant stops growing, to a pot just a little larger.
- **Fertilize sparingly** during the growing season, following product directions.

## Buy a healthy plant

A good purchase avoids many problems:

- **Check the undersides of leaves and stems** for insects, spots or cobwebs.
- **Prefers plants with firm leaves** and new shoots, without rotten roots or a musty smell in the soil.
- **Ask what light conditions it was in** in the nursery. If it was in full sun, don't suddenly take it to a dark corner.
- **Be patient in the first weeks:** it is normal for it to lose some leaves while it adapts to its new place.
- **Keep the new plants separated** from the others for a few days, to check that they do not bring pests.

## Common pests

| Plague | How to recognize | What favors it |
|---|---|---|
| mealybug | cottony balls on stems and leaf axils | weakened plants, poor ventilation |
| red spider | yellow dots and fine cobwebs | dry air and heat |
| Aphids | small insects grouped on shoots | tender shoots, excess fertilizer |
| Substrate midges | small flies around the flower pot | always wet land |

Check the plants when you water them, isolate the affected ones and wipe the leaves with a damp cloth. For heavy infestations, consult a nursery for appropriate treatment.

## Toxic plants: be careful with children and pets

Several common houseplants can be toxic if chewed or ingested, including pothos, Adam's rib, anthuriums, diephenbachia, peace lily, and aloe. They usually cause mouth irritation, drooling or digestive upset. If you have small children, dogs or cats:

- **Learn about each species** before buying it.
- **Locate toxic plants out of reach,** on high or hanging shelves.
- **Consult a veterinarian or a poison control center** if you suspect they have ingested a plant.

## Do plants purify the air?

It is often repeated that plants clean the air in the house. Under laboratory conditions, some species absorb certain substances, but in a real home many plants would be needed to achieve a noticeable effect. For healthy air, **ventilation** is much more effective, as we explain in the [cross ventilation](/articulos/ventilacion-cruzada/) guide. Plants provide well-being, beauty and a feeling of calm, and that is already a lot.

## Decorate with plants

- **Group plants of different heights and textures** instead of scattering them.
- **Use pendants and shelves** to take advantage of the height in small spaces, as we suggest in the guide to [gain space in a small apartment](/articulos/departamento-pequeno-ganar-espacio/).
- **Rotate the pots** from time to time so that they grow evenly.
- **Cleans dust from the leaves,** which reduces the light they receive.

## Frequent errors

- **Choose the plant based on the photo** without considering the light of the place.
- **Water by calendar** without checking the soil.
- **Pots without drainage** or dishes always with water.
- **Toxic plants within reach** of children and pets.
- **Change the plant constantly.**
- **Expect a sun plant to live in a dark hallway.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do leaves turn yellow?

The most common causes are excess watering, lack of light or the natural aging of the oldest leaves. Check soil moisture and drainage first.

### Can I have plants in the bathroom?

Yes, if it has a window with natural light. The humidity of the bathroom favors many tropical plants. In a windowless bathroom, they will only survive with adequate artificial light.

### What do I do with my plants when I travel?

Water them well before leaving, group them in a bright place without direct sun and, for long absences, use self-watering systems or ask someone you trust for help.

## In summary

Choose each plant based on the actual light in the location: direct sun for cacti and succulents, bright indirect light for most foliage tropicals, medium light for philodendrons and peperomias, and low light for only the most tolerant species. Water when the soil asks for it, use pots with drainage and empty the dishes. Take care of humidity in dry climates, keep an eye on pests and place toxic plants away from children and pets. Enjoy them for their beauty, and ventilate the house to have healthy air.
