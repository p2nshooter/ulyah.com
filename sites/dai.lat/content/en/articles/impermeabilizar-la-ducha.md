The shower is the most punished area of ​​the house. He receives water several times a day, with soap, heat and splashes in all directions. When it fails, the problem is rarely seen in the shower itself: It appears as a stain on the ceiling of the apartment below, as swollen paint in the hallway, as a swollen baseboard in the neighboring bedroom, or as ceramic tiles that start to sound hollow. By then, the water has been filtering for months.

A widespread mistake is to think that ceramic or porcelain tile is waterproof. Not so: the pieces may be almost waterproof, but the cement joints allow water to pass through, and the water that passes through needs a barrier underneath. In this guide you will see how to build a watertight shower, what materials are used, how to check that it works before plating, and how to detect and prevent leaks in an existing shower.

## How water is filtered in a shower

- **Because of the joints:** the cement nozzles or set are porous and absorb water, especially if they are cracked or worn.
- **Because of the meetings:** the corners, the meeting between floor and wall and the edge of the shower tray are weak points that move slightly.
- **Through the drain:** if the waterproofing is not well attached to the drain, the water passes along its contour.
- **Due to the perforations:** tap outlets, niches and accessory screws.
- **Due to the pipes:** sometimes the problem is not the waterproofing, but a pipe that leaks inside the wall.

## The components of a waterproof shower

### 1. Sloped base

The shower floor should have a **slope toward the drain**, usually about 1 to 2 percent, so that water doesn't sit still. The slope is built into the base, before waterproofing, not only with ceramic glue.

### 2. waterproof membrane

It is the true barrier against water. There are several families:

| Type | How to apply | Comments |
|---|---|---|
| Flexible cementitious liquid membrane | with a brush or trowel, in several layers | widely used under ceramic, compatible with cementitious adhesives |
| Liquid membrane made of polyurethane or other polymers | with brush or roller | elastic, adapts to complex shapes |
| waterproof sheet | sticks to the base with adhesive | uniform and controlled thickness |

Whatever the system, it must be **designed to go under ceramic in humid areas** and applied according to the manufacturer's instructions, with the thickness and number of layers indicated.

### 3. Reinforcement bands

In the corners, at the meeting between floor and wall and at the joints between different materials, **reinforcement bands or tapes** embedded in the membrane are placed. They absorb small movements that would otherwise open cracks right where the most water reaches.

### 4. Sleeves in perforations

Tap outlets and other perforations are sealed with **sleeves or preformed pieces** compatible with the membrane.

### 5. Compatible drain

The sump or drain must have a wing, flange or union system that allows **connecting the membrane** in a watertight manner. Linear drains, in the form of a channel, allow a slope in only one direction and facilitate the use of large pieces.

### 6. Waterproofing height

In the shower area, the membrane usually goes up the walls **at least above the shower**, usually around 1.8 to 2 meters, and covers the entire shower floor. Outside the shower, it is advisable to waterproof the bathroom floor and go up the walls a few centimeters.

## The flood test: don't skip it

Before placing the ceramic, a **tightness test** is done:

1. Securely cover the drain.
2. Fill the shower with a few centimeters of water, without exceeding the waterproofed height.
3. Mark the water level.
4. Wait a day or two.
5. Check the level and, above all, the ceilings and walls nearby or on the lower floor.

If the level drops more than evaporation explains or stains appear, there is a fault that must be corrected **before plating**. It is much cheaper to repair it now than later, with the ceramic in place.

## Place the ceramic correctly

- **Suitable adhesive** for humid areas, applied so that the piece is supported on its entire surface, without gaps where water accumulates.
- **Epoxy grouts** in the shower: They are much more resistant to water, stains and mold than cement grouts.
- **Sanitary silicone** in plane changes, such as corners and the meeting between floor and wall, instead of a rigid joint.
- **Movement joints** where indicated by the system, especially in large showers.

## Niches, benches and screens

The details that make a shower comfortable are also delicate points:

- **Shampoo niches:** they must be waterproofed on the inside with the same membrane and have the base slightly inclined outwards, so that water does not stay at the bottom.
- **Construction benches:** their upper surface needs a slope and continuous waterproofing with that of the wall and the floor.
- **Screens and accessories:** each screw goes through the waterproofing. Use appropriate plugs and sealants and avoid drilling in wet areas when possible.

## The order of work, in summary

1. Firm base sloping toward the drain.
2. Drain installed and prepared to join the membrane.
3. Primer if the system requires it.
4. Bands in corners, meetings and sleeves in perforations.
5. Membrane in the indicated layers and thicknesses.
6. Flood test.
7. Ceramic with adequate adhesive, epoxy and silicone joints in plane changes.

## Shower tray or built-in shower

- **Prefabricated plate:** made of ceramic, resin or acrylic, it already comes with a slope. It is simpler, but the **meeting between the plate and the wall** must be carefully waterproofed and sealed.
- **Build-in or floor-level shower:** more elegant and accessible, but requires that the slope, membrane and drain be perfectly executed.

In both cases, a small slope or a curb helps contain the water within the shower area.

## Signs that your shower leaks

- **Stains on the ceiling** of the floor below or on neighboring walls.
- **Swollen or peeling paint** near bathroom.
- **Swollen wooden baseboards** next to bathroom.
- **Ceramics that sound hollow** or come loose.
- **Dark, cracked or crumbling joints.**
- **Persistent musty smell.**

Before blaming the waterproofing, rule out a leak in the pipes: turn off all faucets and see if the water meter continues to tick. More clues in the guide on [humidity in walls](/articulos/humedad-en-paredes-causas-y-soluciones/).

## Improvements without demolition

If the shower does not have serious leaks but the seals are deteriorated:

1. **Remove damaged joints** and refill them, ideally with epoxy material.
2. **Change the silicone** in corners and meetings.
3. **Seals the perforations** of faucets and accessories.
4. **Clean with neutral products:** Acidic cleaners wear down cement joints.

If the leak already reaches other environments, the safest thing is to redo the shower with complete waterproofing.

## Ventilation: the other half

A waterproof shower also needs to be dried. The humidity left in the environment favors mold in gaskets and silicones. The guide on [ventilation and mold in the bathroom](/articulos/ventilacion-y-moho-en-el-bano/) explains how to solve it.

## Frequent errors

- **Trust that ceramic is waterproof.**
- **Make the slope only with the ceramic glue**.
- **Do not reinforce corners** or meetings.
- **Drains without union** with the membrane.
- **Skip the flood test.**
- **Cement joints** in heavy-duty showers.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should the membrane dry before plating?

It depends on the product, the thickness and the ambient humidity. The manufacturer indicates the minimum time between layers and before placing the ceramic. Respecting it is part of the system.

### Can I waterproof over old ceramic?

There are systems that allow this in certain conditions, but in general the most reliable solution is to remove the damaged coating and build the complete system from the ground up.

### How often should you check the shower?

A visual check once a year is sufficient in most homes: gaskets, silicones, the contour of the drain and the tap outlets. If cracks or mold appear on the silicones, renew them before water finds its way.

### Who should do this job?

Personnel with experience in bathroom waterproofing. Ask what system they will use, how they will resolve corners and drainage, and if they will flood test before plating.

## In summary

Ceramic does not waterproof: watertightness depends on a base with a slope towards the drain, a continuous waterproof membrane, reinforcement bands at corners and joints, sleeves in the perforations and a drain attached to the membrane. Raise the waterproofing above the shower and always do the flood test before plating. Use adhesives for wet areas, epoxy joints and silicone when changing planes, check joints and silicones every year and ventilate the bathroom well.
