Apartments are increasingly smaller in the large cities of Latin America. In Mexico City, Bogotá, Lima, Santiago or São Paulo, many new homes are between 30 and 50 square meters, and in them you have to sleep, cook, work, store the entire season's clothes and, sometimes, receive the family. The feeling that nothing fits and that everything is always in sight is very common.

The good news is that a well-thought-out small space can be more comfortable than a poorly organized large one. It is not about filling every corner, but about carefully choosing what goes in, where it is stored and how each meter is used. In this guide you will see how to get started, what furniture performs the most, how to take advantage of height, how to separate rooms without walls and what visual tricks make the space feel larger. Almost everything can be applied without works, even in a rented apartment.

## First, less stuff

No smart piece of furniture is worth having more objects than you can fit. Before purchasing organizers:

1. **Check by categories:** clothing, books, cooking, papers, souvenirs, tools.
2. **Keep with what you use and like.** What you haven't used in a year, with some exceptions, you probably don't need.
3. **Donate, sell or recycle** whatever is left over.
4. **Define a place for everything.** What has no place ends up on the table or a chair.
5. **Store out what is in season,** if you have the possibility, or in boxes at the top of the closet.

This step, which costs no money, usually frees up more space than any purchase.

## Measure and plan by functions

Make a simple plan with the measurements of the apartment, the location of windows, doors, sockets and radiators or equipment. Then think about the features you need:

- sleep,
- cook and eat,
- work or study,
- rest and receive,
- save.

In a small apartment, several functions share space. The goal is to move from one to the other quickly: for the dining table to become a desk in a minute, or for the sofa to receive a visitor who is staying overnight.

## Furniture that does more than one thing

| Furniture | What solves | What to check before buying |
|---|---|---|
| Bed with drawers or with folding base | store bedding, suitcases, seasonal clothing | that the drawers can be opened with the available space |
| Sofa bed | occasional guests | comfort of the mattress and ease of the mechanism |
| Extendable or folding table | eat, work and receive | stability and open and closed measurements |
| Folding wall desk | disappearing office | wall and hardware strength |
| Bench or pouf with storage | seat and trunk | make it firm to sit on |
| folding wall bed | bedroom that converts into a living room | professional installation and secure anchoring |

Prefer **furniture with legs** that reveal the floor: they make the space feel lighter than solid furniture resting on the floor.

## Take advantage of the height

Almost all apartments have more space in height than in floor plan.

- **Shelves up to the ceiling,** with the most used items at hand height and the occasional items on top.
- **Shelves over doors and windows,** for books or boxes.
- **Tall furniture in the kitchen** up to the ceiling, instead of leaving a gap that accumulates dust. More ideas in the [kitchen storage](/articulos/almacenamiento-en-la-cocina/) guide.
- **Hooks and bars** for bags, coats, utensils or bicycles.
- **Vertical organizers inside the closet,** with double bars for short clothes. We see it in the guide on [closet and stored without humidity](/articulos/closet-y-guardado-sin-humedad/).

Always secure tall furniture to the wall. In seismic zones, an unanchored bookcase can fall during an earthquake.

## Separate rooms without walls

In a studio apartment or in a living room-dining room-kitchen, dividing with walls takes away light and air. There are alternatives:

- **A rug** defines the living area. More about textiles in the guide to [curtains, textiles and rugs](/articulos/cortinas-textiles-y-alfombras/).
- **An open shelving** separates without blocking light and adds storage.
- **Ceiling to floor curtains** hide the bed when there are visitors.
- **Lightweight sliding panels or screens**.
- **Lighting:** a hanging lamp over the table and another standing next to the sofa create two different environments. We explain it in the [house lighting] guide (/articulos/iluminacion-de-la-casa/).

## Visual tricks that work

- **Light and continuous colors** on walls and ceilings make the limits appear further away. The [interior colors](/articulos/colores-para-interiores/) guide helps you choose them.
- **The same floor throughout the apartment** avoids visual cuts.
- **Large mirrors** in front of or next to the windows multiply light and depth.
- **Curtains hung close to the ceiling** and reaching the floor make the ceiling appear higher.
- **Fewer, but larger pieces of furniture** give a tidier feel than a lot of small pieces of furniture.
- **Transparent materials** such as glass or acrylic, on tables and chairs, take up less visual space.
- **Clear windows:** Do not block the light with furniture or heavy curtains.

## Doors that stay out of the way

Each swing door needs a free area to open. Some ideas:

- **Change swing doors for sliding ones** when possible.
- **Remove closet doors** and use a curtain, or choose sliding doors.
- **Invert the direction of opening** of a door so that it clashes less with the furniture.

In a rented apartment, check with the landlord before changing doors or drilling holes.

## The small kitchen

In many apartments the kitchen is a hallway or a wall within the living room. Some quick ideas:

- **Release the cover:** Leave out only what you use daily, like the blender or coffee maker, and put the rest away.
- **Use the doors** of furniture to hang lids, cloths or spices.
- **A magnetic bar** for knives and metal utensils saves a drawer.
- **A folding table** fixed to the wall can serve as a work and dining surface for two people.
- **Compact or combination appliances,** such as an oven with a microwave, replace two appliances.

The [kitchen layout](/articulos/distribucion-de-la-cocina/) guide explains the measures that should be respected even if the space is minimal.

## Children and pets

With children, low drawers and boxes with wheels under the bed allow them to organize their toys themselves. With pets, it is advisable to assign them a fixed corner for bed, water and food, away from traffic, and choose fabrics that are easy to wash on the sofa. In both cases, fewer objects within reach means less daily clutter.

## Work at home in a small space

- **A wall desk** or a narrow table by the window.
- **A good chair,** even if it is foldable, that allows you to work for several hours without pain.
- **A desk lamp** so you don't depend on the ceiling light.
- **A box or drawer** where you can store everything when you're done, to recover the space at night.

## Clothes, washing and humidity

In small apartments it is tempting to dry clothes inside, but that increases humidity and encourages mold. If there is no other option, use a folding clothesline near an open window and ventilate well. More ideas in the [cross ventilation](/articulos/ventilacion-cruzada/) guide.

## The balcony as another room

A balcony, no matter how small, can expand the useful space: a folding table for breakfast, plants, a chair for reading. Always respect the building regulations and the weight it can support, as we explain in the guide on [safe balconies and terraces](/articulos/balcones-y-terrazas-seguras/).

## Solutions for rented apartments

- **Standing shelves and coat racks** that do not require drilling.
- **Snap-on bars and organizers** inside the closet or in the shower.
- **Adhesive hooks** for light objects.
- **Modular furniture** that you can take to the next home.
- **Textiles and rugs** that change the environment without works.

## Frequent errors

- **Buy organizers before reducing items.**
- **Fill the space with small furniture** that fragments the environment.
- **Cover windows** with tall furniture.
- **Do not anchor bookcases and tall furniture** to the wall.
- **Dry clothes inside without ventilating.**
- **Divide with opaque walls or panels** that remove light and air.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a folding bed suitable?

It can be an excellent solution in studio apartments, because it frees up space during the day. Requires solid installation on a suitable wall and a quality mechanism. When renting, consult with the owner first.

### What size sofa should I choose for a small living room?

One that leaves at least 60 to 80 centimeters free for circulation and that does not block windows or doors. A two-seater sofa with thin arms takes up less space than a three-seater with wide arms. More ideas in the [room: measurements and distribution](/articulos/sala-medidas-y-distribucion/) guide.

### Are dark colors prohibited in small spaces?

No. A dark color on a single wall or in a corner can add depth and character. The important thing is to balance it with good lighting and clear surfaces.

Find more ideas in the section [Interiors and decoration](/temas/interiores-y-decoracion/).

## In summary

In a small apartment, the first step is to have fewer things and a place for each one. Plan by function, choose furniture that fulfills more than one use and take advantage of the height with well-anchored shelves. Separate rooms with rugs, open shelves, curtains and lighting instead of walls. Use light, continuous colors, mirrors, high curtains and furniture with legs. Change swing doors for sliding ones when you can, ventilate when drying clothes and turn the balcony into another room. In a rental, choose solutions that do not require works.
