A beautiful outdoor deck can turn into a skating rink with the first rain. Falls on patios, terraces, outdoor stairs and pool edges are common and can be serious, especially for children and older people. Many times the cause is not carelessness, but a wrong choice: a shiny ceramic designed for interiors, a polished stone, a sealant that leaves the surface smooth, or a patio without a slope where water remains.

Choosing an exterior floor is a technical decision as well as an aesthetic one. It must withstand rain, sun, changes in temperature and, in some areas, frost, and also offer grip when wet. In this guide you'll see how to read anti-slip ratings, what materials work best for each use, how to install and drain an outdoor floor, and how to keep it safe over the years.

## How anti-slip is measured

Manufacturers indicate slip resistance with different classifications depending on the standard they use. The most frequent ones in catalogs in the region are:

| Classification | What does it measure? | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| Classes R9 to R13 | test with shoes on inclined ramp | R9 offers the least grip, R13 the most. For outdoors, R11 or more is usually recommended. |
| Classes A, B and C | test with bare feet and water | for pool edges, outdoor showers and changing rooms. C is the most demanding |
| Dynamic friction coefficient | friction measured with a wet equipment | the higher, the more grip |
| Slippery classes 0 to 3 | classification used in Spain | class 3 is required for outdoors and sloping areas |

There is no need to memorize them. The important thing is **ask for the information when purchasing** and check that the floor is designed for wet outdoors. If the catalog does not indicate any classification, it is a bad sign.

## Most used materials

### Non-slip porcelain tile for outdoors

It is one of the most versatile materials. It absorbs very little water, resists frost, stains and wear, and comes in textured finishes that provide good grip. They imitate stone, wood or concrete. It is advisable to choose pieces specifically classified for the exterior, not the same smooth finish as the interior. The guide on [ceramic or porcelain tile](/articulos/ceramica-o-porcelanato/) explains the differences.

### natural stone

Flagstones, quarries, granites, slates and other stones give a warm and durable look. The grip depends on the **finish**: a polished stone slips; a bush-hammered, flamed or natural textured one holds much better. Some stones are porous and absorb water and stains, so they may need a sealer. More information in the guide to [natural stone at home](/articulos/piedra-natural-en-casa/).

### textured concrete

Concrete swept with a broom, washed to expose the aggregate or stamped with texture offers good grip at low cost. Polished concrete, on the other hand, is slippery in wet outdoors. The guide on [apparent concrete](/articulos/concreto-aparente-cuidados/) talks about its care.

### Cobblestones and permeable pavements

Concrete or clay pavers, placed on a base of sand and gravel, allow some rainwater to infiltrate through the joints. They reduce puddles and runoff into the street. Blocks with spaces for grass combine walkable surface and vegetation.

### Wood and composites

Hardwood or treated wood decks and composite decks are warm to the touch and comfortable to walk on barefoot. They can become slippery with moss or algae in shady, humid areas. They need ventilation from below and maintenance, as we explain in the guide on [wood at home](/articulos/madera-en-casa-especies-y-cuidados/).

### Gravel

Cheap, draining and safe against slipping, but uncomfortable for furniture, wheelchairs and heels. Works well on garden paths.

## Choose according to use

| Place | What to prioritize | Common options |
|---|---|---|
| Patio or terrace for daily use | wet grip, resistance and easy cleaning | exterior porcelain tile, textured stone, swept concrete |
| Exterior stairs | high grip and visible step edge | textured pieces with non-slip nose, lighting |
| Ramps | very high grip and gentle slope | textured concrete, ramp parts |
| pool edge | barefoot grip and don't get too hot | class C pieces, clear stones with texture |
| garden paths | drainage and low maintenance | pavers, gravel, spaced tiles |
| Cold high altitude areas | frost resistance | porcelain tile, low absorption stones |

## The heat of the sun

Dark floors exposed to the sun can become so hot that they are uncomfortable or even dangerous for bare feet and pet paws. On sunny terraces and around swimming pools, prefer **light tones** and materials that heat up less, and provide shade with pergolas or awnings, as we explain in the [pergolas, awnings and shade] guide (/articulos/pergolas-toldos-y-sombra/).

## Slope and drainage

A good exterior floor does not allow water to accumulate:

- **Gentle slope** towards drain or garden, usually about 1 to 2 percent, and always moving water away from the house.
- **Grates and gutters** sufficient for heavy rain.
- **Well made joints** that do not leave water trapped under the pieces.

Puddles don't just slip: they encourage moss, algae and stains, and can leak onto walls and interiors. More details in the guide to [rainwater, gutters and drainage](/articulos/agua-de-lluvia-canaletas-y-drenaje/).

## Installation: what you can't see also matters

- **Firm and compacted base,** with subfloor suitable for traffic.
- **Outdoor adhesives,** flexible and waterproof, applied to the entire surface of the piece, without gaps where water accumulates.
- **Perimeter and intermediate expansion joints**, because the exterior floors expand and contract with the sun.
- **Gasket material** suitable for outdoors.

A poorly installed floor can peel, crack or make a hollow noise over time.

## Maintenance to keep it safe

- **Sweep leaves and dirt** frequently: when wet, they slip.
- **Remove moss and algae** in shaded areas with a brush and appropriate products, following the floor manufacturer's instructions.
- **Use the pressure washer sparingly,** at a safe distance, so as not to damage joints or surfaces.
- **Be careful with sealants:** some leave a smooth, shiny film that reduces grip. If you need to seal a porous stone, choose products with a natural finish or with a non-slip additive.
- **Check for loose pieces** or unevenness that could cause tripping.

## Steps and lighting

The exterior stairs concentrate many falls. In addition to a floor with good grip, it is advisable to:

- **Mark the edge of each step** with a contrast piece or band.
- **Keep all steps the same height.**
- **Install a firm handrail.**
- **Illuminate the steps** with low lights that do not dazzle, as we explain in the [house lighting] guide (/articulos/iluminacion-de-la-casa/).

## Ramps and accessibility

A home without steps at the entrance is more comfortable for everyone: seniors, strollers, wheelchairs, or someone with a leg in a cast. If you have to bridge a gap with a ramp:

- **Gentle slope:** As a reference, many accessibility standards set around 8 percent as the usual maximum in short sections, and less in long sections.
- **Flat rests** at the beginning, at the end and on long ramps.
- **Surface with good grip** also in the wet.
- **Handrails on both sides** and a side edge that prevents a wheel from coming off.

Check the accessibility regulations in your country, which establish precise measurements.

## Where it is not convenient to save

On an exterior floor, the visible material is only part of the cost. If the budget is tight, it is worth prioritizing the correct base, slope, drainage and adhesive, even if that means choosing a simpler piece. A well-installed, inexpensive floor lasts longer and is safer than an expensive one placed on a weak foundation.

## Frequent errors

- **Use ceramic or porcelain tiles indoors** on terraces and patios.
- **Choose polished stone** for outdoors.
- **Apply glossy sealers** that make the surface smooth.
- **Floors without slope** that accumulate water.
- **Dark floors** around the pool in very sunny climates.
- **Ignore moss** in shady areas.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I make a floor I already have non-slip?

There are treatments that increase the grip of certain surfaces, as well as non-slip tapes and strips for steps. Its effectiveness and duration vary; It is advisable to test in a small area and follow the manufacturer's instructions.

### What flooring is best for a pool deck?

One with a high level barefoot rating, light in color so it doesn't get too hot, and with rounded edges. The slope should direct water away from the pool toward a drain.

### Are pavers safe?

Yes, if they are well leveled and compacted. Over time some pieces may sink, which should be corrected to avoid tripping over.

## In summary

An exterior floor must grip wet, resist the weather and drain well. Always ask for the non-slip classification, choose textured finishes instead of polished ones, and pieces designed for outdoors. Adjust the material to the use: patios, stairs, ramps, pools or paths. Slope towards drains, install with outdoor adhesives and gaskets, avoid shiny sealants, prefer light tones where the sun hits hard and keep the floor free of leaves, moss and algae.
