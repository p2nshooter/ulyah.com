A dark stain in the corner of the bedroom, paint that swells and peels on the lower part of a wall, white dust that appears again and again on the plaster, the smell of confinement in the closet. Moisture in the walls is one of the most frequent problems in homes in Latin America, from old houses in Lima or Buenos Aires to new apartments in Bogotá or Santiago.

The most common mistake is to always treat it the same: scrape it, apply an "anti-humidity" paint and hope it doesn't come back. But humidity has different origins and each one requires a different solution. If the cause is not identified, the stain returns, sometimes higher up or in another location. In this guide you will see how to distinguish the three main types of humidity, what simple tests you can do at home and what solutions work in each case.

## The three origins of humidity

| Type | where does it come from | Typical signs |
|---|---|---|
| Capillarity | the water from the soil rises through the pores of the wall | stain in the lower part, up to about a meter, saltpeter, crumbling plaster, worse with prolonged rains |
| Filtration | water enters from outside: roof, terrace, facade, windows or pipes | localized spots with marked edges, appear or grow after rain or near a pipe |
| Condensation | indoor air vapor turns into water on cold surfaces | black mold in corners, behind furniture, on ceilings and around windows, especially in cold weather |

The same home can have more than one type at a time, so it is a good idea to check carefully.

## Simple tests for diagnosis

### The aluminum foil test

1. Dry and clean an affected area of the wall.
2. Glue a square of aluminum foil about 30 centimeters, sealing the edges well with tape.
3. Wait a day or two.
4. Observe:
   - **Drops on the exterior face of the aluminum,** the one facing the room: there is **condensation**.
   - **Moisture on the side that touches the wall:** the humidity comes **from inside the wall**, due to capillarity or filtration.

### See when it appears

- **After each rain:** probably leakage.
- **In winter, in the morning, in closed bedrooms:** probably condensation.
- **Always in the lower part, with no clear relationship with the rain:** probably capillarity.

### Check the water meter

Turn off all the faucets in the house and watch the meter for a while. If it continues moving forward, there is a leak in a pipe, which may be wetting a wall from the inside.

### Use a hygrometer

A hygrometer measures the relative humidity of the air. Inside a home, values ​​between 40 and 60 percent are usually comfortable. If you spend many hours above 70 percent, condensation and mold are very likely.

## Capillary humidity

Brick, adobe, stone or block walls are porous. If they do not have a barrier that blocks the passage of water from the ground, the humidity rises like a wick. When it evaporates, it leaves salts on the surface: **saltpeter** or white efflorescence.

**What works:**

- **Reduce water around the house:** good drainage, slopes that keep rain away from the walls, gutters and downspouts that do not discharge next to the foundation. We see it in the guide to [rainwater, gutters and drainage](/articulos/agua-de-lluvia-canaletas-y-drenaje/).
- **Create a horizontal barrier** with injections of water-repellent products at the base of the wall, carried out by specialists.
- **Use breathable plasters,** such as lime plasters or special sanitation mortars, which allow moisture to escape and house salts.
- **Remove waterproof plasters and paints** that trap water.

**What doesn't work:** Cover the wall with waterproof paint or ceramic. The water does not disappear: it rises higher looking for somewhere to evaporate.

In earth houses, protection against soil moisture is even more important, as we explain in the guide on [adobe and earth construction](/articulos/adobe-y-construccion-con-tierra/).

## Moisture through filtration

Water enters from the outside through a weak point. The most common:

- **Roofs and terraces** with expired or cracked waterproofing.
- **Clogged gutters** that overflow over the wall.
- **Fissures in the façade** or open joints.
- **Poorly sealed windows** or sills without slope or drip.
- **Broken pipes** inside walls or slabs.
- **Planters or pots leaning against walls** without waterproofing.

**What works:** Find and repair the entry point. Once resolved, the wall must dry before repairing the plaster and painting, which can take weeks. On roofs, the [roof thermal insulation] (/articulos/aislamiento-termico-del-techo/) guide reminds that leaks must be resolved before insulating.

## Condensation humidity

The air in a home contains water vapor that we produce when breathing, cooking, showering, washing and drying clothes. When that air hits a cold surface, such as an uninsulated exterior wall, corner, or glass, the vapor turns into droplets. If this is repeated, mold appears.

It is very common in cold or temperate climates with humid winters, such as the highlands, southern Chile, Bogotá or the coast of Lima in winter, and anywhere with poorly ventilated bathrooms and bedrooms.

**What works:**

- **Ventilate daily,** even if it is cold, with short and intense periods. We explain it in the [cross ventilation](/articulos/ventilacion-cruzada/) guide.
- **Extract the steam where it is produced:** extractor in the bathroom and hood in the kitchen, with exit to the outside.
- **Do not dry clothes inside the house** without ventilation.
- **Avoid gas or kerosene stoves with no outlet to the outside:** in addition to the risk of carbon monoxide, they release a lot of water vapor into the environment.
- **Separate the furniture** a few centimeters from the exterior walls.
- **Insulate cold walls** and improve windows, especially in cold climates, as we explain in the guide on the [house for high altitude cold climate](/articulos/casa-para-clima-frio-de-altura/).
- **Use a dehumidifier** in rooms with persistent problems.

Painting with an anti-mold paint can help prevent stains from returning, but if the humidity in the air is not reduced, the mold will return.

## How to Clean Mold Safely

On small surfaces:

1. **Protect yourself** with gloves, mask and glasses.
2. **Ventilate** the room while you clean.
3. **Do not dry scrape it,** because you spread spores.
4. **Use a specific mold cleaner** following the instructions, without mixing it with other products.
5. **Dry the surface well**.

If the mold covers a large surface, returns again and again, or there are people with asthma, allergies, or respiratory problems at home, it is advisable to turn to professionals. ## Repair after drying

Once the cause has been eliminated:

1. **Let the wall dry** as long as necessary.
2. **Remove damaged plaster** and salts with a dry brush.
3. **Repair with compatible,** breathable materials on walls with a history of humidity.
4. **Paint with appropriate paints** that allow the wall to breathe. The [interior and exterior paints](/articulos/pinturas-interior-y-exterior/) guide helps you choose.

## Prevention is cheaper

- **Check roofs, terraces and gutters** before each rainy season.
- **Keep drains clear** on patios and balconies.
- **Ventilate every day** bedrooms, bathrooms and kitchen, also in winter.
- **Look at the base of the walls** and cold corners once a month, especially behind furniture and curtains.
- **Repair soon** any cracks in facades, window seals and shower seals.

A small stain detected in time can be resolved with little money. The same stain ignored for years can end up in new plaster, lifted floors and damaged furniture.

## If you rent

Inform the owner in writing, with photos and dates. Leaks and capillarity are usually the responsibility of whoever owns the property. Condensation depends in part on usage habits, so it's a good idea to ventilate and document what you do.

## Frequent errors

- **Paint over** without looking for the cause.
- **Use waterproof paints** on walls with capillarity.
- **Dry clothes inside** without ventilating.
- **Heaters without exit to the outside** in closed bedrooms.
- **Furniture glued** to cold walls.
- **Dry scrape mold** without protection.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is saltpeter dangerous?

Saltpeter itself is not toxic, but it indicates that water passes through the wall, which over time deteriorates plasters, paints and, in some cases, the wall material itself.

### Does a dehumidifier solve humidity?

It helps in cases of condensation, because it reduces the vapor in the air. It does not solve leaks or capillarity, which require cutting off water entry.

### How long does it take for a wall to dry?

It depends on the thickness, the material and the climate. A thick brick or adobe wall may need weeks or months to resolve the cause.

## In summary

Moisture in walls has three main origins: capillarity from the floor, leaks from the outside and condensation of interior steam. Identify it by its location, its relationship with rain, and the aluminum foil test. Capillarity is combated with drainage, barriers and breathable plasters; leaks, repairing the entry point; condensation, ventilating, extracting steam and insulating cold walls. Never paint over it without resolving the cause, clean the mold with protection and let the wall dry before repairing.
