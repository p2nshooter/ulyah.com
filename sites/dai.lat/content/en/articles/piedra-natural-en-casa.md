Stone is one of the most noble and durable materials in Latin American architecture. The pink quarry of Zacatecas, the green quarry of Oaxaca, the red tezontle of the colonial buildings of Mexico City, the white ashlar of Arequipa or the coral stone of Cartagena and Havana are part of the identity of entire cities. Today, in houses, stone appears in floors, kitchen countertops, bathrooms, facades, stairs and patios.

But not all stones behave the same. Some are hard and almost waterproof; Others are soft, porous, or sensitive to acids, and will stain with a little wine or dull with the wrong cleaner. Choosing well and taking care of it correctly is what separates a floor that improves over the years from one that looks worn out in a short time. In this guide you will see the most used types of stone, their properties, finishes, how to seal it, clean it and remove stains.

## The most common stones

| stone | Origin | Features | Common uses |
|---|---|---|---|
| Granite | igneous | very hard, not very porous, resistant to heat and scratching | kitchen countertops, high-traffic floors, stairs |
| Marble | calcareous metamorphic | elegant, softer, sensitive to acids and stains | floors and bathrooms with little intense use, coatings |
| Travertine | calcareous sedimentary | porous, with cavities that are usually filled | floors, coverings, exteriors in frost-free climates |
| Quartzite | metamorphic | very hard, sometimes confused with marble | covers, floors |
| Slate and flagstones | metamorphic or sedimentary layers | natural texture, good grip | exterior floors, walls, traditional roofs |
| Quarry | volcanic tuff | soft, porous, easy to carve | facades, columns, frames, moderate traffic floors |
| volcanic stone | basalts, tezontle and others | resistant, sometimes porous and light | walls, exterior floors, gardens |
| coral stone | sedimentary of marine origin | porous, with visible fossils | facades and walls in the Caribbean |

Trade names can be confusing: a catalog "granite" may be another rock, and some stones sold as "quartzite" are actually marbles. Always ask for the technical sheet.

## The vinegar test: is it chalky?

Calcareous stones, such as marble, travertine or limestone, react with acids. A home test consists of dropping **a drop of vinegar on a hidden area or cutout**. If it bubbles or leaves a dull mark, the stone is calcareous and should be protected from acids. Remember that the test marks the surface, so never do it in a visible area.

## Finishes: the surface changes everything

- **Polished:** shiny, enhances color and grain. It is slippery with water and shows more acid marks on calcareous stones.
- **Honeycombed or matte:** smooth but without shine. Better hides wear.
- **Flamed:** rough surface obtained with fire, used in non-slip outdoor granites.
- **Bush-hammered or sandblasted:** rough texture, ideal for outdoor stairs and pool edges.
- **Natural or raw:** the face as the stone is separated, typical of slabs.

Outdoors, bathrooms and stairs, prefer textured finishes. We explain it in the guide to [non-slip exterior floors](/articulos/pisos-exteriores-antiderrapantes/).

## What stone to choose for each location?

- **Kitchen countertop:** granite or quartzite, due to its resistance to heat, scratches and stains. The [kitchen countertops](/articulos/cubiertas-de-cocina-materiales/) guide compares them to other materials.
- **High traffic floors:** granite or hard stones with a matte finish.
- **Bathrooms:** marble or travertine, sealed and cared for with acidic cleaners, or more resistant stones in showers with intense use.
- **Facades and patios:** quarries, flagstones, volcanic stones, coral stones or granites with texture, depending on the climate.
- **Zones with frost:** stones with low absorption, which do not crack when the water freezes in their pores.

## Sealing: invisible protection

Many stones have pores that absorb liquids. An impregnating sealant penetrates those pores and reduces the absorption of water and oils without changing the appearance too much.

- **Check if it needs sealing:** Leave a few drops of water on the stone. If they darken the surface in a few minutes, the stone absorbs and should be sealed.
- **Apply the sealer** to clean, dry stone, following the manufacturer's instructions.
- **Renew the seal** when water is easily absorbed again.
- **On porous stones,** sealing before filling the joints prevents the joint material from staining the stone.

Sealing does not make the stone immune to acids: sealed marble is still sensitive to lemon or vinegar.

## Daily cleaning

- **Use neutral cleaners** specific for stone or water with a mild soap.
- **Avoid vinegar, lemon, anti-scale and acidic cleaners** on calcareous stones.
- **Do not use abrasive powders** on polished stones.
- **Sweep or vacuum the sand** frequently: it acts like sandpaper on the floors.
- **Dry up spills** immediately, especially wine, coffee, oil and juices.

## How to remove stains

| stain | Usual method |
|---|---|
| Grease or oil | poultice of an absorbent powder with a suitable solvent, which is allowed to act and is removed |
| Coffee, wine or tea | specific cleaners for stone, following the manufacturer's instructions |
| Rust | specific products for rust on stone, testing first in a hidden area |
| Dull acid marks on marble | professional polishing |
| White efflorescence | dry brushing and humidity control |

A **poultice** is a paste that is placed on the stain, covered with plastic and allowed to dry: as it dries, it absorbs the substance from the pores of the stone. On valuable stones or difficult stains, consult a marble expert before experimenting.

## Installation

- **Firm and flat base,** with adhesives suitable for natural stone. Some light stones stain with gray adhesives and require white adhesives, and certain marbles require special products.
- **Joints** depending on the type of piece and location, with movement joints on large surfaces.
- **Outdoors,** slope and drainage so that water does not remain on the surface or under the pieces.

## Stone in old houses: a heritage that is cared for differently

In old houses and historic centers, stone has resisted centuries because it was built with lime mortars and compatible techniques. When restoring it, it is advisable to respect some rules:

- **Do not use sandblasting or high pressure washers** on quarries and soft stones: they erode the surface and open the pores.
- **Repair the joints with lime mortars,** more flexible and breathable than cement mortars, which can damage the stone around them.
- **Avoid waterproof sealants** that prevent water evaporation on thick walls.
- **Check the protection regulations** if the house is in a historic center: many interventions require authorization.

## Stone and budget

The final price depends not only on the stone, but also on the thickness, the finish, the waste in cuts, transportation and specialized labor. Stones extracted near your city are usually cheaper, have less transport impact and combine naturally with the architecture of the place. Ask for large samples and, if possible, visit a finished work with the same stone before deciding.

## Stone and humidity

Porous stones can absorb moisture from the ground and show efflorescence or stains. For walls and floors above ground, a good moisture barrier and adequate drainage are essential. The guide on [damp in walls](/articulos/humedad-en-paredes-causas-y-soluciones/) explains how to identify the source.

## Frequent errors

- **Clean marble with vinegar or lemon.**
- **Polished stone in showers, stairs or outdoors.**
- **Do not seal porous stones** in kitchens and bathrooms.
- **Abrasive powders** on polished stones.
- **Choose from the photo** without asking for the technical sheet.
- **Ignore the sand** that scratches the floors.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is marble suitable for the kitchen?

It can be used if you accept that it will show acid marks and some stains over time. For a heavy-duty kitchen, granite or quartzite are typically quieter options.

### How often is the stone sealed?

It depends on the stone, the sealer and the use. The water bead test is the best guide: when water is easily absorbed, it's time to reseal.

### Can I put natural stone in the shower?

Yes, with some conditions: a textured finish on the floor, proper sealing, full waterproofing underneath, and neutral cleaners. Avoid acidic anti-scale agents if the stone is calcareous and dry the surface after use to limit water marks.

### Is natural stone cold to live in?

It feels cold to the touch because it conducts body heat well. In hot climates it is an advantage; In cold climates, it should be combined with carpets in living areas and, if possible, with underfloor insulation or underfloor heating.

### Can you restore the shine of a marble floor?

Yes, with a professional polishing that removes a very thin layer from the surface. It is a job for specialists.

## In summary

Granite and quartzite are hard and resistant; Marble, travertine and limestone are calcareous and sensitive to acids; quarries, flagstones, volcanic and coral stones provide regional character and are usually porous. Choose the stone and finish according to the use, with non-slip textures on exteriors, bathrooms and stairs. Seal porous stones and renew the sealing when water is absorbed again, clean with neutral products, avoid acids and abrasives and treat stains with appropriate methods or with professional help.
