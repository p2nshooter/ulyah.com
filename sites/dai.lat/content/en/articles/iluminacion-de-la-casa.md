In many houses, lighting is solved with a spotlight in the center of the ceiling of each room. It illuminates, yes, but in a flat way: it leaves shadows right where you work, dazzles when you look up and turns a cozy room into something similar to an office at night. With the same square footage and a little planning, light can make a space look larger, warmer and much more comfortable.

Furthermore, the arrival of LED technology changed the rules. You no longer buy a light bulb for its watts, but for its lumens, its color temperature and its light quality. In this guide you will see how to think about lighting in layers, how much light each environment needs, how to choose LED spotlights without making mistakes and what mistakes should be avoided.

## Three layers of light

Good lighting combines several types of light that fulfill different functions:

- **General or ambient light:** Illuminates the space evenly to move safely. It can come from ceilings, panels, tracks or recessed lighting.
- **Work Light:** Focuses light where a task is performed, such as the kitchen counter, desk, bathroom mirror, or reading chair.
- **Accent or ambient light:** Highlight a painting, a plant, a wall texture or create a warm atmosphere with low lamps.

When all three layers are present and can be turned on separately, each room adapts to different times of the day.

## Watts, lumens and equivalences

Watts indicate how much energy a light bulb consumes. **Lumens** indicate how much light it emits. With LED technology, a bulb consumes much less than an incandescent bulb to give the same light.

| Old incandescent light bulb | Approximate light | Approximate equivalent LED |
|---|---|---|
| 40W | about 450 lumens | 5 to 6W |
| 60W | about 800 lumens | 8 to 10W |
| 75W | about 1100 lumens | 11 to 13W |
| 100W | about 1500 lumens | 14 to 17W |

The exact values depend on each manufacturer. What you should look at on the packaging are the lumens.

## How much light does each room need?

The amount of light that reaches a surface is measured in **lux**. These figures are indicative for a home:

| Environment or task | indicative level |
|---|---|
| Hallways, stairs | 100 to 150 lux |
| Living room, general light | 100 to 300 lux |
| Dining room, on the table | 200 to 300 lux |
| kitchen cover | 300 to 500 lux |
| reading, desk | 300 to 500 lux |
| bathroom mirror | 300 lux or more on the face |

It is not necessary to measure with a lux meter. It serves as a guide to understand that work areas need much more light than the living room or hallway. Older people also need more light to see with the same clarity.

## color temperature

Color temperature, expressed in kelvin, describes whether light appears warm or cool.

- **2700 to 3000 K, warm light:** Cozy, ideal for living rooms, bedrooms and dining rooms at night.
- **3500 to 4000 K, neutral light:** good for kitchens, bathrooms, laundry rooms and study areas.
- **5000 to 6500 K, cold light or daylight:** very clear and bluish. It is common in many houses in the region because it is perceived as "brighter", but at night it can be cold and uncomfortable.

Two practical tips: do not mix very different temperatures in the same room, and at night prefer warm, dimmer lights, which promote rest. Color temperature also changes how walls look, as we explain in the [interior colors](/articulos/colores-para-interiores/) guide.

## Light quality: the CRI

The **color rendering index**, or CRI, indicates how faithfully colors appear in that light, on a scale of 0 to 100. A CRI of 80 is acceptable for most spaces. In the kitchen, bathroom, dressing room or where there are works of art, **90 or more** is recommended, to see food, skin and the colors of clothing well.

## Environment by environment

### Room

Avoid depending on a single central focus. Combine soft general lighting with floor or table lamps at different heights and some accent lighting. A lamp next to the reading chair is more useful than a very powerful ceiling light. More distribution ideas in the guide about [the room: measurements and distribution] (/articulos/sala-medidas-y-distribucion/).

### Dining room

A hanging lamp above the table focuses attention and creates atmosphere. As a reference, it is usually hung about 75 to 90 centimeters above the table, to illuminate those who are sitting without dazzling. An intensity regulator allows you to go from family dinner to a quieter after-dinner meal.

### Kitchen

The overhead light leaves the deck in shadow when you bend down to work. A **light under tall furniture**, with LED strips or strips, illuminates the work surface directly. Complete with neutral general lighting and, if there is an island, hanging lamps. We see it in the [kitchen layout](/articulos/distribucion-de-la-cocina/) guide.

### Bathroom

A light only above the head produces shadows on the face. The best lighting for the mirror comes **from the front or from the sides**, at face level. Use luminaires suitable for humid environments and a neutral color temperature with good CRI.

### Bedroom

Warm and dim general lighting, night lamps on both sides of the bed and, if there is a closet, an interior or nearby light to see the clothes. Avoid spotlights that are right above your eyes when you are lying down.

### Hallways, stairs and entrance

They need enough light to circulate safely. Motion sensors are practical in hallways and entrances. On stairs, low lights on the wall or on the steps improve visibility without dazzling.

## Avoid glare

- **Do not leave bare bulbs** in the line of sight. Use screens, diffusers or recessed lighting with good protection.
- **Direct light toward surfaces,** not toward eyes.
- **Combines direct and indirect light.** Light that bounces off a light ceiling or wall is softer.
- **Choose matte finishes** on surfaces close to strong lights to avoid reflections.

## Intensity regulators

A regulator or dimmer allows you to adapt the light to each moment. Before installing it, check that the LED bulbs are **dimmable**, because not all of them are. A non-dimmable LED connected to a dimmer may flicker, hum, or become damaged.

## Take advantage of natural light first

Daylight is the best and cheapest:

- **Keep windows clear** and use curtains that can be pulled back completely.
- **Paint ceilings and walls with light colors** in dark rooms.
- **Use mirrors** in front of or next to windows to distribute light.
- **Control direct sun** with blinds or awnings to avoid glare and heat.

In small apartments, well-used natural light makes the space appear larger. We see it in the guide to [gain space in a small apartment](/articulos/departamento-pequeno-ganar-espacio/).

## Outdoor lighting

On patios, balconies and gardens, choose fixtures with water and dust protection appropriate for their location. Warm lights tend to attract fewer insects than cold lights. Illuminate paths, steps and the entrance for added safety, and point the lights downwards so as not to disturb neighbors.

## Savings and security

- **LEDs consume much less** than incandescent bulbs and last many years.
- **Turn off what you don't use** and take advantage of sensors in transit areas.
- **Electrical installations,** new circuits or changes to recessed luminaires must be carried out by qualified electricians, following local regulations.

More ideas to reduce consumption in the [energy saving at home](/articulos/ahorro-de-energia-en-casa/) guide.

## Frequent errors

- **A single central spotlight** per room.
- **Buy by watts** rather than lumens.
- **Mix cold and warm light** in the same environment.
- **Light the mirror only from above.**
- **Connect non-dimmable LEDs** to a dimmer.
- **Leave naked spotlights** in plain sight that dazzle.

## Frequently asked questions

### White light or warm light for the house?

Warm light in living rooms, dining rooms and bedrooms, and neutral light in kitchen, bathroom and work areas. Very cold light is useful in workshops or garages, but is often uncomfortable in rest areas at night.

### How many lights do I need in a room?

It depends on the size, the color of the walls and the use. Rather than counting spotlights, think in layers: sufficient general light, work light where necessary and one or two ambient sources.

### Do LED strips serve as main light?

They can serve as indirect light on ceilings or furniture, but are normally combined with other luminaires. Under kitchen cabinets they are excellent as a work light.

## In summary

Illuminate each room in layers: general light, task light and accent light, turned on separately. Buy bulbs by lumens, choose warm light for resting and neutral light for working, and look for a CRI of 90 or higher where colors matter. Illuminate the kitchen countertop from below the wall units and the bathroom mirror from the front or sides. Avoid glare with diffusers and indirect light, check that the LEDs are dimmable before using a dimmer and make the most of natural light.
