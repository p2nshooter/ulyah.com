In cities, the balcony or terrace is often the only outdoor space in the home. There you drink your morning coffee, grow plants, hang clothes, gather your family or watch the city at sunset. That is why it is advisable to take care of it like any room in the house, and with an important difference: it is a space in height, exposed to the weather, that supports cantilevered weight and from which people or objects can fall.

Most balcony risks can be prevented with simple measures: an adequate railing, not overloading it, keeping drains clean, securing pots and furniture and keeping an eye out for signs of deterioration. In this guide you will see what to check, what rules usually apply, how to live with children and pets and how to take advantage of the space with peace of mind.

## The railing: the first protection

Balcony and terrace railings are regulated in almost all building regulations. Although the exact figures change depending on the country and the height of the fall, there are common criteria:

| Appearance | Usual criterion |
|---|---|
| Height | between about 90 centimeters and 1.10 meters, higher the greater the fall height |
| Separation between bars | that a sphere of about 10 centimeters cannot pass, so that a child's head does not fit |
| Design | without horizontal elements that serve as stairs |
| Resistance | capable of withstanding the push of supported people |
| glass | safety, laminated or tempered according to standard |

If your balcony has a low railing, with widely spaced bars or easy-to-climb horizontal crossbars, talk to building management or a professional about how to improve it. Sheer mesh or additional panels may be temporary solutions in the meantime.

## Be careful what can be scaled

One of the most serious risks for children is climbing on the railing **by standing on something**: a chair, a large pot, a table, a box or a planter next to the edge.

- **Do not place furniture or pots next to the railing.**
- **Do not leave small children alone** on the balcony.
- **Keep the balcony door closed** with a latch out of reach if there are small children in the home.
- **Install protective nets or meshes** if you have cats or children playing nearby.

## How much weight can a balcony hold?

A balcony is designed to support the weight of people and normal use, not any load. Some things weigh much more than they seem:

- **Large pots with wet soil:** a one-meter planter can weigh more than one hundred kilos with the substrate saturated.
- **Jacuzzis, inflatable pools or water tanks:** water weighs about a ton per cubic meter.
- **Many people at once** on a small balcony during a party.
- **Floors or coverings added** over the original.

Before installing anything heavy, consult an engineer or building management. For plants, use light substrates and pots made of light materials.

## Warning signs

Balconies, especially cantilevered concrete ones, can deteriorate when water reaches the reinforcing steel. Pay attention to:

- **Cracks** in the slab, especially at the bottom or where it meets the building.
- **Rust stains** or pieces of concrete that come off and reveal the steel.
- **A balcony that tilts** or appears to have been lowered on its edge.
- **Loose railings,** rusty at the base or with loose anchors.
- **Stagnant water** that does not drain.
- **Loose ceramics** or those that sound hollow.

If you see any of these signs, stop loading the balcony and ask for a professional evaluation. The [exposed concrete](/articulos/concreto-aparente-cuidados/) guide explains why rust in concrete is an important warning.

## Water: slope, drainage and waterproofing

- **The floor must slope** towards the outside or towards the drain, never towards the door of the house.
- **Drains and gargoyles** should be clean of leaves, dirt and trash, especially before rains.
- **Waterproofing** under the floor protects the slab and the home below. If moisture spots appear on your neighbor's ceiling, there may be a fault.
- **When watering the plants,** prevent water from dripping onto the lower balconies or the street.

More information in the guide on [rainwater, gutters and drainage](/articulos/agua-de-lluvia-canaletas-y-drenaje/) and in the guide on [non-slip exterior floors](/articulos/pisos-exteriores-antiderrapantes/).

## Wind: let nothing fall

An object falling from a balcony can cause serious injuries to anyone passing below. Therefore:

- **Secure hanging and railing pots** with firm supports, or place them on the floor.
- **Remove or tie down** umbrellas, awnings, cushions and light furniture when there is strong wind.
- **Do not leave objects on the railing,** such as glasses or small pots.
- **In hurricane or storm areas,** remove everything loose when there is a warning.

## Grills, fire and gas

Grilling on the balcony is part of social life in many cities, but it carries risks:

- **Check building regulations:** many limit or prohibit charcoal grills on balconies.
- **Keep grill away from awnings, dry plants, furniture and curtains.**
- **Gas cylinders** must be upright, protected from the sun and with connections in good condition.
- **Keep a fire extinguisher handy** and never leave a fire unattended.

## Enclosures, awnings and façade

Closing a balcony with glass, installing an awning or changing the railing modifies the façade of the building, which is normally a common asset. In many places you need community authorization and sometimes a municipal permit. In addition, closing a balcony completely can affect the ventilation of the home. To protect you from the sun, vertical awnings or exterior curtains are usually simpler options, as we explain in the [pergolas, awnings and shade] guide (/articulos/pergolas-toldos-y-sombra/).

## Pets on the balcony

- **Cats** can fall chasing an insect or bird: install protective nets.
- **Dogs** should not spend hours in the sun on a balcony without shade or water.
- **Rails with separate bars** may be a risk for small pets.

## Plants on the balcony

A balcony with plants is cooler and more pleasant, but requires some precautions:

- **Choose species according to the light and wind** of your balcony. On high floors the wind dries out the leaves and knocks down tall plants; Small-leaved species and succulents tend to resist better.
- **Use light pots and light substrates,** which reduce the load on the slab.
- **Place plates under the pots,** but empty them after watering: stagnant water breeds mosquitoes and can drip onto neighbors.
- **Simple drip irrigation** saves water and prevents spills.

The guide to [indoor plants according to light](/articulos/plantas-de-interior-segun-la-luz/) helps to choose species according to orientation.

## Annual Review: A Quick Checklist

Once a year, ideally before the rainy or windy season:

1. Firmly move the handrail to check that it is not loose.
2. Check the underside of the slab for cracks, rust, or spalling.
3. Clean drains and gargoyles.
4. Check that the water does not stagnate after watering or raining.
5. Check the joints of the floor and where they meet the façade.
6. Check pot supports, awnings and protective nets.

## Take advantage of the balcony with peace of mind

A secure balcony can become a small garden or outdoor room. Folding furniture, light potted plants, warm lighting and some shade are enough to transform it. The guide to [gain space in a small apartment](/articulos/departamento-pequeno-ganar-espacio/) includes ideas for using it as another room.

## Frequent errors

- **Pots and furniture next to the railing** that allow climbing.
- **Heavy planters** or hot tubs without consulting the structure.
- **Drains clogged** with leaves and dirt.
- **Loose objects** on windy days.
- **Ignore cracks and rust stains** on the slab.
- **Close the balcony** without permission or ventilation.

## Frequently asked questions

### How high should my balcony railing be?

It depends on the standard of your country and the drop height. As a reference, many standards ask for between 90 centimeters and 1.10 meters. Consult local regulations or building management.

### Can I put an inflatable pool on the terrace?

Only if an engineer confirms that the structure supports the weight of the water. Even a small pool can weigh several tons.

### Who should repair a deteriorated balcony in a building?

It depends on the building regulations and the law of each country. In many cases, the structure and façade are the responsibility of the community. Inform management as soon as you see signs of deterioration.

## In summary

A safe balcony has a railing of sufficient height, without large gaps or elements that can be climbed, and nothing next to it that serves as a step. Don't overload it with heavy planters, tanks or hot tubs without consulting an engineer, and address any cracks, rust or leaning. Keep drains clear and slope outward, secure anything that can be blown by the wind, follow rules about grills and enclosures, and protect children and pets.
