A patio or terrace without shade is rarely used. In the hot climates of Latin America, the midday sun turns any open space into a place where no one wants to sit, and the warm floor returns heat back into the house into the evening. With shade, on the other hand, that same space becomes just another room: for eating, working, reading or meeting.

There are many ways to provide shade, from a palm or reed bower to an aluminum pergola with adjustable slats. Each one behaves differently against the sun, rain, wind and the passage of time. In this guide you will see how to choose the type of shade according to orientation and use, what materials are suitable for each climate, what errors produce hot or unsafe spaces, and how to maintain each system.

## First, observe the sun

Before building, observe for a few days where the sun shines and what times you will use the space.

- **Midday sun:** reaches high. It blocks well with horizontal elements, such as a pergola, awning or a tensioned sail.
- **Morning and afternoon sun:** arrives low and sideways, from the east or west. A horizontal deck doesn't stop you. Vertical elements are needed, such as vertical awnings, lattices, exterior curtains or plants.
- **Changes in the year:** outside the tropics, the winter sun is lower and sometimes it is better to let it pass. Near the equator, the sun passes high all year round and horizontal roofs perform a lot.

## Pergolas

A pergola is a structure of posts and beams that can be left open, covered with slats or used as a support for plants or fabrics.

- **Wood:** warm and easy to work with. It must be treated against humidity, fungi and insects such as termites, and protected with oils or exterior varnishes that are periodically renewed. We see it in the guide on [wood at home] (/articulos/madera-en-casa-especies-y-cuidados/).
- **Metal:** galvanized or electrostatically painted steel, or aluminum. It requires less maintenance than wood, but near the sea salinity accelerates corrosion, so it is advisable to choose materials and coatings for marine environments.
- **Concrete:** very durable, although heavy and less flexible if you want to change the design.

**Orientation of the slats:** in a pergola with fixed slats, the spacing, height and orientation of the pieces determine how much sun passes and at what time. Tall, closely spaced slats provide more shade. Oriented east-west, they block the midday sun better.

**Height:** A pergola around 8 to 10 feet tall is usually comfortable. If it is very low it feels oppressive; If it is very high, the morning and afternoon sun enters through the sides.

## Creepers: living shadow

A pergola covered with plants provides one of the most pleasant shades, because the leaves do not get hot like a fabric or sheet and they also breathe. Some considerations:

- **Choose species appropriate to your climate** and the weight that the structure can support. Some woody climbers become very heavy over the years.
- **In climates with winter**, deciduous leaves, such as grapevines, provide shade in summer and let the sun shine in winter.
- **In tropical climates**, there are fast-growing climbers that cover a pergola in a few months. Prefer native or well-adapted species, as we suggest in the guide on [garden with native plants](/articulos/jardin-con-plantas-nativas/).
- **Be aware of thorns, falling flowers, fruit and bees** if the space is used for eating or if there are children.

## Awnings and shade nets

**Retractable awnings:** they open when necessary and are retracted in wind or heavy rain. Acrylic outdoor fabrics maintain their color better in the sun. An awning that accumulates water can deform or break its mechanism, so it must be sloped and retracted during storms.

**Vertical awnings and exterior curtains:** are the best protection for low morning and afternoon sun on balconies and terraces.

**Shade nets:** known in different countries as shade nets, saran or polishade, they are sold with different percentages of shade. They are air permeable and let the heat that accumulates underneath escape, unlike waterproof fabrics, which can trap it if the space is closed on the sides.

## shade sails

Sails or velarias are fabrics stretched between three or four anchor points. They are light, economical and very decorative, but they have requirements:

- **Very firm anchors:** the wind generates great forces on the fabric. The mooring points should go to well-founded posts or structures capable of resisting them, never to a gutter or a weak wall.
- **Tension and pitch:** A loose sail flaps and wears out. A slight inclination prevents water from accumulating.
- **Remove them before strong storms:** in areas with hurricanes or intense winds, it is advisable to dismantle them when there is a weather alert.

## Light roofs: be careful with the heat

Covering a terrace with polycarbonate or sheeting protects against rain, but can create a very hot space:

- **Transparent polycarbonate** allows light and heat to pass through, with an effect similar to that of a greenhouse. Opaline or sun protection versions transmit less heat.
- **Uninsulated sheet metal** gets very hot in the sun, radiates heat downwards, and is noisy in the rain.
- **Leave air vents** at the top and avoid closing all sides.
- **Predict the fall of water:** sufficient slope, gutters and downspouts that do not discharge on the wall of the house or on the neighbor. We explain it in the guide on [rainwater, gutters and drainage](/articulos/agua-de-lluvia-canaletas-y-drenaje/).

## Traditional materials

Palm, reed, cane or bamboo bowers, such as the palapas on the Mexican coast or the guadua structures in Colombia and Ecuador, offer cool, ventilated shade. They require periodic renovation and protection against insects and humidity, and in some cases special care against fire. Well built, they are one of the most pleasant solutions for hot weather.

## Security and permissions

- **Appropriate foundations and anchors** for posts and structures, especially in areas with strong winds or seismic activity.
- **Distance from grills and stoves** to fabrics, palms and light roofs.
- **Permits:** in buildings, condominiums and some municipalities, pergolas and roofs on terraces or facades require authorization. Consult before building. On balconies, also check the allowable weight, as we explain in the guide on [safe balconies and terraces](/articulos/balcones-y-terrazas-seguras/).

## Seasonal maintenance

- **Before the rainy season:** check slopes, gutters, sail tension and awning mechanisms, and remove accumulated leaves.
- **After storms:** check anchors, screws and seams. A weak point today is a break in the next storm.
- **Once or twice a year:** wash the fabrics with water and neutral soap, let them dry flat and store them dry if you remove them.
- **Every few years:** Renew the oil or varnish on the wood and touch up the paint on the metal where bumps or rust appear.

## Frequent errors

- **Use only horizontal shade** on terraces that receive afternoon sun.
- **Closing a terrace with transparent polycarbonate** without ventilation.
- **Anchor shadow sails to weak elements.**
- **Leave awnings extended** in strong wind or heavy rain.
- **Choose vines that are too heavy** for the structure.
- **Use untreated wood** outdoors.

## Frequently asked questions

### What percentage of shade mesh is appropriate?

For spaces where people will be in very sunny climates, high percentage meshes are usually used. For plants, it depends on the species: many need some direct sun. Try a small portion first if you're not sure.

### Does a dark awning give more shade than a light one?

A dark fabric blocks more light, but gets hotter. A light fabric reflects more radiation and remains cooler, although it allows a little more light to pass through. In very hot climates, mid tones are usually a good balance.

### Can I put a pergola on the balcony of an apartment?

It depends on the building regulations, local regulations and the structure of the balcony. A simpler and more reversible solution is vertical awnings or exterior curtains.

## In summary

Choose the shade according to the sun you want to block: horizontal elements for the midday sun and vertical elements for the morning and afternoon sun. Pergolas with slats or vines provide cool and ventilated shade, awnings and meshes are flexible and sails are light but require very firm anchors. Avoid lightweight, unventilated roofs, protect wood and metal depending on the weather, gather fabric in strong winds, and check permits before building.
