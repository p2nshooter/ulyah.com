Earth is one of the oldest building materials in Latin America. Chan Chan, on the northern coast of Peru, was a large adobe city. Paquimé, in northern Mexico, built its buildings with earthen walls. And still today millions of people live in adobe, rammed earth or bahareque houses in the Andes, in the highlands, in northern Mexico, in Central America and in rural areas of almost all countries.

Earth construction has virtues that modern architecture is rediscovering: it is cool in the heat, temperate in the cold, cheap and has low environmental impact. But it also has a serious weak point in a seismic continent: if it is not well designed and reinforced, it can collapse during an earthquake. In this guide you will see the main techniques, their advantages, their risks, how to protect them from humidity and what to check before buying, renovating or building a dirt house.

## The main techniques

| Technique | How to build | Where is traditional |
|---|---|---|
| Adobe | sun-dried mud and straw blocks, held together with clay mortar | Andes, highlands, northern Mexico, arid areas |
| Tapial or stepped wall | moist soil compacted in layers within a formwork | Colombia, Andes, rural areas of several countries |
| Bahareque or quincha | wooden or cane structure filled and plastered with mud | Colombia, Venezuela, Central America, coast of Peru and Chile |
| Compressed earth block | earth, sometimes stabilized with lime or cement, pressed by machine | current projects throughout the region |

Each technique has its own behavior. The **bahareque or quincha**, due to its wood or cane structure, is lighter and more flexible and has shown better behavior against earthquakes than solid adobe, as long as the wood is healthy. The **adobe** and the **tapial** form thick and heavy walls.

## Why earth houses are comfortable

- **Thermal mass:** Thick earthen walls absorb heat during the day and slowly release it at night. In climates with large differences between day and night, such as the highlands or deserts, this keeps the interior stable.
- **Humidity Regulation:** Soil absorbs and releases water vapor, which helps moderate indoor humidity.
- **Good acoustic insulation** thanks to its mass.
- **Low environmental impact:** local material is used, without cooking, and can return to the earth at the end of its useful life.
- **Does not burn:** The soil itself is not combustible, although wooden structures and roofs are.

In hot, humid climates, thermal mass helps less, because the nights are also hot. There the bahareque, lighter, ventilated and protected by large eaves, is usually the traditional option.

## The great risk: earthquakes

Earthen walls are heavy and fragile under lateral forces. During an earthquake they can crack, separate at the corners, and fall outward or inward, dragging the roof with it. Strong earthquakes in Peru, Chile, Mexico and Central America have shown time and again that unreinforced adobe homes are among the most vulnerable.

This does not mean that the land should be abandoned, but that **it should be built with earthquake-resistant criteria**. Some countries have specific rules. In Peru, for example, the technical standard for design and construction with reinforced earth establishes reinforcement requirements, such as rope meshes or geogrids embedded in the plaster, tie beams, and limits on the height of the walls and the size of the openings.

General principles of a safer earth house:

- **A single story,** or at most two with a specific structural design.
- **Simple and symmetrical plant,** without long wings or irregular shapes.
- **Thick, braced walls,** with transverse walls or buttresses at limited distances.
- **Small openings and away from the corners.**
- **A collar or slab beam** continues at the top of all the walls, made of wood or reinforced concrete, that joins them together.
- **Lightweight and well anchored roof** to the collar beam.
- **Reinforcements** such as mesh, reeds or geogrids according to local standards.

The design, evaluation and reinforcement of an earthen house should be carried out by an engineer or architect with experience in this type of construction.

## Boots and hat: protection against water

Raw soil loses resistance when it becomes wet. That's why it is said that a dirt house needs **good boots and a good hat**:

- **Boots:** a foundation and a stone or concrete overlay that elevate the wall above the ground, so that humidity does not rise and splashing rain does not erode the base.
- **Hat:** wide eaves that protect the walls from rain.

Added to this are good drainage around the house, gutters and downspouts that keep water away from the walls, and quick repair of any leaks.

## Plasters: earth or lime, never cement

Earthen walls must be able to "breathe", that is, let moisture out. Suitable plasters are:

- **Mud plasters** with sand and fibers, periodically renewed.
- **Lime plasters,** more water resistant and breathable.
- **Lime or earth paints,** compatible with the wall.

A **cement plaster** on adobe is a common mistake. It is rigid and impermeable to vapor: it traps moisture inside the wall, which weakens behind without being seen, and ends up detaching in plates. If your house already has cement on land and bulges or detachments appear, consult a specialist. ## Health: cracks and vinchucas

In rural areas of several countries in the region, the insects that transmit Chagas disease, known as vinchucas, chinches or chipos depending on the location, hide in cracks in unplastered earth walls and in thatched roofs. Keeping the walls plastered, smooth and without cracks, tidying up the surroundings of the house and following health services campaigns greatly reduces the risk.

## Maintenance of a dirt house

- **Check the plasters** every year and repair eroded or cracked areas with the same type of material.
- **Controls humidity** at the base of the walls and on the ceiling.
- **Keep an eye on the wood** of beams, lintels and bahareque structures: humidity and termites weaken it. We see it in the guide on [wood at home] (/articulos/madera-en-casa-especies-y-cuidados/).
- **Look at the cracks.** The fine and superficial ones are usually in the plaster; Those that go through the wall, widen or appear in corners require a professional evaluation.
- **Keep roofs** in good condition, because a leak can dissolve an earthen wall.

## The earth in current architecture

Architects and builders from all over the region today work with earth in a contemporary way: stabilized rammed earth, compressed earth blocks, bahareque with treated wooden or bamboo structures and lime plasters. These techniques make it possible to take advantage of the virtues of the earth with greater quality control and better structural behavior, always within what the regulations of each country allow.

## If you are going to buy or renovate a dirt house

1. **Have the structure evaluated** by a professional with experience in earthen construction.
2. **Check for cracks, moisture in the base and condition of the roof.**
3. **Find out if there are cement plasters** that hide damage.
4. **Ask about local regulations** on reinforcements and expansions.
5. **Do not add a brick or concrete second story** on top of earthen walls without a structural study.

## Frequent errors

- **Render with cement** earthen walls.
- **Build without collar beam** or reinforcements in seismic zones.
- **Open large openings** or near corners.
- **Support the wall directly on the ground,** without foundation.
- **Heavy ceilings** poorly anchored.
- **Ignore cracks** that grow or go through the wall.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to live in an adobe house?

It depends on how it is built and maintained. A well-designed, reinforced and maintained adobe house can be safe. A house without reinforcement, with cracks and humidity, is vulnerable in an earthquake. A professional evaluation is the best way to find out.

### Can you reinforce an existing adobe house?

Yes, there are reinforcement techniques with meshes, collar beams and other elements. They must be defined on a case-by-case basis, according to local standards and with technical advice.

### Do earthen houses attract moisture?

The soil absorbs and releases moisture, but a well-protected house with good boots, a good hat and breathable plaster stays dry. Problems appear when water reaches the walls continuously. More information in the guide on [humidity in walls](/articulos/humedad-en-paredes-causas-y-soluciones/).

## In summary

Adobe, rammed earth, bahareque and compressed earth block offer comfortable houses, with great thermal mass, humidity regulation and low environmental impact. Its weak point is the response to earthquakes and water. Build or reinforce with earthquake-resistant criteria, with a simple floor plan, small spans, collar beam and well-anchored light roof, following local standards and with professional advice. Protect the walls with good boots and a good hat, use earth or lime plaster and never cement, and keep the walls free of cracks.
