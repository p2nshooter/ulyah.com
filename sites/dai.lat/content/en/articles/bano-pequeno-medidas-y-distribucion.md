In many Latin American homes the bathroom is the smallest room in the house. In a new apartment it can measure just over three square meters, and in an old house it is sometimes a space stolen from a hallway or a patio. Even so, it is one of the most used rooms and one of the most expensive to renovate: it has water, drains, electricity, humidity and finishes that should last for many years.

Therefore, in a small bathroom every centimeter counts. A door that opens the wrong way, a toilet too close to the wall or a shower with no slope can ruin the daily experience. In this guide you will see the indicative measurements that each piece needs, the layouts that best use the space, how to gain storage and what safety rules should not be ignored.

## Indicative measurements of each piece

The exact measurements depend on the models and regulations of each country, but these references are common in bathroom design:

| Element | Indicative measure |
|---|---|
| Free space in front of the toilet | at least 60 to 70 cm |
| From the toilet shaft to a wall or side cabinet | at least 40cm |
| Free space in front of the sink | at least 70cm |
| Sink height | 80 to 85cm |
| Minimal shower | 80 × 80 cm, more comfortable from 90 × 90 cm |
| Clear door passage | at least 70cm |

With these measurements, a complete bathroom with toilet, sink and shower can be resolved in about 3 square meters, for example 1.5 by 2 meters. A half bathroom or guest bathroom, with a toilet and sink, can fit in just over one and a half square meters.

If a person in a wheelchair lives or may live in the house, the measures change quite a bit. An accessible bathroom requires a turning circle of around 1.5 meters in diameter, grab bars and a walk-in shower.

## Distributions that work

### All on one wall

Toilet, sink and shower lined up on the same wall. It is the most economical layout, because it **groups all the plumbing** in a single wet wall. Works great in long, narrow bathrooms.

### Facing Pieces

The sink on one wall and the toilet and shower on the opposite one. Take advantage of wider bathrooms, but the central hallway should be at least 70 centimeters.

### In L

The pieces occupy two adjacent walls. It is useful in almost square bathrooms and leaves the area near the door free.

When planning, it is advisable to locate the **shower at the back**, away from the door, and the sink near the entrance, because it is the room that visitors use the most.

## The door: outward or sliding

In a small bathroom, a door that opens inward can clash with the toilet or sink and take up useful space. There are alternatives:

- **Door that opens outwards:** frees up interior space and also allows easy entry if someone faints or falls leaning against the door.
- **Sliding door** with mortise or exterior rail: does not take up sweeping space. Make sure it closes tightly to maintain privacy.
- **Folding door:** takes up less space than a common door, although it is less robust.

## Shower, tub and screen

In a small bathroom, the **shower** is almost always a better option than the tub. Some decisions that greatly change the result:

- **Shower at floor level:** without tray or step, it visually expands the space and is safer. It requires a well-made slope towards the drain and careful waterproofing, as we explain in the guide to [waterproofing the shower](/articulos/impermeabilizar-la-ducha/).
- **Linear Drain:** A gutter along a wall allows for a one-way slope and large ceramic pieces.
- **Glass partition:** Lets light through and makes the bathroom appear larger. A curtain is cheaper, but visually divides the space.
- **Fixed panel without door:** in showers at least one meter long, a fixed glass may be sufficient to prevent splashing.

## Toilet and sink that take up less space

- **Compact toilets** with less depth gain a few centimeters in front of the bowl.
- **Hanging toilets** with a tank built into the wall leave the floor free and facilitate cleaning, although they require a support structure on the wall.
- **Dual flush,** with two buttons for half and full flush, saves water with each use.
- **Hanging or corner sinks** free up floor space.
- **Shallow sink cabinets** combine storage with comfortable passage.

## Taps and water consumption

The faucets also influence the comfort of a small bathroom. A single lever, with a single lever for flow and temperature, is operated with one hand and takes up less space than two separate faucets. In the shower, a bar with hand shower makes it easy to clean and bathe children or the elderly. **Aerators** in the sink and low-consumption showers mix air with water and reduce waste without being too noticeable in daily use. Before you buy, check your home's water pressure: some large showers require more pressure than many homes with elevated tanks have.

## Storage without taking up the floor

- **Niches in shower wall** for shampoo and soap. They should be constructed with the same waterproofing as the rest of the shower.
- **First aid kit with mirror** over the sink.
- **Shelves above the toilet,** at a height that does not interfere with sitting.
- **Hooks and towel racks on the door** or on the free wall.
- **Drawers instead of doors** in the sink cabinet, because they allow you to see and reach everything.

## Visual tricks

- **Large ceramic pieces** with few joints make the surface look continuous. The guide on [ceramic or porcelain tile](/articulos/ceramica-o-porcelanato/) helps you choose.
- **The same covering on the floor and walls** of the shower unifies the space.
- **Light colors** reflect more light, although a dark accent or a well-chosen intense color can add character.
- **A large mirror,** from side to side of the sink, visually doubles the bathroom.
- **Good lighting in front of the mirror,** not just from the ceiling, as we explain in the [house lighting] guide (/articulos/iluminacion-de-la-casa/).

## Ventilation: essential

A small bathroom without a window accumulates humidity in minutes. If you don't have a window, you need an extractor fan with access to the outside or a ventilation duct. If you have a small window, open it after each shower. Mold on ceilings and joints is the most common consequence of a poorly ventilated bathroom, and we cover it in the guide on [ventilation and mold in the bathroom](/articulos/ventilacion-y-moho-en-el-bano/).

## Security: what cannot be missed

- **Non-slip floor** in the shower and, if possible, throughout the bathroom.
- **Sockets with differential protection** and away from the shower and sink, according to the electrical regulations of your country.
- **Light fixtures suitable for humid environments** near the shower.
- **Support bars** next to the toilet and in the shower if elderly people live in the house.
- **Never install a gas water heater inside the bathroom.** These devices consume oxygen and can produce carbon monoxide, an invisible and deadly gas. In many countries it is expressly prohibited. They must go in a ventilated place and be installed by authorized personnel. We expand on the topic in the guide on [extractor hood and gas in the kitchen] (/articulos/campana-extractora-y-gas-en-la-cocina/).

## Before reforming

1. **Accurately measures** bathroom, toilet drain location and water inlets.
2. **Respect, if you can, the position of the drains,** especially the toilet. Moving it usually requires breaking up the floor and taking care of the slope.
3. **Draw a scale plan** with the actual pieces you plan to buy.
4. **Check the clearances** in front of each piece and the swing of the door.
5. **Plan for waterproofing, ventilation and electrical** before choosing finishes.

## Frequent errors

- **Door that collides with the toilet** or sink.
- **Toilet too close to side wall**.
- **Shower without sufficient slope,** which leaves stagnant water.
- **No ventilation system** in bathrooms without windows.
- **Deep furniture** that narrows the passage.
- **Gas heater inside the bathroom.**

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the minimum size of a full bathroom?

With compact pieces and a good distribution, around 3 square meters, for example 1.5 by 2 meters. Below that surface it can be resolved, but with more comfort limitations.

### Is a screen or a curtain more convenient?

The transparent glass partition maintains the feeling of spaciousness and is easy to clean. The curtain is cheaper and more flexible, but it visually divides the bathroom and gets dirty more quickly.

### Can I put a washing machine in the bathroom?

In some departments it is the only option. If you do, make sure the outlet has differential protection, that the washing machine is far from the shower, and that the bathroom has good ventilation.

## In summary

A complete bathroom can be resolved in about 3 square meters if the free spaces are respected: at least 60 to 70 centimeters in front of the toilet, 40 from its axis to the wall and a shower of at least 80 by 80. Group plumbing on a wall, place the shower at the back, and choose an outward-opening or sliding door. Gain space with suspended pieces, niches and large mirrors, always ventilate and don't forget safety: non-slip floor, protected electricity and never a gas heater inside the bathroom.
