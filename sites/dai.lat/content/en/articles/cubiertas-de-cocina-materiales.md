The kitchen work surface takes more punishment than any other in the house. It is chopped, kneaded, hot pots are placed on it, oil, lemon juice, coffee and wine are poured on it, and it is cleaned several times a day. In Mexico it is called cover, in Colombia and Chile meson, in Argentina and Uruguay countertop, and in other places board or countertop. By any name, it is one of the most visible and most expensive decisions in a kitchen.

There is no perfect material. The one that resists heat best can stain easily, and the one that doesn't stain can be marked with a hot pot. Choosing well is a matter of knowing how you cook, how much maintenance you are willing to do and how much you can invest. In this guide you will see the most used materials in Latin America, their advantages and limitations, details such as thicknesses, edges and plates, and the care that each one needs.

## Granite: the resistant classic

Granite is a very hard natural rock that withstands heat and scratches well. Each plate is different, with its own grains and points. Your points to take care of:

- **Porosity:** varies depending on the variety. Some absorb oil or wine if they are not sealed. A stone sealer, renewed according to use, greatly reduces the problem.
- **Weight:** Base units must be well constructed and level.
- **Joints:** on long or L-shaped roofs there will be visible joints. A good marble maker makes them almost imperceptible.

A simple test to see if it needs sealing: leave a few drops of water on the surface. If they darken the stone in a few minutes, it is time to seal. More about stones in the [natural stone at home](/articulos/piedra-natural-en-casa/) guide.

## Marble: beautiful and delicate

Marble is softer and more porous than granite, and it is a calcareous rock: lemon juice, vinegar, tomato or wine can leave opaque marks when attacking the surface, as well as stains. It works in kitchens where it is acceptable for the surface to age with marks, or in rarely used areas, such as a bar or serving island. For kitchens with intense use it is usually a risky choice.

## compact quartz

It is a material made with a large proportion of crushed quartz joined with resins and pigments. Its advantages are clear: **it is non-porous**, it does not need sealing, it resists stains and scratches and offers uniform colors.

Its limitations:

- **Direct heat:** resins may mark or discolor if a very hot pot is placed on it. Always use trivets.
- **Sun:** some products turn yellow with prolonged exposure to ultraviolet radiation, so they are not recommended for outdoors.
- **Worker Health:** Cutting and polishing compact quartz produces crystalline silica dust, which is very dangerous to the lungs of those who manufacture and install it. Choose workshops that work with wet cutting, dust extraction and respiratory protection, and avoid dry cutting and adjustments indoors.

## Large format porcelain

Large porcelain or sintered stone slabs are highly resistant to heat, stains and scratches, and are non-porous. Its weak point is the **edges and corners**, which can chip with a strong blow, and the manufacturing, which requires specialized workshops. It is a very durable high-end option.

## Laminate: the economical option

Plastic laminate over chipboard or MDF is the most economical covering and comes in many designs. Its great enemy is **water**: if it enters through the edges, the joints or the cut of the tile, the interior board swells and falls apart. To make it last:

- **Seals very well** the cut of the tile and the joints.
- **Dry up spills** around the sink.
- **Do not place hot pots on it:** the laminate burns and peels off.
- **Use cutting board** always.

## stainless steel

It is the material of professional kitchens: hygienic, heat and stain resistant. With use it scratches and acquires a matte patina, something that many people like and others don't. It can also be dented by knocks and is noisier. Clean with water and neutral detergent, drying in the direction of polishing.

## Concrete and cement

Concrete decks have a handcrafted and contemporary look. They are porous and need a **sealant suitable for food contact**, as well as care with acids and fats. Fine cracks may appear over time. The guide on [exposed concrete](/articulos/concreto-aparente-cuidados/) explains how to protect it.

## solid wood

Wood provides warmth and is pleasant to work with. Requires periodic oiling with food-safe oils, care for standing water, especially around the sink, and trivets for heat. Knife marks can be sanded and renewed. More about species and treatments in the [wood at home] guide (/articulos/madera-en-casa-especies-y-cuidados/).

## Tile or ceramic

A tile countertop resists heat very well and is economical, but the **joints** accumulate grease and dirt and the surface is not completely flat for kneading. Epoxy joints are more resistant to stains than cement joints. The guide on [ceramic or porcelain tile](/articulos/ceramica-o-porcelanato/) helps you choose pieces.

## Details that also matter

- **Thickness:** stones usually come in 2 or 3 centimeters. The edges can be visually thickened with a double edge.
- **Edge Profile:** Rounded or beveled edges chip less and are gentler on the hip than a live edge.
- **Overhang for sitting:** in a bar or island with stools, an overhang of 25 to 30 centimeters is usually left. Large overhangs need supports.
- **Sink or sink:** An undermount sink makes cleaning easier, but requires a water-resistant material in the cut, such as stone, quartz or porcelain. In laminate, overlapping tiles are used.
- **Wall protector:** Extend the cover material a few centimeters on the wall, or place a washable covering to protect it from splashes.

## Before ordering the cover

1. **Install the base units first** and check that they are level and firm.
2. **Have the sink, the grill or built-in stove and the taps on hand,** with their cutting templates. The workshop needs them to make the exact holes.
3. **Ask for a template or on-site measurement.** Walls are almost never perfectly straight and a stone countertop does not allow improvised adjustments.
4. **Agree where the joints will go** on long or L-shaped roofs, so that they are in discreet areas.
5. **Ask about the sealing and warranty,** and request in writing the recommended care for the material.

## Daily care for any material

1. **Always use cutting boards**, even if the material resists scratches: you will also protect your knives.
2. **Place trivets** under hot pots and pans.
3. **Clean up spills promptly,** especially oil, wine, coffee, lemon and vinegar.
4. **Use neutral detergents.** Avoid acidic or abrasive cleaners unless indicated by the manufacturer.
5. **Check stone and concrete seals** according to use.

## Frequent errors

- **Choose marble for a kitchen with intense use** without accepting that it will be marked.
- **Rest hot pots** on quartz, laminate or solid surface.
- **Leave water in the seams** of a laminate cover.
- **Do not seal** porous granite or concrete.
- **Dry cutting compact quartz** inside the house.
- **Forget the overhang and supports** on a sit-up bar.

## Frequently asked questions

### Granite or quartz?

Granite resists heat better and each piece is unique, but may need sealing. Quartz does not stain, does not need sealing, and is uniform, but is more sensitive to direct heat. If you cook a lot with large, hot pots, granite tends to be more forgiving.

### What is the cheapest option that lasts?

A good laminate, properly installed and sealed, can last for many years if protected from water and heat. Tile is another economical and heat-resistant alternative.

### Can a damaged cover be repaired?

It depends on the material. The solid surface and wood are sanded and repaired. The stone can be polished and filled with small chips. Water-swollen laminate usually needs to be replaced.

## In summary

Granite is heat and scratch resistant, but it should be sealed. Marble is beautiful and delicate against acids. Compact quartz does not stain or need sealing, but it is afraid of direct heat and cutting requires protection for workers. Large-format porcelain tile is very resistant, laminate is economical but afraid of water, steel is hygienic and scratches, and concrete and wood require maintenance. Choose according to your way of cooking and take care of the details: thickness, edges, tray and wall protector.
