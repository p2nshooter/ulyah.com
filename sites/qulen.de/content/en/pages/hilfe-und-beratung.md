On QULEN we tell encouraging stories and give tips that you can try out on your own. Sometimes that's not enough. If learning doesn't get better for months despite all your efforts, if the fear of exams makes you sick or if there's something completely different behind bad grades, you need people who get to know you personally and support you. This page shows you who is there for this in Germany. It doesn't replace advice, but it shows you the way there.

Getting help is not a sign of weakness. Actually, it's a learning strategy like any other: If you get stuck on a task, ask questions.

## In case of emergency

- **112:** Emergency call for rescue services and fire brigade, free of charge and around the clock. Call if your life is in danger, if you are thinking about harming yourself and no longer feel safe, or if you are afraid that something bad is about to happen to someone else.
- **110:** Police if you are threatened or experience violence.
- **116 117:** medical on-call service if you need urgent medical help outside of office hours but it is not a life-threatening emergency.

## Talk immediately, anonymously and free of charge

- **Number against grief, children's and youth hotline: 116 111.** For children and young people, free and anonymous. You can talk about anything there: stress with grades, fear of exams, trouble with teachers, bullying, heartbreak or worries at home. You can find the current office hours and the online counseling offer on the website of the number against grief.
- **Number against grief, parent hotline: 0800 111 0 550.** For mothers, fathers and other caregivers who are worried about a child, for example because they are suffering from the pressure at school.
- **Telephone pastoral care: 0800 111 0 111, 0800 111 0 222 or 116 123.** Free, anonymous and around the clock, for young people and adults. The TelefonSeelsorge website also offers advice via chat and email.

## At school

You don't have to know who exactly is responsible. Start with the person you trust the most. She can tell you who can help.

- **Trusted Source Teacher:** At many schools, students choose a trusted teacher. They take time for you and discuss with you what should happen with your request.
- **Class management and specialist teachers:** When it comes to a specific subject, the specialist teacher is often the best address. Ask as specifically as possible: What basics am I missing? Where should I start? Is there training material?
- **Advisory teacher:** In some federal states there are specially trained advisory teachers at schools who can help with learning difficulties, questions about school careers and exam anxiety.
- **School social work:** School social workers are not teachers and do not award grades. You can also talk to them about things that have nothing to do with class.
- **School psychological counseling:** All federal states have school psychological counseling centers, usually at the school authority or at a regional school authority. The consultation is free and confidential. Students, parents and teachers can report there if they have severe exam anxiety, persistent learning difficulties or conflicts in the class. You can find out where the responsible office is at your school's office or on the website of your state's education ministry.

## In training

- **Instructor:** Let us know early if you lose track of the vocational school. Many problems can be solved as long as there is still time before the exam.
- **Teachers at the vocational school:** They know what content is important for the exam and are often familiar with support offers at the school.
- **Training advice from the chamber:** The chamber responsible for your profession, for example the Chamber of Commerce and Industry or the Chamber of Crafts, has training advisors. They help with questions about training, conflicts in the company and questions about exams. There you will also find out whether and how compensation for disadvantages in the exam is possible, for example in the event of a disability.
- **Youth and trainee representation:** If there is one in your company, you can contact them or the works council.
- **Employment Agency:** Career advice is also there for you during your training. It provides information about support offers such as assisted training if the learning material or everyday life in the company becomes too much for you. You can find out directly there whether an offer is suitable for you.

## In studies

- **Central Student Advisory Service:** helps if you have doubts about your field of study, questions about orientation and possible changes.
- **Departmental study advice:** answers questions about modules, exams and the course of your studies in your department.
- **Psychological or psychosocial advice:** Many universities and student unions offer free and confidential discussions, for example if you have exam anxiety, procrastination, exhaustion or in a crisis.
- **Examination Office:** is responsible for registration, deadlines, withdrawal and sick leave for examinations. The rules are in your examination regulations and differ from university to university.
- **Advice for students with disabilities or chronic illnesses:** Universities have representatives or advisory centers who also advise on compensating for disadvantages in exams.
- **Learning and writing advice:** Some universities have writing centers or learning workshops that help with planning a term paper or preparing for exams.
- **Social advice:** If money worries make it difficult to study, the student unions provide advice on financing your studies, such as BAföG.

## When exam anxiety becomes too great

A little excitement before an exam is normal and can even make you alert and alert. You can find ideas on how to deal with this in the chapter [Exams & Exam Anxiety](/themen/pruefungen-und-pruefungsangst/). But get support if any of these apply to you:

- You hardly sleep for days before exams or you always have severe physical complaints such as stomach pain, nausea or a racing heart.
- You often call in sick or avoid school, vocational school or lectures to avoid exams.
- The fear doesn't stop after the exam, but rather determines your everyday life.
- You feel depressed, empty or hopeless for weeks.

Good first points of contact are your family doctor's practice or pediatric and adolescent doctor's office, the school psychological counseling service or the psychological counseling service at your university. Physical complaints should always be clarified by a doctor. People with statutory health insurance can also attend a psychotherapeutic consultation without a referral, including in practices that specialize in children and adolescents. The appointment service on 116 117 can help you find an appointment.

## When learning remains difficult despite practice

Sometimes there is more to persistent difficulties than a lack of practice, for example a reading and spelling problem, a math disorder, ADHD or an undetected problem with vision or hearing. Only a specialist can clarify whether this is the case, not an online test or a guide. The first point of contact is the pediatric and adolescent doctor's office or the family doctor's office, the school psychology consultation and, if necessary, a child and adolescent psychiatry practice. If a diagnosis is made, compensation for disadvantages or other help are often possible at school. The federal states set the rules for this, so ask your school directly.

## When things aren't right in class

Bullying, exclusion or insults in the class chat take away the energy you actually need to learn. You don't have to endure this alone. Talk to the trusted teacher, the class leader or school social work. When it comes to cyberbullying, it helps to screenshot messages and posts before blocking the person and reporting the content on the platform. If you are threatened, inform the police. You can also talk about bullying during the number against grief.

## When something is wrong at home

Worries in the family, arguments, violence or neglect often also have an impact on school. Reach out to an adult you trust or the grief line. In an emergency, children and young people can also contact the youth welfare office in their city or district.

## If there is no money for tutoring

Tutoring is not affordable for every family. Families who receive certain social benefits can apply for support for learning support through the education and participation package under certain conditions. You can find out whether this is an option for you and where to apply at your city or district administration or at the job center. Also ask your school about special courses, learning times or opportunities in which older students help younger ones.

## For parents, teachers and trainers

If you are accompanying a child, a student or a trainee and are worried: school psychological counseling is also there for parents and teachers. Parents can reach the parent hotline number against grief and find parenting and family counseling centers in many cities. Trainees can contact their chamber’s training advisory service. Take signals seriously without dramatizing them and ask questions with interest instead of making accusations.

## This is how you prepare for a conversation

Many people are afraid to ask for help because they don't know how to start. These three questions will help you sort your thoughts:

1. What exactly is difficult for you and how long has it been like this?
2. What have you tried and what helped at least a little?
3. What do you want from the conversation: advice, a plan or just for someone to listen to you?

Write your answers on a piece of paper and take it with you. You can also bring a girlfriend, boyfriend or parent with you. And if you're at a loss for words, a sentence like: "I'm struggling right now and don't know where to start."
