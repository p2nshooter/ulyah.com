Aylin lay on her stomach, the bio book on the blanket, a pillow under her elbows. She read the paragraph about photosynthesis for the fourth time. Can and Melis built a marble run on the carpet, and every time a marble whizzed down the tube, Aylin raised her head. After an hour, she knew that leaves are green because they contain chlorophyll. She had already known that beforehand.

## Three beds, a desk

Aylin was fifteen and in the tenth grade. There were final exams at the end of the school year and she wanted to do well. She shared the room with her eight-year-old brother Can and her six-year-old sister Melis. There were three beds, a wardrobe and a desk with crayons, construction paper, notebooks and a half-finished Lego castle.

So Aylin studied on the bed. It was convenient, but that was exactly the problem. After twenty minutes her eyes became heavy. The sheets slipped off the pillow and the highlighter spilled, leaving a yellow stain on the duvet. And when she got tired, the phone was just an arm's length away. She sat over the books for hours and still had the feeling that she could hardly get anything done.

## An experiment with four locations

During learning time, Ms. Hartmann, her class teacher, asked the class: “Where do you actually learn?” The answers ranged from the desk to the kitchen table to the sofa. Then she suggested an experiment: try different places for two weeks and write down after each learning unit how much you accomplished and what bothered you.

Aylin took this seriously. She tried four places: the bed, the kitchen table, the school library and the town library. After each session, she wrote down on an index card: location, time, what was done, what was disturbed. After two weeks, she placed the cards side by side and the result was clearer than she imagined. She had accomplished the least on the bed, except for reading German. Things went well at the kitchen table as long as no one else was sitting there. Things were going surprisingly well in the school library after the sixth period. And in the city library she managed as much in two hours as she would normally do in an entire afternoon.

## A box, a corner, a reading room

Aylin turned the experiment into a plan. First, she cleared the left half of the desk and declared it her study area. Her siblings' craft supplies went into a box under the table. She hung a small clip-on lamp above it because the overhead light cast a shadow on her notebook.

Second, she packed a study box out of a sturdy shoebox: pens, highlighters, ruler, calculator, index cards, college pad, a small clock. This meant she could get started anywhere in two minutes, at her desk or at the kitchen table, and didn't have to search for anything. If the room was noisy, she would put on headphones with no music, just to block the noise.

Third, she went to the city library two afternoons a week. On the first day she asked at the counter for ID and the workplaces. In the reading room there were schoolgirls, students and an older gentleman with a newspaper, all quietly hunched over their things. Aylin noticed how easy it was to collaborate when everyone around her was working.

Fourth, she determined where she would do what. Understand new things and practice tasks: library or desk. Check vocabulary: kitchen table or bus. She was allowed to continue reading on the bed. When the final exams came, Aylin hadn't studied any more hours than before. But the hours had brought more.

## What you can take from it

Aylin didn't find a perfect place to study, but rather several suitable ones. Many people don't have their own room with a large desk. That doesn't have to be the case. The following steps will show you how to work well with the space you have and where to find alternatives.

### What makes a good learning place

A good study space doesn't have to be pretty, it just has to make your work easy. These points help:

| feature | Why it helps | Quick solution |
|---|---|---|
| Good light | You tire less and see your writing without shadows | Daylight from the side, a lamp on the page opposite your writing hand |
| Free space | Nothing distracts your attention, you can find everything immediately | Before studying, put away everything that is not part of the task |
| Just learning material | Games, cell phones and snacks draw attention away | Cell phone out of reach, only the material for the current task on the table |
| table and chair | You are more awake when you sit upright than when you are half lying down | Feet on the floor, forearms loosely on the table |
| Fresh air and water | A stuffy room makes you tired quickly | Ventilate briefly before starting and provide a glass of water |

Many people notice that they get tired more quickly in bed because they usually sleep there. The bed can still be fine for reading. For tasks that require you to write, calculate, or concentrate, a table is almost always better. How to put your cell phone out of the way while studying is shown in [Ben's story about cell phone distraction](/lerngeschichten/handy-ablenkung-beim-lernen/).

### Set up a study space in a small space

If you don't have your own room or desk, these ideas will help:

1. **Pack a study box.** A box or basket with everything you need: pens, highlighters, ruler, calculator, index cards, pad, a clock. This means you can start at any table in just a few minutes and then pack everything up again.
2. **Secure a dedicated corner.** Even half of a table set aside for studying makes a difference. The more often you study in the same place, the faster you will get into work mode there.
3. **Use fixed times.** The kitchen table can be your study space at certain times when no one is cooking or eating there.
4. **Turn towards the wall.** By facing a quiet surface instead of the room, movements are less distracting. A cardboard box can serve as a simple privacy screen.
5. **Provide your own light.** A small lamp makes any table a better place to work.

### Alternatives outside of home

If it's not possible at home or you just need more rest, there are other places:

- **The school library or a self-study room.** Many schools have rooms where you can work after class or during free periods. Ask your class teacher or the office.
- **The city library.** Public libraries often offer workplaces where you can study in peace. You usually need a library card for this. You can find out how much it costs, what rules apply and when it is open at the counter or on your library's website.
- **Youth centers and learning rooms in the district.** Some facilities offer rooms for learning or homework help. You can find out what is available locally or at your school.
- **University and vocational school libraries.** If you are in training or studying, ask about study spaces and group rooms.

Opening times and rules change, for example during the holidays. Check them beforehand so that you don't end up in front of a locked door.

### Sounds and headphones

Not every noise is equally annoying. Many people cope well with constant background noise, but become distracted as soon as someone speaks nearby because they involuntarily listen. You can try these options:

- **Earplugs** for peace of mind while reading and memorizing.
- **Headphones without music** that muffle noise, like Aylin.
- **Calm music without singing** for tasks that don't require you to read a lot, such as tidying up documents or copying flashcards.
- **Practice silence every now and then.** You don't listen to music during exams either. Therefore, study part of the time without background noise.

Keep the volume low to avoid damaging your ears. And put music together beforehand so you don't have to choose anything while you study.

### Different locations for different tasks

Not every learning task needs the same place. If you distribute your locations consciously, you also use short time windows:

| task | Good place | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Understanding new things, calculating difficult tasks | Library, quiet study corner | Needs the most concentration |
| Query vocabulary and flashcards | Kitchen table, bus, waiting room | Short units fit anywhere |
| Reading reading | Armchair, sofa, even the bed | Comfortable is fine here |
| Practice the presentation out loud | Empty room, outside on a walk | You must be able to speak without disturbing others |
| Learn with others | Group room, kitchen table | Talking in the reading room disturbs the others |

### A start and end ritual at the learning place

Having a set routine at the beginning and end will help you get in and out of work more quickly.

**To start:** Clear the table, open the learning box, put water down, put your cell phone away, write the first task on a piece of paper, set the clock. This takes three minutes and tells your head: Here we go.

**Finally:** Write down on a piece of paper where you will continue tomorrow. Put everything in the box and clear the space. This way you can find your way in straight away the next day and don't have to think about where you left off. [Marie's story about learning breaks](/lerngeschichten/lernpausen-richtig-planen/) tells you how to organize the breaks between your learning blocks.

## Typical pitfalls

- **Waiting for the perfect spot.** Start with what you have. A learning box and half a table top are enough to get you started.
- **Everything on the table at the same time.** When there are five compartments lying open, your eyes jump back and forth.
- **The bed for everything.** Reading is possible, but math and writing is usually not.
- **Going to the library without a plan.** If you first think about what you want to do when you're there, you'll quickly lose time.
- **Just look at noise.** Light, air and good posture make just as much difference.

## Briefly asked

### What if there is no quiet space at home?

Then it's worth taking a look at the school library, city library or learning rooms in the district. Also talk to your class teacher. Some schools offer afternoon study times that you can take advantage of.

### Can I also study in a café?

For easier tasks perhaps. For difficult tasks that require you to concentrate for a long time, a quieter place is usually better.

### How often should I change location?

As often as it suits your tasks. A fixed main space helps you get into work mode quickly. You can find more stories about sticking with it in the chapter [Concentration & Motivation](/themen/konzentration-und-motivation/).
