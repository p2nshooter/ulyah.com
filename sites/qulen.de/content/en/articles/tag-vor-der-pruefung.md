At three o'clock in the afternoon, Elif tipped her bag onto the table in the homework group room. Two folders slipped out, the reading material with countless sticky notes, a stack of worksheets for discussion, a booklet with poem analyzes and three printed sample solutions. “I’m going to go over it all again today,” she said. “Tomorrow is German.” Her voice sounded firmer than she felt. Her stomach clenched as she looked at the pile.

## Go through everything again

Elif was sixteen and in the tenth grade of a secondary school. The next morning there was the final written exam in German, the first of several. She had actually learned enough, for weeks and according to plan. Nevertheless, at midday she suddenly had the feeling that she no longer knew anything. Your solution: repeat everything today, every page, until late into the night.

Svenja, who volunteered to help with homework in the afternoon and had just graduated from high school herself, sat down. She leafed through the stack and then asked, “Which of these can you do?” Elif shrugged. “Most of it, actually.” – “And what new things do you want to learn today?” – "Nothing new. Just everything again."

“It’s not possible to do it all again today,” said Svenja calmly. "And you don't have to. Today isn't about getting more into your head. Today is about making sure that tomorrow you can get to grips with what's already in there."

## An afternoon with an end

Together they made a plan on the back of a worksheet. Elif took out her three study sheets, one page each for discussion, poetry analysis and non-fiction text analysis. She covered the right half and answered the questions in the margins from memory. She was confident when structuring a discussion, but wobbled when it came to two stylistic devices. She looked it up, nothing more. She then wrote a single introduction to an old exam topic in twenty minutes. At half past five Svenja said: "That's it. Now pack your bag."

They went through a checklist. At one point Elif faltered: was she allowed to take a dictionary with her? She looked for the school information sheet from the folder. It said that a spelling dictionary was allowed and that the cell phone had to be handed in before the start. She placed the dictionary on top, along with two blue fountain pens, spare cartridges, and her mother's watch because she wasn't sure if there was a clock hanging in the exam room.

That evening, Elif ate with her family, walked around the block with her little brother, and muted the class chat, where everyone asked each other who had learned what. She was in bed at ten and couldn't fall asleep for a long time. Svenja had given her a sentence: "If you're lying awake, it's not a drama. You've been studying for weeks and it doesn't go away overnight." She lay quietly, thought about the sea from last summer and eventually fell asleep.

## The morning and the first ten minutes

The alarm went off at half past six, a second one five minutes later. Elif ate what she always ate and took a bus earlier than necessary. A bunch of people from their class stood in front of the exam room and asked each other about stylistic devices. Elif stood a few meters away at the window, took a sip of water and read her opening sentence, which she had written down on an index card: “First read everything, then decide, then start.”

In the room, she listened to the supervisor's instructions and then calmly read through all the tasks. She was allowed to choose between several topics. For one, she immediately thought of two arguments, so she took that one. In the margins she noted times for the outline, the main part and the reading at the end. Then she started with what she was sure she could do: the introduction.

When she got her results weeks later, she was happy with her grade. But what remained in her memory was that morning. She had been excited but still clear-headed.

## What you can take from it

How much you know is rarely decided in the last 24 hours. It depends on how well you gain access to your knowledge the next day. The following steps apply equally to class work, exams and final exams. [Yusuf's story about exam anxiety](/lerngeschichten/pruefungsangst-bewaeltigen/) shows how you deal with exam anxiety in the weeks beforehand.

### Repeat on the last day instead of learning new things

New material the day before has two disadvantages: it hardly has time to consolidate and, above all, it shows you what you can't yet do. That's unsettling. Therefore, work with what you have already learned, ideally with your own study notes. [Nele's story about learning sheets](/lerngeschichten/lernzettel-schreiben/) tells how you write them.

| Makes sense the day before | Better leave it alone |
|---|---|
| Go through your own learning sheets and quiz yourself | start new chapters or topics |
| a short exercise, for example just an introduction | a complete mock exam until late in the evening |
| specifically look up small gaps | want to close large gaps at the last minute |
| a fixed end time for learning | study until just before bedtime |
| clarify the exam process with others | questioning each other nervously |

If you find a larger gap, decide consciously: If it fits into half an hour, take a look at it. If not, leave it and focus on what you can do. Exams almost always ask more questions than a single topic.

At the end of the afternoon of learning, a short test run in your head helps: Go through the next morning in your mind, from getting up to going to the moment you turn over the task sheet. Anything that seems unclear to you, such as the room or where to hand in your cell phone, can be clarified today.

### Pack your bag and documents the evening before

You don't have to look for what's ready in the evening in the morning. What is allowed in which exam varies depending on the subject, school and federal state. Therefore, ask your teacher in good time or look at your school's information sheet.

- **Writing supplies:** at least two pens that write safely and spare cartridges. Ask whether certain pens are required or erasable pens are excluded.
- **Pencil, ruler, eraser** and the appropriate set square for math.
- **Permitted aids** such as a dictionary, calculator or collection of formulas, only if they are expressly permitted. Also clarify whether your own notes are allowed.
- **A simple clock without internet function,** if there is not one that is clearly visible in the room. Cell phones and smartwatches don't belong in the room.
- **ID or invitation,** if your school requires it, and details of the room and time.
- **Something to drink** and a small snack if permitted.
- **Tissues, a jacket or sweater** if the room is cool, and your glasses if necessary.

Also put out your clothes for the next day and check what time your bus leaves.

### Organize your evening and your sleep without pressure

- **Set a cut-off time for studying** and stick to it. Then put the documents out of sight.
- **Do something quiet and familiar:** have a meal with the family, go for a walk, an episode of your favorite series, music.
- **Mute exam chats.** Questions like “Did you learn this too?” don't help anyone the night before.
- **Write down a starting sentence** for the next morning, for example: “Read everything first, then start.”
- **Go to bed at your usual time.** Going to bed earlier than usual often just means you lie awake longer.
- **Take lying awake calmly.** Lying quietly is better than being annoyed about the time you lost. Many people have a restless night before an exam.

If you've been sleeping poorly for weeks, it doesn't belong in this last evening, but in a timely discussion with your family doctor.

### The morning of the exam: breakfast, route and time buffer

Plan the morning backwards from the start of the exam. An example of an exam at 8:30 a.m. with a 25 minute walk to school:

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 6:30 | get up, second alarm as security |
| 6:45 | Breakfast that you know and tolerate well |
| 7:20 | Check your bag briefly and fill your water bottle |
| 7:30 | go out and take a bus or train earlier than necessary |
| 8:05 | arrive, toilet, take a drink |
| 8:15 | Go into the room, hand over your cell phone, set up your space |

Have a Plan B for getting there if your bus is canceled and have the contact details of the office with you in case you are late. In front of the room, you don't have to join in when others are frantically questioning each other. A quiet place at the edge and a look at your starting sentence are enough.

### The first ten minutes of the exam

1. **Set up your space.** Place pens, supplies and clock where you can easily reach them.
2. **Listen to the instructions from the supervisor.** There you will find out how the sheets are labeled and whether there is a reading or selection time.
3. **Read all tasks once.** Mark the task words such as “explain” or “compare” and, if given, the points per task.
4. **Decide quickly when it comes to elective tasks.** Take the task for which two or three thoughts immediately come to mind, and then don't switch back and forth.
5. **Divide the time.** Write times for each task in the margins and plan time to read through them at the end.
6. **Start with something safe.** A task you can do will get you writing and calm you down.

If everything still slips away in the middle of the exam, [Elias' story about the blackout](/lerngeschichten/blackout-in-der-pruefung/) will help you.

## Typical pitfalls

- **Study through the night.** When you're tired, it's harder for you to grasp what you know.
- **Don't look for resources until the morning.** Rushing in the morning takes away your peace of mind for the exam.
- **Get confused in the hallway.** What others repeat shortly beforehand says nothing about your preparation.
- **Start with the hardest task.** A bumpy start costs time and nerves.
- **Test new learning methods the day before.** Stick to what you did in the previous weeks.

## Briefly asked

### Should I not learn anything the day before?

Yes, but short, calm and with an end. A round of self-assessment with your study notes and a short exercise are enough. Those who do nothing at all often brood more.

### Can I still look at my study notes in the morning?

A quick glance at a card with your starting phrase or a keyword or two is fine if it calms you down. But leave the whole stack at home. When you're frantically scrolling through the pages just before you start, you usually only notice what you don't know for sure.

### What do I do if I can't eat because I'm so excited?

Eat something small that you can tolerate and bring a snack with you if it is allowed. If nausea or excitement often bothers you, talk to your family doctor before the next exams.

### I am sick on the day of the exam. What now?

Report to school as early as possible, preferably before the exam begins. How a case of illness must be proven and whether there is a follow-up appointment is regulated by the school and the federal state. It's best to ask about it beforehand.
