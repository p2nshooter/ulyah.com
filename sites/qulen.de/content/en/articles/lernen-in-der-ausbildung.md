At half past four in the afternoon Tobias came home, took off his work shoes and collapsed onto the sofa. He had been at a construction site with his journeyman since seven in the morning, hauling pipes, installing radiators and laying cables. His arms were heavy, his head empty. The vocational school folder was on the coffee table. Tomorrow there was a test in arithmetic. Tobias looked at the folder. The folder looked back. Tobias reached for the remote control.

## Two worlds

Tobias was seventeen and in the first year of his apprenticeship as a system mechanic for plumbing, heating and air conditioning technology. He had finished secondary school with mediocre grades and was looking forward to the training because he finally wanted to do something with his hands. Things were going well at work. His master praised him, the journeymen took him to the construction sites, and Tobias noticed that he was skilled at work.

Vocational school was a different story. The technical calculations involved heat requirements, pipe cross-sections and pressure losses. In materials technology about metals, corrosion and connection techniques. And then there was German, politics and English. Tobias thought he was done with school. Now he realized that the theory was more difficult than he had expected.

There was also the report book in which he had to enter every week what he had done in the company and at the vocational school. He put it off until several weeks had accumulated and then had to laboriously reconstruct what he had done and when.

## A test with consequences

The math test the next day went poorly. A five. Tobias was ashamed, and when his master found out about it, he called him into the office. Tobias expected thunderstorms. Instead, Master Krause sat down and said: "I also struggled at vocational school. But without the theory you won't get through the exam. And neither will you later, if you want to plan systems yourself."

He told Tobias about his own path. How he had learned back then to connect the calculations from school with what he saw on the construction site. "When you install a radiator today, ask yourself: Why is it exactly this size? How did someone calculate that? Then the technical calculation suddenly comes to life."

And he gave him some practical advice: "Don't study in the evenings when you're exhausted. Study in the mornings or on weekends when you're fresh. And do the report book every Friday, ten minutes before you go home."

## A new weekly rhythm

Tobias sat down at the weekend and thought about when he could realistically learn. His vocational school took place in block classes, several weeks at a time, with long phases in the company in between. During the working weeks he was tired in the evenings, but at six in the morning he had half an hour before he had to leave.

He decided: twenty minutes in the morning for vocational school three days a week, with a coffee at the kitchen table. One hour on Saturday morning for the difficult topics. And on Friday afternoon, ten minutes of reports at work, right after work.

Things were different in the block weeks at the vocational school. He had time in the afternoon and used it to review the day's topics. He and two classmates formed a small study group that met in the school library after class.

## Theory meets construction site

The most important thing that changed Tobias was his view of the construction site. When his journeyman chose a radiator, Tobias asked what criteria he used. When they laid pipes, he asked why that diameter. The journeyman was initially surprised, but then was happy to explain.

At vocational school, Tobias noticed that he could suddenly associate the tasks with images in his head. When doing a task about the heat requirements of a room, he thought about the living room where they had installed a new radiator last week. The formulas were no longer abstract; they described something he knew.

He also created a collection of formulas in which he wrote down a practical example for each formula. And for technical terms he used index cards, which he went through during breaks on the construction site.

## The intermediate exam

Almost a year later, the intermediate exam was due, which was the first part of the final exam for his job. Tobias prepared by calculating old exam questions that the vocational school had provided him with. His report book was complete and neatly kept, as was necessary for admission to the examination.

In the practical part he shone. In the theoretical part there were tasks that would have previously made him despair. This time he knew how to proceed with most of them. The result was a solid “good” in the practical part and a “satisfactory” in the theory. Master Krause patted him on the shoulder. "You see. Head and hands."

## What you can take from it

An apprenticeship combines two learning locations: the company and the vocational school. This is a great opportunity because theory and practice can explain each other. At the same time, it is a challenge to learn after a long day at work. With a realistic rhythm and a few habits, both can be easily combined.

### Find your own rhythm

After a physically or mentally strenuous day at work, your head is often tired. Learning in the evening is difficult. Think about when you realistically have energy:

| point in time | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Early morning | fresh head, calm | need to get up early |
| Immediately after work | Content still present | often tired |
| Late evening | Time available | Concentration drops, sleep suffers |
| weekend | more time at a time | Leisure time is becoming shorter |
| Breaks in operation | short units, such as flashcards | not for difficult topics |

Many trainees do well with short sessions during the week and a longer block on the weekend. Shorter, regular learning times are more effective than infrequent long sessions.

### Block lessons and part-time lessons

Depending on the profession and region, vocational school lessons take place one to two days per week or in blocks lasting several weeks. Both have consequences for your learning:

- **For part-time classes** it's best to review the material shortly after the school day, before work takes over.
- **With block lessons** you can use the afternoons to repeat. During the operational phases, you should occasionally refresh the material so that it is not lost until the next block.

### Combining theory and practice

The biggest advantage of the training is that you see every day what the theory is good for. Use this:

- **Ask questions.** Ask journeymen, colleagues and trainers why something is done that way.
- **Collect examples.** Write down an example from your everyday work for formulas and technical terms.
- **Transfer tasks to practice.** For calculation tasks, imagine where they occur in the company.
- **Have it explained in the company.** Many trainers are happy when trainees show interest in the background.

### Keep the report book regularly

The proof of training, often called the report book, documents what you learn during the training. As a rule, it is a prerequisite for admission to the final examination. So he doesn't remain a mountain:

1. **Fixed time.** For example, every Friday ten minutes after work.
2. **Keywords in everyday life.** Write down briefly what you did during the day, for example on your cell phone.
3. **Write specifically.** Not “construction site”, but “radiator installed, connecting cables laid”.
4. **Have it signed.** Have your trainer sign the booklet regularly, as required by your company.

Your training company determines whether you keep the report book in writing or digitally. You can get information about this there or from your local chamber.

### Learn specifically for vocational school and exams

- **Create a collection of formulas with examples**, especially for specialist calculations.
- **Flashcards for technical terms** that you go through during breaks or on the way to work. [Frida's story about the index box](/lerngeschichten/vokabeln-lernen-mit-karteikasten/) shows how this works.
- Calculate **old exam tasks** that the vocational school or company provides to you.
- **Form learning groups** with other trainees, like in [Noah's story about the learning group](/lerngeschichten/lerngruppe-organisieren/).

[Selin's story about the final exam in training](/lerngeschichten/abschlusspruefung-in-der-ausbildung/) tells how you prepare for the final exam.

### Get support

If you have difficulties at vocational school, you don't have to solve it alone:

- **Instructor:** Speak openly about where you have problems. Many companies actively support their trainees.
- **Teachers at the vocational school:** They know typical difficulties and often have additional material.
- **Support during training:** The employment agency offers support under certain conditions, for example tutoring in specialist theory. You can get information from careers advice.
- **Chambers:** Chambers of industry and commerce as well as chambers of crafts advise trainees on questions relating to training.

## Typical pitfalls

- **Only study in the evenings when you are exhausted.** Find times when you are fresh.
- **Postpone the report book.** Adding gaps lasting weeks is tedious and inaccurate.
- **Dismiss theory as unimportant.** The final exam usually has a theoretical part, and you also need it in your professional life.
- **Don't ask.** Questions are expressly welcome at work and at school.
- **Cancel free time completely.** Recovery is important in order to be able to sustain the double burden in the long term.

## Briefly asked

### How much should I learn alongside my training?

This depends on the profession, the teaching model and your status. Many people get along well with two to three short sessions per week plus a longer one on the weekend. You should plan more time before exams.

### What if my company doesn’t give me time to learn?

The vocational school period is protected by law and your company must give you leave for it. If there are problems, talk to your instructor first. If you have serious conflicts, you can contact your chamber's training advisory service.

### I'm afraid of failing the exam. What to do?

Talk to your company and the vocational school early and take advantage of the support offers.
