A nesting box in which titmice have nested usually no longer looks inviting in the fall. A thick cushion of moss, hair and feathers fills the ground, often containing remains of feces, infertile eggs or even dead young birds. Fleas, mites and other parasites also overwinter in this material and can infect the new brood next spring. That's why every nest box should be cleaned once a year.

This guide explains when the right time is, how to proceed, why cleaning products are taboo and what you should pay attention to if another animal has moved in.

## Why clean at all?

There is no one to clean up in natural tree hollows. There, fungi, beetle larvae and other decomposers ensure that old nesting material disappears over time. In addition, birds in nature do not use the same cave every year. In a nesting box, on the other hand, the material accumulates year after year. This has several consequences:

- **Parasites:** Bird fleas, mites, featherlings and the larvae of louse flies overwinter in the old nest and attack the new nestlings in spring. Severe infestations weaken the young.
- **Lack of space:** If the old nest fills half the box, the new nest will be higher. Then the distance to the entrance hole becomes smaller and cats or martens reach the breeding area more easily.
- **Moisture and mold:** Old nesting material retains moisture and the wood rots more quickly.

Some species of birds do not build in a box that already has an old nest in it.

## The right time

### In autumn

The best period is **autumn, after the end of the breeding season**, i.e. usually **from the end of September to November**. Then late breeders have also raised their young and the boxes are empty.

Those who clean earlier risk disturbing a late brood. If you clean it too late, i.e. in the depths of winter, you may disturb birds that use the box as a **roosting place** or animals that overwinter in it.

### Why not in spring?

Cleaning in February is possible if autumn was missed. But it has disadvantages: the parasites have already overwintered in the old nest, and some birds start looking for a breeding site early on. If you decide to do a late cleanse, do it as early in the year as possible and on a mild day.

## Before opening: Who lives here?

Nesting boxes are not just interesting for birds. In autumn and winter you can also meet other animals there. Therefore: **Knock first, then open carefully.**

### Dormouse and dormouse

Dormouse and dormice use nesting boxes as shelter in summer and autumn. Both species are particularly protected. If you open it and discover a ball nest made of leaves and grass or a sleeping animal, **close the box immediately** and leave it alone until spring. Then clean it when the animal has moved out.

### Bats

Sometimes bats seek shelter in bird boxes. They are also strictly protected. Close the box and leave it alone.

### Bumblebees, wasps and hornets

Bumblebees, wasps and hornets occasionally build nests in nest boxes. Their colonies die off in autumn, only the young queens overwinter elsewhere. You can therefore remove an abandoned wasp or hornet nest in **late autumn or winter**, when there will no longer be any flight operations. Hornets are particularly protected; an active nest may not be easily removed. If you are unsure, the lower nature conservation authority or a nature conservation association can help.

### Birds as sleeping guests

Tits, nuthatches and wrens like to sleep in nest boxes on winter nights. If you find a bird in the box, close it carefully and try another day, preferably at lunchtime when the birds are out and about.

## Step by step: How to clean a nesting box

### What you need

- sturdy gloves
- a dust mask, because old nesting material creates dust and can contain fungal spores and parasites
- a spatula, an old brush or a hand brush
- a garbage bag
- If necessary, a sturdy ladder and a second person to hold it

### Process

1. **Knock and wait briefly.** This is how a sleeping guest can leave the box.
2. **Open carefully.** Open the flap or roof and look inside. If there is another animal in it, close it again.
3. **Remove old nest.** Remove all nesting material and place in trash bag. Dispose of it in the residual waste or bury it away, not in the compost near the house, so that the parasites don't find their way back straight away.
4. **Sweep.** Brush the box dry. Pay attention to nooks and crannies.
5. **Scald if there is a heavy infestation.** If there were a lot of parasites, you can rinse the box with hot water and then let it dry well. But this is usually not necessary.
6. **Check.** Check whether the roof, walls and closure are still intact, whether the entrance hole has been enlarged and whether the suspension is secure. Loosen wire loops around tree trunks to prevent them from becoming ingrown.
7. **Close again.** Lock the flap securely.
8. **Make a note.** Write down which box was occupied and what you found. This way you can see over the years which locations are working well.

## What doesn't belong in the nesting box

- **No cleaning products, no disinfectants, no insect sprays.** Residue harms the birds and their young. Even remedies that are sold as natural have no place in the box.
- **No new nesting material.** Birds build their own nests. Straw or cotton wool in the box is usually thrown out again or attracts moisture. If you want to help, place nesting materials nearby in the spring.
- **No wood shavings bed** for tits. There are exceptions to special boxes for owls or kestrels, which do not build a nest themselves.

## What you can find in the old nest

When cleaning, you can find out a lot about the past breeding season:

| Find | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Moss cushion with hair and feathers | typical tit nest |
| messy ball of stalks, paper and feathers | Sparrow nest |
| Clay plastering at the entrance hole | Kleiber has reduced the size of the hole |
| unhatched eggs | Clutches were abandoned or eggs were infertile |
| dead nestlings | Lack of food, cold, death of a parent or disturbance |
| Ball nest made of leaves | Dormouse or dormouse |
| gray, papery nest | Wasps or hornets |
| bitten entry hole | Woodpecker or squirrel tried to get to the brood |

Dead nestlings are sad, but unfortunately not uncommon, especially after cold and wet spring weeks in which the parents found too few caterpillars. If a box is affected for several years in a row, it's worth taking a look at the location: Is it too hot, too drafty or too close to a source of interference? ## Cleaning as an opportunity to take stock

If you look after several boxes in the garden or in an allotment, you can use the annual cleaning to take stock. To do this, make a simple list: number or location of the box, hole size, orientation, what was found during cleaning and suspected type. After a few years you will recognize patterns. Perhaps a box on the east side of the garage is used by blue tits every year, while one on the south wall always remains empty. Such observations help you to move boxes specifically or add new ones in good locations.

Many conservation groups have been conducting such inspections in parks and forests for decades. Their data shows, for example, how the start of laying of tits shifts with milder springs. If you feel like it, you can join such a group or share your own data via reporting platforms, see [Participate in bird counts](/vogelwissen/vogelzaehlung-mitmachen/).

## How often clean?

As a rule, **one cleaning per year** in autumn is sufficient. Exceptions:

- **After one brood in early summer** you can carefully clean a box when the young have safely fledged and you want to prepare it for a second brood. However, many birds build a new nest for the second brood anyway, often in a different box. If in doubt, leave it alone.
- **Colony houses for sparrows** fill up quickly and can be checked occasionally if necessary as long as there are no brood in them.

## Health and safety

- Wear **gloves and a dust mask** when cleaning. Old nesting material can contain pathogens, fungal spores and parasites.
- **Wash your hands thoroughly afterwards.**
- Never work alone on the ladder and secure it well. Nesting boxes often hang higher than you think.
- Children can help evaluate the findings; adults should remove the nest itself.

## Legal

Wild birds, their nests and eggs are protected by law. However, removing old nesting material **after the breeding season** is expressly desired and serves to protect birds. During the breeding season, i.e. when there are eggs or young in the box, you must not disturb or remove the nest. You don't collect unhatched eggs as a souvenir, but rather dispose of them with the old nest.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I really have to clean every box?** Yes, at least all the ones that were used for breeding. Check unused boxes briefly and remove any cobwebs or leaves.

**What do I do if I can't open the box?** Then a cleaning flap is missing. The best thing to do is to install one or replace the box. The guide [Building a Nesting Box] (/vogelwissen/nistkasten-bauen-anleitung/) provides instructions.

**Can I take the box down in winter?** Better not. Many birds use nesting boxes as a place to sleep on cold nights. Leave him hanging.

**Should I hang the box in a different place after cleaning?** Only if it was not accepted last year or the breeding failed. A box that works well is best left where it is.

## Conclusion

Nest boxes should be cleaned once a year, ideally from late September to November when the breeding season is over. Knock beforehand, look inside carefully and leave dormouse, bats or sleeping birds alone. Remove the old nest completely, sweep the box dry, avoid any chemicals and check the roof, closure and suspension. This will give the birds a clean, safe place to breed next spring, and you'll also find out who lived with you last year.
