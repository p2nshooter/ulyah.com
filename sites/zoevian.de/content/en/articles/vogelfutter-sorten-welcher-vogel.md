Anyone standing in front of the bird food shelf in the garden center is spoiled for choice. Scatter food, winter food, tit mixture, wild bird food, peanuts, dumplings, fat blocks, insect mixtures. Some packs cost little, others a lot more. But not everything that is colorfully packaged is good food, and not every bird eats everything.

This guide explains the most important types of food, shows which birds prefer them, and helps you distinguish good food from bad.

## First: who eats what?

Birds have different preferences depending on their beak shape and lifestyle. A rough classification helps when shopping.

| Group of birds | Typical species | Beak | Favorite food |
|---|---|---|---|
| grain eater | Chaffinch, greenfinch, bullfinch, goldfinch, sparrows | short, thick, conical | Sunflower seeds, hemp, millet, seed mixtures |
| Omnivore with a preference for grains in winter | Great tit, blue tit, nuthatch | pointed, strong | Sunflower seeds, peanuts, fatty foods |
| Soft food eaters | Blackbirds, robins, thrushes, dunnocks, wrens | thin, pointed | Oatmeal, fruit, raisins, insects, soft food |
| Woodpeckers | Great spotted woodpecker, middle spotted woodpecker | chisel-like | Fatty food, peanuts, nuts |
| Corvids | Jay, magpie | strong | whole nuts, acorns, sunflower seeds |

If you want to attract as many species as possible, offer several types of food in different places.

## Sunflower seeds: the universal food

Sunflower seeds are the basis of almost every good diet. They are high in energy, contain a lot of fat and protein and are equally eaten by titmice, finches, sparrows, nuthatches and woodpeckers.

### Striped or black?

- **Black sunflower seeds** have a thinner shell and a higher oil content. They are easier for small birds to crack.
- **Striped kernels** are larger and have a harder shell. They are good for greenfinches, bullfinches and grosbeaks.

### Peeled or unpeeled?

- **Unpeeled kernels** are cheaper and store longer, but leave a lot of shells under the feeder. These shells slowly rot and can inhibit plant growth underneath.
- **Peeled kernels**, often sold as broken sunflower seeds, are more expensive but cleaner. No waste, no piles of shells, and even birds with weaker beaks get along well. However, they spoil more quickly and should be stored in a dry place.

## Peanuts: popular, but with rules

Peanuts are very nutritious and popular with titmice, nuthatches, woodpeckers and jays.

- **Always unsalted and unroasted.** Salt is harmful to birds.
- **Offer in dispenser or chopped.** Whole nuts can cause young birds to suffocate in the spring if parents feed them. A wire mesh dispenser with a mesh size of around six millimeters ensures that only small pieces are picked out.
- **Pay attention to quality.** Peanuts can be contaminated with mold that produces dangerous toxins called aflatoxins. Buy peanuts that are specifically intended for birds and tested for these toxins, and throw away any nuts that smell musty or are discolored.

## Grain mixtures: look closely

Ready-made mixtures are practical, but vary greatly in quality.

### Good components

- Sunflower seeds, as large a proportion as possible
- Hemp seeds
- Millet, canary seed, linseed, rapeseed
- Oatmeal
- chopped peanuts

### Less good components

- **Wheat and whole corn kernels:** are left by many garden birds, but are cheap fillers. They eat pigeons and pheasants, but hardly any small songbirds.
- **Dried peas, lentils, rice:** also fillers.
- **Red, colored grains** or cookie crumbs: unnecessary.

### Ambrosia

Seeds of the **ragweed** (*Ambrosia artemisiifolia*) pose a particular problem. This plant, introduced from North America, produces pollen that can cause very severe allergies and asthma. Their seeds sometimes end up as contaminants in cheap bird food, particularly sunflower seeds. They then germinate under the feeding area. Pay attention to signs such as “ragweed-free” or “ragweed-controlled” and check the area under the feeder in summer for unknown plants with finely pinnate leaves.

## Fatty food: energy for cold days

Fatty food consists of vegetable or animal fat mixed with grains, oat flakes and nuts. It is particularly popular with titmice, woodpeckers, nuthatches and long-tailed tits.

- **Tit balls:** buy them **without plastic net**. Birds can get their claws caught in the nets and be seriously injured. Use a metal holder.
- **Fat blocks and food bells:** practical in matching holders.
- **Homemade:** Beef tallow or coconut oil with grains and oat flakes. You can find instructions in the guide [Make your own fat balls] (/vogelwissen/fettfutter-meisenknoedel-selber-machen/).

In summer, fatty foods become soft and can become rancid. Offer it especially in the cold season.

## Soft food: for blackbirds, robins and co.

Soft food eaters can hardly crack hard grains. Suitable for them:

- **Oat flakes**, preferably soaked in a little vegetable oil so that they don't swell in your stomach
- **Raisins and sultanas**, briefly soaked in water
- **Apples and pears**, halved or in pieces, also fallen fruit
- **Wheat bran**
- **Ready-made soft food mixes** from specialist retailers, often with insects, berries and oat flakes
- **Dried insects**, for example mealworms or shrimp, soaked

Soft food is best served **on a flat feeding table or in a floor tray**, as blackbirds and robins do not like to eat from hanging dispensers. Pay particular attention to hygiene and cat safety.

## Special food

### Nigerseed

Small, black, oily seeds that are particularly popular with **goldfinches and siskins**. They are offered in special silos with very small openings.

### insect food

Dried mealworms, black soldier fly larvae or insect mixtures are a protein-rich supplement, especially for soft food eaters. Soak them in water before serving. Live mealworms are particularly popular, but should be offered in small quantities and in a smooth shell from which they cannot escape.

### Berries and fruits

Dried berries of mountain ash, elderberry or aronia are a good addition. Even better are shrubs in the garden that bear fruit themselves. The guide [Native shrubs for birds] (/vogelwissen/heimische-straeucher-fuer-voegel/) shows a selection.

## Favorite food of individual species

If you want to specifically attract certain birds, these preferences will help:

- **Goldfinch and Siskin:** Niger seed and small seeds from a silo with narrow openings, plus thistle and teasel seeds left in the garden.
- **Bulfinch:** striped and black sunflower seeds, likes to be found in a quiet feeding area near a hedge. In spring it nibbles on the buds of fruit trees.
- **Growbeak:** large, hard seeds that other birds hardly crack, such as striped sunflower seeds. He is shy and usually comes early in the morning.
- **Tailed Tit:** Fat food in small pieces, often in a group. Fine soft food containing insects is also accepted.
- **Great Spotted Woodpecker:** Blocks of fat, peanuts in the dispenser and fat food that you spread into the bark of an old trunk.
- **Nuthatch:** Sunflower seeds and peanuts, which he likes to wedge into cracks in the bark and chop up or hide for later.
- **Dunnock and Wren:** fine soft food, chopped nuts and oat flakes on the ground under bushes.
- **Fieldfare and Redwing:** Apples and fallen fruit on the lawn, especially when there is snow.

## feed throughout the year

- **Autumn:** Start with sunflower seeds and soft food, leave fallen fruit lying around, do not harvest berry bushes.
- **Winter:** Energy-rich food such as fatty food, sunflower seeds and peanuts in the dispenser. Offer a little more on frosty days.
- **Spring:** If you continue to feed, avoid whole nuts and large pieces and instead offer peeled kernels and insect food.
- **Summer:** If at all, small amounts of no fatty food, but fresh water every day. You can find the arguments for summer feeding in the guide [All-season feeding: pros and cons](/vogelwissen/ganzjahresfuetterung-pro-und-contra/).

## What doesn't belong in the food

- Bread, rolls, cakes and cookies
- Salted nuts, chips, pretzel sticks
- Sausage, bacon, cheese and leftovers
- Milk and dairy products
- Moldy, damp or musty smelling food
- Potato peels, cooked rice and noodles

The guide [mistakes when feeding birds] (/vogelwissen/fehler-beim-vogelfuettern/) explains why these foods are harmful and what other mistakes often occur.

## How to recognize good bird food

1. **Read the ingredients list:** A high proportion of sunflower seeds, hemp and millet speaks for quality. A lot of wheat and corn speak against it.
2. **Search for ragweed reference:** “ragweed free” or “tested for ragweed”.
3. **Check smell:** Fresh food smells nutty. A musty, sour or rancid smell indicates spoilage.
4. **Check appearance:** No lumps, no gray or green deposits, no moth webs.
5. **Note origin:** Reputable manufacturers indicate origin and tests.

## Store food properly

- **Dry and cool:** best in a lockable metal container or a plastic bin, not in a plastic bag in a damp basement.
- **Protect from pests:** Mice, rats and food moths love bird food.
- **Don't buy too much:** It's better to use smaller quantities that are used within a few weeks, especially for shelled kernels and peanuts.
- **Store fatty food in a cool place:** in the refrigerator or in a cool place so that it does not go rancid.

## Which food for which feeding place?

| feeding place | Matching lining |
|---|---|
| Feed silo | Sunflower seeds, grain mixture, Niger seed (special silo) |
| Peanut dispenser | unsalted peanuts |
| Dumpling or fat block holder | Fat balls without net, blocks of fat |
| Feeding table or floor bowl | Soft food, oatmeal, fruit, raisins |
| Bird feeder | Grains and mixed feed, only in daily quantities |

The guides [Feeding birds correctly](/vogelwissen/voegel-fuettern-grundlagen/) and [Keeping the feeding place clean](/vogelwissen/futterstelle-hygiene-krankheiten/) describe how to set up the feeding place and keep it clean.

## How much does good food cost?

High-quality feed is more expensive than cheap mixtures, but is often cheaper to use. Cheap mixes contain many ingredients that the birds leave around and then spoil. One kilogram of peeled sunflower seeds, on the other hand, is completely eaten. If you feed a lot, you can save with good quality large packs, but you should pay attention to storage. Many nature conservation associations sell tested feed in their shops.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I mix bird seed myself?** Yes. A simple, good mixture consists of about half of peeled sunflower seeds, plus hemp, millet, oat flakes and chopped peanuts. Only mix as much as you will use in a few weeks.

**Do all birds like sunflower seeds?** Almost all granivores and titmice. On the other hand, they rarely eat blackbirds and robins and need soft food.

**Does bird food have to be organic?** Not necessarily. Fresh, clean, ambrosia-free goods and a sensible composition are more important. However, organic feed can be a good choice if you want to pay attention to pesticides in cultivation.

## Conclusion

Good bird food is not rocket science. Sunflower seeds form the basis, supplemented by peanuts in the dispenser, fatty food without net for tits and woodpeckers and soft food for blackbirds and robins. Pay attention to a sensible composition without fillers, that it is free of ragweed and fresh, store the food in a dry place and offer it appropriately to the feeding place. So many different birds come and everyone finds what they need.
