Budgies are among the most popular pets in Germany. They are lively, sociable, curious and sometimes surprisingly talkative. At the same time, they are among the animals that are most often kept incorrectly: alone in a cage that is too small, without free flight, with a mirror as a substitute for a partner and only with grain food. A budgie that lives like this is not a happy bird.

This guide explains what budgies really need so that they can live a long, healthy and species-appropriate life.

## Where budgies come from

Budgies come from the interior of Australia, where they migrate in large flocks through dry grassy steppes and sparse forests. They travel long distances in search of water and grass seeds and are on the move all day long. This origin explains their main needs:

- **Company:** Budgies are flock birds and suffer when kept alone.
- **Exercise:** They are flying birds that need to fly a lot to stay healthy.
- **Varied diet:** In nature they eat half-ripe grass seeds, herbs and leaves, not just dry grains.
- **Light and circadian rhythm:** They are adapted to plenty of daylight and regular rest periods.

## Never alone: at least with two people

The most important rule is: **Budgies are kept at least in pairs**, preferably in a small group. No human being can replace a budgie with a companion, not even with a lot of attention. Birds kept alone often develop behavioral problems such as feather plucking, constant screaming or excessive fixation on a mirror or plastic animal.

### Which combination?

- **Pairs** of males and females usually harmonize well. To avoid constant breeding, a nesting box is not provided.
- **Two males** often get along well.
- **Two females** are sometimes bitchier, but can also work.
- **Larger groups** with an even number of birds are ideal if there is enough space.

### Male or female?

In adult budgies, the gender can usually be identified by the **wax skin** over the beak: in males it is usually **blue or purple**, in females it is **beige, brownish or light blue with a white border**. Identification is more difficult with young birds and some colors.

## The cage or aviary

### size

A cage is just a retreat and feeding station for budgies, not a place to live permanently. Nevertheless, it must be large enough so that the birds in it can at least flap their wings a few times. Animal welfare organizations and bird experts recommend a cage that is **at least about one meter long**, or more, for two budgies. The most important thing is the **length**, not the height, because birds fly horizontally. Round cages and tall, narrow towers are unsuitable.

Even better is an **room aviary** or your own **bird room**. The guide [planning an aviary and bird room] (/vogelwissen/voliere-und-vogelzimmer-planen/) explains how you can plan both.

### Furnishings

- **Natural branches** from unsprayed fruit trees, hazelnut, willow or birch in different thicknesses instead of smooth plastic rods. This trains the feet and allows the birds to gnaw the bark.
- **No sandpaper sticks**, they hurt your feet.
- **Several food and water bowls** so that no bird displaces another bird's food.
- **A bathing house or shallow bowl**, many budgies like to bathe or roll around in wet lettuce leaves.
- **Bird sand or beech wood granulate** as substrate.
- **Horizontal bars** for the birds to climb.
- **No mirror and no plastic bird**, they do not replace a partner and encourage misbehavior.

### Location

- **Bright, but not in full sun**, without drafts.
- **At eye level** so that the birds don't feel threatened from above.
- **Not in the kitchen**: Fumes from non-stick pans that are overheated are fatally toxic to birds.
- **Not next to TV or speakers.**

## Daily free flight

Budgies need **several hours of free flight each day** in a bird-safe room. This is the only way you can get the exercise you need for your health. Birds without free flight tend to become overweight, muscle wasting and bored.

### This is how you bird-proof the room

- **Close windows and doors**, make windows visible with curtains or stickers.
- **Seal cracks and gaps** behind cabinets where a bird could fall.
- **Remove poisonous plants** such as dieffenbachia, ivy, philodendron, poinsettia and oleander.
- **Secure cables** that the birds could nibble on.
- **Cover open water containers** such as vases, aquariums or toilets.
- **Avoid candles and stovetops** during free flight.
- **Keep other pets** such as cats out of the room.

### A playground outside the cage

An outdoor perch or play tree made of natural branches, with ladders, swings and snacking material, gives the birds a permanent place to go outside the cage. This way they stay there instead of sitting on curtain rods and lamps.

## Light and calm

### Daylight and UV

Normal window panes filter out a large proportion of ultraviolet light. However, birds see in the UV range and also need UV light to produce vitamin D. A special bird lamp with UV content and flicker-free light above the cage can therefore be useful, especially in winter. Get advice from an avian veterinary practice or specialist retailer.

### Night's rest

Budgies need **about 10 to 12 hours of sleep at night** in a quiet, darkened room. A small night light can help if the birds are startled at night so they can orientate themselves and not flutter around in panic.

## feed

The basis is a high-quality **grain mixture for budgies** made from millet varieties and glossy seeds. But it alone is not enough. These include:

- **Fresh food:** Vegetables such as cucumber, pepper, carrot, zucchini, broccoli, lamb's lettuce, chicory, plus herbs such as parsley, dill, basil, chickweed and dandelion.
- **Fruit** in small quantities, such as apples, pears, berries.
- **germinated food** and semi-ripe grass seeds, especially in spring and summer.
- **Footmillet** only as a treat, not in large quantities every day.
- **Grit and a source of limescale** like a cuttlebone.
- **Fresh water**, changed daily.

Poisonous or unsuitable foods include **avocado**, **chocolate**, **caffeine**, **alcohol**, **salted or spiced foods** and raw beans. The guide [feeding pet birds properly] (/vogelwissen/ziervoegel-ernaehrung/) covers the topic in detail.

## Employment

Budgies are intelligent and need variety:

- **Nibble material:** fresh twigs, willow wreaths, pieces of cork, undyed strips of paper.
- **Foraging:** Hide food in cardboard tubes, baskets or under paper so that the birds have to work for it.
- **Toys** made from natural materials, replaced regularly to keep them interesting.
- **Clicker training:** With patience, budgies can be trained in a playful way, for example to climb onto your hand or return to the cage.

## Care and health

- **Claws and beak** usually wear out on their own when dealing with natural branches and nibbles. If they get too long, a veterinary practice should trim them.
- **Daily** Renew food and water, remove droppings from perches.
- **Weekly** clean the cage thoroughly and change the substrate.
- **Health Check:** Learn what healthy budgies look like so you notice changes early. Birds hide illnesses for a very long time. More about this in the guide [Identifying a sick pet bird] (/vogelwissen/kranken-ziervogel-erkennen/).
- **Avian Veterinary Practice:** Find a practice with experience with birds before an emergency occurs.

## Merge new birds

When a new budgie is added, it should first live in **quarantine** in a separate room and be examined by a veterinarian. This way you don't introduce diseases. The birds are then slowly accustomed to each other, first in neighboring cages, then during free flight together.

## The first days in the new home

New budgies are often frightened after moving. A calm start helps them gain confidence:

1. **Set up the cage beforehand:** Food, water, natural branches and a quiet place are ready before the birds arrive.
2. **Allow yourself to rest:** Few visitors in the first few days, no loud music and no free flights. The birds should first get to know their cage as a safe place.
3. **Speak softly:** Talk quietly to the birds when changing food and water. This way they get used to your voice.
4. **Prepare for the first free flight:** After a few days, when the birds are calmly eating and grooming themselves, the first free flight can begin in a bird-safe room. Leave the cage door open so they can find their way back.
5. **Have patience:** Budgies don't become tame by holding them, but by trusting them. Hand-picked millet is a good place to start.

## Typical posture errors

- **Individually kept** with a mirror or plastic bird as a substitute partner
- **Too small, round or tall cage** with no space to fly
- **No or too few free flights**
- **Only grain food** without vegetables, herbs and sprouted food
- **Plastic rods and sandpaper** instead of natural branches
- **Cage in the kitchen** or by a drafty window
- **Not enough sleep at night**, for example by watching TV late into the night

## How old do budgies get?

Well-kept budgies can live **ten years and older**. Anyone who buys budgies is taking on a responsibility for many years and should also think about holiday care and costs for veterinary visits.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do budgies learn to talk?** Some, especially males, imitate words and sounds. But more important than speaking is that they can communicate with other dogs.

**Can a budgie fly freely into the apartment when I'm not there?** No. Free flight only under supervision in a bird-safe room or in a specially designed bird room.

**How ​​much does it cost to keep them?** In addition to purchasing the birds, cage or aviary and furnishings, there are ongoing costs for feed, fresh food, substrate, toys and, above all, visits to the vet. Have a reserve for emergencies, as bird treatments can be expensive.

**Where do I get budgies?** Many budgies are waiting for a new home in animal shelters and bird agencies, often as well-established pairs. Adoption is a good alternative to buying.

**Can I keep budgies with cockatiels?** This is possible if there is enough space and each species has at least one partner of its own species. More about this in the guide [Keeping cockatiels] (/vogelwissen/nymphensittiche-halten/).

## Conclusion

Budgies need three things above all: companionship from other birds, lots of exercise during daily free flights and a varied diet with grains, vegetables, herbs and sprouted food. A long cage or, better yet, an aviary with natural branches, a bird-safe room, sufficient light and night's sleep, activity and an avian veterinary practice complete the picture. Anyone who offers this will be rewarded with happy, healthy birds that will provide joy for many years.
