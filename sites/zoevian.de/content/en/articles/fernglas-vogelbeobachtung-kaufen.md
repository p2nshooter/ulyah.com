A good pair of binoculars changes bird watching fundamentally. Suddenly you see the fine lines on the chest of a song thrush, the yellow eye ring of the blackbird and the tiny white wing bars of a chaffinch sitting in a tree thirty meters away. Many bird species cannot be identified with certainty without binoculars. But the choice is large, and the numbers on the cases are more confusing than helpful.

This guide explains what the information means, which features are really important for bird watching and what you should pay attention to when buying. We deliberately don't mention brand names because there are good models from different manufacturers in every price range.

## What the numbers mean

Each binocular has a designation such as **8×42** or **10×32**.

- The **first number** is the **magnification**. 8x magnifying binoculars show a bird at 80 meters as big as if it were only 10 meters away.
- The **second number** is the **diameter of the objective lenses** in millimeters. Larger lenses collect more light but make the binoculars heavier.

### exit pupil

If you divide the lens diameter by the magnification, you get the **exit pupil** in millimeters. For 8×42 binoculars this is about 5.3 millimeters, for 10×42 it is about 4.2 millimeters. The larger the exit pupil, the brighter the image appears in low light and the easier it is to position the eye correctly behind the eyepiece. Values ​​of five millimeters and more are comfortable at dusk.

### Twilight number

The twilight number, the root of magnification times lens diameter, describes how well you can see details at dusk. For 8×42 it is around 18 and for 10×42 it is around 20. In practice, however, it is less meaningful than the quality of the optics.

## The most important decision: 8x or 10x?

| | 8×42 | 10×42 |
|---|---|---|
| Image size | a little smaller | a little bigger, more details |
| field of view | **bigger**, easier to find birds | smaller |
| Calmness of the image | **calmer**, less shaking | Tremors more visible |
| brightness | a little brighter | a little darker |
| Depth of field | bigger | lower |
| Suitable for | Forest, garden, hedges, moving birds, beginners | open landscape, waterfowl, birds of prey |

For most birders, especially beginners, an **8×42** is the best choice. The larger field of view makes it much easier to find a bird in the bushes and the image is calmer. Anyone who observes a lot on lakes, coasts and in open landscapes often chooses 10×42.

### More compact alternatives

- **8×32 or 10×32:** lighter and smaller, ideal for hiking and traveling. With good quality in daylight, it's hardly worse than 42 models, and a little darker at dusk.
- **8×25 or 10×25:** very small and light, fits in your jacket pocket. Fine for occasional observations, but often too dark and unsettled for serious bird watching.

### Larger models

- **8×56 or 10×50:** very bright, but heavy. More suitable for hunters and twilight observations than for long bird hikes.

## Other important properties

### field of view

The field of view indicates how wide the visible section is at a distance of 1,000 meters, for example “130 m/1,000 m”. A large field of view makes finding and tracking birds easier. With 8×42, values ​​around 130 to 150 meters are good, with 10×42 around 110 to 120 meters.

### Close focusing limit

The closest focusing distance is the shortest distance at which the binoculars can focus. For bird watching in the garden and for butterflies or dragonflies, **two meters or less** is ideal. Some cheap models only focus from five meters onwards, then you will only see the bird at the feeder in front of the window out of focus.

### Construction type: roof edge or Porro

- **Roof prism binoculars** have straight tubes, are compact, robust and are the standard today. Good models have **phase correction** for more contrast and sharpness.
- **Porro binoculars** have the typical offset shape. They often provide a good spatial image at low prices, but are larger and less often waterproof.

### Compensation

The lenses are coated with thin layers that reduce reflections and make the image brighter and more contrasty. Make sure that all glass surfaces are **completely multi-layered**.

### Waterproof and anti-fog

Bird watching takes place in all weathers. **Waterproof** binoculars filled with **nitrogen or argon** won't fog up on the inside when you go out of the warm apartment into the cold.

### Weight

A pair of 8×42 binoculars usually weighs between 600 and 850 grams. That doesn’t sound like much, but it becomes noticeable on your neck after a few hours. A wide, padded carrying strap or a **carrying harness** that distributes the weight across the shoulders can help.

## For glasses wearers

Glasses wearers should pay attention to three things:

1. **Sufficient eye relief:** The distance between the eyepiece and the eye at which the entire image is visible should be at least around 15 to 17 millimeters.
2. **Swivel or folding eyecups:** They are turned all the way in for people who wear glasses so that the glasses come close to the eyepiece.
3. **Diopter compensation:** It compensates for differences between the two eyes. Many people with mild nearsightedness or farsightedness can use binoculars without glasses and simply adjust the focus.

## Price ranges

| Price range | What you can expect |
|---|---|
| Entry level | Decent images in good light, often heavier, weaker edge sharpness, sometimes less waterproof |
| Middle class | very good image quality, waterproof, good close-up, phase correction, completely sufficient for most observers |
| Upper class | Brilliant, color-accurate images right down to the edges, best performance at dusk, very robust, long guarantee |

The difference between entry-level and middle class is often large, and that between middle and upper class is smaller. A good mid-range binocular will accompany you for many years. Used binoculars from high-quality manufacturers are also a good option if the optics are clean and the housing is tight.

## Test the binoculars before purchasing

If possible, test several models in specialist retailers or at events organized by nature conservation associations:

1. **Setting the eye relief:** Open or close the binoculars until you see a circular image.
2. **Setting the diopter:** Focus on the left image using the center drive with your right eye closed, then focus on the right image using the diopter ring with your left eye closed.
3. **Check sharpness:** A target with fine details, such as leaves or writing, should be sharp well to the edge of the image.
4. **Look for color fringes:** Look at a dark branch against a bright sky. Strong colored edges indicate a weaker appearance.
5. **Test focusing:** Focusing should be easy and smooth, without play.
6. **Check position:** Do the binoculars fit comfortably in the hand? Is it too difficult for you?

## How to use binoculars correctly

A common beginner mistake is to look for the bird with binoculars in front of your eyes. Better:

1. **Fix the bird with the naked eye.**
2. **Don't look away** and raise the binoculars in front of your eyes.
3. **Focus.**

With a little practice you will immediately have the bird in the picture. Practice this on birds sitting quietly, such as pigeons on the roof.

## Care

- First remove dust and sand with a blower or a soft brush, then clean with a clean microfiber cloth.
- Do not use household cleaners.
- After rain, let it air dry, do not place it on the heater.
- Use lens and eyepiece caps.

## And the spotting scope?

A **spotting scope** is a single-lens observation telescope with 20x to 60x magnification that is used on a tripod. It is unbeatable for waterfowl on large lakes, wading birds in the Wadden Sea and distant birds of prey. But a good pair of binoculars is enough to get you started. Many nature conservation organizations offer guided tours where you can look through spotting scopes.

## Binoculars for children

Children have smaller eye distance and smaller hands. There are lightweight binoculars for them, such as 6×30 or 8×25, which can be folded wide enough. A robust, waterproof housing is more important than the highest image quality, because children's binoculars sometimes fall down. Toy binoculars with plastic lenses, on the other hand, tend to frustrate because the image is blurry and dark. A good pair of entry-level binoculars that children take seriously can spark a lifelong passion. Teach them the “first fix your eyes, then raise the binoculars” technique early on.

## Accessories that are worth it

- **Carrying harness:** distributes the weight over the shoulders and keeps the binoculars within easy reach in front of the chest.
- **Rain cover for the eyepieces:** a cap that hangs from the strap and can be removed with one hand.
- **Binocular adapter for the smartphone:** enables receipt photos through the binoculars, so-called digiscoping. More about this in the guide [Photographing birds](/vogelwissen/voegel-fotografieren/).
- **Tripod adapter:** Binoculars can also be screwed onto a tripod for long observations at the lake.
- **Cleaning set:** Bellows, brush and microfiber cloth.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I continue to use old binoculars?** Yes, if the lenses are clean, not scratched and not foggy on the inside and both images are congruent. If you see double images, the binoculars are out of alignment and should be repaired or replaced.

**How ​​long do good binoculars last?** If treated carefully, they can last for decades. High-quality manufacturers often offer long guarantees and repair services.

**Do I need binoculars with an image stabilizer?** Stabilized binoculars provide a steady image at high magnification, but are heavy, expensive and require batteries. For most observers, a classic 8×42 is the better choice.

## Conclusion

For bird watching, **8×42 binoculars** are the best choice in most cases: large field of view, quiet image, enough light for twilight. Look for a close focusing distance of two meters or less, full multi-coating, waterproofing, and a weight you can carry for hours. People who wear glasses need sufficient eye relief and retractable eyecups. A solid mid-range model will last for many years.
