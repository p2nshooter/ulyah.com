Canaries have been faithful companions of humans for centuries. They were brought to Europe from their home on the Canary Islands as early as the 16th century, and their singing has thrilled generations of people. Today there are countless colors and shapes, from the classic yellow canary to red, white and crested breeds. But even though canaries are considered “simple” birds, they have clear needs that are not met in many households.

This guide explains how canaries can live appropriately.

## Origin: a finch from the Atlantic islands

The canary is descended from the **Canary Finch**, a small, greenish-yellow striped finch that lives on the Canary Islands, Madeira and the Azores. It is closely related to our native Girlitz. You can find out more about these finches in the guide [Identifying finches](/vogelwissen/finken-erkennen/).

In contrast to budgies and cockatiels, the canary is **not a parrot**, but a **finch**. This is reflected in their behavior: canaries are lively, nimble fliers that usually become less tame than parakeets and primarily engage with other birds of their own kind.

## Breeds at a glance

Over centuries, breeders have developed three major groups of canaries:

- **Singing Canaries:** bred for special singing performances, such as the Harzer Roller with its quiet, rolling song or the Spanish Timbrado with more metallic tones.
- **Color canaries:** in many colors from yellow to red and white to brown and gray tones.
- **Posture Canaries:** bred for particular body shapes, such as crested, ruffled feathers or upright posture.

Some extreme breeding forms have health problems, for example due to very dense or ruffled feathers. When choosing, it is worth paying attention to robust, naturally built birds.

## Don't hold alone

In nature, canaries live in **pairs and flocks**. For a long time, individual males were kept alone in small cages because of their song. From today's perspective, this is not species-appropriate. Canaries need contact with other members of their species, even if they don't bond with people as closely as parrots.

Possible combinations:

- **A pair** of male and female. Without nesting opportunities, they usually do not breed, or only rarely.
- **A group of females** that generally get along well.
- **Several males** only in very large aviaries as they can become territorial during the breeding season.

Socialization with other peaceful finch species is possible in large aviaries. As a rule, canaries should not be kept together with parakeets because of their different lifestyles and sizes.

## Space to fly

Canaries move primarily by flying, hardly climbing. Therefore, above all, they need **length** so that they can fly back and forth between the seats.

- A **long, rectangular cage or indoor aviary** is ideal. Round and high, narrow cages are unsuitable.
- The longer the better: multiple wing beats between two seats should be possible.
- **Free flight** is also good for canaries, but many prefer to stay in a large aviary because they are more shy and reluctant to be caught. A large aviary or bird room is therefore often the best solution.

The guide [planning an aviary and bird room] (/vogelwissen/voliere-und-vogelzimmer-planen/) explains how to plan an aviary.

### Facility

- **Natural branches** in different thicknesses at both ends to keep the flight path clear in the middle
- **Not too many branches and toys**, otherwise there won't be room for flying
- **Several feeding and watering points**
- **A bathhouse or shallow bowl**, as canaries love to bathe, often daily
- **Soil** made of bird sand or beech wood granulate

## Bathing

Canaries are **passionate bathers**. A bathhouse hung on the outside of the cage door or a shallow bowl of lukewarm water should be offered daily if possible, especially during moulting. Afterwards they sit up and brush themselves thoroughly. Change the bath water after every bath because it gets dirty quickly.

## feed

Canaries are grain eaters, but they need more than just grains:

- **Canary food** as a basis, a mixture of, among other things, rapeseed, shiny seed, hemp, linseed and niger seed.
- **Green food** daily: chickweed, dandelion, lamb's lettuce, chicory, cucumber, peppers and other vegetables.
- **Wild herbs and seed heads**: semi-ripe grasses, shepherd's purse, plantain, dandelion and thistle seeds, if they come from uncontaminated areas.
- **Germ feed**: particularly valuable during moulting and breeding season.
- **Egg food** in small quantities during moulting to cover the increased protein requirements.
- **Fruit** in small quantities.
- **Grit and lime**, about a cuttlefish bowl.
- **Fresh water**, changed daily.

More about feed and toxic foods can be found in the guide [Feeding pet birds properly] (/vogelwissen/ziervoegel-ernaehrung/).

### Red factor

Red canaries only retain their color if they consume sufficient **carotenoids** in their food during moulting. Without these dyes, the new feathers become paler. Whether and how colored food is used should be explained to you by experienced owners or a veterinary practice. However, the color is irrelevant to the bird's well-being.

## singing

The well-known song is mainly performed by the **males**. Females usually only chirp quietly or call. Males sing most intensely in spring. During the **molt in late summer** many people stop singing altogether. This is normal and not a sign of illness. Incidentally, a male does not sing less when he lives with other males, on the contrary.

## The moult

Once a year, usually in **late summer**, canaries change their entire plumage. The moult lasts about six to eight weeks and is strenuous for the birds. During this time:

- the birds often appear calmer and somewhat disheveled,
- the males hardly sing,
- do they need more protein, sprouted food and green fodder,
- They particularly enjoy bathing.

A molt outside of normal times or a very long molt may indicate stress, improper lighting, or illness.

## Light and calm

Canaries need a regular daily rhythm. Too long lighting in the evening can disrupt the moulting process. A room with natural daylight and slowly decreasing brightness in the evening is ideal. As with other pet birds, a bird lamp with UV content can be useful if there is little daylight in the room. At night they need peace and darkness, possibly with a weak orientation light.

## Health

Common problems with canaries include:

- **Mites**, such as the red mite, which hides in cracks during the day and sucks blood at night, or air sac mites, which cause breathing noises
- **Too long claws** if the perching branches are too thin and smooth
- **Overweight** with too high-fat food and too little exercise
- **Not laying** in females, an emergency

Learn what a healthy bird looks and behaves like and seek out an **avian veterinary practice** early on. The guide [Identifying a sick pet bird] (/vogelwissen/kranken-ziervogel-erkennen/) describes how you can recognize signs of illness.

## The first days in the new home

Canaries are skittish. A quiet move-in makes it easier for them to get used to:

1. **Prepare the aviary:** Food, water, green fodder, bathhouse and perching branches are set up before the birds arrive.
2. **Keep transport short:** Bring the birds in a small, darkened transport box and place them directly in the aviary.
3. **Provide calm:** In the first few days, no hectic movements in front of the aviary, no loud music, no other pets in the room.
4. **Observe:** Are the birds eating and drinking? Do they groom themselves? These are good signs.
5. **Keep new arrivals separately:** If there are already birds there, the new one will first be quarantined for a few weeks and examined by a vet.

## Typical posture errors

- **Solitary keeping** of a rooster “because of the song”
- **High, round or too small cages** in which flying is not possible
- **Too many perches and toys** blocking the flight path
- **Only grain feed**, without green feed and germinated feed
- **No bath** or fresh bath water too rarely
- **Location by a drafty window** or in the kitchen
- **Too long lighting** in the evening, which disrupts the annual rhythm and the moult

## Canaries and children

Canaries are not pets. They can still be a nice enrichment for families with children if it is clear that they are being watched first and foremost. Children can help with feeding, collecting green fodder and filling the bathhouse, of course under supervision. You learn to take an animal's needs seriously without having to take it into your hands. The responsibility for care, health and veterinary visits always lies with the adults.

## Prevent or accompany breeding

Canaries breed easily in captivity if they can find a nesting basket and nesting material. If you don't want to breed, don't offer nesting opportunities, keep the daily rhythm natural in pairs and don't give excessive egg food in the spring. If you want to breed, you should inform yourself thoroughly beforehand: you need space for the young birds, a concept for handing them over to good owners and knowledge about the nutrition and health of females and chicks. Responsible breeding means only breeding healthy, naturally built birds with each other.

## Life expectancy

Well-kept canaries live around **eight to twelve years**, some older.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do canaries become tame?** Some become trusting and take food from your hand, but most remain rather shy. That's normal. Their lives primarily take place among their own kind.

**Can I keep canaries outside?** This is possible in an outdoor aviary with a frost-free, draft-free shelter. Many canaries benefit from natural light and fresh air.

**Do canaries need toys?** Less than parakeets. More important are flight space, bathroom, fresh twigs and varied food that they can search for and peck.

**Where do I get canaries?** From reputable breeders who keep their birds healthy and in sufficiently large aviaries, or from animal welfare organizations. Many bird agencies are looking for new homes for surrendered canaries.

**Why doesn't my canary rooster sing?** Maybe he's moulting, too young, feeling disturbed or sick. If he also appears fluffy and dull, a visit to the vet is advisable.

## Conclusion

Canaries are finches that need companions, plenty of space to fly in a long cage or aviary, daily bathing and a varied diet with grains, forage, wild herbs and sprouted food. Anyone who supports their moulting in late summer, pays attention to a regular daily rhythm and recognizes illnesses early will be rewarded with lively birds and, from the males, with beautiful singing.
