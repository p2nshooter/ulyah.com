Homemade fatty food is one of the best ways to support birds in winter. It's high in energy, you know exactly what's in it, and it doesn't need the plastic nets that birds can get injured in. It's also a wonderful project for a gray November afternoon, even with children.

In this guide you will find a tried and tested basic recipe, variations for different birds, ideas for shapes and holders and everything you should know about storage, safety and common mistakes.

## Why fatty food?

Small birds use a lot of energy on cold winter nights. A blue tit only weighs around ten grams and has hardly any reserves. In order to survive a long frosty night, it has to absorb enough energy during the day. Fat provides more than twice as much energy per gram as carbohydrates and is therefore particularly valuable in winter.

Fatty food is especially appreciated by these birds:

- **Great Tit, Blue Tit, Coal Tit, Marsh Tit**
- **Long-tailed tit**, which often comes in groups to eat fatty food
- **nuthatch**
- **Great Spotted Woodpecker** and other woodpeckers
- **Robins, Blackbirds and Wrens** when the fatty food is offered in crumbs on the ground or on a feeding table

## Which fat is suitable?

| Fat | Suitability | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **beef tallow** | very good | traditional fatty food, becomes solid, available from the butcher |
| **Coconut fat** | good | purely plant-based, readily available, becomes hard. Make sure you use unhydrogenated coconut oil without additives |
| **Vegetable oil** | just as an addition | a small shot makes the mixture less brittle in frost |
| **Butter, margarine** | unsuitable | too soft, contain water and some salt |
| **Frying fat, bacon, lard with spices** | unsuitable | often salted and seasoned, can harm birds |

Beef tallow forms a particularly firm mass that does not soften so quickly, even at milder temperatures. Coconut oil is a good plant-based alternative. Palm fat is also solid, but is viewed critically by many conservationists due to the deforestation of rainforests for palm oil plantations.

## The basic recipe

### Ingredients for about four to five food bells

- **500 grams of beef tallow or coconut fat**
- **500 grams of feed mix**, for example:
  - 200 grams sunflower seeds (peeled or unpeeled)
  - 150 grams of oat flakes
  - 100 grams chopped, unsalted peanuts
  - 50 grams of hemp, millet or linseed
- **1-2 tablespoons of vegetable oil**, for example rapeseed oil

The mixing ratio **approximately 1:1 by weight** has proven to be effective. With more grains the mixture becomes crumbly, with more fat it stays soft and drips in the sun.

### Preparation

1. **Slowly melt the fat:** Put the fat in a pot and let it melt over low heat. It shouldn't smoke or sizzle.
2. **Let cool slightly:** Remove the pot from the heat and let the fat cool for a few minutes until it is still liquid but no longer hot. This means the oatmeal doesn't burn and the risk of injury is reduced.
3. **Stir in oil:** Add the vegetable oil.
4. **Stir in food:** Gradually stir in the grain mixture until all components are well coated with fat.
5. **Let it set:** Allow the mixture to cool, stirring occasionally, until it becomes thick and can be shaped.
6. **Fill into molds:** Pour the mixture into the desired molds, press it firmly and let it cool completely, preferably overnight in a cool place.

## Molds and holders

### Feeding bell in a clay pot

The classic. You will need small clay pots with a hole in the bottom, a wooden stick or branch about four inches longer than the pot, and a string.

1. Insert the stick through the hole so that it protrudes about five to ten centimeters from the bottom. This will be the perch later.
2. Tie a string to the top of the stick to hang the bell with.
3. Seal the inside of the hole with some putty or a small piece of kitchen paper.
4. Pour in the fat mixture and let it solidify.
5. Hang the bell with the opening facing down.

### Coconut shell

A halved, scraped coconut is a natural food bowl. Drill two or three small holes for hanging, fill the fat mixture into them and hang it with the opening slightly slanted downwards.

### Pine and spruce cones

Cones are a nice craft project, especially for children. Tie a string to the top of the cone and then roll it in the half-cooled fat mixture or spread the mixture between the scales with a spoon.

### feed strain

A piece of branch or trunk about eight to twelve centimeters in diameter, into which you drill several holes two to three centimeters in diameter and depth. Fill the holes with fat mixture. Woodpeckers and nuthatches climb along the trunk like a real tree. Hang the trunk vertically.

### Blocks of fat in shapes

Silicone molds, muffin tins or old yoghurt cups can also be filled. The cooled blocks then fit into a standard metal fat block holder.

### Fat food for sprinkling

For robins and blackbirds, you can crumble some of the mixture after it has cooled and put it in small quantities on a feeding table.

## Variants for different birds

| Variant | Additives | Particularly popular with |
|---|---|---|
| **Tit mixture** | lots of sunflower seeds, chopped peanuts | Tits, nuthatches |
| **Woodpecker mix** | coarsely chopped nuts, few seeds | Great spotted woodpecker, nuthatch |
| **Soft eater mix** | Oatmeal, soaked raisins, dried insects, berries | Robin, blackbird, wren |
| **Finch Mix** | Hemp, millet, linseed, rapeseed | Chaffinch, greenfinch, goldfinch |

## Hang correctly

- **Without net:** Plastic nets are dangerous. Birds can get their claws caught in them, and the nets often end up as waste in nature.
- **In the shade:** Fatty food becomes soft in the sun. A spot on the north side of a tree or under a roof is ideal.
- **Cat safe:** at least 1.5 meters high on a branch that cats cannot reach.
- **With cover nearby:** A bush two to three meters away gives the birds a place to escape.
- **Not too close to the window:** Less than one meter or more than five meters away reduces the risk of birds flying into the window.

You can read more about the perfect feeding place in the guide [Feeding birds correctly] (/vogelwissen/voegel-fuettern-grundlagen/).

## Storage

Homemade fatty food lasts:

- **in the fridge** well packaged for a few weeks,
- **in the freezer** for several months.

Store it in a sealed container to prevent it from absorbing odors. Only hang up as much as the birds will eat in a few days to two weeks. If the food smells rancid, is moldy or has discolored, it should be thrown in the trash.

## Safety during manufacturing

- Hot fat can cause serious burns. Heat it slowly and never over an open flame without supervision.
- Don't let children melt the fat themselves. You can help stir in and shape once the mixture has cooled.
- Do not pour leftover grease down the drain, instead let it cool down and dispose of it in the trash.

## Common mistakes

1. **Salted Ingredients:** Salted peanuts, leftover bacon or seasoned frying fat are unsuitable for birds.
2. **Fat that is too soft:** Butter or margarine does not solidify and sticks feathers together.
3. **Too much heat:** If the oat flakes fry in hot oil, they lose quality and smell burnt.
4. **Fat food in summer:** When it is warm it becomes soft, rancid and can cause feathers to stick together. Offer it especially in the cold season.
5. **Use nets:** Even for homemade dumplings, no nets, but metal holders.
6. **Too large quantities:** It's better to hang a fresh bell more often than a huge one that hangs for weeks.

The guide [Mistakes in Bird Feeding] (/vogelwissen/fehler-beim-vogelfuettern/) collects further pitfalls when feeding.

## Hygiene on fatty food

Grease food holders should also be cleaned regularly. Rinse them with hot water after each bell. If you notice sick birds at your feeder, stop feeding them for a few weeks and clean all holders thoroughly. The guide [Keeping feeding places clean] (/vogelwissen/futterstelle-hygiene-krankheiten/) describes how to keep feeding places clean overall.

## A project for the whole family

Making fatty food is a good opportunity to introduce children to the bird world. Let them put together the grain mixture, collect cones and paint clay pots, only on the outside of course and with non-toxic paints. Then you can observe together which birds come first and keep a small list. The guide [Identifying the most common garden birds] (/vogelwissen/haeufigste-gartenvoegel-erkennen/) will help you determine. Many families make it a tradition and hang the first bells on the first frosty weekend.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why don't birds come to my feeder?** New feeders are sometimes only discovered after a few days. Hang the bell near bushes where tits are already on the move and be patient.

**Can I also offer fatty food in spring?** In cool spring weeks, yes, as long as it stays solid and doesn't go rancid. As soon as it gets warm, you should avoid it.

**Can I replace beef tallow with lard?** Unsalted, unseasoned lard is generally possible, but it will be less solid. Beef tallow or coconut oil are better choices.

**How ​​many bells do I need?** For an average garden, two to three bells or blocks in different places are enough. This means that the birds are spread out and dominant great tits cannot occupy all the places.

**Are peanuts in fatty food dangerous?** Chopped, unsalted peanuts are harmless. Make sure they are fresh and bird tested.

## Conclusion

Homemade fatty food is simple, inexpensive and valuable for birds. Mix beef tallow or coconut fat in a ratio of about 1:1 with high-quality grains, oat flakes and chopped nuts, add a dash of oil and pour the mixture into clay pots, coconut shells, cones or a food log. Hang the food without a net, in the shade and away from cats, store the food in a cool place and offer it especially in the cold season. Then you will soon see titmice, nuthatches and maybe even a great spotted woodpecker at your homemade feeding bell.
