A dull thud on the window and a bird lies on the terrace. Almost everyone who has large windows or a winter garden knows this situation. Bird strikes on glass are one of the most common human-caused causes of death for birds. Estimates by state bird conservation centers suggest that there are more than 100 million dead birds per year in Germany alone. The good news: With the right measures, the risk on your own windows can be greatly reduced.

This guide explains why birds fly into glass, which solutions are proven to work, which don't, and what you can do if a bird has crashed into the glass.

## Why do birds fly into glass?

Birds don't see glass as an obstacle. There are two dangerous situations for them:

### Perspective

If you can see through a glass surface, such as a winter garden, a glazed corner of a house, a glass railing or a bus stop, the bird only sees the sky, the trees or the garden behind it. He flies towards what he thinks is a clear path.

### Reflection

Depending on the light, many windows reflect the sky, trees and bushes. To a bird this looks like a continuation of the garden. Windows that reflect trees or a hedge and modern, highly reflective thermal insulation glazing are particularly dangerous.

### Aggravating factors

- **Feeding areas two to five meters from the window:** Birds that fly up in fright have enough momentum to seriously injure themselves.
- **Plants behind or in front of the glass:** Houseplants near the window or bushes that reflect in a reflection attract birds.
- **Night Lighting:** Illuminated glass surfaces attract birds migrating at night, especially in high-rise buildings.
- **Corner situations:** Two glass surfaces at the corner are particularly tricky because you can see through both.

## Which windows are dangerous?

Check your windows from a bird's perspective. Go outside, stand in the garden and look back at the house. If you can see trees, sky or the opposite hedge in one pane, or see the garden on the other side through a pane of glass, it is risky. Also pay attention to where you have previously found bird prints on the window. These fine dust imprints with wings and head are a sure sign.

## Which is proven to help

### Flat markings

The most effective measure is **patterns on the outside of the pane** that make the glass visible to birds. What is crucial is that the markings are **tight enough**. Birds try to fly through gaps that appear large enough.

The **palm rule** has proven itself: the distances between the markings should not be larger than the palm of your hand, i.e. about ten centimeters. Specialist authorities recommend, for example:

- **vertical stripes** at least about five millimeters wide at a maximum distance of about ten centimeters
- **horizontal stripes** that need to be a little closer together
- **Dot grid** with sufficiently large dots and small spacing

It is important that the markings are attached **outside**. Patterns glued on the inside are often covered up by reflections on the outside.

There are marking tapes, foils with dot or line patterns and pens for glass available in stores. Self-painted patterns with washable window paint or soap also help temporarily.

### External privacy screen

- **Insect screens and fly protection** in front of the window break the reflection and make the glass visible.
- **External blinds, awnings and blinds** work as long as they are closed or half closed.
- **Strings or paracord curtains**, hung vertically at a distance of about ten centimeters from the window, are a cheap and effective solution that hardly disturbs the view outside.

### For new construction and renovations

Anyone building a new home or replacing windows can plan for bird-friendly glass right from the start:

- **Printed or etched glass** with dense patterns
- **Frosted, ribbed or structured glass** for winter gardens, noise barriers and balcony railings
- **Low external reflection glass**
- **Avoid glass corners** and free-standing glass walls

Many specialist bird protection agencies offer guidelines for architects and builders.

## Which helps little or not at all

### Birds of prey silhouettes

The black stickers in the shape of a flying bird of prey are widespread, but they hardly help. Birds do not recognize them as an enemy, but only as an obstacle, and simply fly next to them against the window. One or two stickers on a large area are not nearly enough. An effect is only created when a lot of silhouettes are stuck close together, but then dots or stripes work just as well and usually look better.

### Individual stickers

A butterfly or flower in the middle of the pane makes the window invisible. What is crucial is a **flat**, dense marking.

### UV markings

Films and pens, designed to be visible only in ultraviolet light, have long been promoted as an invisible solution. However, in tests, many of these products performed significantly worse than visible samples. If you want to be on the safe side, choose markings that are also visible to people.

### Curtains hung inside alone

Curtains on the inside can reduce visibility, but do not help against reflections on the outside.

## Solutions for typical situations

| situation | Recommended action |
|---|---|
| large living room window with reflection | Dot or stripe marking on the outside, insect screen, string curtain |
| Winter garden | Striped or dot film, printed or frosted glass for new buildings |
| Glass railing on the balcony | Strip film, frosted or structured glass |
| glazed corner of the house | Mark both sides flatly |
| Window with feeding place in front of it | Remove the feeding area closer than one meter or further than five meters and also mark it |
| Bus stop, noise barrier | Contact the municipality or operator about bird-friendly glass |

The following applies to feeding stations: They should either be very close to the window, so that birds hardly gain any momentum when they fly up, or far enough away, more than five meters. More about this in the guide [Feeding birds correctly](/vogelwissen/voegel-fuettern-grundlagen/).

## Step by step: making a window bird-proof

This is what you do with a typical, highly reflective living room window:

1. **Clarify the cause:** Go into the garden at different times of the day and see when and how strongly the window reflects. It is often particularly critical in the morning or late afternoon.
2. **Choose material:** For a permanent solution, marking tapes or films with a dot or stripe pattern are suitable for the outside. For a quick test, washable window paint or strips of painter's tape are sufficient.
3. **Clean the pane:** In order for films to hold, the glass must be clean, dry and free of grease.
4. **Apply the pattern:** Glue vertical strips from top to bottom, evenly spaced, no more than a hand's width apart. Start at the edge and work your way to the center.
5. **Observe:** Check in the following weeks to see if any prints still appear. If so, compact the pattern on the affected area.
6. **Check the feeding site:** If it is two to five meters away, move it.

Securing a window in this way usually takes less than an hour and will last several years with good films.

## Robin against his own reflection

Sometimes birds do not fly accidentally, but deliberately and repeatedly into a window or car mirror. It's usually robins, chaffinches or wagtails that think their reflection is a rival and want to drive it out of their territory. This can go on for days and severely exhaust the birds. What helps here is to interrupt the reflection from the outside, for example with a cloth, newspaper or foil, until the patrol time is over. A plastic bag over the exterior mirror on the car is sufficient. More about territorial behavior in the guide [Robins in the Garden](/vogelwissen/rotkehlchen-im-garten/).

## First aid after an impact

Many birds are just dazed after a collision. Others have internal injuries that are not visible from the outside.

1. **Take up carefully**, preferably with a cloth.
2. **Place in a box** with air holes on a kitchen towel.
3. **Place in a quiet, dark, not too warm place**, safe from cats and children.
4. **Wait one to two hours.** Don't keep checking.
5. **Open outside:** If the bird seems alert, take the box outside and open it near a bush. A recovered bird flies away immediately.
6. **No water or food** in the beak.
7. **If injuries** such as a hanging wing, blood, crooked head or if the bird does not fly after two hours: call the wild bird station or veterinarian.

More about helping wild birds can be found in the guide [Young bird found: what to do?] (/vogelwissen/jungvogel-gefunden-was-tun/).

## At night: lights off

Many migratory birds migrate at night and use the stars to orient themselves. Brightly lit glass facades and high-rise buildings can attract and confuse them. The same rule applies in your own house: close the curtains or lower the blinds at night, especially during the migration periods in spring and autumn. Only use outdoor lighting in the garden where it is needed and point it downwards.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to mark all windows?** No. Concentrate on the windows that birds have already hit, that reflect heavily or that you can see through.

**Don't markings look ugly?** Subtle dot or stripe patterns are hardly noticeable after a short time. Many people even see them as a design element.

**Do wind chimes in front of the window help?** They can increase the birds' attention somewhat, but do not replace flat markings.

**Will the prints on the window be wiped away?** Yes, the next time you clean the window. Take a photo first so you have proof of which pane is affected.

**Who is responsible for public glass surfaces?** For bus stops, noise barriers or public buildings, it is the municipality or the respective operator. A friendly tip with photos of bird prints often does more than you think.

## Conclusion

Birds don't see glass. They fly into windows because they mistake reflections for landscape or see through the glass. **Dense, flat markings on the outside** on the window according to the palm rule, insect screens, string curtains or external sun protection work best. Birds of prey silhouettes and individual stickers hardly help. Place feeders either very close to or far away from windows and help groggy birds with rest in a dark box. This will make your house much safer for birds.
