For many people, the blackbird is the epitome of the garden bird. Her song is as much a part of the spring evening as the scent of lilacs, and when she tilts her head on the lawn to listen for earthworms, you can't help but look. Almost two hundred years ago the blackbird was still a shy forest bird. It was only in the course of the 19th century that it began to breed in parks and gardens, and today it is at home in almost every city in Germany.

In this guide you will learn how to distinguish between males, females and young birds, how the blackbird year works, what the death of blackbirds caused by the Usutu virus is all about and how you can support blackbirds in your garden.

## Recognize blackbirds

The blackbird belongs to the thrush family. It is about 24 to 27 centimeters long and is therefore significantly larger than a sparrow but smaller than a pigeon. Its upright posture on the ground, its long tail, which it briefly lifts after landing, and its hopping locomotion are typical.

### The male

The male is **deep black** and has a **bright orange-yellow beak** and a **yellow ring around his eye**. Young males in their first year often appear dull black to dark brown, with brownish wings and a darker bill that only turns yellow as the winter progresses.

### The female

The female is **dark brown**, the throat is often slightly lighter and the breast is slightly spotted or dashed. The beak is brownish to dirty yellow. Many people think of female blackbirds as a different species, sometimes even as a thrush, which, strictly speaking, is true.

### Young birds

Newly fledged blackbirds are reddish brown and **lightly spotted** on the chest and back. They often still have a short tail and sit somewhat awkwardly in the bushes or on the lawn. At this stage they are often mistaken for song thrushes. The guide [Distinguishing thrushes](/vogelwissen/drosseln-unterscheiden/) explains how you can distinguish blackbirds from their relatives.

### Partial albinos

Occasionally you will see blackbirds with single white feathers or large white spots. This is a pigmentation disorder that occurs comparatively frequently in blackbirds. Such birds are usually healthy, but are more easily noticed by birds of prey.

## The singing

The blackbird's song is one of the most beautiful in our gardens: deep, fluting verses that sound calm and almost improvised and often end with a quieter, chirping part. The males sing from elevated places, such as roof ridges, antennae and tree tops.

- **When:** from around February to July, most intense at dawn and dusk.
- **Why:** to define territory and attract females.
- **In the city:** Blackbirds sometimes sing in illuminated streets in the middle of the night because artificial light shifts their circadian rhythm.

In addition to singing, the blackbird has striking calls. The excited, rapid **“tix-tix-tix”** or a shrill yelp is a warning call, for example when a cat sneaks through the garden. In the evening you often hear a persistent “tschink-tschink” that blackbirds use to show restlessness before going to sleep. The guide [Learn Bird Sounds](/vogelwissen/vogelstimmen-lernen/) describes how you can reliably recognize bird sounds.

## What blackbirds eat

Blackbirds are omnivores with clear preferences depending on the season.

| season | main food |
|---|---|
| Spring | Earthworms, insect larvae, snails, spiders |
| Summer | Insects, worms, first berries and cherries |
| Autumn | Berries of elderberry, rowan, hawthorn, ivy, fallen fruit |
| Winter | Berries, fallen fruit, at the feeding place oatmeal, raisins, soft food |

The hunting style on the lawn is typical: the blackbird hops a few steps, stops, tilts its head and then strikes with lightning speed. For a long time it was believed that she was listening for worms. Today it is assumed that she primarily looks, because her eyes are on the sides of her head. She also likes to dig through the leaves and throw them forcefully to the side. If you don't remove every leaf in autumn, you can offer her a set table.

At the feeding place, the blackbird prefers to feed on the ground or on a flat feeding place. She enjoys eating **oatmeal soaked in oil or fat, raisins, apples and soft food mixes**. She pecks grains less often. More about this in the guide [Bird food: which type for which bird] (/vogelwissen/vogelfutter-sorten-welcher-vogel/).

## brood and nest

Blackbirds breed early and often. Many pairs begin building nests as early as March, and two, sometimes three broods can follow into the summer.

### Where blackbirds breed

Blackbirds are not cavity nesters. They build their nest freely in dense bushes, hedges, climbing plants and conifers, often at a height of one to three meters. In cities they also use window boxes, wood piles, roof beams and window sills. A nesting box with a round hole is unsuitable for them, but they will occasionally accept a **half-cave** or an open nesting bowl on a protected house wall. More about this in the guide [types of nesting boxes and entrance holes] (/vogelwissen/nistkastentypen-einflugloch/).

### The nest

The female builds a stable shell made of grass, stalks and moss, which she reinforces on the inside with a layer of moist earth or clay and pads with fine stalks. It usually lays four to five blue-green, reddish-brown speckled eggs in the nest.

### Breeding and rearing

- **Breeding period:** about 13 to 14 days, only the female breeds.
- **Nestling time:** about 13 to 15 days. Feed both parents.
- **Brunching season:** The young leave the nest before they can properly fly. They hide in the surrounding area and are cared for by their parents, often primarily by the male, for another two to three weeks, while the female begins the next brood.

Many blackbird broods fail. Magpies, jays, cats, martens and squirrels raid nests, and long dry periods make it difficult to find food. That's why blackbirds try it several times a year.

## A young bird sits on the grass

This happens all the time in spring, especially with blackbirds: a small, spotted bird with a short tail sits in the grass and cannot fly properly. This is usually a **branch** that has left the nest completely normally and continues to be fed by its parents. He usually doesn't need any help. Keep cats away, observe from a distance, and only pick up the bird if it is sitting on a road or is clearly injured. Then you put him in the next bush. The parents find him there again. The guide [Young bird found: what to do?] (/vogelwissen/jungvogel-gefunden-was-tun/) explains this in detail.

## The Usutu virus and the blackbird die-off

Since 2011, a noticeable number of blackbirds have died in Germany in some summers. The cause is usually the **Usutu virus**, a virus originally from Africa that is transmitted by mosquitoes. It first appeared in the Upper Rhine Graben and has since spread across large parts of Germany. Blackbirds are particularly affected, but other bird species can also become ill.

### How do you recognize sick blackbirds?

Sick birds appear apathetic, sit fluffed up and no longer flee. Some stagger or have lost head feathers. Most die within a few days. Outbreaks typically occur in late summer, when there are many mosquitoes around.

### What you can do

- **Report dead or sick blackbirds:** Nature conservation groups and research institutions collect reports to track the spread. Some laboratories examine dead birds that are submitted. Only handle dead animals with gloves or a bag placed over them.
- **Reduce standing water:** Cover rain barrels, empty water in coasters regularly. This means fewer mosquitoes multiply. You should still offer a bird bath, but change the water every one or two days. Information about this can be found in the guide [Bird bath and bird bath] (/vogelwissen/vogeltraenke-vogelbad/).
- **Don't panic:** Blackbird populations have recovered in many regions following outbreaks. A garden with good nesting places and food helps.

Based on current knowledge, the Usutu virus is considered to pose a very low risk to humans. If you have any questions about health, please contact your doctor.

## Do blackbirds move away in winter?

The blackbird is a so-called **partial migrant**. Many blackbirds in Germany stay in their territory all year round, especially the males in cities. Some of the birds, especially females and young birds, migrate to southwestern Europe in autumn. At the same time, blackbirds from Scandinavia and Eastern Europe come to us to overwinter here. The blackbird that sits at your feeding place in January is not necessarily the same one that breeds in your hedge in spring. You can read more about bird migration in the guide [Migratory birds and winter guests] (/vogelwissen/zugvoegel-und-wintergaeste/).

## How to help blackbirds in the garden

### Dense hedges and bushes

Blackbirds need cover to breed. The best options are **native, dense shrubs** such as hawthorn, privet, hornbeam, wild roses or an ivy-covered wall. Do not cut back hedges radically during the breeding season. Between March 1st and September 30th, heavy pruning of hedges and bushes is prohibited under the Federal Nature Conservation Act; Only gentle shaping and care cuts are permitted, and no nest may be disturbed. The guide [Native Shrubs for Birds](/vogelwissen/heimische-straeucher-fuer-voegel/) shows which shrubs are particularly valuable.

### leaves and soil

A lawn that is not constantly mowed and beds with leaves as mulch are ideal hunting grounds for blackbirds. Here you will find worms, beetles and larvae. In the fall, leave some of the leaves under the bushes.

### Berries for fall and winter

Plants such as mountain ash, elderberry, Pfaffenhütchen, ivy, hawthorn and serviceberry provide berries until winter. If you leave fallen fruit lying around, you will often see several blackbirds and fieldfares fighting over it on frosty days.

### Water

Blackbirds bathe extensively, even in winter. A shallow bowl of clean water is a magnet for them.

### Cats and glass

Cats particularly often prey on birds that live close to the ground, such as blackbirds. Bells on the collar only help so much. It makes more sense to make feeding areas and nesting areas cat-safe. You can find tips on this in the guide [Cats and birds in the garden](/vogelwissen/katzen-und-voegel-im-garten/).

## Conclusion

The blackbird is a garden bird with character: the male is black with a yellow beak, the female is brown, and the young are spotted. It sings the most beautiful evening songs, breeds several times a year in dense hedges and looks for worms on the lawn and in the leaves. With dense native bushes, some left-behind leaves, berry bushes and a clean watering hole, you can give her everything she needs. And if a blackbird appears sick in late summer, your report will help researchers better understand the Usutu virus.
