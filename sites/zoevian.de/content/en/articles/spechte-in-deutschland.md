A loud drumming echoes through the forest, or a black and white bird suddenly hangs on the suet ball and hammers on it: woodpeckers are fascinating birds. They climb vertically on tree trunks, carve holes in the wood and play a key role in the forest because their abandoned holes are used by many other animals.

Several species of woodpeckers live in Germany, some of which regularly appear in gardens and parks. This guide will show you how to distinguish them, how they live and how you can help them.

## What makes woodpeckers so special

Woodpeckers are perfectly adapted to their life in trees:

- **Chisel Beak:** straight, strong and pointed for chopping wood and making burrows.
- **Climbing feet:** In most species, two toes point forward and two point backwards. This way they stay securely on the trunk.
- **Support tail:** The tail feathers are particularly stiff and serve as support when climbing.
- **Long Tongue:** It can be extended widely and, in many species, has barbs or a sticky surface to pull insect larvae out of burrows.
- **Shock absorption:** The skull structure, strong neck muscles and a special hyoid bone that wraps around the skull protect the brain from violent blows.

### Drumming instead of singing

Woodpeckers don't sing like songbirds. Instead, they **drum** with quick beak strokes on dry, good-sounding branches. The drumming is used to define territory and to attract mates and can be heard especially in late winter and spring. Each species has a typical drumming rhythm. This drumming has nothing to do with building caves or searching for food; The woodpecker hacks more irregularly and more quietly.

## The great spotted woodpeckers: black and white with red

### Great spotted woodpecker

The great spotted woodpecker is the most common and well-known woodpecker. It is about the size of a blackbird and is found in forests, parks and gardens with older trees.

- **Plumage:** black and white, with **large white shoulder spots** on the black back.
- **Undertail:** **bright red**.
- **Male:** with a **red spot on his neck**.
- **Female:** without red on the head.
- **Young birds:** with **red crown**, which leads to confusion with the middle spotted woodpecker.
- **Drumming:** short, very fast, about a second.
- **Call:** a sharp “kix”.

The great spotted woodpecker eats insect larvae, seeds from pine and spruce cones that it clamps in a "woodpecker forge", nuts and, in spring, occasionally eggs and nestlings of other birds. At the feeding place he loves fatty food and peanuts.

### middle woodpecker

The middle spotted woodpecker is slightly smaller than the great spotted woodpecker and lives primarily in **old oak forests**, riparian forests and orchards.

- **Crown:**red in **both sexes**.
- **Face:** appears whiter because the black cheek stripe does not extend to the beak.
- **Underside:** Flanks finely dashed, undertail **pink** instead of bright red.
- **Voice:** rarely drums; in spring its croaking, drawn-out call “gäh-gäh-gäh” is typical.

The middle spotted woodpecker usually looks for food by poking in the coarse bark of old trees rather than by hacking. It relies on old trees with rough bark and is a characteristic species of natural oak forests.

### Lesser spotted woodpecker

The lesser spotted woodpecker is the smallest native woodpecker, barely larger than a sparrow.

- **Back:** **cross-banded** black and white, without large white shoulder spots.
- **Undertail:** without red.
- **Male:** with **red head**, female with whitish head.
- **Call:** a series of high-pitched “kie-kie-kie” calls.

It often hides in the thin branches of the treetops and is therefore easily overlooked. He likes softwood species such as willow, poplar and alder, riparian forests and old orchards.

## Green woodpecker and gray woodpecker

### Green woodpecker

The green woodpecker is a large, **green** woodpecker with a **red cap**, a **black eye area** and a yellow-green rump that is conspicuous in flight. The male's beard stripe is red with a black edge, while the female's beard is completely black.

Its call is a loud, resounding **“klü-klü-klü-klü”** that sounds like a laugh and has earned it the nickname “laughing bird”. The green woodpecker is an **ant specialist**. It usually looks for food **on the ground** in lawns, meadows and pastures, where it uses its long, sticky tongue to retrieve ants and their pupae from their nests. In winter it sometimes digs deep holes in the snow to get to anthills. A garden with unsprayed lawn and ants can attract it.

### Gray woodpecker

The gray woodpecker is similar to the green woodpecker, but is smaller and has a **gray head**. Only the male has a small **red spot on his forehead**. Its call is a series of descending, slowing whistles. It is significantly rarer than the green woodpecker and lives in old deciduous and mixed forests.

## The black woodpecker

The black woodpecker is the largest woodpecker in Europe, almost as big as a crow.

- **Plumage:** completely **black**.
- **Male:** with **solid red cap** from forehead to back of head.
- **Female:** only with **red spot on the back of the head**.
- **Eyes:** bright.
- **Drumming:** very loud, powerful and lasting a good two seconds, often heard over a kilometer away.
- **Calls:** in flight a loud “krrü-krrü-krrü”, while sitting a plaintive “kliöh”.

The black woodpecker creates large burrows with oval entrances in old, thick beech and pine trees. These caves are later used by stock pigeons, jackdaws, rough owls, bats and even pine martens. That is why the black woodpecker is considered the “master builder of the forest”.

## The wryneck: a woodpecker that doesn't peck

The wryneck is an unusual woodpecker. It is **bark-colored brown-grey patterned**, well camouflaged and can twist its neck like a snake if it feels threatened. He doesn't dig holes, but uses existing tree holes and also **nesting boxes**. It is a **migratory bird** that moves to Africa in the winter and mainly eats ants. It has become rare in many regions.

## Woodpeckers in the mountains

In the Alps and in some low mountain ranges there are also the **three-toed woodpecker** with a yellow top on the male and the **white-backed woodpecker** with a white underback. Both rely on old, deadwood-rich forests.

## The woodpecker year

- **January to March:** Courtship begins. Woodpeckers drum and call, pairs find each other, and they are particularly easy to see on bare trees.
- **April and May:** Burrowing and breeding. Many species make a new cave every year, and the shavings are then fresh on the ground under the tree. The young are fed with insect larvae and can often be heard loudly coming from the cave shortly before they fly out.
- **June and July:** The young great spotted woodpeckers with their red crowns follow their parents and sometimes come with them to the feeding place.
- **Fall and Winter:** Great Spotted Woodpeckers switch to seeds and work on pine and spruce cones at their woodpecker forges. Dozens of chopped cones can often be found under such trees. Green woodpeckers dig for ants in the lawn.

A freshly made cave hole with light-colored chips underneath reveals that a family of woodpeckers is currently moving in. Then keep your distance and observe from a distance.

## Woodpeckers in the garden

### feed

It is mainly **great spotted woodpeckers** that come to the feeding area, and occasionally also medium-sized and lesser woodpeckers. You like:

- **Fat food**, for example in a food log with drilled holes
- **unsalted peanuts** in a wire dispenser
- **Walnuts** and sunflower seeds

How to build a food log can be found in the guide [Make your own fat balls] (/vogelwissen/fettfutter-meisenknoedel-selber-machen/).

### Habitat

- Leave **old trees** and **dead wood** standing as long as they are safe. Dead branches and trunks are both a source of food and a hollow tree.
- **Lawns without poison** and with ants for the green woodpecker.
- **Fruit trees** that are not excessively thinned out.

### Woodpeckers and nesting boxes

Great spotted woodpeckers sometimes enlarge the entrance holes of nest boxes to access eggs or young. A **metal protective ring** around the entrance hole or a box made of wood concrete prevents this. More about this in the guide [Nest box types and entrance hole] (/vogelwissen/nistkastentypen-einflugloch/).

## Woodpecker holes in the facade

Insulated facades with plaster often sound like rotten wood to woodpeckers. Some woodpeckers hack holes in it to search for insects, create a burrow, or drum. This is annoying, but the birds are protected and must not be injured or killed. The following are helpful:

- **Close holes quickly** before using as a den, making sure there are no animals in them.
- **Shiny, movable tape** or sheeting on affected areas to temporarily drive away the woodpecker.
- **A woodpecker box** nearby as an alternative.
- For new buildings: **hard, smooth plaster** and insulation that doesn't sound hollow.

If a cave is already occupied, it must not be closed during the breeding season. If you have any questions, the lower nature conservation authority can help.

## Watching woodpeckers

The best time for woodpecker viewing is **late winter and early spring**, when the trees are still bare and the woodpeckers are drumming and calling. Follow the drumming, look for chop marks on trunks and cones in bark cracks and pay attention to the wave-like flight. Binoculars are very helpful, see [Binoculars for bird watching](/vogelwissen/fernglas-vogelbeobachtung-kaufen/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do woodpeckers get headaches from hacking?** According to current knowledge, the structure of the skull, muscles and the short duration of the blows protect the brain so well that woodpeckers are not harmed.

**Do woodpeckers damage trees?** Healthy trees are rarely damaged. Woodpeckers primarily look for insect larvae in diseased or dead branches and are therefore more likely to be helpers in the forest.

**Why is a woodpecker drumming on my gutter?** Metal sounds particularly loud. For a woodpecker, a gutter or lamppost is an excellent tool for signaling its territory. This usually stops on its own after the courtship period.

## Conclusion

You can recognize woodpeckers by their climbing behavior, their chisel beaks and their drumming. The great spotted woodpecker is black and white with a red undertail, the middle spotted woodpecker has a red crown in both sexes, the lesser spotted woodpecker is sparrow-sized and cross-banded, the green woodpecker is green with a laughing call, the gray-headed woodpecker is grey-headed and the black woodpecker is crow-sized and black. With old trees, dead wood, unsprayed lawns and a food log, you can bring woodpeckers close by, and with a little patience you can hear their drumming from your own garden in spring.
