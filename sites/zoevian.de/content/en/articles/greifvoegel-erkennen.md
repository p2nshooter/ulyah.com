A large bird circles over the field, a small hawk appears to stand still in the air, a gray flash shoots through the garden and knocks a tit from the feeder: birds of prey are fascinating. Many people say “an eagle” about any large bird in the sky, but it is usually a common buzzard. With a little practice, the most common birds of prey can be easily distinguished, often by looking at their silhouette.

This guide introduces you to the birds of prey that you see most often in Germany and shows what you should pay attention to when identifying them.

## What is important when determining

Birds of prey are usually seen in flight, often far away and against the bright sky. Colors can then hardly be recognized. The more important are:

1. **Size:** compared to well-known birds such as crow or pigeon.
2. **Wing shape:** wide or narrow, round or pointed, fingered or closed.
3. **Tail shape:** long or short, straight, rounded or forked.
4. **Type of flight:** circling, shaking, gliding, rowing, diving.
5. **Habitat and behavior:** Does the bird sit on a fence post in the field, hunt between houses, or circle over a lake?

## Common buzzard

The common buzzard is the **most common bird of prey** in Germany. Almost every large bird of prey you see circling on a roadside post or over a field is a buzzard.

- **Size:** Slightly larger than a crow, wingspan around 1.1 to 1.3 meters.
- **Flight image:** **wide, round wings** with fingered tips, **short, broad, rounded tail**, short neck.
- **Plumage:** very variable, from almost white to dark brown. Often with a light chest band.
- **Behaviour:** often circles in thermals, sits for long periods on fence posts, trees or power poles, hunts mice in meadows and fields.
- **Call:** a meowing **“hiaa”** that you often hear before you see the bird.

## Kestrel

The kestrel is the most common falcon and lives in agricultural landscapes as well as in cities, where it nests on church towers and tall buildings.

- **Size:** about the size of a pigeon, but slimmer.
- **Flight image:** **pointed, narrow wings** and a **long tail**.
- **Typical behavior:** He **shakes**, which means he stands in the air with rapid flaps of his wings and looks for mice. No other common bird of prey does this so regularly.
- **Male:** **gray head and gray tail** with black terminal band, **reddish-brown back** with dark spots.
- **Female:** overall brown with dark banding, including on the tail.
- **Call:** a high, fast “kikikiki”.

## Sparrowhawk

The sparrowhawk is a hunter of small birds and is the bird of prey that appears most often **in the garden**, often at feeders.

- **Size:** Males smaller than a pigeon, females about the size of a pigeon. The females are significantly larger than the males.
- **Flight image:** **short, round wings** and a **long tail**, in flight an alternation of fast wing beats and short gliding phases.
- **Male:** slate gray upperside, **orange-reddish banded underside**.
- **Female:** grey-brown upperside, gray banded underside, light stripe above eyes.
- **Eyes:** yellow to orange.
- **Behavior:** hunts close over hedges and between houses in a surprise attack.

When a sparrowhawk hits a chickadee at your feeder, it's hard to see, but completely natural. Sparrowhawks are important for a healthy balance and often prey on weak or inattentive birds. Cover near the feeding place helps against targeted attacks, see [Feeding birds correctly](/vogelwissen/voegel-fuettern-grundlagen/).

## Hawk

The hawk is the sparrowhawk's large relative.

- **Size:** Males about the size of a crow, females the size of a buzzard.
- **Flight image:** Similar to the Sparrowhawk, but **bulkier**, with a broader chest, longer wings in relation to the tail and a rounded tail end.
- **Plumage:** Adult birds are gray with a finely banded, light underside and a **distinct white streak above the eyes**. Young birds brown with longitudinal stripes on the chest.
- **Habitat:** Forests, increasingly large cities with parks where it hunts pigeons and crows.

**Sparrowhawk or Goshawk?** Size is the most important characteristic, but difficult to estimate from a distance. Pay attention to the overall impression: The sparrowhawk appears delicate and has a slender tail with a rather straight end, the hawk appears powerful, “heavy” and has a rounded tail end.

## Red kite

The red kite is a particularly elegant bird of prey and a responsible species for Germany: a large part of the world population, according to estimates around half, breeds here.

- **Size:** larger than a buzzard, wingspan up to about 1.7 meters.
- **Flight image:** **long, narrow, angled wings** and a **deeply forked, rusty red tail**, which it constantly turns to steer when flying.
- **Plumage:** rusty red with a light gray head, large **white areas** on the underside of the wings.
- **Behavior:** sails low and slowly over fields and meadows, often while mowing or plowing when mice are disturbed.

## Black kite

The Black Kite is similar to the Red Kite, but is **darker brown**, and its tail is **only flatly forked**, sometimes almost straight with its feathers spread. It likes to live near water and also eats dead fish. It is a migratory bird and is with us from around March to August.

**Red Kite or Black Kite?** The deep forked tail and reddish color speak for the Red Kite, the flat-notched tail and dark plumage for the Black Kite.

## Peregrine Falcon

The peregrine falcon is the fastest bird in the world. When diving for prey, it reaches speeds of well over 200 kilometers per hour, and according to some measurements even significantly more.

- **Size:** about the size of a crow, compact.
- **Flight image:** pointed wings wide at the base, shorter tail than the kestrel.
- **Plumage:** slate gray upperside, light, cross-banded underside, a conspicuous **black “whisker” streak** under the eye.
- **Habitat:** Rocks, but also church towers, power plants and high-rise buildings in cities where it hunts pigeons.

The peregrine falcon was almost extinct in Germany due to pesticides such as DDT and persecution. Thanks to strict protective measures, he has recovered.

## More birds of prey

- **Tree Hawk:** slender, with sickle-shaped wings and **rust red “pants”**. Hunts dragonflies and swallows in flight, in summer here.
- **Marsh Harrier:** flies low over reeds and fields, wings held in a flat **V**. Male tricolor with gray wing areas.
- **Sea eagle:** huge, wingspan over two meters, with **“board-like” wings** and a **white, wedge-shaped tail** in adult birds. Especially in the north and east on lakes and coasts.
- **Osprey:** dark above, light below, plunges feet first into the water to catch fish.

## Owls are not birds of prey in the strict sense

Owls also hunt other animals and have hooked beaks and powerful talons. However, they belong to a separate group of birds and are mainly active at night. In gardens and settlements you are most likely to encounter the **Tawny Owl** with its well-known “huhu-huuu”, the **Long-eared Owl** with long feathered ears, which sometimes sleeps in groups in coniferous trees in cemeteries in winter, and the **Little Owl** in orchards. The **barn owl** with its heart-shaped, bright face breeds in barns and church towers. There are also special nesting aids for them, see [nest box types and entrance hole](/vogelwissen/nistkastentypen-einflugloch/).

## Typical observation situations

- **A large bird on a fence post by the highway:** almost always a common buzzard.
- **A bird standing in the air over an embankment:** a shaking kestrel.
- **A gray shadow darting through the garden and causing titmice to fly:** a sparrowhawk.
- **A large, reddish, fork-tailed bird over a freshly mown field:** a red kite.
- **Pigeons that suddenly scattered in panic at a church tower:** perhaps a peregrine falcon.

## Birds of prey and people

All birds of prey are strictly protected in Germany. Nevertheless, they are still illegally persecuted, poisoned or their nests destroyed. Anyone who finds a dead or injured bird of prey should inform the police or the lower nature conservation authority, especially if poisoning is suspected. Don't touch suspicious dead animals.

Many birds of prey benefit from **structured landscapes** with hedges, meadows and old trees in which they can find prey and breed. Large species such as red kites and sea eagles are particularly sensitive to wind turbines, power lines and road traffic.

## Watch birds of prey

- **Autumn** is a good time because many birds of prey migrate then. Hundreds can be seen in one day at well-known migratory points. The guide [Bird watching in Germany: the best places] (/vogelwissen/vogelbeobachtung-orte-deutschland/) provides information on good observation locations.
- **Fields being mowed** in early summer attract kites, buzzards and kestrels.
- **Winter landscapes** are good for pole buzzards and occasionally northern rough-legged hawks.
- **Binoculars** are essential. More about this in the guide [Binoculars for bird watching] (/vogelwissen/fernglas-vogelbeobachtung-kaufen/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Are there eagles in my area?** Probably not. Golden eagles live in the Alps, sea eagles mainly in the north and east. Large birds of prey above fields are usually buzzards or kites.

**Can a bird of prey attack my cat or small dog?** This practically doesn't happen. Although hawks prey on animals up to the size of rabbits, pets are not a normal prey pattern.

**Why do crows hate birds of prey?** Crows and other birds often pursue birds of prey in groups and drive them away to protect their nests. This is called “hate.”

## Conclusion

Birds of prey are primarily identified by their silhouette and behavior. Broad, round wings and a short tail belong to the common buzzard, pointed wings and shaking belong to the kestrel, short round wings and a long tail belong to the sparrowhawk and goshawk, a deeply forked rusty red tail belong to the red kite. The peregrine falcon is compact and extremely fast, the sea eagle is huge with a white tail. With these features, you'll soon realize that not every big bird in the sky is an eagle, and that's what makes observing them so exciting.
