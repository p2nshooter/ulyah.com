Anyone who feeds birds wants to help them. Nevertheless, mistakes still happen that do more harm than good to the animals. Some are obvious, like putting bread in the feeder. Others are less well known, such as a bird feeder three meters from the living room window or the plastic nets around suet balls. The good news: All of these mistakes are easy to avoid.

This guide collects the 15 most common bird feeding mistakes, explains why they are problematic, and shows you how to do it better.

## Feed errors

### 1. Bread and baked goods

Bread is probably the most common mistake of all, especially at ponds where ducks and swans are fed. Bread has little nutritional value for birds, swells in the stomach and quickly becomes moldy. Mold can cause serious respiratory illnesses in birds. Waterfowl that are fed a lot of bread also suffer from malnutrition, and the water is polluted by the leftovers. Feeding waterfowl is therefore prohibited in many cities.

**Better:** Sunflower seeds, oatmeal and high-quality feed mixes for garden birds. Waterfowl usually find enough natural food.

### 2. Salted and spiced foods

Salted peanuts, chips, pretzel sticks, bacon rinds or sausage contain a lot of salt. Birds can only tolerate salt in very small amounts; too much can damage their kidneys.

**Better:** Only offer peanuts unsalted and unroasted, not human food.

### 3. Milk and dairy products

Birds cannot digest lactose. Milk, cheese or yogurt lead to diarrhea and digestive problems.

**Better:** clean water in a shallow trough. The guide [bird bath and bird bath] (/vogelwissen/vogeltraenke-vogelbad/) explains how to set them up.

### 4. Cheap food with fillers

Cheap mixtures often consist largely of wheat, corn and other grains that small garden birds rarely eat. They stay lying around, spoil and attract rats. Some cheap mixtures also contain seeds of ragweed, a highly allergenic plant.

**Better:** Mixtures with a high proportion of sunflower seeds, hemp and millet, with a note that they are free of ragweed. The guide [Bird food: which type for which bird] (/vogelwissen/vogelfutter-sorten-welcher-vogel/) provides an overview.

### 5. Whole peanuts in breeding season

In spring, parent birds feed their young. If they feed whole peanuts or large chunks, the nestlings can suffocate.

**Better:** Only offer peanuts in a close-meshed wire dispenser from which the birds can only peck small pieces, or chopped.

### 6. Spoiled food

Damp, clumpy, musty smelling or moldy food is dangerous. For example, spoiled peanuts can contain aflatoxins, very toxic mold toxins.

**Better:** Store food in a dry and cool place, only give out small amounts and remove any leftovers regularly.

## Error at the feeding station

### 7. Fat balls in a plastic net

The colorful nets are practical but dangerous. Birds can get their claws or tongues caught in it and be seriously injured or, in the worst case, die. In addition, the nets often end up in the environment later as plastic waste.

**Better:** Buy dumplings without a net and hang them in a metal holder, or make your own fat food. The instructions [Make your own fat balls] (/vogelwissen/fettfutter-meisenknoedel-selber-machen/) show you how to do this.

### 8. The dirty feeder

In classic feeders, the birds sit in the food and defecate in it. This is how diseases such as salmonellosis or trichomoniasis spread. A dirty feeder can become a source of illness, especially if there are many visitors.

**Better:** Use feed silos in which the feed stays clean. Remove droppings and debris from open feeders every day and clean them thoroughly on a regular basis. More about this in the guide [Keeping the feeding area clean] (/vogelwissen/futterstelle-hygiene-krankheiten/).

### 9. One large feeding station instead of several small ones

If all the birds come to one place, there will be fights, the weaker birds will be left out, and disease will spread more quickly.

**Better:** several small feeding stations a few meters apart, with different types of food. This means that even shy species have a chance.

### 10. The wrong distance from the window

A bird feeder that is two to five meters in front of a large pane of glass is dangerous. Birds that fly up in fright gain enough speed to seriously injure themselves on the glass and do not recognize the glass as an obstacle.

**Better:** Place feeding stations either very close to the target, less than one meter, or more than five meters away. Make large windows additionally visible with patterns. Information on this can be found in the guide [Prevent bird strikes on glass panes] (/vogelwissen/vogelschlag-glasscheiben-verhindern/).

### 11. Feeding places within cat reach

A feeding table right next to a dense hedge or a bird feeder on a low post that a cat can jump onto becomes a trap.

**Better:** Hang feeders at least 1.5 meters high on a smooth post or thin branch, with a clear view all around and an escape route two to three meters away. More about this in the guide [Cats and birds in the garden](/vogelwissen/katzen-und-voegel-im-garten/).

## Errors in how and when

### 12. Too much food at once

A lot doesn't help much. Large amounts of food are left lying around, become wet and spoil. They also attract rats and mice.

**Better:** only give out as much food as will be eaten within one or two days. A little more on frosty days, less on mild days.

### 13. Irregular feeding during frost

Birds become accustomed to a food source. If it's suddenly empty on a freezing morning, you'll lose valuable time and energy searching.

**Better:** If you start feeding in winter, you should continue to feed reliably at least during a period of frost. If you're traveling, ask neighbors to take over the feeding station or slowly reduce feeding beforehand.

### 14. Forgot water

Many people feed but don't think about water. Birds need water all year round to drink and bathe, in summer when it is hot and in winter when everything is frozen over.

**Better:** a shallow, clean waterer with water changed daily.

### 15. Feeding instead of creating habitat

Perhaps the biggest mistake is the idea that feeding alone is enough. Feeding stations are particularly helpful for common species. Many birds whose populations are declining only benefit from better habitats: native hedgerows, wildflowers, insects, nesting sites.

**Better:** Combine feeding with a natural garden. The guides [Create a bird-friendly garden](/vogelwissen/vogelfreundlicher-garten-anlegen/) and [Native shrubs for birds](/vogelwissen/heimische-straeucher-fuer-voegel/) provide ideas for this.

## More misunderstandings

### “Feeding in summer is forbidden”

That's not true. In Germany there is no general ban on feeding garden birds in summer. Whether it makes sense is debated. The arguments are presented in the guide [All-season feeding: pros and cons](/vogelwissen/ganzjahresfuetterung-pro-und-contra/).

### “Birds become dependent on feeding”

Research shows that birds only get some of their food from feeders and continue to use natural food sources. They don't forget how to look for food.

### “Rice makes birds pop.”

A persistent myth that is known from weddings. Uncooked rice does not swell in the bird's stomach to the point where it endangers birds. Nevertheless, rice is not a particularly suitable food for garden birds, and cooked rice spoils quickly.

### “Forage on the ground is more natural”

This is true for ground feeders such as blackbirds or robins, but food on the ground becomes dirty particularly quickly and attracts rats. A flat feeding table with a roof is a good compromise.

## Special cases: waterfowl, pigeons and wild animals

### Ducks and swans

Many people feed ducks with bread at the pond, often with children. What is well-intentioned is harmful in several ways: the birds are fed incorrectly, move to one place in large numbers, leftovers rot in the water and promote algae, and rats are attracted. In many parks there are signs indicating a feeding ban. If you want to experience waterbirds, you would rather watch them than feed them. The guide [Identifying water birds] (/vogelwissen/wasservoegel-erkennen/) describes which species you see in ponds and lakes.

### City pigeons

Feeding city pigeons is also prohibited in many communities because large flocks at feeding stations lead to conflicts. If wood pigeons eat away the food for small birds in the garden, feeding silos with small openings and a protective grille around the feeding table can help.

### Squirrels, hedgehogs and mice

Squirrels like to raid food silos. That's not a drama, but if food consumption is very high, a separate feeding place with nuts for the squirrels, a little away from the bird feeders, helps. Hedgehogs should not be given bird seed, milk or fruit; There is special hedgehog food for them, and a natural garden is usually sufficient. Mice and rats come mainly where there is a lot of food on the ground.

## This is what a good feeding corner looks like

An example of how you can implement the rules in this guide: A feed silo with sunflower seeds and a metal holder with homemade fat feed hang on a smooth metal post, around 1.6 meters high and five to six meters from the living room window. There is a bush two meters away where the birds can take refuge in case of danger. A little further, in a place with a clear view, there is a small feeding table with a roof for oatmeal and raisins. The waterer is in partial shade, a few meters away from the food. All areas can be seen from the window, so you can quickly notice any dirt or sick birds.

## Checklist: How to do it right

- High-quality food without fillers, with reference to the absence of ragweed
- No bread, no leftovers, no salt, no milk
- Peanuts only unsalted, in a dispenser or chopped
- Fat balls without net, in metal holders
- Feed silos instead of open feed houses, or daily cleaning
- Several small feeding stations instead of one large one
- Distance to windows under one or over five meters
- Cat safe and with a clear view
- Only daily amounts, remove residues regularly
- Feed reliably, especially in frost
- Fresh water every day
- Additionally native plants and nesting places in the garden

## Conclusion

Most bird feeding mistakes are made with good intentions. If you pay attention to suitable food, clean feeding places, a safe place and fresh water, you really help the birds. A glance at this list at the beginning of each feeding season is enough to avoid the most important pitfalls. And if you also design a piece of garden close to nature, you give the birds something that no feeding place can replace.
