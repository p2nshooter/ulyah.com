Finches are the grain specialists among songbirds. With their powerful, cone-shaped beak they crack seeds, kernels and buds that are inaccessible to other birds. Many of them are among the most colorful birds in our gardens: the red face of the goldfinch, the pink breast of the bullfinch, the bright yellow of the chickadee. And because finches like to come to feeding places, they are particularly easy to observe.

This guide will introduce you to the finches you see most often in Germany, with their most important characteristics, their voices and tips on how to attract them to the garden.

## What defines finches

Finches have some common characteristics that will help you recognize them as a group:

- **Cone-shaped beak:** short, thick and tapered, ideal for cracking seeds.
- **Notched Tail:** In many species the tail is slightly forked at the end.
- **Wavy flight:** Finches fly in arcs, with short wing beats and periods of folding their wings.
- **Social Way of Life:** Outside of the breeding season, many species move around in groups.
- **Different genders:** In most species, males are more conspicuously colored than females.

Finches also initially feed their young with insects, and most species later feed them a porridge made from seeds.

## The chaffinch

The chaffinch is one of the most common breeding birds in Germany and can be found almost everywhere there are trees.

The **male** has a **blue-gray crown and neck**, a **reddish-brown breast and cheeks**, a brown back and a greenish rump. The most certain features are the **two white wing bars** and the white tail edges, which are noticeable in flight. The **female** is plain olive gray to grey-brown, but shows the same white wing bars.

His vocals are a powerful, blaring verse that speeds up and ends with a signature flourish. Many people also know its call, a metallic “pink” or “fink” that gave it its name. At the feeding place he likes to look for fallen kernels on the ground.

## The Brambling

The Brambling is the Nordic relative of the Chaffinch and only comes to us in **winter**. It breeds in the birch and coniferous forests of Scandinavia and Russia.

In winter plumage, the male has an **orange breast and shoulders**, a black-speckled head and a **white rump** that is clearly visible in flight. The female is paler. In years when the beech trees produce particularly large numbers of beechnuts, bramblings sometimes come to Central Europe in huge flocks that can number hundreds of thousands or even millions of birds. In the garden they often mingle with chaffinches.

## The Greenfinch

The greenfinch is a powerful, sparrow-sized finch with a **thick, light-colored beak**. The **male** is **olive green** with **bright yellow markings** on the wing edges and tail. The female is a dull gray-green with less yellow.

In spring you can often hear his drawn-out, nasal “dschuuuii”, as well as a trilling song, which he likes to sing in flight with slow, fluttering wing beats. Greenfinches often sit in one place at the feeder for a long time and crack sunflower seeds one after the other.

In many regions, greenfinches have become significantly rarer in recent years. An important cause is trichomoniasis, a parasitic disease that spreads through dirty feeding places and watering troughs. The guide [Keeping the feeding area clean] (/vogelwissen/futterstelle-hygiene-krankheiten/) explains how you can prevent this.

## The goldfinch

The goldfinch, also known as the goldfinch, is one of the most colorful birds in Europe. It has a **red face**, a **black and white head**, a light brown back and black wings with a broad, **golden yellow wing bar**. Males and females look almost identical. Young birds do not yet have a red head, but they do have yellow wing bars.

Its song is a cheerful, sparkling chirp, its call a typical “stiglit”, from which its name is derived. Goldfinches prefer to eat seeds from **thistles, teasels, burs, sunflowers and dandelions**, which they skillfully pull out of the seed heads with their pointed beaks. Anyone who leaves seed heads in the garden in autumn has a good chance of visitors. They particularly like niger seeds and small sunflower seeds at the feeder.

## The bullfinch

The bullfinch or **bullfinch** is a stocky, quiet finch with a short, very thick, black beak and a **black cap**. The **male** has a **bright pink breast** and cheeks, a gray back and a white rump. The **female** has a **gray-brown breast** but the same black cap and white rump.

Bullfinches are quiet birds. Above all, you hear their soft, wistful call, a muffled “djü”. They often live together in pairs all year round. In spring they eat buds from fruit trees and bushes, and in autumn and winter they eat seeds and berries, such as ash, mountain ash and viburnum. At the feeding place they like to come to quiet places near hedges.

## The grosbeak

The grosbeak is the largest and strongest native finch. Its huge conical beak, which is steel blue in spring, even cracks cherry and plum seeds. It has a large, orange-brown head, a gray neck, a black throat patch and striking **white wing panels** that flash in flight.

Grosbeaks are shy and often stay high in the treetops. They usually come to feeding stations early in the morning, especially if striped sunflower seeds are offered there. Their call is a sharp, metallic “zig”.

## The siskin

The siskin is a small, slender, yellow-green finch. The **male** has a **black cap** and black chin, yellow breast and cheeks and **yellow wing bars**. The female is lighter and more streaked.

Siskins breed primarily in coniferous forests. In winter they move around in groups and eat seeds from **alder and birch trees**, often hanging upside down like tits. In some winters they often come to feeding places, especially silos with niger seeds or small sunflower seeds.

## The Girlitz

The Girlitz is the smallest native finch, barely larger than a blue tit. It is **yellow with dark markings**, has a **bright yellow rump** and a very short, stubby beak. The male is brighter yellow than the female.

Its song is unmistakable: a fast, high-pitched, **clinking chirp**, reminiscent of the clinking of broken glass, and often delivered from an antenna or tree top. The Girlitz is a summer bird that returns from the south in spring and loves gardens, cemeteries and parks with loose trees.

## The bloodlinnet

The linnet tends to live in open landscapes with hedges, on the outskirts and in vineyards. In spring the **male** has a **crimson forehead and chest**, a gray head and a warm brown back. The female is streaked brown. Both show white edges on the wings and tail. His song is a varied, chirping chatter. Where there are wild herbs with seeds, it can also be observed on the outskirts of settlements.

## More finches

- **Red siskin:** small, streaked brown, with **red forehead** and black chin. Often in birch trees in winter.
- **Spruce Crossbill:** Its **crossed beak tips** are perfect for picking seeds out of spruce cones. Males are brick red, females are greenish. It breeds in coniferous forests, sometimes in the middle of winter.

## Attracting finches to the garden

### feed

Finches love **sunflower seeds**, the basis of every feeding. **Niger seed** in a special silo is ideal for goldfinches and siskins, and **striped sunflower seeds** is ideal for grosbeaks and bullfinches. Chaffinches like to forage on the ground beneath the feeder. More about this in the guide [Bird food: which type for which bird] (/vogelwissen/vogelfutter-sorten-welcher-vogel/).

### Leave seeds standing

The most natural finch food grows in the garden: seeds of sunflowers, thistles, teasels, evening primrose, dandelions, chicory and grasses. Leave the seed heads in the fall and cut them off in the spring. This means that goldfinches, linnets and greenfinches find food until the winter.

### Trees and bushes

Alders and birches attract siskins and redpolls, and beech trees attract bramblings in mast years. Hedges with hawthorn, viburnum and wild roses provide food and nesting places for bullfinches. You can find a list of plants in the guide [Native Shrubs for Birds](/vogelwissen/heimische-straeucher-fuer-voegel/).

### Hygiene

Because finches sit at feeding stations for long periods of time and are sociable, they are particularly susceptible to diseases such as trichomoniasis and salmonellosis. Clean feed silos and fresh water every day are therefore particularly important.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do I see more female chaffinches in winter?** Female chaffinches migrate south more often than males, but birds come to us from the north. The relationship can vary greatly regionally.

**Is the canary a finch?** Yes, the Canary Finch, the wild form of the canary, is a close relative of our Finch. More about this in the guide [Keeping a canary](/vogelwissen/kanarienvogel-halten/).

**How ​​do I distinguish greenfinches from greenfinches?** The greenfinch is much larger, stronger and has a thick, light-colored beak. The Girlitz is tiny, dashed and has a short, stubby beak. The guide [Confusion pairs in garden birds] (/vogelwissen/verwechslungspaare-gartenvoegel/) describes other difficult pairs.

## Conclusion

You can recognize finches by their conical beak and wavy flight. The individual species differ primarily in their color patterns: white wing bands on the chaffinch, yellow markings on the greenfinch, red face on the goldfinch, black cap and pink breast on the bullfinch, huge beak on the grosbeak, black cap and yellow bands on the siskin, bright yellow on the siskin. You can bring many of them into your garden with sunflower seeds, niger seeds, left-over seed heads and clean feeding places.
