A cage is at best a sleeping place and a feeding station for pet birds. Budgies, cockatiels and canaries only lead a species-appropriate life when they can fly, daily and extensively. The best solution for this is a large **indoor aviary**, your own **bird room** or an **outdoor aviary** with a shelter. Planning such a system is an exciting project, but many details are important: the right size, safe materials, well-thought-out furnishings and easy cleaning.

This guide will help you, step by step.

## Which solution suits you?

| Solution | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Large cage with daily free flight** | little space required, cheap | Free flights must be supervised daily and the room must be bird-safe |
| **Indoor aviary** | plenty of flight space, birds are safe even without supervision | needs space in a room, costs for construction or purchase |
| **Bird room** | maximum freedom of movement, most natural living in the apartment | An entire room is occupied, furnishings and hygiene become more complex |
| **Outdoor aviary with shelter** | Daylight, fresh air, lots of space | Protection from weather, frost and predators is necessary, please note permits |

For many owners, an **indoor aviary in combination with free flight** is a good compromise.

## The right size

The basic rule is: **as big as possible**, and above all **as long as possible**. Birds fly horizontally, not vertically. An aviary that is two meters high but only half a meter wide is of little use.

As a rough guide:

- **For two to four budgies or canaries**, an indoor aviary should be at least **two meters long** so that the birds can flap their wings several times.
- **For cockatiels** rather longer and wider due to their size and long tail.
- **The depth** should be such that the birds can turn comfortably, for parakeets at least about one meter.
- **The height** should be chosen so that you can reach or walk in comfortably. The birds prefer to sit in the upper area.

Animal protection organizations and bird associations publish recommendations on minimum dimensions. But these minimum values ​​are just the minimum. Anyone who can offer more space is doing their birds a big favor.

## Material and construction method

### Grid

- **Mesh size:** A mesh size of around **12 to 13 millimeters** is suitable for budgies, canaries and cockatiels. Larger meshes pose the risk of birds sticking their heads through and getting caught.
- **Material:** Aviary wire made of **stainless steel** or powder-coated wire without zinc or lead components, which can become toxic when gnawed. Galvanized wire should be cleaned thoroughly before use or should be avoided entirely, especially if parakeets like to chew.
- **Horizontal struts** on at least one side make climbing easier.

### frame

- **Wooden frames** made of untreated solid wood are popular, but can be gnawed by parakeets. Metal protective strips on edges help.
- **Aluminum or stainless steel frames** are durable and hygienic.
- **No painted or chemically treated wood** indoors.

### floor

- **Base tray** made of plastic or metal that can be pulled out or easily cleaned.
- **Soil:** Bird sand, beech wood granules or a mixture, changed regularly.
- For outdoor aviaries: a **solid floor** or a grid embedded under the ground so that rats and martens cannot dig in.

### Doors

- **Double door system** or lock: Especially in walk-in aviaries and bird rooms, a small antechamber prevents birds from escaping when entering.
- **Small feeding flaps** that allow bowls to be changed without opening the large door.

## Facility

### Seating

- **Natural branches** in different thicknesses from unsprayed fruit trees, hazelnut, willow, birch, maple or beech. They train the feet and provide snacking material.
- **Attach at both ends** of the aviary so that there is a **free flight path** in the middle.
- **Do not place on top of each other** to prevent birds from defecating on others.
- **Replace regularly** as soon as the branches are heavily dirty or gnawed.

### Feeding and watering places

- **Several bowls** in different places so that lower-ranking birds are not crowded out.
- **Not under seats** to prevent droppings from falling in.
- **Easily accessible** for daily cleaning.

### bathroom

A shallow bath bowl or bath house belongs in every aviary. Many birds bathe every day.

### Employment

- fresh branches with buds and leaves
- Food toy with grains hidden in it
- Ropes, ladders, swings for parakeets
- Roots, cork tubes and climbing trees

Make sure that the facility does not block the flight space.

### Plants

In outdoor aviaries and large bird rooms, non-toxic plants can provide structure and hiding places. However, many plants are nibbled. Only use plants that are considered non-toxic to birds and do your research beforehand. For example, yew, ivy, oleander, dieffenbachia, philodendron, poinsettia and many onion plants are poisonous.

## Light

### Daylight

An aviary should be **bright**, but not in full midday sun behind a glass pane, where it can overheat. A place where the birds can choose between sun and shade is ideal.

### Artificial light

Normal window panes hardly let in any ultraviolet light. For birds that see in the UV range and need UV light to produce vitamin D, a special bird lamp with flicker-free light and UV content can be useful. It is installed above the aviary, with a distance and protective grille so that the birds do not burn themselves.

### Daily rhythm

A **timer** ensures a regular day-night rhythm. A **weak night light** helps to avoid panic in the event of nighttime disturbances, especially for cockatiels. More about this in the guide [Keeping cockatiels] (/vogelwissen/nymphensittiche-halten/).

## The bird room

A dedicated bird room offers the birds the most space and freedom. When setting up:

- **Secure windows** with fly screens so that you can ventilate without birds escaping.
- **Make glass surfaces** visible with curtains or patterns.
- **Secure or route sockets and cables**.
- **No poisonous plants**, no open water containers.
- **Walls and floors** made of easy-to-clean, non-toxic materials, such as washable, solvent-free paint and moisture-proof flooring.
- **Climbing trees, outdoor seating and natural branches** in various places, with clear flight paths in between.
- **A lock** or at least a habit of only opening the door when all the birds are visible.

## The outdoor aviary

An outdoor aviary provides fresh air, natural light and rain for bathing. To be safe, she needs:

- **A shelter** that is frost-free, draft-free and dry. Budgies, cockatiels and canaries cannot tolerate long-term cold and wet conditions. Depending on the species and region, the shelter must be heated in winter.
- **A solid roof** over part of the aviary to provide protection from rain, sun and wild bird droppings, which can transmit disease.
- **Protection from predators:** Cats, martens, birds of prey and rats. Double grilles on the sides or a gap between the inner and outer grilles prevent predators from reaching the birds through the grille.
- **A safe surface** that cannot be dug into.
- **Enough shade** in summer.

Before construction, it is worth taking a look at the local building regulations and consulting with the neighbors. For larger buildings, a permit may be required.

## hygiene

A good aviary is easy to keep clean:

- **Daily:** Clean and refill food and water bowls, remove feces from seats, change bath water.
- **Weekly:** Change the substrate, clean the base tray, check the branches.
- **Monthly or as needed:** thorough cleaning of the entire aviary with hot water and a well-rinsed detergent suitable for birds.
- **Air regularly** without creating drafts.

If you keep several birds, you should also pay attention to **mites** that can hide in cracks in wooden frames.

## Build your own indoor aviary

If you are handy, you can build an indoor aviary to fit a niche or corner of a room. A proven process:

1. **Determine dimensions:** Measure the space, taking windows, radiators and sockets into account. An aviary right next to the heater dries out the air and becomes too warm.
2. **Build frame:** made of untreated squared timber or aluminum profiles, with cross braces for stability.
3. **Attach the grid:** Stretch the aviary wire with the appropriate mesh size tightly and secure it with staples or strips so that no sharp wire ends protrude inwards.
4. **Plan for doors:** a large door for cleaning and setting up, plus small flaps for food and water.
5. **Insert floor tray:** a flat tray that can be pulled out or a wipeable floor with a rim.
6. **Assemble the wheels:** The aviary can be moved for cleaning with lockable wheels.
7. **Set up:** Natural branches, bowls, bath and activity, with a free flight path in the middle.

Before moving in, everything should be thoroughly cleaned and aired out, especially if wood, glue or paint was used.

## Typical mistakes when planning

- **High instead of long:** The aviary is impressively high, but too short for flying.
- **Too much setup:** Tree branches, swings, and toys block any trajectory.
- **Incorrect mesh:** Too large mesh or wire with toxic coatings.
- **Unfavorable location:** Drafts at the window, heat at the radiator, noise next to the TV.
- **Difficult to clean:** Hard-to-reach corners and fixed floors.
- **No lock:** With walk-in aviaries, birds escape when entering.

## Costs and effort

A finished, good quality indoor aviary costs significantly more than a simple cage, but will last for many years. Building it yourself can be cheaper, but requires craftsmanship and careful selection of materials. For a bird room, the main costs are for furnishing and possible renovations. An outdoor aviary is the most complex solution, but also offers the most advantages.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I keep different species of birds in an aviary?** This is possible if the size is sufficient and the species are compatible, such as budgies and cockatiels. Canaries and parakeets should usually be kept separately. Each species needs at least one partner of its own kind.

**Do birds in a large aviary still need free flight?** A really large aviary or bird room can replace free flight. For smaller indoor aviaries, additional free flight makes sense.

**How ​​do I prevent boredom in the aviary?** Through regularly changing branches and toys, foraging, fresh twigs, bathing and, above all, being with other animals.

## Conclusion

A well-planned aviary or bird room transforms the lives of pet birds. Plan as big and, above all, as long as possible, use safe materials such as stainless steel wire with the right mesh size, set up natural branches at the ends and leave a clear flight path. Provide light, bathroom, activity and easy cleaning. Outdoor aviaries also provide a frost-free shelter and protection from predators.
