Hardly any other bird is as present at the feeder as the tits. They come quickly, grab a kernel and fly to the nearest bush to chop it up at their leisure. The two most common species, great tit and blue tit, live in almost every garden, park and forest in Germany. At first glance they look similar, but they differ in size, coloring, behavior and nesting requirements.

This guide will show you how to tell the two apart, how a tit year works and what you can do to ensure that they breed with you and get through the winter well.

## The two at a glance

| feature | Great tit | Blue tit |
|---|---|---|
| size | about 14 cm, the size of a sparrow | about 11–12 cm, significantly smaller |
| Weight | about 16-21g | about 9-12g |
| head | shiny black, white cheeks | sky blue cap, white cheeks, black eye stripe |
| chest | yellow with black longitudinal band | yellow, at most a short, dark spot |
| Wings | greenish gray with a white band | blue with a narrow white band |
| Entrance hole in the nesting box | about 32mm | about 26-28mm |
| Typical behavior | self-confident, often on the ground under the feeder | performs acrobatic exercises on thin branches and hanging food |

If you only want to pay attention to one characteristic, then head on: **Black means great tit, blue means blue tit.**

## The great tit in portrait

The great tit is the largest tit in Central Europe and one of the most common bird species. Its name has nothing to do with cabbage, but probably goes back to “coal”, i.e. the coal-black head.

### Distinguish between males and females

The gender of the great tit can often be identified. The black band on the chest is wide in the male and widens into a large black field between the legs. In the **female it is narrow** and often runs out towards the bottom. Young great tits in their first summer have yellowish cheeks instead of white and appear paler overall.

### voice

Great tits are incredibly versatile. The best known is the spring song, a bright “zi-zi-bä” or “zi-bä, zi-bä”, which can be heard even on mild January days. Many people call it the “bell call.” There are also warning calls, a scolding “tscherrr” and many other sounds. If you want to listen to bird sounds, our guide [Learn bird sounds] (/vogelwissen/vogelstimmen-lernen/) is a good place to start.

### food

In summer, great tits primarily eat insects, spiders and caterpillars. In winter they switch to seeds, nuts and berries and then particularly enjoy coming to feeding places. They are very capable of learning: In England, great tits used to learn to pick the tops of milk bottles left on doorsteps, and this knowledge spread among the birds.

## The blue tit in portrait

The blue tit is smaller, more delicate and more colorful. Their blue cap reflects ultraviolet light, which we humans cannot see but is clearly visible to titmice. Researchers assume that females pay attention to this coloring when choosing a partner.

### males and females

Both sexes of the blue tit look almost identical to our eyes. The male is usually colored a little more strongly blue, but this is hard to tell without a direct comparison. Young birds have yellowish cheeks and a more greenish-gray cap.

### Acrobat in the branches

Typical of the blue tit is its agility. It hangs upside down on the tips of branches, looks for aphids and small caterpillars in buds and pecks at reed stalks for insects. At the feeding place, she prefers hanging options such as feeding columns or dumpling holders, which are difficult for larger birds to hold on to.

### voice

The singing is a fine, high “zi-zi-zirrr” that turns into a trill. The warning call sounds similar to that of the great tit, but brighter.

## The tit year

### Winter: Together through the cold time

In autumn and winter, great and blue tits often move in mixed flocks, along with coal tits, long-tailed tits, nuthatches and goldcrests. More eyes see more dangers, and a food source is discovered more quickly. They spend the nights alone in tree hollows, wall cracks or nesting boxes. A nesting box that was cleaned in autumn is therefore also valuable in winter.

### Late winter and spring: territories and partners

From January onwards, the males begin to sing and demarcate territories. The pairs find each other and in March and April they look for a breeding hole. Both species are **cave breeders**: They need a protected cavity with a small entrance, such as a woodpecker hole, a rotten branch hole or a nesting box. We explain good nesting boxes and their correct hole size in the guide [Nesting box types and entrance hole] (/vogelwissen/nistkastentypen-einflugloch/).

### Nest building and brooding

The female builds a nest out of moss and pads the hollow with hair, feathers and animal wool. It usually lays one egg a day from mid-April onwards. Great tits often have six to twelve eggs, blue tits seven to thirteen, depending on the food supply and the age of the female. Breeding takes place for about two weeks by the female, who is fed by the male during this time.

### Rearing: Thousands of caterpillars

The most strenuous time begins after hatching. The young need soft, protein-rich food, especially caterpillars. On a good day, a pair of tits will fly to the nest several hundred times. Over the entire nesting period of almost three weeks, several thousand caterpillars come together. That's why titmice are also valuable helpers for orchardists.

Breeding is closely coordinated with the time when there are the most caterpillars, namely shortly after the leaves of oak and fruit trees emerge. If this point is pushed forward due to mild winters, the tits also have to breed earlier, which they do not always succeed in doing.

### Fly out

The young leave the nest at around 18 to 21 days, often all within a few hours. They can then flap, but cannot yet fly well, and are fed by their parents for about two weeks. If you see a young tit sitting in the bushes during this time, that is normal. You can read about when a young bird really needs help in the guide [Young bird found](/vogelwissen/jungvogel-gefunden-was-tun/).

Great tits breed a second time in good years, blue tits usually only breed once.

## How long do titmice live?

The life of a titmouse is dangerous. Many young birds do not survive their first winter because food becomes scarce, cats or sparrowhawks strike or they have accidents on window panes. Most titmice only live two to three years. However, individual ringed birds became significantly older. The population still remains stable because titmice raise many young.

## Dangers and diseases

### Lack of food during breeding season

When cold, wet weather lasts for weeks in spring, the parents find too few caterpillars. Then some or all of the nestlings often die. There's nothing you can do about the weather, but a garden with native shrubs and trees offers more insects than lawns and thuja. The guide to [native shrubs for birds](/vogelwissen/heimische-straeucher-fuer-voegel/) shows which trees are particularly valuable.

### Blue tit death

In spring 2020, people in several regions of Germany found a noticeable number of sick and dead blue tits. The birds appeared apathetic, bloated and had breathing problems. The cause was proven to be a bacterium, *Suttonella ornithocola*, which causes pneumonia. Since then, the disease has recurred in some springs. If you notice sick tits at your feeding place, you should temporarily stop feeding them, clean the feeding and watering places thoroughly and report the observation to the responsible nature conservation associations. The guide [Keeping feeding places clean] (/vogelwissen/futterstelle-hygiene-krankheiten/) explains how to keep feeding places clean.

### Cats and panes of glass

Domestic cats and glass surfaces are among the most common causes of death for garden birds. Hang nesting boxes where cats can't reach them, and make large windows visible to birds. You can find information about this in the guides [Cats and birds in the garden](/vogelwissen/katzen-und-voegel-im-garten/) and [Preventing bird strikes on glass panes](/vogelwissen/vogelschlag-glasscheiben-verhindern/).

## How to help great and blue tits

### nesting boxes

Tits are very fond of nesting boxes. An entry hole of 26 to 28 millimeters is suitable for the blue tit and 32 millimeters for the great tit. Blue tits can also use a 32-millimeter hole, but then they compete with the stronger great tit. If you want to help both species, hang two boxes with different holes some distance apart. Our [construction instructions](/vogelwissen/nistkasten-bauen-anleitung/) shows you how to build a nesting box yourself, and the guide [How to hang the nesting box correctly](/vogelwissen/nistkasten-richtig-aufhaengen/) explains where it should be hung.

### feed

Tits like to eat at the feeding place:

- Sunflower seeds, peeled or unpeeled
- Peanuts, unsalted and unroasted, preferably chopped or in a close-meshed dispenser
- Fatty food, for example homemade food bells or suet balls without a plastic net
- Oatmeal in fat

Which mixtures make sense and which mistakes you should avoid can be found in the guide [Feeding birds correctly] (/vogelwissen/voegel-fuettern-grundlagen/). The instructions [Make your own fat balls] (/vogelwissen/fettfutter-meisenknoedel-selber-machen/) explain how you can make your own fatty food.

### An insect-rich garden

The best food for titmice is insects. A garden with native trees and bushes, a corner with wildflowers, some dead wood and no insecticide is worth more to tits than any bird feeder. Especially during the breeding season, the success of a tit family depends on how many caterpillars it finds in the area. The guide [Creating a bird-friendly garden] (/vogelwissen/vogelfreundlicher-garten-anlegen/) provides ideas for the conversion.

### water

Tits also like to drink and bathe. A flat, clean bird bath is particularly helpful in hot summers and frosty days. You can read how to set them up in the guide [bird bath and bird bath] (/vogelwissen/vogeltraenke-vogelbad/).

## More tits you should know

In addition to the great and blue tits, other species of tits live in Germany and occasionally appear in gardens:

- **Coal Tit:** smallest tit, black head with large white neck spot, no yellow breast. Occurs mainly in gardens with conifers.
- **Swamp Tit:** grey-brown, small shiny black cap, black chin spot. Their call sounds like “pitschü”.
- **Willow Tit:** Confusingly similar to the Marsh Tit, with a dull cap and light wing patches. The best way to tell the difference between the two is their voice.
- **Crested Tit:** unmistakably with a pointed, black and white speckled hood. Lives mainly in coniferous forests.
- **Tailed Tit:** strictly speaking belongs to its own family. Tiny, with a very long tail, moves in family groups.

The guide about [confusing pairs in garden birds](/vogelwissen/verwechslungspaare-gartenvoegel/) explains how you can tell the difficult pairs such as marsh and willow tits apart.

## Conclusion

Great tits and blue tits are the ambassadors of the bird world in the garden. You can recognize the great tit by its black head and the longitudinal band on the yellow breast, the blue tit by its sky-blue cap and its small, acrobatic shape. Both need caves for breeding, lots of caterpillars for their young and a reliable supply of food in winter. With two suitable nesting boxes, a clean feeder and a garden full of native plants, you have a good chance of following them up close all year round.
