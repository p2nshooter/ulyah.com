The most beautiful nesting box is of no use if it is hanging in the wrong place. In May, a box in the blazing midday sun becomes an oven, one on the weather side becomes a stalactite cave, and one that a cat can reach from a branch becomes a trap. If you follow a few simple rules, you will significantly increase the chance that birds will move in and raise their young safely.

This guide explains where, how and when to hang a nesting box, how many boxes can fit in a garden and how to protect them from predators.

## The right time

### Best in autumn

Many bird conservationists recommend hanging up nesting boxes **in the fall**. This has two advantages: The birds get to know the box over the winter and often use it as a place to sleep on cold nights. And the wood loses its fresh smell by spring.

### By the end of February at the latest

If you didn't get around to it in the fall: It's usually still in time by **end of February or beginning of March**. Tits begin exploring breeding cavities in late winter. Boxes hung later are sometimes accepted for a second brood or by late-breeding species such as pied flycatchers, but the chances decrease.

## The right height

| Box type | Recommended height |
|---|---|
| Tits box (cave, 26-32 mm hole) | about 1.5 to 3.5 meters |
| Half cave for robins | about 1 to 2 meters, well hidden |
| Half cave for black redstart, spotted flycatcher | about 2 to 4 meters on buildings |
| Sparrow colony house | at least 3 meters on buildings |
| Starling box | at least 3 meters |
| Swift box | at least 5 to 6 meters, below the edge of the roof |

The height is primarily a question of safety. Higher is usually safer from cats and curious people, but the box must still be accessible with a ladder for cleaning in the fall. For most tit boxes, **two to three meters** is a good compromise.

## The correct direction

The entrance hole should face **east or southeast**. This means that the morning sun reaches the box, but the hot midday and afternoon sun does not shine directly on the front. At the same time, the hole faces away from the **weather side**, which in Germany is usually in the west, so that rain and wind don't whip in.

- **East / Southeast:** ideal
- **Northeast:** good, especially in warm regions
- **South:** only in the shade of a tree, otherwise you risk overheating
- **West / Southwest:** unfavorable, weather side

If a box is hung in a location where it gets full sun in midsummer, it can get so hot inside that young birds die. A spot in **partial shade**, such as under the canopy of a tree, is therefore particularly good.

## Slightly tilted forward

Hang the box **vertically or slightly tilted forward**, never tilted backwards. This means that rain runs off the roof and not into the entrance hole.

## Clear approach path

Birds like to fly straight to their box. There should therefore be a **free approach** of a few meters in front of the entry hole, without dense branches directly in front of the hole. However, a nearby branch on which the parents can briefly pause before slipping in is welcome, as long as it is not so close that a cat can reach the box.

## How many boxes and what spacing?

### Same kind

Many birds defend a territory against members of their species. Two tit boxes with the same hole size should therefore hang **at least about ten meters** apart. Otherwise the couples argue and often only one is used.

### Different types

Boxes for different species, such as one with a 26-millimeter hole for blue tits and one with a 32-millimeter hole for great tits, can be hung closer together because the species are less likely to perceive each other as competitors.

### Exceptions: colony breeders

House sparrows, starlings in some areas, house martins and swifts like to breed in company. For them, several breeding places can and should be located right next to each other, for example in a colony house. More about this in the guides [House sparrow: how to help sparrows](/vogelwissen/haussperling-spatzen-helfen/) and [Swallows and swifts in the house](/vogelwissen/schwalben-mauersegler-helfen/).

### Rule of thumb for the garden

In an average garden of a few hundred square meters there is usually space for **two to four boxes**: for example a box for blue tits, one for great tits, a half-cave for robins or black redstarts and, on the wall of the house, a sparrow colony house.

## Protection from robbers

### Cats

Cats climb skillfully and reach nest boxes if they are too low or are accessible via branches, fences and walls.

- **No branch, wall or fence directly under or next to the box** from which a cat can reach.
- **Smooth trunk or post:** A box on a smooth post is harder to reach than on a gnarled tree.
- **Cat repellent belt:** A ring of blunt plastic or metal rods placed around the trunk at a height of about two meters keeps cats and martens from climbing up. It must not restrict the growth of the tree and must be checked regularly.

More about this in the guide [Cats and birds in the garden](/vogelwissen/katzen-und-voegel-im-garten/).

### Martens, raccoons and squirrels

Martens and raccoons can reach through the entrance hole with their paws. A sufficiently deep box with a distance of at least 17 centimeters from the hole to the ground protects the brood. A **front plate**, an additional board with a hole in front of the actual entrance hole, extends the path even further. Squirrels and woodpeckers sometimes enlarge the entrance hole to get to eggs and young. A **metal ring** around the hole prevents this.

### Magpies and jays

They collect eggs and nestlings from open half-cavities. Half caves should therefore be well hidden in dense vegetation. With cave boxes, sufficient depth helps again. You should avoid a **perch** under the hole, as it is particularly helpful for robbers.

## Fasten correctly

### On the tree

Don't drive steel nails into trees. They can damage the wood and are dangerous for chainsaws during subsequent felling work. Better are:

- **Aluminum nails** that are soft and will not damage the saw
- a **hanging bar with wire hanger** that is hung over a branch
- a **band or wire with protective tube** around the trunk, which is checked and loosened every year to prevent it from growing in

### On buildings

You can attach boxes to house walls, under eaves or on sheds using screws and dowels. Make sure that there are no windowsills, canopies or garbage can houses underneath that can be used as a climbing aid for cats. **Built-in stones** are also possible on buildings and are integrated directly into the facade.

### On the balcony

A nesting box can also be used on a balcony if it is quiet, is not used constantly and there is greenery nearby. Hang the box as high as possible on the wall, facing east. Ask the house community or the landlord beforehand.

### In public spaces

You can't just hang boxes on trees in parks, street trees or in the forest. Ask the parks department, the municipality or the forest owner. Many municipalities and nature conservation groups have their own nesting box projects that you can take part in.

## Special locations

### fruit trees

Fruit trees are ideal locations for tit boxes. The tits eat caterpillars and aphids there in spring and thus help the fruit grower. Make sure that the box is out of the way and not disturbed during harvesting and tree pruning.

### Conifers

Coal tits and crested tits feel at home in gardens with spruce, pine or larch trees. A box with a 26 to 28 millimeter hole on a conifer is a good choice for them.

### Edge of the forest and orchard

If you have access to the edge of a forest or an orchard, you can hang up boxes for redstarts, pied flycatchers or tree sparrows after consultation with the owner. These species particularly benefit from additional breeding sites because old cave trees are missing in many places.

## What to do if the box remains empty?

Not every caste is accepted in the first year. Possible reasons:

- **Too many boxes** nearby for the same species
- **Wrong hole size** for the birds that live in your yard
- **Unfavorable place**: too sunny, too windy, too restless or too close to a feeding station
- **Too little food** in the area, for example because insects are missing
- **Predators** nearby to scare away the birds

Try another location. It is often enough to move the box to a quieter, partially shaded place. And sometimes it just takes a year for the birds to discover it. Which box types are available for which species can be found in the guide [nest box types and entry hole] (/vogelwissen/nistkastentypen-einflugloch/).

## During the breeding season

When birds have moved in, the most important thing is: **Quiet.** Don't open the box to look and don't stand directly in front of it. Breeding birds may abandon a clutch of eggs if disturbed. It is better to observe from a distance, for example from the window with binoculars. If you want to take a look inside without disturbing, you can install a small camera before the breeding season, see [Setting up a nesting box camera](/vogelwissen/nistkastenkamera-einrichten/).

## After the breeding season

In late autumn, when there are definitely no more brood in the box, it is cleaned. Old nesting material is removed to prevent parasites from accumulating. How and when this happens is described in the guide [cleaning the nest box] (/vogelwissen/nistkasten-reinigen/).

## Checklist

- Hang in autumn or by the end of February at the latest
- Height for tits: about two to three meters
- Entry hole to the east or southeast
- Partial shade, no blazing midday sun
- Vertical or slightly tilted forward
- Clear approach path
- At least ten meters away from boxes of the same type
- No branches, fences or wall ledges as a climbing aid for cats
- Fastened with aluminum nails or hanging brackets, no steel nails in the tree
- Rest during the breeding season, cleaning in late autumn

## Conclusion

A nesting box will be accepted if it is hanging safely, quietly and at a good temperature. Choose a height of two to three meters, align the entrance hole to the east or southeast, pay attention to partial shade and a clear approach path and make sure that cats and martens cannot get close. Hang it up in autumn or in February at the latest, keep enough distance from boxes of the same type and leave the birds alone during the breeding season. Then there is a good chance that you will see the first stalks in the beak of a titmouse in spring.
