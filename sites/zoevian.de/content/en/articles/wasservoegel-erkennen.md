A walk by the city pond, quarry pond or river is one of the easiest ways to see lots of birds up close. Waterfowl are usually large, conspicuous and less shy than songbirds in the bushes. Nevertheless, it is often difficult to determine: Is the black chicken with the white spot a coot or a moorhen? Is the black and white duck a tufted duck or a goldeneye duck? And why does the mallard suddenly look so different in summer?

This guide introduces you to the most common waterbirds on German waters, sorted by group and with the most important characteristics.

## Ducks

### Mallard

The mallard is the most common duck and can be found on almost every body of water.

- **Drake (male):** **bottle green head**, a narrow **white neck ring**, a brown breast and a gray body. The beak is yellow.
- **Female:** **brown speckled**, beak orange with dark spot.
- **Both:** a **blue shimmering wing panel**, the so-called mirror, with a white edge.

In summer the drakes moult into a **plain dress** and then look almost like females. Only the yellow beak gives them away. During this time they cannot fly for a few weeks. In autumn they get their magnificent plumage back.

At city ponds you can often see mallards with unusual colors, such as white or very dark birds. These are mostly mixed breeds with domestic ducks.

### Tufted duck

The tufted duck is a **diving duck** that dives for mussels, snails and insect larvae.

- **Drake:** **black with bright white flanks**, a drooping **feather** on the back of the head, **yellow eyes**.
- **Female:** dark brown, with a small crest and often a light spot at the base of the beak.

### Pochard

Also a diving duck. The drake has a **chestnut brown head**, a black chest and a **light gray body**. The female is grey-brown with a washed out head.

### Goldeneye

The drake is black and white with a dark green, triangular head and a **round white spot in front of the yellow eye**. Golden-eye ducks breed in tree hollows and come to larger lakes and rivers more often in winter.

### Teal

The smallest domestic duck. The drake has a **chestnut brown head with a green eye band** and a yellow triangle on its tail. It prefers shallow, calm waters with riparian vegetation.

## Ground ducks and diving ducks

A simple trick helps to assign ducks to a group from a distance: How do they look for their food?

- **Ground ducks** such as mallards, teals and shovelers do not completely submerge. They tip their front body into the water so that only their rear and tail protrude vertically, and thus search for plants and small animals at the bottom of shallow waters. When they fly up, they jump almost vertically out of the water.
- **Diving ducks** such as egrets, pochards and goldeneyes dive completely, often several meters deep, and remain missing for up to half a minute or longer. To fly up, they need a short run-up, during which they run over the water. They lie deeper in the water than bottom ducks.
- **Mergansers** such as the goosander are also diving ducks, but have a long, narrow bill with saw-like horn teeth that they use to hold fish.

Anyone who knows this difference narrows down the possibilities at first glance.

## Geese and swans

### Gray goose

The **gray goose** is the ancestral form of the domestic goose: grey-brown, with an **orange beak** and pink legs. It breeds in many lakes and parks and is often not very shy.

### Canada goose and Egyptian goose

Two geese that are not originally from Europe but have spread here:

- **Canada Goose:** large, with **black neck and head** and a **white chin band**.
- **Egyptian goose:** actually a half-goose, beige-brown with a **dark eye spot** and large white wing areas in flight. She is often very territorial.

### Mute Swan

The **mute swan** is the well-known white park swan with an **orange bill and a black hump** at the base of the bill. Young swans are gray-brown. Mute swans defend their nest and their young very determinedly; keep your distance during the breeding season.

In winter, **whooper swans** from the north come to some regions and have a **yellow-black beak** without humps.

## Rails: Coot and Moorhen

### Coot

The coot is a rounded, **all black** water bird with a **white bill and white forehead plate**, the “pale”. It dives for aquatic plants and has broad lobes rather than webbed toes. Coots are very belligerent and often engage in loud chases across the water.

### Moorhen

The moorhen is slightly smaller and more elegant. It has a **red forehead plate with a yellow beak tip**, a **white line on its flanks**, and white undertail feathers that it constantly flips up while swimming. It likes to stay on the banks and in the vegetation and often runs across meadows near the water.

| feature | Coot | Moorhen |
|---|---|---|
| forehead and beak | **white** | **red with yellow tip** |
| Flanks | black | **white line** |
| behavior | open water, diving | Shore, vegetation, wags its tail |

## grebe

### Great Crested Grebe

The grebe is an elegant diver with a long neck, a pointed beak and a **black double hood** and **red-brown feather collar**. In spring, pairs perform a famous **courtship**: they swim opposite each other, shake their heads, dive for plants, and stand up chest to chest with the plants in their beaks. Young grebes are striped in black and white and often ride on their parents' backs.

### Little Grebe

The little grebe is small, rounded and dark in summer with **red-brown cheek** and a light spot at the base of the beak. It dives quickly and is often difficult to find. Its call is a loud, neighing trill.

## Herons and cormorants

### Gray heron

The great blue heron is large, **grey**, with a long neck, long legs and a **dagger-like beak**. It often stands motionless on the bank or in meadows and waits for fish, frogs or mice. When flying, it draws its neck in an S-shape, whereas storks fly with their necks stretched.

### Great Egret

The **Great Egret** is a large, **all white** heron that has become much more common in Germany in recent decades, especially in winter. It is about the same size as the gray heron and is often found in fields and meadows.

### Cormorant

The cormorant is a large, dark water bird with a hooked beak. It is typical to see him sitting on a post or branch with **wings spread** after diving to dry his feathers. Cormorants breed in colonies and are controversial among fishermen, but they are part of the natural bird life on water.

## More species on the water

### Kingfisher

A small bird with **bright blue-turquoise back** and **orange underside**. Most of the time you only see it as a blue flash flying close over the water and hear its high-pitched whistle. He sits on branches above the water and dives head first for small fish. It breeds in tubes that it digs into steep bank walls.

### Black-headed gull

The most common seagull inland. In summer she has a **chocolate brown head**, in winter she only has a **dark spot behind her eye**. Beak and legs are red. Black-headed gulls often follow plows in fields and are common in cities near bodies of water.

### Gray Wagtail and Wagtail

The **mountain wagtail** bobs along streams and rivers with its particularly long tail. It has a **yellow underside** and a gray back. The **wagtail** is black, white and gray and can also be found far from water.

## Watch waterfowl

- **Winter** is the best time: many ducks, geese and grebes from the north spend the winter on our lakes and rivers, and the drakes wear their magnificent plumage.
- **Quiet morning hours** are ideal before there are a lot of walkers out and about.
- **Binoculars** help identify ducks from a greater distance. The guide [Binoculars for bird watching] (/vogelwissen/fernglas-vogelbeobachtung-kaufen/) gives tips on choosing.
- **Keep your distance:** Waterfowl should not be frightened, especially during the breeding season and when there is frost. Every escape costs her energy.

The guide [Bird watching in Germany: the best places] (/vogelwissen/vogelbeobachtung-orte-deutschland/) presents good places for water bird watching.

## Please do not feed bread

At many ponds, ducks and swans are fed bread. This is well-intentioned, but it is harmful: bread is unhealthy for waterfowl, leftovers rot in the water and promote algae growth and a lack of oxygen, and large gatherings of birds spread disease. Feeding waterfowl is therefore prohibited in many cities. More about this in the guide [Mistakes when feeding birds] (/vogelwissen/fehler-beim-vogelfuettern/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do all female ducks look so similar?** Females breed alone and must be well camouflaged at the nest. The color of the beak, the shape of the head and the wing area, which is colored differently in each species, help to determine this.

**What should I do if I see a duck with ducklings on the road?** Back up traffic if it's safe to do so and let the family go on their way. Do not catch the chicks or they will be separated from their mother.

**Why do herons stand still for so long?** Gray herons are perched hunters. They wait motionless until a fish or frog comes within range and then strike with lightning speed. A heron that doesn't seem to breathe for a quarter of an hour is highly concentrated.

**How ​​can I tell if a swan is becoming aggressive?** A mute swan swimming towards you with its wings arched and its neck arched is defending its territory. Then go back a little.

**Are Canada goose and Egyptian goose a problem?** They are alien species that have expanded rapidly. In some regions they compete with native species or pollute bathing areas. Their dealings are regulated by authorities.

## Conclusion

Many species of birds can be observed up close in ponds and lakes. You can recognize the mallard by the green head of the drake and the blue field of wings, the tufted duck by its crest and white flanks, the coot by its white forehead, the moorhen by its red forehead and bobbing tail, the great crested grebe by its feathered hood, the gray heron by its S-shaped neck in flight and the cormorant by its outstretched wings. With binoculars, a little patience and consideration, every walk along the water becomes a little journey of discovery.
