Hardly any other topic causes as much controversy among garden neighbors as cats and birds. For some, their own cat is a family member that naturally roams around outside. For others, it is the reason why the blackbird brood in the hedge disappears every year. Both sides have their reasons, and the good news is that there are many ways to significantly reduce the risk to birds without cats or their owners suffering.

This guide breaks down the problem, shows what cat owners can do, and explains how to design your yard to be safer for birds.

## How big is the problem?

There are many millions of domestic cats living in Germany, as well as feral domestic cats without owners. Cats are also well fed and hunters. The hunting instinct is innate and does not depend on hunger. How many birds prey on cats in total can only be roughly estimated because many prey animals are never brought home. However, experts agree that domestic cats in urban areas are a significant factor in bird losses, especially for young birds and species that live close to the ground.

Those particularly at risk are:

- **Branches**, i.e. young birds that have left the nest but cannot yet fly well
- **ground-breeding species** such as robins, wrens and dunnocks
- **Birds at feeders and waterers** that are distracted
- **bathing birds** that fly more poorly with wet feathers

At the same time, cats are not the only and usually not the main cause of the decline of many bird species. Loss of habitat, lack of insects and glass surfaces play a major role. Still, the problem is real, and every bird that isn't captured counts.

## What cat owners can do

### 1. Leave indoors in the morning during breeding season

The most effective measure is simple: keep your cat indoors **from around May to July in the early morning hours**. At this time most young birds are out and about and the parents feed particularly intensively. Cats also like to hunt at dusk and at night.

### 2. Play daily

Research from the UK suggests that **daily hunting games** with the cat, such as with a toy rod or feather feather, along with protein-rich food can reduce the number of prey animals brought home. A few minutes of play a day is also good for the cat.

### 3. A colorful ruff

A **strikingly colored fabric ruff** over the collar makes the cat more visible to birds. Birds see colors very well and react to the colorful signal. In studies, such ruffs have significantly reduced the number of birds captured without significantly disturbing the cat. It is important to have a safety collar that opens if the cat gets caught somewhere.

### 4. Bell?

Bells on the collar can help, but the effect is limited. Many cats learn to move in such a way that the bell barely rings. Bells also warn adult birds in particular, but not young birds that cannot yet escape. If you use a bell, combine it with other measures.

### 5. Neuter, tag, register

Neutered cats roam less. Many cities and municipalities have **cat protection ordinances** that require castration, identification and registration for outdoor cats. This not only protects birds, but also prevents the misery of feral cats.

### 6. Secured freewheel

A **cat-safe fenced garden**, a cat net on the balcony or an outdoor enclosure offer the cat fresh air without being able to chase birds in the neighborhood. The following applies to indoor cats: activities, scratching posts and window seats with a view make life indoors varied.

## What bird lovers can do in the garden

### Place feeding stations safely

- **Hang high:** at least 1.5 meters above the ground, on a smooth post or thin branch that a cat cannot jump onto.
- **Clear view:** not directly next to a dense hedge where a cat can lurk. A distance of two to three meters from the nearest cover is ideal.
- **No food on the floor:** Floor feeding places are particularly easy for cats to reach. If you want to feed blackbirds and robins, use a raised feeding table.

More about this in the guide [Feeding birds correctly](/vogelwissen/voegel-fuettern-grundlagen/).

### Protect nesting boxes

- **Height:** Tits boxes about two to three meters high.
- **No climbing aids:** no branch, fence, wall ledge or window sill directly under or next to the box.
- **Deep boxes:** At least 17 centimeters from the entrance hole to the ground so that a cat's paw cannot reach the nest.
- **Cat repellent belt:** A ring of blunt plastic rods around the trunk at a height of around two meters prevents climbing. It must not grow in and is checked annually.

The guide [Hanging the nesting box correctly] (/vogelwissen/nistkasten-richtig-aufhaengen/) provides tips on the correct location.

### Plant thorny bushes

Dense, thorny bushes such as hawthorn, blackthorn, wild roses and barberry are difficult for cats to access and provide safe nesting sites for free-nesting birds such as blackbirds. Thorny planting can also serve as a natural barrier around nesting boxes and feeding areas. You can find a selection in the guide [Native shrubs for birds] (/vogelwissen/heimische-straeucher-fuer-voegel/).

### Set up drinking troughs safely

Bathing birds are particularly vulnerable. Place bird baths with a **clear view all around**, preferably elevated on a base and not next to dense vegetation. More about this in the guide [bird bath and bird bath](/vogelwissen/vogeltraenke-vogelbad/).

### Keep strange cats away

If neighborhood cats regularly hunt in your garden, a few gentle measures can help:

- **Water Sprinkler with Motion Sensor:** Cats don't like a sudden shower. These devices are harmless and work well for many cats.
- **Rough or spiky ground cover** in favorite places, such as bark mulch or twigs.
- **Plants** that cats should avoid, such as the harp bush species sold as the “piss off plant,” have very different effects.
- **Ultrasound devices** are controversial. Some cats respond, others don't, and the devices can also bother hedgehogs and other animals.

Poison, traps or violence against cats are prohibited and are not a solution.

### Talk to the neighbors

A friendly conversation often helps more than any device. Many cat owners don't even know that their cat is chasing birds in their neighbor's garden. If you talk about the measures that help cat owners, such as keeping them indoors in the morning during breeding season or wearing a colorful ruff, many people will be willing to do something. A common goal unites us: Hardly any cat owner is happy about dead birds in front of the door.

## What to do if the cat brings a bird?

A bird caught in a cat's mouth is **always an emergency**, even if it looks unharmed. Cat saliva contains bacteria that can cause a fatal infection through even the smallest wounds. The bird needs an antibiotic quickly.

1. Carefully place the bird in a box with air holes.
2. Keep quiet and dark.
3. Immediately call a **wild bird station or veterinary practice** and take the bird there.
4. Do not feed or put water in its beak.

The guide [Young bird found: what to do?] (/vogelwissen/jungvogel-gefunden-was-tun/) provides more information on first aid.

## Feral cats

In addition to domestic cats with homes, there are feral cats in many cities and villages that live without a permanent owner. They are often sick, malnourished and multiply uncontrollably. They are just as dangerous to birds as domestic cats, but life on the streets is hard for the cats themselves. In many places, animal welfare associations catch such cats, have them neutered, given medical care and released again, or provide tame animals. If you notice feral cats in your area, contact your local animal welfare association. Do not feed feral cats without consultation, as this will attract more animals and worsen the problem.

## A plan for the breeding season

If you live in a neighborhood with a lot of outdoor cats, you can achieve a lot with a simple plan:

1. **March:** Check nesting boxes, attach cat repellent belts, place thorny branches around nesting areas.
2. **April:** Talk to neighbors about breeding season, suggest colorful ruffs as an idea.
3. **May to July:** Leave cats indoors in the morning, change feeding places to raised silos, check lawns for branches when mowing.
4. **August:** Sharing experiences: Were fewer birds brought? What worked?

In this way, a controversial topic becomes a shared task.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I drive a strange cat out of my garden?** Yes, with harmless means such as a water sprinkler or by scaring them away. Injuring, catching or poisoning is prohibited.

**Are indoor cats unhappy?** Not necessarily. With enough activity, scratching opportunities, window seats and attention, cats can live a good life indoors. A secured balcony or garden is a nice addition.

**Do fences help against cats?** Normal garden fences overcome cats effortlessly. Special cat-proof fences with inward-sloping nets keep your own cat in the garden, but do not prevent other cats from climbing in.

**What about martens and birds of prey?** Stone martens also plunder nests and sparrowhawks hunt at feeding sites. Both belong to nature. Deep boxes and defensive belts help against martens; against sparrowhawks, cover near the feeding area is sufficient.

## Myths and facts

| Claim | Which is true |
|---|---|
| “Fed cats don’t hunt.” | Incorrect. The hunting instinct is independent of hunger. |
| “Cats only catch sick birds.” | Only partially. Cats often prey on healthy young birds that are still inexperienced. |
| “A little bell solves the problem.” | It only helps so much, especially with young birds. |
| “Cats are to blame for the bird decline.” | They are an important factor in settlements, but not the only one. Loss of habitat and glass are also significant. |
| “There’s nothing you can do.” | But: Breeding season rules, colorful ruffs, safe feeding places and thorny bushes have been proven to help. |

## Conclusion

Cats and birds can live in the same garden if cat owners and bird lovers work together. Cat owners can help most if they leave their cat indoors in the morning during the breeding season, play with it every day, use a colorful ruff and have it neutered. Bird lovers secure feeding places, water troughs and nesting boxes, plant thorny bushes and use gentle means to keep strange cats away. And a friendly conversation over the garden fence is often the first and most effective step.
