QURM wants to help you hike better prepared and safer. Nevertheless, the following applies: You undertake every tour at your own responsibility. This disclaimer explains the limits of our advice.

## No daily route information

Paths in Germany are constantly changing. After storms, trees lie sideways, slopes slide after heavy rain, bridges are closed, stages are rerouted, huts change operators or close. In national parks, paths can be temporarily closed to protect animals and plants, and after forest fires, entire areas sometimes remain inaccessible for a long time. We check our guides regularly, but we cannot cover every route on a daily basis. Lengths, walking times and altitudes are guidelines.

## No mountain guidance and no training

Our texts explain the basics, such as how to deal with thunderstorms, orientation and sure-footedness. They do not replace training at a mountain school or an Alpine club, a guided tour or a first aid course. Via ferratas, glacier paths and pathless tours require training and equipment that goes beyond what a text can convey.

## No medical advice

Advice on first aid, heat, hypothermia, ticks or altitude is general information. They do not replace medical advice. If you have previous illnesses, are pregnant, are taking medication or want to travel with a baby, talk to your doctor beforehand. If you have any questions about your dog’s health, please contact the veterinary practice.

We are only liable for damages resulting from the use or non-use of the information provided in the event of intent or gross negligence, to the extent permitted by law. This applies in particular to accidents, injuries, getting lost, property damage or rescue costs.

## External links

The operators of external sites to which we link are solely responsible. At the time of linking, no legal violations were apparent.

## If in doubt: ask on site, turn back, 112

If you are unsure, ask locally: at the tourist information office, at the hut, at the national park administration. When you're on the road, it's better to turn back too early than too late. In an emergency, dial **112**. The most important rules can be found on the page [Safe on the Road](/sicher-unterwegs/).
