Most hiking accidents in Germany do not happen in the high mountains, but on normal routes: when descending, when it is wet, when you are tired in the afternoon. This page summarizes what you should keep in mind before and during each tour. It doesn't replace a first aid course, but it will help you stay calm in an emergency.

## Emergency call: 112

You can reach **112** anywhere in Germany and throughout the EU, free of charge, without an area code and without credit. The rescue service, emergency doctor, fire department and, in the mountains, the **mountain rescue service** are alerted via the control center. If you don't have a network with your own provider, your cell phone will try to reach 112 via another available network. If that doesn't help, go a little higher or to an open area and try again.

The **five W questions** will help you on the phone:

1. **Where** is the emergency? Path name, marking, last signpost, nearby peak or hut, ideally coordinates from the map app.
2. **What happened?
3. **How ​​many** people are affected?
4. **What** injuries or complaints are there?
5. **Waiting** for questions. Don't hang up first or first.

## Rescue points in the forest

In many forests there are signs with a number, such as “rescue point” or “rescue meeting point” with an abbreviation for the district. These points are stored at the control centers and can be accessed by emergency vehicles. If you pass a sign like this, it's worth taking a look at it: in an emergency, you can give the number.

## The alpine distress signal

Without cell phone reception, the Alpine emergency signal helps: give a signal **six times per minute**, i.e. a call, a whistle or a light signal about every ten seconds, then a one-minute break, then repeat. The answer is three times per minute. A whistle on your backpack carries you much further than your voice and costs very little weight.

## Before you go

- **Check the weather:** The mountain weather reports from the weather services and Alpine clubs are suitable for the Alps. If thunderstorms are forecast, start early and be back in the early afternoon.
- **Plan the tour realistically:** Walking time, altitude and difficulty must suit the weakest person in the group.
- **Tell someone:** Where you are going and when you plan to be back.
- **Charging the battery:** A cell phone with an offline map is a good tool, but only with power. A small power bank is included.

## Whatever belongs in the backpack

First aid kit with blister plaster and emergency blanket, rain jacket, a warm layer, enough to drink, something to eat, headlamp, charged cell phone, map or offline map and a whistle. More about this in our [Equipment](/wegweiser/ausruestung/) section.

## The most important rule: turn back

Turning back is not a defeat, but a decision. Turn back if a thunderstorm approaches, if the route is more difficult than expected, if someone in the group is exhausted or if the planned time for the return trip has already been significantly exceeded.
