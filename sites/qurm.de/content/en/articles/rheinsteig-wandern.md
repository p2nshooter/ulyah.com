Hardly any long-distance hiking trail in Germany offers as many postcard views as the Rheinsteig. For around 320 kilometers it leads on the right bank of the Rhine from Bonn to Wiesbaden, through the Siebengebirge, over steep vineyards, past castles and through the UNESCO World Heritage Upper Middle Rhine Valley with the Loreley. Ships pass by down in the valley, trains roll along both banks, and up on the heights it is often surprisingly quiet. However, anyone who underestimates the path will quickly notice: the Rheinsteig is not a comfortable riverside path, but rather a high-altitude path with many steep ascents and descents.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Length around 320 kilometers, from Bonn to Wiesbaden-Biebrich, usually divided into around 20 daily stages.
> - The path almost always runs on the heights and repeatedly climbs up from the Rhine Valley. Good fitness and surefootedness are necessary.
> - Best time: Spring with flowering slopes and autumn with grape harvest and colors. The southern slopes are hot in midsummer.
> - Almost every stage location has a train station. Long-distance hikes without a car are easy.
> - The Rheinburgenweg on the left bank of the Rhine is the counterpart between Bingen and Rolandseck.

## Character of the path

The Rheinsteig was opened in 2005 and is recognized as a premium trail. It is marked throughout with its own logo, and there are also access routes from the train stations and places in the valley, which are separately signposted. The constant change is typical: forest paths on the heights, viewpoints above the river, steep vineyard paths with steps and ladders, small valleys that the path runs out of and climbs back up, and historic town centers with half-timbered buildings.

Because the path oscillates so often between the river and the heights, the elevation gain adds up. Even stages of 15 to 20 kilometers can feel like a mountain tour. Therefore, don't just plan by kilometers, but calculate the walking time using meters in altitude, as described in [Calculate walking time](/ratgeber/gehzeit-berechnen/).

## The course in four sections

### Bonn to Neuwied: Siebengebirge and wine town Linz

The start in Bonn quickly leads to the Siebengebirge, one of the oldest nature reserves in Germany. Here, with the Drachenfels and other peaks of volcanic origin, the first strong climbs and wide views over the Bay of Cologne await you. After that it gets quieter: forest paths, stream valleys and small towns like Unkel and Linz am Rhein with their colorful half-timbered buildings. Via Bad Hönningen we head towards Neuwied, where the path makes a wide arc through the hinterland.

### Neuwied to Koblenz: forests and the Ehrenbreitstein Fortress

This section is more wooded and less spectacular, but often lonely and pleasantly shady. The highlight is the Ehrenbreitstein Fortress opposite the Deutsches Eck, where the Moselle and Rhine flow together. From the fortress you have one of the most beautiful views of Koblenz.

### Koblenz to Kaub: Middle Rhine Valley and Loreley

The actual world heritage begins from Lahnstein. The path leads past the Marksburg via Braubach, a hilltop castle that was never destroyed, and continues via Kamp-Bornhofen with a view of the “enemy brothers”, two neighboring castles. At St. Goarshausen you climb up to the Loreley Rock, where the Rhine is particularly narrow and deep. After that it gets wild and steep: slate paths, vineyard terraces and small side valleys all the way to Kaub with Pfalzgrafenstein Castle in the middle of the river.

### Kaub to Wiesbaden: Rheingau

The valley opens up behind Lorch and Assmannshausen, known for red wine. Above the Niederwald monument near Rüdesheim we head into the Rheingau with its gentler vineyards, monasteries and estates. The last stages via Eltville to Wiesbaden-Biebrich are less steep and lead through an open cultural landscape.

## How difficult is the Rheinsteig?

| aspect | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Condition | medium to high, lots of steep climbs almost every day |
| Sure-footedness | necessary on vineyard paths, rocks and steps, slippery when wet |
| Orientation | simple, marked throughout |
| Supply | good, places in the valley, but often a descent and ascent again is necessary |
| Suitability for beginners | Individual stages yes, the whole route is more for experienced people |

The steepest passages have railings, steel cables or steps. You don't have to be afraid of heights, but when it's wet, the slate steps and roots are slippery. Sturdy hiking shoes with good tread are mandatory. Our guide [buy hiking boots] (/ratgeber/wanderschuhe-kaufen/) explains what is important.

## Planning: stages, accommodation and luggage

The usual division includes around 20 daily stages between 10 and 23 kilometers. Many hikers adapt them, taking shorter days or combining two short stages. Because almost every village in the valley has a train station, you can freely choose the stages and break off in between.

**Accommodation:** There are plenty of hotels, guesthouses, wineries and holiday apartments. During the grape harvest in September and October and on holiday weekends, accommodation in popular locations sells out quickly. Then book early.

**Luggage:** Several providers organize luggage transport from accommodation to accommodation. Then you hike with a light daypack, which is a great relief given the many climbs. More about planning a multi-day tour can be found in [Plan your first long-distance hike](/ratgeber/erste-fernwanderung-planen/).

**In sections:** Many people hike the Rheinsteig in weekend sections over several years. This is convenient thanks to the good rail connections: park your car at your destination or travel entirely by train, walk the stage, return by train. Tips for this can be found in [Hiking without a car](/ratgeber/wandern-ohne-auto/).

## Best season

- **Spring (April to June):** fresh greenery, flowering slopes, pleasant temperatures. Ideal for the steep sections.
- **Summer:** possible, but it gets very hot in the south-facing vineyards. Start early, take plenty of water with you and read our guide about [heat while hiking](/ratgeber/hitze-beim-wandern/).
- **Autumn (September to October):** Grape harvest, Federweißer, colorful forests and often clear visibility. The best time, but also the fullest.
- **Winter:** Individual stages are attractive on dry days. Many restaurants will then be closed and slate paths may be icy.

## Rheinsteig or Rheinburgenweg?

The Rheinburgenweg runs on the left bank of the Rhine between Rolandseck and Bingen. It is shorter, also well marked and leads past castles such as Rheinfels and Stahleck. The Rheinsteig is considered somewhat wilder and more demanding, the Rheinburgenweg is considered somewhat gentler. With the ferries across the Rhine you can combine both routes and create circular tours.

## Loreley, customs castles and Rhine romance

Few landscapes in Germany are as charged with stories as the Middle Rhine Valley. The rock of the Loreley became famous through Romantic poetry: Clemens Brentano invented the figure of the seductive sorceress around 1800, and Heinrich Heine's poem "I don't know, what should it mean", later set to music by Friedrich Silcher, made her world famous. The real background is more sober: At this point the Rhine is narrow, deep and used to have dangerous rocks and currents where ships had accidents.

Many castles along the way were built not just for protection, but to make money. In the Middle Ages, the sovereigns levied customs duties on the Rhine, and every castle overlooking the river was a possible customs station. Pfalzgrafenstein Castle near Kaub, in the middle of the river on a rocky island, was built for exactly this purpose in the 14th century. Anyone who knows this will see the chain of castles on the heights with different eyes.

## Vineyards and grape harvest

On the Middle Rhine and in the Rheingau, wine is grown on sometimes very steep slopes, especially Riesling. The Rheinsteig often leads through the middle of the vineyards, over narrow paths and slate stairs. Remember that the vineyards are cultivated private land. It's not okay to pick grapes even if they're hanging close enough to touch. During the harvest season in September and October, there is work everywhere, tractors and caterpillars are on the paths, and sometimes individual paths are closed. Give priority to winemakers and follow detours. To compensate, there are ostrich taverns and wine festivals in many places where you can try Federweißer and onion cake after the stage.

## Trains, ferries and ships

Both banks of the Middle Rhine have a railway line with regional trains that go to almost every town. This makes the Rheinsteig one of the best long-distance routes for train travelers. The downside: Many freight trains run on both routes at night, and the narrow valley reflects the noise. If you are a sensitive sleeper, ask for a room on the side facing away from the river when booking or stay overnight in a place higher up.

There is no bridge over the Rhine between Koblenz and Mainz. Instead, car and passenger ferries connect the banks, for example between St. Goar and St. Goarshausen or between Bingen and Rüdesheim. During the season, excursion boats also travel between the towns in the valley. A boat trip back to the starting point is a nice, relaxed end to a stage and is easy on the knees after the descents.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need for the entire Rheinsteig?

You should plan around three weeks for the entire route if you do it in one go. Sporty hikers can do it in two weeks, but then forego breaks and sightseeing.

### Which stage is the most beautiful for a day trip?

The section around the Loreley between St. Goarshausen and Kaub is very popular. It offers steep vineyard paths, castles and views of the narrow valley. Plan a whole day because of the altitude.

### Can I walk the Rheinsteig with my dog?

Yes, in most sections. You may need to carry small dogs on ladders and very steep steps, and leashes are required in the vineyards. More about this in [Hiking with a dog](/ratgeber/wandern-mit-hund/).

### Is the Rheinsteig suitable for families with children?

The entire route is too long and too steep for children, but individual sections are ideal: castles, viewpoints, ships and trains make the route exciting for children. Choose short sections of eight to twelve kilometers where there is a train station or ferry to make the return journey easier, and pay particular attention to small children on vineyard stairs and viewpoints without railings.
