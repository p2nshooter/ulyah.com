The Harzer Hexenstieg is the classic in northern Germany: around 100 kilometers across the Harz, from Osterode in the west to Thale in the east. On the way there is the Brocken, at 1,141 meters the highest mountain in Northern Germany, ancient mining landscapes with ponds and ditches that are part of the World Heritage, the Harz National Park with its changing spruce forests and finally the Bodetal, one of the most impressive rocky gorges north of the Alps. Because the route is short enough for a week's holiday and is easily accessible by train and bus, it is the first long-distance hike for many people.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Around 100 kilometers from Osterode am Harz to Thale, usually in five to six daily stages.
> - There are two options between Altenau and Rübeland: over the Brocken or south on the dam route.
> - Marking: a separate signpost with a witch motif, plus signposts at the junctions.
> - The Brocken has a harsh low mountain climate with a lot of fog, wind and precipitation. Even in summer it can be cold up there.
> - The Bodetal between Treseburg and Thale is the crowning but rocky conclusion.

## Course and stages

The division of the stages is not officially defined. This division, which you can adapt depending on your condition, has proven itself:

| stage | route | Character |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Osterode to Buntenbock or Clausthal-Zellerfeld | Climb to the plateaus, ponds and ditches of the Upper Harz water shelf |
| 2 | Clausthal-Zellerfeld to Altenau | Water management, dam ditches, forests |
| 3 | Altenau to Torfhaus and to the Brocken or to Drei Annen Hohne | National Park, moors, Brocken climb |
| 4 | Brocken to Rübeland | Descent via Schierke, rocks, Bodetal above |
| 5 | Rübeland to Treseburg | Dams, forests, soil |
| 6 | Treseburg to Thale | Bodetal, rocks, witches' dance place in sight |

Sporty hikers combine individual stages and complete the route in four to five days. If you want to have time for breaks, museums and the Brockenkuppe, plan six days. How to calculate the walking time with altitude meters can be found in [Calculate walking time](/ratgeber/gehzeit-berechnen/).

## The sections in detail

### Upper Harz water shelf

The first stages lead through a landscape that people have shaped for mining over centuries. Ponds, ditches and underground watercourses provided energy for the silver and ore mines. Today the Upper Harz Water Shelf is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The path often follows old ditch paths, which run almost flat along the slope and are pleasant to walk.

### Harz and Brocken National Park

From Altenau you enter the national park. Many spruce forests have died here in recent years due to drought and bark beetle infestation. What initially seems desolate is intentional in the national park: nature is allowed to develop itself, and a young, mixed forest is already growing between the gray trunks. Be aware that standing dead trees can fall during storms. Avoid the forest in strong winds.

Via Torfhaus and past raised moors you reach the Brocken. On the hilltop are the train station, hotel, restaurant and the Brockenhaus with an exhibition. When the weather is clear, the view extends far over the northern German lowlands. However, on many days the summit is covered in fog.

### Bodetal

The last section follows the Bode through a deeply cut granite valley. Steep rock faces, piles of rock and the sound of the river make it the most beautiful part of the trail for many. The path is rocky and rooty, with steps in some places. On the rocks above the valley are the Witches' Dance Place and the Rosstrappe, around which numerous legends are entwined.

## Brocken variant or southern route?

The Hexenstieg splits between Altenau and Rübeland. The **Brocken variant** leads over the highest peak and is the more popular route. The **southern variant** runs over dams and through quieter forests, is a little longer and is a good alternative in bad weather or fog on the Brocken. If you want to experience both, you can take the Harz narrow-gauge railway to the Brocken on a rest day.

## Weather on the Brocken

Because of its location, the Brocken is the first high mountain to be hit by westerly winds from the Atlantic. Its climate is similar to that of significantly higher Alpine peaks: it is shrouded in fog on many days of the year, is often windy and is considerably cooler than the foothills. Snow can lie from October to May.

- Even in summer, pack a warm layer, hat and rain jacket as described in [The Onion Principle](/ratgeber/zwiebelprinzip-wanderbekleidung/).
- Check the weather report before climbing. If there is a thunderstorm warning, you stay down, more about this in [Thunderstorms while hiking](/ratgeber/gewitter-beim-wandern/).
- In dense fog the path is well marked, but stick strictly to the paths.

## Arrival and return journey

Osterode am Harz and Thale can be reached by train. In between, bus lines and the Harz narrow-gauge railway connect the towns, so you can arrange stages flexibly or shorten them in bad weather. Check timetables in advance, especially on weekends and off-season. You can find general tips in [Hiking without a car](/ratgeber/wandern-ohne-auto/).

## Accommodations and luggage

There are hotels, guesthouses, holiday apartments and some youth hostels along the way. The number of beds on the Brocken itself is limited. If you want to stay overnight there, book early. Several providers organize package tours with luggage transport. If you carry your luggage yourself, our guide [How to pack a hiking backpack correctly] (/ratgeber/rucksack-packen-und-einstellen/) will help you.

## The Bodetal as a day tour

If you only have one day, the Bodetal between Treseburg and Thale is the best choice. The path follows the river for about ten kilometers through the narrow granite gorge, past rocks, boulders and small rapids. Striking points are the Devil's Bridge and the Bodekessel, a deep, swirling pool between rocks. The path is narrow, rocky in places and has a few steps. It is slippery when wet and is sometimes closed in winter.

A cable car leads from Thale to Hexentanzplatz and a chairlift to Rosstrappe. Both rock plateaus offer a view of the gorge from above. A beautiful loop connects the path through the valley with the return path over the heights. Because there are a lot of people out and about on nice weekends, it's worth starting early.

## Packing list for the Hexenstieg

A 30 to 35 liter backpack is sufficient for a week with accommodation, and a daypack with luggage transport is sufficient. The important things are:

- Well-worn hiking boots with non-slip soles for roots and granite slabs
- Rain jacket and a warm layer, even in summer because of the Brocken climate
- Hat and light gloves from September to May
- 1.5 to 2 liters of water, because there are sometimes no refreshments between places
- First aid kit with blister plaster, see [Hiking First Aid Kit](/ratgeber/erste-hilfe-set-wandern/)
- Mobile phone with offline map in case fog makes the signposts difficult to see

## Nature in the Harz National Park

A large part of the Hexenstieg leads through the Harz National Park. Anyone who hikes here will see extensive areas of dead spruce trees that were attacked by bark beetles in the dry summers of recent years. In the national park, many of these areas are left to their own devices, and a new, mixed forest is already growing between the gray trunks. This seems unusual at first, but it impressively shows how a forest renews itself.

The Harz is also home to the lynx, which has been reintroduced here since 2000. You'll hardly see the shy cat in the wild, but you will in the display enclosure near Bad Harzburg. With a bit of luck, you'll spot deer, woodpeckers and rare plants of the raised moors along the way.

## Combine with the narrow-gauge railway

A special feature of the Harz is the Harz narrow-gauge railway, whose steam trains run, among other things, on the Brocken. If you want to shorten a stage or save yourself a climb, you can connect individual sections by train, for example from Wernigerode or Drei Annen Hohne. For families, a trip on the steam train is an experience in itself. Please plan the travel times, as the trains do not run every minute.

## The best time to travel

The Hexenstieg is most beautiful in late spring and autumn, when the temperatures are pleasant and the forests shine in fresh green or bright colors. In summer the main paths are busier and on hot days the shady forest areas are welcome. In winter there is often a lot of snow on the Brocken and conditions can be harsh. Then the lower sections are particularly suitable.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Harzer Hexenstieg suitable for beginners?

Yes, for people with basic fitness who have already done day hikes of 15 to 20 kilometers. The paths are well marked, but often rocky and rooty. Sturdy shoes with good tread are important.

### Is there a hiking pass with stamps?

Yes, there are stamp points for the Harz hiking pin along the Hexenstieg. Many hikers collect the stamps as a souvenir. You can get the stamp booklet from the region's tourist information centers.

### Can I walk the Hexenstieg in the opposite direction?

Yes, the marking works in both directions. From Thale you start with the Bodetal and have the Brocken in the middle. However, the climb out of the Bodetal is quite steep right from the start.

### Can I camp on the Hexenstieg?

Wild camping is not permitted in the Harz National Park and is usually prohibited outside of it. Stay overnight in hotels, guesthouses, youth hostels or campsites along the route.

### How strenuous is the climb to the Brocken?

At over 1,140 meters, the Brocken is the highest mountain in northern Germany. The climbs are technically easy, but long and steadily increasing. With normal fitness and enough time it can be done easily. Expect wind, fog and significantly lower temperatures at the top than in the valley.

### Are there enough places to stop for refreshments along the way?

On most stages you will pass inns, forest restaurants or kiosks, for example in Torfhaus or on the Brocken. But don't rely on them being open: closed days, seasonal opening times and staff shortages mean that some places are unexpectedly closed. So take enough water and provisions with you for each day to get you to your destination even without a stop.
