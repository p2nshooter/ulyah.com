Stand on the highest mountain in Germany: For many, the Zugspitze with its 2,962 meters is a lifelong goal. Once you get to the top, you're often surprised because the summit is anything but lonely. Two cable cars and a cog railway take many people up every day, and there are restaurants, viewing terraces and a hut on the summit platform. Anyone who climbs on foot shares the summit with people in sneakers and summer dresses.

That shouldn't obscure the fact that the climb is serious. All routes to the Zugspitze are long, have well over 2,000 meters in altitude and lead through high alpine terrain with secured passages in the upper part. This guide presents the best-known routes and helps you to realistically assess whether and how you can approach the Zugspitze on foot.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The Zugspitze is 2,962 meters high. All climbs are long, strenuous and alpine in the upper part.
> - The Reintal is the longest but technically easiest route. Most people split it up over two days.
> - The route through the Höllental is shorter, but requires via ferrata equipment and leads over a glacier remnant.
> - Even on the easiest route you need sure-footedness, a head for heights, very good physical condition and stable weather.
> - There can be snow on the summit ridge and on the Zugspitzplatt even in summer. Find out about the conditions in advance.

## The routes at a glance

| route | Starting point | Character | Requirements |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reintal | Garmisch-Partenkirchen | very long, long valley hike, scree at the top and secured summit structure | very good fitness, sure-footedness, usually two days |

## Through the Reintal

The Reintal is the classic route and is considered the technically easiest approach. It begins in Garmisch-Partenkirchen, leads through the Partnachklamm and then many kilometers through the long, wild Reintal, along the young Partnach. On the way are the Bockhütte and the Reintalangerhütte, which are popular with many hikers as an overnight destination.

From there the path climbs steeper to the Knorrhütte and continues over the barren, stony Zugspitzplatt. Here you feel like you are in a lunar landscape: limestone cliffs, scree, remnants of the Schneeferner. The last section from the Platt to the summit is secured with wire ropes and steps and requires sure-footedness and a head for heights.

In total, this route covers more than 20 kilometers and climbs over 2,000 meters in altitude. If you want to complete the climb in one day, you need excellent fitness and a very early start. Most people spend the night at the Reintalangerhütte or the Knorrhütte and climb to the summit on the second day. You should reserve cabin spaces early.

## Through the valley of hell

The Höllental is shorter than the Reintal, but significantly more demanding. It begins in Hammersbach and leads through the impressive Höllentalklamm, which is only open in the summer season, to the Höllentalangerhütte. This is followed by passages such as the “board”, a ledge with iron pins, and a ladder. The path then crosses the Höllentalferner, a remnant of a glacier that, depending on the season and conditions, can have crevasses and bare ice. A long via ferrata leads to the summit.

For this route you need via ferrata equipment with a harness, via ferrata set and helmet, often also crampons or at least Grödel for the glacier. Experience in via ferrata is mandatory. Many people hire a mountain guide for the Höllental, especially for the first time. And here too, many people spend the night in the hut.

## The way from Austria

From Ehrwald in Tyrol, another path leads to the summit via the Wiener-Neustädter Hütte and a steep, secured path known as the “Stopselzieh”. This route is also not for beginners. It is steep, partially exposed and requires sure-footedness and a head for heights. Falling rocks can be an issue if there are a lot of people overhead.

## Why the Zugspitze is not a beginner’s mountain

The infrastructure at the summit belies the requirements. Every year hikers have to be rescued by the mountain rescue service because they underestimated the climb. Typical problems are:

- **Exhaustion:** The routes are long, and the build-up to the summit comes when your strength is already weakening.
- **Weather change:** At this altitude, a thunderstorm or a cold snap with snow can be dangerous, even in midsummer.
- **Snow and ice:** On the Zugspitzplatt and on the summit passages there is often snow until the summer, and after cold air outbreaks it can also snow in August.
- **Altitude:** Some people experience headaches or nausea at nearly 3,000 meters, especially after a quick climb.
- **Descent:** Anyone who ascends on foot and wants to descend on foot often underestimates the strain on their knees and concentration.

Many experienced mountaineers therefore recommend doing a few other mountain tours with an altitude of 1,000 to 1,500 meters before the Zugspitze, including over secured passages, and getting to know your own capabilities.

## Equipment for the climb

- Mountain boots with a firm, non-slip sole, at least category B
- Weather protection: hardshell jacket, rain pants, warm insulation layer, hat and gloves even in summer
- Sufficient water and food; There are huts along the way, but in between there are long stretches without supplies
- Sun protection: sunglasses, high sun protection factor sunscreen, hat
- Headlamp, first aid kit, emergency blanket, charged cell phone
- For the Höllental you also need a via ferrata set, helmet, harness and usually crampons
- Cabin sleeping bag if you're staying overnight

## Down by train

Many who go up on foot take the train down. This is easy on the knees and is a sensible decision on a long day. On the German side, the Zugspitze cable car leads down to Eibsee and the cog railway with glacier cable car leads over the Zugspitzplatt to Garmisch-Partenkirchen. On the Austrian side, the Tyrolean Zugspitzbahn runs to Ehrwald. Check timetables, prices and the last descent before the tour.

## Alternatives for a Zugspitze day

If you want to experience the Zugspitze without daring to make the long climb, you have alternatives: take the train to the summit and enjoy the panorama, walk around the Eibsee or hike through the Partnachklamm, which forms the beginning of the Reintalweg. This way you get to know the area and can decide later whether you are confident enough to climb.

## Here's how to prepare

If you want to climb the Zugspitze on foot, you shouldn't wait until July. Good preparation begins months in advance:

1. **Build up endurance:** Regular hikes at 800 to 1,200 meters in altitude, later two days in a row, are the best training. Climbing stairs and cycling complement this well.
2. **Gain mountain experience:** Walk some red-marked mountain trails with scree and secured areas beforehand so you know how you feel there.
3. **Reserve huts early:** The huts on the Zugspitze routes are quickly booked out on summer weekends. If you are flexible, choose a day of the week.
4. **Ask for conditions:** It's worth calling the hut shortly before the tour. The innkeepers know whether there is still snow on the Platt or whether the glacier in the Höllental is bare.
5. **Choose weather window:** A stable high-pressure area with little chance of thunderstorms is ideal. If the situation is unsafe, you can postpone the tour.

## At the summit

The actual summit with the golden cross is located a little away from the platform and can be reached via a short, secured climbing passage. On nice days there are queues. If you're already tired, the platform with its view is a completely worthy ending. At the top there is also an Alpine club hut, the Munich House, which offers overnight accommodation, as well as the restaurants of the mountain railways. Plan enough time for the way back, whether on foot or by train, and keep an eye on the last descent.

## Height: when it becomes a problem

At nearly 10,000 feet the air is thinner, and some people react to this, especially after a quick climb or a night at high altitude. Typical signs include headaches, loss of appetite, nausea, fatigue and poor sleep. The symptoms are usually mild and disappear as soon as you are lower again. Things get serious when the headache gets worse despite a break, you feel confused, stagger or can hardly breathe even at rest. Then the following applies: do not continue climbing, but descend or take the train to the valley and seek medical help. If you want to spend the night at the summit, you should know that sleep is often restless at this altitude. Drinking a lot, walking slowly and avoiding alcohol help the body adapt.

## With children to the Zugspitze?

The summit is also an experience for families by train, and there are paths on the Zugspitzplatt in summer that are suitable for short explorations with children. However, the routes are too long for an ascent on foot and the upper part is too alpine. Experienced, sure-footed young people who have already done several large mountain tours can complete the Reintal with an overnight stay accompanied by adults. Smaller children are better off on the paths to Partnachklamm, Eibsee or an alpine pasture.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the best time to climb?

Usually from July to September, when most of the snow has melted and the huts are open. The exact conditions change every year. Find out in advance from the huts or the mountain rescue service and look at the mountain weather report.

### Can I do the Zugspitze in one day?

Very fit and experienced mountaineers can do this, starting at night and then descending by train. For most people, spending the night in a hut is the safer and more pleasant option.

### Do I need a mountain guide?

Not mandatory for the Reintal with good preparation. A guided tour of the Höllental makes sense if you have no experience with via ferratas and glaciers.

### Is there water along the way?

You can buy drinks and often fill up with water at the huts along the way. In between there are long sections without reliable sources, especially on the rocky Zugspitzplatt. Therefore, take enough water with you for each section and ask at the huts where you can refill.
