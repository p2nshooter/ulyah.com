The Palatinate Forest is one of the largest contiguous forest areas in Germany and, together with the French Northern Vosges, forms a cross-border UNESCO biosphere reserve. Its peaks are not high; the Kalmit, the highest point, reaches almost 673 meters. Nevertheless, hiking here is anything but boring. Red sandstone cliffs in the most bizarre shapes rise out of the forests, castles sit enthroned on mountain spurs, and on almost every corner there is a Palatinate Forest Association hut with Palatinate cuisine and spritzer. Hut hiking is its own culture here, and it makes the Palatinate Forest one of the most sociable hiking areas in Germany.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Typical features are red sandstone cliffs, castles and dense deciduous and coniferous forests, with many short, steep climbs.
> - Highlights: Dahner Felsenland, Teufelstisch, Altschlossfelsen, Trifels Castle, Kalmit and the wine route on the eastern edge.
> - The approximately 100 huts of the Palatinate Forest Association are mostly open on weekends, some also during the week.
> - Sandstone paths are sandy when dry and slippery when wet. Good shoes are important.
> - Spending the night in the forest is only permitted at designated trekking sites.

## Landscape and character

The Palatinate Forest is a low mountain range with many valleys, hilltops and rocky reefs. The paths often lead on narrow paths through pine, beech and oak forests that glow in autumn. The Buntsandstein is characteristic: it forms rocks that were shaped by wind and water into mushrooms, towers and caves, and colors the paths red. On the eastern edge, the Palatinate Forest slopes steeply down to the Rhine plain. There, on the Haardt, wine, chestnuts and almonds grow, and the paths offer wide views across the plain to the Odenwald.

## Tours and destinations

### Dahn rock country

The most impressive rocks are located around Dahn in the south of the Palatinate Forest. The **Dahner Felsenpfad** is a circular path that leads past numerous rock towers, with ladders and steps in some places. Castles such as the Dahner castle group or Berwartstein Castle near Erlenbach, which is one of the few castles in the Palatinate that is still inhabited, are nearby. Many rocks are climbing destinations, so you'll often see roped parties.

### Devil's table

Near Hinterweidenthal is the Devil's Table, a huge rock mushroom made of sandstone: a wide, flat plate lies on a narrow base, as if someone had set a table for giants. It is the landmark of the Palatinate Forest and can be reached within a short distance from the adventure park. If you want to hike more, you can combine it with circular trails through the surrounding forests.

### Old castle rocks near Eppenbrunn

Near the French border, the Altschlossfelsen stretches through the forest as a long rock formation. The walls are formed by wind and water into honeycombs and bands that glow in the evening sun. A circular path leads along the group of rocks. It is light and also suitable for families.

### Trifels Castle near Annweiler

The Reichsburg Trifels lies on a rock above Annweiler. In the Middle Ages, imperial jewels were kept here, and the English King Richard the Lionheart was imprisoned here for a time in the 12th century. A hiking trail leads from Annweiler to the castle and on to the neighboring castle ruins of Anebos and Scharfenberg, which make for a nice walk.

### Kalmit and the Haardt

The Kalmit above Maikammer is the highest elevation in the Palatinate Forest. From the viewing platform the view extends over the Rhine plain. Steep paths lead up from the wine towns on the Wine Route. The **Pfälzer Weinsteig**, a long-distance hiking trail that connects forests, vineyards and castles, runs along the Haardtrand.

## Hut hiking: a Palatinate specialty

The Palatinate Forest Association and other associations operate around a hundred huts, many of which are run on a voluntary basis. They offer simple, regional dishes such as Saumagen, liver dumplings, bratwurst with cabbage or steamed dumplings with wine sauce, along with spritzer and cake. The huts are a regular meeting point for families, clubs and hikers.

Good to know:

- Many huts only open on weekends and public holidays. Check the opening times before the tour.
- Most huts do not have overnight accommodation. Some hiking homes offer beds.
- Cash is still a safer choice at many huts.
- On nice weekends, popular huts are full at lunchtime. Be there early or come in later.

## The sweet chestnut in the Palatinate Forest

Extensive chestnut forests grow on the Haardt. In October, many people collect the fruit, which is called “Keschde” in the Palatinate, and there are specially marked chestnut paths around Edenkoben. Only collect on the side of the road, only for your own use and not in private chestnut groves. The covers are very prickly: sturdy shoes and gloves help, and children should not walk barefoot under the trees. Do not confuse the fruit with horse chestnuts, which are not edible.

## Trekking places

Wild camping is prohibited in the Palatinate Forest as everywhere in Germany. However, the nature park has set up trekking sites: small, simple campsites in the forest that you book in advance. This makes a multi-day tour with a tent legally possible. Our guide about [trekking places in Germany](/ratgeber/trekkingplaetze-deutschland/) describes how this works.

## Safety and equipment

- Sandstone paths often have loose material that acts like ball bearings. Treaded soles and concentration on the descent are important.
- The rocks are not a playground: many have vertical drops without railings. Children belong by hand.
- The paths are well marked, but the network is dense and branched. A map or app with offline data helps, as described in [Hiking apps and GPX tracks](/ratgeber/wander-apps-und-gpx/).
- In summer, the forests are at risk of forest fires when it is dry. Smoking and open flames are prohibited.

## An example round: three castles near Annweiler

One of the most beautiful day tours in the Palatinate Forest connects the three castles via Annweiler. From the town you climb through the forest to the Reichsburg Trifels, which is visible from afar on its rock. After the visit, we continue along narrow paths to the Anebos ruins, which consist only of the remains of walls on a rocky reef, and on to the Scharfenberg castle ruins, popularly known as “Münz”, with their mighty keep. The path leads back to Annweiler through the valley. Allow four to five hours for the tour including sightseeing. There are several short, steep climbs along the way, but no difficult sections.

## What belongs in the backpack

A small backpack is enough for a day tour in the Palatinate Forest. It's important to wear shoes with good tread for the sandy paths, a rain jacket, enough water because not every hut is open, and cash for refreshments. In summer, a hat and sunscreen protect you on the open rocks, and long pants and checking in the evening help against ticks, as described in [Ticks while hiking](/ratgeber/zecken-fsme-borreliose/).

## Long-distance hiking trails through the Palatinate Forest

If you want to spend several days on the road, you will find two well-known long-distance routes in the Palatinate Forest. The **Palatinate Forest Path** leads around 140 kilometers from Kaiserslautern through the middle of the forest mountains south to the French border nearSilence-Rechtenbach. It is a path for everyone who likes silence, rocks and long forest passages. The **Pfälzer Weinsteig** runs for around 160 kilometers on the eastern edge of Bockenheim in the north toSilence-Rechtenbach. It commutes between vineyards, castles and the edge of the Haardt forest and offers particularly many views over the Rhine plain. Both routes are divided into stages, and at many stage locations you can reach them by train or bus, so you can also do individual sections as a day tour.

## Arrival by train and bus

The Palatinate Forest is easily accessible without a car. The Rhine-Neckar S-Bahn runs from Mannheim and Ludwigshafen via Neustadt an der Weinstrasse through the narrow valley of the Hochspeyerbach to Kaiserslautern. Train stations such as Lambrecht, Weidenthal or Frankenstein are located directly on hiking trails. In the south, the railway line from Landau to Pirmasens connects the town of Annweiler with the castles around the Trifels. There are buses around Dahn and in Wasgau that are coordinated with the trains. Check the timetables in advance, as in some valleys there are only a few connections on weekends and evenings.

## Spritzer, Dubbeglass and a sensible stop

Part of the hut culture is the wine spritzer, which in the Palatinate is traditionally served in the **Dubbeglas**, a half-liter glass with round depressions, the “Dubbe”, which make the glass easy to grip. Palatinate spritzer is usually strongly mixed. On a hike with descent, heat and sandstone paths, that's a bad combination. If you still have to go far, order a juice spritzer or a grape juice spritzer and save the wine for the last stop before the train station. Many huts also offer homemade lemonade and coffee, and you can almost always get water refills upon request.

## Forest change in the Palatinate Forest

As in many German forests, dry summers and bark beetles have left their mark in the Palatinate Forest in recent years. Spruce trees in particular have died, and in some places open areas have emerged where young deciduous trees are growing back. For hikers this means: Dead trees can fall or lose branches during a storm. Avoid the forest in strong winds, pay attention to closures after storms and follow signposted detours.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which season is the most beautiful?

Spring with the almond blossom on the wine route in March and April and autumn with chestnuts, grape harvest and colorful forests. In summer the forests provide a lot of shade.

### Is the Palatinate Forest suitable for children?

Very. Rocks, caves, castles and huts with playgrounds make tours exciting for children. Keep the routes short and plan breaks as described in [Hiking with school children](/ratgeber/wandern-mit-schulkindern/).

### How do I recognize the hiking trails?

The Palatinate Forest has a dense network of colored markings, often bars, crosses or dots in different colors, as well as signs for long-distance routes. What the different signs mean is explained in [Understanding Waymarks](/ratgeber/wegmarkierungen-verstehen/).

### Where can I stay in the Palatinate Forest?

There are guesthouses, inns and holiday apartments in the towns. The Palatinate Forest Association and the Friends of Nature also run several hiking homes and houses with beds, which are particularly practical on multi-day tours but should be booked early. If you are traveling with a tent, you can use the bookable trekking sites in the forest.
