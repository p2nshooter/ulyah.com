Between Bamberg, Bayreuth and Nuremberg lies a landscape that offers a surprising amount in a small space. Bright rock towers rise from wooded slopes, rivers such as Wiesent and Püttlach meander in the valleys, castles sit on the rocky spurs, and hundreds of caves are hidden underground. There are also half-timbered villages, orchards and a density of small breweries that is unrivaled in Europe. Franconian Switzerland is not a high mountain region; its altitudes are usually between 400 and 600 meters. This is precisely why it is ideal for day tours with variety, for families and for anyone who wants to combine hiking with culture and contemplation.

The region owes its name to the Romantic travelers who compared the rocky landscape with Switzerland in the 19th century, similar to [Saxon Switzerland](/ratgeber/saechsische-schweiz-tagestouren/). This guide presents the most beautiful destinations and gives tips on getting there, seasons and planning.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Rocks made of limestone and dolomite, deep valleys, castles and numerous caves, including two well-known show caves, are typical.
> - The tours are mostly short to medium in length, with many small climbs, but no alpine difficulties.
> - In spring, thousands of cherry trees bloom around Walberla, and in autumn the beech forests shine.
> - You can reach many starting points by train and bus from Nuremberg, Bamberg and Forchheim.
> - Climbing rocks and caves are sensitive habitats. Stay on the paths and observe closures.

## Landscape and character

Franconian Switzerland belongs to the Franconian Alb, a low mountain range made of limestone that was formed at the bottom of a sea many millions of years ago. The hard rocks that today protrude from the slopes as rock towers and rock faces were formed in the reefs of this sea. Water has dissolved the limestone over long periods of time, creating caves, sinkholes and dry valleys. The rivers have cut deeply, so that the paths often swing between sunny plateaus and shady valleys.

For hikers this means: many short, sometimes steep climbs, narrow paths along rocks, viewpoints with views over the valleys and almost always a village nearby. The network of paths is dense and well marked. How the different signs work is explained in [Understanding Waymarks](/ratgeber/wegmarkierungen-verstehen/).

## The most beautiful destinations

### Pottenstein and the Devil's Cave

Pottenstein is picturesquely located under a castle on a rock and is one of the most popular starting points in the region. On the outskirts is the **Teufelshöhle**, one of the most famous show caves in Germany, which you can visit with a guided tour. A beautiful circular path connects the town with the Schöngrundsee, rock formations in the forest and viewpoints over the Püttlachtal. There is also a summer toboggan run and a climbing forest around Pottenstein, which makes the place particularly attractive for families.

### Tüchersfeld and the Püttlachtal

A few kilometers further is Tüchersfeld, a village whose houses are nestled between bizarre rock towers. It is one of the most famous photo motifs in Franconian Switzerland and houses the Franconian Switzerland Museum with exhibitions on the history, crafts and everyday life of the region. A hiking trail follows the Püttlach valley between Tüchersfeld and Pottenstein and can be easily combined with a return trip by bus.

### Goessweinstein

Gößweinstein is a place of pilgrimage high above the Wiesenttal. The basilica, the work of the Baroque architect Balthasar Neumann, characterizes the townscape, and Gößweinstein Castle stands on a rock on the edge of the town. Paths around the town lead to numerous viewing points from which you can see far into the valleys. A popular descent leads down to Behringersmühle, where Wiesent, Püttlach and Ailsbach flow together.

### Muggendorf, Streitberg and the Neideck castle ruins

The towns of Muggendorf and Streitberg are in the middle Wiesenttal. The **Neideck castle ruins** rise above the valley, the remains of which are among the landmarks of Franconian Switzerland. In Streitberg you can visit the **Binghöhle**, a stalactite cave with many shapes. From Muggendorf, paths lead to the **Druid Grove**, a forest with scattered, moss-covered boulders that looks like a labyrinth, and to the **Riesenburg**, a huge rock formation with arches and passages that emerged from a collapsed cave.

### The Walberla

On the western edge of Franconian Switzerland, above Kirchehrenbach, rises the **Walberla**, as the Ehrenbürg is popularly called. The table mountain with its two peaks was already inhabited in prehistoric times and is now a nature reserve with dry grass, orchids and a wide view as far as Bamberg. At the beginning of May, the Walberla Festival, one of the oldest folk festivals in Franconia, takes place on the plateau. Walberla is a special destination in April: This is when countless cherry trees bloom on the slopes and in the surrounding villages.

## Arrival without a car

Franconian Switzerland is easily accessible by public transport. From Nuremberg and Bamberg you take the train to Forchheim. From there a regional train runs through the Wiesenttal to Ebermannstadt. On selected days in the summer, a historic steam train runs further into the valley from Ebermannstadt, which is very popular with families. Buses connect the towns in Wiesenttal, Pottenstein, Gößweinstein and Tüchersfeld. During the season, additional leisure buses run on weekends that are aimed at hikers and cyclists.

If you don't have a car, you can plan wonderful long hikes: from one place to the next and back by bus. How this works and what you should pay attention to when it comes to timetables can be found in [Hiking without a car](/ratgeber/wandern-ohne-auto/). On sunny weekends, the parking spaces at the well-known destinations quickly fill up, another reason to come by bus and train.

## Breweries and refreshments

Upper Franconia is known for its many small breweries, and Franconian Switzerland is no exception. Many villages have their own brewery with a tavern or a beer cellar under the trees where you can stop off after the tour. Around Gräfenberg, the Fünf-Seidla-Steig connects several breweries to form a circular route that is particularly popular on weekends. The same applies to stopping off as everywhere else: Many inns have closed days, often at the beginning of the week, and beer cellars usually only open when the weather is good. If you still have a descent ahead of you, it's better to drink water or a spritzer first. You can find information about eating and drinking on the go in [Provisions and drinking while hiking](/ratgeber/proviant-und-trinken/).

## Protect rocks, climbing and caves

The rocks of Franconian Switzerland are among the most important climbing areas in Germany, and on nice days you can see rope teams on many of the walls. The following applies to hikers: Keep your distance from the base of the wall, as rock can fall from above, and do not enter any rocky heads that are closed. Some rocks are temporarily closed due to breeding birds such as peregrine falcons and eagle owls.

Caves are habitat for bats that spend the winter there. Many caves are therefore closed in the winter months or may not be entered. Aside from the show caves, entering caves without experience, equipment and permission is dangerous and often forbidden. The show caves, on the other hand, offer safe tours where you can learn a lot about the formation of stalactites. Wear a jacket there because caves have cool temperatures all year round.

## The best season

- **Spring:** In April, the cherry blossoms transform the slopes around the Walberla and in the Wiesenttal into a sea of white. There is a lot going on on the weekends when the flowers are in peak bloom. Those who come during the week have more peace and quiet.
- **Summer:** The shady valleys are pleasant, but it can get hot on the open plateaus. A swim in the river is possible in some places, and show caves offer cooling off. Before hot days, read [Heat while hiking](/ratgeber/hitze-beim-wandern/).
- **Autumn:** Beech forests in yellow and red, clear views and harvest time on the orchards. For many the best time.
- **Winter:** Quiet paths and hoarfrost on the rocks. Some inns, beer halls and attractions are closed and rock paths may be icy.

## With children in Franconian Switzerland

Hardly any other region is so child-friendly: rocks to marvel at, castles to conquer, caves with guided tours, streams and short paths with many destinations. Choose tours with little distance and lots of experiences, such as the tour around Pottenstein with the Devil's Cave or the path to the giant castle. Hold children's hands at viewing points with edges, as there are not railings everywhere. For more ideas, see [Hiking with School Children](/ratgeber/wandern-mit-schulkindern/) and [Hiking with Toddlers](/ratgeber/wandern-mit-kleinkindern/).

## What belongs in the backpack

For a day tour, a small backpack with water, provisions, a rain jacket and a warm layer for visiting the cave is sufficient. Sturdy shoes with good tread are important because limestone and roots become slippery when wet. Long pants and checking in the evening help against ticks, as described in [Ticks while hiking](/ratgeber/zecken-fsme-borreliose/). Cash is still practical in small taverns and beer cellars.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days should I plan for Franconian Switzerland?

For a first impression, a long weekend with two to three day tours, such as Pottenstein, Gößweinstein and Walberla, is enough. If you want to explore the region thoroughly, you will find enough material for a whole week, because the paths are varied and the places are close to each other.

### Is there a long-distance hiking trail through the region?

Yes, several supra-regional paths lead through Franconian Switzerland, including the Franconian Trail, which crosses Franconia from north to south. Many hikers combine individual sections of such trails with circular tours and spend the night in a fixed location.

### Can I take my dog ​​into the show caves?

Dogs are not allowed in most of the show caves, partly because of the bats and the narrowness of the corridors. Your dog is welcome on the hiking trails and on a leash in nature reserves. More about this can be found in [Hiking with a dog](/ratgeber/wandern-mit-hund/).

### Can I camp in Franconian Switzerland?

As is the case everywhere in Germany, wild camping is not permitted. There are campsites in several valleys in the region, many of them directly on the Wiesent, and numerous guesthouses and holiday apartments in the villages.
