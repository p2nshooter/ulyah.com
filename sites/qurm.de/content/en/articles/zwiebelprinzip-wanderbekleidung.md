When hiking, the climate changes constantly. On the climb you sweat in the sun, on the windy summit you freeze after five minutes, in the shady valley it gets damp and cool. A single thick jacket cannot handle it. The solution has been tried and tested for decades: the onion principle. You wear several thin layers on top of each other, which you put on or take off depending on your effort and the weather.

This guide explains the three layers, the right materials and common mistakes that even experienced hikers still make.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Three layers: a base layer that wicks away moisture, a warming midlayer and an outer layer to protect against wind and rain.
> - Cotton stores sweat and cools you down. Functional fibers and merino wool are the better choice.
> - Take off layers before you sweat profusely and put them on before you get cold.
> - It can get cold in the mountains even in summer. A warm layer always belongs in your backpack.
> - Pants, hat, gloves and socks are part of the system.

## Why layers?

Your body produces a lot of heat when hiking, especially on the climb. This heat has to go away, otherwise you will sweat profusely. Sweat itself is not a problem as long as it can evaporate. But if your clothes get wet and you stop, they will cool you down quickly. This is exactly what happens at the summit, when taking a break or when the wind picks up.

You can fine-tune the thermal insulation with several layers: just the shirt when climbing, the fleece jacket on top when taking a break, and the hardshell as a finishing touch in wind and rain. Each layer has its own task.

## Layer 1: the base layer

The base layer lies directly on the skin. Their main job is to wick moisture away from the body so you feel dry. Two groups of materials are suitable for this:

| material | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Synthetic (polyester, polyamide) | dries very quickly, robust, inexpensive | absorbs odors quickly |
| Merino wool | Warms even when damp, doesn't smell for a long time, pleasant on the skin | dries slower, more expensive, more sensitive |
| Blended fabric | combines the advantages of both | varies depending on the mixture |

A quick-drying synthetic shirt is a good choice for day trips in summer. Merino shows its strengths on multi-day tours where you can't wash every day because it hardly smells even after several days.

**Cotton is not suitable for the base layer.** A cotton T-shirt soaks up sweat, dries slowly and takes away heat during your break. What is pleasant in everyday life becomes a problem on the cold summit. This also applies to underwear and socks.

## Layer 2: the insulation layer

The intermediate layer keeps the heat. It is put on when you move less or it gets colder. Typical variants:

- **Fleece jacket or sweater:** robust, breathable, warms even when damp, but not windproof. Fleece comes in different thicknesses; For most summer tours, a thin to medium fleece is sufficient.
- **Lightweight synthetic fiber insulation jacket:** Warms well in relation to its weight, stays warm even when wet and packs down small.
- **Down jacket:** very warm and extremely light, but sensitive to moisture. Ideal for breaks in cold and dry weather, for hut tours in autumn or for evenings at the hut.

For most hikes in the low mountain ranges from spring to fall, a thin fleece is the most versatile choice. In the Alps, in autumn or winter, it is also worth wearing an insulating jacket for breaks.

## Layer 3: weather protection

The outer layer protects against wind and rain. There are two types here:

- **Hardshell:** a waterproof, windproof jacket with a membrane or coating. It is the most important layer for rainy weather and belongs in your backpack on every mountain tour.
- **Softshell:** windproof or windproof, water-repellent, but not completely waterproof. But significantly more breathable and comfortable when moving. Good in dry, windy weather.

Many hikers combine a light windbreaker for dry days and a hardshell for rain. If you have to choose a jacket, go for the hardshell. A softshell in constant rain won't keep you dry.

## Don't forget your legs

The principle also applies to trousers. The following have proven themselves:

- **Synthetic hiking pants**, often with stretch for freedom of movement. They dry quickly and are robust.
- **Zip-off pants** where you can remove the legs. Practical when it gets warm at midday.
- **Rain pants** as weather protection. They weigh little and are worth their weight in gold in constant rain, wind and wet grass.
- **Long johns made of merino or synthetic** for cold days or autumn tours in the mountains.

Jeans are unsuitable for the same reason as cotton shirts: they get wet, heavy and don't dry.

## Head, hands, feet

You don't lose more heat through your head than other uncovered parts of your body, but it is often the only uncovered part. A thin hat and light gloves weigh almost nothing and make a big difference on the windy summit. A tube scarf is versatile: as a headband, neck scarf or hat. In summer, a cap or hat protects you from the sun.

The same applies to the feet as to the base layer: Merino or functional socks instead of cotton. Take a pair to change into on multi-day tours.

## This is how you use the system on the go

The best equipment is of little use if you use it incorrectly. A few rules from practice:

1. **Start slightly shivering.** If you're pleasantly warm in all layers at the parking lot, you'll be sweating after ten minutes of climbing. Better take off a layer before you go.
2. **React early.** Take off layers before you really sweat and put them on before you get cold. This is a little annoying, but effective.
3. **Break = jacket on.** If you stop, put the insulation layer on immediately, not after a few minutes.
4. **Take wind seriously.** Even a light wind causes the perceived temperature to drop significantly. On the ridge, a windbreaker is often more important than a thick fleece jacket.
5. **Change wet ones.** On longer tours, a dry shirt can be very comfortable for the summit or break.

## Clothing for sun protection

In midsummer and in the mountains, clothing is not only thermal protection, but also sun protection. With increasing altitude, UV radiation increases and is additionally reflected on snowfields or light limestone. A light, long-sleeved functional shirt in a light color is often more comfortable on sunny days than a tank top with a thick layer of sunscreen. A cap with a neck protection or a hat with a brim protects the face, ears and neck, which would otherwise quickly burn. Make sure that the clothing fits loosely and allows air to pass through so that you don't overheat.

## Care to maintain function

Functional clothing will last for many years if you treat it properly. Wash them with a mild detergent and avoid fabric softener, as it sticks the fibers and impairs moisture transport. You can wash Merino on the wool program or by hand and dry it lying down so that it doesn't become felted or worn out.

You should also wash rain jackets with a membrane regularly. Dirt and skin oil impair breathability. If the outside no longer allows water to roll off but instead becomes soaked, re-impregnation helps, depending on the manufacturer's instructions as a wash impregnation or as a spray. Some jackets also react to heat, for example by putting them in the dryer for a short time if the label allows this.

## Example: What belongs in the backpack

| tour | On the body | In the backpack |
|---|---|---|
| Summer day in the low mountain range | Functional shirt, hiking pants, cap | Windbreaker or light rain jacket, thin fleece |
| Mountain tour in summer | Functional shirt, hiking pants | Fleece, hardshell, rain pants, hat, thin gloves |
| Autumn tour in the mountains | Long sleeve shirt, hiking pants | Fleece, insulation jacket, hardshell, rain pants, hat, gloves |
| Multi-day hut tour | Base layer, hiking pants | second shirt, fleece, hardshell, rain pants, warm clothing for the hut |

## The onion principle in winter

In winter the system remains the same, only the layers become warmer. Thicker merino or functional shirts with long sleeves and long underwear are suitable as a base layer. The insulation layer often consists of a fleece and an additional warm jacket made of down or synthetic fiber for breaks. On the outside, a windproof jacket protects against wind and snowfall. The biggest danger in winter is sweating wetly on the climb and then cooling down in the cold. So it's better to slow down and take off a layer early. Having a second pair of gloves and a spare hat in your backpack is not an over-the-top precaution in winter, as wet gloves no longer provide warmth. ## Dress children properly

The onion principle applies just as much to children, except that they are less likely to notice when they are too warm or too cold. Therefore, check regularly with your hand on the back of the neck whether the child feels sweaty or cool. Children run, romp around and then sit still by the stream for long periods of time, so that they have to be dressed and undressed frequently. Light layers that the child can quickly put on and take off themselves are more practical than a thick jacket. For children, a change of clothes always belongs in their parents' backpack.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I really need expensive functional clothing?

Not necessarily. Many inexpensive sports shirts made of polyester serve their purpose. What's more important than the brand is that you don't use cotton at all and have the three layers together.

### Isn't Merino too warm in summer?

Thin merino shirts are surprisingly comfortable, even when it's warm. However, they dry more slowly than synthetics. On very hot days with a lot of sweat, a light synthetic fiber shirt is often the better choice.

### Why am I cold during the break despite wearing my fleece?

Often the base layer is wet with sweat and the wind cools you down even more. During the break, put a windproof jacket over the fleece and look for a place protected from the wind.

### What is a hybrid jacket?

A hybrid jacket combines different materials in one garment. The chest, shoulders and back are usually lined with warming, windproof insulation, while the sides and sleeves are made of stretchy, breathable fabric. This keeps the torso warm and allows excess heat to escape through the sides. Hybrid jackets are particularly suitable for active tours in cool, dry weather.
