The Westweg is the classic long-distance hiking trail in the Black Forest and one of the oldest in Germany. The Black Forest Association created it around 1900, and since then it has led from Pforzheim in the north to Basel in the south, always along the highest elevations of the low mountain range. On the way you will experience everything that defines the Black Forest: deep fir forests, high moors, cirque lakes, wide areas with heather, farms with low roofs and, on clear days, the view over the Rhine plain to the Vosges and the Alps.

At around 285 kilometers, the Westweg is a real challenge. But it is well marked, varied and can be divided into sections. This guide will help you plan.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Length: around 285 kilometers from Pforzheim to Basel, a little more or less depending on the variant.
> - Duration: usually twelve to fourteen daily stages; many walk it in two or three sections.
> - Marking: a red diamond on a white background.
> - Highlights: Hornisgrinde, Mummelsee, Kinzigtal, Feldberg, Belchen and the view of the Alps.
> - In the south the path divides into a western and an eastern variant.

## History

The Westweg begins at Kupferhammer in Pforzheim, on the edge of the northern Black Forest. The first stages lead through extensive forests via Dobel and Forbach into the Murgtal and then up to the ridge. There you reach the **Hornisgrinde**, the highest elevation in the northern Black Forest. The summit area is a raised moor over which a boardwalk leads. Very close by is the **Mummelsee**, a karst lake with many legends surrounding it.

In the middle part it goes over the Alexanderschanze down to **Hausach im Kinzigtal**, the lowest point of the path, and then back up into the Middle Black Forest, past wide heights and farms to **Titisee**.

In the southern Black Forest the path follows the highest peaks of the low mountain range. The western variant leads over the **Feldberg**, at 1,493 meters the highest mountain in Baden-Württemberg, as well as over **Belchen** and **Blauen**, from which you can see the Alpine chain when visibility is good. The eastern variant is quieter and away from the big peaks. Both end in **Basel** on the Rhine.

## The sections at a glance

The official classification includes around twelve to fourteen stages, depending on the variant. The following overview summarizes the sections that are well suited for a hiking week or a long weekend:

| Section | Roughly from – to | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Northern Black Forest | Pforzheim – Alexanderschanze | dense forests, Murgtal, Hornisgrinde and Mummelsee, Grinden |
| Middle Black Forest | Alexanderschanze – Titisee | Descent into the Kinzigtal, plateaus, farms, wide views |
| Southern Black Forest West | Titisee – Feldberg – Belchen – Basel | highest peaks, views of the Alps, alpine feel |
| Southern Black Forest East | Titisee – Basel | quieter, fewer peaks, more isolation |

The daily stages are usually between 20 and 30 kilometers long, some a little shorter or longer. There are also considerable elevation gain, especially on the ascent and descent into the Kinzig Valley and the southern Black Forest.

## How challenging is the Westweg?

Technically the west route is easy. There are no climbing areas and hardly any exposed passages. The challenge lies in the length of the stages and the total elevation gain over many days. Anyone who is well trained and has already hiked for several days in a row will get along well. For beginners, it is advisable to first walk a section, such as the first four or five stages up to Alexanderschanze or Hausach.

Keep in mind that the weather on the highlands can be much harsher than in the valleys. There is often wind and fog on the Hornisgrinde and Feldberg, and it can get very cold up there even in summer.

## Marking and orientation

The Westweg is marked with a red diamond on a white background. The Black Forest Association's network of trails is dense and very well signposted. At many intersections there are yellow signposts with location and distance information. At the division in the south you have to pay attention to which variant you follow, because both have the red diamond. A map or GPX file helps here.

## Overnight stay and luggage

There are inns, guesthouses, hotels and hiking homes run by the Black Forest Association along the Westweg. Some stage locations are very small, so it's worth booking early. Some accommodations are a little out of the way and offer a pick-up service if you call ahead.

Several tour operators offer the Westweg as a flat rate with luggage transport. Then you only carry a light daypack. If you carry your luggage yourself, you should keep it as light as possible, because with 25 kilometers and many meters of altitude, every kilo counts.

Wild camping is not permitted in the Black Forest. However, there is a network of trekking sites in the Black Forest Central/North Nature Park that you can book online. Some of them are located near Westweg. Find out about the location and rules in advance.

## Arrival and departure

Pforzheim and Basel are well connected to the long-distance rail network. Many places along the way, such as Forbach, Hausach and Titisee, also have a train station. This means you can easily divide the Westweg into sections and walk it over several years. In many Black Forest towns you will receive a guest card as an overnight guest, with which you can use buses and trains in the region free of charge. Check with your accommodation.

## The best season

The hiking season runs from May to October. The mountain meadows are in bloom in May and June, the days are long in summer, and the visibility is often particularly good in autumn. There can still be snowfields at high altitudes until May, and snow can be expected from November onwards. In winter, the Westweg can only be accessed at higher altitudes with snowshoes or skis.

## What belongs in the backpack

- Well-worn hiking boots for the many kilometers on forest and gravel paths
- Rain jacket and warm layer, also for summer
- At least 1.5 to 2 liters of water, more on hot days; not every stage has places to stop for refreshments
- Sun protection for the open grinds and summits
- Blister plasters and first aid kit
- Map or offline map, especially for directions to accommodation off the route

## A week on the Westweg: this is what it can look like

Many working people don't have time for two weeks at a time. A proven solution is to spread the Westweg over two vacation weeks in different years. In the first year you walk the northern part from Pforzheim to Hausach im Kinzigtal, in the second year you walk the south from Hausach to Basel. As a train station, Hausach is an ideal changing point.

A week in the north could look like this: Arrival in Pforzheim the day before, then five or six days of hiking via Dobel, Forbach, the area around the Hornisgrinde and the Alexanderschanze to Hausach. A reserve day helps when the weather isn't good or your feet need a break. In the south, it's worth planning a day without luggage at the Feldberg or Belchen in order to enjoy the peaks in peace.

## Typical mistakes

**Stages too long at the beginning:** The first few days in the northern Black Forest seem easy because many sections run on wide forest paths. But the kilometers add up, and if you start with 30 kilometers, you'll pay for it on the third day with heavy legs and blisters.

**Underestimate water:** There are fewer springs on the heights than you would expect in the forest, and some inns have closed days. Check the night before where you can stop or refill along the way.

**Closed days for restaurants:** Especially in small towns, restaurants have fixed days of rest, often at the beginning of the week. Anyone who arrives in the evening and can't find anything to eat is annoyed. When booking, ask where you can eat on the day of arrival.

## Nature on the Westweg: Grinden, Karseen and capercaillie

On the heights of the northern Black Forest you hike across the **Grinden**, treeless plateaus with heather, snail grass and small moors. They are not a natural state, but rather arise from centuries of grazing. Today they are cared for, often with the help of cattle, goats and sheep, so that the open landscape and its rare plants are preserved. The **Karseen** like the Mummelsee are traces of the Ice Age: small glaciers carved out hollows on the slopes in which water collected after melting.

The Westweg leads around Ruhestein and Hornisgrinde through the Black Forest National Park, which was founded in 2014. Here the forest is allowed to develop again in large areas without human intervention. The capercaillie also lives in these forests, a large, very shy forest bird that is endangered in the Black Forest. Disturbances cost him vital energy, especially in winter and during courtship in spring. Therefore, stay on the paths and keep dogs on a leash.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which variant in the south is nicer?

The western variant is the classic one, with the large peaks of Feldberg and Belchen and a view of the Alps. It is better known and more frequently visited. The eastern variant is quieter and more original. Those who go for the first time usually choose the western one.

### Can I walk the Westweg with a dog?

Yes, the Westweg is easy to do with a dog. Pay attention to leash requirements in protected areas, such as on the Hornisgrinde and Feldberg, and ask the accommodation in advance.

### How many days do I need at least?

Very athletic hikers can complete the Westweg in ten to eleven days. For a relaxed tour with time for breaks, twelve to fourteen days are more realistic.

### Is the Westweg worth it in autumn?

Yes, autumn is one of the most beautiful times. In September and October the air is often clear, and in high-pressure weather the Rhine plain is sometimes covered in a sea of ​​fog while you hike above in the sun. However, the days are getting shorter and some inns are closing for the season or shortening their opening hours. So plan your stages with daylight and refreshments in mind.
