For many people in Germany, the Rennsteig is the epitome of a long-distance hike. It runs almost continuously along the ridge of the Thuringian Forest, the Thuringian Slate Mountains and the Franconian Forest, from Hörschel on the Werra near Eisenach to Blankenstein on the Saale. On the way there are no spectacular rock faces or high mountain-style summit crosses, but long forest passages, wide views of clearings, old boundary stones and an astonishing peace, even though the path is so well known.

If you want to walk the entire Rennsteig trail, you don't need any alpine experience. Good fitness, decent shoes and a little planning are enough. That's precisely why it's one of the best first long-distance hikes you can do in Germany.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Length: around 170 kilometers between Hörschel (Eisenach) and Blankenstein, a little more or less depending on the source and route.
> - Duration: usually five to eight hiking days, depending on the pace and stage division.
> - Marking: a white “R” on trees, stones and posts, plus many signposts.
> - Character: Ridge path with lots of forest, moderate gradients and good infrastructure.
> - Best time: May to October; In winter there is often snow on the hills.

## Where the Rennsteig runs

The path begins in the west in Hörschel, a district of Eisenach, directly on the Werra. From there it climbs to the ridge of the Thuringian Forest and, with a few exceptions, stays at the top. It passes well-known places such as Inselsberg, Oberhof and the area around Masserberg and Neuhaus am Rennweg, then crosses the Thuringian Slate Mountains and finally reaches the Franconian Forest, where it commutes across the border into Bavaria and back before ending in Blankenstein an der Saale.

Historically, the Rennsteig was an old border and connecting route over the ridge. Along the route you will find numerous historical boundary stones with coats of arms and dates. Many hikers take part in a little tradition: They take a pebble from the Werra with them in Hörschel and throw it into the Saale at the end in Blankenstein. If you go in the opposite direction, you do it the other way around.

A highlight in the literal sense is the section near the Großer Beerberg, at 982 meters the highest mountain in Thuringia. The summit itself is in a protected area and is not directly accessible, but the Rennsteig leads past it. The Großer Inselsberg with viewing platforms and restaurants, on the other hand, is a popular stopover in the first few days.

## How long you need

Most people divide the Rennsteig into six to eight stages. Sporty hikers can do it in five days, but then with daily stages of over 30 kilometers. For a relaxed tour with time for breaks, observation towers and occasional stops, you should plan on seven or eight days.

| Planning | stages | Average per day | For whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sporty | 5 | about 33-35 km | experienced, trained hikers |
| Balanced | 6–7 | about 24-28 km | Experienced hikers in good condition |
| Cozy | 8–9 | about 19-21 km | Beginners and connoisseurs |

The elevation gain is spread over many small ups and downs. Steep ramps are rare, but the total over several days should not be underestimated. The climb out of the Werra Valley on the first day and the way up to the Inselsberg are particularly demanding on your legs right from the start.

## A possible division of stages

There is no single correct layout because it depends on where you want to stay. A commonly chosen structure with seven days looks something like this:

1. **Hörschel – area around Ruhla or Glasbach:** Climb from the Werra valley to the ridge, first boundary stones, lots of forest.
2. **To the Inselsberg and further towards Ebertswiese:** with the Großer Inselsberg as a striking viewpoint.
3. **To Oberhof:** well-known winter sports area, good supplies.
4. **Through the Großer Beerberg area towards Schmücke and Allzunah:** one of the highest sections.
5. **Towards Masserberg:** quiet ridge passages with vast spruce and mixed forests.
6. **Towards Neuhaus am Rennweg and Spechtsbrunn:** Transition into the Slate Mountains.
7. **Across the Franconian Forest to Blankenstein:** Descent to the Saale and end at Wegstein.

You can adapt the exact stage locations to your accommodations. Many guesthouses and hotels are not located directly on the path, but one to three kilometers away in a town in the valley. Be sure to take these additional routes into account, as they quickly add up to half a day of hiking over a week.

## Overnight accommodation and luggage

There are inns, guesthouses, hotels and some hiking huts along the Rennsteig. In high season and on holiday weekends, it's worth booking early, especially in smaller towns with few beds. Wild camping is not permitted in Thuringia, like almost everywhere in Germany. However, there are some designated places where sleeping in a tent is permitted; Find out about the current status from the regional tourism associations in advance.

For many people, transporting luggage is a great relief. Several regional providers take your luggage from accommodation to accommodation. Then you only carry a daypack with water, provisions, a rain jacket and a first aid kit. If you prefer to remain independent, a backpack with a volume of 30 to 40 liters is sufficient for a tour with permanent accommodation.

## Marking and orientation

The Rennsteig is marked with a white “R” that you can find on trees, stones and posts. There are also signposts at intersections. Overall, the markings are very good, and in many places the Rennsteig cycle path, which has its own signs, runs parallel. Be careful not to accidentally follow the cycle path, which in some places leads along wider forest paths away from the hiking trail.

Despite good markings, you should have a map or an offline map on your phone with you, especially for the routes to your accommodation away from the ridge. You can find a GPX file of the entire route at the official offices and on the common tour portals.

## The best season

The classic season runs from May to October. In late spring the forest is freshly green, but it can still be cool at higher altitudes and there is occasionally some remnant snow. Summer brings long days, but also warm thunderstorms in the afternoon. Many people consider autumn to be the most beautiful time: clear views, colorful beech trees, less activity. From November it quickly becomes uncomfortable, and in winter the ridge is a destination for cross-country skiing, not for hikers with long-distance plans.

A note about May: Around the traditional Rennsteig run, one of the largest landscape runs in Europe, some sections and accommodations are heavily occupied. Check the date before setting your tour.

## What you should take with you

The Rennsteig is a low mountain path, but at over 900 meters altitude the weather can be harsh. Fog, wind and rain are also possible in summer.

- Well-worn hiking boots with non-slip soles, for forest paths with roots and stones
- Rain jacket and a warm midlayer, even in July
- At least 1.5 liters of water per stage, more on long sections; There are places to stop for refreshments, but not every few kilometers
- Blister plasters and a small first aid kit
- Sun protection for the open sections
- Cash for small huts and inns that don't take cards everywhere

## Typical mistakes when planning

The most common mistake is setting the stages too ambitiously. On the map, 30 kilometers per day seems doable, but after the third day you feel every incline. It's better to plan an extra day and use it as a buffer for rain, blisters or simply for a longer break at the observation tower.

A second error concerns arrival and departure. Hörschel and Blankenstein are small towns. Check the bus connections and train stations in the area in advance, especially for the way back from the end point if you are tired and just want to get home.

Thirdly, many people underestimate the routes to accommodation. If you descend three kilometers into the valley every evening and have to go back up again in the morning, you will end up walking significantly more than the official route length.

## Song, greeting and tradition

Hardly any hiking trail in Germany has such a lively culture of its own as the Rennsteig. Hikers here traditionally greet each other with “Gut Runst!”, and many know the Rennsteig song, which the Thuringian composer Herbert Roth wrote in the 1950s and which is still sung at festivals and inns today. At the starting point near Eisenach, before or after the tour, it is worth visiting the Wartburg, which is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and where Martin Luther translated the New Testament into German. This turns a long-distance hike into a short journey through the history of Thuringia.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Rennsteig suitable for beginners?

Yes, if you choose your stages wisely. Technically the route is easy, there are no exposed areas. The challenge lies in endurance over several days. If you have previously hiked 20 kilometers in one day and still felt good the next day, planning a leisurely Rennsteig hike is realistic.

### Can I also walk the Rennsteig in sections?

Very good actually. Many hike it in weekend sections over several years. The bus connection to some places on the ridge makes it easy to plan long-distance hikes with a return journey.

### Which direction should I go?

The classic direction is from west to east, i.e. from Hörschel to Blankenstein. There is no compelling reason for this. However, most descriptions and suggested stages follow this direction, which makes planning easier.

### Can I take my dog ​​with me?

Basically yes. Please note leash requirements in protected areas and towns and ask the accommodation in advance whether dogs are welcome. Plan to provide water for your dog, as there are not streams everywhere on the ridge.

### Is there a short route to get to know each other?

Yes. For a first impression, a day's stage around the Großer Inselsberg or the section near Oberhof, where you can easily arrive and depart by bus or train, is ideal. This way you can see whether you like the long forest passages before you plan a whole week.
