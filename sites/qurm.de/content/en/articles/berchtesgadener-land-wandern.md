In the extreme southeast of Germany lies a mountain landscape that amazes even experienced Alpine hikers. The Königssee nestles like a fjord between almost vertical rock walls, the Watzmann towers above it with its famous east face, and all around rise Hochkalter, Steinernes Meer and Hoher Göll. Berchtesgaden National Park is the only Alpine national park in Germany. For hikers, it offers tours for every requirement, from the flat bank tour to the strenuous hut hike. Here we introduce the classics and explain how you can plan them well.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The Berchtesgaden National Park is the only Alpine national park in Germany. Special rules apply: stay on the paths, dogs on a leash, no drones.
> - You can only get to the banks of Lake Königssee on foot or by electric boat. Plan the return journey and the last departure.
> - Easy tours: Malerwinkel, Obersee with Fischunkelalm, Wimbachklamm.
> - More demanding: Jenner with descent, Watzmannhaus. The Watzmann-Hocheck and the crossing are alpine tours for experienced people.
> - In midsummer the valley towns and boats are very crowded. Starting early is doubly worthwhile.

## Easy tours

### Malerwinkel at Königssee

From the Königssee district, a short, well-developed path leads to Malerwinkel, a viewing point with the famous view over the lake to the St. Bartholomä church and the Watzmann east face. The journey there and back takes just under an hour. If you want more, you can extend the Rabenwand path to another viewing point. Ideal as an introduction on the day of arrival or for families.

### Obersee and Fischunkelalm

You take the boat to the Salet pier at the southern end of the Königssee. From there a short path leads to Obersee, a smaller, quieter lake framed by high walls. A narrow bank path leads further to the Fischunkelalm, a simple alpine pasture with refreshments, and if there is enough water you can see the Röthbachfall cascading over the rock face. The path is easy, but rooty on the bank and slippery when wet. Plan to return by boat, as queues form at the pier in summer.

### Wimbachklamm and Wimbachtal

The Wimbachklamm near Ramsau is a short but impressive gorge through which wooden walkways and stairs lead. The water roars beneath you and sprays fine mist. Behind the gorge opens up the Wimbachtal, a wide debris flow valley between Watzmann and Hochkalter. A wide path leads gently uphill to the Wimbach Castle with a stop. You can read more about gorges and their entrance in [Glammen in Bavaria](/ratgeber/klammen-in-bayern/).

## Moderately difficult tours

### Jenner

The Jenner is the local mountain above the Königssee. With the Jennerbahn you can easily reach just below the summit and reach the viewing platform via a short, secured path. From above you can see the Königssee, the Watzmann and, on a clear day, far into Salzburg. If you descend on foot, you go back into the valley via alpine pastures such as the Königsbergalm. The descent is long and puts strain on the knees, poles help, as described in [Use hiking poles correctly](/ratgeber/wanderstoecke-richtig-nutzen/).

### Watzmann House

The Watzmannhaus is located at around 1,930 meters on the Hocheck ridge of the Watzmann. The climb from the Wimbach Bridge leads past several alpine pastures, most recently the Falzalm, and overcomes many meters in altitude on a long, steadily steep path to the hut. The trail is a well laid out mountain path, but long and strenuous. The reward is a great view over the Berchtesgadener Land. Many spend the night in the hut and return the next day. [Sleeping in the hut](/ratgeber/huettenuebernachtung/) explains how this works.

## For experienced mountaineers

A path leads from the Watzmannhaus to Hocheck, the first of the Watzmann peaks. The crossing over the Mittelspitze to the Südspitze is a long, exposed tour with rope insurance and a very long descent. It is only suitable for experienced, dizzy-free and fit mountaineers in stable weather. The east face is a climbing route and not an option for hikers. Serious accidents happen every year on the ridge and in the face, often due to overconfidence or changes in the weather.

## On the way in the national park

The national park protects a high mountain landscape with rare plants and animals. To ensure that it is preserved, a few rules apply:

- Stay on the marked trails.
- Dogs must be kept on a leash.
- Camping and spending the night outdoors are prohibited.
- Drones are not allowed to take off.
- Plants stay where they are, including the edelweiss.
- You take the rubbish back into the valley with you.

You can get information about paths, closures and guided tours at the National Park Center in Berchtesgaden, which is worth a visit even when it rains.

## Planning and arrival

Berchtesgaden can be reached by train, from there buses run to Königssee, Ramsau and the valley stations. During the summer holidays, the parking spaces at Königssee are full early. The following applies to the boats on the Königssee: The first trip is the quietest, and you shouldn't miss the last return trip. Check the season's travel times beforehand.

## With children in Berchtesgadener Land

Families will find many destinations here that even little legs can do. The path to Malerwinkel is short and flat. The boat trip across the Königssee is an experience in itself for children, and during the trip a trumpet signal is traditionally played on the Echo Wall, which echoes off the rock walls. At Obersee, children can play on the shore as long as they don't go into the cold, deep water. The Wimbachklamm impresses with its footbridges and rushing water. For longer journeys with children, the tips from [Hiking with School Children](/ratgeber/wandern-mit-schulkindern/) will help.

## When it rains

When it rains constantly, gorge paths and riverside paths are slippery and the peaks are covered in clouds. Then it's worth visiting the Haus der Berge, the information center of the National Park in Berchtesgaden, a trip to the salt mine or a walk through the Berchtesgaden market. Short valley hikes, for example along the Ramsauer Ache, are nice even in light rain. If there is a thunderstorm warning, avoid all tours at altitude and read our guide about [thunderstorms while hiking](/ratgeber/gewitter-beim-wandern/).

## What belongs in the backpack

For easy tours on the lake, comfortable hiking boots, a rain jacket, water and a snack are enough. For Jenner, Watzmannhaus and everything above you need mountain boots, clothing for wind and cold, sun protection, enough water and a first aid kit. More about this in [Hiking First Aid Kit](/ratgeber/erste-hilfe-set-wandern/).

## The Berchtesgaden National Park

A large part of the mountains around Königssee belong to the Berchtesgaden National Park, Germany's only national park in the Alps. It was founded in 1978 and protects an impressive high mountain landscape from the lakeshore to the peaks. There are clear rules in the national park: stay on the paths, keep dogs on a leash, avoid drones and only spend the night in huts, not in tents.

The national park administration operates information centers where you can find out about trails, animals and plants. For families, there are adventure trails and guided hikes with rangers, which are particularly popular with children.

## Salt, history and culture

The Berchtesgadener Land is not just a hiking area. The Berchtesgaden Salt Mine has been in operation since the early 16th century and can be visited, an ideal excursion for rainy days with children. Historical places in the area are also reminiscent of the National Socialist era. The Obersalzberg documentation provides factual information about this history and is an important visit for those interested in history.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you hike around Lake Königssee?

No. The banks consist largely of steep rock faces. Hiking trails are only available in individual sections, such as around St. Bartholomä and from Salet to Obersee. The boat is therefore part of almost every tour.

### Is St. Bartholomew worth visiting?

Yes. The pilgrimage church with the red onion domes is located on a peninsula in front of the Watzmann east wall. A short path leads to the Eiskapelle, a snow field at the foot of the wall. Entering the ice caves and the snowfield is life-threatening due to the risk of collapse.

### When is the best time to travel?

June and September. In June the alpine pastures are in bloom, in September it is quieter, the view is often clear and the huts are still open.

### Do I need a mountain guide for the Watzmann?

Strong, sure-footed hikers do not need a mountain guide for the Watzmannhaus. The Watzmann crossing, on the other hand, is a long, exposed mountain tour with climbing areas that requires a lot of experience, sure-footedness and a head for heights. If you don't have this experience, you should only attempt it with a state-certified mountain guide.

### How do I get to Berchtesgadener Land without a car?

You take the train to Berchtesgaden, from there buses take you to Königssee, Ramsau and many starting points. With a guest card, many buses in the region are cheaper or free for overnight guests. Check with your accommodation about the conditions.

### How crowded is it at Königssee?

Very crowded in summer and on nice weekends, especially in the mornings at the jetties. Those who start early or come during the week have more peace and quiet. Remote paths in the national park are significantly quieter, even in high season.

Even the early morning or late afternoon are often wonderfully quiet on the lakeshore and offer the most beautiful light.

Then the lake seems transformed.
