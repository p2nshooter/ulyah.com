For many hikers, autumn is the most beautiful season. The heat of summer is over, the air is clear, and on some days the view from the summit extends to mountain ranges that disappear into the haze in summer. Beech, maple and larches color the slopes in yellow, orange and red, there is hoarfrost on the meadows in the morning, and the major excursion destinations become quieter after the summer holidays. At the same time, autumn requires slightly different planning: the days are getting shorter, wet leaves make paths slippery, fog can make orientation difficult, hunts take place in many forests, and huts and inns close after the season.

This guide shows you how to plan autumn tours so that you can enjoy the beautiful aspects and avoid the typical autumn pitfalls.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Plan for sunset: arrive at your destination at least an hour before dark and always take a headlamp.
> - Wet leaves cover roots, stones and holes. When descending, go slower and use sticks.
> - Pay attention to hunting signs, stay on the trails, and wear conspicuous clothing on hunting days.
> - Many mountain huts close in October. Check the opening times of huts, mountain railways and inns in advance.
> - Only eat mushrooms that you know for sure and only collect them where allowed.

## Plan shorter days correctly

At the end of September it is still around twelve hours of light in Germany, at the end of October it is only around ten hours, and with the switch to winter time on the last weekend in October it suddenly gets dark an hour earlier in the afternoon. In the forest and in valleys with high mountains all around, it gets dark even earlier than the calendar says.

How to keep time under control:

- **Before the tour, check** when the sun sets and calculate back from there. Plan an hour buffer.
- **Start earlier than in summer.** Mornings are often the prettiest in fall, with mist in the valleys and soft light.
- **Set a turnaround time.** If you haven't reached the peak or turning point by a certain time, you turn back.
- **Choose shorter tours.** A tour that fits comfortably into one day in June will be scarce in November.
- **Headlamp always with you:** It weighs little and can prevent an emergency. Check the batteries beforehand.

As always, you calculate the walking time from the distance and altitude, as described in [Calculate walking time](/ratgeber/gehzeit-berechnen/). In autumn, however, there is often a bit of extra time because the ground is more slippery and you have to stop longer at viewpoints.

## Wet leaves and slippery paths

Autumn leaves are beautiful to look at, but treacherous on the trail. A layer of wet leaves is as slippery as soft soap, especially on rocks, wooden steps and bridges. At the same time, the leaves hide roots, stones, holes and stream channels. Many falls in autumn do not occur in difficult terrain, but rather on easy downhill forest paths.

What helps:

- **Shoes with good tread.** Worn soles are particularly dangerous in autumn. More about choosing shoes can be found in [Buy hiking shoes](/ratgeber/wanderschuhe-kaufen/).
- **Hiking poles** provide two additional points of support, especially when descending. [How to use hiking poles correctly](/ratgeber/wanderstoecke-richtig-nutzen/) explains how to use them correctly.
- **Shorter steps** and keep your weight over your feet instead of striding out.
- **Avoid wood and smooth rocks** if there is an alternative, and take steps covered in leaves carefully.

## Fog and inversion weather

In autumn, fog often forms in the valleys when the high-pressure weather is calm. Cold air gathers below, while up in the mountains it is sunny and surprisingly mild. These inversion weather conditions give hikers on the peaks one of the most beautiful sights of the year: a sea of ​​fog from which only the highest peaks protrude. Down in the valley, however, it stays gray and damp, sometimes all day long.

When it comes to planning, this means: A gray morning in the valley does not necessarily mean bad weather on the mountain. Many weather services specify the upper limit of fog for inversion locations. At the same time, dense fog can make orientation very difficult, especially on open plateaus without trees and on paths with few markings. Download your map offline, pay attention to the signs and don't leave if visibility is poor. [Map and Compass](/ratgeber/karte-und-kompass/) explains how you can orientate yourself without a battery.

## Hunting season: consideration and safety

From October to January, large driven hunts, also known as driven hunts, take place in many German forests. Helpers and dogs drive the game through the forest to the hunters, who are waiting at fixed stands. Such hunts are necessary to regulate game populations and limit damage to the forest. They are not a cause for concern for hikers if you follow a few rules:

- **Please pay attention to signs:** Hunts are announced at the entrances with signs such as “Attention Battue” or “Caution Hunt”. Take them seriously and choose a different route on these days if possible.
- **Stay on the trail:** Cross-country hiking is particularly dangerous on hunting days and disrupts the hunt.
- **Wear conspicuous clothing:** A bright or orange jacket or hat will make you highly visible.
- **Dogs on a leash:** A dog running loose can be mistaken for a hunting dog or a wild animal and gets caught in the crossfire.
- **Instructions follow:** If hunters or helpers approach you and ask for a redirection, follow them.

Even outside of big hunts, the following applies: twilight is the time when many animals are active and hunting takes place. If you are out in the forest at dusk, stay on wide paths and stay calm.

## Huts, cable cars and inns according to the season

In the Alps, many managed huts close between the end of September and mid-October. Mountain railways often take a break for maintenance in November, and some buses to the valleys run less frequently after the season. In the low mountain ranges, forest restaurants and club huts often have shortened opening times in autumn or only open on weekends.

Therefore, check before every autumn tour:

1. Is the hut or refreshment stop along the way still open and on which days?
2. Does the cable car that you plan to go up or down run?
3. What bus and train connections are there on the day of the tour?

If you find closed huts, you are on the safe side with your own provisions and enough water. Instructions for packing can be found in [Provisions and Drinking When Hiking](/ratgeber/proviant-und-trinken/).

## Mushrooms, chestnuts and fruits along the way

Autumn invites you to collect. Chestnuts, beechnuts, hazelnuts, blackberries and, on some paths, mushrooms make the way to the treasure hunt. A few basic rules:

- **Only eat mushrooms if you know them for sure.** Several deadly poisonous species grow in Germany, including the green death cap, which can easily be confused with edible mushrooms. If in doubt, a mushroom advice center can help. Many cities and clubs offer consultations in the fall.
- **Observe collecting rules:** You can collect many mushrooms and fruits in small quantities for your own use. However, collecting is usually prohibited in nature reserves and national parks, and some mushroom species are under special protection.
- **Respect private areas:** Fruit in orchards and chestnuts in well-kept groves belong to the owners. Only collect what is lying on the side of the path and is clearly free, or ask.
- **Do not eat horse chestnuts:** They are inedible and slightly poisonous. You can recognize chestnuts by their prickly shell with many fine spines and the tip of the fruit.

Chestnut collecting is a tradition of its own around the Palatinate Forest, as described in [Palatinate Forest hiking](/ratgeber/pfaelzerwald-wandern/).

## Clothing for fall

Hardly any other season brings such large temperature differences in one day. It can be frosty in the morning, almost summery in the sun at midday, and cold again in the shade in the afternoon. The [onion principle](/ratgeber/zwiebelprinzip-wanderbekleidung/) is therefore indispensable in autumn: a functional layer on the skin, a fleece or a light insulation jacket and a rain jacket as protection against wind and showers. Thin gloves and a hat weigh almost nothing and make a big difference during breaks and on windy peaks. Snow can fall in the mountains from October onwards, even if it is still mild in the valley.

## Autumn storms

In autumn the first strong storms hit Germany. During a storm, the forest is the wrong place: branches break off and trees, especially spruces that are dead or weakened by bark beetles, can fall. Postpone forest tours if there are storm warnings and avoid paths that are closed or that have a lot of broken wood in the days afterwards. You can receive severe weather warnings via the warning apps described in [Hiking apps and GPX tracks](/ratgeber/wander-apps-und-gpx/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Are ticks still active in autumn?

Yes. Ticks are active until late autumn in mild temperatures, and sometimes even beyond in mild winters. Therefore, look for autumn tours, as described in [Ticks while Hiking](/ratgeber/zecken-fsme-borreliose/).

### When is the foliage color at its most beautiful?

This depends on altitude, region and weather. In the high altitudes of the low mountain ranges and in the Alps, the coloring begins earlier, often at the end of September; in lower altitudes it usually reaches its peak in October. A sunny autumn with cool nights brings particularly strong colors. Many tourist regions publish current information on leaf color in autumn.

### Am I even allowed to go into the forest on hunting days?

Basically yes, the right of entry also applies during a hunt. However, out of consideration and for your own safety, you should avoid announced hunting areas or choose a different route, stay on the paths and follow the hunters' instructions.

### What do I do when the darkness surprises me?

Stay calm, turn on your headlamp and follow the path you know or that is well marked. Walk slower than you would during the day. If you lose your orientation or the path becomes difficult, stop, put on warm clothing and, if in doubt, call for help on the emergency number 112. What to do then is explained by [Emergency on the Mountain](/ratgeber/notfall-am-berg/).
