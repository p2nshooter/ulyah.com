If you go hiking by car, you are tied to the parking lot and always have to go back there at the end. Using buses and trains opens up a completely different type of hiking: the route hike. You start at a train station, walk over a ridge, through a valley or a whole section of a long-distance hiking trail and get back on at the end at another train station. In addition, there are relaxed return journeys without being tired at the wheel, no searching for parking spaces in overcrowded hiking car parks and a smaller ecological footprint. In Germany, this is possible in many regions, but requires some planning.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Long-distance hikes from station to station are easier using public transport than round trips by car.
> - Plan the tour from behind: First check the last safe return connection, then decide on the start time.
> - Buses often run infrequently on weekends and in rural areas. On-call buses must be ordered in advance.
> - Many holiday regions offer guest cards with which buses and trains in the region are free.
> - Check construction sites and alternative traffic before driving, and always have a plan B ready.

## Why long-distance hikes are so attractive

On a round trip you always have to go back to the car. This limits the choice of paths. However, by bus and train you can:

- **walk long long-distance routes in stages**, such as the [Rheinsteig](/ratgeber/rheinsteig-wandern/) or the [Rennsteig](/ratgeber/rennsteig-wandern/), almost all of whose stage locations have rail connections,
- **Cancel flexibly** if the weather changes or your strength weakens,
- **walk from one valley to the next** without having to go back the same way,
- **stop after the tour**, even with a glass of wine or beer, without thinking about driving.

## Planning from behind

The most important principle is: plan from the end.

1. **Set destination:** At which stop or train station should the tour end?
2. **Check last connection:** When does the last train or bus leave from there? Don't choose this last connection as your destination, but rather the second to last connection. So you have a buffer.
3. **Calculate walking time:** Calculate the pure walking time with elevation meters as described in [Calculate walking time](/ratgeber/gehzeit-berechnen/) and plan breaks accordingly.
4. **Derive start time:** Subtract walking time, breaks and buffer from the return journey. This way you know when you have to be at the start at the latest.
5. **Select outbound journey:** Find a connection that will get you to the starting point on time or earlier.

Also make note of intermediate stops along the route where you can shorten the tour.

## Read timetables correctly

- **Weekends and public holidays:** In rural areas, buses run less frequently on Saturdays and sometimes not at all on Sundays. This is the most important trap, especially for hikers.
- **Holiday schedules:** School buses are canceled during the holidays. Some lines then run much less often.
- **Seasonal hiking buses:** In many hiking and mountain regions, additional buses run in summer to starting points that are otherwise not connected.
- **Call buses and shared call taxis:** They run according to a timetable, but only if you have booked in advance, often at least an hour in advance. The telephone number is in the timetable. Order on time and have delivery when you order.
- **Construction sites and replacement traffic:** Check the evening before and in the morning whether trains are canceled or replaced by buses.

The train timetable information knows most buses and trains in Germany. Regional transport associations often have their own apps with more detailed information about on-call buses and stops.

## Tickets

**Deutschlandticket:** With the Deutschlandticket you can travel on local transport throughout Germany, i.e. with regional trains, S-Bahn, subways, trams and buses. Long-distance trains such as IC and ICE are not included. For hikers who regularly use local transport, it is often the easiest solution. You can find the exact conditions and the current price from the transport companies.

**Country and group tickets:** For groups without a Germany ticket, there are day tickets for several people in many federal states.

**Guest cards:** Many holiday regions give overnight guests a guest card, with which buses and trains in the region are free. Examples are the KONUS guest card in the Black Forest and the GUTi card in the Bavarian Forest. Ask about this when booking your accommodation. This saves money and makes spontaneous route tours easy. More about the regions can be found in [Westweg im Schwarzwald](/ratgeber/westweg-schwarzwald/) and [Bavarian Forest](/ratgeber/bayerischer-wald-gipfel/).

## On the way: little tricks

- **Take a photo of the timetable** at the stop at the start and finish. Timetable notices sometimes show information that is missing from apps.
- **Offline map and timetable:** You cannot check in valleys without a network. Download the connection beforehand.
- **Save battery:** A small power bank secures tickets, timetables and maps.
- **At the stop on time:** The bus doesn't wait in the countryside. Arrive five to ten minutes early.
- **Get in cleanly:** Dust off muddy shoes before boarding, push your poles together and put your backpack on your lap or in the luggage rack, not on the seat.

## Plan B if the connection breaks down

Trains are canceled, buses are leaving, tours are taking longer. Helps in these cases:

- to have saved the number of a taxi company in the region,
- knowing an alternative stop that you can reach on foot,
- enough water, provisions and a warm layer in your backpack in case you have to wait longer,
- In mountainous regions, the telephone number of the accommodation if you arrive later.

## An example: planning a long-distance hike

Suppose you want to walk a stage of a long-distance hiking trail in the low mountain range on a Saturday: 16 kilometers and 450 meters in altitude from one train station in the valley to another.

1. According to the rule of thumb, this results in around five hours of pure walking time. With breaks and a stop, you plan six and a half hours.
2. A regional train leaves the destination station every hour on Saturday afternoon, the last one at 7 p.m. You choose the train at 6 p.m. as your destination so that you have an hour in reserve.
3. If you count back six and a half hours, you have to leave the starting station at half past twelve at the latest. Better earlier if the journey takes longer.
4. The outward journey with a change takes one and a half hours. You take the train that arrives at the starting station at ten o'clock.
5. There are two places with bus stops on the way. You note the departure times on Saturday as an emergency exit.

This creates a day without rush, with a buffer for breaks, photos and in the event that a train is canceled.

## Multi-day tours without a car

Even for multi-day hikes, not using a car is often the better choice. You travel to the starting point, go from accommodation to accommodation and take the train home from the destination. No car has to be brought back to the starting point. On many long-distance hiking trails, providers organize luggage transport so that you only carry a daypack. [Plan your first long-distance hike](/ratgeber/erste-fernwanderung-planen/) describes how you plan such a tour.

## Combine bike and hiking shoes

If the starting point of a tour is a few kilometers from the nearest train station, a bike can sometimes help. You ride your bike to the start, lock it in a safe place and pick it up again at the end of a circular hike. For long-distance hikes, you can also park your bike at your destination and take the bus or train to the start in the morning. It is usually possible to take your bike on regional trains, but often only with your own bike ticket and with limited spaces. The Deutschlandticket generally does not include taking bicycles with you, but individual federal states and associations have their own regulations.

## Luggage for tours lasting several days

If you are traveling without a car for several days and don't want to carry everything, you have various options. On many long-distance routes, providers take luggage from accommodation to accommodation. You can also send some of your luggage in advance via parcel service to the accommodation at your destination, such as fresh clothes for the journey home; ask there in advance whether they accept packages. There are lockers at larger stations, but almost never at smaller stations. Many hotels keep luggage after you check out until the evening so that you can go hiking on the day of departure.

## Good for the climate and the region

When you go on a day trip by car, a large part of the environmental impact of the entire trip often occurs on the journey. Traveling by bus and train is much more climate-friendly. At the same time, you relieve the burden on the excursion destinations whose parking spaces are overflowing on nice weekends and whose access roads are clogged. And because you don't have to go back to the car at the end, there is often time for a stop in town, from which the region's inns and farm shops benefit.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I take hiking poles and large backpacks?

Yes. Push poles together or store them in a backpack, tips down or with protective caps. Large backpacks belong in the luggage rack or in front of your feet.

### Can I take my dog ​​with me?

In most transport associations yes, sometimes with your own ticket and a muzzle requirement for large dogs. The rules differ depending on the association. More tips can be found in [Hiking with a dog](/ratgeber/wandern-mit-hund/).

### Is it worth it with children?

Yes. For children, the train journey is often part of the adventure. Plan short routes with stops along the way so that you remain flexible.

### Is the Deutschlandticket also valid on hiking buses and mountain railways?

It usually applies to regular local buses, including many seasonal hiking buses that belong to a transport association. Cable cars, cog railways and private excursion buses, on the other hand, are usually exempt and have their own tariffs. Check this with the respective operator before the tour so that you don't experience any surprises at the valley station.
