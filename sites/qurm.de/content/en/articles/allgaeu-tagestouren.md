For many, the Allgäu is the epitome of hiking in Germany: green slopes where cows with bells graze, Alps with Kässpatzen and buttermilk, clear mountain lakes and behind them the rocky peaks of the Allgäu Alps. The region ranges from gentle hills in the foothills to rugged two-thousanders on the Austrian border. The selection is correspondingly large, from a leisurely alpine hike with children to an exposed ridge crossing. We present tours that show the Allgäu in its entire spectrum and explain what you need to pay attention to.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The hiking season in the high mountains usually runs from June to October. In early summer, old snow can still lie in shady gullies.
> - Many tours can be shortened using mountain railways. Check operating times and maintenance breaks in spring and fall.
> - Ridge paths like the Nagelfluhkette require sure-footedness and a head for heights. They are slippery when wet.
> - There are cows in the pastures in summer. Keep your distance, especially with dogs, as described in [Cows in the pasture](/ratgeber/kuehe-auf-der-weide/).
> - Summer thunderstorms often occur in the early afternoon. Start early and be on the way back by lunchtime.

## For beginners and families

### Gaisalpseen near Oberstdorf

From Oberstdorf or Reichenbach, a mountain path leads to the managed Gaisalpe and on to the Lower Gaisalpsee, a clear mountain lake under the rock faces of Rubihorn and Entschenkopf. The path is well developed, but steadily climbing. For families with sure-footed children, the route to the Alpe is a nice destination; the climb to the lake is steeper and rockier. If you like, you can continue up to the Upper Gaisalpsee. Allow half a day to a full day for the tour.

### Around Oberstdorf in the valley

The valley paths around Oberstdorf are suitable for rest days or uncertain weather conditions: to Christlessee with its turquoise-green water, across the Loretto meadows or into the Trettachtal. These paths are almost flat and are also easy to do with a stroller. If you have small children, the tips from [Hiking with Small Children](/ratgeber/wandern-mit-kleinkindern/) will help.

## For experienced people

### Grünten: the “guardian of the Allgäu”

The Grünten rises as an advanced peak far into the Allgäu foothills. That's why the view from the summit with the striking transmission tower is particularly wide: over the hilly foothills of the Alps, the lakes and the chain of the Allgäu Alps. Mountain paths lead up from Rettenberg or Burgberg, with an altitude difference of around 900 to 1,000 meters, depending on the starting point. There are managed huts along the way. The last few meters to the summit cross require some sure-footedness.

### Tegelberg via Schwangau

On the Tegelberg you hike high above the royal castles. From the mountain station of the Tegelbergbahn or on foot from the valley you can reach viewing points with views of Neuschwanstein Castle, Lake Forggensee and the foothills of the Alps. A popular descent leads back to the valley via mountain paths. Some paths on Tegelberg are steep and rocky. Find out about the condition on site, as sections are occasionally closed after storms.

## For those who are sure-footed and free from vertigo

### Ridge walk on the Nagelfluhkette

The Nagelfluh chain is a long mountain ridge between Immenstadt and Oberstaufen, named after its rock: Nagelfluh is a natural concrete made of pebbles and sand. The ridge path connects several peaks, including Mittag, Steineberg, Stuiben, Buralpkopf, Rindalphorn and Hochgrat. With the Mittagbahn near Immenstadt and the Hochgratbahn near Oberstaufen, the crossing can be organized as a route tour.

The tour is long and demanding. On Steineberg, a steep iron ladder leads over a rocky step, and in several places there are narrow paths with steep grassy sides. When wet, the rock is slippery and the grassy sides become dangerous. Only walk the ridge in stable, dry weather and allow enough time. [Mountain trails: blue, red, black](/ratgeber/bergwege-schwierigkeit/) explains what the colors of the signposts reveal about the difficulty.

## Alps, refreshments and Viehscheid

In the Allgäu the managed alpine pastures are called **Alps**. In summer, many offer simple dishes, snacks with their own cheese, buttermilk and cakes. They are not restaurants: the alpine farmers look after the livestock, cheese dairy and guests at the same time. Be patient, take cash with you and ask about opening times. Many Alps close after the Viehscheid.

The **Viehscheid** in September is a highlight of the Allgäu year. The herds return decorated from the summer pastures to the valley and are assigned to their owners. In many places this is celebrated with festivals. Anyone hiking on these days should be considerate and make room on paths with cattle drives.

## Security in the Allgäu

- **Thunderstorms:** Thermal thunderstorms often form over the mountains in summer. Start early, watch the clouds and read our guide about [thunderstorms while hiking](/ratgeber/gewitter-beim-wandern/).
- **Exposed paths:** Many accidents in the Allgäu occur due to slipping on steep grass. Hiking shoes with good tread and concentration on the descent are crucial.
- **Old snow:** In June there may be steep snowfields on northern slopes. Turn back without equipment and experience.
- **Emergency call:** The Euro emergency number 112 also works over foreign networks. What to do in an emergency is stated in [Emergency on the Mountain](/ratgeber/notfall-am-berg/).

## Arrival

Oberstdorf, Immenstadt, Sonthofen and Oberstaufen have rail connections, and buses run from there to the valleys. During peak season, parking lots at popular trailheads fill up early. You can travel more relaxed by bus and train and go on long-distance tours. Tips on this can be found in [Hiking without a car](/ratgeber/wandern-ohne-auto/).

## Packing list for an Allgäu mountain tour

Even when the sun shines over the valley, it can be windy and cool at 1,800 meters. For a day tour in the Allgäu high mountains, the following should be in your backpack:

- Mountain boots with firm, non-slip soles, not sneakers because steep grassy slopes are slippery
- Rain jacket and a warm midlayer based on the [onion principle](/ratgeber/zwiebelprinzip-wanderbekleidung/)
- Sun protection: hat, glasses, sunscreen, as UV radiation increases significantly with altitude
- at least 1.5 liters of water, more in midsummer
- Provisions, even if there is an Alp on the way, because opening times change
- First aid kit, emergency blanket and a charged cell phone
- Hiking poles for long descents

## With the mountain railway: save time, protect your knees

Many Allgäu peaks can be shortened with a mountain railway: Nebelhorn and Fellhorn near Oberstdorf, Hochgrat near Oberstaufen, Mittag near Immenstadt or Tegelberg near Schwangau. A train takes you up quickly, but it doesn't make the journey any easier. Anyone who gets out at the top immediately finds themselves in alpine terrain without having warmed up during the climb. Give yourself a few minutes to arrive and check the final descent before you head out. If you run downhill, protect your knees with sticks and an even, short step. Many people consciously decide to take the train on the descent: the ascent on foot is less stressful on the joints than a long descent.

## Allgäu in early summer and autumn

In early summer, the Allgäu mountains entice you with flower meadows and rushing streams. However, keep in mind that old snow often remains on northern slopes and gullies until June. Steep, hard snow fields are dangerous without the right equipment, even if the path in the valley has long been dry. Before going on higher tours, find out about the current conditions at huts, cable cars or tourist information centers.

Many people consider autumn to be the most beautiful time for hiking. The weather is often stable, the visibility is clear and the paths are emptier than in midsummer. However, the days are becoming significantly shorter and many Alps close after the Viehscheid in September. Plan tours so that you are back in the valley well before dark and take a headlamp with you.

## Respect for grazing animals and nature

In many Allgäu Alps, cattle graze in summer, often right on the hiking trail. Keep your distance, especially from mother cows with calves, keep dogs on a short leash and let them go in an emergency if cattle attack. Always close pasture gates behind you. More about this in the guide [Cows on pasture](/ratgeber/kuehe-auf-der-weide/).

Many areas in the Allgäu are protected. Stay on the marked paths, do not pick protected plants and take your rubbish back into the valley. Shortcuts across meadows and slopes promote erosion and disturb wildlife.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the best time for hiking in the Allgäu?

For summit tours July to early October when the snow has melted. In September the visibility is often particularly clear and the paths are quieter than during the summer holidays.

### Can I go to the Nagelfluhkette with children?

The entire ridge crossing is not suitable for children. If you have sure-footed schoolchildren, you can take short sections from the cable cars, for example to the nearest peak and back.

### Do I need a hut reservation for day tours?

No, only for overnight stays. However, patience may be necessary for lunch in popular Alps on weekends.

### Can I travel to Allgäu with the Deutschlandticket?

Yes, the Deutschlandticket is valid on regional trains and local public transport, including on the train routes to the Allgäu and on many local buses. Mountain railways are not included, and some bus lines or guest cards have their own rules. If in doubt, ask at the tourist information office.

### How early should I set off on an Allgäu mountain tour?

In summer it's best to do it early in the morning. Then it's cooler, the paths are emptier, and you'll be back before warm storms form in the afternoon. Also check the first and last ride on the cable car if you want to use it.
