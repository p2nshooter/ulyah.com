The Malerweg is one of the most famous long-distance hiking trails in Germany, and there are good reasons for that. For around 115 kilometers it leads through the rock world of Saxon Switzerland: over table mountains and through narrow grounds, past viewpoints that attracted Romantic painters as early as the 18th and 19th centuries. This is where the name comes from. Artists like Adrian Ludwig Richter and Caspar David Friedrich traveled in this landscape, and you can still recognize some of their motifs today.

The path is laid out as a loop that begins and ends at Pirna. It can be easily experienced without a car because the S-Bahn in the Elbe Valley and the ferries connect many destinations. If you want to walk it, you should be sure-footed: steps, ladders and rock paths are part of it.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Length: around 115 kilometers in eight stages, as a circular route from Pirna-Liebethal.
> - Stage lengths: usually between around 11 and 18 kilometers, often with many meters of elevation gain over a short distance.
> - Character: varied, with steps, steps and rocky paths; Surefootedness is important.
> - Marking: own Malerweg sign with a stylized M.
> - First on the right of the Elbe through the rock world, then on the left of the Elbe over the table mountains back to Pirna.

## Course shortly

The Malerweg begins in Liebethaler Grund near Pirna, a narrow valley with sandstone walls where there is also a large monument to Richard Wagner. From there it leads on the right side of the Elbe through the famous rock world with Bastei, Hohnstein, the Brand and the Kirnitzschtal to the rear of Saxon Switzerland and finally down to Schmilka on the Elbe, just before the Czech border.

On the left bank of the Elbe, the path returns over the table mountains, past striking elevations such as Kaiserkrone, Papststein and Pfaffenstein, with a view of Königstein Fortress, before returning to Pirna. The left half of the Elbe is quieter, more open and has a completely different character than the dramatic rocks of the first days.

## The stages at a glance

The official classification includes eight stages. The information on length and elevation varies slightly depending on the source because the path has been relocated in some places in recent years.

| stage | From – To | Character |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Liebethal – city of Wehlen | gentle entry through Liebethaler Grund and Uttewalder Grund |
| 2 | City of Wehlen – Hohnstein | Bastei, Schwedenlocher, Amselgrund; very popular and crowded |
| 3 | Hohnstein – Altendorf | over the fire with a wide view, many steps |
| 4 | Altendorf – Neumannmühle | Kirnitzschtal, rock world, varied paths |
| 5 | Neumannmühle – Schmilka | rear Saxon Switzerland, wild and lonely, sometimes demanding |
| 6 | Schmilka – Gohrisch | Change to the left side of the Elbe, table mountains and views |
| 7 | Gohrisch – White | Pfaffenstein, view of the Königstein Fortress |
| 8 | White – Pirna | Quiet end with views of the Elbe, arrival in the old town |

The stages are not long on paper. Many are less than 15 kilometers. However, don't underestimate them: the numerous stairs, ascents and descents and rocky paths cost time and energy. Allow five to seven hours including breaks for most stages.

## What distinguishes each section

**The first two days** offer some of the highlights: the quiet Uttewalder Grund with its famous rock gate and then the Bastei with its famous bridge over the rock towers. This is where you will meet most other people, as the Bastei is also a destination for day trips. If you start early, you sometimes have the bridge almost to yourself.

**The middle part** around Hohnstein, the Brand and the Kirnitzschtal is characterized by deep areas and viewpoints of the rocky world. The Brand is a well-known viewing balcony with a restaurant. The descent from the fire leads down a long staircase.

**The back of Saxon Switzerland** is the wildest part. Large parts of the core zone of the national park are located here, and the paths are narrower and lonelier. This area was affected by the 2022 forest fire. As a result, the route was temporarily changed and diversions may continue to occur.

**The stages on the left bank of the Elbe** lead over more open plateaus, past fields and villages and up to the table mountains. They are less spectacular, but very atmospheric, especially in the evening light, and much quieter.

## Overnight stay and luggage

There is accommodation at all stage locations, from guesthouses to hotels. In high season and around public holidays, many beds are fully booked early. Therefore, plan several months in advance if you have fixed dates.

Several regional providers have package deals with luggage transport. This is particularly pleasant on the Malerweg because you are much safer and more relaxed when climbing stairs and ladders with a light daypack. If you carry your own luggage, keep it as light as possible. A 30 to 35 liter backpack is sufficient for a tour with permanent accommodation.

Wild camping is prohibited in the national park. Traditional overnight stays in Boofen under rock overhangs are only permitted in designated places and under certain conditions. Find out more from the national park administration beforehand.

## Arrival and shortcuts

The Malerweg is ideal for train travelers. Pirna is on the S-Bahn line from Dresden through the Elbe Valley, which continues to Bad Schandau and Schöna. You can reach several stage locations by S-Bahn, ferry, bus or the Kirnitzschtalbahn. This means you can do individual stages as day tours or shorten a stage if the weather is bad.

## Where the name comes from

According to tradition, the fact that the area is now called Saxon Switzerland goes back to two Swiss artists who worked at the Dresden Art Academy in the 18th century: the painter Anton Graff and the engraver Adrian Zingg. The rocky landscape reminded them of their homeland, and their views made the region famous among artists and travelers. Many more followed during the Romantic period, and their forays gradually gave rise to a network of paths that are now considered a model for the Painter's Path.

Along the way, it's worth stopping occasionally and looking at the landscape through the eyes of that time: the rocks in the morning mist, the silhouette of a table mountain against the light, the deep grounds with their old beech trees. Many viewpoints still have names that are reminiscent of this era.

## Equipment

- Hiking shoes with grippy soles; many steps are made of sandstone and are slippery when wet
- Hiking poles for long stair descents that put a lot of strain on the knees
- Rain jacket and warm layer, even in the cool summer conditions
- Sufficient water, more than you think in summer because it gets hot on the rocks
- Headlamp in case a stage takes longer
- A map or offline map, as there is often no network in the grounds

## The best season

Spring and fall are ideal. In April and May the trees bloom, the grounds are green and cool. In October the beech trees shine and the light is particularly beautiful. In summer it can get very hot on the rocks, which is also when there is busiest activity. In winter many steps and stairs are icy; then the Malerweg only makes sense for experienced hikers with Grödeln.

## Typical mistakes when planning

**Scheduling too tight:** Because the stages look short, some hikers plan sightseeing or long journeys in the afternoon. However, the many steps and photo stops cost more time than expected. Give yourself a generous buffer at each stage.

**Forget your knees:** The long climbs down stairs, for example from the fire or from the mesas, are hard on your joints. If you are prone to knee problems, you should take poles with you and walk slowly downhill.

**Don't check for closures:** After severe weather, storms and the 2022 forest fire, individual sections were temporarily closed or rerouted. A quick look at the pages of the national park administration and the regional tourism association before you start will save you any unpleasant surprises.

**On the weekend to the Bastei:** If you walk the second stage on a sunny Saturday at lunchtime, you will share the Bastei bridge with a lot of people. If possible, schedule the stage on a weekday or start early.

## Protect sandstone and national park

The Elbe sandstone is soft. What wind and water have formed over thousands of years, shoes, sticks and scratched initials scratch in just a few years. Therefore, stay on the marked paths, especially in the core zone of the national park, where strict trail regulations apply. If you are not climbing yourself, stay away from climbing rocks and do not take any plants, stones or moss with you.

Anyone who observes climbers on the rock towers will see a special tradition: Saxon Switzerland is considered a cradle of free climbing. Strict rules that protect the sensitive rock have been in effect here for more than a hundred years. Magnesia is forbidden, as are clamping devices made of metal; they are secured with knotted loops in cracks and on the few rings. These rules show how seriously the protection of sandstone is taken in the region.

## Use rest days wisely

If you take a day off, you will find many destinations along the Malerweg that can be reached without much effort. The Königstein Fortress high above the Elbe is worth half a day, as is a trip on one of the historic paddle steamers on the Elbe or on the Kirnitzschtalbahn to the Lichtenhain waterfall. Because of its location and transport connections, Bad Schandau is a good location for a day of rest.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Malerweg suitable for beginners?

With a bit of fitness and surefootedness, yes. Technically there are no climbing areas, but there are plenty of stairs, ladders and exposed viewing points. If you are afraid of heights, you should look at the stairs beforehand.

### Can I do the Painter's Trail in less than eight days?

Sporty hikers sometimes combine two short stages in one day. But then you have to expect a lot of altitude. Eight days are a good time for a relaxed tour.

### Can my dog ​​come with me?

Yes, but it is compulsory to keep on a leash in the national park. Some ladders and stairs are difficult or impossible for dogs. Check the variants or plan workarounds beforehand.

### Is a trip to Bohemian Switzerland worth it?

Yes, if you have time. Beyond the border, the rocky landscape continues in the Czech Bohemian Switzerland National Park. The best-known destination is the Prebischtor, the largest natural sandstone gate in Europe, which you can reach on a day trip from Schmilka. The 2022 forest fire also hit large areas on the Czech side, so check beforehand which paths are open.
