Weather is the most important factor for safety in the mountains. A thunderstorm on the ridge, fog on a plateau or a cold snap in summer have turned many harmless hikes into dangerous situations. At the same time, hardly anything is as predictable as the weather for the next 24 hours if you know what to look out for. If you read the mountain weather report correctly and watch the sky along the way, you will recognize most dangers in good time. This guide explains the most important terms, the typical cloud images and simple decision-making rules.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Use a weather report specifically for the mountains, not just the forecast for the valley town.
> - Pay attention to the tendency of thunderstorms, wind at summit height, zero degree limit and visibility.
> - Cumulus clouds that grow high into the sky in the morning herald thunderstorms in the afternoon.
> - Veiled clouds with a ring around the sun often indicate a deterioration in the weather in the next 12 to 24 hours.
> - Set a turnaround time before the tour and stick to it, even if the summit is close.

## Use the correct weather report

The weather forecast in a general weather app is usually for a location in the valley. At 2,000 meters the weather can be completely different: colder, windier, with clouds that no one can see below. Therefore, use reports that are made for mountainous regions. The German Weather Service publishes forecasts and warnings for the Alps and low mountain ranges, the Alpine clubs offer their own Alpine weather reports, and the weather services of neighboring countries also have good mountain weather forecasts for their regions. Read the detailed text the evening before and look at the current version again on the morning of the tour.

## The most important terms

| term | Meaning | Why it matters to you |
|---|---|---|
| Thunderstorm tendency | Probability and timing of thunderstorms | determines when you have to be off the summit |
| Zero degree limit | Altitude at which the air temperature is 0 °C | Above that, snow, slippery conditions, cold, also possible in summer |
| Wind at summit height | Wind speed in the ridges | strong winds make ridges dangerous and cool |
| Visibility, cloud base | at what height clouds cover the mountains | Orientation in the fog becomes difficult |
| Fronts | Boundary between air masses, often with changes in the weather | rapid drop in temperature, wind, precipitation |
| Hairdryer | warm, dry downdraft on the north side of the Alps | clear visibility but very strong wind at altitude |

### temperature and altitude

In the atmosphere, the temperature decreases with altitude, on average by around 0.6 to 0.7 degrees per 100 meters of altitude. If it is 22 degrees in the valley at 600 meters, it is roughly 12 to 13 degrees at 2,000 meters, and it feels significantly colder when there is wind. Wind increases the chill significantly, especially if clothing is wet from sweating. Therefore, always pack a warm layer, as described in [The Onion Principle](/ratgeber/zwiebelprinzip-wanderbekleidung/).

## Reading clouds

When you're on the road, the sky is your best weather forecast. You should know these cloud pictures:

| Cloud | Appearance | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Fair weather cumulus | small, flat cluster clouds with a flat bottom | harmless, typical for sunny days |
| Growing cumulus clouds (Cumulus congestus) | tall towers that shoot up mid-morning, sharp edges like cauliflower | Thunderstorms possible in the next few hours |
| Storm cloud (cumulonimbus) | huge tower with a dark base, frayed at the top or widened as an anvil | Thunderstorms, heavy rain, hail, squalls. Get to safety immediately |
| Turret clouds (Altocumulus castellanus) | small towers at medium height, often in the morning | Indication of unstable air, thunderstorms likely later in the day |
| Veil clouds (Cirrus, Cirrostratus) | fine feathers or a milky veil, often with a ring around the sun | Warm front approaching, rain possible in 12 to 24 hours |
| Lenticular clouds | smooth, lens-shaped clouds that stand still over peaks | Foehn or strong wind at altitude |
| Low hanging layer clouds | gray ceiling, peaks disappear | poor visibility, drizzle, problems with orientation |

The most important clue in summer is the **speed at which cumulus clouds grow**. If tall towers with sharp edges can be seen in the late morning, the risk of thunderstorms is high in the afternoon. More about thunderstorms can be found in [Thunderstorms while hiking](/ratgeber/gewitter-beim-wandern/).

## Typical weather conditions

### Summer heat storms

On hot, humid days, thunderstorms often form over the mountains as the slopes heat up and warm air rises. The morning is usually clear, cumulus clouds grow from midday onwards, and thunderstorms develop in the afternoon. The rule is: start early and be on the way back or in a hut by early afternoon at the latest.

### Cold front

A cold front brings a rapid change in the weather: the wind picks up and shifts, a dark wall of clouds approaches, the temperature drops several degrees within a short period of time, and it can rain heavily, thunderstorms or snow at altitude. Cold fronts are easy to predict. If the weather forecast says it will move through in the afternoon, plan a short tour in the morning.

### hairdryer

During Föhn, warm, dry wind blows from the south over the Alps and falls on the north side. The view is then exceptionally clear and the mountains seem close enough to touch. However, at heights and on ridges the wind can reach gale force. Watch out for lenticular clouds and the wind forecast. Föhn often ends with a rapid change in the weather, the Föhn collapse, with rain and cooling.

### Inversion in autumn

In autumn and winter, cold, moist air often lies in the valleys, while it is sunny and mild in the mountains. This is the time of the seas of fog. Anyone who climbs out of the gray valley stands in the sun. The downside: In the fog below it can be slippery and the days are short.

## Signs on the way

- The clouds are getting darker and growing quickly.
- The wind suddenly picks up or changes.
- The temperature drops noticeably.
- You hear distant rumbles of thunder. Thunderstorms are usually only a few kilometers away.
- The altimeter on your watch shows increasing values ​​at the same altitude: the air pressure is falling.
- The visibility is unusually clear and sounds carry far: often a sign of dampness ahead.

## Decision rules

1. **Determine turnaround time:** Before you start, determine when you need to be at the summit or turning point. When the time is reached, you turn back, no matter how close the goal is.
2. **Know Plan B:** Know shortcuts, huts and train stations on your route that you can avoid.
3. **If in doubt, go deeper:** If the weather is uncertain, choose a tour in the forest or in the valley instead of on the ridge.
4. **Group:** Decide based on the weakest member, not the most ambitious.

[Calculate walking time](/ratgeber/gehzeit-berechnen/) explains how you can realistically calculate the time for your tour so that the turnaround time works.

## Wind on the mountain

Wind is often underestimated in the mountains. On ridges and peaks it usually blows much more strongly than in the valley, and even a strong breeze can cause the perceived temperature to drop significantly. When the wind is gusty, it becomes dangerous on narrow, exposed paths because a gust can throw you off balance.

In the weather report, pay attention to wind speeds and gusts at the summit, not just in the valley. If there is a storm warning or strong foehn, avoid ridges, peaks and exposed paths and choose a tour in the forest or in protected valleys. A windproof jacket and a hat belong in your backpack even in summer.

## Fog and poor visibility

Fog can appear in the mountains within minutes and reduce visibility to a few meters. Then markings are difficult to find and paths easily get lost on meadows or scree. In such cases, stay on the marked path, go from marker to marker and, if in doubt, turn back.

A tour recording on your smartphone or GPS device helps you find your way back. But don't rely on technology alone, as batteries can quickly run out in the cold. A map and compass are never a bad idea in the mountains.

## spring and autumn

Conditions are particularly changeable in the transitional seasons. In spring there is often still snow on northern slopes and paths can be icy in the morning. In autumn the days are short, the nights are cold, and hoarfrost or the first snow make paths slippery. Start early, plan reserves and find out about the conditions at altitude, not just in the valley.

## Frequently asked questions

### How reliable are weather apps for the mountains?

Often good for temperature and large-scale precipitation, but only limited for local thunderstorms because they occur on a very small scale. Combine app, text report and your own observation.

### What does a 30 percent chance of thunderstorms mean?

That there is a certain probability of thunderstorms occurring in a region on that day, not that there will be thunderstorms 30 percent of the time. For you this means: Plan so that you are not in an exposed location in the afternoon.

### Can it snow on the mountains in summer?

Yes. When cold air breaks in, the zero degree limit can drop to below 2,000 meters in midsummer. Then paths at altitude are icy or snowy.

### Which weather services are suitable for the Alps?

The mountain weather reports from national weather services, such as the German Weather Service, GeoSphere Austria and MeteoSchweiz, are well suited. The Alpine clubs also publish their own Alpine club weather report with information for mountaineers. Compare several sources and pay attention to the information for the altitude at which you are traveling.

### What do I do if I'm surprised by a change in the weather while I'm on the road?

Make the decision early. When clouds build up, the wind shifts, or the temperature suddenly drops, turning back is usually the best choice. Leave ridges and peaks, avoid individual trees or metal cables during thunderstorms and move quickly to lower terrain. More about this in the guide [Thunderstorms while hiking](/ratgeber/gewitter-beim-wandern/).

It's better to turn back too early than to get caught in a thunderstorm: the mountain stays still and you can come back.

This applies in the high mountains as well as in the low mountain ranges.
