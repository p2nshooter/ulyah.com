In Germany it is almost a certainty: at some point on a hike it will rain. In the low mountain ranges, a shower often comes without warning; in the mountains, a change in the weather can turn a summer day into a wet, cold afternoon in an hour. A good rain jacket is therefore, along with shoes, the most important piece of clothing for outdoors. It should keep out rain and wind, at the same time let sweat out, be light and fit small in your backpack. No jacket can do everything perfectly. If you understand the technical terms, you can find the jacket that suits your tours and, with the right care, keep it functional for many years.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Water column: 10,000 millimeters and more are common for hiking jackets and are well suited for constant rain with a backpack.
> - The RET value shows breathability: the lower, the better. Below 6 is considered very good, 6 to 13 is considered good.
> - 3-layer jackets are robust and made for a lot of use, 2.5-layer jackets are light and packable.
> - More important than values ​​in the brochure: a hood with a peak that turns with your head, ventilation openings and enough space for a warm layer underneath.
> - Washing and re-waterproofing keeps the jacket breathable. Fabric softener destroys the function.

## Hardshell, softshell or poncho?

| Type | Strengths | Weaknesses | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hardshell with membrane | waterproof and windproof, even in constant rain | less breathable than softshell, rustles | Hiking in any weather, mountain tours |
| Softshell | very breathable, stretchy, comfortable | just showers and drizzle, no continuous rain | dry, windy weather, activity |
| Poncho | also covers the backpack, very airy | flutters in the wind, inconvenient on the ridge | Paths in the forest, warm rainy days |
| Lightweight rain jacket without membrane (coated) | cheap, easy | sweats quickly inside | occasional use, short tours |

For most hikers, a hardshell jacket with a membrane or dense coating is the right basic equipment. A softshell can complement it on dry days, but it cannot replace it.

## What the water column means

The water column indicates how high a column of water can be on the fabric before water pushes through. It is given in millimeters. The higher, the more pressure the fabric can withstand. This is important because the backpack presses the fabric on the shoulders and hips against the body and additional pressure is created when kneeling or sitting on wet ground.

- From around 1,300 millimeters onwards, a material is considered waterproof in many test standards.
- For hiking jackets that are worn with a backpack in constant rain, 10,000 millimeters and more make sense.
- Values ​​beyond 20,000 millimeters hardly provide any advantages when hiking. Processing and seams are more important.

A high water column is of no use if seams and zippers are leaky. Look for **welded or taped seams** and covered or water-repellent zippers.

## Breathability: the RET value

You sweat when hiking, especially uphill. A jacket that doesn't allow water vapor to escape will make you wet from the inside. Breathability is often indicated by the **RET value**, the resistance to the passage of water vapor. The lower the better:

| RET value | Classification |
|---|---|
| under 6 | very breathable |
| 6 to 13 | good |
| 13 to 20 | satisfactory |
| over 20 | low |

Some manufacturers instead provide an MVTR value, which describes the amount of vapor in grams per square meter per day. The following applies here: the higher, the better. The values ​​of different measurement methods are not directly comparable.

Even the best membrane has its limits when you go uphill with a backpack at 18 degrees. Then ventilation openings, a slower pace and the right layer underneath help, as described in [The Onion Principle](/ratgeber/zwiebelprinzip-wanderbekleidung/).

## Laminates: 2, 2.5 or 3 layers

- **2-layer jackets:** The outer fabric and membrane are connected, and a loose lining hangs on the inside. Comfortable, slightly heavier, popular for everyday use and light tours.
- **2.5-layer jackets:** Instead of a lining, a printed film protects the membrane. Very light and packs small, but less robust. Ideal as an emergency jacket that usually stays in your backpack.
- **3-layer jackets:** Outer fabric, membrane and inner fabric are firmly connected. Robust, durable, good under heavy backpacks. The choice for frequent use and mountain tours.

## Cut and details that count in the rain

- **Hood:** It should have a stiffened peak, be able to be adjusted in width and depth and rotate with your head. Try it in the store: can you look to the side without looking into the hood?
- **Length:** The jacket should cover the bottom so that water does not run into the waistband.
- **Sleeves:** Long enough, even with arms raised, with adjustable cuffs.
- **Ventilation:** Zippers under the armpits allow plenty of heat out without letting rain in.
- **Pockets:** set high so you can reach them with a hip belt.
- **Place:** Try on the jacket with a fleece layer underneath.

## Rain pants, gaiters and backpack protection

Lightweight rain pants are useful for mountain tours, multi-day tours and cool weather. Side zippers allow you to put it on over your shoes. Gaiters keep water and mud out of your shoes when you walk through wet grass. There are rain covers for the backpack. But they never hold completely tight because water runs down the back. Pack your sleeping bag, change of clothes and electronics in waterproof stuff sacks. How to pack a backpack sensibly can be found in [Packing a hiking backpack correctly](/ratgeber/rucksack-packen-und-einstellen/).

## Care and impregnation

The surface of a rain jacket is treated with a water-repellent finish. It lets water roll off. If this effect wears off, the outer material becomes saturated. The membrane remains tight, but the jacket hardly breathes and you feel wet inside.

This is how you care properly:

1. **Washing:** according to the care label with a special detergent for functional clothing or a mild liquid detergent, without fabric softener. Dirt and skin oil clog pores more than frequent washing.
2. **Rinse well** so that no detergent residue remains.
3. **Heat:** Many waterproofing products are reactivated by heat, such as tumble drying on a low setting or ironing on a low setting with a cloth in between. Pay attention to the care label.
4. **Re-impregnate** if water no longer runs off despite washing: with spray or wash-in impregnation.

Many manufacturers have switched to impregnations without perfluorinated and polyfluorinated chemicals because these substances accumulate in the environment. The new products shed slightly less and need to be refreshed more often, but they are more environmentally friendly.

## When a jacket needs to be replaced

Abraded areas on the shoulders, loose seam tapes, tears or a membrane that is detached from the outer fabric are signs that the jacket is no longer doing its job reliably. Small holes can be closed with repair patches. Many outdoor stores and manufacturers offer repair services, which are often cheaper than buying new.

## Membranes: how they work

Most waterproof hiking jackets have a thin membrane between the outer fabric and the inside. There are two basic principles. **Microporous membranes** contain countless tiny pores that are too small for water droplets but large enough for individual water vapor molecules. They work even with little sweat and allow steam to escape quite quickly. **Porous membranes**, usually made of polyurethane, transport moisture chemically through the material. They work better the warmer and humid it is inside, and are often cheaper.

For a long time, many high-quality membranes were made of PTFE, a plastic containing fluorine. There are now also membranes made of expanded polyethylene and other materials that do not require per- and polyfluorinated chemicals. What ultimately matters to you as a hiker is how tight, breathable and durable the entire jacket is, not just the membrane.

## Find the right size

Always try on a rain jacket as you would wear it on the go: with a warm layer underneath and, ideally, with your backpack. Raise your arms above your head as if you were reaching for a handle. If the jacket rides up and exposes your stomach, it is too short or too tight. Squat down, turn your upper body and close all zippers and Velcro fasteners. The jacket should not be tight anywhere, but also not so loose that it flaps in the wind. Anyone who goes via ferrata also checks whether the hood fits over a helmet.

## Buy sustainably and use it for a long time

The most environmentally friendly jacket is the one you wear for a long time. A well-made jacket that you wash regularly, re-waterproof and have minor damage repaired will often last ten years or more. When purchasing, pay attention to replacement parts and repair offers from the manufacturer, materials made from recycled fibers and impregnation without fluorine-containing chemicals. Used outdoor jackets from second-hand stores or online exchanges are a good alternative if the seams and membrane are still intact.

## Rain jackets for children

When hiking, children are often closer to wet grass, puddles and streams than adults. A robust jacket with some room to grow, a well-fitting hood and reflective details makes sense. Good children's jackets do not have cords in the hood and neck area that children can get caught on. For small children who sit and play a lot, rain pants or a mud suit are often more important than the jacket alone. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is an umbrella enough?

On wide paths in low mountain ranges when there is little wind, a light trekking umbrella is surprisingly comfortable because it doesn't make you sweat. On narrow paths, in windy conditions and in the mountains, it is not an alternative to a jacket.

### Why am I wet inside even though I have a rain jacket?

Usually it's sweat that can't escape quickly enough, or the outer material is soaked because the waterproofing has worn off. Leaky seams are rarely to blame.

### Do I need a rain jacket in summer?

Yes. Summer thunderstorms bring heavy rain and often a significant drop in temperature. Wet and in the wind, you cool down quickly even at 15 degrees. More about this in [Thunderstorms while hiking](/ratgeber/gewitter-beim-wandern/).

### What is the difference between waterproof and water-repellent?

Water-repellent means that water rolls off the surface as long as there is little rain and there is no pressure. If it rains for a long time, it will eventually penetrate. Waterproof means that the fabric, usually with a membrane or coating, remains tight even in constant rain and under the pressure of a backpack, and that seams and zippers are also protected.
