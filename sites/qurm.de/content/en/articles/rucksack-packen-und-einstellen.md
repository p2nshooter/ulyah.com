You won't even notice a backpack that fits well after an hour. A backpack that doesn't fit well reminds you of itself with every step you take: it pulls on your shoulders, pinches the small of your back, and rocks back and forth with every step. The causes are usually not the model, but rather the wrong size, an unsuitable back length or an unfavorable packing method.

This guide will show you which volume makes sense for which tour, how to correctly adjust a backpack and how to pack it so that it is stable and comfortable to wear.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - For day tours 20 to 30 liters are usually enough, for hut tours 30 to 40 liters, for tours with a tent 50 liters or more.
> - The back length must fit your upper body, not your height.
> - The hip belt carries most of the weight on heavier backpacks. It sits on the hip bones, not the stomach.
> - Heavy things should be placed close to the back and at about shoulder blade height.
> - The overall weight should remain as low as possible. Any item you don't need is free to wear.

## What size do you need?

The volume of a backpack is measured in liters. The right size depends on how long you are traveling, where you are staying and what you need to carry.

| tour | Volume | Typical content |
|---|---|---|
| Half day tour, summer | 15-20L | Water, snack, rain jacket, first aid |
| Day tour in the low mountain ranges | 20-30L | additional warm layer, lunch, card |
| Mountain tour with weather protection | 25-35L | Hardshell, rain pants, warm layers, more water |
| Multi-day hut tour | 30-40L | Change of clothes, sleeping bag, laundry kit |
| Long-distance hike with guesthouses | 30-40L | like a hut tour, depending on the season |
| Trekking with tent and stove | 50-70L | Tent, sleeping bag, sleeping mat, stove, food |

A common mistake is a backpack that is too big. What there is space gets filled, and you end up carrying things you don't need. It's better to choose the smallest backpack that fits your equipment sensibly.

## What you should pay attention to when buying

In addition to volume, there are a few features that make a good hiking backpack:

- **Carrying system:** For small daypacks, a padded back is sufficient. From around 30 liters and with heavy luggage, a stable frame and a padded hip belt are important, which transfer the weight to the hips.
- **Back ventilation:** Mesh backs spaced from the body ensure less sweat. On the other hand, the load is a little further away from the body, which is less stable with heavy loads. Contact backs sit closer to the body and are suitable for heavier loads and rough terrain.
- **Adjustable back length:** Many larger models can be adjusted in back length. This is especially helpful if you are between sizes.
- **Women's models:** They often have shorter back lengths, narrower shoulder straps and differently shaped hip straps. Try on both versions, because what matters is what fits.
- **Accessibility:** A front entry, top compartments and hip belt pockets make it easy to access the things you often need on the go.
- **Rain cover:** Some backpacks have an integrated rain cover, for others you can purchase one. It is very useful in the rain.

## Adjust the backpack correctly

Even the best backpack wears poorly if it is not adjusted. Take your time to do this, preferably with a weight of around seven to ten kilograms in your backpack.

1. **Loosen all straps.** Shoulder straps, hip belt, position adjustment straps and chest strap.
2. **Check back length.** For adjustable models, adjust the length so that the hip belt sits on the hip bones and the shoulder straps run over the shoulders without a gap.
3. **Close the hip belt.** Put the backpack on and fasten the hip belt so that its center lies approximately on the upper hip bones. Tighten it so that it fits securely but doesn't constrict.
4. **Tighten the shoulder straps.** Only tighten them enough so that they fit snugly on the body. They are intended to stabilize the backpack, not to carry the entire weight.
5. **Adjust the position adjustment straps.** These small straps at the top of the shoulder straps pull the backpack closer to the body. An angle of around 30 to 45 degrees to the backpack is a good guideline.
6. **Close the chest strap.** It connects the shoulder straps and prevents them from slipping outwards. It sits approximately at breastbone height without hindering breathing.

For heavy backpacks, most of the weight should be on your hips. When going uphill, you can tighten the position adjustment straps slightly so that the backpack sits closer to your body. You can loosen them a bit downhill and on long, flat stretches.

## This is how you pack properly

How you pack affects how stable the backpack is. The most important rule: **Keep heavy things close to your back and at about shoulder blade height.** This way the center of gravity is close to your body and the backpack doesn't pull you backwards.

| area | What belongs in it |
|---|---|
| Below | light, voluminous things that you only need in the evening: sleeping bag, sleeping bag, change of clothes |
| Middle, close to the back | heavy things: water, provisions, stove, tent |
| Middle, outside | medium-weight items: warm clothing, rain pants |
| Above | Things you need quickly: rain jacket, fleece, first aid kit |
| Lid compartment | Small things: map, sunscreen, headlamp, snacks |
| Hip belt pockets | Cell phone, pocket knife, lip balm, small snack |

Fill cavities with soft things so that nothing slips. A backpack in which everything sits firmly does not rock and is much more comfortable to wear.

## Pack waterproof

Most backpacks are not waterproof. Even with a rain cover, moisture can penetrate through the back in continuous rain. Therefore, pack everything that needs to stay dry in dry bags or plastic bags: a change of clothes, sleeping bag, first aid kit, electronics and papers. A large, light garbage bag as an inner lining is a cheap and proven solution.

## How heavy can the backpack be?

There is no binding limit because body type, fitness and tour are different. As a rough guide, a total weight of no more than a fifth to a quarter of your own body weight is often given for multi-day tours. For day trips, the backpack should be significantly lighter. In children the values ​​are much lower.

The easiest way to save weight is to cut it out. Unpack after the tour and look at what you didn't use. Of course, a first aid kit, rain gear and warm clothing are always included, even if you don't need them. But the third T-shirt, the full shampoo bottle or the heavy book can stay at home.

## If it still pushes

- **Pain in the shoulders:** The hip belt is not supporting enough. Tighten it and loosen the shoulder straps a little.
- **Pressure on the hips:** The hip belt is too low or too tight. Correct the position or loosen it slightly. On some models the shape of the strap can be adjusted.
- **Numb arms or tingling:** The shoulder straps press on nerves. Check back length and chest strap.
- **Back pain:** The center of gravity is too far away from the body. Pack heavy things closer to your back.
- **Scrubbed areas:** Rubbing dirt, seams, or wet clothing. Tape the area early.

## Backpacks for children

Children like to carry their own backpack, and this strengthens the feeling of belonging. But it should remain light. As a rule of thumb, a child's filled backpack should weigh no more than about a tenth of their body weight. Models with 8 to 15 liters, padded shoulder straps, a chest strap and reflective elements are well suited. Include a small water bottle, a snack, a light jacket and perhaps a favorite item such as a magnifying glass or binoculars. The adults carry the heavy water and equipment for everyone.

## Save weight without sacrificing safety

Anyone who regularly travels for longer periods quickly learns that every kilo counts. A simple first step is a kitchen scale: weigh each piece of equipment and write the values ​​in a list. Many people are amazed at how heavy toiletry bags, a change of clothes or a pillow become. Many trekkers talk about the **base weight**, the weight of the equipment without water, provisions and fuel. You can save the most on the large items: backpack, tent and sleeping system consisting of a sleeping bag and sleeping pad. Little things like outer packaging, an oversized first aid bag with doubles, or a second pair of shoes also add up.

In a group you can share equipment: a stove, a first aid kit, sunscreen for everyone. What you should never leave out are rain gear, a warm layer, first aid supplies, a headlamp and enough water. Traveling light is pleasant, but only as long as you don't freeze when the weather changes.

## With your backpack on trains and planes

On trains and buses, the backpack belongs in the luggage rack or between the seats, not in the aisle. You put the poles together in the side of your backpack, with the tips pointing downwards and preferably with rubber caps. If you're flying on a hiking trip, protect straps and buckles with a transport cover or a large duffel bag so that they don't get caught on the luggage carousel. Pocket knives and hiking sticks belong in checked luggage. Gas cartridges for the stove are not allowed to fly with you; you buy them at the destination.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I also use a daypack for a hut tour?

If you pack very light and the backpack is 30 liters or more, yes. A good hip belt is important because the load with water, food and a change of clothes can quickly reach seven to nine kilograms.

### Do I need a hydration bladder?

That's a matter of taste. A hydration bladder lets you drink as you walk and helps you hydrate regularly. Bottles are easier to fill and clean and show you how much you've drunk.

### How do I care for my backpack?

Empty it after each tour, shake out crumbs and sand, wipe with a damp cloth and leave to dry in an airy place. For thorough cleaning, lukewarm water with mild soap is sufficient. You should only use the washing machine if the manufacturer allows it.

### How do I find out my back length?

Let us help you: Tilt your head forward and feel for the clearly protruding vertebra at the transition from the neck to the back. From there, a second person measures along the spine to the imaginary line between the top edges of your pelvic bones. Compare this length with the manufacturer’s information. In specialist shops this is often done with a special tape measure.
