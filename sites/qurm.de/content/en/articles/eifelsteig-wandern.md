The Eifelsteig is a long-distance hiking trail with quiet sounds. It has no high mountain peaks and no famous castles on the river, but a landscape that completely changes every two days. It stretches for around 313 kilometers from Aachen-Kornelimünster to Trier, from the high moors of the High Fens over dams and beech forests in the Eifel National Park, past limestone cliffs, volcanic cones and deep blue maars, until it ends at the Porta Nigra in Germany's oldest city. Anyone who enjoys long, varied hikes and without crowds will find one of the most beautiful trails in the country here.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Around 313 kilometers in 15 stages, from Aachen-Kornelimünster to Trier, marked throughout and recognized as a premium route.
> - Stages usually between 15 and 28 kilometers, with noticeable but rarely extreme differences in altitude.
> - Landscapes: High Fens, Rurtal and Rursee, Eifel National Park, Kalkeifel, Vulkaneifel with maars, Kylltal.
> - Best time: May to October. In the Fens, boardwalks can be slippery after rain and in winter.
> - Some stages are long and sparsely populated. Plan provisions and accommodation in advance.

## The course

### High Fens and Rurtal

Right at the beginning, the Eifelsteig leads into the border area with Belgium, onto the plateau of the High Fens. The raised moor with its cotton grass meadows, wooden walkways and low birch trees seems almost Scandinavian. It is a sensitive landscape in which you must definitely stay on the paths. Then it goes down into the Rur valley to Monschau, a town with half-timbered houses and slate roofs, nestled tightly in the valley.

### Rursee and Eifel National Park

The following stages lead to the Rursee, a branched reservoir, and the Eifel National Park. On the Kermeter, a large beech forest ridge, you hike through a forest that is allowed to develop into wilderness again. Dead wood remains and paths blocked by fallen trees are sometimes relocated. Therefore, pay attention to current information from the national park.

### Kalkeifel: Blankenheim and Gerolstein

Behind Gemünd the landscape becomes more open. Blankenheim with the source of the Ahr in the middle of the town is a nice stopover. In the Kalkeifel around Gerolstein, bright dolomite rocks rise from the forests, and several viewing points offer wide views. This shows that the Eifel was once a seabed: fossils can be found in the limestone layers.

### Vulkaneifel: Maare and Manderscheid

For many, this section is the highlight. Near Daun, the Daun Maars are close together, circular lakes in explosion funnels of former volcanoes. The Weinfelder Maar with its small chapel on the bank is one of the most famous photo motifs in the Eifel. We continue to Manderscheid with two castle ruins above the Lieser valley.

### Kylltal to Trier

The final stages follow the Kyll valley with its meanders, rock faces and forests. Shortly before the destination you reach the heights above Trier and descend into the city, where the Porta Nigra and the Roman buildings provide a worthy finale.

## Character and demands

| aspect | Assessment |

The Eifelsteig is well suited for people who have already done a few day hikes of 20 kilometers and are now taking the step on their first long-distance hike. The stages can be divided in many places. [Calculate walking time](/ratgeber/gehzeit-berechnen/) explains how you can realistically estimate walking times for longer stages.

## Planning

**Stages:** The official classification includes 15 stages. If you want to walk less than 20 kilometers a day, divide long stages and plan three weeks instead of two. Many hosts along the trail offer pick-up and drop-off services to the waypoint. Ask about this when booking.

**Accommodation:** In small stage towns there are often only a few beds. Book early, especially for weekends and public holidays. Package deals with luggage transport are organized by regional providers.

**Provisions and water:** There are sometimes no stops between places for hours. Take enough water and provisions with you, as described in [Provisions and Drinking While Hiking](/ratgeber/proviant-und-trinken/), and check the opening times of inns in advance, as many have rest days.

**Getting there:** Aachen and Trier are easily accessible by train. Many stage locations in the middle, on the other hand, are located away from the railway lines and can only be reached by bus, which rarely runs on weekends. It's worth taking a closer look at the timetables for individual stages. Tips can be found in [Hiking without a car](/ratgeber/wandern-ohne-auto/).

## Seasons

- **Spring:** Daffodil blossoms in some stream valleys in the Northern Eifel, fresh beech green, pleasant temperatures. In the Fens, paths can still be wet.
- **Summer:** Shady forests make even warm days bearable, and the maars invite you to cool off. Swimming is only permitted in designated areas.
- **Autumn:** golden beech forests, fog over the maars, clear views. For many, the most beautiful time of year.
- **Winter:** The High Fens can be snowy and is particularly quiet then. Boardwalks become slippery, days are short and many accommodations have company holidays.

## Consideration in protected areas

The Eifelsteig leads through the Eifel National Park, through nature reserves and through the sensitive raised moor of the Fens. In parts of the Fens, access restrictions apply at times, for example when there is a risk of forest fires in spring. Stay on the marked paths, take your trash with you, keep dogs on a leash and follow local signs. Spending the night in the open air is not permitted. Our article about [trekking places in Germany](/ratgeber/trekkingplaetze-deutschland/) explains where it is legally possible to sleep in the open air.

## On the move in the High Fens

The first stages are particularly scenic, but have their own challenges. The Fens is high and open and the weather can be harsher here than in the valleys. Fog often rolls in quickly and then the paths and horizon become blurred. The boardwalks across the moor are slippery after rain and hoarfrost. Waterproof shoes with good tread are more important here than on any other part of the trail, and a warm layer should be in your backpack even in summer.

Next to the footbridges the ground is often deeply wet. So stay on the wood even when things get tight. Anyone who steps aside damages the moor vegetation, which takes many decades to grow, and can get stuck in the mud.

## Walk the Eifelsteig in sections

Many people don't have two or three weeks off at a time. The Eifelsteig is easy to hike on weekends or short vacations. These blocks have proven themselves:

- **North Eifel:** Kornelimünster to Monschau and on to Rursee, with Venn and Rurtal.
- **National Park:** from Rursee over the Kermeter to Gemünd.
- **Kalkeifel:** Blankenheim to Gerolstein with the dolomite rocks.
- **Volcanic Eifel:** Gerolstein via Daun and the maars to Manderscheid.
- **Kylltal:** from Manderscheid to Trier.

You need two to four days for each block. Getting to the intermediate stages sometimes requires taking a bus and train with a change, so plan time for this on the day of arrival.

## Arrival and return journey by bus and train

The Eifelsteig begins in Aachen-Kornelimünster and ends in Trier; both places are easily accessible by bus and train. On the way, the path crosses several railway lines, such as the Eifel line between Cologne and Trier, on which places like Gerolstein are located. This makes it easy to walk the route in sections or to shorten a stage if the weather changes.

With the Deutschlandticket you can use regional trains and many bus lines in the Eifel. In rural areas, buses sometimes only run rarely, especially on weekends. So plan your return journey in advance and save the connections.

## Refreshments and accommodations

Along the Eifelsteig there are inns, guesthouses and hotels that are geared towards hikers. In smaller towns, however, restaurants often have fixed days off, often at the beginning of the week. Before each stage, check where you can stop along the way and take enough food and water with you. In high season and on weekends you should book accommodation well in advance.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do I need to climb the Eifelsteig?

Anyone who walks the 15 official stages will be on the road for two weeks, with a rest day for a good 15 to 16 days. You should plan on three weeks with shorter day periods.

### Which stage is suitable for a day trip?

The stages in the Vulkaneifel around the Dauner Maare and the stage into the High Fens are particularly varied and easy to reach from Daun or Monschau.

### Do I need hiking poles?

Not necessarily, but they relieve the strain on your knees on the many descents into stream valleys. How to use them correctly can be found in [Use hiking poles correctly](/ratgeber/wanderstoecke-richtig-nutzen/).

### Is the Eifelsteig well marked?

Yes, the Eifelsteig is marked throughout with its own signpost, and there are signposts at junctions. A map or an app with the route is still useful, especially in the High Fens, where fog can make orientation difficult.

### Can I hike on the Eifelsteig with luggage transport?

Yes, regional providers organize package tours with booked accommodation and luggage transport from stage to stage. So you only hike with a light daypack. The Eifel is also well suited for individualists because many accommodations are close to the route.

### Which stage is particularly beautiful?

That depends on taste. Many hikers appreciate the sections through the High Fens with its moorland, the stages around Lake Rursee and the Vulkaneifel with its maars. Read the descriptions of the stages and choose according to landscape, length and journey.

For a first impression, a day's route that you can reach and leave by train is suitable.

### Can my dog ​​go on the Eifelsteig?

Yes, the trail is generally dog-friendly, and many accommodations in the Eifel accept dogs, often for a small additional charge. However, in the Eifel National Park and in nature reserves such as the High Fens, leashes are required so that wild and breeding birds can have peace and quiet. Plan enough water for your dog on long journeys, as there are not streams everywhere, and when booking each accommodation, specifically ask whether dogs are welcome.
