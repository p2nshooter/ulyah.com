A night in a mountain hut is one of the most beautiful experiences in the mountains. In the evening, people from all over the world sit at long wooden tables, there are cheese dumplings or lentils with spaetzle, outside the sky turns red, and when the stars come out, it is as quiet as it ever gets in the valley. The next morning you start early, with cool air and the whole day ahead of you. Anyone who spends the night in a hut for the first time has many questions: Do I have to make a reservation? What is a cabin sleeping bag? How's the food going? This guide explains the processes and unwritten rules.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Make your reservations well in advance, often weeks or months in advance during high season and on weekends. Many huts use an online reservation system.
> - A sleeping bag is mandatory in Alpine club huts. Blankets and pillows are available.
> - There are sleeping places in the camp (multi-bed room) or in small rooms. Earplugs and a headlamp belong in your backpack.
> - The usual quiet time in the huts is from 10 p.m. Mountain boots stay in the boot room, wet clothes go in the drying room.
> - If you can't come: cancel. This is the only way someone else can get your spot.

## Which huts are there?

In the Alpine region, many huts are operated by the sections of the Alpine clubs, such as the German or Austrian Alpine Club. There are also private mountain inns and alpine huts with overnight stays. In the low mountain ranges you will find hiking homes run by hiking clubs and nature lovers' houses.

Alpine club huts are divided into categories that reveal something about their location and facilities: from simple, high-altitude huts that can only be reached on foot and have basic supplies, to easily accessible houses with more comfort. The higher and more remote the hut, the easier it is. Water, electricity and showers are often scarce there.

## Reserve

Most huts are open in summer, depending on the altitude, from around mid-June to the end of September. Popular huts on well-known tours are fully booked early on weekends and holidays. This is how you do it:

1. **Reserve early**, for well-known multi-day tours often in winter or spring.
2. **Online, by email or by telephone:** Many Alpine Club huts use an online system through which you can see available places. Smaller cabins accept reservations by phone or email.
3. **Please provide information:** Number of people, camp or room, half board desired, membership in the Alpine Club, dietary requirements.
4. **Please note deposit:** Some huts require a deposit, which will be forfeited if canceled at short notice.
5. **Cancellation if plans change:** Weather, illness, change of plans, call or cancel online. Places that have not been canceled will otherwise remain empty, while others will have been turned away.

Without a reservation, you will not be turned away from Alpine club huts in bad weather or in emergencies, but this could mean a place on a bench or the ground.

## What belongs in the backpack

- **Cabin sleeping bag:** a thin sleeping bag made of cotton, silk or synthetic fiber that lies between you and the hut blanket. It is mandatory in Alpine club huts for hygienic reasons.
- **Earplugs:** In a camp of 20 people, someone is always snoring.
- **Headlamp:** for going to the toilet at night without waking everyone up.
- **Hut shoes:** Many huts provide hut slippers; your own light shoes are more comfortable.
- **Small travel size towel and toiletry bag**.
- **Change of clothing** for the evening so that sweaty hiking clothes can dry.
- **Cash:** Not every hut accepts cards, and cell service is not available everywhere.
- **ID and, if applicable, membership card** of the Alpine Club.
- **Charging cable and small power bank:** Sockets are rare in many huts.

[How to pack a hiking backpack correctly](/ratgeber/rucksack-packen-und-einstellen/) explains how you can stow everything away well.

## Arrive at the hut

1. **Shoes off:** Mountain boots go in the boot room, sticks and often backpacks stay in the entrance area. You enter the hut in hut shoes.
2. **Register:** Report to the hut staff, state your name and the reservation. You will be assigned your camp or room.
3. **Enter:** Many huts keep a hut book or ask about your next destination. This is important for safety: If you don't arrive, someone knows where to look.
4. **Dry wet things:** Hang up wet jackets, socks and shoes in the drying room. Take all of your belongings back with you in the morning.

## Eating at the hut

Most huts offer **half board**: dinner with several courses and breakfast. Dinner is usually at a fixed time for everyone. You can also eat according to the menu. Many Alpine Club huts offer inexpensive, simple **mountaineering meals** for members and often cheap tea water to fill up the thermos flask.

If you eat vegetarian, vegan or have allergies, please indicate this when making your reservation. Huts at high altitudes are served by cable car, material lift or helicopter. This explains prices and a limited selection. Eating your own food in the guest room is not welcome in many huts. If in doubt, ask.

## Sleeping in camp

In the **warehouse**, also known as the mattress warehouse, several mattresses are located next to each other, sometimes on two levels. It's cheap and comfortable, but cramped. Two to six people sleep in **rooms**, often in bunk beds.

Consideration in the warehouse:

- Pack your things for the evening and the next morning in advance so that you don't end up rummaging through rustling bags at night.
- Stick to the **hut quiet**, usually from 10 p.m.
- Those who leave very early pack the evening before and leave the camp quietly.
- Ventilation is good, but coordinate with the others.

## Water, electricity and garbage

In many huts, water is precious. It comes from a source, from rainwater or has to be brought up with great effort. Showers are often only available for a fee and with limited time, and in some huts they are not available at all. Electricity is often generated using solar systems or generators. Take your rubbish back into the valley with you. Every can that remains at the hut has to be transported away with great effort.

## Winter rooms

Many Alpine Club huts have a **winter room** that is also open out of season or accessible with an Alpine Club key. It is simply furnished, mostly with blankets and a stove, but no utilities. Winter rooms are intended for experienced mountaineers on winter and ski tours and are not an alternative to reservations in summer.

## The first night in a hut with children

For families, the first night in a hut is an adventure. Choose a hut that can be reached within a few meters of altitude, ask about family rooms and plan enough time for arrival. Tips for this can be found in [The first night in the hut with children](/ratgeber/erste-huettennacht-mit-kindern/).

## Hut sleeping bag and hygiene

A sleeping bag is mandatory in Alpine club huts. It is placed under the blankets of the cabin and ensures that the bed linen does not have to be washed after each guest, saving water and energy. Lightweight models made of silk, cotton or synthetic fibers only weigh a few hundred grams.

Washing is only possible to a limited extent in many huts. Bring biodegradable soap, a small towel and wet wipes in case of emergency and do not dispose of the wet wipes in the cabin toilet.

## Cabin peace and consideration

Most huts are closed from around 10 p.m. If you want to start early the next morning, pack your backpack in the evening and leave the camp quietly. A headlamp with red light disturbs sleeping neighbors less than a bright beam of light.

In the camp, consideration counts: plastic bags rustle loudly, alarm clocks should vibrate instead of ringing, and conversations belong in the guest room. Earplugs help if someone snores.

## Pay at the hut

You can now pay by card at many huts, but not at all, because the network and technology are not a given at high altitudes. Therefore, take enough cash with you for accommodation, food and drinks. Alpine club members usually pay reduced overnight rates at the Alpine club's huts.

## Cancel and cancel

If your plans change, cancel the reservation as early as possible. Many huts have waiting lists, and a free bed can allow another hiker to join the tour. Uncancelled reservations are a big problem for hut owners, and some huts charge a fee for last-minute cancellations or no-shows.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to be a member of the Alpine Club?

No. Alpine club huts are open to everyone. However, members pay significantly less for overnight stays, sometimes have priority when it comes to space allocation and receive discounts on meals. For multi-day tours, membership often pays for itself after just a few nights.

### When do I have to arrive at the hut?

Plan your arrival in the afternoon, at the latest before dinner. If you are late, call the hut. Otherwise there is a risk of worry and your place may be taken.

### Can I stay in the hut with my dog?

Many huts do not allow dogs in the camp. Some cabins have special rooms for guests with dogs. Ask specifically when making your reservation.

### Can I shower in the hut?

Not everywhere. Many huts only have basic washrooms because water and energy are scarce. Where there are showers, they are often chargeable and have a limited time. In high-altitude huts, expect a cat wash.

### Is there vegetarian food in huts?

Yes, in most huts, often vegan dishes too. If you have special dietary requirements or allergies, it is best to mention this when making your reservation.

### How early should I leave in the morning?

The day starts early in huts. Breakfast is often served from 6 or 6.30 a.m., sometimes even earlier for busy tours. Especially in summer, it's worth setting off early because thunderstorms usually form in the afternoon and the climb is easier in the cool of the morning. Arrange a very early start the evening before with the hut staff; Some huts then prepare a simple breakfast to take away. It's best to pay in the evening so you don't have to look for anyone in the morning.

### Can I leave my luggage at the hut while I go on a summit tour?

If you stay in the same hut for two nights, this is usually not a problem: speak to the staff and leave your luggage neatly packed in the place shown to you. Even if you only stay one night and pass the hut on the way back, you can often leave excess luggage in the entrance area. Ask beforehand and always take valuables, ID and money with you to the summit.
