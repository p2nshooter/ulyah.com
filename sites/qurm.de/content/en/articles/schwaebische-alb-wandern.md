Anyone who looks south from Stuttgart will see a long, wooded wall on the horizon: the Albtrauf, the steep northern edge of the Swabian Alb. Behind it lies a plateau with juniper heaths, flocks of sheep, beech forests and wide fields, crossed by deep valleys. Beneath the surface lies a world of caves where Ice Age people created some of humanity's oldest works of art. There are also castles on rock spurs, waterfalls and springs in bright blue.

The Swabian Alb stretches for around 200 kilometers across Baden-Württemberg to the edge of Bavaria. It is not a high mountain range, its highest peaks reach a good 1,000 meters. For day tours and hikes lasting several days, it is one of the most varied low mountain ranges in Germany. This guide presents the most beautiful destinations and helps with planning.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The Albtrauf is the steep northwestern edge of the Alb with rocks, viewpoints and castles. Many of the most beautiful paths are located here.
> - The Alb is a karst mountain range: caves, springs like the Blautopf and seeping rivers are part of it.
> - The caves in the Ach and Lone valleys with their Ice Age art are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
> - The Albsteig follows the northern edge of the Alb for around 350 kilometers and is one of the largest long-distance hiking trails in southern Germany.
> - It is often windier and cooler on the plateau than in the foothills. A warm layer belongs in your backpack even in summer.

## Landscape and creation

The Swabian Alb consists primarily of limestone that was deposited around 150 million years ago at the bottom of a tropical sea. Where sponge reefs grew back then, particularly hard rocks were formed, which today protrude from the slopes as rocks. Over time, the landscape rose and tilted slightly to the southeast. On the north-western edge, erosion is still eating away at the edge to this day, forming the Albtrauf, which sometimes rises several hundred meters above the foreland.

Because lime is dissolved by water, rain on the plateau quickly seeps into the ground. Streams are rare there, but there are underground river systems, caves and sinkholes. Where the water comes to light again, powerful springs like the Blautopf arise. A special natural spectacle lies near Immendingen: There, the young Danube completely or partially seeps into its bed on many days of the year, and some of its water emerges again a few kilometers further south in the Aachtopf.

## The most beautiful destinations

### At the nightmare

The Albtrauf is the most spectacular side of the Alb. You hike along narrow paths along the edge of the slope, from one viewing rock to the next, with a view over the foreland to the Black Forest. The sections around Bad Urach with the **Uracher Waterfall**, which plunges over a moss-covered limestone tuff edge into the depths, and the area around Lenningen and Beuren with their rocks and castle ruins are particularly well known. Many paths on the eaves are steep and rooty, but offer a new view at almost every bend.

### Hohenzollern Castle and Lichtenstein Castle

Two of the most famous buildings in southern Germany are on the Alb. The **Hohenzollern Castle** sits enthroned on an isolated mountain cone near Hechingen and can be seen from afar on clear days. Paths lead up to the castle from below, and you have one of the most beautiful views of it from the Zeller Horn, a viewing point on the eaves. **Lichtenstein Castle** sits on a narrow rock high above the Echaztal. It was built in the 19th century, inspired by Wilhelm Hauff's novel "Lichtenstein", and is still a fairytale castle straight out of a picture book.

### Caves: from stalactite caves to world heritage

There are thousands of caves in the Alb. Some have been developed as show caves, such as the **Bear Cave** and the **Nebelhöhle** in the Sonnenbühl and Lichtenstein area, where you can see stalactites and traces of Ice Age cave bears. The caves in the **Achtal** near Blaubeuren and in the **Lonetal** near Niederstotzingen are particularly special. In them, archaeologists found figurines made of mammoth ivory and flutes made of bone, which are around 40,000 years old and are among the oldest works of art and musical instruments known to mankind. Since 2017, these caves have been a UNESCO World Heritage Site as “Caves and Ice Age Art of the Swabian Alb”. The caves themselves are mostly protected; You can see the finds in museums in the region, and hiking trails lead through the valleys to the cave entrances.

### The Blautopf in Blaubeuren

The Blautopf is a karst spring on the edge of Blaubeuren, whose water glows an intense blue depending on the light. Behind it lies an extensive cave system that can only be explored by experienced cave divers. A short path leads around the spring pot, and from here you can take a beautiful hike onto the rocks above the Blautal. Bathing and entering the water are prohibited.

### Juniper heaths and the biosphere area

**Juniper heaths** are typical for the plateau: open, lean slopes with juniper bushes, silver thistles, gentians and orchids. They were created through centuries of sheep grazing and are still cared for today by itinerant shepherds. A large part of the middle Alb belongs to the **Swabian Alb Biosphere Reserve**, which has been recognized by UNESCO. In the center is the former Münsingen military training area, a wide, less fragmented landscape that can now be hiked on designated paths. Be sure to stay on the paths there, as there may still be remains of old ammunition lying in the ground.

## The Albsteig

If you want to spend several days on the trail, you will find one of southern Germany's classic long-distance hiking trails in the **Albsteig**. It follows the northern edge of the Alb for around 350 kilometers from Donauwörth to Tuttlingen and is looked after by the Swabian Alb Association, one of the largest hiking associations in Germany, which was founded in 1888. The path is also known as Hauptwanderweg 1. It connects many of the highlights mentioned: eaves, castles, caves and viewing rocks. Most people do it in sections, as there are train or bus connections at many stage locations. [Plan your first long-distance hike](/ratgeber/erste-fernwanderung-planen/) describes how you plan such a tour.

## Arrival and mobility

Many starting points on the Albtrauf are in valleys that are accessible by railway, such as the Ermstal to Bad Urach, the Blautal with Blaubeuren or the Lauter valley. On the plateau, buses connect the towns, although often less frequently on weekends. During the hiking season, additional leisure buses run in some regions to take hikers and cyclists to the starting points. Check the timetables in advance and plan your tour from the end, as described in [Hiking without a car](/ratgeber/wandern-ohne-auto/).

## Weather on the Alb

The plateau of the Alb is significantly higher than the foothills, and you can feel it. It is often a few degrees cooler, windier and snowier in winter than in Stuttgart or Ulm. It is not without reason that the Alb is popularly referred to as a rough country. Fog can linger for a long time on autumn days while it is clear down in the valley, or vice versa. So take a warm layer and a rain jacket with you, even in summer. In midsummer, thunderstorms move across the open plateaus; [Thunderstorm while hiking](/ratgeber/gewitter-beim-wandern/) explains what to do then.

## The best season

- **Spring:** In April and May, pasqueflowers and orchids bloom on the heaths, the beech forests on the eaves sprout, and the waterfalls have lots of water.
- **Summer:** Long days, juniper heaths full of flowers and butterflies. It gets hot in the open areas, the forests on the eaves offer shade.
- **Autumn:** The beech forests change color, silver thistles open on the heaths, and the distant view from the eaves is often excellent.
- **Winter:** There is snow on the plateau in many years, and there are cross-country ski trails and winter hiking trails. Steep eaves paths can be icy and dangerous.

## Consideration on the way

The juniper heaths and rocks of the Alb are sensitive habitats with rare plants. Do not pick flowers, especially orchids, gentians or thistles, which are protected areas. Close pasture gates behind you, keep dogs on a leash when there are flocks of sheep and follow the shepherds' instructions. How you behave around herds with livestock guard dogs is written in [Cows on the Pasture](/ratgeber/kuehe-auf-der-weide/). Some rocks are closed to peregrine falcons and eagle owls during the breeding season; the closures are signposted.

## With children on the Alb

The Alb is a great destination for families. Show caves, castles, waterfalls and streams with flat banks make short hikes an experience. Many caves and castles are just a short walk from the car park or bus stop and can be connected to form small circuits. Hold children's hands at the eaves, as many viewing rocks are not secured. More ideas can be found in [Hiking with school children](/ratgeber/wandern-mit-schulkindern/).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the highest mountain in the Swabian Alb?

The highest peak is Lemberg near Gosheim at over 1,000 meters. In its surroundings, on the Großer Heuberg, there are several peaks that exceed the thousand meter mark. A lookout tower on Lviv offers a wide panoramic view.

### Can I see Ice Age art from the caves?

Yes. The most famous finds, including small figurines made from mammoth ivory, are exhibited in museums in the region and in Stuttgart. In Blaubeuren, a museum shows finds from the caves of the Achtal. A hike through the Ach or Lone valley to the cave entrances can easily be combined with a visit to the museum.

### How strenuous are the paths on the Albtrauf?

The routes are mostly moderate, but the change between valley and plateau often means short, steep climbs of 200 to 400 meters in altitude. The paths are narrow in places, rooty and slippery when wet. With basic fitness and sturdy shoes, they are easily doable. [Calculate walking time](/ratgeber/gehzeit-berechnen/) explains how you can realistically estimate your walking time.

### Are there overnight stays in hiking hostels on the Alb?

Yes. The Swabian Alb Association and other associations operate several hiking homes with overnight accommodation, which are particularly popular with groups and families. There are also inns, guesthouses and holiday apartments in many places.
