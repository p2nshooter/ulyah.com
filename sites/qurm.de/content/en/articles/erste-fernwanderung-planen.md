At some point the day trip is no longer enough. You want to start walking in the morning, arrive somewhere else in the evening and continue walking the next day, several days in a row, until you have a whole way behind you. A long-distance hike changes hiking: everyday life moves far away, the day takes on a simple rhythm, and you experience a landscape in its entire context. To ensure that your first multi-day tour is a wonderful experience and does not fail due to blisters, stages that are too long or accommodations that are fully booked, it is worth planning carefully. This guide will guide you through the most important decisions.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Well-marked trails lasting three to seven days with good infrastructure and rail connections are suitable for starters.
> - Plan the stages shorter than your longest day tours. 12 to 20 kilometers a day is a good framework for the first tour.
> - Decide early on whether you will carry your luggage yourself or use a luggage transport service. This determines your backpack, stages and budget.
> - Book accommodation for weekends, vacations and public holidays in good time.
> - Test your shoes, backpack and stage length beforehand on two or three long day hikes.

## Step 1: Find the right path

These features are helpful for your first long-distance hike:

- **Manageable length:** three to seven hiking days. This way you can test how your body reacts to several days in a row.
- **Good marking:** A continuously signposted path takes away your worries about orientation. Quality trails and premium trails are particularly reliably marked. What the signs mean is explained in [Understanding Waymarks](/ratgeber/wegmarkierungen-verstehen/).
- **Infrastructure:** Places with accommodation and shopping facilities approximately 15 to 20 kilometers apart.
- **Exit options:** Train stations or bus lines on the way if the weather, knees or motivation strike.

Good beginner trails in Germany include the [Harzer Hexenstieg](/ratgeber/harzer-hexenstieg/) with around 100 kilometers, the [Malerweg](/ratgeber/malerweg-saechsische-schweiz/) in Saxon Switzerland with eight short stages or individual sections of longer trails such as [Rheinsteig](/ratgeber/rheinsteig-wandern/) or [Eifelsteig](/ratgeber/eifelsteig-wandern/). The [Rennsteig](/ratgeber/rennsteig-wandern/) is longer, but runs on a ridge with few steep sections.

## Step 2: Set stages

The most common mistake is to plan the stages too long. A route that you do well on a single day feels completely different the third day in a row with tired legs and a backpack.

| Experience | A sensible day’s route |
|---|---|
| First multi-day tour without heavy luggage | 12 to 18 km, up to around 600 meters altitude |
| First multi-day tour with your own luggage | 10 to 15 km |
| Practiced, good condition | 18 to 25 km |

Always calculate the walking time based on altitude, as described in [Calculate walking time](/ratgeber/gehzeit-berechnen/), and plan breaks and sightseeing accordingly. From five days onwards it is worth taking a rest day or a deliberately short day in the middle. Many official stage classifications are intended for experienced hikers. You can share them at any time.

## Step 3: Book accommodation

At the end of each stage you need a bed. The options range from hotels and inns to guest houses, holiday apartments and youth hostels to hiking huts. In the Alps you spend the night in mountain huts, as explained in [Sleeping in a hut](/ratgeber/huettenuebernachtung/). If you want to sleep outside, you can find legal places for a tent at [trekking sites](/ratgeber/trekkingplaetze-deutschland/).

Tips for booking:

- First decide on the stage locations, then book, using the most scarce accommodation. In small places with few beds, the whole planning is decided.
- Ask if hosts will pick up hikers from the trail and bring them back the next morning. This is common on many trails and opens up accommodations off the route.
- Clarify whether there will be dinner in the evening or where the nearest restaurant is, and whether you can buy provisions for the next day.
- Be aware of cancellation policies in case you need to postpone the tour due to weather or injury.

## Step 4: With or without luggage transport?

On many well-known long-distance hiking trails, companies offer luggage transport: your suitcase travels to the next accommodation every morning and you only hike with a daypack. There are often complete package deals with pre-booked accommodation.

| | Carry your own luggage | Luggage transport |
|---|---|---|
| Backpack | 30 to 40 liters, 7 to 10 kg | 20 to 25 liters, 3 to 5 kg |
| flexibility | high, stages can be changed spontaneously | low, stage locations fixed |
| Costs | lower | higher |
| Burden | significantly higher, especially uphill | small amount |
| feeling | independent, “real” way | relaxed enjoyment |

Both are good choices. For the first tour with many meters of altitude, transporting luggage is a great relief. If you carry it yourself, our guide [Hiking backpack: size, adjustment and packing] (/ratgeber/rucksack-packen-und-einstellen/) will help you.

## Step 5: Packing list for a week with accommodation

Pack according to the principle: as little as possible, as much as necessary. You wash clothes in the sink on the go.

- broken-in hiking boots and light shoes for the evening
- two to three sets of functional underwear and hiking socks
- hiking pants, shorts or zip-offs
- a warming layer of fleece or wool
- Rain jacket and, depending on the season, rain pants, see [Rain jacket for hiking](/ratgeber/regenjacke-wandern/)
- Cap, sun hat, sunglasses, sunscreen
- First aid kit with blister plaster, see [Hiking First Aid Kit](/ratgeber/erste-hilfe-set-wandern/)
- Drinking bottles for at least 1.5 liters
- Mobile phone with offline map, charging cable, small power bank
- ID, health insurance card, cash, copies of bookings
- Travel size toiletry bag, small amount of detergent, clothesline

## Step 6: Prepare and train

In the weeks leading up to the trip, go on two or three long day hikes with the shoes and backpack you plan to take with you, ideally on two consecutive days. This way you can see whether shoes are pinching, belts are rubbing or whether the planned stage length is right. You must break in new hiking boots first, as described in [Buy hiking boots](/ratgeber/wanderschuhe-kaufen/).

## On the go: small rules, big impact

- Start early in the morning. Then you have time in reserve and can do the shorter pieces in the midday heat.
- At the first pressure point, take care of your feet immediately, not just when there is a blister.
- Drink regularly, even if you are not thirsty.
- Let someone at home know where you are going.
- Give yourself the freedom to shorten a stage. Arriving is more important than persevering.

## After the first tour

After you return, record what went well and what didn't: Were the stages too long or too short? What did you never need, what was missing? How did your feet and back feel? These notes are the best basis for your next long-distance hike. Many people notice after the first tour that they want more and dare to take a longer route or a tour with their own luggage the next year.

## Prepare feet, knees and back

On a long-distance hike, it's often not fitness, but feet, knees and back that decide whether you're happy or frustrated. Break in your shoes well beforehand, wear suitable hiking socks and tape sensitive areas at the first sign of redness, before a blister forms. Hiking poles relieve the strain on the knees, especially when descending.

Keep your backpack as light as possible. Every kilogram you don't carry protects your back and joints. Before you leave, check each item: Do you really need it every day? You can buy many things like detergent or replacement batteries on the go.

## If something goes wrong

Even the best planning does not protect against rainy days, blisters or a cold. Therefore, plan a buffer day right from the start and find out where buses and trains run along the route. This means you can shorten a stage or take a rest day without canceling the entire tour.

Save the phone numbers of your accommodations and give your route planning to a trusted person. If you're traveling alone, it's best to check in briefly every evening.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a long-distance hike cost?

This mainly depends on the accommodation. The cheapest are youth hostels, nature friends' houses and trekking sites, the most expensive are package tours with a hotel and luggage transport. Compare the region's offers before you book.

### Can I go on a long-distance hike alone?

Yes. This is safe on well-marked paths with accommodation and is a special experience for many. Leave your route with friends or family and get in touch briefly in the evening.

### What time of year is best for your first tour?

Late spring and early fall. The temperatures are pleasant, the days are long enough and the accommodation is mostly open.

### How many kilometers a day are realistic?

For beginners, about 15 to 20 kilometers a day is usually easily manageable on moderately difficult terrain. Many meters in altitude, difficult terrain or heat significantly shorten the sensible route. Plan the first few days to be shorter and increase as you notice how your body reacts.

### Do I have to book accommodation in advance?

In high season and on popular routes, yes, especially on weekends and in places with few accommodations. Out of season you can be more flexible, but you should still secure the next one or two nights. Keep booking confirmations handy.

### Which paths are suitable for your first long-distance hike?

Well-marked paths with moderate stages, plenty of accommodation and good bus and train connections are ideal. In Germany, for example, there are sections of the Rheinsteig, the Rennsteig or the Malerweg. You can find more ideas in our route descriptions such as [Rennsteig hiking](/ratgeber/rennsteig-wandern/) or [Malerweg in Saxon Switzerland](/ratgeber/malerweg-saechsische-schweiz/).

### What should definitely be in your first aid kit?

Blister plasters, tape, a small disinfectant, painkillers that you can tolerate, personal medications, sunscreen and insect repellent. You can find a detailed list in the guide [first aid kit for hiking] (/ratgeber/erste-hilfe-set-wandern/).

### Is an app enough or do I need a hiking guide?

Both complement each other. A printed hiking guide or the trail's official website provides stage descriptions, accommodation lists and background information, while an app with downloaded maps shows where you are along the way. Download the maps offline before the tour, as reception is often weak in valleys and forests, and take a power bank with you. A paper map at a scale of 1:25,000 or 1:50,000 as a reserve weighs little and does not require a battery.
