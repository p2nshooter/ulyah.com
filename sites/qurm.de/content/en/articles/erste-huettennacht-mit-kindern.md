“Are we really sleeping on the mountain today?” This question, asked with shining eyes on the way up, is one of the most beautiful moments of family life. A night in a hut is a real adventure for children: mattresses, dinner at long tables with strangers, a sunset over the peaks, a starry sky that you never see in the city, and breakfast in the morning while there is still fog down in the valley. To ensure that the adventure doesn't turn into a strenuous night, it's important to choose the hut, a short approach and good preparation.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Choose a hut with a short, easy approach: for school children about 300 to 600 meters in altitude on a blue path.
> - Family-friendly cabins have family rooms, play facilities and children's meals. Many Alpine clubs mark such huts.
> - Make reservations early and ask specifically for a family room instead of a large warehouse.
> - Packing list for children: sleeping bag, headlamp, slippers, warm pajamas, cuddly toy, small game.
> - Plan for a quiet afternoon and have a plan B for bad weather.

## Find the right hut

For the first night, less is more. Good cabins for families have these features:

- **Short approach:** Two to three hours of walking with children, on a wide, well-marked path with no exposed areas. Preferably a blue path, as described in [mountain paths blue, red, black](/ratgeber/bergwege-schwierigkeit/).
- **Moderate altitude:** For the first night, a hut in the range of around 1,200 to 1,800 meters is sufficient. This is high enough for a mountain experience, and children usually sleep well there.
- **Family room:** A private room with four to six beds is much more relaxing for the first night than a camp with 30 strangers.
- **Something to discover:** A stream, a small lake, rocks for climbing, animals on the alpine pasture or a playground next to the hut.
- **Emergency Accessibility:** Having a supply vehicle driveway or material ropeway nearby is reassuring if a child becomes ill.

Many Alpine club sections distinguish particularly family-friendly huts. Tourist information centers and hut keepers are also happy to provide information. If you don't want to go to the Alps yet, you can spend the first night in a hiking hostel in the low mountain range.

## Reserve

Family rooms are limited and go quickly on weekends and holidays. So make your reservation early, state the number and age of the children and ask:

- family room or small warehouse,
- children's portions and vegetarian dishes,
- Slippers in children's sizes,
- Discounts for children and families with Alpine Club membership.

[Sleeping in the Hut](/ratgeber/huettenuebernachtung/) explains how reservations, half board and hut rules generally work.

## Packing list for children

| subject | Why |
|---|---|
| Child-sized hut sleeping bag | Mandatory in Alpine club huts, test at home |
| Headlamp | for going to the toilet at night and as a big highlight |
| Slippers or thick socks | No mountain boots in the hut |
| warm pajamas | It can get cold in huts at night |
| Cuddly toy or sleeping object | a piece of home in a foreign environment |
| Change of clothes | After playing the Bach everything is wet |
| Hat and fleece jacket | Evenings on the terrace are fresh |
| small deck of cards or book | for the evening in the dining room |
| Toothbrush, small towel | Washrooms are basic |
| personal medications | and a small first aid kit |

Still, keep the backpacks light. The children only carry their own light clothes, as described in [Hiking with School Children](/ratgeber/wandern-mit-schulkindern/).

## The process: from ascent to breakfast

### Ascension

Don't start too late so you arrive early in the afternoon. Plan plenty of breaks and make the climb part of the adventure: Who will see the hut first? Arriving shouldn't be rushed, but with time for cocoa and cake.

### Afternoon

The afternoon at the hut is often the best part for children. They play by the stream, build cairns off the path, watch marmots or cows from a safe distance and explore the surrounding area. Adults should stay within sight as there are often steep slopes around huts.

### Dinner

In many huts, dinner is served at a fixed time for everyone. It's exciting for children to sit at a table with strangers. Many huts offer children's portions. Afterwards it's worth taking a look outside: sunset, afterglow on the rocks and later, when it's clear, the starry sky.

### The night

Prepare the sleeping places before dinner: spread out the sleeping bag, headlamp and slippers ready, cuddly toy on the pillow. Show the children the way to the toilet while it is light. An adult accompanies younger children at night. The huts are closed from 10 p.m. Earplugs help adults when there is snoring in the next room.

### Morning

Mountaineers leave early, but families don't have to. Enjoy breakfast, pack in peace and see if any animals can be seen near the hut in the morning. Check the drying room and room before you leave. Cuddly toys are surprisingly often left lying down.

## Plan B in case of weather

If thunderstorms are forecast, postpone the tour. Even if it rains, the night in the hut can still be nice: games in the dining room, stories, rain on the roof. However, the descent on wet paths takes longer. Check the weather forecast as described in [Read mountain weather](/ratgeber/bergwetter-lesen/) and inform the hut if you are not coming.

## To try it out: a night in the garden

If your child has never slept in a sleeping bag outside, try it out at home first: one night in the garden, on the balcony or in the living room in a sleeping bag, with a headlamp. This way you know how it reacts and the night in the cabin loses its terror.

## What children learn in the hut

A night in a hut is more than just an excursion. Children experience that water and electricity cannot be taken for granted, that rubbish is taken away, that being considerate of others is part of sleeping together and that a day without a screen can be full of experiences. Many hut owners like to tell you how the hut is supplied, with a material cable car, helicopter or mule. Let the children ask questions. Such conversations are often remembered longer than the summit.

Give the children small tasks: put their own shoes in the boot room, spread out their own sleeping bag, put their headlamp ready to hand, pack their own backpack in the morning. Those who are given responsibility feel big and the adults have less to do.

## When a child cannot sleep at night

Some children don't sleep well on their first night in a strange place. That's normal. A familiar sleep ritual helps: whispering a short story, the cuddly toy, the headlamp next to the pillow. If a child is lying awake, go quietly with them outside the door and look at the starry sky. This is often the moment that everyone remembers for the longest time.

## Explain hut rules in a child-friendly way

There are rules in a hut that children don't know at home. Mountain boots stay in the boot room; hut shoes are worn indoors. There are a lot of people sleeping next to each other in the camp, so it's quiet after a certain time. Water and electricity are valuable because they are often laboriously produced or brought up.

Explain these rules before the tour as part of the adventure. Children like to follow rules if they understand why. Many people are proud when they make sure that their shoes are in the right place or that the lights are turned off.

## Eating and drinking for children on the go

Children use up a lot of energy hiking in the mountains, but often don't feel like taking long breaks. Small, frequent snacks keep them happy: nuts, dried fruit, bread, cheese cubes or a granola bar. A favorite snack for the summit is a good motivation.

Make sure that children drink regularly, as they often don't notice thirst until late. Having your own small water bottle that you carry yourself makes drinking more interesting. At the hut there are usually child-friendly dishes and sometimes smaller portions, ask about them at dinner.

## Security around the cabin

Huts are often located on steep terrain. Before playing, decide with the children where they are allowed to spend time outside and explain why slopes, streams and material cableways are taboo. In the evening and at night, a headlamp helps you find your way to the toilet safely, as the paths are often unlit. Grazing animals around the hut also need space.

## Frequently asked questions

### At what age does a night in the hut make sense?

With children who can do the climb themselves, usually from around five or six years old. It is possible with younger children if the hut is very easily accessible, but requires a lot of planning.

### Do children have problems at heights?

In normal huts in the German Alps, the height is usually not a problem for healthy children. If a child complains of a headache, nausea or unusual tiredness, take it seriously and if in doubt, step away. If you have previous illnesses, talk to your pediatrician beforehand.

### How much does a night in a hut cost for families?

This depends on the cabin, room type and membership. Children and young people pay significantly less in many huts, and families with Alpine Club membership receive additional discounts. Ask when making your reservation.

### What to do if a child no longer wants to go up?

Plan short stages with intermediate goals, such as “to the next bridge” or “to the big stone,” and incorporate games. Breaks with snacks work wonders. If the child is still exhausted, turning back is not a defeat. Next time, choose an easier hut that can be reached more quickly.

### Do children need their own sleeping bag?

Yes, a sleeping bag is mandatory in most Alpine club huts, even for children. Lightweight models made of cotton, silk or synthetic fibers are small and weigh little.

### Does it make sense to stay in the same cabin for two nights?

For families, this is often the more relaxed option. On the first day you climb up, on the second day you take a short tour in the area without much luggage, to a lake, a small peak or simply to the stream, and return in the afternoon. This way, children get to know the hut in peace, have time to play and don't have to climb uphill with a full backpack two days in a row. When making your reservation, ask whether two nights are possible, as weekends are quickly booked up in high season.
