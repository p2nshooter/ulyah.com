A trip can be ruined before it begins at an airline counter: a passport that expires in a few months, a travel authorization for a minor that was missing, a vaccination certificate that the destination country required. Entry requirements change depending on the nationality, the country of destination, the means of transportation and even the age of the traveler, and they change over time.

This guide does not replace official information, which is the only one that is valid at a border, but it helps you know **what to ask and where**. You will see when an identity document may be enough and when a passport is needed, how to find out if you need a visa, what is required of minors, what documents should be carried for driving or for health reasons and how to protect yourself if you lose one.

## Where to check the requirements

Before purchasing tickets, consult:

- **The chancery or ministry of foreign affairs of your country,** which usually publishes entry requirements to other countries for its citizens.
- **The consulate or embassy of the destination country.**
- **The immigration authority of the destination country.**
- **Your airline,** which may deny you boarding if you do not meet the destination or stopover requirements.

Check back a few days before you leave: rules may change.

## Passport or identity document

For many trips within the region, the national identity document may be sufficient, thanks to regional agreements. Some examples:

- **Mercosur and Associated States:** Citizens of several South American countries can circulate between them with their current identity document.
- **Andean Community:** there are agreements on movement with an identity document among its member countries.
- **Central America:** some countries in the region have free mobility agreements for their nationals.

However, **not all documents work** and there are conditions: the document must be current and in good condition, and some countries only accept certain types of ID. Additionally, if you are traveling by plane with stops in other countries, or if your nationality is not included in the agreement, you will need a passport. When in doubt, **travel with a passport**.

### Passport validity

Many countries require that the passport be valid for the entire stay, and some airlines and destinations require **at least six months of validity** from the date of entry. Check the expiration date before purchasing tickets and renew early: the procedures can take weeks.

## Do I need a visa?

It depends on your nationality and the destination. Many countries in the region do not require a tourist visa for citizens of other Latin American countries for short stays, but there are exceptions, and the requirements for people of other nationalities are very varied.

When finding out, pay attention to:

- **The maximum length of stay** allowed without a visa.
- **If the visa is electronic** or must be processed at a consulate.
- **If departure ticket is requested,** accommodation reservation or proof of funds.
- **If there are entry or exit fees**.

## Minors

Many countries in the region have strict rules for the exit or entry of minors, especially when they travel **with only one of their parents, with other relatives or alone**. It is common to require a **travel authorization signed by the parents**, sometimes before a notary or before an authority, and documents that prove relationship, such as a birth certificate.

The requirements depend on the country of residence of the minor and the country of destination. Find out in advance, because some procedures require an appointment.

## Driving in another country

If you plan to rent a car or travel with your own:

- **Bring your valid driver's license.**
- **Check if the country requires an international driving permit,** which is processed in your country before leaving.
- **If you cross a border with your vehicle,** you will need car papers, insurance valid in the other country, and often a temporary vehicle import permit.

More details in the guides on [driving in Latin America](/bitacora/conducir-en-america-latina/) and [crossing borders by land](/bitacora/cruzar-fronteras-por-tierra/).

## Health documents

- **International certificate of vaccination against yellow fever:** some countries require it for travelers arriving from areas with risk of transmission, and the vaccine is recommended for visiting certain areas of the Amazon and other tropical regions. More information in the guide on [traveler health and vaccines](/bitacora/salud-del-viajero-y-vacunas/).
- **Medical prescriptions** for the medications you take, especially if they are controlled. Take them in their original packaging.
- **Travel insurance policy,** with the assistance telephone number at hand. We see it in the guide on [travel insurance](/bitacora/seguro-de-viaje/).

## Customs: what you carry also counts

- **Food, plants, seeds and animal products:** several countries have very strict health controls and fines for not declaring them. If you doubt, declare.
- **Cash:** Above certain amounts it is usually mandatory to declare it.
- **Medicines:** bring them with a prescription and in reasonable quantities for your stay.
- **Valuables and professional equipment:** in some cases it is advisable to declare them when leaving to avoid problems when you return.

## Traveling with pets

Taking a dog or cat to another country requires its own documentation. The usual thing is an **official health certificate** issued shortly before the trip, the **current rabies vaccine** and, in many cases, an identification microchip and recent antiparasitic treatments. Each country sets its requirements through its official health service, and airlines have their own rules regarding transportation in the cabin or hold. Start finding out several weeks in advance, because some deadlines are strict.

## Work remotely while traveling

Entering as a tourist does not usually authorize you to work in the country, even if your employer is somewhere else. Several countries in the region have created specific visas for people who work remotely for long stays, with income and health insurance requirements. If you plan to spend months working with your computer, check which regime corresponds to your case.

## Copies and backups

1. **Scan or photograph** passport, ID, driver's license, visas, insurance and reservations.
2. **Save copies in the cloud** and in your email, with access from another device.
3. **Bring a paper copy** separate from the originals.
4. **Write down the telephone numbers** of your bank, your insurance and the consulate of your country at the destination.
5. **Share your itinerary** with someone you trust.

Some countries offer their citizens a consular traveler registry, useful for receiving warnings and assistance in emergencies.

## If you lose a document

- **Make a report** to your local police and ask for a copy.
- **Contact your country's consulate or embassy,** which may issue a provisional travel document or emergency passport.
- **Notify the airline** if the problem affects your return flight.
- **Lock cards** if you lost them too. We see it in the guide on [money, cards and change](/bitacora/dinero-tarjetas-y-cambio/).

## Checklist before leaving

| Document | Review |
|---|---|
| Passport or identity document | validity and acceptance at the destination |
| Visa or electronic authorization | if needed and approved |
| Tickets and reservations | names written the same as on the document |
| Travel authorization for minors | signatures, legalizations and copies |
| Driver's license and international permit | validity and requirement of the country |
| Vaccination certificate | if the destination or route demands it |
| travel insurance | coverage, dates and telephone number for assistance |

## Frequent errors

- **Passport with little validity** that the airline does not accept.
- **Assume that the ID is valid** for any destination or stopover.
- **Traveling with a minor** without the required authorization.
- **Different names** between the ticket and the document.
- **Do not declare food** in countries with strict controls.
- **Carry all documents together** in the same bag.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I enter another country with just my ID?

In some cases yes, thanks to regional agreements, but it depends on your nationality, the destination and the type of document. Confirm it with the immigration authority of the destination or with your foreign ministry, and consider traveling with a passport if you have doubts.

### What happens if I have dual nationality?

Some countries require their citizens to enter and leave with their national document. Find out more at the consulates of both countries before traveling.

### Can they deny me entry even if I have the documents?

Yes. The final decision is made by the immigration authority at the border. They may ask you to explain the reason for the trip, show your departure ticket, your reservations or your financial means. Keep that information handy and respond clearly.

### How long before should I review the requirements?

When planning the trip and again a few days before leaving. If you need a visa, new passport or authorizations for minors, start several weeks in advance.

More preparation guides in the [Planning and Budgeting](/destinos/planificacion-y-presupuesto/) section.

## In summary

Check entry requirements from official sources when planning and again before leaving. For some regional trips, an identification document may be sufficient, but if in doubt, travel with a passport and check its validity. Find out if you need a visa, prepare authorizations for minors in advance, bring a license and, if applicable, an international driving permit, and the health certificates required by your route. Declare what corresponds to customs, keep digital and paper copies and have your consulate contacts on hand.
