Accommodation is much more than a place to sleep. Determine how much you will walk at night, how you will get from the terminal, whether or not you will be able to rest and, to a large extent, how calm you will feel during the trip. A well-located hostel with good staff can be the best source of advice in a city; a cheap apartment in an unknown area, a source of worries.

In Latin America there are options for all budgets: hostels with shared bedrooms, family lodgings, boutique hotels in colonial mansions, rental apartments, mountain cabins, jungle lodges and family homes that receive travelers. In this guide you will see how to choose the type of accommodation, how to assess the location, how to read reviews, how to avoid scams when booking, what to check when you arrive and what to take into account depending on the type of place.

## Accommodation types

| Type | Advantages | Keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| Hostel | economic, social environment, information from other travelers | noise, shared spaces and lockers for your things |
| Family accommodation or hotel | close treatment, good price | simpler services and reception that is sometimes not permanent |
| Hotel | permanent reception, services, more access control | higher prices in tourist areas |
| Rental apartment | space, kitchen, feeling of home | less support for problems and more risk of false ads |
| Lodge or rural cabin | nature, tranquility, guided experiences | remote access, limited services and distance from medical centers |
| Homestay or community tourism | cultural immersion, direct support to communities | House rules and simple amenities |

The choice depends on your style and your budget, as we explain in the guide on [realistic travel budget](/bitacora/presupuesto-de-viaje-realista/), but safety should weigh as much as price.

## Location first

Good accommodation in a bad location is still a problem. Before booking:

- **Look at the map:** look for areas with shops, restaurants, lighting and movement of people, close to what you want to visit.
- **Check how to get there:** distance from the terminal or airport, transportation available and what happens if you arrive at night.
- **Look for comments about the neighborhood,** especially those that mention what the area is like after dark.
- **Ask the accommodation** about the areas to avoid and how to get around.

The guide on [safety in large cities](/bitacora/seguridad-en-grandes-ciudades/) expands on how to get around neighborhoods.

## How to read reviews

The opinions of other travelers are the most useful tool to choose, if you know how to read them:

- **Prioritize recent ones:** an accommodation can change owners, staff or status in a few months.
- **Read the negatives:** look for patterns. An isolated complaint about breakfast matters little; several about thefts, doors that do not close or absent staff, a lot.
- **Search for key words:** security, lock, safe, noise, cleanliness, hot water, reception, neighborhood, night arrival.
- **Look at the traveler photos,** which are usually more realistic than the official ones.
- **Look at the accommodation's responses:** How it responds to complaints says a lot about how it will resolve your problems.

## Book without falling into scams

- **Always pay within the booking platform** or directly to the accommodation through verifiable channels. Be wary of anyone who asks you to pay by bank transfer or by methods that are difficult to trace "to obtain a discount."
- **Be wary of prices that are too low** for the area and the season.
- **Check that the accommodation exists:** look for it on maps, on various platforms and on their own networks or website.
- **Save the confirmation** and messages with the accommodation.
- **Use a credit card** when possible because it offers more protection against improper charges.
- **Review cancellation policy** before paying.

In several countries, tourism service providers must be registered in an official registry; In Colombia, for example, there is the National Tourism Registry. Having a property display its registration number is a good sign.

## Upon arrival: check the room

Take a few minutes to check a few details before unpacking:

1. **Lock:** the door must close well from the inside and outside. If there is a door that connects to another room, check that it is closed.
2. **Windows:** especially on ground floors, they must close securely.
3. **Safe:** Use this for your passport, money, and gadgets you don't have with you, or ask if the front desk stores valuables.
4. **Emergency exits:** Locate the closest one and the stairs.
5. **Fire extinguishers and smoke detectors,** if available.
6. **Hot water and shower:** Check how they work before you need them.
7. **Emergency contact:** ask for the phone number of the reception or host, especially if there is no twenty-four hour reception.

If something doesn't convince you, ask to change rooms as soon as possible.

## Gas heaters and electric showers

Two very common details in the region deserve special attention:

- **Gas Heaters:** In some locations, hot water is provided by gas heaters installed inside the bathroom or home. If poorly installed or unventilated, they can release carbon monoxide, an invisible, odorless gas. Make sure there is ventilation, do not completely close the bathroom windows, and if you experience a headache, dizziness, or nausea while showering, leave immediately and report it.
- **Electric showers:** Many showers heat the water with an electric resistance in the head itself. Adjust the temperature before entering, do not touch the head or switch with a wet body, and report if you see bare wires or sparks.

## Earthquakes, fires and other risks

- **Earthquakes:** in seismic zones, ask what to do in case of an earthquake and where the safe areas of the building are.
- **Tsunamis:** on the coast of countries like Chile, Peru or Ecuador, look at the evacuation signs to high areas.
- **Fires:** in old wooden buildings or those with many renovations, know the way out.
- **Hurricanes:** If you travel to the Caribbean in season, ask about the storm accommodation plan; We see it in the guide on the [hurricane season in the Caribbean](/bitacora/temporada-de-huracanes-caribe/).

## Hostels: shared dormitories

- **Use the lockers** and bring your own padlock.
- **Keep valuables** inside the locker or with you, even while you sleep.
- **Choose small dorms** or, if you prefer, women-only dorms, which many hostels offer.
- **Read reviews about the atmosphere:** some hostels are very festive and others are quiet; Choose according to what you are looking for.
- **Take advantage of the staff:** They are often a great source of local information.

## Apartments and rental houses

- **Verify the host's identity and reviews.**
- **Ask what access is like:** gate, keys, codes and what happens if you arrive late.
- **Check the area** with special attention, because there is no reception to guide you.
- **Have an emergency plan:** who to call if there is a problem with the faucet, water or electricity.
- **Check the space upon arrival:** Rental platforms often prohibit cameras in private spaces like bedrooms and bathrooms. If you find something that worries you, let the platform know.

## Rural and jungle accommodations

Jungle lodges, mountain cabins and rural accommodation offer unique experiences, but they are far from everything:

- **Ask about access** and what the transfer is like, especially if you arrive by boat or on dirt roads.
- **Ask what they do in a medical emergency** and how far the nearest health center is.
- **Check electricity and water:** some have electricity for only a few hours and rain or well water.
- **Check the mosquito nets** and bring a flashlight.
- **Choose operators with good opinions** about their guides and their treatment of the communities.

More information in the guide about [travel to the Amazon](/bitacora/viajar-a-la-amazonia/). If you stay with families or communities, the guide on [community tourism and cultural respect](/bitacora/turismo-comunitario-y-respeto-cultural/) will help you do so respectfully.

## Travel alone or alone

- **Choose accommodations with permanent reception** or with good reviews from other people who traveled alone.
- **Do not share your room number** out loud or on social media.
- **Use the interior lock** and don't open it to someone you don't expect without checking with the reception.
- **Arrive during daylight** at least the first night in each city.

## Your documents and valuables

- **Do not leave your passport as a deposit:** Offer a copy or a card payment if they ask for a guarantee.
- **Keep valuables** in the safe or at the front desk, and ask for a receipt if you hand them over.
- **Do not leave anything visible** in the room, especially on the ground floor or with windows facing the street.
- **Carry digital copies** of your documents, as we explain in the guide on [travel documents](/bitacora/documentos-de-viaje/).

## If something goes wrong

- **Talk to the accommodation first** and leave a written record.
- **If you booked through a platform,** contact their customer service as soon as possible and provide photos.
- **In case of theft,** file a police report, necessary for insurance. The guide to [travel insurance](/bitacora/seguro-de-viaje/) explains what the policies cover.
- **Don't hesitate to leave** if you don't feel safe: your peace of mind is worth more than a paid night.

## Frequent errors

- **Choose only by price** without looking at the neighborhood.
- **Read only positive reviews.**
- **Pay by transfer** to a host outside the platform.
- **Do not check the lock** or the windows upon arrival.
- **Ignore the gas heater** in an unvented bathroom.
- **Leave your passport** as collateral.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are hostels in Latin America safe?

Many are very safe and well managed. Choose those with recent good reviews, lockers and permanent reception, and take care of your valuables.

### Is a hotel or a rental apartment better?

Depends. The hotel offers immediate reception and support; the apartment, more space and kitchen. In an unknown city, especially the first night, accommodation with a reception usually gives more peace of mind.

### What do I do if the accommodation does not look like the photos?

Document the situation with photos, talk to the accommodation and, if you booked through a platform, immediately contact their customer service.

### Should I reserve in advance?

In high season, holidays and destinations with little offer, yes. In low season you can book the first nights and decide the rest as you go.

## In summary

Choose the type of accommodation according to your style, but give security as much weight as price. Prioritize a good location, read recent and negative reviews, pay within platforms or through verifiable channels and be wary of prices that are too low. Upon arrival, check locks, windows, safes, emergency exits, and the hot water system, paying special attention to gas heaters and electric showers. Adapt the precautions to remote hostels, apartments or lodges, do not leave your passport as collateral and, if something does not convince you, change accommodation.
