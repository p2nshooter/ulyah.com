In much of Latin America, the bus is the backbone of the trip. It links capitals and remote towns, crosses mountain ranges and deserts, covers thousands of kilometers of coastline and is often the most economical and flexible way to get around. In Argentina, Chile or Peru there are double-decker buses with seats that convert into beds; in Mexico, first class buses that connect the entire country; in Central America, from comfortable intercity services to the colorful local buses that stop in every town.

Traveling by bus can be a great experience or an endless night, depending on how you plan it. In this guide you will see what types of service exist, how to buy tickets, how the terminals work, how to take care of your luggage, what to take into account on night trips and on mountain routes, and what to bring to arrive rested.

## service classes

The names change depending on the country and company, but the logic is similar:

| Class | What do you usually offer? | What trips for? |
|---|---|---|
| Conventional or economical | seats with little recline, sometimes frequent stops | short trips or very tight budget |
| Executive or first class | wide seats, air conditioning, bathroom, few stops | multi-hour day trips |
| Semi-bed | seats that recline quite a bit, with leg rests | night trips on a moderate budget |
| bed | Wider seats that recline almost completely | long night trips |
| Full bed or suite | seats that convert into a flat bed, sometimes with curtains | very long journeys in which you want to sleep well |

In Brazil we talk about conventional, executive and leito services; in Mexico, first class, executive or luxury. Before buying, look at photos of the seats and read what each service includes: food, blankets, plugs, Wi-Fi or individual screens.

## Buy the ticket

- **Online or at the terminal:** Many companies sell online, and at the terminals there are windows for each company.
- **With your document:** in several countries the ticket is issued in the name of the passenger and the passport or identification document is requested when boarding.
- **In advance on key dates:** long holidays, school vacations and regional festivals sell out tickets.
- **Choose your seat wisely:** on double-decker buses, the top deck offers better views but moves more when cornering; the one below is usually quieter. Avoid seats next to the bathroom.
- **Compare companies** by reputation and not just by price, especially on long and mountain routes.

## The terminals

Each country has its name: land terminal in Peru and Ecuador, bus terminal in Argentina and Uruguay, bus terminal in Chile and Colombia, bus central in Mexico or rodoviária in Brazil.

- **Arrive early,** at least 30 to 45 minutes early in large terminals.
- **Check if there is a terminal use fee,** which in some countries is paid separately from the ticket to access the platform.
- **Look for the platform or platform** indicated on your ticket and confirm with staff.
- **Take care of your things:** Terminals are busy places, ideal for carelessness. Do not leave the backpack unattended or prop it away from you.
- **Use official taxis** from the terminal or transportation apps when arriving, especially at night.

## The luggage

- **Luggage in hold:** When you deliver it, you will be given a receipt or label. Save it: you will need it to remove it.
- **Take valuables with you:** documents, money, phone, computer, camera and medications always in your hand luggage.
- **The handbag at your feet,** not in the luggage rack above, where you can't see it.
- **Use padlocks** on the suitcase zippers.
- **Do not accept food or drinks from strangers** during the trip.

## Travel at night

Night buses save one night's accommodation and take advantage of the travel hours to sleep. Some considerations:

- **Choose recognized companies** with buses in good condition and two drivers on long journeys.
- **Use the seat belt** if the seat has one.
- **On dangerous mountain routes,** some people prefer to travel during the day, when visibility is greater and the landscape can also be enjoyed.
- **Plan your arrival:** Arriving at four in the morning to an unknown city can be uncomfortable. Find out if the terminal has a waiting room and how to get to the accommodation.

## To arrive rested

- **Coat in layers:** The air conditioning on many buses is very cold, even in the tropics.
- **Travel pillow, eye mask and earplugs**.
- **Water and something to eat:** There are not always stops when you need them.
- **Toilet paper and disinfectant gel:** The bus bathroom is not always well stocked.
- **Pills for motion sickness,** if you tend to get dizzy on curves, consult with a pharmacist or doctor first.
- **External battery** for the phone.
- **Comfortable socks or tights** and loose clothing.

## Stops and meals en route

On long journeys, the bus usually stops at stops or restaurants along the route, and in some countries vendors with homemade food come up at certain stops. If you eat at these places, choose freshly cooked and hot dishes, and drink bottled water. Before getting off, ask how long the stop will last and memorize the license plate or bus number: in large stops there may be several similar buses parked at the same time.

## Traveling by bus with children

With children, daytime trips that are not too long usually work better. Choose seats together, bring games, books and headphones, and some familiar food. Find out the company's policy: in some countries small children can travel on the lap of an adult on certain services, but on long journeys it is better for them to have their own seat.

## High altitude routes

Some routes cross Andean passes at high altitudes, above 4000 meters, such as the routes between Cusco and Puno or the crossings of the mountain range between Argentina and Chile. If you come from sea level, you may feel a headache or shortness of breath. Drink water, avoid heavy meals before the trip and consult the guide on [altitude sickness](/bitacora/mal-de-altura-soroche/). Bring a coat: it is very cold on the high passes, especially at night.

## Unforeseen events on the route

- **Delays:** on long journeys they are common due to traffic, controls, works or weather. Leave room if you have a flight or reservation later.
- **Road blockades:** In some countries, protests or walkouts can cut off routes for hours or days. Check the local news before you go out.
- **Rain and landslides:** In the rainy season, some mountain routes are affected.
- **Bus changes:** sometimes the company changes the vehicle or combines services. Ask and keep an eye on your luggage.

## Cross borders by bus

On international buses, all passengers get off at border posts for immigration procedures and, often, for luggage control. Have your passport or document on hand, the immigration card if they gave it to you when you entered, and declare what corresponds. We explain it in the guide on [crossing borders by land](/bitacora/cruzar-fronteras-por-tierra/).

## Local buses: another experience

In addition to long-distance buses, in each country there are local buses, minibuses, buses and vans that connect nearby towns. In Guatemala, the old colorfully painted school buses are famous, and in rural areas of Colombia, the wooden chivas. They are cheap and authentic, but they are usually full and with fewer amenities. For short and day trips they can be a great experience; For long journeys with luggage, a long-distance service is usually more comfortable.

## Frequent errors

- **Choose only for the price** on long and mountain routes.
- **Keep documents or the computer** in checked luggage.
- **Forget your coat** because of the air conditioning.
- **Do not buy in advance** on holidays.
- **Plan tight connections** after a long trip.
- **Leave the backpack unattended** in the terminal.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the bus or the plane better?

It depends on the distance, budget and time available. For journeys of more than fifteen or twenty hours, an internal flight can compensate. For medium distances, the night bus is usually a good combination of price and time.

### Do the buses have a bathroom?

Most long distance services do, although their status varies. Local and some economic buses do not have it.

### Can I bring food on board?

In general yes, although it is advisable to choose foods without strong odors. Some services include a meal or snack.

## In summary

Choose the class of service according to the duration of the trip: executive for the day, semi-sleeper or bed for the night. Buy in advance on key dates, with your document, and choose your seat carefully. Arrive at the terminal on time, pay the fee if applicable and take care of your things. Take valuables with you, bundle up for the air conditioning and prepare for the altitude on the Andean routes. Leave room for delays and blockages, and plan your arrival if you're traveling at night.
