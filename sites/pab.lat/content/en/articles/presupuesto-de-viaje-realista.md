"How much does it cost to travel through Latin America?" It is one of the most frequently asked questions by those preparing a trip, and also one of the most difficult to answer. The region includes very cheap destinations and others that are surprisingly expensive, countries with stable currencies and others with high inflation, seasons in which prices double and travelers who spend ten times more than others visiting the same place. Therefore, any general figure is of little use.

What does work is a method: divide the trip into items, investigate the current prices of each one in your destinations and add a margin for the unexpected. In this guide you will see how to do it step by step, which expenses tend to be forgotten, which destinations tend to be more expensive, how the season and inflation influence, where it is advisable to save and where not, and how to keep track during the trip.

## Why budgets fail

Most travel budgets fall short for the same reasons:

- **Only accommodation and food is calculated,** forgetting transportation, entrance fees and excursions.
- **Outdated prices are used,** from blogs or forums from years ago.
- **Bank commissions**, currency exchanges and fees are not included.
- **Expensive excursions are underestimated,** which in some destinations are almost mandatory.
- **There is no room for unforeseen events** such as a missed flight or a sick day.

## Step 1: define your travel style

The same itinerary can cost very different figures depending on how you travel. Before crunching the numbers, decide your style in each game:

| Departure | Economical | Intermediate | Comfortable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accommodation | shared dormitories or simple hostels | private rooms in hostels or small hotels | hotels with more services |
| Food | markets, menu of the day and own cuisine | local restaurants and some whims | high-end restaurants frequently |
| Transportation | line buses and public transport | higher category buses and some internal flights | internal flights and private transfers |
| Activities | free walks, museums and free days | some organized excursions | private excursions and special experiences |

Many travelers combine: they sleep in simple places and spend more on experiences, or the other way around. The important thing is to decide before, because it changes the result a lot.

## Step 2: Fixed expenses before leaving

These are the ones you pay only once, usually before the trip:

- **International flights** round trip.
- **Travel insurance,** essential. We explain it in the guide on [travel insurance](/bitacora/seguro-de-viaje/).
- **Vaccinations and medical consultation,** if your itinerary requires them. More information in the guide on [traveler health and vaccines](/bitacora/salud-del-viajero-y-vacunas/).
- **Documents:** passport renewal, visas or electronic authorizations, depending on your nationality. We see it in the guide on [travel documents](/bitacora/documentos-de-viaje/).
- **Equipment:** backpack, footwear, technical clothing or adapters that you do not have.

## Step 3: daily spending

Daily spending is the heart of the budget. To calculate it:

1. **Divide the trip by destinations** and write down how many nights you will spend in each one.
2. **Investigate current prices** for accommodation on the dates of your trip, on booking platforms and on the websites of the accommodations themselves.
3. **Estimate food:** look up the price of a menu of the day, a meal at a local restaurant, and a basic purchase at the market or supermarket.
4. **Calculate local transportation:** metro, city buses, taxis or applications.
5. **Add tickets and activities** that you will do in each place.
6. **Multiply** the daily expense of each destination by the nights you will spend there.

Use recent sources, such as the official websites of museums, parks and transportation companies, and traveler reviews from recent months. Prices change, especially in countries with high inflation.

## Step 4: the big travel expenses

In almost all itineraries there are some experiences that, by themselves, represent an important part of the budget:

- **Entrance tickets to emblematic places** with trains, buses or associated permits, such as Machu Picchu.
- **Multi-day excursions** such as those to the Uyuni salt flat or long walks through Patagonia.
- **Destinations with high entry fees,** such as some islands and national parks.
- **Diving, cruises or scenic flights.**
- **Internal flights,** which save days of travel but can cost more than you expect.

Calculate these expenses separately and decide which ones are a priority. Sometimes it is better to give up one and enjoy another calmly.

## Step 5: invisible expenses

They're small, but they add up:

- **Bank commissions** for payments abroad and for withdrawals at ATMs. We see it in the guide on [money, cards and change](/bitacora/dinero-tarjetas-y-cambio/).
- **Tips** in restaurants, excursions and transfers.
- **Port, airport, border, tourist or environmental fees**.
- **Transfers to the airport** and bus terminals, often far from the center.
- **Baggage on low-cost flights,** which is usually charged separately.
- **Mobile data:** a local card or roaming plan.
- **Laundry, bottled water, public bathrooms** and other daily expenses.
- **Souvenirs and purchases.**

## Step 6: the margin for unforeseen events

Always add a margin, for example between ten and fifteen percent of the total, for what cannot be foreseen: a canceled bus, an extra night, a doctor's consultation, a change of plans or an opportunity that you do not want to miss. Also, have a separate **emergency fund**, on another card, that is not part of the daily budget.

## Most and least expensive destinations

Relative prices change with the economy of each country and with the exchange rate, but some trends tend to remain the same:

| They tend to be more expensive | They tend to be cheaper |
|---|---|
| Argentine and Chilean Patagonia, due to the distances and the short season | much of Bolivia |
| the Galapagos Islands, for taxes, flights and boats | rural areas and towns of the Andes |
| very touristy beach destinations in the Caribbean | much of Central America outside of luxury destinations |
| islands with limited access and environmental fees | medium-sized cities off the most visited circuits |

Within the same country, prices vary greatly between the capital, tourist destinations and rural areas. The same dish can cost much more on a tourist street than two blocks away.

## High season and low season

- **High season:** local school holidays, Easter, New Year's Eve and the months of best weather in each destination. Prices for accommodation and flights can go up a lot, and it is best to book in advance.
- **Low season:** lower prices and fewer people, in exchange for more rain, some closed services or reduced frequencies.
- **Shoulder Seasons:** often offer the best balance of price and weather.

Keep in mind that in the southern hemisphere summer holidays are in January and February, and winter holidays are in July.

## Inflation and exchange rate

In some countries in the region, inflation and exchange rate variations can alter prices in a few weeks. That's why:

- **Check the economic news** of the countries you will visit before you leave.
- **Ask in what currency it is best to pay** each service and if there are differences between paying by card or cash.
- **Update your quote** during your trip if prices change.

## How to save without losing quality

- **Menu of the day:** In many countries, midday lunch with soup, main dish and drink is the most economical and filling meal.
- **Markets:** Fruit, juices and foods at local markets are cheap and tasty. We see it in the guide on [traditional markets](/bitacora/mercados-tradicionales/).
- **Night buses:** on long journeys, they save one night's accommodation, although they are more tiring. More information in the guide about [long distance buses](/bitacora/buses-de-larga-distancia/).
- **Travel slower:** changing locations every day multiplies transportation costs; Staying longer in each destination reduces costs and fatigue.
- **Free days and walking tours:** Many museums have free days, and in several cities there are walking tours where you leave a voluntary tip.
- **Cook occasionally** if the accommodation has a kitchen.
- **Compare excursions** between several authorized agencies, looking at what each one includes.

## Where it is not convenient to save

- **Travel insurance:** an uncovered medical problem can cost more than the entire trip.
- **Safe transportation:** choose bus companies and boat operators with a good reputation, even if they cost a little more.
- **Guides in risky activities:** in the mountains, volcanoes, jungle or sea, an accredited guide is worth what it costs.
- **Accommodation in unsafe areas:** Saving a few coins in exchange for sleeping in a neighborhood you don't know is rarely worth it. The guide on [choosing secure hosting](/bitacora/elegir-alojamiento-seguro/) expands on this point.
- **Health:** safe water, food in good condition and adequate medicines.

## Keep track during the trip

- **Write down each expense,** in an app or a simple spreadsheet, in local currency and yours.
- **Review the total every few days** and compare it to what was planned.
- **Adjust in time:** If one destination was more expensive, compensate in the next one or cut back on a less important activity.
- **Separate the day's money** from the rest so you don't overspend without realizing it.

## A simple template

| Departure | How to estimate it | Where to save |
|---|---|---|
| International flights | search with flexible dates and in advance | fly in shoulder season |
| Insurance and health | budgets from various insurance companies and medical centers | not saving on coverage |
| Accommodation | price per night in each destination, on your dates | longer stays and simple accommodation |
| Food | menu of the day, market and restaurant | lunches instead of expensive dinners |
| Transportation | buses, internal flights and transfers | night buses and fewer changes of destination |
| Activities | priority tickets and excursions | free days and free walks |
| invisible expenses | commissions, rates, tips, data | commission-free cards abroad |
| Unforeseen | a percentage of the total | do not touch it unless necessary |

## Frequent errors

- **Use general figures** from the internet instead of researching each destination.
- **Forget banking commissions** and fees.
- **Do not separate major excursions** from daily expenses.
- **Travel without margin** for unforeseen events.
- **Save on insurance** or secure transportation.
- **Don't keep track** until the money runs out.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much money do I need per day?

It depends on the destination, the season and your travel style. Calculate your own daily expenses with current prices for accommodation, food, transportation and activities in each location, and add a margin.

### Is it better to reserve everything in advance?

It is advisable to book in advance what is sold out or increases in price, such as flights, accommodation in high season and tickets with quotas, and leave flexibility in the rest.

### Is it advisable to carry all the money in cash?

No. Combine cards and cash, and distribute the money in different places. Carrying large sums of cash is an unnecessary risk.

### How do I save on an expensive destination?

Look for accommodations with kitchens, eat at midday, use public transportation, prioritize the experiences that matter most to you, and consider traveling in the shoulder season.

## In summary

A realistic budget is built with method, not with general figures. Define your travel style, add up the fixed expenses, calculate the daily expenses of each destination with current prices, separate the big excursions and don't forget the invisible expenses. Add a margin for unforeseen events and a separate emergency fund. Take into account the season, inflation and exchange rate, save on what does not put your safety at risk and keep track throughout the trip to adjust in time.
