If you really want to know a Latin American city, enter its market. There the smells of freshly ground coffee mix, the piles of fruits you didn't know, the medicinal herb stalls, the dining rooms where the workers have lunch and the lifelong conversations between sellers and customers. A market tells more about the cuisine, agriculture and daily life of a place than many museums.

Each country has its own: municipal markets in the center of capitals, weekly markets in mountain towns, seafood markets next to ports, craft markets. In this guide you will see how to visit them, when it is best to go, what to try, how to buy and haggle respectfully, how to take care of your health and your belongings and what products it is better not to buy.

## When to go

- **Early in the morning:** The products are fresher, there are fewer people, and the vendors have time to chat.
- **At noon:** is the time of the dining rooms, where the day's lunch is served at a good price.
- **On market days:** many towns have a weekly market day when producers come from all over the area. It is the busiest time, and sometimes the only one when there are certain products.
- **Avoid closing hours** if you're looking for variety: many stalls will have already emptied.

## How to tour a market

1. **Take a complete look before buying.** This way you see what's there, compare and discover the areas: fruits, vegetables, meats, foods, herbs, crafts.
2. **Look where locals shop and eat.** Stalls with regular customers are usually good.
3. **Question.** Vendors are often proud of their products and will explain how to eat a fruit or how to prepare an ingredient.
4. **Try before you buy** when offered, especially fruits and cheeses.
5. **Bring your own bag** and cash in small bills.
6. **Say hello before asking prices.** A "good morning" or "good afternoon" opens conversations and is part of everyday courtesy in almost the entire region.

## What to try

### Fruits you may not know

Latin American markets are a paradise of fruits: soursop, cherimoya, lucuma, granadilla, pitahaya, mamey, passion fruit, sapote, tamarillo, star fruit and many more depending on the region and season. Freshly made natural juices are one of the best ways to try them.

### The market canteens

In many markets there are stalls where a menu of the day is served: soup, main dish and drink at an affordable price. Depending on the country, it is called a meal, a menu, an executive lunch, a corrientazo or simply lunch. It is one of the most authentic and economical ways to eat.

### Takeaway products

Coffee, chocolate, spices, dried chilies, aged cheeses, regional sweets and fabrics are common souvenirs. Before buying food to take to your country, check what is allowed at the destination customs, as we explain in the guide on [travel documents](/bitacora/documentos-de-viaje/).

### Fish and seafood

In the coastal markets, the fish of the day is the protagonist, and many have stalls where they cook it to order. To recognize freshness, check that the eyes are bright and not sunken, the gills are red, and the smell is soft, of the sea and not strong. Dishes with raw or barely marinated fish, such as ceviche, require very fresh products: eat them in trusted places with a lot of customers.

### Herbs and traditional knowledge

In many markets there are aisles dedicated to medicinal herbs, incense and ritual objects, heirs of Andean, Mesoamerican or Afro-descendant knowledge. They are spaces to look with respect and ask questions with curiosity. Ask permission before photographing and do not consume unknown remedies without knowing what they contain, especially if you take medication or have an illness.

## Bargain with respect

- **At food and vegetable stalls,** prices are usually fixed and low. Haggling there is not common.
- **At craft markets,** polite negotiation may be part of the custom in some places, but not all.
- **If you negotiate, do it with a smile** and a reasonable figure. Remember that a small difference for you can be important for the seller.
- **Don't haggle for the sake of haggling:** if the price seems fair to you, pay it.
- **Buy directly from the producer** when you can, especially in crafts. We see it in the guide on [community tourism and cultural respect](/bitacora/turismo-comunitario-y-respeto-cultural/).

## Photographs

The markets are very photogenic, but the people who work in them are not part of the scenery. **Ask for permission before photographing anyone**, especially in indigenous communities, where some people prefer not to be photographed. Buying something at the stall before asking for a photo is usually well received.

## Eat carefully

- **Choose positions with a lot of movement,** where food is prepared and consumed quickly.
- **Prefers what is cooked at the moment** and served hot.
- **Peel or wash with safe water** the fruits you are going to eat.
- **Ask if the water or ice** in the juices are drinkable.
- **Bring disinfectant gel** for your hands.

More tips in the guide on [safe street food](/bitacora/comida-callejera-segura/).

## Take care of your things

In narrow, crowded hallways, carelessness is an opportunity for pickpockets:

- **Carry your backpack or bag in front.**
- **Take out only the money you are going to use** and keep the rest in a safe place.
- **Do not leave your phone** on counters.

## What should not be bought

- **Wildlife products:** skins, feathers, turtle shells, corals, stuffed or live animals. Their trade is usually illegal and threatens endangered species.
- **Archaeological or supposedly ancient pieces:** Their purchase and export are prohibited in many countries, and many are fakes.
- **Plants or seeds** that you will not be able to pass through customs.

## Frequent errors

- **Go at closing time** and find empty stalls.
- **Pay with large bills** that sellers cannot change.
- **Photographing people without permission.**
- **Haggle at the food stalls.**
- **Buy wildlife products** or archaeological pieces.
- **Neglect your backpack** in hallways with many people.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to eat in the markets?

In general yes, if you choose busy stalls, freshly cooked food and take care of the water and ice. Many travelers remember the market meals as the best part of their trip.

### Do you accept cards in the markets?

Some stalls accept electronic payments or transfers, but cash in small bills is still most convenient. More information in the guide about [money, cards and change](/bitacora/dinero-tarjetas-y-cambio/).

### How much time to dedicate to a large market?

To explore it calmly, try something and have lunch, reserve at least two or three hours. The largest markets, or the town markets on market day, can easily take up half a day if you are interested in cooking or crafts.

### What day is best to visit a town market?

The market day, when the area's producers arrive. Check at the tourist office or your accommodation what day it is.

## In summary

Markets are the best gateway to the cuisine and daily life of each place. Go early or on a market day, take a look around before buying, ask and try. Eat in the market canteens, discover new fruits and learn their local names. Bargain only where it is customary and always with respect, ask permission before photographing, take care of your health and your belongings, and do not buy wildlife or archaeological pieces.
