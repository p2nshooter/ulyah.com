A trip through Latin America can include in a few weeks a Caribbean beach at thirty degrees, an Andean city where the sunrise is close to zero, a humid jungle where it rains every afternoon and a capital where you want to have dinner in a good restaurant. Carrying clothes for each situation separately is impossible without carrying a huge suitcase, and a huge suitcase is exactly what you least want when climbing stairs in a colonial town, loading luggage on a night bus or boarding a small plane with a weight limit.

The solution is to choose versatile pieces that combine with each other and work in layers. With a little planning, a medium-sized backpack or small suitcase is enough for a long trip. In this guide you will see how to choose luggage, how the layering system works, what fabrics and shoes to bring, what you need in each environment, how to solve electricity and how to wash on the road.

## The challenge: several climates on the same trip

Before making the list, write down the climates you will encounter:

- **Humid heat:** Caribbean and tropical Pacific coasts, jungles and lowlands.
- **Dry heat with cold nights:** deserts, especially high altitude ones.
- **Mountain cold:** Andean cities and excursions above 2500 meters, with intense sun during the day and cold at night.
- **Temperate and changeable climate:** cities in the south of the continent and Patagonia, with wind and rain.

Also remember that in the southern hemisphere the seasons are inverted with respect to the north, and that in tropical areas the rainy season matters more than the temperature. Check the weather in each destination for the exact dates of your trip.

## Backpack or suitcase

| Option | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| travel backpack | comfortable on stairs, cobblestone streets, beaches and public transport | you can load your back if you weigh too much |
| suitcase with wheels | practical in airports, hotels and cities with good sidewalks | uncomfortable on cobblestones, sand, stairs and dirt roads |
| Backpack with wheels | combine both | slightly heavier than a simple backpack |

For trips that combine cities, towns, buses and nature, a backpack between forty and fifty-five liters is usually sufficient, along with a small day pack. A good rule of thumb: your luggage should not weigh more than you can comfortably carry for half an hour walking. When you leave, fill it only about two-thirds full: the free space makes it easy to close and leaves room for whatever you buy along the way.

## The layer system

Instead of wearing a thick coat that you'll only wear a few days, combine thin layers that you can add or take away:

1. **Base layer:** Short or long sleeve t-shirt that draws sweat away from the skin.
2. **Intermediate layer:** a light fleece or a thin down or synthetic fiber jacket, which compresses and is very warm for its weight.
3. **Outer layer:** a waterproof and windproof jacket with a hood.

With these three pieces you can go from the heat of midday to the cold of a sunrise excursion in the mountains. Add a hat, thin gloves and a tubular collar, which take up very little space and are appreciated at height.

## Fabrics: what works and what doesn't

- **Technical synthetic fibers:** they dry quickly, weigh little and do not wrinkle. Ideal for humid heat and physical activity.
- **Merino wool:** is warm, regulates temperature and retains less odor than other fibers, so you can use it for several days. It is more expensive, but very versatile.
- **Lightweight linen and cotton:** cool in hot cities, but slow to dry.
- **Heavy cotton and jeans:** Heavy, slow to dry and cold when wet. He wears at most a pair of jeans, or none at all.

Choose neutral colors that combine with each other: with just a few items you can create many outfits.

## Footwear

Footwear takes up a lot of space and weighs a lot, so limit yourself to three pairs maximum:

- **Lightweight walking or trekking shoes,** comfortable for cities and trails. Try them before the trip.
- **Sandals** for the beach, shared showers and hot days.
- **A more presentable pair,** optional, if you plan to go out to dinner or go to the theater.

If you are going on long hikes in the mountains, boots that support the ankle can replace sneakers. Wear the heaviest ones when traveling so as not to carry them in your backpack.

## Clothes for every environment

### Beach and tropical coast

- Swimsuit and a lycra t-shirt with UV protection for the water.
- Light and loose clothing.
- Wide-brimmed hat and sunglasses.
- A sarong or a microfiber towel, which dries quickly.

### jungle

- Long, light, light-colored shirts and pants that protect against mosquitoes and the sun.
- High socks. Many jungle lodges lend rubber boots; ask before bringing yours.
- Quick-drying clothing, because humidity prevents anything from drying completely.

More ideas in the guide about [travel to the Amazon](/bitacora/viajar-a-la-amazonia/).

### Height and mountains

- The complete three-layer system.
- Hat, gloves and tubular collar.
- Trekking pants and, if you go hiking at dawn, a thermal layer for your legs.
- High sunscreen and lip balm: the sun at altitude is very intense.

The guide to [altitude sickness](/bitacora/mal-de-altura-soroche/) explains how to adapt to altitude.

### City

- A slightly more elegant set for restaurants or shows.
- Clothing that covers shoulders and knees for visiting churches and temples, where it is often requested.
- A light scarf or bandana, useful for the cold of air conditioning and to cover yourself in religious places.

## Rain and humidity

- **Waterproof jacket** with a hood, the same one you use as an outer layer.
- **Waterproof cover for the backpack,** useful in buses, boats and downpours.
- **Watertight bags** to protect documents and electronic devices on boat excursions.
- **Cloth or compression bags** to separate wet clothes from dry ones.

A small umbrella is useful in cities, but in the mountains or in the wind the jacket is much more practical.

## Electronics, plugs and voltage

In Latin America, several types of plugs and two main voltages coexist.

| Countries | Usual plugs | Approximate voltage |
|---|---|---|
| Mexico, Central America, Colombia, Ecuador, Dominican Republic | types A and B, with flat pins | 110 to 127 volts |
| Peru and Bolivia | types A and C | 220 to 230 volts |
| Chile | types C and L | 220 volts |
| Argentina | type I, flat angled pins | 220 volts |
| Uruguay and Paraguay | several, especially C | 220 volts |
| Brazil | type N and sometimes C | 127 or 220 volts, depending on the city |

The information is indicative and may vary between buildings. A **universal adapter** solves almost all cases. Most phone and computer chargers work with any voltage between 100 and 240 volts, but check the label; Appliances such as hair dryers or straighteners may require a transformer.

Also carry:

- An external battery for long days of hiking or on the bus.
- A small power strip or charger with several ports, useful in accommodations with few outlets.
- Replacement cables.

## Toiletry bag and first aid kit

- Products in solid format, such as shampoo or bar soap, that weigh less and do not spill.
- Small containers for liquids: in many airports, carry-on luggage only accepts containers of up to one hundred milliliters in a transparent bag.
- Sunscreen, repellent and lip balm.
- A basic first aid kit and your prescription medications, as we explain in the guide on [traveler health and vaccines](/bitacora/salud-del-viajero-y-vacunas/).

## Documents and money

- Passport or identity document, with paper and digital copies.
- A fanny pack or interior pocket for money and passport when traveling.
- A small padlock for hostel lockers and zippers.
- The cards distributed in two different places.

More information in the guides on [travel documents](/bitacora/documentos-de-viaje/) and [money, cards and change](/bitacora/dinero-tarjetas-y-cambio/).

## Wash on the road

In many cities and towns in the region there are laundries that charge per kilo of clothes and return them clean and folded in a few hours or the next day. It is an economical solution that allows you to travel with little clothing. For everyday use, a little bar soap or sachet detergent and a bungee cord allow you to wash small items in the sink. The quick-drying fabrics will be ready in the morning.

## Buy at the destination

It is not necessary to carry everything from home. In the Andean markets you will find hats, gloves and wool or alpaca clothing at good prices, and in large cities there are clothing and mountain equipment stores. Keep in mind, however, that branded technical items are usually more expensive and have less variety of sizes than in other countries. Buy what is simple and typical at the destination, and take from home what is technical and what you already know looks good on you.

## Baggage limits on flights, trains and small planes

- **Low-cost flights:** Many airlines only include one small personal item in the base fare and charge separately for carry-on and checked bags. Check the measurements and weight before purchasing.
- **Tourist trains:** some, such as those to Machu Picchu, limit luggage to a small carry-on piece. We see it in the guide on [tourist trains](/bitacora/trenes-turisticos/).
- **Light aircraft and boats:** Flights to remote areas and some boats have strict weight limits. Ask beforehand and leave what you don't need in an accommodation that stores luggage.

## Frequent errors

- **Carry too many clothes** "just in case."
- **Travel only in summer clothing** to an itinerary that includes altitude.
- **Carry several jeans** that are heavy and do not dry.
- **Brand new shoes** on the trip.
- **Forget the plug adapter**.
- **Do not check the baggage limits** of low-cost airlines, trains and small planes.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many kilos should my luggage weigh?

There is no single figure, but many travelers are comfortable with a backpack weighing between ten and twelve kilos for long trips. The important thing is that you can carry it effortlessly for a while.

### Do I need a down jacket if I go to the mountains?

A light down or synthetic fiber jacket is very useful in the Andes and Patagonia, because it is very warm and takes up little space. Combined with a waterproof jacket, it covers almost all situations.

### Is it better to carry a backpack or suitcase?

If your trip combines towns, buses, beaches and nature, a backpack is usually more practical. If you move mainly between cities and hotels, a suitcase with wheels may be more comfortable.

### Can I buy warm clothes there?

Yes, especially simple woolen garments in the Andean markets. Technical jackets should be worn from home.

## In summary

For a multi-climate trip, choose a backpack or small suitcase and fill it only two-thirds full. Use the layering system, with a base layer, a light coat and a waterproof jacket, and prefer quick-drying fabrics and coordinating colors. Limit your shoes to three pairs, protect documents and gadgets from the rain, carry a universal adapter and a basic first aid kit, and wash on the go instead of carrying more clothes. Check the luggage limits on flights, trains and small planes, and buy simple and typical items at the destination.
