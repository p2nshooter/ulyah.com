The Amazon is the largest rainforest on the planet and extends across nine countries: Brazil, Peru, Colombia, Ecuador, Bolivia, Venezuela, Guyana, Suriname and French Guiana. Immense rivers, forests that flood every year, pink dolphins, macaws, monkeys and an enormous diversity of indigenous peoples make it one of the most fascinating destinations in the world. Also one of the most delicate, for the traveler and for the ecosystem itself.

A good trip to the jungle is not improvised. It requires choosing the entrance door and the time of year well, preparing your health in advance, carrying the appropriate equipment and, above all, choosing operators who work with respect for nature and the communities. In this guide you will see how to organize it step by step.

## The entrance doors

| Country | Access city | Comments |
|---|---|---|
| Brazil | Manaus | the great city of the Brazilian Amazon, at the meeting of the Negro River with the Solimões |
| Peru | Iquitos | It can only be reached by plane or by river, with no road connection to the rest of the country. |
| Peru | Puerto Maldonado | access to the Tambopata reserve, in the southern Amazon |
| Colombia | Leticia | on the triple border with Brazil and Peru |
| Ecuador | Coca and Lago Agrio | access to areas such as Yasuní and Cuyabeno |
| Bolivia | Rurrenabaque | access to Madidi Park and the Beni pampas |

Each entrance door offers different landscapes and experiences. The areas closest to cities tend to be more intervened; To see well-preserved jungle and more fauna, you need to go several hours by boat.

## High water and low water

In much of the Amazon, river levels rise and fall by several meters throughout the year. The dates change depending on the region, but the cycle transforms the trip:

- **High water:** the forest is flooded and you can canoe through the trees. There is less hiking, but the boat excursions are spectacular.
- **Low water:** beaches appear in the rivers, there are more passable trails and the fauna is concentrated near the water.

There is no "best" time: it depends on what you want to do. Ask the operators in the area what happens on the dates of your trip.

## Lodge, cruise or community tourism

- **Lodges in the jungle:** accommodations away from the cities, with daily guided excursions. They are the most common option.
- **River cruises:** boats that navigate rivers and disembark for excursions. They allow you to cover long distances with comfort.
- **Community tourism:** accommodations and excursions managed by local or indigenous communities. They offer a unique look at life in the jungle and leave the benefits in the community. We see it in the guide on [community tourism and cultural respect](/bitacora/turismo-comunitario-y-respeto-cultural/).
- **Passenger ships:** On some routes, slow boats connect cities for several days, with passengers sleeping in hammocks. It is an intense and very local experience, which requires patience and care with belongings.

## What is a typical day at a lodge like?

Although each accommodation organizes its program, many follow a similar rhythm, marked by light and heat:

| Moment | Usual activity |
|---|---|
| Before dawn | canoe trip to hear the awakening of the jungle and see birds |
| Mid morning | guided walk along trails, with explanations about plants and fauna |
| Noon | lunch and rest in the hottest hours |
| afternoon | boat trip, lake or community visit |
| sunset | observation of dolphins, birds or alligators from the water |
| night | night walk to see insects, frogs and other animals |

The early hours are part of the experience: the fauna is most active in the first and last hours of the day. Going to bed early helps you enjoy the sunrise.

## How many days to dedicate

Two nights are usually not enough: a good part of the time is spent traveling. With **three or four nights** in a remote lodge you can experience the jungle more calmly: hikes, canoe outings at sunrise and sunset, night observation and time to simply listen to the forest.

## Health: prepare in time

Visit a travel medicine center several weeks before you leave. Some points that are usually discussed:

- **Yellow fever:** the vaccine is recommended for many Amazonian areas, and some countries require the certificate for travelers coming from risk areas. It must be applied in advance.
- **Malaria:** in some areas there is a risk, and the doctor may prescribe preventive medication depending on the place and time.
- **Dengue and other mosquito-borne diseases:** There is no vaccine available for all travelers, so protection against bites is essential.
- **Water:** drink only treated or bottled water.

More information in the guide on [traveler health and vaccines](/bitacora/salud-del-viajero-y-vacunas/).

### Mosquito protection

- **Effective repellent** with ingredients such as DEET or icaridin, applied according to instructions.
- **Lightweight clothing with long sleeves and long pants,** in light colors.
- **Mosquito net** over the bed if the accommodation does not have netted rooms.
- **Be more careful at sunrise and sunset.**

## What to bring

- **Quick-drying clothing** with long sleeves and light colors.
- **Lightweight raincoat:** It rains often in the jungle, even in the dry season.
- **Rubber boots:** many lodges lend them; ask before.
- **Headlamp** with spare batteries.
- **Watertight bags** for documents, phone and camera.
- **Binoculars** are essential to see birds and monkeys high in the trees.
- **Cash:** in many areas there are no ATMs or card payments. We see it in the guide on [money, cards and change](/bitacora/dinero-tarjetas-y-cambio/).
- **Personal medications** and a small first aid kit.

The guide on [luggage for varied climates](/bitacora/equipaje-para-climas-variados/) helps you combine the jungle with other travel destinations.

## Fauna: see without disturbing

The jungle is not a zoo: the animals are camouflaged, they move and do not always appear. A good guide knows where to look and how to approach without disturbing.

- **Choose operators who do not handle animals.** In some Amazonian cities, photos are offered with captured sloths, monkeys or parrots: these practices harm animals and fuel wildlife trafficking. Avoid them.
- **Do not feed the animals** or leave food scraps.
- **Be silent** on walks: you will see and hear much more.
- **Do not buy products** made with feathers, fur, teeth or shells.

For bird lovers, the Amazon is a paradise. More ideas in the guide on [bird watching](/bitacora/observacion-de-aves/).

## Safety in the jungle

- **Always walk with a guide** and along the indicated trails.
- **Watch where you step and where you put your hands:** trunks, branches and leaves can hide insects or snakes.
- **Wear a life jacket** on boats.
- **Follow the guide's instructions** on where to swim in the river.
- **Check your shoes** before putting them on in the morning.

## Ceremonies with plants

In some Amazonian areas, ceremonies with ayahuasca or other psychoactive plants are offered to tourists. They involve real health risks, especially for people who take certain medications or have heart or psychiatric problems, and abuse by false guides has been reported. They are not part of an ordinary tourist trip; If you are interested in the topic, educate yourself thoroughly and consult a doctor.

## Respect for indigenous peoples

The Amazon is home to many indigenous peoples, some in voluntary isolation. Visit communities only through initiatives that they manage or approve, ask permission before photographing, and buy crafts directly from those who produce them. Never try to contact people in isolation.

## Frequent errors

- **Stay only two nights** near the city and expect to see lots of wildlife.
- **Get vaccinated at the last minute.**
- **Do not wear repellent or long-sleeved clothing.**
- **Take photos with captured wild animals.**
- **Go cashless** to areas without ATMs.
- **Choose the cheapest operator** without reviewing their reputation or practices.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which country is best to visit the Amazon?

They all offer valuable experiences. The choice depends on your itinerary: Iquitos or Puerto Maldonado if you are in Peru, Leticia if you are traveling through Colombia, Coca or Lago Agrio from Ecuador, Manaus in Brazil or Rurrenabaque in Bolivia.

### Can you travel to the Amazon with children?

Yes, in lodges prepared for families and with the appropriate vaccines and protection measures. Consult your pediatrician first.

### Is there electricity and internet connection in the lodges?

It varies a lot. Some lodges have electricity only a few hours a day, generated by solar panels or generators, and the internet connection can be slow or non-existent. Bring charged batteries and take the opportunity to disconnect for a few days.

### Is it dangerous to bathe in the Amazon rivers?

In some places it is safe and part of the experience, but always following the instructions of the local guide, who knows the currents and fauna of each area.

## In summary

Choose the gateway based on your itinerary, understand the high and low water cycle, and spend at least three or four nights in the jungle. Visit a travel medicine center in time for the yellow fever vaccine and malaria advice, and always protect yourself from mosquitoes. Bring long-sleeved clothing, a raincoat, a flashlight, dry bags, binoculars, and cash. Choose responsible operators who do not handle animals, respect the communities and always walk with a guide.
