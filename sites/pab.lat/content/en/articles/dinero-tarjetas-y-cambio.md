Paying for a taxi in a mountain town, leaving a tip in a restaurant in Buenos Aires, withdrawing cash at an ATM in Cartagena or buying crafts in a market in Oaxaca: money accompanies every moment of the trip, and managing it well saves much more than it seems. Banking fees, unfavorable exchange rates, ATMs that charge for each withdrawal or the famous dynamic conversion in the card machine can add up to a significant amount at the end of the trip.

Latin America is also diverse in this: each country has its currency, some use the US dollar, some accept cards almost everywhere and others still depend heavily on cash. In this guide you will see how to combine cards and cash, how to avoid unnecessary fees, where to exchange money, how to use ATMs safely, what tipping customs exist and what to do if you lose a card.

In some countries there are or have been various exchange rates, restrictions on access to foreign currency or sudden changes in the price. Before traveling, find out about the current situation of the country you are going to visit from official or reliable financial sources.

## Card, cash or a combination

**Credit and debit cards** are widely accepted in hotels, restaurants, supermarkets, shops and transportation in cities. Contactless and telephone payments are becoming more common.

**Cash** remains essential for:

- markets and street stalls,
- taxis and local transport in many cities,
- small towns and rural areas,
- tips,
- entrances to some sites and public bathrooms.

The best strategy is to **combine**: use the card for large expenses and always carry some cash in local currency, in small bills.

## Commissions to avoid

- **Foreign transaction fee:** Many banks charge a percentage for each purchase in another currency. Some cards do not charge this fee; Compare before traveling.
- **Fee for withdrawal at ATMs:** The ATM in the country may charge a fixed fee for withdrawal, and your bank may charge another.
- **Dynamic currency conversion:** When the card machine or ATM offers to charge you in your country's currency instead of the local currency, an unfavorable exchange rate is usually applied. **Always choose to pay in local currency.**
- **Exchange at airports:** airport exchange offices usually offer worse rates. Change only the minimum to get to your accommodation.

## before leaving

1. **Check with your bank** what fees apply abroad and if you must activate the use of the card outside the country.
2. **Carry at least two cards** from different networks, stored separately.
3. **Check that you will receive security codes** for payments and online banking, for example by SMS with roaming activated or through the bank app.
4. **Write down the emergency telephone numbers** of your banks to block cards.
5. **Carry some cash** in an easy-to-exchange currency as a reserve.

## ATMs with security

- **Use ATMs inside banks,** shopping centers or crowded places, and during the day.
- **Covers the keyboard** when typing the key.
- **Check that the card slot** does not have any loose or added pieces.
- **Withdraw reasonable amounts** so as not to make too many withdrawals with commission, but without carrying large sums with you.
- **Put the money away before leaving** the cashier's cubicle.
- **If the ATM holds your card,** call your bank immediately.

## Where to change money

| Option | Advantages | Precautions |
|---|---|---|
| Authorized exchange houses | They usually offer good rates | compare several and count the money on the counter |
| Banks | insurance | Limited hours, sometimes long procedures |
| ATMs | practical | withdrawal fees and fees from your bank |
| money changers on the street | fast | risk of counterfeit bills and scams; better to avoid them |

If you carry dollars in cash, make sure they are **in good condition**: in many places in the region, torn, stained or very worn bills are rejected.

## Tips: customs of each country

Customs change and it is advisable to ask in each place, but as a guide:

- **In restaurants,** in many countries around 10 percent is usually left. In several of them the suggested tip already appears on the bill as "service"; Check before adding another.
- **In cafes and bars,** it is common to round up or leave a few coins.
- **For tour guides and drivers,** a tip based on the quality of service is customary.
- **In taxis,** in many places tipping is not expected, although rounding up is appreciated.
- **For car caretakers, porters and cleaning staff,** it is common to leave a small amount.

## Taxes and benefits for tourists

In most countries, value added tax is already included in the prices. Some countries offer tax exemptions or refunds to foreign visitors on certain services, such as accommodation paid for with a foreign card or in foreign currency. Conditions change, so check with your accommodation and official sources.

## Payments with the phone and local wallets

In several countries in the region, instant payments between accounts, QR codes and local digital wallets have become popular, which many businesses prefer for their speed. They are usually designed for residents with bank accounts in the country, and are not always accessible to a visitor. Use them if they are practical, but don't depend on them: always carry an international card and cash.

## Think in local currency

The first days in a new country, prices can be confusing, especially with currencies that have many zeros. Some ideas to avoid mistakes:

- **Memorize a simple equivalence** between the local currency and yours, for example how much a thousand or ten thousand units represent.
- **Use a conversion app** that works offline.
- **Check the change** that they give you back, without rushing.
- **Look at the published prices** in markets and transport before asking.

## If you travel to several countries

On a trip through several countries, try to spend each country's currency before crossing the border, because exchanging leftover bills in another country is usually expensive. Save only a small amount for initial transportation when you arrive at the next destination, and find out if there are reliable ATMs or exchange offices at the border. ## Take care of your money

- **Distribute the cash** between your wallet, an inside pocket, and your accommodation luggage.
- **Do not display large bills** when paying.
- **Use an inside fanny pack** in crowded areas.
- **Leave in the accommodation safe** what you don't need that day.

More tips in the guide on [safety in large cities](/bitacora/seguridad-en-grandes-ciudades/).

## If you lose your card or it is stolen

1. **Block her immediately** from the bank app or by phone.
2. **Review recent movements** and report any unacknowledged charges.
3. **Make a police report** if it was a robbery; Your insurance or your bank may request it.
4. **Use your backup card** while you resolve the issue.
5. **If you run out of money,** international money transfer services allow someone you trust to transfer cash to you.

## Frequent errors

- **Accept dynamic conversion** and pay in your country's currency.
- **Change all money at the airport.**
- **Travel with a single card.**
- **Bring torn or worn dollar bills.**
- **Exchange money with street money changers.**
- **Not having cash** when arriving in a small town.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it advisable to carry dollars?

In dollarized countries, such as Ecuador, El Salvador or Panama, it is the currency of daily use. In others, a few dollars in good condition can serve as a reserve to exchange at exchange offices, but the local currency is what you will need on the street.

### Can I pay with the phone?

In cities, more and more businesses accept contactless payments. Also carry a physical card and cash as backup.

### Is a credit or debit card better?

For payments in stores, the credit card usually offers more protection against fraud and improper charges. To withdraw cash, debit is usually more convenient, because credit withdrawals can have high interest and fees. Carrying one of each type is a good combination.

### How much cash should I carry?

What you need for the day: meals, transportation, tickets and some margin. The rest, in the accommodation or on cards.

## In summary

Combine large spending cards and cash in local currency for markets, taxis, tips and small towns. Choose cards without commission abroad, carry at least two and always pay in the local currency to avoid dynamic conversion. Use safe ATMs, change at authorized exchange houses and not on the street, and carry dollars in good condition if you need them. Learn about tips and taxes in each country, distribute the money, and block any lost cards immediately.
