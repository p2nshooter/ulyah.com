There are journeys in which the destination is the path. In Latin America, some roads cross landscapes so extraordinary that traveling them becomes the main reason for the trip: routes that border fjords and glaciers in Chilean Patagonia, that climb to more than four thousand meters between colorful hills in northern Argentina, that wind along the Atlantic coast between jungle and sea or that cross the Baja California desert between giant cacti and turquoise bays.

Traveling them requires, however, more preparation than a trip on the highway. There are sections of gravel, mountain passes that close due to snow, ferries that must be reserved, long distances without fuel and curves that do not forgive distractions. In this guide you will see some of the most beautiful scenic routes in the region, what to keep in mind for each one and how to prepare to enjoy them safely.

## The Southern Highway

Chile's Route 7, known as Carretera Austral, runs more than a thousand kilometers through northern Patagonia, approximately from Puerto Montt to Villa O'Higgins. It crosses one of the least populated regions on the continent, with rainforests, turquoise rivers, hanging glaciers and immense lakes.

Some of its best-known places are Pumalín Park, the hanging snowdrift of Queulat National Park, the marble formations of Lake General Carrera, which can be visited by boat, and the town of Caleta Tortel, built on wooden walkways.

- **Gravel:** Although there are more and more paved sections, there are still many kilometers of gravel. Drive slowly and carefully on curves.
- **Ferry:** in some sections, the route is interrupted and continues by boat. In high season it is advisable to reserve.
- **Fuel and money:** gas stations and ATMs are scarce; charge whenever you can and carry cash.
- **Climate:** It rains frequently even in summer. The best time is usually from December to March.

## Route 40

Route 40 runs through Argentina from north to south for more than five thousand kilometers, from the border with Bolivia to the southern tip of Patagonia, largely following the Andes mountain range. Few walk through it all at once; The usual thing is to choose sections:

- **In the north,** it crosses valleys and ravines at high altitude, and in the Acay pass, in Salta, it reaches one of its highest points, at almost five thousand meters.
- **In Cuyo,** it passes through vineyard regions and desert landscapes.
- **In northern Patagonia,** the stretch of the Seven Lakes, between San Martín de los Andes and Villa La Angostura, is one of the most beautiful in the country.
- **In southern Patagonia,** it crosses immense steppes on the way to El Calafate and El Chaltén, with intense wind and long distances between towns.

## The ravines of northern Argentina

The Argentine northwest brings together some of the most photogenic roads in the region:

- **Quebrada de las Conchas,** on the route between Salta and Cafayate, with red rock formations such as the Devil's Throat and the Amphitheater.
- **Cuesta del Obispo and the Tin Tin straight,** on the way to Cachi, through the Los Cardones National Park, populated by large cacti.
- **Quebrada de Humahuaca,** in Jujuy, declared a world heritage site, with towns like Purmamarca and its Cerro de los Siete Colores.
- **Cuesta de Lipán,** which goes up from Purmamarca to the Salinas Grandes, at more than four thousand meters.

Climb little by little, because several sections reach high altitudes, as we explain in the guide on [altitude sickness](/bitacora/mal-de-altura-soroche/).

## Cross the Andes by road

The Cristo Redentor Pass, between Mendoza and Santiago, is one of the busiest crossings in the mountain range. On the Argentine side, the route passes next to the Aconcagua Provincial Park, with views of the highest mountain in America; On the Chilean side, it descends the famous Caracoles slope, with dozens of sharp curves. In winter, the pass can close for days due to snow, and in high season long lines form at the checkpoints. Check the status of the crossing on the same day and read the guide on [crossing borders by land](/bitacora/cruzar-fronteras-por-tierra/).

## Peru: from Colca to the Cordillera Blanca

From Arequipa, the road to the Colca Canyon crosses a reserve with vicuñas and reaches a volcano viewpoint at almost five thousand meters before descending into the valley, with its pre-Hispanic agricultural terraces and viewpoints where condors can be seen. In the Cordillera Blanca, the roads leaving Huaraz climb between lagoons and snow-capped mountains, and some cross the mountain range through high-altitude tunnels. They are spectacular routes, with tight curves, cliffs and unprotected sections. Many travelers prefer to do them on excursions with local drivers.

## Ecuador: the Avenue of Volcanoes

The Andean section of the Pan-American Highway between Quito and the south of the country is called the Avenue of the Volcanoes, because it passes next to some of the great Ecuadorian volcanoes, such as Cotopaxi or Chimborazo. From the Latacunga area, the so-called Quilotoa circuit runs through Andean towns to a lagoon inside a crater. Fog and rain are common, so drive with your lights on and take your time.

## Mexico: the Transpeninsular

Federal Highway 1 runs through the Baja California peninsula from Tijuana to Cabo San Lucas, for about 1,700 kilometers. The path alternates deserts with giant cacti, ancient missions, fishing villages and bays with turquoise water in the Sea of ​​Cortez, such as Concepción Bay. It is a long-distance route without services, with narrow sections and no shoulders, so plan your fuel and avoid driving at night, when loose cattle and potholes are difficult to see.

## Brazil: green coast and southern mountains

- **The coast between Rio de Janeiro and Santos** combines Atlantic jungle, islands, beaches and towns like Paraty, with its colonial center. During high season and long weekends, traffic can be heavy.
- **The Serra do Rio do Rastro,** in Santa Catarina, descends from the plateau towards the coast with a succession of closed curves that has become one of the most famous images of the south of the country. Fog is frequent; If there is no visibility, wait.

## before leaving

1. **Choose the right vehicle:** For sections of gravel, fords or mountain roads, a truck with good height or an all-terrain vehicle provides more security.
2. **Check the rental contract:** some agencies do not allow driving on gravel, crossing borders or climbing certain passes, and the insurance may not cover damages in those cases.
3. **Check the spare tire,** the jack and the tools.
4. **Plan fuel:** On remote routes, charge whenever you find a station, even if you don't have an empty tank.
5. **Download offline maps:** There is no signal on many routes.
6. **Check the status of the routes and passes** on the same day, especially in winter or the rainy season.
7. **Book ferries and accommodations** in high season.

## Driving in the mountains

- **Low down in low gears** on long slopes, so as not to overheat the brakes.
- **On narrow roads,** reduce speed before each curve and stay well to your right.
- **Honk** before blind curves on one-lane roads, if customary in the area.
- **Give way** to large vehicles and, in general, to those who climb in very narrow sections, unless the signs indicate otherwise.
- **Take altitude into account:** The engine performs less and passengers may feel altitude sickness.

## Gravel, dust and fords

- **Moderate speed:** on gravel, sudden braking and turns make you lose control.
- **Distance:** keep distance from the vehicle in front to avoid the stones it raises and the dust that prevents you from seeing.
- **Tires:** Check the pressure and carry a spare wheel in good condition.
- **Fords:** Before crossing a river or flooded section, check the depth and current. If you doubt, do not cross.

## Stops, photos and rhythm

On a scenic route, the temptation to stop anywhere for a photo is enormous. Do it only at viewpoints or in places where you can completely move away from the road, never on curves, bridges or sections without visibility.

Also calculate that you will advance much more slowly than the map indicates: between curves, gravel, works and stops, the average speed can be very low. We explain it in the guide on [real distances and times](/bitacora/distancias-y-tiempos-reales/). It is better to travel fewer kilometers and enjoy them than to spend the entire day behind the wheel.

## Roads with a bad reputation

Some mountain routes are famous for their danger, such as the ancient Yungas road in Bolivia, known as the road of death, which today is traveled mainly by mountain bike with tour operators, or some mountain sections in southern Colombia. If a road has that reputation, do your research, consider traveling with an experienced local driver, and don't travel it in the rain, fog, or at night.

## Frequent errors

- **Underestimate times** on mountain and gravel routes.
- **Running out of fuel** between distant towns.
- **Do not review the rental contract** regarding gravel and borders.
- **Stop on curves** or in places without visibility to take photos.
- **Driving at night** on routes with livestock and without lighting.
- **Do not check the status of the mountain passes** before leaving.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need an all-terrain vehicle for the Carretera Austral?

Not necessarily. Many travelers travel through it in common cars with good height, driving slowly on the gravel. A taller and more robust vehicle gives more comfort and a margin of safety.

### What is the best time for these routes?

It depends on the region. In Patagonia, the southern summer, approximately from December to March. In northern Argentina and the central Andes, the dry season, when there is less rain and cuts. In Baja California, outside the intense summer heat.

### Can they be done by bus or with excursions?

Many of these routes have regular buses or organized excursions, a good option if you don't want to drive. Consult the guide on [long distance buses](/bitacora/buses-de-larga-distancia/).

### Is it safe to drive on these routes?

With preparation and prudence, yes. The main risks are inappropriate speed, fatigue, weather and night driving.

## In summary

The scenic routes of Latin America, from the Carretera Austral and Route 40 to the ravines of northern Argentina, the Colca, the Avenue of the Volcanoes, Baja California or the green coast of Brazil, are trips in themselves. Prepare the vehicle well, review the rental contract, plan fuel, maps and ferries and check the status of routes and passes. Drive slowly on mountains and gravel roads, stopping only in safe places and calculate your times generously: on these roads, haste is the worst travel companion.
