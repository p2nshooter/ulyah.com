Many of the most memorable experiences of a trip to Latin America begin on a dock. A ferry to Isla Mujeres or Cozumel, a boat that jumps over the waves on the way to the Rosario Islands, a sailboat between the islands of Guna Yala, a boat for several days through the Chilean fjords, a boat with hammocks through the Amazon or a boat that crosses Lake Titicaca to the floating islands of the aurochs. The sea, rivers and lakes are paths as important as roads.

But not all journeys are the same. Between a modern ferry with hundreds of passengers and a small boat on the open sea there is a huge difference in comfort and safety. Knowing what type of boat you are going to take, how to choose the operator, what to do with luggage and how to prevent seasickness completely changes the experience. In this guide you will see all that, plus how the weather and season influence it.

## Types of boat

| Type | Where is it common | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Ferry or large ferry | busy short routes and intersections with vehicles | stable, with cover and seats; regular hours |
| fast catamaran | coastal journeys of one or two hours | faster; can move quite a bit in rough seas |
| Boat or panga | nearby islands, roadless coastal towns, excursions | fast, exposed to sun and water; hits with the waves |
| sailboat | multi-day crossings, such as between Panama and Colombia | unique experience, depending on the weather and the captain |
| river boat | Amazonian and inland rivers | slow, with hammocks or cabins; multi-day trips |
| lake boat | lakes like Titicaca or Nicaragua | calm or rough routes depending on the wind |

Schedules, operators and fares change, and many routes reduce their departures in the low season. Always confirm them in advance.

## Choose operator and vessel

On routes with several options, price should not be the only criterion:

- **Authorized operators:** buy at official dock ticket offices, company channels or recognized agencies, not from sellers who approach you on the street.
- **Condition of the boat:** A well-maintained boat, with an engine in good condition and a hull without visible damage, says a lot about the operator.
- **Two engines in the open sea:** On long boat trips, many boats have two engines, which allows you to return if one fails.
- **Safety equipment:** Enough vests for everyone, including children's sizes, radio and lights.
- **Recent opinions:** Other travelers often tell how the crossing was and how the crew behaved.

**Do not get on an overloaded boat,** even if they tell you it is normal. Wait for the next one.

## Vests and safety on board

- **On boats and small boats, wear the vest** during the entire trip, well adjusted. On many routes it is mandatory.
- **On large ferries,** locate the life jackets, lifeboats and exits when boarding, and pay attention to the instructions.
- **Children** must wear vests of their size. If you are traveling with small children, bring your own if you are not sure there will be any.
- **Stay seated** while the boat is sailing, especially on speed boats.
- **Avoid night crossings** in small boats.

## The weather rules

The state of the sea or the lake decides whether a crossing is a pleasant walk or a very uncomfortable experience, and even whether it can be sailed.

- **Check the wind and wave forecast** before a boat ride.
- **Accept cancellations:** In many countries, the maritime authority or port captain suspends departures when the sea is dangerous. It's for your safety.
- **Leave room in the itinerary:** If you have a flight after returning from an island, do not plan the return crossing on the same day if you can avoid it.
- **Know the season:** In the Caribbean, hurricane season runs from June to November, and in some months the trade winds stir up the sea. We see it in the guide on the [hurricane season in the Caribbean](/bitacora/temporada-de-huracanes-caribe/).

## Speed boats: where to sit

On a boat hitting the waves, where you sit changes the experience a lot:

- **The rear** tends to move less and receive fewer hits than the bow.
- **The bow** is the area that jumps the most; Repeated blows can cause pain and even back injuries.
- **If you have back problems,** you are pregnant or traveling with small children, ask for a place in the back and ask beforehand if the trip is suitable for you.
- **Hold on well** and bend your knees slightly for cushioning.

## The dizziness

Dizziness appears when the inner ear perceives a movement that the eyes do not confirm. Some ways to prevent it:

- **Look at the horizon** and avoid reading or looking at your phone.
- **Seek fresh air** and, if you can, sit in the center or back, where movement is less.
- **Eat something light** before going out and avoid alcohol and heavy foods.
- **Consult your pharmacy or doctor** about anti-sickness medications; Many must take time before boarding and can get sleepy.
- **Ginger** helps some people.

## Protect luggage

On boats, luggage is usually stacked in the bow or under a tarp, and it often arrives wet.

- **Wrap the backpack** in a waterproof cover or sturdy plastic bags.
- **Carry with you, in a waterproof bag,** your passport, money, phone and electronic devices.
- **Travel light:** on some boats and small planes there are weight limits and excess is charged.
- **Mark your luggage** to quickly recognize it when disembarking.

The guide on [luggage for various climates](/bitacora/equipaje-para-climas-variados/) includes ideas for protecting clothing and gadgets from moisture.

## Tickets, rates and schedules

- **Buy your return ticket** in advance during high season, or confirm return times.
- **Check fees:** Many docks and islands charge port, environmental or entry fees. San Andrés in Colombia requires a tourist card, and Morro de São Paulo in Brazil charges a user fee for visitors.
- **Take cash:** Many small docks do not accept cards.
- **Arrive early:** on ferries with vehicles or with immigration procedures, boarding closes before departure.

## Cross borders by water

Some crossings cross borders, such as ferries between Buenos Aires and Uruguay, boats between the Colombian coast and Panama or sailboats between Panama and Colombia. In all cases, you have to register your departure from one country and your entry into the other at the corresponding immigration offices. On sailboats, choose captains with good references and make sure they take care of the paperwork or tell you how to do it. We see this in the guide on [crossing borders by land](/bitacora/cruzar-fronteras-por-tierra/), whose principles also apply to crossings by water.

## Travel along Amazonian rivers

In the Amazon, the rivers are the roads. Passenger boats of several days and speed boats circulate between cities such as Manaus, Belém, Iquitos or Leticia.

- **On slow ships,** many passengers sleep in hammocks on open decks. Bring your hammock, ropes and a mosquito net, as well as a luggage lock.
- **Food and water:** ask what the ticket includes and bring drinking water and some food in reserve.
- **Speed ​​boats:** They are more expensive but they shorten the time a lot; also here, vest and protected luggage.
- **Health:** use repellent and long clothes at dusk, as we explain in the guide on [traveling to the Amazon](/bitacora/viajar-a-la-amazonia/).

## High altitude lakes

Sailing on Lake Titicaca, at more than 3,800 meters above sea level, has its particularities: the sun burns very hot due to the reflection of the water and the altitude, and the wind can be very cold, especially in the afternoon. Bring sunscreen, a hat, sunglasses and a coat, and water for the journey. On the lake, as in the sea, respect the instructions of the boatmen when the wind raises waves.

## Frequent errors

- **Choose the cheapest boat** without looking at its condition or safety equipment.
- **Sitting on the bow** of a speedboat in rough seas.
- **Do not use the vest** in small boats.
- **Carry your passport and phone in the backpack** that travels in the bow.
- **Plan a flight** on the same day of a trip that may be cancelled.
- **Take anti-sickness medication** when you are already dizzy.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are ferries safe in Latin America?

Large ferries on the busiest routes are usually safe and regular. The greatest risk is in small boats that are overloaded, poorly maintained or sailing in bad weather. Choose authorized operators and don't upload if you see something that doesn't convince you.

### What do I do if I get very dizzy?

Go to the back or center, look at the horizon and breathe fresh air. For the next trip, consult a pharmacy or your doctor about preventive medications and take them in advance.

### Can I carry a large suitcase on a boat?

Sometimes yes, but it is uncomfortable and may have limits or extra costs. For trips with many boats, a backpack is much more practical.

### What happens if the trip is canceled due to bad weather?

It is usually rescheduled for the next available time or day. Ask the conditions when purchasing and check if your [travel insurance](/bitacora/seguro-de-viaje/) covers costs for delays.

## In summary

Ferries, catamarans, motorboats, sailboats and river boats are an essential part of traveling through Latin America. Choose authorized operators and boats in good condition, with life jackets for everyone, and do not board overloaded boats. Respect the weather and cancellations, leave room in the itinerary and avoid night crossings in small boats. On speedboats, sit in the back, prevent motion sickness and protect documents and devices in waterproof bags. Thus, the journey will be part of the pleasure of the trip and not an endurance test.
