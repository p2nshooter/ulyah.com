No one plans to get sick on a trip, sprain an ankle on a trail, or lose their suitcase on a layover. But it happens, and when it happens far from home, without knowing the country's health system and perhaps without speaking the language, good insurance makes the difference between a bad time and an economic catastrophe. An overnight stay or an emergency medical transfer can cost much more than the entire trip.

The problem is that travel insurance looks very similar in advertising and differs a lot in the fine print. In this guide you will see what types of products exist, what coverages really matter, what exclusions should be reviewed, what happens with the insurance included in credit cards and what to do, step by step, if you need to use it during your trip.

## Travel insurance or travel assistance

In many countries in the region, two types of products that seem the same are sold:

- **Travel insurance:** a policy issued by an insurance company, which indemnifies or pays covered expenses according to the conditions of the contract.
- **Travel Assistance:** a service that coordinates and directly pays for medical care and other services within certain limits, through a network of providers.

In practice, both can work well. The important thing is not the name, but what it covers, up to what amount, with what exclusions and how care is requested.

## The coverages that matter most

| Coverage | Why is it important | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Medical expenses due to illness or accident | It's the most expensive thing that can happen | the maximum amount, the deductible and if they pay directly to the hospital |
| Medical transfer and repatriation | An ambulance plane transfer can be very expensive | that is included and under what conditions |
| Medications | treatments during the trip | limits and need for prescription |
| Emergency dentistry | a toothache can ruin the trip | amount and cases covered |
| Trip cancellation and interruption | recover what you paid if you cannot travel or must return | accepted reasons and required documentation |
| Lost or delayed luggage | urgent purchases and compensation | deadlines and receipts |
| Civil liability | damages you cause to third parties | limits |
| legal assistance | problems with authorities or accidents | If it includes lawyers or bail bonds |

## Exclusions that should be read with a magnifying glass

- **Pre-existing illnesses:** many policies do not cover health problems prior to the trip, or only cover emergencies arising from them with low limits. If you have a chronic illness, look for coverage that includes it.
- **Adventure sports and activities:** diving, rafting, skiing, paragliding or high-altitude trekking may be excluded or require an extension. Check especially if you travel to the Andes, as we explain in the guide on [altitude sickness](/bitacora/mal-de-altura-soroche/), or if you plan to climb volcanoes or dive.
- **Age:** some policies reduce coverage or increase the price after a certain age.
- **Pregnancy:** there are usually limits after certain weeks.
- **Alcohol and drugs:** accidents that occur under the influence are usually excluded.
- **Known events:** If you take out insurance when a hurricane already has a name or a strike has already been announced, it is likely that it will not cover its consequences. We explain it in the guide on [hurricane season](/bitacora/temporada-de-huracanes-caribe/).
- **Maximum duration per trip:** Some coverage is only valid for trips of a certain number of days.

## Is it mandatory?

For most tourist trips within the region it is not mandatory, but there are exceptions: some countries require visitors to have health insurance during their stay, as is the case in Cuba, and the requirements may change. Check it when reviewing the [travel documents](/bitacora/documentos-de-viaje/). Although it is not mandatory, it is highly recommended.

## Credit card insurance

Many credit cards include insurance or travel assistance. It may be enough, but check:

- **If you require to pay the fare with the card** or activate coverage before traveling.
- **The maximum amounts,** which are sometimes low for medical expenses.
- **Who is covered:** only the owner or also the family.
- **The exclusions** and the procedure to use it.
- **The maximum duration** of each covered trip.

## How to choose

1. **Define your needs:** destination, duration, age, planned activities and health status.
2. **Compare two or three options** with the same coverage, not just by price.
3. **Read the general conditions,** not just the commercial summary.
4. **Check the helpline** and if it is open 24 hours a day, in your language.
5. **If you travel several times a year,** consider annual multi-trip coverage.
6. **Hire it as soon as you buy the trip,** so that the cancellation coverage starts as soon as possible.

## Three situations to understand how it works

These hypothetical examples help you see what questions to ask when reading a policy:

- **A sprain on a trail.** You call assistance, they refer you to a clinic in their network and the care is paid for directly, except for the deductible if there is one. If the hike was an excluded high mountain trekking, you could have to pay for everything.
- **A strong gastroenteritis.** A video call consultation or a medical visit to the hotel can solve it. Save the prescription and medication invoices for reimbursement.
- **A sick family member at home.** If the policy includes the interruption of the trip due to a serious illness of a family member, it may cover early return. You will need medical certificates to prove it.

## Telemedicine and remote consultations

Many coverages today include medical consultations by telephone or video call. They are very useful for minor problems, such as a throat infection or digestive discomfort, because they avoid traveling and waiting. Save access to that service on your phone before you leave.

## Children and elderly people

If you are traveling as a family, check that all people are covered, with adequate amounts for each one. For older people, pay special attention to the limits based on age, previous illnesses and the price, which usually increases over the years. A policy that seems cheap may have reduced coverage just for those who need it most.

## If you get sick or have an accident

1. **In a serious emergency, go to the nearest hospital** or call emergency services. Your life comes first.
2. **Call your insurance assistance center** as soon as possible, ideally before being treated if it is not an emergency. Many policies require prior authorization to pay expenses.
3. **Follow their instructions:** They will tell you which medical center to go to or how to proceed.
4. **Keep all receipts:** medical reports, prescriptions, invoices and medication receipts.
5. **Ask for the medical report** with diagnosis, treatment and dates.
6. **Submit the claim** within the period indicated in the policy.

## If your luggage is stolen or lost

- **Theft:** make a report to the local police within the next few hours and ask for a copy. Without a complaint, many policies do not cover.
- **Baggage lost on a flight:** claim at the airline counter before leaving the airport and ask for proof of the claim.
- **Urgent purchases:** keep receipts for what you buy due to delayed luggage.

## Keep information at hand

- **The policy number** and the assistance telephone number saved on the phone and on paper.
- **A copy of the policy** in your email or in the cloud.
- **Share the data** with someone you trust at home.

## Frequent errors

- **Choose only on price** without looking at medical amounts.
- **Do not declare previous illnesses** or do not check if they are covered.
- **Practice adventure sports** without adequate coverage.
- **Going to the hospital without notifying assistance** when it is not an emergency.
- **Losing bills and medical reports.**
- **Contract insurance when the problem is already known.**

## Frequently asked questions

### How much medical coverage do I need?

It depends on the destination and your health. In countries where private care is expensive, a high amount is advisable. Compare options and don't choose the minimum coverage to save little money.

### Does insurance cover if I miss a flight?

Some policies cover expenses for connection delays or losses in certain cases. Review the conditions and keep the airline receipts.

### What happens if the hospital asks me to pay in advance?

It happens, especially if you did not call support before or if the center does not belong to their network. Pay only if it is essential, save all receipts and reports and contact the insurance as soon as possible to manage the reimbursement.

### Do I need insurance if my country has health agreements with the destination?

Some countries have healthcare agreements, but they usually cover only certain services and do not include transfers or repatriation. Insurance is still recommended.

## In summary

Travel insurance or assistance protects you from medical expenses, transfers, cancellations and losses that can cost much more than the trip. Review the medical amounts, deductible, repatriation and cancellation, and carefully read the exclusions for previous illnesses, adventure sports, height, age and known events. Check what your credit card covers, contract when booking and, if you need it, call assistance before being assisted when it is not an emergency and save all receipts.
