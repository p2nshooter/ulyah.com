Whoever imagines South American beaches usually thinks of the Caribbean or the Brazilian coast. But the Pacific, which bathes Colombia, Ecuador, Peru and Chile for thousands of kilometers, offers a variety of landscapes that are difficult to match: rainforests that fall into the sea in Chocó, fishing villages on the Ecuadorian coast, sunny beaches all year round in the north of Peru, cliffs in front of Lima, deserts that end in the ocean in the north of Chile and cold, green bays further south.

It is a coast of contrasts and also misunderstandings: many travelers arrive expecting warm water and find a cold sea, or plan a day at the beach in Lima in the middle of a gray winter. Understanding how this ocean works helps you choose the right time and destination. In this guide you will see what influences the climate of the coast, what each section offers, when to go, where to surf or see wildlife and how to swim safely.

## A coast marked by the Humboldt Current

Off the coasts of Chile and Peru, the Humboldt Current runs from south to north, a mass of cold water that, together with the outcrop of deep waters, explains much of the character of this coast:

- **Cold water:** Even in summer, the sea in much of Chile and central and southern Peru is much colder than in the Caribbean.
- **Sea very rich in life:** the deep waters bring nutrients that feed enormous quantities of plankton and fish, the basis of one of the most important fisheries in the world and colonies of birds and sea lions.
- **Deserts by the sea:** The cold air over the ocean generates little rain, and that is why the Sechura deserts in Peru and the Atacama deserts in Chile reach the shore.
- **Coastal fogs:** In winter, a layer of low clouds often covers the coast. In Peru they speak of garúa for fine drizzle, and in northern Chile, camanchaca for dense fog.

Further north, near the border between Peru and Ecuador, the waters become warmer and the landscape changes: dry forests, mangroves and, in Colombia, one of the rainiest jungles on the planet appear.

## El Niño: when the sea warms

Every few years, the waters of the equatorial Pacific warm more than normal in the phenomenon known as El Niño. On the coast of Ecuador and northern Peru it can bring intense rains, floods and floods, and in the sea it alters the distribution of fish and fauna. The opposite phenomenon, with colder than normal waters, is known as La Niña.

If you travel in an El Niño year, follow local news and authorities' advisories, especially in the rainy season, and check the status of the routes before traveling.

## The Colombian Pacific

The department of Chocó is a strip of tropical jungle wedged between the mountain range and the ocean, with some of the highest rainfall in the world. Places like Nuquí and Bahía Solano do not have a road connection with the interior of the country: they can be reached by small plane or by sea. In exchange, they offer dark beaches surrounded by jungle, waterfalls that fall almost on the sand, hot springs and a very lively Afro-Colombian culture, with traditional music such as currulao, based on the marimba. The marimba music and songs of the Colombian South Pacific and the Ecuadorian province of Esmeraldas are recognized by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage.

Between approximately July and October, humpback whales arrive in these waters to reproduce and breed. Bring clothing that dries quickly, is waterproof and repellent, and expect that it will rain even if it is whale season. Accommodations are simple and often run by local families and communities.

## The coast of Ecuador

The Ecuadorian coast combines fishing villages, surf beaches and natural parks. In the north, the province of Esmeraldas has a strong Afro-Ecuadorian identity. Further south, the so-called Spondylus Route, which takes its name from a shell highly valued by the ancient cultures of the region, runs through towns such as Puerto López, Ayampe, Olón and Montañita.

- **Puerto López and the Machalilla National Park:** starting point to see humpback whales, approximately between June and September, and to visit the Isla de la Plata, with sea birds such as blue-footed boobies.
- **Montañita:** surf town with a lot of nightlife.
- **Quieter beaches** in neighboring towns, for those looking for rest.

On the Ecuadorian coast, the warmest and rainiest season usually runs from December to May; The rest of the year the sky is cloudier and the water is somewhat cooler, although it is whale season.

## Northern Peru: sun almost all year round

On the northern Peruvian coast, around Máncora, Punta Sal, Los Órganos and Vichayito, the sun shines most of the year and the water is warmer than in the rest of the country. It is an area of ​​beach, surfing and fishing towns, with an offer that ranges from simple hostels to beachfront hotels.

A little further south, near Trujillo, the town of Huanchaco preserves an ancient tradition: fishermen still go to sea in caballitos de totora, reed boats that are represented in the ceramics of ancient cultures of the region. From there it is easy to visit Chan Chan and the temples of the Moche culture; We see it in the guide on [archaeological sites](/bitacora/sitios-arqueologicos/).

In the Chicama area, north of Trujillo, one of the longest left waves in the world breaks, famous among surfers.

## Lima and the central and southern coast of Peru

Lima overlooks the Pacific from high cliffs. In summer, approximately from December to March, the sun and heat fill the beaches of the city and the southern resorts; In winter, the sky is usually gray for weeks, with clouds and humidity, although it almost never rains heavily. The Miraflores boardwalk is ideal for walking, cycling or paragliding, and the city is one of the gastronomic capitals of the region.

Further south, the Paracas National Reserve protects a desert peninsula by the sea. From the port there are boat trips to the Ballestas Islands, with sea lions, Humboldt penguins and thousands of birds, passing by the geoglyph known as the Candelabro. Excursions depend on the state of the sea, so leave room in your itinerary.

## The coast of northern Chile

In northern Chile, the Atacama Desert reaches to the sea. Cities like Arica and Iquique have a mild climate almost all year round, with very little rain, and very busy urban beaches in summer. The dunes surrounding Iquique are a well-known spot for paragliding. Further south, the coast alternates fishing coves, national parks and deserted beaches of great beauty.

North of La Serena, the Humboldt Penguin National Reserve protects islands where penguins nest and sea lions and dolphins live; The excursions leave by boat from nearby coves, with quotas and visiting rules. It combines the coast with the desert interior, as we explain in the guide on the [Atacama Desert and the deserts] (/bitacora/desierto-de-atacama-y-desiertos/).

## Central and southern Chile

Valparaíso, with its historic center declared a world heritage site, its hills of colorful houses and its old elevators, is one of the most fascinating cities on the coast. Next to it, Viña del Mar concentrates beaches, avenues and nightlife. Further south, Pichilemu is a reference destination for big wave surfing.

In the south, the landscape becomes green and rainy. The Chiloé archipelago, with its stilt houses and wooden churches, several of which have been declared world heritage sites, offers a slow pace and a very own island culture. The sea here is cold even in the middle of summer.

## Surfing in the Pacific

The South American Pacific coast is one of the great surf destinations in the world, with consistent waves for much of the year.

- **For beginners:** look for schools on beaches with gentle waves and sandy bottoms; In the north of Peru and the Ecuadorian coast there are many options.
- **Neoprene suit:** essential in Chile and in central and southern Peru, due to the water temperature.
- **Respect priorities:** On each wave there are turn rules that local surfers expect you to know.
- **Rock bottoms and currents:** Always ask the locals before entering.

## Marine fauna

The richness of the Humboldt Sea and the warm waters of the north translates into extraordinary fauna: humpback whales in season, dolphins, sea lions, Humboldt penguins, pelicans, boobies and a multitude of seabirds. Choose operators who respect distances and do not chase the animals; We explain it in the guide on [whale and turtle watching](/bitacora/avistamiento-de-ballenas-y-tortugas/).

## Bathe safely

- **Waves and currents:** Many Pacific beaches have strong waves and back currents. Swim only where there are lifeguards and respect the flags, as we explain in the guide on [rip currents and flags](/bitacora/corrientes-de-resaca-y-banderas/).
- **Cold water:** Cold is tiring and reduces the ability to react. Enter little by little and do not go far from the shore.
- **Tsunamis:** the coast of Chile and Peru is a seismic zone. Pay attention to evacuation route signs and, if you feel a strong and prolonged earthquake near the sea, go immediately to higher ground, without waiting for an official alert.
- **Sun:** ultraviolet radiation is intense even if there are clouds or fog. Wear sunscreen, a hat, and glasses.

## Flavors of the coast

Pacific cuisine revolves around the sea: ceviches and tiraditos in Peru, encocados with coconut milk on the coast of Ecuador and Colombia, seafood and caldillos in Chile. He eats in busy and cool places, especially raw fish, and prefers ceviche at midday, as is customary in many areas of Peru. More ideas in the guide on [regional dishes by country](/bitacora/platos-regionales-por-pais/).

## Frequent errors

- **Expect warm water** in Chile or central Peru.
- **Plan beach in Lima** in the middle of winter.
- **Bathing on beaches with strong waves** without a lifeguard.
- **Ignore tsunami evacuation routes**.
- **Arrive at Chocó without a raincoat** or quick-drying clothing.
- **Schedule boat excursions** without margin due to the state of the sea.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is the warmest water in the South American Pacific?

On the Colombian coast, in Ecuador and in the extreme north of Peru. To the south of these areas, the Humboldt Current greatly cools the sea.

### When is it better to go to the beaches of Lima?

In the southern summer, approximately from December to March, when there is sun and heat. In winter, the sky is usually gray and the sea is very cold.

### Can you see whales in the Pacific?

Yes. Humpback whales visit the coasts of Colombia and Ecuador approximately between June and October, depending on the area, and there are sightings of other species further south.

### Is it a good area to learn surfing?

Yes. Northern Peru and the Ecuadorian coast have many schools and waves suitable for beginners, with warmer water.

## In summary

The South American Pacific coast runs from the Chocó rainforest to the deserts of Peru and Chile, passing through fishing villages, cliffs and historic cities. The Humboldt Current cools the sea and fills it with life, while El Niño can alter the climate some years. Choose the area according to the season: northern Peru and Ecuador for warmer water, Lima in summer, northern Chile almost all year round and Chocó in whale season. Swim only with lifeguards, respect flags and evacuation routes, and enjoy surfing, wildlife, and marine cuisine with respect for the ocean.
