Few landscapes are as impressive as a desert. In Latin America there are some of the most extraordinary on the planet: the Atacama, in northern Chile, one of the driest places on Earth; the Uyuni salt flat, in Bolivia, a white plain that seems to have no end; the dunes of Ica and the Nazca lines in Peru; the sculpted rock valleys of Ischigualasto and Talampaya in Argentina; or the deserts of northern Mexico, with their giant cacti and extinct volcanoes.

They are places of silence, impossible colors and night skies full of stars, but also extreme conditions: scorching sun during the day, subzero cold at night, altitude, long distances without services and very little water. With good preparation, they can be traveled with peace of mind. In this guide you will see the main deserts in the region, when to visit them, how to protect yourself from the sun, cold and altitude, how to move safely and how to take care of these fragile ecosystems.

## The deserts of the region

## Atacama: the driest desert

The Atacama Desert is considered one of the driest non-polar places in the world; In some areas, rainfall is so scarce that years can pass without significant rainfall. The town of San Pedro de Atacama, at about 2400 meters above sea level, is the base for most excursions.

- **Valley of the Moon:** formations of salt, clay and sand that turn reddish tones at sunset.
- **Tatio Geysers:** a geothermal field at more than four thousand meters, which is visited at dawn, when the steam columns are best seen and the temperature can be several degrees below zero.
- **Altiplanic lagoons:** high altitude lagoons surrounded by volcanoes, at more than four thousand meters.
- **Salar de Atacama:** salty lagoons where flamingos feed.

Several of these places are managed by indigenous Lickanantay communities, who charge their own entrance fee and establish visiting rules. Respect them: they are part of a territory with a thousand-year history.

Acclimatize before going up to geysers or high altitude lagoons, starting with lower excursions, as we explain in the guide on [altitude sickness] (/bitacora/mal-de-altura-soroche/).

## The Uyuni salt flat and southern Bolivia

The Uyuni salt flat, at about 3,650 meters above sea level, is the largest salt desert in the world. Its appearance changes a lot depending on the time:

- **Rainy season** approximately from January to March: a thin layer of water turns the salt flat into a mirror that reflects the sky. Some areas may be inaccessible due to water.
- **Dry season:** The salt flat is a huge white, cracked surface that allows you to explore it thoroughly, visit islands with giant cacti and play with perspective in photos.

Many travelers take multi-day 4x4 excursions through southern Lípez, with colorful lagoons, flamingos, geysers and volcanic landscapes, often to the border with Chile to continue towards San Pedro de Atacama. The accommodations along the route are simple and very cold at night.

**Choose the operator well:** ask about the condition of the vehicles, the seat belts, the experience of the drivers, the rest between shifts and if they carry oxygen and a first aid kit. Read recent reviews and be wary of offers that are too cheap. If you cross into Chile, review the guide on [crossing borders by land](/bitacora/cruzar-fronteras-por-tierra/).

## Deserts of Peru: Ica, Huacachina and Nazca

The Peruvian coast is largely desert. Near the city of Ica, the Huacachina oasis, a small lagoon surrounded by enormous dunes, is famous for sandboarding and sand dune vehicle rides. Choose operators who wear seat belts and drive carefully: rollovers in the dunes are not uncommon.

Further south, the Nazca lines, geoglyphs drawn on the desert floor about two thousand years ago, are best seen from the air on small plane overflights or, to a more limited extent, from roadside viewpoints. If you do the flyover, choose authorized companies and keep in mind that sharp turns can make you dizzy.

## Argentina: Ischigualasto, Talampaya and the Puna

The parks of Ischigualasto, known as Valley of the Moon, and Talampaya, in the provinces of San Juan and La Rioja, together form a world heritage site for their landscape and their fossils, which allow us to study the time when the first dinosaurs appeared. Visits are made through regulated circuits, often in a caravan of vehicles or with a guide.

In the northwest, the Puna is a high-altitude plateau with salt flats, volcanoes, and adobe towns. The Salinas Grandes, between Jujuy and Salta, are one of its best-known postcards. The routes are long and with few services, so plan your fuel carefully.

## Mexico: from Pinacate to Wirikuta

Northern Mexico is dominated by deserts of great beauty. In Sonora, the El Pinacate and Gran Desierto de Altar Biosphere Reserve combines volcanic craters and one of the largest active dune fields in North America, and is registered as a world heritage site. In the Chihuahua Desert, the Wirikuta region, near Real de Catorce, is sacred territory for the Wixárika people, who have been pilgrims there for centuries. Visit it with respect, without collecting plants or interfering in ceremonies.

In summer, temperatures in these deserts can far exceed forty degrees. Spring and autumn are the most pleasant times.

## Sand by the sea: La Guajira and the Lençóis

In the extreme north of Colombia, the La Guajira peninsula combines desert, dunes that fall into the Caribbean and the culture of the Wayuu people. The roads are dirt and sand, so most travelers go on off-road tours with local drivers.

In the northeast of Brazil, the Lençóis Maranhenses National Park is a landscape of white dunes that, although it is not a desert in the strict sense because it receives a lot of rain, looks like one. After the rainy season, lagoons of crystalline water form between the dunes; They tend to be most crowded between approximately June and September.

## Hot during the day, cold at night

In the desert, the lack of humidity means that the heat of the day is lost quickly when the sun sets. In high-altitude deserts, such as the Atacama or the Bolivian highlands, the difference between midday and early morning can exceed twenty degrees.

- **Dress in layers:** a light t-shirt for the day and a warm jacket, hat and gloves for dawn and night.
- **On sunrise excursions,** bring all the warm clothing you have.
- **In the highland shelters,** a sleeping bag for intense cold makes a big difference.

## Sun, dryness and altitude

Ultraviolet radiation in high-altitude deserts is among the highest in the world.

- **High protection sunscreen,** renewed often, and lip balm with filter.
- **Sunglasses with UV filter** and wide-brimmed hat.
- **Abundant water:** Dry air dehydrates without you noticing. Carry more than you think you need.
- **Moisturizer and nasal serum:** Dryness irritates the skin, lips and nose.

## Driving in the desert

- **Plan fuel:** stations can be hundreds of kilometers away.
- **Carry water and food in reserve** in the vehicle.
- **Do not leave the roads:** soft sand, wet salt flats and uneven terrain trap vehicles.
- **Notify your route** and do not have a phone signal.
- **Drive during the day:** Livestock, wildlife and potholes are difficult to see at night.

## Summer rains and flash floods

Although it may seem contradictory, it also rains in deserts, and when it does it can be dangerous. In the highlands, summer rains, between approximately December and March, sometimes cut roads and close border crossings; In Chile they are known as highland winter. In ravines and canyons, a distant storm can cause flash flooding. Do not camp in dry riverbeds and check the status of the roads before leaving.

The rains also bring happy surprises: in years with a rainier winter than usual, the south of the Atacama Desert can be covered in flowers in the phenomenon known as the flowering desert, which usually occurs around September and October.

## Skies to look at the stars

The dry air, altitude and low light pollution make northern Chile one of the best places in the world for astronomy, and some of the great international observatories are concentrated there. In San Pedro de Atacama and the Elqui Valley there are night excursions with telescopes and explanations of the southern sky.

- **Choose nights with no moon** or little moon to see more stars.
- **Wear warmly:** desert nights are cold.
- **Let your eyes adjust** to the darkness for a few minutes, without looking at the phone screen.

## Take care of the desert

Deserts seem empty and indestructible, but they are very fragile ecosystems.

- **Do not leave the marked trails:** Vehicle tracks can remain in the desert soil for decades. In Nazca, stepping or driving outside the permitted areas can damage the lines.
- **Do not take stones, fossils or archaeological remains:** in addition to damaging heritage, it is illegal in many countries.
- **Do not write or form figures** with stones on the ground or salt flats.
- **Take all your trash with you,** because in dry climates it takes a long time to degrade.
- **Respect sacred sites** and community norms.
- **Do not disturb the fauna:** vicuñas, flamingos, foxes and vizcachas live at the limit of their resources.

If you are interested in flamingos and other birds of high altitude lagoons, the guide on [bird watching](/bitacora/observacion-de-aves/) will give you ideas.

## What to bring

- Layered clothing, including a warm jacket, hat and gloves.
- High sunscreen, lip balm, sunglasses and hat.
- Large reusable bottle and rehydration pills or salts.
- Moisturizing cream and nasal serum.
- Headlamp.
- External battery: In shelters, electricity may be limited.
- Warm sleeping bag for multi-day excursions in the highlands.
- Cash for community tickets and purchases in small towns.

The guide on [luggage for various climates](/bitacora/equipaje-para-climas-variados/) helps you combine these items with the rest of your trip.

## Frequent errors

- **Climb more than four thousand meters** the first day.
- **Wear only summer clothes** to a high-altitude desert.
- **Drink little water** because you do not feel thirsty.
- **Choose the cheapest operator** to tour Uyuni or the dunes.
- **Go off the roads** with the vehicle.
- **Camping in the bed** of a dry river.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best time to visit the Atacama?

You can visit all year round. The southern spring and autumn offer more pleasant temperatures; In summer, the rains from the highlands can close some high altitude roads, and in winter the nights are very cold.

### When is the mirror effect seen in Uyuni?

In the rainy season, approximately from January to March, when a thin layer of water covers part of the salt flat. It depends on each year, so ask the operators before traveling.

### Is sandboarding dangerous in Huacachina?

Like any adventure activity, it has risks. Start on soft dunes, follow the guides' instructions and choose sand dune vehicles with seat belts and cautious drivers.

### Can you visit the desert with children?

Yes, with precautions: avoid extreme altitude with small children without consulting the pediatrician, protect them from the sun and cold, and carry plenty of water.

## In summary

The deserts of Latin America, from the Atacama and Uyuni to Nazca, Ischigualasto, Sonora, La Guajira or the Lençóis, offer some of the most impressive landscapes on the continent. Prepare for the heat of the day and the cold of the night with layered clothing, protect yourself from the intense sun, and drink water even if you are not thirsty. Acclimatize before climbing to high altitude, choose responsible operators, plan fuel, and stay off the trails. Enjoy its starry skies and take care of these fragile ecosystems: don't take anything, leave nothing and respect the communities that inhabit them.
