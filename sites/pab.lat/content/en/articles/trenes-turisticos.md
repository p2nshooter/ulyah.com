In much of Latin America, the passenger train stopped being the main means of transportation decades ago, replaced by buses and airplanes. But in many countries, railway routes survive, or have been reborn, that are in themselves a destination: tracks that climb the Andes mountain range, cross canyons deeper than the Colorado, descend through the Atlantic jungle to the sea or cross the Yucatan peninsula. Traveling in them is a slow and privileged way of looking at the landscape.

These trains are also pieces of history. Many were built in the late 19th or early 20th centuries with astonishing feats of engineering: switchbacks, viaducts, tunnels, and seemingly impossible grades. In this guide you will see some of the best-known routes in the region, how to book them, what to keep in mind on high-altitude trains, what to bring and how to make the most of the trip.

## Some notable trains

| Train | Country | Tour | Keep in mind |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trains to Machu Picchu | Peru | from Cusco or Ollantaytambo to Machu Picchu Pueblo | various companies and classes; limited luggage |
| Train from Cusco to Puno | Peru | from the Cusco valley to Lake Titicaca through the high plateau | full day at high altitude |
| Andean Central Railway | Peru | from Lima to Huancayo, crossing the Andes | one of the highest routes in the world; occasional tourist outings |
| Chepe | Mexico | from Chihuahua to Los Mochis through the Copper Canyon | stops at canyon viewpoints |
| Mayan Train | Mexico | circuit through the Yucatan Peninsula and Chiapas | connects cities, beaches and archaeological zones |
| Train to the Clouds | Argentina | from the area of San Antonio de los Cobres, in Salta, to the La Polvorilla viaduct | more than four thousand meters of altitude; combines bus and train sections |
| End of the World Train | Argentina | near Ushuaia, towards the Tierra del Fuego National Park | narrow gauge train with prison history |
| La Trochita | Argentina | sections from Esquel and El Maitén, in Patagonia | steam locomotives and very narrow gauge |
| Serra Verde Express | Brazil | from Curitiba to Morretes through the Serra do Mar | Atlantic jungle, bridges and tunnels |
| Maria Fumaça | Brazil | from São João del-Rei to Tiradentes, in Minas Gerais | steam train between two colonial cities |
| Train from Oruro to Uyuni | Bolivia | from the altiplano to the Uyuni salt flat | regular transport with few weekly frequencies |

The schedules, frequencies, rates and even the existence of some services change over time. Several trains have been suspended and reopened in recent years due to construction, natural disasters or management changes. Always confirm information on official channels before planning.

## Why choose the train

- **The landscape as the protagonist:** without worrying about driving, you can look out the window for hours.
- **Access to special places:** some sections run through canyons, ravines or jungles without parallel roads.
- **Comfort:** more room to move than on a bus, and on many trains, dining cars or on-board service.
- **Living history:** old stations, restored locomotives and stories from the workers who built the tracks.

## Trains to Machu Picchu

The most common way to get to Machu Picchu is by train to Machu Picchu Pueblo, also known as Aguas Calientes, and from there by bus or on foot to the citadel.

- **Departure points:** The trains leave from the Cusco area or Ollantaytambo, in the Sacred Valley. The departure station from Cusco may change depending on the season, so check your ticket carefully.
- **Classes:** there are basic, intermediate and luxury services, with differences in panoramic windows, food and price.
- **Baggage:** trains usually limit luggage to a small carry-on piece, about five kilos. Leave the large suitcase at your accommodation in Cusco or the Sacred Valley.
- **Reservation:** in high season, tickets and entrances to Machu Picchu sell out. Coordinate the train schedules with the time slot of your entry.

The guide to [archaeological sites](/bitacora/sitios-arqueologicos/) explains how to plan your visit to the citadel.

## High altitude trains

Some of the most spectacular routes in the region exceed four thousand meters above sea level. The Central Andean Railway, between Lima and Huancayo, reaches almost 4,800 meters at its highest point, and the Train to the Clouds reaches the La Polvorilla viaduct, at more than 4,200 meters.

- **Acclimatize before:** Spending a few days at altitude before the trip reduces the risk of discomfort. We explain it in the guide on [altitude sickness](/bitacora/mal-de-altura-soroche/).
- **Hydrate and eat lightly** during the journey.
- **Bring a coat:** It is cold in the highlands, especially at dawn and outside the car.
- **Consult your doctor** if you have heart or respiratory problems before high altitude travel.

## El Chepe and the Copper Canyon

The Chihuahua to the Pacific Railway, known as Chepe, runs through some of the most impressive landscapes in Mexico: the Copper Canyon, a system of canyons in the Tarahumara mountain range, territory of the Rarámuri people. The route goes up from the coast of Sinaloa to the mountains of Chihuahua with bridges and tunnels, and makes stops at viewpoints such as Divisadero, from where you look out into the abyss.

Many travelers divide the tour into several days, stopping in towns like Creel or in hotels next to the canyons to walk, visit communities or look out at the viewpoints. Respect the Rarámuri communities, ask permission before photographing and buy crafts directly from those who make them.

## The Mayan Train

Inaugurated in stages from the end of 2023, the Mayan Train runs through the Yucatan Peninsula and part of Chiapas, linking destinations such as Cancún, the coast of Quintana Roo, Mérida, Campeche and Palenque. It is an option to combine beaches, colonial cities, cenotes and archaeological zones without driving. Its construction generated debates about the environmental impact on the jungle and the cave and cenote systems. As with any new service, check the current schedules and the location of each station with respect to the city center, because some are at a certain distance.

## Steam trains and heritage

In several countries, associations and companies keep historical trains in operation that are true museums in motion.

- **La Trochita,** in Argentine Patagonia, runs on a track just seventy-five centimeters wide, with steam locomotives from the early twentieth century.
- **The End of the World Train,** near Ushuaia, remembers the history of the city's old prison, whose inmates worked cutting down the forests.
- **The Maria Fumaça,** in Minas Gerais, connects São João del-Rei with Tiradentes, two cities with very well preserved colonial architecture.

They are short tours, ideal for families and history lovers, which tend to be more frequent in high season and on weekends.

## Trains that are also transportation

Some trains are not tourist tours, but rather regular services used by local people that also serve travelers. In Bolivia, for example, the train that connects Oruro with Uyuni allows you to reach the salt flats by crossing the high plateau. In Brazil, the passenger train between Vitória and the Belo Horizonte region is one of the few long-distance services still operating in the country. These trains are usually cheap and authentic, but with fewer frequencies and fewer amenities than tourist trains. ## How to reserve

1. **Purchase through the official channels** of each railway company or from authorized agencies.
2. **Book in advance** during high season and holidays, especially on trains with few departures.
3. **Check the exact station** of departure and arrival, and how to get there.
4. **Bring the document** with which you bought the ticket: on many trains they ask for it when boarding.
5. **Read the change and cancellation conditions**, which are usually strict.
6. **Calculate the connections with a margin** if after the train you have a bus, a flight or a timed ticket.

## Choose a seat and enjoy the trip

- **Ask which side the landscapes are best seen** on your tour; staff and frequent travelers usually know this.
- **Choose window** if the train assigns seats.
- **Take advantage of the stops** at viewpoints to stretch your legs and photograph without reflections.
- **Listen to the explanations** of the guides on board, when they exist: they tell stories that you will not find in any brochure.

## What to bring

- Water and something to eat, if the train does not have service on board or if it is allowed.
- Layers of clothing: Cars may be cold due to air conditioning or altitude.
- Sunscreen and sunglasses for outdoor stops.
- External battery and memory card with space for photos.
- Medication for motion sickness, if you need it on routes with many curves.
- Cash for small purchases at stations and towns.

## Photography from the train

The windows are reflective and sometimes dirty, so hold the lens close to the glass, wear dark clothing to reduce reflections, and take advantage of the curved sections to photograph the locomotive and carriages. Never look through open doors or windows or get off in unauthorized places for a photo.

## Frequent errors

- **Do not book in advance** trains with few departures.
- **Carrying too much luggage** on trains with strict limits.
- **Do not check the departure station,** which may change depending on the season.
- **Plan tight links** after the train.
- **Underestimate the altitude** on Andean trains.
- **Assume that a train is running** without confirming that it is not suspended.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are there long distance trains like in Europe?

Very few. The region's passenger rail network was greatly reduced in the twentieth century, and today long trips are made primarily by bus or plane. The trains that operate are, for the most part, tourist or regional.

### Is it worth paying for a higher class?

It depends on the route. On scenic trains, larger windows and onboard service can make a difference; On short journeys, the basic class is usually sufficient.

### Can you travel with children?

Yes, and it is usually an experience that they love. On high-speed trains, consult your pediatrician first and bring a coat and something to entertain them.

### What train do you recommend for a first time?

If you travel through Peru, the train to Machu Picchu is almost inevitable. In Mexico, the Chepe offers extraordinary landscapes, and in Brazil, the route from Curitiba to Morretes is short, accessible and very beautiful.

## In summary

The tourist trains of Latin America travel through mountain ranges, canyons, jungles and plateaus with engineering works that are part of the history of each country. Choose the route according to your interests, confirm that the service is working, book through official channels and in advance, check the departure station and the luggage limit and calculate the connections with margin. On high-speed trains, acclimatize beforehand and bring a coat. And above all, put your phone away from time to time: the best memory is the landscape that passes through the window.
