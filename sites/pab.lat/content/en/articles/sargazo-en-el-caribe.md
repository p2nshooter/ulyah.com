Whoever travels to the Caribbean expects white sand and turquoise water. But for more than a decade, many beaches in the region receive enormous quantities of sargassum in certain seasons: brown algae that float in the sea, reach the shore in thick strips and, when decomposed, change the color of the water and give off a strong smell. The phenomenon affects the Mexican Caribbean, several islands in the Antilles, the coast of Central America and other places, and has become another factor to take into account when planning a beach vacation.

Sargassum is not a reason to cancel a trip, but it is a reason to plan it with information. In this guide you will see what it is, why it arrives, in what months there is usually more, which areas receive the least, how to consult the monitoring maps, what effects it has on health and what to do if your beach wakes up covered in algae.

## What is sargassum

Sargassum is a type of brown macroalgae that floats freely in the ocean thanks to small vesicles filled with gas. In the high seas it forms large spots that serve as shelter and food for fish, young turtles and many other organisms. The problem appears when these stains reach the coasts in enormous quantities.

Since around 2011, scientists have observed the formation of a large sargassum belt in the tropical Atlantic, stretching from the coasts of West Africa to the Caribbean. Its growth has been linked to factors such as nutrient input to the ocean and changes in temperature and ocean currents, and continues to be the subject of research.

## When does it arrive

The arrival of sargassum varies greatly from year to year, but in the Mexican Caribbean and other areas it usually follows a seasonal pattern:

| Era | The usual |
|---|---|
| winter | less sargassum on many beaches |
| Spring | Arrivals begin in several areas |
| summer | It is usually the time of greatest quantity |
| Autumn | arrivals tend to decrease |

There are years with very little sargassum and years with massive arrivals. Therefore, rather than relying on the calendar, it is advisable to consult the forecasts and monitoring maps close to the date of the trip.

## Which areas receive more and which less?

Beaches facing east and the open sea, where the wind and currents push the sargassum, are usually the most affected. On the other hand, they usually receive less:

- **Coasts protected** by islands or facing west, such as the western side of some islands.
- **The beaches on the Gulf of Mexico side** of the Yucatan Peninsula.
- **Certain northern beaches** of some islands and peninsulas, protected from the dominant currents.

The situation changes each season, so check the updated information for your specific destination.

## How to check the status of the beaches

- **Local monitoring networks:** In Quintana Roo, for example, there is a monitoring network that publishes maps with a colored traffic light on the amount of sargassum on each beach.
- **Local authorities and coastal protection services,** reporting on cleanups and barriers.
- **Satellite forecasts:** Universities and scientific institutions publish monthly estimates of the amount of sargassum in the Caribbean.
- **Your accommodation:** ask directly how the beach is in the previous days and what measures they take.

## Is it dangerous for health?

Fresh sargassum in water is not toxic to the touch. The problem appears when it accumulates on the shore and **decomposes**: it releases gases such as hydrogen sulfide, with a rotten egg smell, which in high concentrations can irritate the eyes and respiratory tract.

- **People with asthma or other respiratory illnesses,** pregnant women, and young children should avoid prolonged exposure to large decaying accumulations.
- **Some people feel itchy skin** when bathing in sargassum, due to the small organisms that live in it.
- **Swimming between large spots** is uncomfortable and can hide objects or fauna.

If you have symptoms, stay away from the area and seek medical attention if they do not improve.

## If your beach wakes up with sargassum

All is not lost. Some alternatives:

- **Change beaches:** Often, a few kilometers away there are less affected beaches, especially on protected coasts.
- **Visit cenotes, lagoons and rivers:** in the Yucatan Peninsula, cenotes offer fresh, crystal-clear water, and lagoons like Bacalar are a great alternative.
- **Explore ruins and towns:** the region has archaeological sites, colonial towns and gastronomy for several days. We see it in the guide on [archaeological sites](/bitacora/sitios-arqueologicos/).
- **Choose water activities away from shore:** some snorkeling excursions at remote reefs may not be affected. More information in the guide on [responsible snorkeling and reefs](/bitacora/snorkel-y-arrecifes-responsable/).

## How to read a sargassum map

Monitoring maps often use a color code similar to that of a traffic light. Although each network has its own criteria, the logic is similar: green or blue tones indicate little or no presence of sargassum, yellows and oranges indicate moderate or abundant quantities, and reds or purples signal massive arrivals. Always look at the update date, because the situation of a beach can change in a few days with the wind and currents, and compare several sources when possible.

## Photos and social networks: be careful with expectations

Many images of perfect beaches that circulate on the Internet were taken in seasons without sargassum or on especially clean days. To get a realistic idea, look for recent dated photos, check monitoring maps, and ask your accommodation. This way you will avoid getting a surprise when you arrive.

## Choose accommodation

- **Ask about beach cleanliness:** many hotels have teams that remove sargassum every morning.
- **Ask if there are barriers** in the sea that divert sargassum before reaching the shore.
- **Prefers flexible reservations** in the peak season.
- **Assess the pool and other facilities** in case the beach is not in good condition.

## Also an environmental phenomenon

Sargassum in large quantities affects coastal ecosystems: when it decomposes on the shore, it reduces the oxygen in the nearby water, can damage seagrass beds and reefs, and makes it difficult for turtles to access nesting beaches. Authorities and scientists are working on its monitoring, collection and possible uses, such as the manufacture of construction materials or fertilizers. As a visitor, please respect the collection areas and do not walk over the barriers or cleaning equipment.

## Plan the trip with sargassum in mind

1. **Choose the season:** If the beach is the top priority, the winter months are usually safer in many areas.
2. **Check monitoring maps** and forecasts before booking and before leaving.
3. **Consider less exposed destinations** or with nearby alternatives.
4. **Book flexibly** and with insurance that covers changes. We see it in the guide on [travel insurance](/bitacora/seguro-de-viaje/).
5. **Combine the beach** with other experiences so that the trip does not depend only on the state of the sea.

The season of greatest sargassum coincides in part with the hurricane season, as we explain in the guide on the [hurricane season in the Caribbean](/bitacora/temporada-de-huracanes-caribe/).

## Frequent errors

- **Book without consulting** the recent state of the beaches.
- **To think that the entire Caribbean** is equally affected.
- **Bathing between large stains** of decomposing sargassum.
- **Staying on the beach affected** with asthma or respiratory problems.
- **Walking over barriers** or interfering with cleaning crews.
- **Plan a trip** that depends solely on the beach during the peak sargassum season.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a way to know if there will be sargassum on my vacation?

Not for sure, but satellite forecasts and local maps give a good idea a few weeks in advance. Review the information shortly before traveling.

### Does sargassum disappear quickly from the beach?

It depends on the quantity that arrives and the cleanliness. On beaches with daily cleaning teams, the shore may be in good condition in the morning even if sargassum continues to arrive.

### Does sargassum affect snorkeling and diving?

It can reduce visibility near the coast and make entrances from the beach uncomfortable. Boat trips to remote reefs are usually less affected, although it depends on the day.

### Can I bathe if there is a little sargassum?

With a small amount and fresh, many people bathe without problems. Avoid large accumulations of decay and salt the water if you notice itching.

## In summary

Sargassum is a floating macroalgae that arrives in large quantities on many Caribbean beaches, especially from spring to summer, with a lot of variation between years. Coasts exposed to the open sea tend to be the most affected, and protected or Gulf-side coasts less so. Check monitoring maps and forecasts, ask your accommodation about cleanliness and barriers, avoid large decaying accumulations if you have respiratory problems and have alternatives such as cenotes, lagoons and archaeological sites.
