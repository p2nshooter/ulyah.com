Munich is a big city that often feels like a small town. The old town is compact, the Isar flows through the middle of the city, and in just a few minutes you can be in the city's largest park or in a shady beer garden from Marienplatz. There are also world-class museums, magnificent castles and, when the weather is clear, the Alps on the horizon. This guide will help you discover the city in two to three days without rushing from sight to sight.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The old town between the main train station, Marienplatz, Odeonsplatz and Isartor is easy to explore on foot.
> - The carillon in the town hall tower at Marienplatz plays daily at 11 a.m. and 12 p.m., and also at 5 p.m. in the warm season.
> - In the English Garden, people surf a standing wave in the Eisbach, just a few steps from the city center.
> - In traditional beer gardens you are allowed to bring your own food. You buy drinks on site.
> - Hotel prices rise sharply during the Oktoberfest in late summer and autumn. Plan early or travel at a different time.

## The old town: from Marienplatz to Odeonsplatz

**Marienplatz** is the heart of the city. Here is the neo-Gothic **New Town Hall** with the famous glockenspiel, where colorful figures turn to music. Plan a few minutes before the start, then the place will be full of people.

Located nearby:

- **The Frauenkirche** with its two towers and the striking domes, the landmark of Munich.
- **The Old Peter**, the oldest parish church in the city. Anyone who climbs the many steps to the tower will be rewarded with a view of the Alps in foehn weather.
- **The Viktualienmarkt** with stalls for fruit, cheese, meat, flowers and delicatessen, with a beer garden in the middle. A good place for a lunchtime snack.

North of Marienplatz the path leads to the **Residenz**, the former city palace of the Bavarian rulers. The museum rooms and the treasury show how splendidly the government was ruled here. Next to it are the **Hofgarten**, the **Odeonsplatz** with the Feldherrnhalle and the yellow **Theatinerkirche**.

## English Garden and Isar

The **English Garden** is one of the largest inner-city parks in the world and extends far north from the old town. There are meadows, streams, forests and beer gardens here.

- **The Eisbach Wave** at the southern end of the park is a standing wave that surfers ride all year round. Watching is free. Only very experienced surfers should go into the water because the current is strong and dangerous.
- **The Monopteros**, a small round temple on a hill, offers a beautiful view of the towers of the old town.
- **The Chinese Tower** is a large beer garden in the middle of the park, a meeting place for everyone on summer days.

The **Isar** flows through the entire city. In the south, around the Flaucher, there are gravel banks where Munich residents grill and sunbathe on warm days. The Isar is a mountain river: the water is cold and the current is often stronger than it looks. If you choose to swim, stay in shallow, quiet areas and pay attention to cues. More about this in the guide [Swimming in lakes and rivers](/reisen/baden-in-seen-und-fluessen/).

## Museums

Munich has one of the densest museum landscapes in Germany. A selection:

- **German Museum** on the Museum Island in the Isar, one of the largest technology and science museums in the world. You need at least half a day to visit.
- **Art area** north of the old town with the Alte Pinakothek, the Pinakothek der Moderne, the Lenbachhaus, the Glyptothek and other houses. Some museums are open on Sundays for a symbolic entry fee. A house may be closed for renovation, check this beforehand.
- **BMW World and Museum** at the Olympic Park for technology and car enthusiasts.
- **NS Documentation Center** at Königsplatz, which explains Munich's role during the National Socialist era.

## Olympic Park and Nymphenburg Palace

The **Olympic Park** was built for the 1972 Games. The curved tent roof over the stadium and halls is still an architectural landmark today. From the Olympic Tower you can see over the entire city, and on a clear day all the way to the mountains.

**Nymphenburg Palace** in the west was the summer residence of the Wittelsbach family. The castle, the park castles and the extensive park with canals and avenues make a good half-day trip. You can easily reach the castle from the center by tram.

## Beer gardens: the most important rules

The beer garden in Munich is more than just a bar, it is a piece of everyday culture. A few rules will help you:

- **Your own food is allowed.** In traditional beer gardens you are allowed to bring your snack with you. This can often be seen on tables without tablecloths. Serviced areas with tablecloths are reserved for guests who order there.
- **You buy drinks on site,** mostly self-service at the bar. Deposits are charged on many jugs and glasses.
- **Sharing is normal.** If there is room at a long table, ask briefly and sit down.
- **Non-alcoholic drinks** such as Spezi or apple spritzer are available everywhere. A shandy is beer with lemonade.

## Eating in Munich

- **Weißwurst** with sweet mustard and pretzels is traditionally eaten in the morning.
- **Leberkäs** in a roll is a quick snack for in between meals.
- **Roast pork** with dumplings and sauce is a Sunday classic.
- **Obatzda** is a spicy cheese spread, perfect with pretzels in the beer garden.
- **Steckerlfisch** is grilled over an open fire in summer.

You can now find vegetarian and vegan dishes in almost every pub, and there is also a large international cuisine, especially in Maxvorstadt, the Glockenbachviertel and the Westend.

## Oktoberfest and other major events

The **Oktoberfest** on Theresienwiese begins in September and ends at the beginning of October. During this time, hotels are often fully booked months in advance and prices rise significantly. Large trade fairs also drive up prices. If you want to experience Munich quietly, avoid these dates or book very early.

Other highlights of the year include the beer garden season in summer, festivals in the Olympic Park and the Christmas markets in December. Tips on this can be found in the guide [Visit Christmas markets in Germany] (/reisen/weihnachtsmaerkte/).

## Out and about in the city

Munich has a dense network of subway, S-Bahn, tram and bus. For a city trip, a day ticket or the Deutschlandticket, which is valid in the Munich transport network, is usually sufficient.

- **Validate paper tickets.** You must stamp strip cards and some single tickets at the machines on the platform or on the bus before the journey.
- **From the airport** S-Bahn trains take you to the city center in about 40 minutes.
- **Munich is easy to explore by bike**. There are many cycle paths and rental bike offers.

Driving into the city center is rarely worth it. The old town is partly a pedestrian zone and parking garages are expensive. More about traveling by train in the guide [On vacation by train](/reisen/mit-der-bahn-in-den-urlaub/).

## Where to stay? The quarters in comparison

The location of your accommodation determines how relaxing the trip will be. A good connection to the subway or S-Bahn is often more important than proximity to Marienplatz.

| Quarter | Good for | To be noted |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Old town | short distances on foot | expensive, sometimes quiet in the evenings |
| Around the main station | Arrival by train, many hotels | busy, not beautiful everywhere |
| Maxvorstadt | Museums, cafes, university | popular, book early |
| Glockenbachviertel and Isarvorstadt | Going out, Isar nearby | lively on weekends |
| Schwabing | English garden, quieter streets | a little further from the old town |
| Haidhausen | Charm, good location east of the Isar | fewer hotels, more guesthouses |

Families often live more relaxedly in a holiday apartment on the edge of a quiet neighborhood with a park nearby. The guide compares which type of accommodation suits which trip [hotel, guesthouse, holiday apartment or farm?] (/reisen/hotel-pension-ferienwohnung/).

## A plan for two to three days

**Day 1: Old Town and English Garden**

- Morning: Marienplatz with carillon, Frauenkirche, Alter Peter.
- Lunch: Snack at the Viktualienmarkt.
- Afternoon: Residenz or Hofgarten, then to the Eisbachwelle and through the English Garden to the Monopteros.
- Evening: Beer garden at the Chinese Tower or inn in the old town.

**Day 2: Museums and Nymphenburg**

- Morning: German Museum or art area.
- Afternoon: Nymphenburg Palace with park.
- Evening: Glockenbachviertel or Maxvorstadt.

**Day 3: Excursion**

- A lake in the surrounding area, such as Starnberger See, Ammersee or Tegernsee. More in the guide [Bavarian Lakes](/reisen/bayerische-seen/).
- The **Dachau Concentration Camp Memorial** north of the city, a place of remembrance that you can reach by S-Bahn and bus. Plan enough time and rest.
- Neuschwanstein Castle near Füssen. The journey takes around two hours each way by train and bus. You should reserve tickets for the castle in advance.

How you can generally plan a city weekend can be found in the guide [plan a city weekend] (/reisen/staedtewochenende-planen/).

## Travel time

- **May to September** is beer garden time, the city lives outside.
- **End of September and beginning of October** is Oktoberfest time, crowded and expensive.
- **Autumn** brings clear foehn days with views of the Alps and pleasant temperatures.
- **December** is Christmas market time.
- **January to March** is quiet, ideal for museums and cheaper hotels.

More about this in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Arrive spontaneously at the Oktoberfest.** Accommodation is then scarce and expensive.
2. **Swim in the Eisbach.** The current is dangerous.
3. **Do not validate the strip card.** It will be expensive if there is an inspection.
4. **Unpack your own snack in the served beer garden area.** That's what the tables without tablecloths are for.
5. **Too many museums in one day.** Better to see one in peace than three in a hurry.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do you need for Munich?

Two days are enough for the old town, the English Garden and a large museum. With three days there is time for Nymphenburg and a trip to the lakes or the mountains.

### Is Munich expensive?

Accommodation is among the most expensive in Germany, especially at Oktoberfest and during large trade fairs. Many experiences cost little: parks, walks along the Isar, churches, the Eisbach wave and a snack in the beer garden. Tips in the guide [Plan your vacation budget honestly] (/reisen/urlaubsbudget-planen/).

### Is Munich suitable for children?

Yes. The German Museum with its hands-on areas, the Hellabrunn Zoo, the Olympic Park and the beer gardens with playgrounds make the city family-friendly. Further tips in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/). You can find more cities under [City Trips](/themen/staedtereisen/).
