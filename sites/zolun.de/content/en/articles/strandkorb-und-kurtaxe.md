You come to the beach, the sun is shining, the sea is sparkling, and there is a small house at the entrance to the beach where someone asks for your spa card. Further back there are hundreds of beach chairs lined up, some with locks, some open. One sign shows a dog, another a crossed-out fire. For many people traveling to the German coast for the first time, the system of tourist taxes, beach chair rentals and beach sections initially seems confusing. This guide explains how a day at the beach on the North and Baltic Seas works, what it costs and what rules apply.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - In many coastal towns you pay a tourist tax per night, also known as a tourist tax or guest contribution. For this you get a guest card.
> - The guest card is often your entry to the beach and brings benefits such as discounts or free bus rides. The services differ from place to place.
> - In some places, day visitors pay a beach entrance fee or a day ticket.
> - You can rent beach chairs by the hour, day or week from landlords directly on the beach or online in advance.
> - Beaches are often divided into sections for families, dogs, naturism or water sports. Pay attention to the signs at the entrances.

## What is the tourist tax?

The tourist tax is a tax that municipalities with the status of a health resort, seaside resort or resort are allowed to charge their overnight guests. Depending on the location, it is called tourist tax, tourist contribution, guest contribution or tourism tax. The communities use the money to finance tourist facilities: beach cleaning, lifeguards, toilets and showers on the beach, spa parks, promenades, events and often parts of local transport.

Each municipality determines the amount itself. It is usually calculated per person per night and is often higher in the high season than in the low season. Children often receive discounts or exemptions, as do people with severe disabilities. You can find the exact rates on the resort's website or in your booking confirmation.

### How to pay the tourist tax

Your accommodation will usually take care of this. Hotels, guesthouses, holiday apartments and campsites register you at the location, collect the tourist tax and give you the guest card. Sometimes it is included in the room rate, sometimes it is charged extra. Ask when booking so there are no surprises when paying. How to pay attention to all additional costs when booking a holiday apartment can be found in the guide [Book a holiday apartment without unpleasant surprises] (/reisen/ferienwohnung-buchen/).

### What the guest card brings

The guest card, often also called the spa card, is your proof that you have paid the tourist tax. Depending on the location, you get:

- **free access to the beach** on guarded or paid sections,
- **Discounts** in museums, swimming pools, on guided tours or events,
- **free or cheap bus rides** in the town or region,
- **Access to spa concerts, readings or beach events**.

Always carry your guest card with you on the beach. At some beach entrances there are controls and without a card a refund may be required.

## Beach entry for day guests

If you don't stay overnight in town but just go to the beach for a day, you can pay a beach entrance fee in many seaside resorts or buy a day spa card. This is particularly common on popular beaches in Schleswig-Holstein and on some islands. But there are also many beaches that are freely accessible, especially on the Baltic Sea in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and in less touristy sections. You can find the rules on the town's website or at the machines and houses at the entrance to the beach.

## The beach chair: a German original

The beach chair is a German invention. The Rostock basket maker Wilhelm Bartelmann built it in 1882 for a customer who, despite rheumatism, wanted to sit on the beach, protected from wind and sun. It quickly became an integral part of the beach scene on the Baltic Sea and later on the North Sea.

Rounder baskets with a curved roof are typical for the Baltic Sea, while more angular, angular models are typical for the North Sea. Both have an adjustable backrest, extendable footrests, small shelves and a lock that you can use to lock the basket in the evening.

### How to rent a beach chair

1. **Find a rental company.** Most beaches have beach chair rentals with a small house or cart on the beach. Many also offer online booking.
2. **Choose a period.** Hours, days or a whole week are usually possible. Weekly rentals are cheaper per day.
3. **Select your seat.** In the front row you have a clear view of the sea, further back there is more peace and quiet. Ask if the basket is near showers, toilets or the rescue tower.
4. **Pick up the key.** You will receive a key for the lock on the basket. Return it at the end or you will often be charged a fee.

Prices vary depending on location, season and location. In high season and on popular beaches, it is worth reserving the basket a few days in advance, especially for a whole week.

### Beach chair etiquette

- **Do not put in other people's baskets.** Even if a basket is empty and open, it belongs to someone.
- **Do not move the basket yourself.** Many landlords allow small rotations to take advantage of the sun or wind, but not carrying it away.
- **Lock up in the evening.** Otherwise it can happen that there is sand, moisture or rubbish in the basket in the morning.
- **Leave clean.** Garbage belongs in the bins at the beach entrance.

## Beach sections and their rules

Many beaches are divided into sections. The classification is stated on signs at the beach entrances and often on maps in the tourist information office.

| Section | What awaits you |
| --- | --- |
| Family or bathing beach | mostly guarded, without dogs, often with play equipment and toilets nearby |
| Dog beach | Dogs allowed, often on a leash on the way there |
| Nudist beach | Bathing and sunbathing without clothing is particularly common on the Baltic Sea |
| Textile beach | Swimwear desired |
| Surf or water sports beach | for kiters, surfers and sailors, swimming is often restricted |
| Natural beach | little infrastructure, often unguarded, sometimes in protected areas |

### Dogs on the beach

During the bathing season, dogs are not allowed on most beaches, except on designated dog beaches. Outside of the season, the rules are often more relaxed, and dogs are sometimes allowed on the beach in all parts of the beach. Check the rules on site, as violations can result in a fine. The guide [Holidays with Dogs in Germany](/reisen/urlaub-mit-hund/) explains what else you should know about vacationing with your four-legged friend.

### Sand castles, holes and windbreaks

For many people, building sandcastles is part of their beach vacation. However, in some places there are rules, such as the size of castles or digging deep holes. Deep holes can collapse and become dangerous for children, and people stumble into them at night. Refill holes at the end of the day. Fabric windbreaks are permitted on most beaches, sometimes only in certain areas or up to a certain height.

### Grilling, fire and glass

Fires and barbecues are prohibited on most beaches, except in specifically designated areas. This also applies to disposable grills. Glass bottles are discouraged or prohibited on many beaches because shards are dangerous. And drones are generally not allowed to fly in nature reserves and national parks.

## Beach life out of season

The bathing season with lifeguards, fully occupied rows of beach chairs and open kiosks lasts from May or June to September in most places. Before and after the beach changes. Many beach chairs are dismantled in autumn and stored over the winter, lifeguard services end and some snack bars close. But you have the space almost to yourself.

The low season is ideal for walks, kite flying, shell searching and long conversations in the wind. The tourist tax is then often lower and accommodation is cheaper. Please note, however, that without a lifeguard there is no one watching over you in the water, and that autumn and winter storms increase the surf significantly. In many places, dogs are allowed on more areas out of season, and riders are allowed on some beaches at certain times. The guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany](/reisen/beste-reisezeit/) shows when which season is suitable for your coastal vacation.

## Plan beach days cleverly

- **On the North Sea, follow the tide.** Swimming is most pleasant around high water. More about this in the guide [Understanding ebb and flow](/reisen/ebbe-und-flut/).
- **Swim in patrolled areas**, especially with children. [Swim safely in the North and Baltic Seas](/reisen/sicher-baden-am-meer/) explains what the flags mean.
- **Come early** if you're looking for a good spot without a beach chair.
- **Be aware of wind.** An offshore wind makes the beach warmer but can blow air mattresses out to sea.
- **Don't underestimate sun protection.** Wind and water make the sun seem more harmless than it is.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Forgot the guest card in the room.** Otherwise you will have to pay extra during checks.
2. **Do not take tourist tax into account.** For a family it can amount to a significant amount over a week.
3. **Look for a beach chair spontaneously in high season.** Good spots are often taken on popular beaches.
4. **Going to the family beach with your dog.** This can result in a fine. Dog beaches are usually signposted.
5. **Leave holes open.** They are a tripping hazard and can collapse.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to pay tourist tax if I only stay one night?

In places that charge a tourist tax, it usually applies from the first overnight stay. The arrival and departure days usually count together as one day. The exact rules are in the local statutes.

### Can I go to the beach without a beach chair?

Yes, of course. A towel, a beach shelter or a windbreak are sufficient. The beach chair is particularly comfortable on windy days and for longer stays.

### Where can I find out the rules for a specific beach?

At the beach entrance, in the tourist information center and on the town website. The times of lifeguards and the location of dog and nudist beaches are also listed there. You can find more information about the coast at [North Sea & Baltic Sea](/themen/nordsee-und-ostsee/).
