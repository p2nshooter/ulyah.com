Anyone who hears “North Frisian Islands” usually thinks of Sylt first: thatched roof, champagne, sports cars in the parking lot. This picture is not entirely wrong, but it only shows a small section. Even on Sylt there are quiet villages on the mudflats, and right next to them are islands that tick completely differently: the green, rural Föhr, the dune-rich Amrum with its huge sandy beach, the quiet Pellworm behind its dike and the Halligen, tiny islands without a high protective dike on which the houses stand on artificial hills. This guide will help you assess the islands based on their character so that your vacation matches what you really want.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - You can reach Sylt via the Hindenburgdamm by train, cars travel on a car train. You can reach all other islands and the Halligen by ship.
> - Föhr and Amrum are served from Dagebüll, Pellworm from Nordstrand. Cars are allowed on all three islands, but they are rarely necessary.
> - Sylt is more diverse than its reputation: lively in Westerland, quiet in places like Keitum or Morsum, with strong surf on the west coast.
> - Föhr is green and familiar, Amrum is wild with dunes and the Kniepsand, Pellworm is agricultural and quiet.
> - On the Halligen you can experience the North Sea closer than anywhere else, as a day trip or, for those brave enough to seek peace and quiet, with an overnight stay.

## An overview in advance

All islands are located in or on the Schleswig-Holstein Wadden Sea National Park, which is part of the Wadden Sea UNESCO World Heritage Site. The tides shape everyday life: On the side facing the mainland, the mudflats are exposed at low tide, and the open North Sea rolls in on the western beaches of Sylt and Amrum. We explain how to read the tide calendar in [Understanding ebb and flow](/reisen/ebbe-und-flut/).

## Sylt: bigger and quieter than its reputation

Sylt is the largest German North Sea island and stretches long and narrow from north to south. The Hindenburg Dam has connected the island to the mainland since 1927. It is built exclusively for the train: you take the train to Westerland, or you put your car on a car train in Niebüll and stay in the car during the crossing. Another option is the car ferry from the Danish island of Rømø to List in the north of the island. In high season and on weekends there may be waiting times for the car train. If you travel without a car, things are much easier because there are buses on the island and cycle paths connect the towns.

### Lively or quiet: the places

**Westerland** is the urban center with a promenade, shops, restaurants and a lot of activity, especially in summer. **wenningstedt** and **Kampen** in the north are smaller, Kampen is known for its exclusive image and the Red Cliff, a cliff made of reddish sand. **List** is the northernmost municipality in Germany, with a harbor and wide dune landscapes in the area. In the south are **Rantum** at the narrowest part of the island and **Hörnum** with its harbor and lighthouse. Things get quieter on the east side facing the mudflats: **Keitum** is considered one of the most beautiful villages on the island with old captain's houses, thatched roofs and gardens, **Morsum** is rural and known for the Morsum cliff, a geologically special steep edge.

### Surf to the west, mudflats to the east

The western beaches are long, sandy and open to the North Sea. The surf is noticeable here, strong on some days, and dangerous currents can develop. Only swim in guarded areas and pay attention to the flags. More about this can be found in [Swim safely on the North and Baltic Seas](/reisen/sicher-baden-am-meer/). On the east side there are mudflats, salt marshes and quiet paths along the dike. This site is suitable for walks, bike tours and guided mudflat hikes.

## Föhr: the green island in the lee

Föhr is sheltered between Sylt, Amrum and the mainland. The ferry from Dagebüll takes you to Wyk, the only town on the island, with a harbor, a small old town and a beach promenade. The rest of the island consists of villages with thatched roof houses, meadows, pastures and fields. The north is flat marshland, the south is slightly higher. The large old village churches and the cemeteries with decorated gravestones tell of the island's seafaring history.

The sandy beach stretches along the south coast and is well suited for families because the waves are usually gentle due to the sheltered location. The tide also works here: in some sections the water recedes a long way when the tide is low, so plan your bathing time using the tide calendar. Föhr is flat and can be easily explored by bike. The nickname “Frisian Caribbean” is an advertising slogan, but it captures the relaxed mood on a sunny beach day quite well.

## Amrum: dunes, forest and the Kniepsand

You can reach Amrum with the same ferry connection, the ships usually go via Wyk auf Föhr to Wittdün. The island is smaller than Föhr, but the landscape is wilder: high dunes stretch across the west side, behind them is a forest, and on the east side there are meadows and mudflats. The villages of Wittdün, Süddorf, Nebel, Norddorf and Steenodde are manageable. Nebel is considered particularly picturesque with its old Frisian houses.

The centerpiece is the Kniepsand, a huge sandy area off the west coast. It is so wide that you have to walk quite a while from the edge of the dune until you reach the water. There is endless space for children and a real walk for the beach bag. So pack light and think about where you set up your beach spot. As on Sylt, the following applies: only swim in guarded areas.

If you want to experience Amrum and Föhr on one vacation, you can take the ferry between the two islands. When the tide is low, guided mudflat hikes between the islands are also offered. Such tours are only safe with a local guide, as the tidal creeks fill up quickly when water rises.

## Pellworm: the quiet island behind the dike

Pellworm is located in front of the Nordstrand peninsula, from where the ferry takes you across. The island is flat, green and protected all around by a high dike, as large parts are lower than the water level at high tide. There is no wide sandy beach like on Amrum. Bathing is possible in designated areas on the dike around high water, ask locally where this is possible. Pellworm is agricultural, the houses are spread across the entire island, and there is no large town center with a promenade.

This makes Pellworm a destination for people who really want to switch off: cycling on dike paths, wide skies, sheep, bird songs. A well-known landmark is the ruins of the old church tower. If you expect a choice of restaurants in the evening or a sandy beach for the children, Föhr or Amrum are a better choice.

## The Halligen: Life on the terp

The Halligen are small islands in the Wadden Sea that are not protected by high dikes. During storm surges, the flat land is flooded, and then only the terps, artificially raised hills on which houses, stables and sometimes a church stand, rise above the water. This event is called Landunter and occurs mainly in autumn and winter. The most famous of the ten Halligen include Hooge and Langeneß, some others are inhabited by very few people or are used for bird protection. On some terps there is still a fething, a pond in which rainwater was previously collected.

### Day trip or overnight stay

Most guests visit a Hallig as a day trip with an excursion boat from the mainland or one of the neighboring islands. You then have a few hours to take a tour, take a look at the church and have tea on the terp. That's enough for an impression.

An overnight stay shows the Hallig from a completely different side: When the last excursion boat has left, it becomes so quiet that you can hear wind, birds and water. There are few accommodations, shopping options are limited, and during storms the connection to the mainland can be lost. Therefore, plan supplies, medication and some time buffer. For people who want to experience nature without distraction, an overnight stay in Hallig is a special experience.

Narrow lorry dams lead to some Halligs, which primarily serve the residents. As a rule, you can't get to Hallig with your own car. We explain how ferries, luggage and shopping work on islands without your own car in [Taking the ferry to the island](/reisen/faehre-und-inselalltag/).

## Which island suits whom?

| What you're looking for | Good choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family with small children | Foehr | Protected beach, gentle waves, short distances |
| Family with older children | Amrum, Sylt | Dunes, Kniepsand or surf, a lot to discover |
| Cycling without mountains | Föhr, Pellworm, Sylt | Flat islands with paths along the dike and through the villages |
| Real silence | Pellworm, Halligen | No hustle and bustle, hardly any evening program |
| Choice of restaurants and shops | Sylt | Especially around Westerland |
| Arrival without ferry | Sylt | Train over the Hindenburgdamm |
| Wild dune landscape | Amrum, Sylt | High dunes and wide sandy areas |

An example from everyday life: After a stressful spring, a couple from Hamburg mainly wants to read, ride a bike and go to bed early. Sylt would be feasible if you choose a quiet village on the mudflats, but Pellworm or Amrum are closer to your wish list. A family with two children of primary school age who want to build sandcastles and swim every day feels at home on Föhr and goes on a day trip to a Hallig from there.

If none of the North Frisian Islands appeal to you, it's worth taking a look at the [East Frisian Islands](/reisen/ostfriesische-inseln/), which are predominantly car-free.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Judge Sylt only by image.** Choose the location consciously. Westerland and Keitum are worlds apart.
2. **Underestimate the knee psand.** With small children, a cooler bag and a parasol, the walk to the water will be long. Take a handcart or pack light.
3. **Swim outside of guarded areas in the west of Sylt or Amrum.** Surf and currents must be taken seriously there.
4. **Take your car with you, even though you hardly need it.** Especially on Föhr and Amrum, the bike and bus are often completely sufficient.
5. **Plan an overnight stay in Hallig without supplies and buffers.** In a storm, the ship can break down and there are hardly any shops.
6. **Check ferries and car trains shortly before the trip.** Clarify departure times and reservations directly with the providers before securing accommodation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I experience Sylt in a relaxed manner without a car?

Yes. The train takes you directly to Westerland, buses connect the towns, and a well-developed network of cycle paths leads across the island. Many accommodations are close to a bus stop. Before booking, check how far it is from your accommodation to the nearest stop.

### When can I experience Landunter on a Hallig?

Landunter mainly occurs during storm surges in autumn and winter and cannot be planned. If you want to experience this, you need an overnight stay at the right time and a little luck. Then the following applies: stay on the terp and follow the instructions of the residents.

### Which island is suitable for a first North Sea vacation?

Föhr is a good place to start for families because the beach, town and paths are manageable. If you like to have a lot of choice and want to travel without a ferry, choose Sylt and look for a quiet place. You can find more island portraits in the [Islands](/themen/inseln/) section.
