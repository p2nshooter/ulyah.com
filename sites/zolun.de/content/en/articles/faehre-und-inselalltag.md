The ferry sets off, the mainland becomes smaller, seagulls accompany the ship, and at some point the island appears on the horizon. For many, their vacation begins at this very moment. To ensure that the crossing remains relaxed, it is worth doing some preparation. Island ferries do not work like a bus that runs every half hour. Some only go at certain times, some don't take cars, and on many islands you don't need a car anyway. This guide explains how the ferries to the German islands work, how to organize your luggage and vehicle and what to expect in everyday life on the island.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Many North Sea islands are car-free. Your car will then remain in a parking space at the mainland port, which you should often reserve in advance.
> - Some ferries to East Frisian Islands run depending on the tide. The departure times change daily and depend on the flood.
> - For islands with car traffic, such as Föhr, Amrum, Borkum or Norderney, you usually have to book parking spaces on the ferry well in advance.
> - You can reach large Baltic Sea islands such as Rügen, Usedom and Fehmarn via bridges or dams, without the need for a ferry.
> - You can check in your luggage separately with many shipping companies and have it picked up at the island port or even at your accommodation.

## Which islands need a ferry?

You can't reach every German island by ship. Some are connected to the mainland via bridges or causeways, others only by ferry, some also by plane.

## Tidal and non-tidal ferries

On the East Frisian coast the water between the mainland and islands is shallow. At low tide, the channels to some islands are partially dry or too shallow for ships. That's why the ferries to Juist, Baltrum, Spiekeroog and Wangerooge only run at certain times around flood times. Because the flood level changes every day, the departure times also change daily. Departures to Langeoog are significantly less dependent on the tides, and for Juist there are also faster ships with slightly more flexible times during the season. The shipping companies know the current status.

For Norderney and Borkum, on the other hand, the ferries run largely independently of the tides, often every hour or more frequently during the season. In North Frisia, the ferries to Föhr and Amrum run according to a fixed timetable.

**What this means for you:**

1. **Check timetable before booking.** Plan your arrival according to departure times, not the other way around.
2. **Plan buffer.** If your ferry leaves early in the morning, an overnight stay on the mainland may make sense.
3. **During storms and low tides** cancellations or postponements may occur. The shipping companies provide information on their websites.
4. **Adjust the train.** Some island trains and buses are coordinated with the ferry times, for example in Norddeich Mole.

The guide [Understanding Ebb and Flow] (/reisen/ebbe-und-flut/) explains how ebb and flow work.

## Tickets and booking

You can buy ferry tickets from the respective shipping company, online, at the counter in the port or sometimes in conjunction with a train ticket. For islands without car traffic, reservations for passengers are often not necessary, but are recommended in high season. The situation is different for cars: parking spaces on the ferries to Föhr, Amrum, Borkum or Norderney are often fully booked months in advance during the holidays.

When booking, pay attention to:

- **Round trip:** A return ticket is often cheaper than two single tickets.
- **Tourist tax:** On some islands it is collected with the ferry ticket or upon arrival. More about this in the guide [beach chair, tourist tax and beach sections] (/reisen/strandkorb-und-kurtaxe/).
- **Bicycles:** They usually cost extra and require a reservation during the season.
- **Dogs:** There is also often a small fee for dogs.
- **Discounts:** Children, families and people with severe disabilities often receive cheaper rates.

## Where to go by car?

If you drive to the coast and want to go to a car-free island, the car stays on the mainland. There are parking spaces and parking garages at all major ferry ports, most of which are chargeable, often with daily or weekly rates. During the season they become scarce. Many operators therefore offer online reservations.

Some parking lots are a little outside and have a shuttle bus to the port. Allow time for this, especially if your ferry runs at a fixed time depending on the tide.

For islands with car traffic, the question arises as to whether you even want to take your car with you. On Föhr or Amrum you can get around easily by bus and bike, and car transport is expensive. On Sylt, the car train runs from Niebüll over the Hindenburgdamm. The same applies here: book early in high season.

## Luggage: travel light or check in

On car-free islands, you carry your luggage yourself, pull it on a handcart or have it transported. Many shipping companies offer to check in suitcases and bags separately. You drop off your luggage at the mainland port, it travels in containers and you pick it up at the island port. On some islands you can even have it delivered directly to your accommodation for an additional fee.

Island luggage tips:

- **It's better to have two medium bags than one huge suitcase.** They're easier to carry and load.
- **Label your luggage well** with your name, island address and telephone number.
- **Important items in hand luggage:** Medication, keys, documents, charging cable, a jacket.
- **Be at the port on time.** There are deadlines for checking in your luggage.

## Traveling on car-free islands

There is no private car traffic on islands such as Juist, Baltrum, Langeoog, Spiekeroog, Wangerooge, Hiddensee, Helgoland or the Halligen. Here you get around on foot, by bike, in horse-drawn carriages or small electric vehicles that transport goods and luggage. On Langeoog and Wangerooge, small island trains run from the harbor to the village, and on Spiekeroog there is a historic horse-drawn tram for excursions.

You can rent bikes on almost every island. On some islands such as Baltrum or Hiddensee, cycling is restricted or unusual in certain areas because the paths are narrow or pedestrians have priority. Find out more locally.

For many, the lack of cars is the biggest attraction. It's quieter, the air is clear, children can run around more freely.

## Everyday life on the island: what’s different

Life on an island has its own rhythm:

- **Shopping:** Supermarkets and shops are available, but are often smaller and slightly more expensive than on the mainland. You have to bring some things with you in advance.
- **Medical care:** There are doctor's offices and pharmacies on larger islands, but often only limited on small islands. Take sufficient quantities of important medications with you.
- **Evening life:** Things get quiet early in the evening on small islands. You should often make reservations for restaurants during the season.
- **Weather:** Ferries may be canceled during storms. Allow some buffer when you leave, especially if you have a train or flight to catch.
- **Low season:** In winter, many shops and restaurants on smaller islands are closed or have limited opening hours.

## With children and older people on the island

An island trip with small children or older relatives requires a little more planning. The ferries usually have interior rooms with seats, toilets and a small snack bar, and on larger ships there are also elevators between the decks. Check with the shipping company if someone uses a wheelchair or walker. Most ports and ships are now easily accessible, but not every pier and not every small excursion boat.

On car-free islands, the distances between the port, town and accommodation are sometimes longer than the map suggests. A handcart, which many accommodations or rental companies offer, is worth its weight in gold for luggage and tired children's legs. Strollers with large wheels make better progress on sandy paths than small buggies. And for older people, it is worth choosing accommodation close to the town center or clarifying in advance whether luggage and guests can be picked up from the port. Many more tips for families can be found in the guide [Holidays in Germany with Children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Don't look at the ferry timetable until the day of arrival.** For tide-dependent ferries, the next departure may be hours later.
2. **Book the car parking space on the ferry too late.** They are quickly taken during the holidays.
3. **Do not reserve the parking space on the mainland.** Full parking spaces cost time and nerves.
4. **Bringing too much luggage on a car-free island.** Everything must be carried or transported.
5. **Do not plan a buffer when departing.** Storms or delays can jeopardize the connection.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do you get seasick on the ferry?

The crossings to most German islands are short and lead through protected waters. Seasickness is rare there. On longer routes, such as to Helgoland, and in windy conditions it can rock. Then a place in the middle of the ship, a view of the horizon and fresh air will help.

### Can I take my dog ​​on the ferry?

Generally yes, usually for a small fee and with a leash requirement. On some ships there are areas where dogs are not allowed. More about this in the guide [Holidays with a dog in Germany] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-hund/).

### Can I get on the ferry with the Germany ticket?

Most island ferries are private connections and are not included in the Germany ticket. Individual ferry and bus connections may be included depending on the region. Check this in advance, for example in the guide [Go on vacation with the Germany ticket] (/reisen/deutschlandticket-im-urlaub/). For more island themes, see [Islands](/themen/inseln/).
