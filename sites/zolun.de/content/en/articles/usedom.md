Usedom has a reputation that is older than most holiday resorts on the Baltic Sea. Berlin families traveled to this coast by train as early as the 19th century, and the white villas of the imperial baths still speak of this time today. At the same time, the island is much more than a promenade and a pier: behind the long sandy beach lie the Achterwasser, small villages with thatched roofs, lakes and forests, and in the east, after a few steps, Poland begins. This guide sorts through the island and helps you find the location that suits your vacation.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Usedom is located in the Pomeranian Bay in the very north-east of Germany. The larger part belongs to Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, the eastern tip with Świnoujście (Swinemünde) belongs to Poland.
> - On the Baltic Sea side, a fine sandy beach of around 40 kilometers stretches almost uninterrupted along the coast.
> - The imperial resorts of Ahlbeck, Heringsdorf and Bansin are known for their resort architecture, piers and a very long promenade.
> - The amber baths Zempin, Koserow, Loddin and Ückeritz as well as the hinterland, which is called Achterland here, are quieter.
> - You can reach the island via two bridges, at Wolgast and at Zecherin. The Usedomer Bäderbahn connects the towns to Świnoujście.

## Orientation: how Usedom is structured

On the map, Usedom looks like a narrow strip of land between the open Baltic Sea in the north and several large bodies of water in the south. Once you understand this, planning becomes easier.

- **The outer coast** in the north is the classic bathing coast with sandy beaches, dunes, pine forest and seaside resorts.
- **The Achterwasser** is a wide, shallow lagoon between the center of the island and the mainland. Here the water is calmer, there are small harbors, reed banks and spectacular sunsets.
- **The Achterland** in the south is hilly, agricultural and quiet, with lakes, avenues and villages like Benz, Mellenthin or in the Lieper Winkel.
- **The Polish part** around Świnoujście has a wide beach, a large harbor and its own promenade that seamlessly connects to the German one.
- **The north** around Peenemünde and Karlshagen is flatter and wooded, with history that should not be overlooked here.

At the narrowest point near Koserow, only a few hundred meters separate the Baltic Sea from the Achterwasser. From there you can swim in the sea in the morning and watch the sunset over the reeds on the bay in the evening.

## The imperial baths: Ahlbeck, Heringsdorf and Bansin

The three towns in the east of the island have grown together and now form one community. They owe their nickname to the time around 1900, when the nobility and wealthy bourgeoisie from Berlin and Stettin spent their summers here.

**Ahlbeck** is the easternmost of the three towns and borders directly on Poland. The historic pier with its restaurant building from 1898 is well known and is one of the most photographed motifs on the Baltic Sea coast. The atmosphere is a little more familiar than in Heringsdorf.

**Heringsdorf** is the elegant center with villas, hotels, a concert shell and the long pier that leads far out into the sea. This is the busiest place, especially in the evening on the promenade.

**Bansin** is located in the west, is smaller and quieter and has a beautiful beach promenade with front row villas. Forest and the small lakes of the hinterland begin behind the town.

The three towns are connected by a beach promenade that continues to Świnoujście. On foot or by bike it is the most beautiful way to get to know the imperial baths. The villas with their balconies, turrets and bright facades are typical spa architecture. Many now house holiday apartments and small hotels.

**Good to know:** In the Imperial Baths, prices and demand are highest in high season. If you want to live here, book early or come in the early and late season, when the promenade is quieter and the light is often at its most beautiful.

## The amber baths and the center of the island

Between Bansin and Zinnowitz there are four smaller towns that call themselves amber baths: **Ückeritz, Loddin, Koserow and Zempin**. The name reminds us that amber can sometimes be found on the beach after autumn and winter storms.

Everything is a bit smaller here: fewer large hotels, more holiday apartments, campsites and guesthouses, wide beaches with lots of space. In **Koserow** there is the Schlitzlsberg, a wooded cliff with a view over the coast, and on the beach you will find the old salt huts where fishermen used to salt their catch. **Ückeritz** is known for a large campsite directly behind the dunes, **Loddin** and **Kölpinsee** for quiet beaches and the nearby Achterwasser.

For families looking for a beach without the hustle and bustle, the Amber Baths are often a better choice than the Imperial Baths. You can still get to Heringsdorf quickly with the Bäderbahn if you want an evening promenade.

## Zinnowitz, Karlshagen, Trassenheide and Peenemünde

The northwest of the island has its own mix. **Zinnowitz** is the largest seaside resort in this section, with a pier, promenade, hotels and a diving gondola where you can look under the surface of the water at the pier. **Trassenheide** and **Karlshagen** are quieter, with a wide beach and lots of pine forest. In Trassenheide there are several excursion destinations for families in bad weather.

In the far north lies **Peenemünde**. This is where the Wehrmacht developed rocket weapons during World War II, and thousands of forced laborers and prisoners had to work under inhumane conditions for the program. The Historical-Technical Museum in the former power plant explains this history without romanticizing it. Plan at least half a day for the visit. The exhibition is only partially suitable for younger children. Many families combine a visit to the museum with an afternoon on the beach in Karlshagen.

## Achterland and Lieper Winkel: the quiet side

Anyone who only knows the seaside resorts has only seen half of Usedom. South of the main road, the island becomes hilly, the streets become narrow and lined with avenues, and villages with brick churches and thatched-roof houses lie between fields.

- **Benz** with its Dutch windmill and church on the hill is a popular destination for painters and photographers.
- **Mellenthin** has a moated castle with a café and brewery, surrounded by a park.
- **The Lieper Winkel** is a peninsula in the Achterwasser with small fishing villages such as Rankwitz and Warthe. It's so quiet here that you can hear the wind in the reeds.
- **The lakes** near Bansin and in the south, such as the Gothensee and the Schmollensee, are good alternatives to the Baltic Sea when the sea is still cold or the wind is strong.

Part of the island belongs to the Usedom Island Nature Park. The best way to get to know this landscape is by bike, on hiking trails or on a canoe tour on the Achterwasser.

## Detour to Świnoujście

The border with Poland is barely noticeable on the promenade: a sign, a change of paving stones, then we move on. Świnoujście, German Swinoujscie, has a very wide sandy beach, a spa park, restaurants and a lively market.

A few practical points:

- **Take ID with you.** Border controls are rare in the Schengen area, but you should have a valid ID card or passport with you.
- **Currency:** In Poland you pay with złoty. Many shops accept cards, some also take euros, but then often at an unfavorable rate.
- **Bäderbahn:** The Usedomer Bäderbahn runs to Świnoujście. So you can go on the trip without a car.
- **Bicycle:** Świnoujście can be reached from Ahlbeck in a short time by bicycle, via the promenade or cycle paths behind it.

## Arrival and travel on the island

**By car:** Usedom is connected to the mainland via two bridges, in the north at Wolgast and in the south at Zecherin near Anklam. Both bridges open to shipping traffic at fixed times. During these minutes the traffic is at a standstill, and on summer Saturdays there are long traffic jams anyway. If you can, travel on a weekday or plan to arrive early in the morning or in the evening.

**By train:** The Usedomer Bäderbahn runs from the mainland via Wolgast to the island and connects the seaside resorts to Świnoujście. There is a connection to the long-distance transport network in Züssow, among others. The Deutschlandticket is valid for regional transport. How you can plan your vacation can be found in the guide [Go on vacation with the Germany ticket] (/reisen/deutschlandticket-im-urlaub/).

**By bike:** The Baltic Sea coastal cycle path leads across the island, as well as cycle paths through the Achterland and around the Achterwasser. Many paths are flat, but there are also hills in the hinterland. You can rent bikes and e-bikes in all major towns. More about this in the guide [Bicycle on Vacation](/reisen/fahrrad-im-urlaub/).

**On site:** A spa tax is charged in the seaside resorts, which you usually pay through your accommodation. The spa card often offers discounts at events or on local transport. The guide [beach chair and tourist tax](/reisen/strandkorb-und-kurtaxe/) explains what the tourist tax and beach chair are all about.

## Travel time and weather

Usedom is considered one of the sunniest regions in Germany. That doesn't change the fact that the Baltic Sea can be fresh even in summer and the weather changes quickly.

- **June to September** is bathing season. Beaches, roads and accommodation are at their busiest in July and August.
- **May and September** are often the most pleasant months: long days, less busy, good conditions for cycling.
- **Autumn and winter** belong to walkers. The promenade during storms, amber searches after storms and empty beaches have their own charm. Many hotels in the Imperial Baths are open all year round.

You can find out more about seasons, holiday times and weather throughout Germany in the guide [Best travel time for vacation in Germany](/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

## Bathe safely

The beaches on the outer coast are mostly flat, but there are still currents, especially in onshore winds and near the groynes. Swim in patrolled areas, pay attention to the lifeguard flags and get out of the water immediately in the event of a thunderstorm. Children should always stay within reach of an adult in the water. The most important rules are summarized in the guide [Swimming safely by the sea](/reisen/sicher-baden-am-meer/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Arrive on Saturday in high season.** This is when the traffic in front of the bridges is heaviest.
2. **Only stay in the Kaiserbäder.** Achterland, Lieper Winkel and Bernsteinbäder show a completely different island.
3. **Forgot your ID for Świnoujście.** Checks are rare, but possible.
4. **Travel by car every day.** Baths, buses and bikes are often faster and more relaxed on the island.
5. **If the wind is from the west, hope for calm water.** Then it's worth a day of swimming at one of the inland lakes or the Achterwasser.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Usedom or Rügen nicer?

That depends on what you're looking for. Usedom has the longer, continuous sandy beach and the famous promenade of the Imperial Baths. Rügen is larger, has more diverse landscapes and has a unique natural experience with the chalk cliffs. The guide [Plan a Rügen vacation](/reisen/ruegen/) gives an overview of Rügen.

### Do you need a car on Usedom?

Not necessarily. The Bäderbahn connects most seaside resorts, as well as buses and good cycle paths. For trips to Achterland or Lieper Winkel, a car or an e-bike is practical.

### Is Usedom suitable for families with children?

Yes. Flat, wide sandy beaches, many holiday apartments and campsites as well as excursion destinations for rainy days make the island family-friendly. The amber baths are particularly relaxing. Further tips can be found in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/). You can find more islands under [Islands](/themen/inseln/).
