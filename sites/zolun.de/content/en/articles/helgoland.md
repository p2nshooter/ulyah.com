Helgoland is the only German offshore island. It lies far out in the German Bight, so far that you can't see anything of it from the mainland. Anyone who goes here experiences a short sea voyage, red rocks in the middle of the sea, seabirds in the thousands, seals on the beach and an island without cars. At the same time, Heligoland is a destination that needs to be well planned: the crossing takes time, the weather is a deciding factor, and if you only stay a few hours, you can especially see the harbor. This guide will help you find the right type of visit.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Helgoland is located around 60 to 70 kilometers off the coast in the North Sea and is part of Schleswig-Holstein.
> - The island consists of two parts: the main island with red sandstone cliffs and the smaller dune with sandy beaches and an airfield.
> - You can reach Helgoland by ship from several ports on the coast and from Hamburg or by small plane.
> - There are no cars and cycling is generally not allowed on the island. Everything is within walking distance.
> - Heligoland is outside the EU customs territory. You can buy certain goods duty-free, but allowances apply when you return.

## Main island and dune: two islands in one

Until a storm surge in the 18th century, the main island and dune were connected. Today a narrow arm of the sea separates them, across which a small ferry runs.

**The main island** is the well-known rock made of red sandstone. It is divided into three areas:

- **The Unterland** at the harbor with a promenade, shops, restaurants, hotels and the colorful lobster stands where lobster fishermen used to store their equipment and are now cafes, shops and small galleries.
- **The Oberland** on the plateau with houses, church, lighthouse and the circular path on the edge of the cliff. A staircase and an elevator lead to the Oberland.
- **The Mittelland** in between, a section of terrain that was created by the demolitions after the Second World War.

**The dune** is flat, sandy and covered with dune grass. Here are the beaches, a small airfield, a campsite, bungalows and restaurants. In winter the dune is best known for its gray seals.

## The Lange Anna and the cliff edge path

The circular route across the Oberland is the most beautiful walk on the island and takes about one to two hours. It leads along the edge of the red cliffs, with a view far over the sea, the dune and, with a clear view, ships passing by.

At the northwest end stands the **Lange Anna**, a free-standing rock tower made of red sandstone, Heligoland's landmark. The rock is at great risk from wind and waves and must not be walked on. You can take photos of it from the path, especially in the late afternoon, when the sun makes the sandstone shine.

Always stay on the paths and behind the barriers. The sandstone is brittle and there are frequent breaks on the edge.

## The Guillemot Rock: Seabirds up close

On the west side of the main island lies the **Lummenfelsen**, one of the smallest nature reserves in Germany and at the same time one of the most impressive. Thousands of seabirds breed on the narrow cliffs from spring to summer:

- **gannets**, large white birds with yellowish heads that plunge spectacularly into the sea.
- **guillemots** standing close together on the rock ledges.
- **Kittiwakes**, **razorbills** and **fulmars**.

From the cliff edge path you can watch the birds from a few meters away without disturbing them. A special event is the **guillemot jump** in early summer: the still flightless guillemot chicks jump from the rocks into the sea in the evening, where the parent birds are waiting for them.

In spring and autumn, Helgoland is also a well-known resting place for migratory birds. Then many bird watchers come to the island because rare species can appear here. More about bird migration and seals on the coast can be found in the guide [Seals and migratory birds] (/reisen/seehunde-und-zugvoegel/).

## Seals on the dune

**Grey seals** and **seals** live on the dune. Gray seals are the largest predators in Germany. In winter, around November to January, they give birth to their young on the dune beach. Then the white seal pups are a big draw, and rangers make sure visitors keep their distance.

The most important rules:

- **Stay at least 30 meters away** from all seals, even if they appear calm or sleeping.
- **Never stand between an animal and the water.** Seals then feel threatened and may bite.
- **Keep dogs on a leash** or leave them at the property.
- **Do not feed the animals** and never touch young animals.

Even in summer, seals often lie on the beach of the dune, sometimes in the middle of the bathers. The distance rule applies all year round.

## Swimming on Heligoland

The dune's beaches are among the most beautiful in the North Sea: light sand, clear water and surprisingly little activity once the day guests have left. There is a guarded swimming beach in season. However, the water remains fresh even in midsummer, often significantly cooler than on the Baltic Sea.

Pay attention to currents and lifeguard instructions, and keep your distance from seals in the water. General rules for safe swimming can be found in the guide [Swimming safely by the sea] (/reisen/sicher-baden-am-meer/).

## Arrival: ship or plane

**By ship:** During the season, seaside resort ships and fast catamarans travel to Helgoland from several ports, including from Cuxhaven, Büsum and Bremerhaven as well as from Hamburg. Depending on the departure port and ship, the journey time is between around two and four hours. In winter the offer is significantly smaller, and there is often only one connection per day.

On some connections, passengers are traditionally brought from the ship to shore in small boats, the so-called Börteboots. This disembarkation is an experience in itself, but requires some surefootedness.

**By Air:** Small propeller planes fly to the dune from airfields on the coast. The flight only lasts a quarter to half an hour and is a good choice for people who get seasick easily. The machines are small and luggage is limited.

**Sea sickness:** The North Sea can be restless even on sunny days. If you are sensitive, sit in the middle of the ship and as far down as possible, look at the horizon, eat a light meal beforehand and, if necessary, take a medication from the pharmacy in good time. Trips may be canceled during storms. Therefore, plan a buffer on the return journey, especially if there is a train or flight afterwards.

## Day trip or overnight stay?

Most visitors come as day guests. Depending on your connection, you have three to four hours on the island. That's enough for the circular route across the Oberland, a look at the Lange Anna, a fish sandwich and a stroll through the shops. There is hardly any time for the dune.

If you stay for one or more nights, you will experience a different island. As soon as the ships leave in the afternoon, everything becomes quiet. In the evening, the cliff edge path belongs to the few overnight guests, the birds circle in the evening light, and in the morning you have the dune almost to yourself.

## Shop duty-free

Helgoland is politically part of Germany, but lies outside the customs and VAT area of the EU. That's why goods such as spirits, tobacco, perfume or confectionery are often cheaper here. For many day visitors, shopping is an integral part of the trip.

Please note:

- When returning to the mainland, **free allowances** apply, especially for alcohol and tobacco. Anything above that must be declared customs. Customs gives the current borders.
- Random checks are carried out upon arrival at the mainland port.
- Not everything is automatically cheaper. Compare the prices with what you pay at home.

## A look into history

Heligoland has an eventful history. The island was Danish for a long time, then British and came to Germany in 1890 in exchange for rights in East Africa. Hoffmann von Fallersleben wrote the Song of the Germans on Heligoland in 1841. During the Second World War, the island was converted into a fortress and heavily bombed. After the war, the British tried to destroy the fortifications with a massive explosion. It was not until 1952 that the Heligolanders were allowed to return and rebuild their island.

You can see traces of this time everywhere: in the Mittelland, in the sober houses of the post-war period and on tours through the bunkers. The Helgoland Museum tells the history of the island and its inhabitants.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Plan the day trip too tightly.** Check the time your ship will be in port and decide in advance whether you want to see Oberland or Dune.
2. **Plan without a buffer on the return journey.** Trips are canceled during storms, especially in winter.
3. **Get too close to seals.** Stay 30 meters away, even for the perfect photo.
4. **Arrive by bike or scooter.** Cycling is generally not permitted on the island.
5. **Ignore free quantities.** Duty-free purchases above the permitted quantity become expensive.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a day trip to Helgoland worth it?

Yes, if you see the crossing as part of the experience and concentrate on one or two focal points. If you want to experience seals, beaches and birds in peace, you should stay overnight at least once.

### When is the best time for Heligoland?

Spring and early summer are suitable for seabirds at Lummenfelsen, spring and autumn for migratory birds, and winter for baby seals. You can swim in midsummer. More about the seasons in the guide [Best travel time for vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

### Is Heligoland suitable for children?

Yes. Children love the boat ride, the seals and the birds. However, a day trip is long for small children. One or two nights on the dune are often more relaxing.
