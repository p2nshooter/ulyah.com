Berlin is overwhelming for almost everyone on their first visit. The city is huge, it doesn't have one center, but several, and there are often more kilometers between two sights than the city map suggests. Anyone who tries to tick off Museum Island, the Wall, the Reichstag, Kreuzberg and Charlottenburg in one weekend will spend a lot of time in the subway and little time in the city. The good news: With a little orientation, Berlin becomes manageable. This guide will show you how the city is structured, how to plan Museum Island and Wall locations sensibly, why you need to register for the Reichstag dome, how local transport works with its fare zones and when it's worth taking a detour to Potsdam.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Berlin consists of many neighborhoods with their own character. Plan one or two areas per day, not the entire city.
> - The Museum Island is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. For many museums, it's worth getting a time slot ticket that you book online in advance.
> - You can only visit the Reichstag dome after prior registration with the German Bundestag.
> - Local transport is divided into tariff areas A, B and C. For Potsdam and BER airport you need area C.
> - The Deutschlandticket is valid on all local buses and trains in Berlin and Brandenburg.

## Reading Berlin: Mitte, neighborhoods and real distances

Berlin has grown together from many individual cities and villages and was divided for decades. That's why there are two historical centers: Mitte in the former eastern part with Museum Island, Unter den Linden and Brandenburg Gate and the City West around Kurfürstendamm and Zoo station. In between and around there are the neighborhoods, residential areas with their own squares, markets, cafés and shops. Berliners live in their neighborhood, and as a guest the best way to experience the city is the same way: you choose an area and walk there.

A helpful orientation is the Ringbahn, an S-Bahn line that circles the city center in a large circle. Because of its shape it is often called the “dog head”. Almost everything you would want to see on a first visit is within this ring or just outside of it.

| area | What awaits you | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| middle | Museum Island, Unter den Linden, Brandenburg Gate, government district, television tower | Classics, history, museums |
| City West | Kurfürstendamm, Memorial Church, Zoo, near Charlottenburg Palace | Shopping spree, castle and park |
| Prenzlauer Berg | Old buildings, squares, cafés, close to Bernauer Straße | Quiet stroll, Wall Memorial |
| Kreuzberg and Friedrichshain | Canal banks, Spree, East Side Gallery, lively streets | Evenings, street art, life by the water |
| Neukölln and Tempelhof | Kiezleben, Tempelhofer Feld | Expanse, picnic, cycling |

Plan realistically: It is several kilometers from the Brandenburg Gate to Kurfürstendamm, and from Mitte to Wannsee you take the S-Bahn for quite a while. [Plan a city weekend](/reisen/staedtewochenende-planen/) shows how you can plan a city weekend without rushing, with fixed anchors per day and enough breaks.

## The Museum Island and its time slot tickets

On the northern tip of the Spree Island in Mitte there are five large museums close together: the Altes Museum with art from antiquity, the Neues Museum with the Egyptian Museum and the famous bust of Nefertiti, the Alte Nationalgalerie with paintings and sculpture from the 19th century, the Bode Museum with sculptures and the coin cabinet and the Pergamon Museum. Together they form the Museum Island, which has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999. The Pergamon Museum has been extensively renovated for years and is completely or partially closed during this time. Check the current status on the official website of the Berlin State Museums.

How to plan your visit:

1. **Choose one or two museums.** All five in one day is too much. Decide according to your interest: antiquity and Egypt, painting or sculpture.
2. **Book a time slot ticket.** Many houses offer entry at fixed times. Booking online saves you queues, especially on weekends and during holidays.
3. **Check closing days and opening times.** Many museums in Germany have a closing day, often on Mondays. The current times are on the official pages.
4. **Calculate combination tickets.** There are area and multi-day tickets. They are only worth it if you actually visit several houses.

Around the Museum Island are the Berlin Cathedral, the Lustgarten and the Humboldt Forum in the rebuilt Berlin Palace. If you need some fresh air after a morning at the museum, sit in the pleasure garden or walk along the Spree.

## History on site: Wall, division and memory

From 1961 to 1989 the wall divided the city. Today it has almost disappeared, but in many places a double row of paving stones in the ground marks its course. Three places will help you understand the division.

### Berlin Wall Memorial

On Bernauer Strasse, the central memorial stretches along the former border strip. There is still a piece of wall here with a hinterland wall and a watchtower, so you can see how wide and deep the border fortifications were. Boards, floor markings and a documentation center with a viewing platform tell of escapes, tunnels and demolished houses. Plan to spend at least two hours here.

### East Side Gallery and Checkpoint Charlie

The East Side Gallery in Friedrichshain is the longest surviving piece of the Wall. After the fall of the Berlin Wall, artists from many countries painted it; today it is an open-air gallery on the banks of the Spree. There is a replica checkpoint at Checkpoint Charlie, the former border crossing on Friedrichstrasse. The place is touristy and often crowded. If you want to find out more about the border, you are better off at the Tear Palace at Friedrichstrasse train station, the former check-in hall for travel between East and West.

### Reichstag dome only with registration

The glass dome on the Reichstag building, the seat of the German Bundestag, is one of the most visited destinations in the city. A visit is only possible after prior registration via the Bundestag website. Register early, take your ID with you and expect a security check. The dome may be closed at short notice for parliamentary reasons.

Nearby are the Brandenburg Gate and the Memorial to the Murdered Jews of Europe with its underground information center. The Topography of Terror, a documentation center on the site of the former Gestapo and SS headquarters, is also easily accessible from there. These places require time and peace and quiet, so don't plan them between two shopping stops.

## Understanding local transport: tariff areas A, B and C

Berlin has a dense network of S-Bahn, subway, tram and bus. The tram runs mainly in the former eastern part of the city. All means of transport belong to a common tariff, so you only need one ticket.

| Tariff area | What it includes | Relevant to you if |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | City center within the Ringbahn | you only move in the center |
| b | Outskirts up to the city limits | you live on the outskirts of the city or go to Wannsee |
| C | Surrounding area in Brandenburg | you go to Potsdam or BER airport |

Most guests get by with a ticket for areas A and B. To travel to or from BER airport and for Potsdam you need A, B and C. In addition to single tickets, there are day and multi-day tickets as well as tourist cards with discounts for museums and excursions. Which one is worth it depends on how often you drive per day. Do the math quickly instead of choosing the most expensive option. You sometimes have to validate paper tickets that you don't buy in the app before the journey. The Deutschlandticket is valid in all tariff areas. What else it covers can be found in [Go on vacation with the Germany ticket](/reisen/deutschlandticket-im-urlaub/).

On weekends, many subway lines also run at night, and night buses take over during the week. By the way, a seat at the top of the double-decker bus is a cheap city tour. And a note for pedestrians: The red-marked cycle paths are not sidewalks; cyclists move quickly there.

## Green and water: breaks that define Berlin

Berlin is amazingly green and filled with water. The **Tiergarten** in the middle of the city is big enough to get lost and is suitable for a walk between the Brandenburg Gate and the Victory Column. A **boat trip on the Spree** shows you the government district and the Museum Island from the water and is a comfortable way to see a lot on tired days.

The **Tempelhofer Feld** is the former Tempelhof airport site. Today, people ride bikes, skateboard and fly kites on the old runways. The space in the middle of the city is unique. The field is only open during the day, times depend on the season. On hot days, the lakes on the outskirts of the city, such as the Wannsee or the Müggelsee, are also attractive. How to swim there safely is explained in [Bathing in Lakes and Rivers](/reisen/baden-in-seen-und-fluessen/).

## Trip to Potsdam

Potsdam, the state capital of Brandenburg, borders directly on Berlin and is easily accessible by S-Bahn and regional train. The palaces and parks of Potsdam and Berlin are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. Sanssouci Park is home to Sanssouci Palace, Frederick the Great's summer residence, and the New Palace, as well as gardens, pavilions and long avenues. Cecilienhof Palace, where the Potsdam Conference took place in 1945, is located in the New Garden. In the city itself, the Dutch Quarter with its brick houses and the Glienicke Bridge, which became known for the exchange of agents during the Cold War, are worth a visit.

There are tickets with fixed entry times for Sanssouci Palace. Book it in advance and find out about opening times and seasons on the official website of the Prussian Palaces and Gardens Foundation. The park is spacious, comfortable shoes are required. A whole day is not too much for Potsdam. If you're still looking for peace and quiet by the water after Berlin, you'll find it in [Spreewald](/reisen/spreewald/), which is also easily accessible by train.

## Typical mistakes when visiting Berlin for the first time

1. **Too many neighborhoods in one day.** Stay in one area and explore on foot.
2. **Want to spontaneously visit the Reichstag dome.** You won't be able to get in without registering.
3. **Go to the airport or Potsdam with an AB ticket.** You need area C for this.
4. **All museums on Museum Island in one day.** Two houses with a break are more than enough.
5. **Want to shop on Sundays.** Most stores are closed on Sundays. Plan shopping for Saturday.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do I need for Berlin?

Two to three days are enough for a first impression if you concentrate on the center, the wall locations and a neighborhood. With one day for Potsdam and one for museums, four to five days are more relaxed.

### Where is the best place to stay on my first visit?

Within the Ringbahn and near an S-Bahn or U-Bahn station. Mitte is central, Prenzlauer Berg and Friedrichshain offer more neighborhood life. Good connections are more important than the district. You can find more ideas for city trips in the [City Trips](/themen/staedtereisen/) section.
