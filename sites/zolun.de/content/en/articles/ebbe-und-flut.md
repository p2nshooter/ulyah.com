In the evening you spread your towel out on the water, the next morning the beach is suddenly hundreds of meters wider in front of you and the boats in the harbor are sitting askew in the mud. Anyone who goes on holiday on the North Sea for the first time will experience the tides as a small miracle. They determine when you can swim, when a trip to the mudflats is possible and when the ferry leaves for the island. This guide explains how ebb and flow arise, how to read a tide calendar and what you need to pay attention to so that the mudflats remain an experience and do not become a danger.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - On the German North Sea, high and low water change about twice a day. A complete cycle takes around twelve and a half hours.
> - The times shift back by almost an hour every day. A tide calendar for your location is therefore mandatory.
> - The difference between high and low water, the tidal range, on the German North Sea coast is roughly between one and a half and almost four meters, depending on the location.
> - Longer mudflat hikes should be in the hands of local mudflat guides. When water comes up, tidal creeks fill up faster than you think.
> - There are practically no noticeable tides on the Baltic Sea. Water levels change there mainly due to wind.

## How ebb and flow arise

Tides are caused by the gravitational pull of the moon and, to a lesser extent, the sun. The moon pulls the water of the oceans on the side of the Earth facing it slightly toward itself. On the opposite side, the movement of the Earth and Moon around their common center of gravity also creates a mountain of water. Because the earth rotates beneath these two mountains of water, a coastal location typically experiences high water twice a day and low water twice a day.

A complete cycle from one flood to the next lasts on average about twelve hours and 25 minutes. So the water goes out for around six hours, the low tide, and around six hours up, the high tide. Because the moon takes a little longer than a day to orbit the Earth, the high tide times are postponed by an average of almost 50 minutes every day. If you had flooding at 10 a.m. on Monday, you're more likely to have it around 11 a.m. on Tuesday.

The terms are often mixed up in everyday life. Strictly speaking, ebb and flow describe the movement, i.e. the outgoing and the incoming water. High tide and low tide are the times of highest and lowest water levels.

## Spring tides and neap tides

Not every flood is equally strong. When the sun, moon and earth are in line, i.e. during the new moon and full moon, their powers increase. Then the tidal range is particularly large, it is called a spring tide. The water rises higher and falls further. During a half moon, the sun and moon are at right angles to each other and their forces are partially weakened. The tidal range is smaller, this is called neap tide.

For your holiday this means: During spring tide, the mudflats continue to dry out, mudflat hikes can be longer, but the rising water also comes faster and higher. During neap tides the differences are less pronounced.

## The tidal range on the German coast

How much the water rises and falls depends on the location. The shape of the coast, estuaries and bays influence the tidal wave. On the German North Sea coast, the average tidal range is roughly between one and a half meters in some places on the North Frisian Islands and just under four meters in the Jade near Wilhelmshaven. In the large estuaries of the Elbe, Weser and Ems, the tides extend far into the interior, and the Elbe can be felt as far as Hamburg.

| Location (examples) | Mean tidal range (coarse) | What you notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Westerland on Sylt | almost 2 meters | Surf on the open west beach, mudflats on the east side of the island |
| Büsum | a good 3 meters | Wide mudflats in front of the dike, the harbor is partly dry |
| Cuxhaven | around 3 meters | Mudflat hikes to Neuwerk, ships on the Elbe |
| Wilhelmshaven | almost 4 meters | big difference, Jade Bays almost completely dry at low tide |
| Norderney | around 2.5 meters | Mud flats on the south side, ferry dependent on tide |

The values are average values. Wind and air pressure can significantly change the actual water level on individual days. When there is an onshore storm the water rises higher; when there is an offshore wind it falls deeper.

## Read a tide calendar

The tide calendar, also known as the tide table, is your most important planning tool on the North Sea. You can get it at tourist information centers, at harbor authorities, and often in your accommodation. The Federal Maritime and Hydrographic Agency publishes official forecasts. Many places also offer their own calendars or apps.

This is how you read it:

1. **Choose the right location.** The times differ by many minutes between neighboring locations. Use the reference location closest to your beach.
2. **Find high and low tides.** There are usually two high and two low tide times per day, sometimes only three times if one falls on the following day.
3. **Pay attention to summer time.** Check whether the times are in Central European Summer Time or need to be converted.
4. **Note water levels.** Some tables also show the expected height. Particularly high values ​​indicate spring tide.
5. **Plan buffer.** The times are calculations in advance. Wind can move them.

## Swim with the tides

On many North Sea beaches, swimming is most pleasant around high water. Then the water reaches the beach and you don't have to walk far. When the tide is low, the sea is so far out in some places that swimming is hardly possible. On the western beaches of the islands such as Sylt, Amrum or Borkum, the water is often still accessible even at low tide, but there is more surf and current there.

It becomes dangerous if you swim far out when the water is receding or stand on a sandbank when the water is coming up. ## The Wadden Sea: World natural heritage on your doorstep

The Wadden Sea stretches from the Netherlands through Germany to Denmark. It is one of the largest contiguous mudflat landscapes in the world and is protected as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. There are three national parks on the German side: the Lower Saxony, the Hamburg and the Schleswig-Holstein Wadden Sea.

When the tide is low, the seabed here becomes dry over a large area. What initially looks like a gray area of ​​mud is full of life: lugworms, whose droppings can be seen everywhere, cockles, shore crabs and mud snails in huge numbers. For migratory birds, the mudflats are one of the most important rest stops on their way between the Arctic and Africa. You can find out more about this in the guide [Seals, cranes and migratory birds] (/reisen/seehunde-und-zugvoegel/).

Protection rules apply in the national parks. Certain zones may not be entered or may only be entered on designated paths, especially during breeding and resting times. Information boards, national park houses and information centers explain to you on site what is permitted.

## Wadden hiking: beautiful, but only with respect

For many people, a mudflat hike is part of their North Sea holiday. Walking barefoot through the mud, discovering lugworms and seeing the horizon without water is a special experience. At the same time, the mudflats are not a harmless walk.

**The Dangers:**

- **Rising water:** The tide does not come as a wave, but first fills the tidal creeks, i.e. the channels in the mudflats. Suddenly there is a stream between you and the bank that is deeper and has more current than it looks.
- **Fog:** On the open mud flats you will quickly lose your orientation in fog.
- **Thunderstorm:** On the flat surface you are the highest point far and wide.
- **Soft mud:** In some areas you sink deep, which takes energy and time.

**This is how you go safely into the mudflats:**

1. **Always with a mudflat guide for longer hikes.** Guided tours are available in almost all coastal towns. Mudflat guides know tidal creeks, tides and weather.
2. **Never travel to islands or Halligen alone.** Hikes to Neuwerk or between islands can only be undertaken with an experienced guide.
3. **Alone only near the shore** and only when the water is running out, with a look at the clock and the tide calendar.
4. **Turn back at low tide at the latest**, rather sooner.
5. **Appropriate equipment:** Barefoot or with wading shoes or old sneakers, sun protection, water, weatherproof clothing, a charged cell phone.

## The tides and ferry traffic

For some islands the tides determine the timetable. The ferries to islands like Juist, Baltrum or Spiekeroog run depending on the tide, i.e. only at certain times around high water. The departure times change daily. Therefore, plan your arrival according to the shipping company's timetable, not according to your desired time. Other islands such as Norderney or Borkum have more tidal connections. The guide [Taking the ferry to the island](/reisen/faehre-und-inselalltag/) explains more about this.

## And the Baltic Sea?

On the Baltic Sea, tides play practically no role in everyday life. The Baltic Sea is an almost closed sea with a narrow connection to the North Sea, the tidal waves hardly get in. The tidal range on the German Baltic Sea coast is only a few centimeters.

Nevertheless, the water level on the Baltic Sea can change significantly due to wind. If it blows strongly from one direction for a long time, it pushes the water towards or away from a coast. Strong north-easterly or easterly storms can cause storm flooding on the German Baltic Sea coast. If you are looking for a comparison between the two seas, you will find it in the guide [North Sea or Baltic Sea?](/reisen/nordsee-oder-ostsee/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Use the tide calendar of another location.** The times differ between neighboring locations.
2. **Assume the times are the same every day.** They vary by almost an hour each day.
3. **Walk far into the mudflats alone.** Streams fill up behind you, often unnoticed.
4. **Do not check the ferry time to the island.** Tidal ferries do not run as requested.
5. **Want to swim during low tide.** Plan beach days around high tide, if possible.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the best time to swim in the North Sea?

Typically about two hours before to two hours after high water. Then the water reaches the beach. Nevertheless, follow the advice of the lifeguards, as the current can change with the tide.

### Can I go to the mudflats with children?

Yes, guided family mudflat hikes are very popular and an experience for children. Many mudflat guides offer shorter tours especially for families. If you are alone with children, you should only stay within sight of the bank and when the water is draining.

### Are there also mudflat hikes on the Baltic Sea?

No, there is no Wadden Sea with regularly dry areas on the Baltic Sea. Steep coasts, bays and long sandy beaches are typical there. You can find out more about both coasts at [North Sea & Baltic Sea](/themen/nordsee-und-ostsee/).
