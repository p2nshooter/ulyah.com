The German Baltic Sea coast is longer and more diverse than many people think. It begins at the Danish border with fjords that cut deep into the country, leads past the lively Bay of Lübeck, white resort architecture in Mecklenburg, quiet bays and wind-blown forests on the Darß and ends at the Polish border on Usedom. In between there are Hanseatic cities with brick Gothic architecture, cliffs with chalk cliffs and countless sandy beaches. This guide introduces you to the regions so that you can find the section that suits your vacation.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The German Baltic Sea coast is divided into Schleswig-Holstein and Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania.
> - In Schleswig-Holstein, fjords, bays and lively seaside resorts characterize the coast, in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania seaside resorts with seaside architecture, bays, peninsulas and large islands.
> - The Baltic Sea hardly has any tides. You can swim at any time of day, the water is usually calmer than on the North Sea.
> - Many beaches are fine sand and gently sloping, as well as cliffs and natural beaches.
> - Hanseatic cities like Lübeck, Wismar and Stralsund are located directly on the coast and offer culture for rainy days.

## What makes the Baltic Sea coast special

Unlike the North Sea, the water in the Baltic Sea does not follow the rhythm of the tides. The water level fluctuates only slightly, mainly due to wind. So you can bathe whenever you want. The water is less salty than in the North Sea and becomes pleasantly warm in shallow areas in summer. Many beaches are wide, fine sand and gently sloping, which makes them attractive for families.

Also typical are piers that lead far out into the sea, resort architecture from the 19th and early 20th centuries with white villas and decorated balconies, as well as cliffs where the sea slowly erodes the land. You can find the detailed comparison of both seas in the guide [North Sea or Baltic Sea?] (/reisen/nordsee-oder-ostsee/).

## Schleswig-Holstein: fjords and bays

### Flensburg Fjord

In the far north, the Flensburg Fjord cuts deep into the country, with Germany on one side and Denmark on the other. Flensburg itself is a lively port city with historic merchants' farms and a museum harbor. On the fjord there are quiet places like Glücksburg with its moated castle. The region is ideal for sailors and for everyone who likes it tranquil.

### Schlei and Eckernförder Bay

The Schlei is a narrow, long arm of the sea that stretches almost 40 kilometers into the country. On its banks are Kappeln, the fishing village of Maasholm and Arnis, which is considered the smallest town in Germany. Schleswig with the cathedral and the Haithabu Viking Museum is a worthwhile excursion. Eckernförde Bay has a long sandy beach right in front of the city.

### Kiel Fjord and Probstei

The state capital Kiel is located on the Kiel Fjord, where ferries to Scandinavia and large cruise ships dock. Every June, Kiel Week, one of the largest sailing events in the world, takes place here. On the east bank is Laboe with the naval memorial and a museum submarine on the beach. The Probstei with places like Schönberg has long, natural beaches.

### Hohwacht Bay and Fehmarn

The Hohwacht Bay is quiet and close to nature, with cliffs and sandy beaches. The island of Fehmarn is connected to the mainland via the Fehmarn Sound Bridge and is presented in detail in the guide [Fehmarn](/reisen/fehmarn/).

### Bay of Lübeck

The Bay of Lübeck is the most famous and busiest Baltic Sea region in Schleswig-Holstein. Beach resorts are lined up here: Travemünde at the mouth of the Trave with a beach promenade and large ferries, Timmendorfer Strand, Scharbeutz, Haffkrug, Sierksdorf, Neustadt, Grömitz, Kellenhusen and Dahme. The places are easily accessible, offer a lot of infrastructure and are correspondingly full in summer. The Brodten cliff rises between Travemünde and Niendorf, a beautiful hiking trail with a view over the bay.

Lübeck itself, the old queen of the Hanseatic League with the Holsten Gate and an old town that is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is just a few kilometers inland.

## Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania: seaside resorts, bays and islands

### Wismar, Poel Island and Klützer Winkel

Mecklenburg begins right behind the state border. Wismar is a Hanseatic city with a large market square, brick churches and harbor and, together with Stralsund, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. In front of it is the island of Poel with quiet beaches. To the west of Wismar extends the Klützer Winkel with the Baltic Sea resort of Boltenhagen and its pier.

### Kühlungsborn, Heiligendamm and Warnemünde

Some of Mecklenburg's most famous seaside resorts lie between Wismar and Rostock. Heiligendamm was founded at the end of the 18th century and is considered the oldest seaside resort in Germany. Its white buildings have earned it the nickname “White City by the Sea”. From here the historic Molli narrow-gauge railway runs to Kühlungsborn and Bad Doberan. Kühlungsborn is a large seaside resort with a long promenade and resort architecture.

Warnemünde is the seaside resort of the Hanseatic city of Rostock, with one of the widest beaches on the German Baltic coast, a lighthouse, the Alter Strom with fishing boats and large cruise ships that pass in front of the beach promenade.

### Fischland-Darß-Zingst

A narrow peninsula begins northeast of Rostock between the Baltic Sea and the Bodden: Fischland, Darß and Zingst. Ahrenshoop is a traditional artists' place with galleries and thatched houses. The Darß is forested, the western beach is considered one of the most beautiful natural beaches in Germany, with trees shaped by the wind, the so-called wind refugees. Zingst is a family seaside resort with a long pier.

A large part of the region belongs to the Western Pomerania Lagoon Landscape National Park. Bodden are shallow, brackish lagoons between the mainland and offshore peninsulas. In autumn, tens of thousands of cranes rest here, a natural spectacle that is described in the guidebook [Seals, Cranes and Migratory Birds](/reisen/seehunde-und-zugvoegel/).

### Stralsund, Rügen and Hiddensee

Stralsund is the gateway to Rügen. The old town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the Oceaneum shows the underwater world of the northern seas. Via the Rügendamm and the Rügen Bridge you reach Germany's largest island, which is presented in detail in the guide [Rügen](/reisen/ruegen/). The car-free island of Hiddensee is located west of Rügen.

### Greifswald and Usedom

Greifswald is a university town with brick Gothic architecture and the picturesque fishing village of Wieck with its wooden bascule bridge. In the far east lies Usedom, whose imperial spas Ahlbeck, Heringsdorf and Bansin are known for their spa architecture. More about this in the guide [Usedom](/reisen/usedom/).

## The Baltic Sea throughout the year

The classic bathing season on the Baltic Sea lasts from June to the beginning of September. During this time, beach chairs are set up, lifeguards are on duty and all beach bars are open. It gets busiest during the summer holidays in the major federal states, especially on weekends and at the Bay of Lübeck.

May and June are often sunny and not yet overcrowded. The water is still cool, so swimming is more for the hardy. For many, September is an insider tip: the sea has warmed up over the summer, the holidays are over and prices are falling. In October, the time of the cranes begins on the Bodden coast, and the forests on the Darß begin to change color.

In winter it gets quiet on the Baltic Sea. Many seaside resorts are still open, with wellness hotels, winter walks on empty beaches and sometimes ice floes on the shore. Around New Year's Eve, some places, such as Usedom, Rügen or Warnemünde, are well attended again. You can find the right season for your destination in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany](/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

## If the weather doesn't cooperate

There are gray days on the Baltic Sea too. Then the Hanseatic cities with their churches, museums and cafés, the Oceaneum in Stralsund, the European Hanseatic Museum in Lübeck or a visit to one of the many adventure pools on the coast are worth it. A walk on a long pier is an experience, even in windy conditions, if you're well wrapped up. The guide [Rainy days on vacation] (/reisen/regentage-im-urlaub/) collects further ideas.

## Arrival and mobility

Many Baltic Sea towns are easily accessible by train, such as Kiel, Lübeck-Travemünde, Timmendorfer Strand, Wismar, Rostock-Warnemünde, Stralsund and the large towns on Rügen and Usedom. You can reach smaller towns by bus, which often runs more frequently during the season. The guide [On vacation with the Germany ticket] (/reisen/deutschlandticket-im-urlaub/) explains how you can get to the coast with the Germany ticket.

The Baltic Sea Coastal Cycle Path leads through all regions and is a nice way to get to know the coast. In contrast to the North Sea, there are also hilly sections on the Baltic Sea, for example on cliffs. More about this in [Bicycle on vacation](/reisen/fahrrad-im-urlaub/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Underestimate the Bay of Lübeck during the summer holidays.** Plan early if you want to come in high season.
2. **Step too close to cliffs.** Edges can give way. Keep your distance and respect barriers.
3. **Only search in well-known locations.** Smaller locations are often quieter and cheaper.
4. **Confuse the Bodden with the sea.** In the Bodden you swim in calm, shallow brackish water, not in the open Baltic Sea.
5. **Forget the tourist tax.** Many seaside resorts charge it. More about this in [Beach chair, tourist tax and beach sections](/reisen/strandkorb-und-kurtaxe/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Baltic Sea warmer than the North Sea?

In shallow, sheltered places, the Baltic Sea water often becomes pleasantly warm in summer. On the open coast, temperatures vary less than many people think. Weather, wind and location are crucial.

### Are there jellyfish in the Baltic Sea?

Yes, especially in late summer. Most species are harmless, some can sting easily. The guide [Swimming safely in the North and Baltic Seas](/reisen/sicher-baden-am-meer/) explains what you do then.

### Which Baltic Sea region is the quietest?

Away from the large seaside resorts, you will find a lot of peace and quiet, for example at the Schlei, in the Probstei, on the island of Poel, on the Darß and in small towns in Western Pomerania. You can find more coastal topics at [North Sea & Baltic Sea](/themen/nordsee-und-ostsee/).
