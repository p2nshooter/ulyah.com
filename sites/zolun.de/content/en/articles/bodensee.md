Anyone planning a holiday on Lake Constance for the first time will be amazed at the choice: dozens of shore towns, three islands, ships in all directions and three countries around a single lake. Everything looks equally beautiful in the brochure. Once you're there, you quickly notice that an evening in the old town of Konstanz feels completely different than a week in a village on the Höri. This guide sorts out the lake for you: which shores have which character, what distinguishes the islands of Lindau, Mainau and Reichenau, how ships and car ferries work and what you should pack for a detour to Austria and Switzerland.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Germany, Austria and Switzerland border each other on Lake Constance. On the German side, Baden-Württemberg and Bavaria share the bank.
> - The lake consists of the large Obersee with the Überlinger See as its northwestern arm and the smaller, quieter Untersee.
> - The three most famous islands are very different: Lindau is an old town, Mainau is a flower and castle park, Reichenau is a monastery island and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
> - Liners and car ferries save you long detours around the shore. Timetables apply per season, check them with the shipping companies.
> - For Switzerland you need ID, francs or card and a look at your mobile phone tariff.

## One lake, three countries: brief orientation

The Rhine is the key to Lake Constance. It flows into the lake from the Alps in the southeast, between Austria and Switzerland, and leaves it again at the western end of the Untersee near Stein am Rhein. A little later he falls over the Rhine Falls near Schaffhausen.

The Obersee is the large, open part. Here are Lindau, Friedrichshafen and Bregenz, opposite the Swiss banks with Romanshorn and Rorschach. Lake Überlingen stretches to the northwest with Meersburg, Überlingen and the island of Mainau. Konstanz is located at the narrow point where the Obersee merges into the Seerhein, which leads to the Untersee. The Untersee is flatter, smaller and quieter, with the Reichenau, the Höri peninsula and Radolfzell.

All three countries belong to the Schengen area, so there are usually no systematic border controls. However, controls are possible at any time and Switzerland is not a member of the EU. This plays a role for customs, money and cell phones.

## The banks and their character

### Lindau and the Bavarian shore

The Bavarian bank is short. Its center is Lindau with an old town on an island that is connected to the mainland via a road bridge and a railway embankment. To the west there are quiet places with orchards and banks, which are more comfortable in summer than in the cities. Bregenz in Austria is close from here.

### Friedrichshafen and the Württemberg bank

Friedrichshafen is a lively city with a long waterfront and a close connection to the history of the Zeppelin airships. There are places like Langenargen and Kressbronn between Friedrichshafen and Lindau, and small wine towns like Hagnau to the west towards Meersburg. The shore is flat and good for cycling.

### Meersburg, Überlingen and Lake Überlingen

Meersburg lies steeply on a slope: the waterfront promenade below, the old town with the old and new castles above. The poet Annette von Droste-Hülshoff lived here. Vines grow all around. Überlingen has a long waterfront promenade and an old town with a large Gothic cathedral that is well worth seeing. In between are the baroque pilgrimage church of Birnau high above the water and Unteruhldingen with an open-air museum that reproduces pile dwellings. The prehistoric pile-dwelling settlements around the Alps are a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and some of the sites are on Lake Constance. Meersburg itself does not have a train station; you can reach it by bus, boat or ferry.

### Constancy

Konstanz is the largest city on the lake and university town, with an old town, harbor and the council building, which is reminiscent of the Council of Constance in the early 15th century. The city borders directly on the Swiss Kreuzlingen, you can walk across. From here you can easily reach the Mainau, the Reichenau and the Untersee, and the car ferry to Meersburg leaves from the Staad district.

### The Untersee with Höri and Radolfzell

It's getting quieter at Untersee. Radolfzell has an old town and the Mettnau peninsula, a nature reserve on the water. The Höri is a rural peninsula with small villages where artists and writers used to live, including Hermann Hesse in Gaienhofen and the painter Otto Dix in Hemmenhofen. Opposite are Swiss towns such as Steckborn and, at the mouth of the Rhine, Stein am Rhein with its painted facades.

## Three islands: Lindau, Mainau and Reichenau

| island | What defines them | Access | Fits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lindau | Old town with harbor, lion and lighthouse at the harbor entrance | Road bridge and railway embankment | Stroll through town, evenings at the harbor, location with train connection |
| Mainau | Park and gardens with castle, different flowers depending on the season | Footbridge from the mainland, ship | Day trip, garden lovers, families |
| Reichenau | Monastery island with Romanesque churches, vegetable cultivation, lots of peace and quiet | Road embankment from the mainland | Culture-loving, quiet cycling days |

**Lindau** is not a natural island, but a small town. The harbor entrance with the Bavarian Lion and the lighthouse is one of the most famous images of the lake. Lindau has two train stations, one on the island and one on the mainland in Reutin. Check your connection carefully to see where you get off.

**The Mainau** is a private park and palace complex and costs entry. Tulips and other bulbous flowers bloom in spring, roses in summer and dahlias in late summer. Plan a half to full day and come early on nice weekends because it gets crowded. Cars remain in the parking lot on the mainland.

**The Reichenau** is the largest island in the lake and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2000. The monastery was founded in the early Middle Ages and was an important center of illumination. Three Romanesque churches in the districts of Mittelzell, Oberzell and Niederzell have been preserved, with famous wall paintings in Oberzell. There are vegetable fields and greenhouses between the churches. The island is flat and the best way to explore it is by bike.

## On the water: ships and car ferries

On Lake Constance, the ship is a real means of transport. Liners connect the towns on Obersee and Überlinger See, and separate ships travel on the Untersee and the High Rhine to Schaffhausen. The lines mainly run from spring to autumn; in winter the offering is significantly smaller. Many guests combine the outward journey by ship and the return journey by train, bus or bike.

Two car ferries are important for drivers. The ferry between Konstanz and Meersburg saves you the long journey around Lake Überlingen and runs at short intervals all year round. A ferry to Switzerland runs between Friedrichshafen and Romanshorn. Both also accommodate pedestrians and cyclists.

This is how you plan the ship days:

1. **Load the current timetable.** Timetables change with the season. Check it on the shipping company's website, not in old brochures.
2. **Note the last return trip.** If you have to go back in the evening, first check when the last ship leaves and, if necessary, plan a replacement by train or bus.
3. **Think bicycles in good time.** It is possible to take bicycles on many ships, but it is limited and often subject to a charge.
4. **Check guest cards.** Some places issue guest cards that include bus, train or discounts. Check with your accommodation.
5. **Please note the weather.** Trips may be canceled in the event of a storm.

## Cycling on the shore

The Lake Constance cycle path leads around the lake through all three countries. It is well signposted and mostly flat, which is why it is also popular for families and occasional cyclists. You don't have to drive it as a big tour. Many choose a fixed location and make daily trips, taking a ship or train for the return journey. On nice weekends in summer the path near the towns is very busy. Then plan more time and drive considerately on promenades. We explain how you can take your bike with you by train or car or rent it locally in [Bicycle on Vacation](/reisen/fahrrad-im-urlaub/).

## Trips to Austria and Switzerland

For a day in Bregenz or Stein am Rhein you clarify these points beforehand:

- **ID:** Take your ID card or passport with you, even for short trips. Children need their own document.
- **Money:** In Austria you pay with euros, in Switzerland with Swiss francs. Cards are usually accepted, euros sometimes too, but you can often get change in francs.
- **Mobile phone:** Austria is part of the EU and the EU roaming rules apply there. Switzerland is not one of them, roaming can be expensive there. On the bank, your cell phone sometimes logs into a Swiss network on the German side. Check your tariff and select the network manually if necessary.
- **Customs:** Free allowances apply for goods from Switzerland. If you are planning larger purchases, check the current rules at customs.
- **Car:** You need a vignette for motorways in Austria and Switzerland. Check the current rules before entering the highway.
- **Health insurance:** The European Health Insurance Card on the back of your health card is also valid in Austria and Switzerland. Ask your health insurance company what it covers.
- **Tickets:** The Deutschlandticket is generally only valid in Germany. The timetable information will show you whether a cross-border section is included.

## Swimming in Lake Constance

The water is clear, but still fresh in early summer. Shallow bays and the lower lake warm up faster than the deep middle of the upper lake. For swimming, there are beach baths, which usually charge an entrance fee and often have a lifeguard, and freely accessible bathing areas.

The lake is also a drinking water reservoir for many people in Baden-Württemberg. So leave your soap and shampoo on land and take your rubbish with you. Keep your distance from harbor entrances, jetties and shipping channels. The rules of the Lake Constance Shipping Regulations apply to boats, canoes and stand-up paddling, ask the rental company.

The weather can change quickly on Lake Constance. There are flashing orange storm warning lights on many harbors and shorelines: slow flashing is a cautionary message, fast flashing is a storm warning. Then get out of the water and paddle back to shore. What else you should pay attention to when swimming in lakes can be found in [Swimming in Lakes and Rivers](/reisen/baden-in-seen-und-fluessen/).

## Choose the right location

The lake is too large to fully explore from one location. Choose your location according to your focus:

- **Konstanz** for city life, good train connections and short distances to Mainau, Reichenau and Switzerland.
- **Lindau** for island flair, the east of the lake and a detour to Bregenz.
- **Meersburg or Überlingen** for vineyards, promenades and many boat connections. Consider the steep streets in Meersburg and the lack of a train.
- **Friedrichshafen and the surrounding area** for a central location with a train station and flat cycling land.
- **Untersee and Höri** for peace, nature and rural accommodation, preferably by bike or car.

You can easily reach Lindau, Friedrichshafen, Radolfzell and Konstanz by train, and the Lake Constance Belt Railway runs along the north bank. In high season, accommodation on the shore is booked early. You can read which weeks are quieter in [Best travel time for vacation in Germany](/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Want to pack everything into one week.** If you visit a different shore every day, you spend more time in the car than on the water. Choose two or three focal points.
2. **Take the car for every excursion.** Parking spaces in riverside towns are scarce in summer. Ships, trains and bikes are often faster and more beautiful.
3. **Do not check the return journey.** The last ship leaves earlier than many people think.
4. **Forget roaming.** Switch off data roaming or check your tariff before your cell phone logs into Switzerland.
5. **Visit Mainau on Sunday lunchtime.** During the week and early in the day it is much quieter there.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do you need for Lake Constance?

A long weekend at a fixed location is enough to get a first impression. If you want to experience islands, bike tours and trips to neighboring countries, it's better to plan a week.

### Is Lake Constance suitable for families?

Yes. Flat cycle paths, beaches, boat trips and the Mainau make the lake varied for children. When choosing your accommodation, make sure it is in a quiet location and has a short distance to the water. For more waterfront destinations, see [Lakes and Rivers](/themen/seen-und-fluesse/).
