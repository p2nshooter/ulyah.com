Not every vacation has to last two weeks. A long weekend on the Baltic Sea, three days in a city or four days hiking in the low mountain ranges can be surprisingly relaxing, especially if they are well planned. In Germany, the holidays help with this: If you cleverly add one or two days of vacation in between, you can get many days off in a row from just a few days of vacation. This guide explains how bridging days work, which holidays apply where and how you can plan a short vacation without spending half of the time in traffic jams.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - A bridge day is a working day between a public holiday and the weekend. One day of vacation creates four days off in a row.
> - Classic bridging days occur after Ascension Day and Corpus Christi, which always fall on a Thursday.
> - Some public holidays only apply in certain federal states. Check which ones apply where you live.
> - On long weekends, streets, trains and accommodation are full. Book early and travel countercyclically.
> - For a short vacation, destinations with a short journey are worthwhile. More than three hours each way takes up too much time.

## This is how bridge days work

If a public holiday falls on a Thursday, only Friday lies between it and the weekend. If you take vacation this Friday, you have four days off, from Thursday to Sunday. If a public holiday falls on a Tuesday, the same applies to the Monday before.

You can get even more out of it when several holidays are close together, such as around Easter, May and Christmas. For example, with four days of vacation around Easter you get ten days off in a row because Good Friday and Easter Monday are national holidays.

## Nationwide holidays

These public holidays apply throughout Germany:

- New Year's Day on January 1st
- Good Friday and Easter Monday, depending on the date of Easter
- Labor Day on May 1st
- Ascension Day, always a Thursday, 39 days after Easter Sunday
- Whit Monday, 50 days after Easter Sunday
- German Unity Day on October 3rd
- Boxing Day on December 25th and 26th

## Regional holidays

There are also public holidays that only apply in individual federal states. A selection:

- **Three Kings** on January 6th in Baden-Württemberg, Bavaria and Saxony-Anhalt.
- **International Women's Day** on March 8th in Berlin and Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania.
- **Corpus Christi**, a Thursday 60 days after Easter Sunday, including in Baden-Württemberg, Bavaria, Hesse, North Rhine-Westphalia, Rhineland-Palatinate and Saarland.
- **Assumption of Mary** on August 15th in Saarland and parts of Bavaria.
- **World Children's Day** on September 20th in Thuringia.
- **Reformation Day** on October 31st in many northern and eastern German countries.
- **All Saints Day** on November 1st in Baden-Württemberg, Bavaria, North Rhine-Westphalia, Rhineland-Palatinate and Saarland.
- **Penitance and Prayer Day** in November in Saxony.

This is doubly important for short vacations: a public holiday where you live gives you a day off. A public holiday in a neighboring state, on the other hand, brings you full streets and holiday resorts because everyone is out and about there.

## The most popular bridge days

**Ascension Day:** The Thursday in May is free nationwide, the Friday after is one of the most popular vacation days of the year. The weather is often good and many people use the long weekend for their first trip to the sea. In 2027, Ascension Day falls on May 6th.

**Corpus Christi:** Also a Thursday, but only a holiday in some federal states. In these countries, the following Friday is a popular bridge day, and nearby excursion destinations are correspondingly full. In 2027, Corpus Christi falls on May 27th.

**Easter and Whitsun:** Around the Easter days and the Whitsun weekend there are long free periods with just a few days of vacation. In many federal states there are also school holidays during this time.

**German Unity Day and Christmas:** Depending on which day of the week they fall on, there are also bridge days in autumn and at the end of the year. For many people, the period between Christmas and New Year is a quiet time for a short vacation.

## Avoid traffic jams and crowds

On long weekends, millions of people want to go at the same time. You notice this on highways, on trains and at popular destinations. How to stay more relaxed:

- **Travel anti-cyclically:** On the holiday itself, travel early in the morning or in the afternoon instead of the evening before after work when everyone is out and about.
- **Postpone return journey:** If you can, you can only travel back on Monday instead of Sunday afternoon.
- **Train instead of car:** For many destinations, the train is the more relaxed choice on long weekends. But reserve seats because the trains are also full. More about this in the guide [Going on vacation by train](/reisen/mit-der-bahn-in-den-urlaub/).
- **Choose less well-known destinations:** Instead of the most famous seaside resort, a small town in the hinterland, instead of the most famous old town, a quieter neighboring town.
- **Book early:** Accommodation for Ascension and Pentecost on the coast often sells out months in advance and prices go up.

## Goals for three to four days

A short vacation depends on arriving quickly. As a rule of thumb, the journey should not take longer than three hours each way. Some ideas:

- **City trip:** Two to three days are enough to get a good impression of cities like Hamburg, Munich or Dresden. More in the guides [Discover Hamburg](/reisen/hamburg-entdecken/), [Munich](/reisen/muenchen-staedtereise/) and [Dresden](/reisen/dresden-staedtereise/).
- **Small old towns:** A long weekend in a half-timbered town with excursions into the surrounding area. Ideas in the guide [Small old towns in Germany] (/reisen/kleine-altstaedte/).
- **Baltic Sea:** Baltic Sea resorts can be quickly reached from many large cities in the north and east. More in the guide [The Baltic Sea coast from west to east] (/reisen/ostseekueste-regionen/).
- **Lakes and rivers:** A few days in the Spreewald, on the Mecklenburg Lake District or on the Middle Rhine. More in the guides [Spreewald](/reisen/spreewald/) and [Middle Rhine](/reisen/mittelrhein/).
- **Hiking in the low mountain ranges:** Harz, Eifel, Sauerland, Thuringian Forest or Saxon Switzerland are well suited for a few days at a fixed location.

## Make good use of your short vacation

- **Choose a location.** For three or four days, each change of accommodation takes too much time.
- **Less program.** Two highlights per day are enough. The rest is time to recover.
- **Book in advance:** Book restaurants, tours and admissions with time slots on long weekends in advance.
- **Pack light.** A small suitcase or backpack is enough for a few days. Tips in the guide [packing list for vacation in Germany] (/reisen/packliste-urlaub/).
- **Use the weather flexibly.** Plan for a rainy day. Ideas in the guide [Rainy days on vacation](/reisen/regentage-im-urlaub/).

## Three examples for a long weekend

**Ascension Day on the Baltic Sea:** Leave early on Thursday, arrive at midday and take your first walk on the beach. Friday a bike ride along the coast, Saturday a trip to a Hanseatic city like Wismar or Stralsund, Sunday sleep in, breakfast with a sea view and return in the early afternoon before the big return traffic begins.

**Three day city trip by train:** Arrive by train on Friday morning, leave your luggage at the hotel and explore the old town on foot. Saturday a large museum and a concert or theater in the evening. On Sunday a trip to the surrounding area or to a river, back by train in the evening.

**Hiking in the low mountain range:** Accommodation with a good breakfast as a fixed location, a circular hike of ten to fifteen kilometers every day, and a stop at the inn in the evening. If you arrive by train, choose a location with a train station and hiking trails right from your front door.

## Short vacation with children

With children, short trips are often even more relaxing than long ones: the journey is made quickly and no one gets homesick. Choose a destination with a playground, nature and water nearby and only plan one trip per day. Bridge days around Ascension Day and Corpus Christi often fall during school-free periods. More tips in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

## Spread vacation days throughout the year

Many people take their annual vacation in one go in the summer and hardly have any days left for the rest of the year. Recreation researchers, on the other hand, recommend not only taking longer vacations but also taking shorter breaks throughout the year. A plan at the beginning of the year helps:

1. **Mark holidays in the calendar** that apply where you live.
2. **Identify bridge days**, especially after Thursday holidays and before Tuesday holidays.
3. **Check school holidays** if you have children or want to avoid holiday periods. The dates differ between the federal states.
4. **Discuss with colleagues**, because many people want popular bridging days.
5. **Book accommodation early** for dates that are set.

More about holiday times and seasons in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

## Keep an eye on costs

Accommodation is more expensive on long weekends, and many properties require a minimum stay of three or four nights. Therefore, check whether a later weekend without a public holiday is cheaper. For families, holiday apartments are also worthwhile for short holidays if there is no high final cleaning fee. Tips for cost planning in the guide [Plan your holiday budget honestly] (/reisen/urlaubsbudget-planen/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Driving too far.** For three days, six hours of travel each way is too much.
2. **Leave on the Wednesday evening before Ascension Day.** That's when the highways are at their busiest.
3. **Book late.** Popular locations book up early on long weekends.
4. **Overlook regional holidays.** A holiday in a neighboring country means full excursion destinations.
5. **Overload the short vacation with a program.** Relaxation requires free time.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a bridge day?

A working day between a public holiday and the weekend. Anyone who takes vacation on this day combines the holiday and the weekend into several days off in a row.

### Which public holidays apply in my federal state?

The national holidays apply everywhere. Each federal state determines which regional holidays are added. The state governments publish the current lists.

### Is a short vacation even worth it?

Yes, if the journey is short and the program is not too busy. Just three days away from everyday life can be noticeably relaxing, especially as a complement to a longer vacation during the year. For more planning topics, see [Travel Planning](/themen/reiseplanung/).
