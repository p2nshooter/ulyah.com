Rügen is not an island that can be understood in a weekend. Germany's largest island has so many faces that a holiday in Binz feels completely different than a week in a fishing village on the Mönchgut or in a farmhouse in the quiet west. White chalk cliffs over turquoise water, piers with resort architecture, beech forests, bays with reed belts, a steam train and the car-free island of Hiddensee off the coast: Rügen offers something for almost every type of holiday. This guide will help you sort through the island and find the right location.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Rügen is the largest island in Germany and is connected to the mainland via the Rügen Dam and the Rügen Bridge near Stralsund. You don't need a ferry.
> - The most famous seaside resorts are on the east coast: Binz, Sellin, Baabe and Göhren, with piers and resort architecture.
> - The famous chalk cliffs are located in the Jasmund National Park, and the old beech forests there are part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site.
> - Cape Arkona in the north, the Mönchgut in the southeast and the lagoon landscape in the west are quieter and close to nature.
> - In high season, the roads to the island and the seaside resorts are often full. Train, bus and bike are good alternatives.

## Orientation: how Rügen is structured

Rügen has a rugged shape with many peninsulas, bays and bays, the shallow lagoons between the parts of the country. Anyone coming from Stralsund reaches the island in the southwest. The capital Bergen is roughly in the middle and is the most important train and bus hub.

Roughly five areas can be distinguished:

- **The east coast** with the large seaside resorts of Binz, Sellin, Baabe and Göhren and the long sandy beaches.
- **Jasmund** in the northeast with the national park, chalk cliffs and the port town of Sassnitz.
- **Wittow and Cape Arkona** in the far north, flat, windy and rural.
- **The Mönchgut** in the southeast, a peninsula with fishing villages, hills and beaches.
- **The West** with the lagoon landscape, small harbors and the ferry port to Hiddensee.

## The seaside resorts on the east coast

### Binz

Binz is the largest and most famous seaside resort on the island. The long beach promenade is lined with white villas with decorated balconies, the pier leads far into the sea, and the fine sandy beach stretches for kilometers to the north. Binz has many hotels, restaurants and shops and can be reached directly by train. In high season it is lively and crowded.

North of Binz lies **Prora**, a building complex over four kilometers long that was planned as a seaside resort for the organization “Kraft durch Freude” during the National Socialist era and was never completed. Today parts have been converted into holiday apartments and hotels, and a documentation center explains the history of the place.

### Sellin, Baabe and Göhren

**Sellin** is known for its pier with the striking bridge house and for a staircase that leads from the high bank down to the bridge. Wilhelmstrasse with its spa architecture houses is one of the most beautiful streets on the island. **Baabe** is smaller and family-friendly, with a fine beach and a quiet town center. **Göhren** is located on a ridge at the beginning of the Mönchgut and has two beaches, one north and one south of the headland.

The seaside resorts are connected by the **Rasender Roland** narrow-gauge railway, which commutes with steam locomotives between Lauterbach, Putbus, Binz, Sellin, Baabe and Göhren. It is not a fast means of transport, but it is an experience.

## Jasmund: chalk cliffs and beech forests

The Jasmund National Park is the most famous natural area in Rügen. Here, white chalk cliffs drop steeply down to the Baltic Sea, and old beech forests grow above them, which, along with other beech forests in Europe, are part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site. The painter Caspar David Friedrich made the chalk coast famous in the 19th century.

The most famous rock is the **Königsstuhl**. At the Königsstuhl National Park Center you can visit an exhibition and look out over the chalk coast via a viewing path. Plan your visit and come early on nice summer days because it gets crowded.

A hike on the **Hochuferweg** between Sassnitz and the Königsstuhl is often nicer and quieter. The path leads through the beech forest on the edge of the cliffs, with ever new views, such as the Victoria View. You should plan a few hours for the route. In some places you can take stairs down to the stone beach.

**Safety:** The chalk cliffs are unstable. Parts break off again and again, especially after frost and heavy rain. Stay away from the edge and the base of the rocks, and be aware of restricted areas.

**Sassnitz** is the gateway to the national park, with a city harbor, a long pier and the Mukran ferry port from which ships depart for Scandinavia.

## Cape Arkona and Wittow

In the far north lies **Kap Arkona** with two lighthouses, the older of which was built in the 19th century according to plans by Karl Friedrich Schinkel. Next to it are the remains of a Slavic castle rampart. From the cape a path leads down to the tiny fishing village **Vitt** with thatched houses and a small octagonal chapel.

Cars remain in a parking lot in Putgarten. From there you walk, cycle or take a small train to the cape. The Wittow peninsula is flat and agricultural, with long natural beaches on the north coast.

## The Mönchgut and the southeast

The Mönchgut is a hilly peninsula in the southeast with small towns such as Middelhagen, Groß Zicker, Klein Zicker and Thiessow. The landscape almost seems like a miniature version of Rügen: cliffs, hills with a wide view, the banks of the lagoon and bathing beaches. A large part belongs to the Southeast Rügen Biosphere Reserve. The Mönchgut is ideal for quiet holidays with hikes and bike tours.

Further west is **Putbus**, a classical residential town with white houses around a circular square, the circus, and a large castle park. The Racing Roland leaves the nearby **Lauterbach** and boats leave from the harbor to the small island of Vilm.

The **Granitz Hunting Lodge** sits on a hill in the forest between Binz and Sellin. From its tower you have a wide view over the island.

## The West and Hiddensee

The west of Rügen is the quiet side of the island. Bodden, reeds, small harbors and villages characterize the picture. Here you can cycle, paddle, sail or watch birds. In autumn, thousands of cranes rest in the region, as described in the guidebook [Seals, Cranes and Migratory Birds](/reisen/seehunde-und-zugvoegel/).

Ferries run from **Schaprode** to **Hiddensee**, a narrow, car-free island west of Rügen. Hiddensee is quiet, pristine and has long been popular with artists. The poet Gerhart Hauptmann lived here; his house in the monastery is now a museum. You can get around the island on foot, by bike or in a horse-drawn carriage. You can find out more about ferries and everyday life on the island in the guide [Taking the ferry to the island](/reisen/faehre-und-inselalltag/).

The Störtebeker Festival takes place in **Ralswiek** in summer, an open-air theater about the legendary pirate with ships, horses and fireworks.

## Choose the right location

| If you are looking... | then it's particularly suitable |
| --- | --- |
| Beach, promenade, restaurants, good train connections | Binz |
| Resort architecture with a little more peace and quiet | Sellin, Baabe |
| Hikes in the national park | Sassnitz, Lohme |
| Quiet holidays in small villages | Mönchgut, Wittow |
| Water sports and lagoon landscape | West Rügen, Wiek, Dranske |
| Car-free days | Hiddensee |

Rügen is big. Walks from one end to the other can take a long time during the season. Therefore, choose a location from which you can easily reach most of your desired destinations instead of trying to cover the entire island.

## Arrival and travel on the island

**By train:** Rügen is well connected to the rail network. Regional trains run from Stralsund to Bergen, Binz and Sassnitz, and long-distance trains also come to Binz during the season. There is a bus network on the island that connects most places, with busier service in season. The guide [Going on vacation with the Germany ticket] (/reisen/deutschlandticket-im-urlaub/) explains how to travel with the Germany ticket.

**By car:** You can reach the island via the Rügen Bridge without waiting for a ferry. In high season and on weekends, however, there are often traffic jams in front of the bridge and on the main road to Bergen and the seaside resorts. Many seaside resorts have large parking spaces on the outskirts.

**By bike:** Rügen has a good network of cycle paths, the Baltic Sea cycle path leads across the island. Note that unlike the North Sea, there are hills on Rügen, especially on Jasmund and the Mönchgut. You can rent e-bikes in many places. More tips can be found in the guide [Bicycle on Vacation](/reisen/fahrrad-im-urlaub/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Want to see the whole island in a few days.** Choose two or three focal points.
2. **On summer weekends by car to Königsstuhl.** It's more relaxed early in the morning or by bus and hike.
3. **Walk too close to the edge of the chalk.** Crashes happen all the time.
4. **Arrival on Saturday in high season.** This is when the traffic in front of the bridge is heaviest.
5. **Only know the east coast.** Mönchgut, Wittow and the west show the quiet side of the island.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do you need for Rügen?

A long weekend at a fixed location is enough to get a first impression. If you want to experience seaside resorts, the national park, Cape Arkona and Hiddensee, you better plan a week or more.

### Is Rügen suitable for families?

Yes. The flat sandy beaches of the east coast, the Racing Roland, boat trips and short hikes make the island varied for children. For families, Baabe, Göhren, Binz or a holiday farm in the interior of the island are ideal.

### When is the best time to travel to Rügen?

You can swim from around June to September. Spring and autumn are quieter and ideal for hiking, and in autumn the cranes come. More about this in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/). You can find more islands under [Islands](/themen/inseln/).
