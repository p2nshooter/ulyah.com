Dresden is a city of great contrasts. On one side of the Elbe there is a baroque old town that was rebuilt over decades after the destruction in the Second World War, with domes, towers and palaces that glow in the warm light in the evenings. On the other side is the Neustadt with its backyards, bars, street art and one of the liveliest trendy districts in eastern Germany. In between, the Elbe flows through wide meadows where historic paddle steamers sail. And half an hour away, Saxon Switzerland begins, one of the most impressive rocky landscapes in Europe. This guide will help you combine all of this in two to three days.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The most important sights in the old town are close together and can be easily reached on foot.
> - You need a timed ticket for the historic Green Vault in the Residenzschloss. Book it in advance.
> - The Neustadt on the other side of the Elbe is the district for cafés, pubs and evenings.
> - Historic paddle steamers travel on the Elbe to Pillnitz and Saxon Switzerland.
> - You can reach the foot of the Bastei in just over half an hour by S-Bahn.

## The old town

**The Frauenkirche** on Neumarkt is the symbol of Dresden. The baroque church with its mighty stone dome was destroyed in February 1945 and remained a ruin for decades. It was rebuilt with donations from all over the world and rededicated in 2005. Dark stones in the light facade come from the old church. The interior is bright and festive, and from the dome you have a wide view over the city and the Elbe. The church is a place of worship: viewing is restricted during services and concerts.

**The Zwinger** is a magnificent baroque complex with pavilions, galleries and a large inner courtyard. This is where the Old Masters Painting Gallery is located, whose most famous work is the Sistine Madonna by Raphael. Other museums show porcelain, scientific instruments and weapons.

**The Semperoper** on Theaterplatz is one of the most beautiful opera houses in the world. If you don't attend a performance, you can take a guided tour. Remaining tickets for performances are sometimes available at short notice.

**The Residenzschloss** houses several museums, including the **Green Vault**, one of the richest treasuries in Europe with jewels, goldsmiths' art and valuable works of art. The Historic Green Vault shows the treasures in the original baroque rooms. To visit you need a ticket with fixed entry.

The **Procession of Princes** runs along the outer wall of the stable yard, a mural made of Meissen porcelain tiles that is over a hundred meters long and shows the rulers of Saxony in a long equestrian procession.

## Brühl's terrace and banks of the Elbe

The **Brühlsche Terrasse** high above the Elbe is often referred to as the balcony of Europe. From here you can look across the river to the Neustadt and the Elbe meadows. A walk here is particularly beautiful in the evening, when the domes and towers of the old town are illuminated. The paddle steamers leave below the terrace.

A classic is the view from the Neustadt side of the Elbe to the old town silhouette, the so-called Canaletto view, named after the painter who captured it in the 18th century. It is most beautiful in the early evening.

## The New Town

You can reach the **Innere Neustadt** via the Augustus Bridge. The **Golden Rider**, a statue of August the Strong, shines at the Neustädter Markt. The main street leads north as a wide avenue.

Further north begins the **Äußere Neustadt**, the trendy district of the city. Here you will find small shops, cafes, international restaurants, pubs and music clubs. A highlight is the **Kunsthofpassage**, a series of inner courtyards with imaginatively designed facades. When it rains, water runs through a pipe system on the wall of a house and creates music.

Nearby is **Pfunds Molkerei**, a dairy shop from 1880 with hand-painted tiles that is considered one of the most beautiful shops in the world.

## With the paddle steamer on the Elbe

Dresden has a fleet of historic paddle steamers that is one of the oldest and largest of its kind in the world. During the season, the ships travel from the old town up and down the Elbe.

- **To Pillnitz:** Past the Elbe castles and under the **Blue Wonder**, a steel bridge from the 19th century, through to **Pillnitz Castle** with its park and the Chinese-inspired palace on the bank.
- **To Saxon Switzerland:** Up the Elbe to Rathen, Königstein or Bad Schandau through the rocky landscape.
- **To Meißen:** Down the Elbe to the porcelain city with castle and cathedral.

The journey upstream takes significantly longer than back. Many people travel there by boat and back by S-Bahn.

At the Blauer Wunder in Loschwitz, two historic mountain railways also run up the slopes of the Elbe: a funicular and a suspension railway, both with beautiful views over the valley.

## Excursions into the surrounding area

**Saxon Switzerland:** Bizarre sandstone cliffs rise out of the Elbe valley southeast of Dresden. The most famous destination is the **Bastei** with the stone Bastei bridge, from which you can look deep down onto the Elbe. You take the S-Bahn to the health resort of Rathen, take a small ferry across the Elbe and climb up a hiking trail. The **Königstein Fortress** on a table mountain is another worthwhile destination. Come early because the Bastei is very crowded on weekends.

**Moritzburg:** The Moritzburg hunting lodge is located like an island in a large pond north of the city. Many people know it as the film set for a popular Christmas fairy tale. A historic narrow-gauge railway with a steam locomotive runs there from Radebeul.

**Meißen:** The porcelain city with Albrechtsburg and cathedral can be reached by S-Bahn in around half an hour. More in the guide [Small Old Towns in Germany](/reisen/kleine-altstaedte/).

**Radebeul:** At the foot of the vineyards downstream of the Elbe are wineries where you can try Saxon wine.

## Eating in Dresden

- **Dresden Christstollen** is the most famous pastry in the city, especially during Advent.
- **Eierschecke** is a Saxon sheet cake with quark and a layer of egg cream, perfect for afternoon coffee.
- **Quarkkäulchen** are small fried quark pancakes with applesauce.
- **Sauerbraten** and dumplings are the hearty classics in traditional restaurants.
- **Saxon wine** from the Elbe Valley is a discovery, especially white wines.

In the Neustadt you will find a large selection of international, vegetarian and vegan cuisine.

## A look into history

Anyone who visits Dresden encounters history at every turn. Under August the Strong and his successors, the city became one of the most magnificent residences in Europe in the 18th century. In the final months of the Second World War, air raids in February 1945 destroyed large parts of the city center, killing tens of thousands of people. During the GDR era, some buildings such as the Zwinger and Semperoper were restored, but the Frauenkirche remained a ruin as a memorial. It was only after reunification that the Neumarkt with its town houses was rebuilt. These layers of splendor, destruction and reconstruction still characterize the city today. Exhibitions in the Frauenkirche and in the city museums tell the story.

## Out and about in the city

Dresden has a good tram network with which you can reach all districts. The Deutschlandticket is valid on trams, buses and S-Bahn trains in the region; you need your own tickets for the paddle steamers and the mountain railways. The Old Town is compact and best explored on foot. You can easily cycle along the river on the Elbe cycle path. More about this in the guide [Bicycle on Vacation](/reisen/fahrrad-im-urlaub/).

Dresden has two large train stations: the main train station south of the old town and the Neustadt train station. The guide [On vacation by train](/reisen/mit-der-bahn-in-den-urlaub/) explains how you can travel comfortably by train.

## A plan for two to three days

**Day 1: Old Town**

- Morning: Frauenkirche with dome climb, Neumarkt, procession of princes.
- Lunch: Lunch in the old town.
- Afternoon: Zwinger with picture gallery or residential palace with green vault.
- Evening: Walk across the Brühlsche Terrasse, concert or opera.

**Day 2: Neustadt and Elbe**

- Morning: Over the Augustus Bridge to the Golden Rider and into the Outer Neustadt with the Kunsthofpassage.
- Afternoon: Paddle steamer to Pillnitz, back by bus and tram or boat.
- Evening: Eating and going out in the Neustadt.

**Day 3: Saxon Switzerland**

- Take the S-Bahn to the spa town of Rathen, hike to the Bastei, continue to Königstein or Bad Schandau, return by train or boat.

The guide [plan a city weekend] (/reisen/staedtewochenende-planen/) explains how you can generally plan a city weekend.

## Travel time

- **Spring and autumn** are ideal for the city and Saxon Switzerland: pleasant temperatures, less crowds.
- **Summer** is high season for paddle steamers and Elbe meadows, with open-air events on the river.
- **Advent** is Striezelmarkt time, when the city is full of lights and visitors. More about this in the guide [Visit Christmas markets in Germany] (/reisen/weihnachtsmaerkte/).
- **Winter** is quiet, ideal for museums and concerts.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Head for the Green Vault without a timed ticket.** Admissions are limited and often fully booked.
2. **Only see the old town.** The new town shows a completely different Dresden.
3. **Go to the Bastei at midday on weekends.** Start early or choose a weekday.
4. **Want to take the steamer back up the Elbe.** The journey against the current takes a long time.
5. **If you want to visit the Frauenkirche during a service.** Check the opening times.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do you need for Dresden?

Two days are enough for the old town and new town. A third day includes Saxon Switzerland, which you definitely shouldn't miss.

### Is Dresden interesting with children?

Yes. Paddle steamer rides, the mountain railways in Loschwitz, Moritzburg with the narrow-gauge railway, the zoo and hikes in Saxon Switzerland delight children. Tips in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

### Can Dresden be combined with Berlin?

Good. The two cities are around two hours apart by train. Tips for the capital in the guide [Berlin on your first visit](/reisen/berlin-erster-besuch/). You can find more cities under [City Trips](/themen/staedtereisen/).
