“When is the best time to go?” This question arises as soon as the vacation request is due. The answer depends less on the weather than many people think, because no one can promise you sun in Germany. You can influence something else: whether you arrive in the middle of the high season or in a quiet week before, whether your weekend in Cologne happens to coincide with a big trade fair and whether there are still beach chairs on the Baltic Sea beach in October. This guide will show you how the coast, lakes and cities change over the course of the year, why the school holidays are different every year and how you can use a short checklist to find travel days that suit you.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - There is no generally best time to travel. There is the best time for your destination and what you plan to do there.
> - The coast thrives on summer, but often feels most relaxed in late spring and early fall.
> - At lakes the season runs from around Easter to October. Tour boats and rental companies often operate to a limited extent outside of this time.
> - Cities go all year round. There, trade fairs, festivals and holidays determine crowds and room prices, not the weather.
> - The summer holidays begin at a different date in each federal state and rotate from year to year. The holiday calendars of the countries are binding.

## Seasons by destination: what really changes

### On the coast

On the North and Baltic Seas, the actual season extends roughly from Easter to the autumn holidays. That's when the beach chairs are set up, lifeguards guard the swimming areas in many places, and July and August are the busiest months. The sea warms up more slowly than the air and also gives off heat more slowly. That's why the water is often most pleasant only in late summer, when the evenings are getting cooler.

Spring brings long, bright days, rapeseed blooms in May in Schleswig-Holstein and Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, and the places are not yet full. But for most people the water is too cold for swimming. Autumn is the time for space, wind and bird migration. In winter it becomes quiet and the time of storm surges begins on the North Sea.

### At lakes

Lakes follow a similar rhythm. In spring the fruit trees bloom on Lake Constance, in summer people swim, paddle and sail, and in autumn the shores change color. Shallow lakes warm up faster than deep ones, so a small lake in Brandenburg can be pleasant in June, while a deep alpine lake in Upper Bavaria still remains cool. There are often a lot of mosquitoes in early summer in water-rich, swampy areas such as the Spreewald or the Mecklenburg Lake District. In winter, most boat rentals are closed and many ships only sail to a limited extent or not at all.

### In cities

Cities don’t actually have an off-season. Spring and autumn are good for long walks. In midsummer, the heat builds up in city centers, and during Advent, Christmas markets attract a lot of guests on weekends. January and February are quiet, and theaters and concert halls have full seasons.

## School holidays: why they are different every year

In Germany, each federal state sets its own school holidays. To ensure that not all families leave at the same time, the states coordinate long-term arrangements during the summer holidays via the Conference of Ministers of Education and Cultural Affairs. The countries are divided into groups and the groups swap places from year to year. A country that starts at the end of June one year will not have its turn until July a few years later. The summer holidays are spread over a total of around three months.

Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg are exceptions. They traditionally have late summer holidays, starting around the end of July or beginning of August and extending into September. The remaining holidays, i.e. Easter, Whitsun, autumn and winter holidays, are largely regulated by each country alone. Bavaria and Baden-Württemberg, for example, have longer Whitsun holidays, while some countries have no winter holidays at all.

For your planning this means:

1. **Know your own holidays.** Families with school children are bound to this. The state ministries of education publish the binding dates.
2. **See other people's holidays.** When a populous country like North Rhine-Westphalia starts its summer holidays, you can clearly feel it on the highways, on the trains and on the coasts. If only a few countries are on holiday in your desired week, it will be more relaxed everywhere.
3. **Use the edges.** If you're not tied to vacation, the quietest summer weeks often come at the very beginning or end of the entire vacation period.

## Public holidays and bridge days

There are nine public holidays nationwide: New Year's Day, Good Friday, Easter Monday, May 1st, Ascension Day, Whit Monday, German Unity Day on October 3rd and Christmas Day and Boxing Day. There are also public holidays that only apply in individual countries, such as Epiphany, Corpus Christi, Reformation Day and All Saints' Day. What these are in your destination is stated in the holiday regulations of the respective country.

The movable holidays in spring are particularly important for short vacations. Ascension Day and Corpus Christi always fall on a Thursday. With Friday as a bridge day, there are four days off, and many people take advantage of that. On these weekends and over Easter and Pentecost, coastal towns, lakeshores and popular cities are often as crowded as in midsummer. Trains are particularly in demand on Wednesday afternoons and Sunday evenings, as there are traffic jams on the motorways.

If you want to take advantage of bridging days, book early and, if possible, arrive on Wednesday morning or Thursday afternoon. And remember the holiday calm: Supermarkets and most shops are closed on public holidays. Shop ahead if you live in a vacation rental.

## Low season on the coast: peace with small catches

Many regular guests swear by the time before and after the big holidays. The beaches are empty, you can still find accommodation even at short notice, and the prices are often lower than in high season. In spring and autumn, large flocks of migratory birds rest in the Wadden Sea, and in autumn cranes gather in Western Pomerania. We explain how you can observe them without disturbing them in [Seals, cranes and migratory birds](/reisen/seehunde-und-zugvoegel/).

However, you should know the catches:

- **Beach chairs** are taken down after the season in many places. A windbreaker or warm clothing then replaces them.
- **Guarded swimming areas** are usually only available in high season. Without a lifeguard, you swim at your own risk; special caution is required in cold water.
- **Restaurants, cafés and small shops** sometimes have company holidays or open less frequently in quiet months.
- **Ferries, excursion boats and buses** run on a lighter schedule. During storms, crossings may be canceled.

Before booking, ask what is open during your week and choose a larger place with year-round life in winter rather than a small village where most of the houses are empty. Weather warnings are published by the German Weather Service and information on water levels and storm surges by the Federal Maritime and Hydrographic Agency.

## Big events that fill cities

In cities, it's not the season but the calendar that determines how crowded it gets. These are the types of events you should know:

- **Trade fairs.** Frankfurt am Main, Hanover, Cologne, Düsseldorf, Berlin, Munich, Leipzig and Nuremberg are large trade fair cities. During large trade fairs, hotels are scarce and significantly more expensive, even in surrounding areas.
- **Folk festivals.** The Oktoberfest in Munich and the Cannstatter Wasen in Stuttgart attract guests from all over the world. Accommodations are then allocated early. More about this in [Munich away from the Oktoberfest](/reisen/muenchen-staedtereise/).
- **Carnival.** In Cologne, Düsseldorf and Mainz, the great days around Rose Monday are an exception that you can love or avoid.
- **Christmas markets.** On Advent weekends, large markets and the trains there are very full. How you can experience them in a relaxed manner can be found in [Visit Christmas markets in Germany](/reisen/weihnachtsmaerkte/).
- **Port and sailing festivals.** The harbor birthday in Hamburg in May and the Kiel Week in June are among the best known.

Before every city trip, check the event and trade fair calendar on the official website of the city or the trade fair company, as the dates change from year to year.

## Checklist: this is how you find your travel days

Go through these steps with a calendar next to you:

1. **Clarify the main goal.** Swimming, cycling, museums or just peace and quiet? This narrows down the appropriate months more than any weather rule.
2. **Determine the time window.** Check the monthly overview to see when your goal for this activity fits well.
3. **Compare holidays.** Check your own holidays and those of the major federal states in the same period.
4. **Mark public holidays.** Enter public holidays and bridge days, including those that only apply in the target country.
5. **Check events.** Take a look at the trade fair and event calendar for cities.
6. **Check the low season.** Ask whether ferries, boats, beach chair rentals and restaurants are operating during your week.
7. **Arrange arrival and departure times.** A Tuesday or Wednesday is almost always more relaxed than the first day of the holiday or a Saturday.
8. **Plan buffer.** Set aside a day for bad weather. You can find ideas for this in [Rainy Day on Vacation](/reisen/regentage-im-urlaub/).

An example: A couple from Leipzig without children would like to swim and cycle on the Baltic Sea. A good time is the second half of June, before the holidays begin in many countries, or the first half of September, when school is back in session in the north and the water is often still pleasant.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Bet on good weather.** No month is guaranteed to have sun. Plan a vacation that works even in wind and rain.
2. **Arrive on the first day of vacation.** This is when the streets and trains are the most crowded. A day later often saves hours and nerves.
3. **Bridge days overlooked.** A short trip over Ascension Day is not an insider tip. Book early or choose another weekend.
4. **Book without looking at the trade fair calendar.** A supposedly quiet weekend in a trade fair city can be the most expensive of the year.
5. **No questions asked in low season.** If you are going to a small island in November, clarify ferries and opening times beforehand.
6. **Confusing air and water.** A warm June day doesn't mean the sea is warm. Always expect refreshing water on the coast and lakes.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the sea on the North and Baltic Seas warmest?

Usually in late summer because the water slowly stores the summer heat. How warm it gets in a particular year depends on the weather in the weeks before and the wind. Many seaside resorts and rescue stations display the current water temperature on the beach.

### Where can I find the exact vacation and public holiday dates?

The state ministries of education publish the mandatory school holidays, and the Conference of Education Ministers provides an overview. Every country regulates public holidays in its public holiday law. We have put together further official sources for timetables, tides and weather in [Travel Check](/reisecheck/).
