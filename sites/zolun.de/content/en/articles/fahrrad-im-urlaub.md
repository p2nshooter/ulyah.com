Germany is an excellent country for cycling holidays. Well-signposted long-distance cycle paths follow rivers and coasts, quiet side roads connect small towns, and almost everywhere there are accommodations that are geared towards cyclists. Whether you ride along a river for a week or go on day trips from the holiday resort: by bike you can experience a landscape more intensively than in a car and still go further than on foot. This guide will help you plan, from the route to luggage to the rules of the road.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - River cycle paths are flat and well signposted, ideal for beginners and families.
> - Plan daily stages of 40 to 60 kilometers, significantly less with children.
> - Accommodations with bicycle storage, drying room and tools make the trip more relaxing. Many organizers offer luggage transport.
> - E-bikes can be rented in almost all holiday regions, even for individual days.
> - To ride your bike on the train you need a reservation on long-distance trains, and on local trains you usually need your own bike card.

## Which type of cycling holiday is right for you?

**Distance tour:** You drive to the next town every day and spend the night somewhere else. This is the classic cycle tour along a river or coast. It requires more planning, but offers new impressions every day.

**Sternfahrt:** You live in a fixed location and go on day trips from there. This is more relaxed because the luggage stays in the accommodation and is particularly suitable for families and regions with many destinations nearby, such as islands, lake landscapes or the outskirts of a city.

**Combination:** Many people combine both: a few days in one place, then a stage further to the next location.

## Well-known long-distance cycle routes

Germany has a dense network of long-distance cycle routes. Many are tested by cycling associations and rated with stars. A selection:

- **Elbe Cycle Path:** One of the most popular cycle paths in Germany, from the Czech border through the Elbe Sandstone Mountains, past Dresden and Meißen to the North Sea.
- **Weser cycle path:** Flat, quiet and well developed, from Hann. Münden through the Weserbergland to Cuxhaven.
- **Danube Cycle Path:** From the source in the Black Forest through Swabia and Bavaria to Passau and on to Austria.
- **Mosel cycle path:** Along the vineyards between Trier and Koblenz, with many small wine towns.
- **Rhine cycle path:** Along the Middle Rhine past castles and rocks, more about this in the guide [Middle Rhine](/reisen/mittelrhein/).
- **Baltic Sea Coast Cycle Path:** From Flensburg to Usedom, with beach, cliffs and seaside resorts.
- **North Sea Coast Cycle Path:** Behind the dike with a view of mudflats and meadows, often with strong winds.
- **Lake Constance cycle path:** Once around the lake through Germany, Austria and Switzerland. More in the guide [Lake Constance](/reisen/bodensee/).

The best way to cycle along river cycle paths is downstream. The gradient is small, but you often have the prevailing wind at your back.

## Realistic daily stages

Many people underestimate how much time a day of cycling takes: breaks, sightseeing, photos, detours and stops are all part of it.

| Who drives | Recommended stage per day |
| --- | --- |
| Casual cyclists without an e-bike | 40 to 50 km |
| Experienced cyclists | 60 to 80 km |
| With e-bike | 50 to 80 km, depending on battery and terrain |
| Family with children aged around eight and over | 20 to 35 km |
| Family with trailer or child seat | 25 to 40 km |

After three to four days of driving, plan a rest day where you only drive briefly or not at all. On the coast and on open stretches, the wind determines how strenuous a stage will be. Headwinds can turn a flat route into a mountain stage.

## Accommodation for cyclists

On cycling trips you need accommodation that can be booked for one night and can accommodate your bike safely. Many companies specialize in this and carry a corresponding quality mark. Typical services:

- lockable room for bicycles,
- drying room for wet clothes,
- tools and repair kit,
- hearty breakfast and packed lunch,
- Information about routes and workshops.

During the season, good accommodations on popular cycle paths are quickly booked out. For route tours, reserve nights in advance. More about the types of accommodation in the guide [Hotel, guesthouse, holiday apartment or farm?] (/reisen/hotel-pension-ferienwohnung/).

## Luggage: carry or have transported?

**Carry it yourself:** You'll get by fine with two luggage bags on the rear wheel and a small handlebar bag. Pack as light as possible: functional clothing that dries quickly, a rain jacket, a warm layer for the evening and the essentials for personal care.

**Have it transported:** Many tour operators and some accommodations will take your luggage from hotel to hotel. You only travel with a day bag. This is more convenient, especially for families and on hilly routes, and can often be booked without a package holiday.

## E-bike and rental bike

E-bikes have changed cycling travel. Climbs, headwinds and longer stages become easier, and mixed groups with different levels of fitness ride together more relaxed.

- **Plan battery:** Range depends on weight, terrain, wind and assistance level. Charge the battery every evening and ask the accommodation where this is possible.
- **Rent instead of taking with you:** There are bicycle and e-bike rentals in almost all holiday regions, often with a child seat, trailer and helmet. Some providers allow you to return the item to another location.
- **Take a test ride:** If you've never ridden an e-bike before, get used to the acceleration and weight on a quiet route.

## The bike on the train and ferry

- **Long-distance transport:** Bicycles are only allowed on trains with a bicycle compartment, and you need a bicycle card with a parking space reservation. During the season the places are fully booked early.
- **Local transport:** You usually need your own bike card; the Germany ticket is not valid for bikes in most regions. You can refuse to take your bike with you.
- **Ferries:** You can take your bike with you on many ferries to the islands and across rivers for a fee. However, on some car-free islands, owning bikes is unnecessary or impractical because rental bikes are available on site.

More about traveling by train in the guide [On vacation by train](/reisen/mit-der-bahn-in-den-urlaub/).

## Road traffic rules

- **Light:** Your bike needs working front and rear lights as well as reflectors.
- **Bike paths:** If a bike path is marked with a blue sign, you must use it. You can usually drive on the street without a sign.
- **Helmet:** There is no obligation to wear a helmet in Germany, but a helmet is strongly recommended.
- **Alcohol:** You can also commit a criminal offense if you ride drunk on your bike. After the beer garden, leave the bike in doubt.
- **Children:** Children up to eight years old must ride on the sidewalk, children up to ten are allowed. An accompanying adult may then also use the sidewalk.
- **In the forest:** The rules for cycling in the forest differ between the federal states. In Baden-Württemberg, for example, cycling is generally not permitted on paths less than two meters wide.

## Cycling tours with children

- **Short stages** with destinations that delight children: playground, swimming lake, ice cream parlor, farm.
- Choose **low-traffic routes**, especially river cycle paths and paths on old railway lines.
- **Trailer or child seat** for small children, tandem pole or trailer for children who cannot drive far yet.
- **Plan for lots of breaks and snacks**. Tired children on bikes are a safety risk.

Further tips in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

## Navigation and signage

Most long-distance cycle routes are consistently signposted with their own symbols, often additionally with green and white signposts that indicate distances to the nearest towns. Nevertheless, a map or a navigation app is still worthwhile as an addition, especially in the case of diversions due to construction sites or flooding.

- **Load offline maps:** In rural areas and river valleys, cell phone reception is not good everywhere.
- **Mobile phone holder on the handlebars:** This way you can see the route without stopping. A power bank extends the battery.
- **Paper cycling maps** are available from tourist information centers and show elevation profiles, accommodation and workshops.
- **Check elevation:** Away from the rivers, a seemingly short stage can have a lot of inclines.

## Weather on the bike trip

Plan for any cycling trip with rain. A good rain jacket, waterproof panniers and overshoes make a wet day bearable. During a thunderstorm, seek shelter in a building, not under individual trees. On hot days you start early, take your lunch break in the shade and drink more than you think.

## Packing list for the bike trip

- Repair kit, spare tube, tire lever and air pump.
- Multitool and some chain oil.
- Good lock, preferably a U-lock or folding lock.
- Rain jacket and rain pants, gloves and a warm layer.
- Padded cycling shorts or saddle cover for longer tours.
- Sunscreen, water bottles and snacks.
- First aid kit and cell phone with map app and battery.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Plan stages that are too long.** Better to be shorter and with time for breaks and sightseeing.
2. **Without reservation on the ICE.** The bike is not allowed without a parking space.
3. **Forget the wind.** On the coast it determines the pace and mood.
4. **Set off with heavy luggage.** You feel every kilo on the mountain.
5. **Being on the road without a repair kit.** A breakdown far from town can cost you the whole day.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which cycle path is suitable for beginners?

River cycle paths such as the Weser, Elbe or Moselle cycle paths are flat, well signposted and have many accommodations and train stations where you can break off.

### What do I do if I break down on the road?

You can usually repair a flat tire in fifteen minutes with a spare tube and pump. In the event of major damage, bicycle repair shops in the towns along the cycle path can help, and many prefer to repair bikes for travelers. Some rental companies and breakdown services will pick you up by bike. Before you travel, make note of workshops along the route.

### Do I need an e-bike?

Not necessarily. On flat river cycle paths, most people get along well without it. An e-bike is a great help in hilly regions, in headwinds on the coast or in groups with different levels of fitness.

### When is the best time for a cycling holiday?

May, June and September are ideal: pleasant temperatures, long days and less activity than during the summer holidays. More in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/). For more planning topics, see [Travel Planning](/themen/reiseplanung/).
