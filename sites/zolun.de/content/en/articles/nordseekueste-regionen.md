On the map, the German North Sea coast looks like a long, uniform line of dykes, meadows and mudflats. Anyone who travels there quickly notices how different the regions are. In East Frisia there are windmills and tea rooms, in Cuxhaven huge container ships pass by on the Elbe, in Dithmarschen cabbage fields stretch to the horizon, and in North Frisia you can see from dykes on Halligen, which lie like small ships in the sea. This guide introduces you to the regions from the mainland so that you can find the place that suits you. The islands off the coast have their own advisors.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The mainland coast extends from the mouth of the Ems at the Dutch border to the Danish border and is roughly divided into East Frisia, the region around Jade Bay and Weser, the Elbe-Weser triangle with Cuxhaven, Dithmarschen and North Frisia.
> - On the mainland, mudflats are the norm. Wide sandy beaches like in St. Peter-Ording are the exception; you often swim on green beaches or small sandy sections in front of the dike.
> - Small settlements with cutter harbors characterize the coast. They are also the ferry ports to many islands.
> - The entire area is located in the Wadden Sea National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
> - You can easily reach many places by train, but for small villages you need a bus, bike or car.

## How the coast is structured

Almost everywhere on the German North Sea coast a dike protects the flat land from the sea. Behind the dike lies the marsh, fertile, low land, some of which lies below sea level. In front of the dike there are salt marshes and mudflats, which dry up when the water is low. The guide [Understanding ebb and flow] (/reisen/ebbe-und-flut/) explains how the tides work.

The siege locations are typical. A sluice is a passage in the dike through which water flows from the hinterland into the sea. Small harbors have been created on many sluices, where shrimp boats now moor and ferries leave for the islands. These places usually end in “-siel” and often have a special charm.

Bathing beaches on the mainland are often green beaches, i.e. meadows on the dike, or sandy beaches with beach chairs. Swimming takes place around floods. When the tide is low, the sea is often far out. If you're looking for wide sandy beaches with surf, you're more likely to find them on the islands or in St. Peter-Ording.

## East Frisia: mills, tea and cutter harbors

East Frisia lies in the northwest of Lower Saxony between the mouth of the Ems and the Jade Bay. The region is known for its tea culture, red brick houses, windmills and inland fen canals. Off the coast lie the seven inhabited East Frisian Islands, which are presented in the guide [The East Frisian Islands in comparison](/reisen/ostfriesische-inseln/).

Well-known places on the coast:

- **Greetsiel:** A picturesque fishing village with a cutter harbor and two windmills at the entrance to the town, the so-called twin mills. Very popular with day trippers during the day.
- **North-Norddeich:** Larger town with ferry port to Norderney and Juist, beach and the National Park House seal station. Easily accessible by train.
- **Neßmersiel, Dornumersiel, Bensersiel, Neuharlingersiel, Harlesiel:** A chain of small villages with harbors, beach sections and ferries to Baltrum, Langeoog, Spiekeroog and Wangerooge.
- **Carolinensiel:** Pretty place with a historic museum harbor on the Harle.

East Frisia is well suited for quiet holidays, cycling tours on flat paths and trips to islands. If you come for the first time, you shouldn't miss a tea ceremony with Kluntje, i.e. rock candy, and cream.

## Jade Bay, Wilhelmshaven and Butjadingen

East of East Frisia, the Jade Bay cuts deep into the country. On its western side lies Wilhelmshaven, a port town with a southern beach, marine museum and the Wadden Sea visitor center. On the south bank is Dangast, a small seaside resort with a traditional spa house that is known for its rhubarb cake.

The Butjadingen peninsula between the Jade Bay and the Weser is rural and quiet. Places like Tossens, Burhave and Eckwarderhörne have beaches and campsites, and Fedderwardersiel has a small cutter harbor. The region is suitable for families who like it relaxed and for cyclists.

## The Elbe-Weser triangle and Cuxhaven

Between the Weser and the Elbe lies the Wurster North Sea coast with places like Wremen and Dorum. It's rural here, with dikes, cutter harbors and mudflat hikes.

**Bremerhaven** on the Weser is a large city with maritime museums, including the German Emigration Center and the Climate House, which makes the world's climate zones tangible. A good trip on rainy days.

**Cuxhaven** at the mouth of the Elbe is one of the largest seaside resorts in Germany. The districts of Duhnen, Döse and Sahlenburg have sandy beaches with beach chairs, and the Kugelbake is the city's landmark. At the Alte Liebe viewing platform you can watch ships from all over the world on their way to Hamburg. When the tide is low, mudflat wagons, horse-drawn carriages, travel through the mudflats to the island of Neuwerk, which belongs to Hamburg. Guided mudflat hikes are also offered there.

## Dithmarschen: cabbage, crabs and wide marshes

North of the Elbe begins Schleswig-Holstein and with it Dithmarschen. The region is agricultural, famous for cabbage cultivation. In late summer, the people of Dithmarschen celebrate their Cabbage Days.

**Büsum** is the most famous seaside resort in the region, with a harbor, family lagoon and mudflat hikes. **Friedrichskoog** has a seal station where orphaned young animals are raised. **Meldorf** with its cathedral is located somewhat inland and is a nice excursion.

Dithmarschen is down-to-earth, less pretentious than some island towns and often cheaper.

## North Frisia: Halligen, lighthouses and St. Peter-Ording

North Frisia is the northernmost region of the German North Sea coast. Here are the North Frisian Islands and Halligen, which the guide [Sylt, Föhr, Amrum, Pellworm and the Halligen] (/reisen/nordfriesische-inseln/) presents.

**St. Peter-Ording** on the Eiderstedt peninsula is an exception on the mainland coast with its kilometer-long, very wide sandy beach. The stilt houses, restaurants and beach huts on stilts in the sand are typical. The place is popular with families, kiters and beach sailors. In some parts of the beach you are allowed to drive your car onto the beach and park there, which comes as a surprise to many.

**Westerhever** has one of the most famous lighthouses in Germany, which stands alone on a terp in the salt marshes. **Tönning** on the Eider is home to the Multimar Wattforum, an adventure center about the national park.

**Husum** is the “gray city by the sea”, as the poet Theodor Storm, who was born here, called it. In spring, millions of crocuses bloom in the castle park. The harbor reaches right into the middle of the city.

Further north are **Nordstrand**, a peninsula with a ferry connection to Pellworm, **Schlüttsiel** with ships to the Halligen and **Dagebüll**, the ferry port to Föhr and Amrum. Shortly before the Danish border is **Niebüll**, from where the car trains to Sylt leave.

## What comes to the table on the coast

Regional cuisine is part of a holiday on the North Sea. The most famous dish is the crab roll. The small North Sea crabs, actually shrimps, are cooked directly on board the cutters and sold fresh in many ports, sometimes still unpulled. Pulen is an art of its own that locals are happy to show you. There are also herring, plaice and, depending on the season, mussels.

In East Frisia, tea time is a ritual. The strong East Frisian tea is poured over a piece of rock candy, then a spoonful of cream is carefully added, which rises as a cloud in the tea. Traditionally there is no stirring. In North Frisia you might order a Pharisee in a café, a coffee with rum under a cream topping, and a piece of Frisian cake made from puff pastry, plum jam and cream. In Dithmarschen, a lot of things revolve around cabbage in autumn, from stuffed cabbage to creative new dishes.

Many farm shops behind the dike also sell lamb from dike sheep, cheese, potatoes and fruit from the marsh. A detour is worth it, especially if you have a holiday apartment with a kitchen.

## Arrival and mobility

Many coastal towns can be reached by train, such as Norddeich Mole, Wilhelmshaven, Bremerhaven, Cuxhaven, Büsum, Husum, Dagebüll Mole and St. Peter-Ording. For smaller towns you often need the bus, which doesn't always run frequently. Check the connections beforehand. The guide [On vacation with the Germany ticket](/reisen/deutschlandticket-im-urlaub/) explains how you can get to the coast with the Germany ticket, and general tips for traveling by train can be found in [On vacation by train](/reisen/mit-der-bahn-in-den-urlaub/).

The bicycle is the ideal means of transport locally. The coast is flat, there are many cycle paths on and behind the dike, and the North Sea Coast Cycle Path runs along the entire coast. But pay attention to the wind, which often blows strongly along the coast. More about this in the guide [Bicycle on Vacation](/reisen/fahrrad-im-urlaub/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Expect wide sandy beaches everywhere.** On the mainland, mudflats are the norm. Find out beforehand what the beach looks like where you are.
2. **Plan without a tide calendar.** Swimming, mudflat hiking and some ferries depend on the tides.
3. **Do not check bus connections.** Small towns are often harder to reach without a car.
4. **Only want to come when the sun is shining.** The North Sea is beautiful even when it is windy and cloudy. Pack weatherproof clothing.
5. **Forget the tourist tax.** Many places charge it. More about this in [Beach chair, tourist tax and beach sections](/reisen/strandkorb-und-kurtaxe/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is the most beautiful place on the North Sea coast for swimming?

St. Peter-Ording, Cuxhaven and many islands are particularly suitable for classic bathing days with sand. In many other places you can swim on green beaches or small sandy beaches, ideally around high water. The flat areas are often ideal for children.

### Is the North Sea also worth it outside of the summer holidays?

Yes. Spring and fall are quieter, often cheaper and ideal for walks, bike rides and bird watching. In autumn, large flocks of birds also migrate over the coast.

### Which region is cheapest?

This depends heavily on the season and accommodation. Places on the mainland, such as in Dithmarschen, Butjadingen or on the Wurster Coast, are often cheaper than popular island locations. You can find the direct comparison between the seas in the guide [North Sea or Baltic Sea?](/reisen/nordsee-oder-ostsee/) and other coastal topics under [North Sea & Baltic Sea](/themen/nordsee-und-ostsee/).
