For many people, their dog is a member of the family and going on vacation without them is out of the question. Germany is a good travel destination for this: many holiday apartments accept dogs, the coast has its own dog beaches, in many restaurants the dog lies under the table as a matter of course, and hiking trails lead through forests and across meadows. At the same time, there are rules that you should know so that the vacation remains relaxed for people, dogs and everyone else. This guide summarizes the most important things.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Ask each accommodation explicitly whether and how many dogs are allowed and how much it costs.
> - Dogs are prohibited on many beaches during the season. There are designated dog beaches for this.
> - During the breeding and breeding season in spring and early summer, leashes are required in forests and fields in many regions.
> - The dog must be secured in the car. In the summer he is never allowed to stay in a parked car.
> - Be aware of heat, blue-green algae in lakes, salt water and grazing animals in the mountains.

## Find the right accommodation

Not every accommodation that allows dogs is also dog-friendly. A few questions will help you choose:

- **How many and which dogs are allowed?** Some hosts only accept small dogs or just one dog.
- **How ​​much does the dog cost?** There is usually a fee per night, but sometimes there is a higher final cleaning fee.
- **Is there a fenced garden?** This makes mornings and evenings much more relaxing.
- **How ​​far is it to the nearest exercise area** and the nearest dog beach?
- **Is the accommodation on a busy road?** This is a risk for dogs with hunting instincts.

Holiday apartments and holiday homes are often the best choice for dog owners because you have your own times and the dog doesn't have to wait in a hotel room. Many guesthouses, farms and campsites also accept dogs. The guide [Hotel, guesthouse, holiday apartment or farm?] (/reisen/hotel-pension-ferienwohnung/) offers a comparison of the types of accommodation.

**Rules in the accommodation:** Dogs usually do not belong on beds and sofas. Bring your own blanket, remove hair before you leave, and don't leave the dog alone for hours if it's not used to it. Barking in unfamiliar surroundings quickly annoys neighbors.

## Dog beaches on the North and Baltic Seas

There are fixed rules for dogs in many places on the coast:

- **Bathing beaches:** During the season, usually from spring to autumn, dogs are often prohibited on guarded beaches.
- **Dog beaches:** Designated areas where dogs are allowed to run, play and swim. Some have poop bag dispensers and water points.
- **Out of season** dogs are often allowed on the other beaches, usually on a leash.
- **Fees:** Some resorts charge a separate fee or levy for dogs.

The exact times and periods can be found on the community websites and at the beach entrances. Stick to them because the rules are a compromise between bathers, nature conservation and dog owners. More about the customs on the beach in the guide [beach chair and tourist tax] (/reisen/strandkorb-und-kurtaxe/).

**In the Wadden Sea and in protected areas** dogs almost always have to be on a leash. Breeding and resting birds are sensitive to dogs running loose, and seals on the beach must not be disturbed. More about this in the guide [Seals and migratory birds] (/reisen/seehunde-und-zugvoegel/).

## It is compulsory to keep on a leash in forests and fields

The federal states and municipalities regulate differently whether your dog is allowed to run freely. Basically:

- **Breeding and breeding season:** In many federal states, leashes are mandatory in forests and open countryside in spring and early summer because wild animals have their young during this time.
- **Nature reserves and national parks:** Leashes are generally required here all year round.
- **Within localities:** Many municipalities require a leash in parks, pedestrian areas and on promenades.
- **Poop bags:** Leakage belongs in the bag and in the trash can everywhere, including in the forest and on meadows where food for animals grows.

Even if it is not mandatory, the leash is useful if your dog is chasing game. A dog that chases a deer can fatally injure it, and the owner faces severe penalties.

## Arrival by car

- **Securing:** In the car, the dog must be secured like cargo, for example in a transport box in the trunk or with a safety harness on the belt. An unsecured dog is a danger to itself and all passengers when it comes to an emergency stop.
- **Breaks:** Every two to three hours take a break with water, a short walk and an opportunity to relax.
- **Heat:** Never leave your dog in a parked car in the summer, not even for a few minutes and not in the shade. The car heats up dangerously in a short period of time.
- **Food:** Only feed a small portion before long journeys to prevent nausea.

## Arrival by train

Dogs are allowed to travel on German trains. Small dogs in a transport box usually travel free of charge. Larger dogs usually need their own ticket, often half price, and must be on a leash and wear a muzzle. Practice wearing the muzzle before traveling so it's not new to the dog. More about train travel in the guide [Go on vacation by train](/reisen/mit-der-bahn-in-den-urlaub/).

Dogs are usually allowed on ferries to the islands, often for a small fee. Find out beforehand whether there are any restrictions. More about this in the guide [Ferry and everyday life on the island](/reisen/faehre-und-inselalltag/).

## On the way: restaurants, museums and excursions

Dogs are welcome in many German restaurants, cafés and beer gardens, especially on terraces. Ask briefly before you sit down and ask for a bowl of water. However, grocery stores, many museums, swimming pools and some excursion boats are taboo for dogs.

Therefore, plan trips so that the dog can be there: hikes, walks on the beach, boat trips with a dog permit, beer gardens and outdoor strolls through the city. For museum visits, you split up or look for local dog care.

## The first days at the holiday destination

A new environment is exciting for many dogs. Smells, noises and unfamiliar paths can be unsettling. How to help your dog arrive:

- **Explore the surroundings first.** After arriving, take a quiet walk around the accommodation before unpacking your suitcase.
- **Maintain usual routines.** Feeding times, walks and rest periods like at home provide security.
- **The first few days on a leash.** Even dogs with a secure recall are often distracted in unfamiliar surroundings.
- **Don't leave it alone for a long time.** If you want the dog to wait in the accommodation, practice this with short periods of time.
- **Set up a quiet place.** Your own blanket in a fixed place quickly becomes a safe place to retreat.

## Health and dangers

- **Heat:** Dogs hardly sweat. In summer, go for walks early in the morning and evening and avoid hot asphalt and sand in the midday sun.
- **Salt water:** If you swallow a lot of water by the sea, you will easily get diarrhea or vomit. Bring fresh water with you and let your dog drink regularly.
- **Blue-green algae:** Blue-green algae can form in lakes in warm summers. If the water is greenish, cloudy or streaks, do not let the dog in or drink it. Blue-green algae can be life-threatening for dogs.
- **Ticks:** Check your dog after walks in the woods and meadows and talk to the vet about tick protection before the trip.
- **Grazing animals:** In the mountains and on alpine pastures, cows with calves are sensitive to dogs. Keep your distance, put the dog on a short leash and let go if a cow attacks so that both can get to safety.
- **Vet at vacation destination:** Before you travel, write down the address of a veterinarian and an emergency veterinary service in the area.

## Entry with a dog from abroad

Anyone traveling to Germany with a dog from another EU country needs an EU pet passport, identification with a microchip and a valid rabies vaccination. Stricter rules apply to entry from countries outside the EU. Certain dog breeds are not allowed to be imported into Germany or only to a limited extent. Find out from the vet and the authorities early on.

## Packing list for the dog

- Food for the whole trip in the usual variety, bowls and a water bottle for on the go.
- Leash, spare leash, collar or harness with an address tag and your cell phone number.
- Poop bags in sufficient quantities.
- Blanket or dog bed and a favorite toy.
- Towel for wet paws and sandy fur.
- Muzzle for trains and certain situations.
- Small first aid kit with tick tweezers, bandage and paw protection.
- Vaccination certificate or EU pet passport and medication that the dog needs regularly.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Do not specifically ask the property.** “Pets on request” does not automatically mean yes.
2. **Let them run free on the beach.** This is usually forbidden during the season and is expensive.
3. **Ignore the requirement to keep on a leash during the breeding and breeding season.** It protects wild animals and also applies to well-behaved dogs.
4. **Leave the dog waiting in the car.** In the summer this can be fatal.
5. **Jump into the greenish water.** Blue-green algae is dangerous for dogs.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which regions are particularly dog-friendly?

The North Sea coast with many dog ​​beaches, low mountain ranges with long hiking trails and rural regions with many holiday homes are popular with dog owners. But what matters is the individual accommodation and its surroundings.

### Can my dog ​​come to the islands?

On most islands, yes. You often pay a fee on ferries, and the same rules apply on the beach as on the coast. More about the islands in the guides [East Frisian Islands](/reisen/ostfriesische-inseln/) and [North Frisian Islands](/reisen/nordfriesische-inseln/).

### When is the best time to travel with a dog?

Spring and autumn are ideal: pleasant temperatures, less crowds and more free beach areas on the coast. In midsummer, heat and beach bans make vacationing with dogs more difficult. More in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/). For more planning topics, see [Travel Planning](/themen/reiseplanung/).
