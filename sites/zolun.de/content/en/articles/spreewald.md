An hour southeast of Berlin, the Spree branches out into a labyrinth of hundreds of small watercourses. Between them lie meadows, alder forests, gardens and villages, whose farms used to be reached almost exclusively by boat. The Spreewald is a unique landscape, a UNESCO biosphere reserve and one of the most popular excursion destinations in eastern Germany. If you want to really experience it, you should glide slowly over the water at least once, be it in a boat with a ferryman or in a canoe yourself. This guide explains how the Spreewald is structured and how to plan your visit.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The Spreewald is located in Brandenburg, about 100 kilometers southeast of Berlin, and is easily accessible by regional train.
> - The Spree divides here into a dense network of watercourses, the Fließe. The landscape is protected as a biosphere reserve.
> - The Upper Spree Forest around Lübbenau, Lehde and Burg is better known and busier, the Lower Spree Forest around Lübben and Schlepzig is quieter and wilder.
> - A boat trip with a ferryman is classic. You can paddle yourself in a canoe or kayak.
> - The Spreewald is flat and ideal for cycling, for example on the Gurkenradweg.

## Orientation: Upper and Lower Spreewald

The Spreewald is divided into two parts that differ significantly.

**The Oberspreewald** is the larger and better known part. Here are Lübbenau with the large barge harbor, the village of Lehde with its farms on the water and the widely scattered community of Burg. The river network is particularly dense, with many farms standing on small islands. During the season, most of the boats and paddlers travel here.

**The Unterspreewald** north of Lübben is smaller, more wooded and much quieter. Here the landscape appears more pristine, and on some rivers you hardly meet anyone for hours. Villages like Schlepzig are good starting points for paddling tours.

In between is **Lübben**, the district town, with a harbor, castle island and connection to both parts.

## The most important places

**Lübbenau** is the gateway to the Oberspreewald. Boat trips in all directions start from the large harbor in the old town. The city has a castle with a park, shops selling regional products and many restaurants. In summer it is lively here, and much quieter in the mornings and early evenings.

**Lehde** is located just a few kilometers from Lübbenau and is probably the most famous Spreewald village. The old wooden houses with their gardens are located directly on the rivers, and many farms can still be reached today primarily via the water. An open-air museum shows how people used to live and farm here. In summer, mail in Lehde is traditionally delivered by barge.

**Burg** is a scattered settlement whose houses are spread widely across meadows and rivers. From a lookout tower you can look out over the flat landscape. There is also a thermal bath in Burg, which is a good alternative in bad weather.

**Lübben** connects the Upper and Lower Spreewald, has a pretty old town, a harbor and the castle island with a playground and water landscape for children.

**Schlepzig** in the Unterspreewald is a small village with half-timbered houses, inns and boat rentals, ideal for quiet tours on the rivers.

## Boat trip or paddling yourself?

**The boat trip** is the classic Spreewald experience. A ferryman pushes the flat wooden boat through the river with a long pole, talks about the landscape and its inhabitants, and you sit back. There are short tours lasting one to two hours and longer tours with a stop at an inn. Most start in Lübbenau, Lübben, Burg and Schlepzig. There are rarely fixed departure times: the barges leave when they are full.

**Canoe and kayak** give you more freedom. You determine the pace and route and also come across narrow rivers where no large boats sail. There are rental companies in all major towns, often with maps, waterproof barrels and tour suggestions.

Important for paddlers:

- **Observe the direction of travel and closures.** Some rivers are closed at certain times to protect breeding birds, others may only be traveled in one direction. The signs on the banks apply.
- There are **locks** on many rivers. At some you operate the gates yourself, at others a lock keeper helps. Plan some time and follow the instructions on site.
- **Be considerate of barges.** The large barges are heavy and less maneuverable. Drive right and let them pass.
- **Do not moor where it is private.** Many properties extend right to the water. Rest at designated places and in restaurants on the bank.
- **Life jacket for children** is included, even if the water is calm.

Three to four hours are enough for a first tour. The current is weak, but the journey back against the wind is sometimes more strenuous than expected.

## Cycling in the Spreewald

The Spreewald is flat and ideal for cycling. Many paths lead along the river, over small wooden bridges and through meadows and forests.

- **The Gurkenradweg** is a large circular route through the Spreewald and its surrounding area. It is divided into stages and connects the most important places. You'll need several days to complete the entire route; individual sections are well suited for day trips.
- **The Spree Cycle Path** follows the river from its source to Berlin and crosses the Spree Forest.
- **Short tours** from Lübbenau to Lehde and on to Burg are also easy to do with children.

Some paths are unpaved and muddy after rain. You can rent bicycles and e-bikes in Lübbenau, Lübben and Burg. More tips in the guide [Bicycle on Vacation](/reisen/fahrrad-im-urlaub/).

## Sorbian culture

The Spreewald belongs to the settlement area of the Sorbs, who are also called Wends here. They are a recognized national minority with their own language and culture. You can see this in the bilingual town signs, in traditional costumes at festivals and in customs around Easter when artistically decorated Easter eggs are created. Museums in the region and festivals throughout the year provide an insight into this culture, which is much older than tourism.

## Food and regional products

The Spreewald is known for its cucumbers. Spreewald cucumbers are a protected regional specialty and are offered in many variations, from sour to sweet and sour, with mustard, dill or chili. You can try and buy at many farms and stands.

More classics:

- **jacket potatoes with quark and linseed oil**, a simple, very regional dish.
- **Fish from the rivers and ponds**, such as pike, zander or eel, in many waterfront inns.
- **Horseradish**, which is traditionally grown here.

Many inns are located directly on the rivers and can also be reached by boat or canoe. The popular houses are full at lunchtime in season. So plan to stop outside of peak hours or make a reservation.

## Arrival and accommodation

**By train:** Lübben and Lübbenau are on the railway line between Berlin and Cottbus. Regional trains take about an hour from Berlin. From Lübbenau train station you can reach the old town and the harbor on foot. The Deutschlandticket is valid for regional transport. More about this in the guide [Going on vacation by train](/reisen/mit-der-bahn-in-den-urlaub/).

**By car:** You can quickly reach the Spreewald via the motorway between Berlin and Cottbus. There are large, paid parking spaces in Lübbenau and Burg that fill up early on summer weekends.

**Accommodation:** Guest houses, holiday apartments on old farms, hotels in Lübbenau and Lübben as well as campsites on the water. Accommodations directly on a river with their own jetty are particularly attractive. The guide compares which type suits you [hotel, guesthouse, holiday apartment or farm?] (/reisen/hotel-pension-ferienwohnung/).

## Travel time

- **May and June** are ideal: fresh greenery, pleasant temperatures and not that many visitors.
- **July and August** are high season. The punt harbors are full and there are lots of mosquitoes on warm evenings.
- **September and October** bring golden colors, calmer flow and the cucumber harvest. For many, autumn is the most beautiful time.
- **In winter** some ferry operators offer winter punt rides with blankets and warm drinks. Many rental companies then closed.

More about holidays and seasons in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

## In the rain

A rainy day in the Spreewald doesn't have to be a wasted day. Boat trips often take place even in light rain; some boats have a roof. There are also museums, the thermal baths in Burg and, in the wider area, a large swimming area under a hall roof. More ideas in the guide [Rainy days on vacation](/reisen/regentage-im-urlaub/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Only see Lübbenau.** The Unterspreewald and small villages show the quiet side.
2. **Without mosquito protection on summer evenings.** At dusk it quickly becomes unpleasant on the rivers.
3. **Drive on closed rivers.** The closures protect breeding birds and apply to everyone.
4. **Lunch in the most popular inns without a reservation.** Then waiting is inevitable.
5. **Plan paddling tours that are too long.** Locks and headwinds take more time than you think.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Spreewald worth a day trip from Berlin?

Yes. You can get to Lübbenau in an hour by regional train, take a boat trip or a paddling tour and be back in the evening. If you want to get to know the Unterspreewald and the villages, it's better to stay for two or three days. Tips for the capital can be found in the guide [Berlin on the first visit](/reisen/berlin-erster-besuch/).

### Is the Spreewald suitable for children?

Very. Children love boat trips, paddling, the open-air museum in Lehde and the water playgrounds. Short paddling tours with breaks are ideal for small children. Further tips in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

### Can you swim in the Spreewald?

Yes, at designated bathing areas and lakes in the area. In many places, swimming is not welcome or permitted in the rivers themselves due to boat traffic and nature conservation. More about swimming in inland waters in the guide [Swimming in lakes and rivers] (/reisen/baden-in-seen-und-fluessen/). For more waterfront destinations, see [Lakes & Rivers](/themen/seen-und-fluesse/).
