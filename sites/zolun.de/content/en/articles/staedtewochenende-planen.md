Arrival on Friday evening, three museums, two districts and a tower on Saturday, quick tour of the old town on Sunday and then onto the crowded train with aching feet. Many people come back from a city weekend more tired than when they left. This is rarely due to the city, but almost always due to the plan. Forty-eight hours is short, and if you try to see everything, you'll end up seeing little properly. This guide will show you a method that works in every city, whether in Leipzig, Bremen or Freiburg: the right location of the accommodation, a plan with two fixed anchors per day and plenty of air, honestly calculated city passes and a departure day on which you don't rush through the pedestrian zone with your suitcase.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Property location saves more time than any optimized plan. Choose them based on ways, not just price.
> - Plan a maximum of two fixed program items per day and consciously leave time free in between.
> - Large museums often work with time slot tickets and have a day off, often Monday. Check both on the official site.
> - A City Card is only worth it if you calculate what you actually use.
> - On the day of departure, your luggage will determine how relaxed you are. Clarify storage when booking.

## The location of the accommodation is a deciding factor

Cheap accommodation on the outskirts of the city seems tempting, but it costs you time and energy every day on a short weekend. A half-hour train ride twice a day quickly adds up to half an afternoon. Before booking, don't just check the price, but also the routes.

This is how you do it:

1. **Identify two to three places that are most important to you**, such as a museum, the old town, and a riverside park.
2. **Place the accommodation next to it on the map.** It is ideal if you can reach the most important places on foot or by taking a direct line without changing trains.
3. **Check the route from the train station.** If you come by train, you will be happy to find accommodation that can be reached from the main train station by line or on foot.
4. **Pay attention to noise.** Old town streets and nightlife areas are lively until late on weekends. Ask for a room facing the courtyard if you want to sleep peacefully.
5. **Arrange check-in, luggage and breakfast.** Can you arrive early and leave bags? Is there breakfast or a bakery nearby?

Being close to the train station is practical, but the area directly next to the train station is not the nicest in every city. A residential area with a tram connection is often the best choice: quieter, with cafés and bakeries where locals also shop, and still quickly in the center.

## The plan: two anchors per day and lots of air

An anchor is a program item with a fixed time or high priority: a museum with a time slot ticket, a city tour, a concert, a reserved table or the weekly market on Saturday morning. You should plan a maximum of two of these per day, one in the morning and one in the late afternoon or evening. Everything else is a buffer: strolling through a neighborhood, looking into a backyard, taking a break by the water.

This method has two advantages. If something takes longer, nothing will slip. And you have time for what really makes a city, namely the things that aren't in any plan. One day, if possible, place the two anchors in the same area so that you don't drive across town.

| period | program | Why so |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Friday evening | Arrive, check in, walk around the neighborhood, have dinner nearby | Orientation without pressure, sleep early |
| Saturday morning | Anchor 1: large museum with time slot ticket | Fresh minds, the halls are often emptier in the morning |
| Saturday lunchtime | Eating near the museum | No long journeys with hungry stomachs |
| Saturday afternoon | Buffer: park, river, a neighborhood without a plan | Discover the city instead of working through it |
| Late afternoon | Break at the accommodation | Strength for the evening |
| Saturday evening | Anchor 2: Concert, theater or reserved meal | A climax with a fixed time |
| Sunday morning | Check out, drop off luggage, anchor 3: viewpoint or small museum | A final impression near the train station |
| Sunday afternoon | Lunch, departure with buffer | No rush to the train |

### In pairs or in a group: who chooses the anchors?

When several people travel together, everyone wants something different: she wants to go to the art museum, he wants to go to the flea market, her friend wants to go to the botanical garden. Instead of trying to find a compromise that doesn't excite anyone, each person chooses an anchor for the weekend. The others come along or use the time for themselves and their own program. A separate afternoon with a meeting point in the café afterwards is surprisingly good for many couples and groups of friends.

## Getting around the city: tickets on site

Before you travel, download the local transport association's app or note where you can get tickets. In some cities you buy from the driver on the bus, in others you only buy from a vending machine or via an app, and trams sometimes have their own vending machines on board. Also check whether a day or group ticket is cheaper than individual trips. It's best to walk short distances in the city center because that's how you discover the most.

## Museums, time slot tickets and days off

Many large museums sell tickets for a fixed time slot, especially for popular special exhibitions. On weekends the good times are often snapped up early. So book your most important museum visit online in advance and plan to be there shortly before your time slot.

Also pay attention to rest days. Many museums in Germany are closed on Mondays, some on another day of the week. Some houses are open longer one evening a week. Special times often apply on public holidays. Rely only on the official website of the hotel, not on map apps or older travel guides.

Other practical points:

- **Bags:** You have to leave large backpacks and bags at the cloakroom or in lockers in many museums. A small daypack saves time.
- **Special exhibitions:** They often cost extra or require their own time slot.
- **Audio guides:** Some houses offer them via app. Bring headphones and a charged battery.
- **Churches:** Visits are usually not possible during church services, especially on Sunday mornings.
- **Sunday:** Shops in Germany are generally closed on Sundays, except at train stations, airports and on individual shopping Sundays. If you want to stroll, plan it for Saturday.

## Calculate city passes and city cards honestly

Many cities offer tickets for one or more days that combine local transport and free or reduced admission. Some are worth it, some are not. Whether you save depends entirely on your program. Therefore calculate like this:

1. **Write your real list.** Only write down what you are sure to visit, not everything that is theoretically possible.
2. **Read the services carefully.** Is entry free or reduced? Does local transport apply to the entire city or just to certain zones? Does validity count in hours from first use or in calendar days?
3. **Add individual prices.** Check the official websites of the museums and transport companies to see what your list would cost individually.
4. **Compare.** The card is only worth it if the sum of the individual prices is significantly higher than the card price.
5. **Think about your own discounts.** Children pay little or nothing in many museums, and there are often discounts for students. Anyone who has a Germany ticket already travels by bus and train.

An example: A couple with a Germany ticket is planning a large museum and a harbor tour. The City Card only offers a discount for both, its biggest component is local transport, which they both already have. The card is not worth it here. However, a family without a local transport ticket who visits three museums included can save money.

Beware of card logic: Anyone who has bought an expensive card tends to pack as much as possible into it to make it worthwhile. This means the quiet plan is gone.

## Luggage on arrival and departure days

Luggage is the biggest brake on the day of arrival and departure. You have these options:

- **At the Hotel:** Many hotels accept luggage before check-in and after check-out. Ask briefly when booking.
- **In the holiday apartment:** There is usually no reception and fixed arrival times. Check with the host if you can leave bags earlier.
- **At the train station:** Many larger train stations have lockers or luggage storage. Size, pay and availability vary, and subjects are sometimes full on weekends. Check this in advance on the train station information pages.
- **Travel light:** One bag is enough for two nights, and you can carry it for half a day if necessary.

## Eating, taking breaks and dividing your energy

City trips are more tiring than they look. Standing for long periods in museums and walking on pavement is more tiring than a hike in the countryside. Every two to three hours, sit down, have a drink, and just watch for a while. Parks, riverfronts, libraries and churches are good places to breathe.

It's worth making a reservation for Saturday evening, as popular restaurants are often fully booked. It's best to eat lunch where you are instead of driving across town for a specific restaurant. On Sunday morning many bakeries open for a few hours, handy for breakfast if your accommodation doesn't offer one. A refillable water bottle and comfortable, worn-in shoes should be packed in your luggage.

## The day of departure: relaxed instead of rushed

Plan Sunday backwards from departure. Place the last item on the program near the train station or your parking lot, eat in peace before departure and be at the platform with a buffer. Long-distance trains are often full on Sunday afternoons and evenings, so it's worth reserving a seat. More about tickets, reservations and delays can be found in [Go on vacation by train](/reisen/mit-der-bahn-in-den-urlaub/).

If you come by car, check in advance whether the accommodation has a parking space or where a parking garage is located. Many inner cities have an environmental zone in which you can only drive with a suitable sticker.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Plan too much.** Three museums in one day will leave your legs tired. Two anchors are enough.
2. **Book based on price only.** Long journeys eat up the time you think you have saved.
3. **Overlook Monday.** If you leave on Monday and then want to go to the museum, you will often find yourself in front of a locked door.
4. **Buy the City Card automatically.** Calculate first, then buy.
5. **Want to shop on Sundays.** Most stores are closed.
6. **Pack your departure day to capacity.** A missed train costs more nerves than a missed program item.

## Frequently asked questions

### Arriving on Friday or Saturday?

If possible, Friday afternoon or evening. You then start Saturday well rested and have two full days. If you only stay one night, it is better to plan a single anchor per day.

### Does the method also work with children?

Yes, and even particularly good. Replace an anchor with something for the kids, like a playground, zoo, or hands-on museum, and plan for longer breaks.

### What to do if it rains all weekend?

Swap the order, not the plan: museums, market halls and churches move forward, the park moves to the driest section. An umbrella and waterproof shoes do the rest. Many cities show a quiet, beautiful side in the rain.
