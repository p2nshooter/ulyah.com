Most people pack too much and still forget something important. Luckily, this is rarely a problem when traveling within Germany, as almost everything can be bought on the way. Nevertheless, a well-thought-out packing list saves time, money and nerves: you carry less, find what you're looking for more quickly and don't find yourself without a jacket on the first rainy day. This guide shows you a basic list for every trip and the extras for the beach, mountains, city, family and dog.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Pack according to the onion principle: several thin layers instead of a thick jacket. The weather in Germany changes quickly.
> - Rain jacket and comfortable, worn-in shoes belong in every luggage, even in midsummer.
> - Documents, medication and charging cables are more important than the third pair of shoes.
> - If your accommodation has a washing machine, there will be enough clothes for a week.
> - By train and ferry, you should only take as much with you as you can carry up the stairs yourself.

## The basic list for every trip

**Documents and money**

- ID card or passport.
- Health insurance card, vaccination certificate and allergy certificate if required.
- Driver's license and vehicle documents if arriving by car.
- Booking confirmations, tickets and reservations, preferably digitally and as printouts.
- Debit card, credit card and some cash. Small businesses sometimes only take cash.
- Emergency numbers and the address of the accommodation on paper in case the cell phone is empty.

**Clothing according to the onion principle**

- Underwear and socks for about a week.
- T-shirts and one or two long-sleeved tops.
- A warm sweater or fleece jacket, even in summer for cool evenings.
- A waterproof rain jacket that folds up small.
- A pair of comfortable long pants and a pair of shorts or a skirt.
- Sleepwear.
- Two pairs of shoes: worn-in, comfortable shoes for long journeys and a light second pair.

**Body Care**

- Toothbrush, toothpaste, shower gel and shampoo in small bottles or solids.
- Deodorant, comb or brush, razor.
- Sunscreen with a high sun protection factor and lip care with sun protection.
- Glasses or contact lenses with care products.

**Technology**

- Cell phone with charging cable and power adapter.
- Power bank for long days out.
- Headphones for the journey.
- Multiple sockets, because sockets are often scarce in holiday apartments and old houses.

## The first aid kit

There are pharmacies almost everywhere in Germany, but not always when you need them. A small first aid kit covers the most common complaints:

- Medication that you take regularly, in sufficient quantities plus a reserve.
- Remedies for pain and fever.
- Remedies for diarrhea and nausea, and also for motion sickness on boat trips.
- Plasters, blister plasters, a small gauze bandage and disinfectant.
- Tick ​​tweezers or tick card.
- Mosquito repellent and a remedy for itching after bites.
- Thermometer, especially with children.

Outside office hours, you can reach the medical on-call service nationwide on 116 117. In an emergency, dial 112.

## Extras for the beach and coast

The North and Baltic Seas have their own weather. Even on sunny days the wind can be chilly.

- Bathing suits, preferably two so that one can dry.
- A large beach towel and a towel for drying off.
- Windbreaker or fleece jacket for a walk on the beach.
- Sun hat or cap, sunglasses and plenty of sunscreen. The wind belies the power of the sun.
- Bathing shoes for stony sections of the Baltic Sea and for the mudflats.
- Beach shelter or windbreak if you don't rent a beach chair. More about this in the guide [Beach chair and tourist tax] (/reisen/strandkorb-und-kurtaxe/).
- A bag for wet things and a small cooler bag.

## Extras for mountains and hiking

In the Alps and low mountain ranges, the weather can change within an hour.

- Sturdy, broken-in hiking boots with good tread.
- Technical shirts that dry quickly and a warm layer.
- Rain jacket and rain pants.
- Hat and gloves, even in summer for summits and mountain railways.
- Daypack with a water bottle, provisions and a small first aid kit.
- Hiking map or a map app with downloaded offline maps.
- Hiking poles if you're used to them.

## Extras for the city

- Comfortable shoes that allow you to walk ten kilometers a day. New shoes on a city trip are a bad idea.
- A small bag that you wear on the front of your body to protect against pickpockets in crowded trains and markets.
- A fancier outfit for the opera, concert or a good restaurant.
- A small umbrella.
- A reusable drinking bottle. There are public drinking fountains in many cities.

## Extras with children

- Change of clothes for every day, more for small children.
- Mud trousers and rubber boots for rainy days.
- The favorite cuddly toy and a familiar sleep book.
- Games, painting materials and radio plays for the journey.
- Snacks and water bottles for on the go.
- Sand toys, which you can also buy locally if necessary.
- Children's travel first aid kit with dosages for your child's age.

More about this in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

## Extras with dog

- Food, bowls and water bottle for on the go.
- Leash, spare leash and harness with address tag.
- Poop bags in large quantities.
- Blanket or dog bed and favorite toys.
- Towel for wet paws.
- Muzzle for trains and certain situations.
- Vaccination certificate or EU pet passport.

More tips in the guide [Holidays with a dog in Germany] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-hund/).

## Extras for holiday apartments and camping

When booking, ask what is available at the property. Often missing:

- Bed linen and towels if not included in the price.
- Dishwashing liquid, sponge, tea towel and garbage bags for the first few days.
- Salt, pepper, oil and coffee in small quantities.
- A sharp knife if you like cooking.
- Detergent if there is a washing machine.

There are separate lists for camping, for example with headlamps, CEE adapters and long pegs. More in the guide [Camping in Germany](/reisen/camping-in-deutschland/).

## Pack according to the season

**Spring:** In April and May, warm sunny days alternate with cool showers, and it can still be frosty at night. Bring a warm jacket, sweater, and rain jacket, as well as light clothing for sunny afternoons. If you hike in the mountains, you have to expect snow at higher altitudes.

**Summer:** In June, July and August you will need light, breathable clothing, sun protection and swimwear. Nevertheless, don't underestimate the evenings on the coast and in the mountains. When it's hot in the city, a drinking bottle and a hat help. In summer, thunderstorms often come in the afternoon, so a light rain jacket should always be in your daypack.

**Autumn:** September and October are often mild and clear, but the days become shorter and the nights cooler. Dress like a glove and take waterproof shoes with you, as leaves and rain make paths slippery. A windproof jacket is ideal for autumn storms on the coast.

**Winter:** From December to February you will need a warm winter jacket, hat, scarf, gloves and shoes with thick, non-slip soles. At Christmas markets and on winter walks you stand and walk outside for a long time. Thermal underwear makes cold days more bearable. Separate lists of helmets, ski goggles and functional clothing apply to ski holidays.

## The digital packing list

Many travelers use a note-taking app or spreadsheet as a packing list, which they copy and adapt before each trip. This has advantages: the list is always with you, you can share it with fellow travelers and add what was missing or unnecessary after the trip. If you prefer to work on paper, print out the list and check it off as you pack. Both are better than packing from memory the night before you leave.

## What you can safely leave at home

- **The hairdryer:** Almost all hotels and many holiday apartments have one.
- **Large quantities of cosmetics:** There are drugstores in every town.
- **A separate outfit for every day:** Combined clothing in a few colors is enough.
- **Paper books in large numbers:** An e-reader or a book to swap saves weight.
- **Valuables you don't need:** expensive jewelry and unnecessary electronics.

## This is how you pack wisely

- **Write a list and check it off.** A list that you reuse for each trip only gets better over time.
- **Heavy things down** and close to your back when wearing a backpack.
- **Roll clothes instead of folding them.** This saves space and prevents creases.
- **A change of clothes in your hand luggage** in case your suitcase or luggage does not arrive on time.
- **Liquids in a bag** so that nothing leaks.
- **Complete packing the evening before** so that the day of departure starts relaxed. In the morning all that's left is a toothbrush, charging cable and medication.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Don't pack a rain jacket.** A shower is almost certain.
2. **Take new shoes with you on a hike or city trip.** Blisters ruin any trip.
3. **Forgot the charging cable.** This happens to everyone, pack them first.
4. **Calculate medication carefully.** Take something in reserve with you.
5. **Pack too much.** What you can't carry becomes a burden.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much clothing do I need for two weeks?

A washing machine will last you about a week's worth of clothes. Without a washing machine, you can wash small items in the sink or use a laundry facility.

### Do I need warm clothing in summer?

Yes. It can be cool on the coast, in the mountains and in the evenings even in July. A sweater and a rain jacket are always included. More about the weather in the guide [Best travel time for vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

### Suitcase or backpack?

A suitcase is convenient for traveling by car and at a fixed location. If you travel by train and ferry, change trains frequently or have to climb stairs in old towns, you are often better off with a travel backpack or a small rolling suitcase.

### What do I do if I have forgotten something?

In Germany you can get almost everything in local drugstores, supermarkets and pharmacies. Only in small towns and islands are the selection and opening times limited, and most shops are closed on Sundays. For more planning topics, see [Travel Planning](/themen/reiseplanung/).
