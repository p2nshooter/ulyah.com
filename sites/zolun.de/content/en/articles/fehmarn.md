Fehmarn is different from the large bathing islands of the Baltic Sea. There are no magnificent imperial baths and no chalk cliffs, but wide fields, a large piece of sky, small harbors and a wind that almost always blows. That's exactly what makes the island so special for many people: families like the flat beaches, surfers and kiters like the reliable breeze, cyclists like the flat paths and those seeking peace and quiet like the evenings at the lighthouse. And because the island can be reached via a bridge, the holiday begins without a ferry schedule. This guide shows you how Fehmarn is structured and which place suits your vacation.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Fehmarn is located in the Baltic Sea off the coast of Schleswig-Holstein and is connected to the mainland via the Fehmarnsund Bridge.
> - The main town is Burg on Fehmarn. The island's most famous bathing beach is located on the southern beach in Burgtiefe.
> - The island is flat, agricultural and exposed to the wind almost everywhere. This makes it one of the most popular areas for windsurfing and kitesurfing in Germany.
> - On the north and west coasts there are quiet natural beaches, lighthouses and a large bird sanctuary.
> - Ferries run from Puttgarden in the north to Rødby in Denmark. A day trip is easily doable.

## Orientation: how Fehmarn is structured

Fehmarn has the rough shape of an irregular square. The Fehmarnsund Bridge connects the south of the island with the mainland near Großenbrode. A few kilometers further is Burg, the only larger town. Villages, farms and small harbors are scattered all around.

- **The South** with the castle, castle depths and the south beach is the tourist center with a promenade, harbor, restaurants and holiday apartments.
- **The west** with Orth, Lemkenhafen and the Wallnau bird sanctuary is quieter, with salt marshes, beach lakes and small harbors.
- **The north** with Puttgarden, the Grüner Brink and the Marienleuchte lighthouse is flat and open, with natural beaches and the ferry port.
- **The East** around Katharinenhof and Staberhuk has a slightly higher coast, stone beaches and lots of peace.

The distances are small. From Burg you can reach almost any point on the island by bike in less than an hour. If you have an e-bike, you can circle Fehmarn in one day.

## Castle and the south beach

**Burg auf Fehmarn** is a small town center with a brick church, market square, shops, cafes and restaurants. When it rains and in the evening, Burg is the place where there is something going on on the island. There is a market once a week, and in summer there are festivals and concerts.

A short distance to the south is **Burgtiefe** with the **South Beach**. The fine sandy beach slopes gently into the water; behind it runs a promenade with beach chairs, kiosks and playgrounds. It is the most comfortable beach on the island for families with small children. Right next door is the Burger Binnensee, a calm body of water where many beginners make their first attempts with a surfboard or stand-up paddle.

You will find most hotels, holiday apartments and apartment complexes around the south beach. If you like to eat on foot in the evening and want to go to the beach in the morning without a car, this is the most practical place to live.

## Natural beaches and quiet coast

If you are looking for space and less activity, you will find wonderful beaches on the north and west coasts.

- **Green Brink** in the north is a long natural beach in front of a nature reserve with beach lakes and dunes. There is hardly any development here, but there is a lot of sky and often birds.
- **Wulfener Hals** in the southwest is a headland with a beach, campsites and surfing schools.
- **Katharinenhof** in the east is located below a small cliff. The beach is partly rocky, but it is quiet and close to nature.
- **Beaches near Gold and Puttgarden** in the north are popular with surfers and also offer space for bathers on calm days.

Many sections are not guarded. Pay attention to the instructions on site, do not swim alone and avoid areas near groynes and currents. Especially in offshore winds, air mattresses and swimming animals drift out quickly. The most important rules can be found in the guide [Swim safely by the sea] (/reisen/sicher-baden-am-meer/).

## Wind and water sports

Fehmarn has hardly any forest and no mountains to slow the wind. That's why the island is one of the best-known areas for windsurfing and kitesurfing in Germany. Depending on the wind direction, athletes will always find a coast with suitable conditions, whether it's shallow water or waves on the open sea.

For beginners, there are several surfing and kite schools that offer courses for children and adults. Good to know:

- **Beginners learn in shallow water.** Inland lakes and shallow bays are safer than the open Baltic Sea.
- **Kitesurfing only with a course.** Kites develop a lot of power. Without instruction, the sport is dangerous for you and others.
- **Observe kite zones.** Kiting is not permitted everywhere on beaches and in protected areas. The permitted areas are signposted.
- **Pack a wetsuit.** Even in summer, the water is often so fresh that a suit makes longer sessions more comfortable.

Fehmarn is also a destination for sailors: there are harbors in Burgtiefe, Orth and Lemkenhafen from which trips to the Danish islands are possible.

## Nature, birds and lighthouses

On the west coast is the **Wallnau Water Bird Reserve**, a protected area with ponds, meadows and observation stands. Thousands of migratory birds rest here in spring and autumn, and numerous species breed in summer. A visitor center explains what you see. Binoculars are worth it. If you are interested in bird migration on the coast, also read the guide [Seals and Migratory Birds](/reisen/seehunde-und-zugvoegel/).

Fehmarn has several **lighthouses** that are among the most beautiful photo motifs on the island: Flügge in the southwest, Marienleuchte in the north, Westermarkelsdorf in the northwest and Staberhuk in the southeast. The Flügge lighthouse can be climbed at certain times and offers a wide view over the island and the sound. A bike ride from lighthouse to lighthouse is a nice day plan.

In **Lemkenhafen** there is an old windmill that is open as a museum, and the small harbor with its fishing boats is particularly atmospheric in the evening.

## Trip to Denmark

From the ferry port **Puttgarden** in the north, ferries run regularly to **Rødby** on the Danish island of Lolland. The crossing takes around three quarters of an hour. A day trip, also on foot or by bike, is easily possible.

- Take a valid ID card or passport with you. There are partial controls at the Danish border.
- In Denmark you pay with Danish kroner, cards are accepted almost everywhere.
- Lolland has beaches, small towns and a large safari park that is popular with families.

A tunnel is being built under the Fehmarnbelt between Fehmarn and Lolland, which will shorten the journey to Denmark in the future. The ferries will remain connected until completion and there may be diversions around the construction sites.

## Arrival and travel on the island

**By car:** You can quickly reach the Fehmarnsund Bridge from Lübeck via the motorway and the federal road. The bridge is heavily exposed to the wind. During storms it can be closed to caravans, trailers and other wind-sensitive vehicles. If you are traveling with a trailer or motorhome, check traffic reports before driving.

**By train:** Regional trains run from Lübeck towards Fehmarn. Due to the construction work in connection with the Fehmarnbelt crossing, there are frequent closures and replacement bus services on the route. Therefore, check the current connection shortly before your trip. The Germany ticket is valid for regional transport, more information can be found in the guide [Go on vacation with the Germany ticket] (/reisen/deutschlandticket-im-urlaub/).

**By bike:** The island is flat and has a good network of cycle paths and quiet country roads. The headwind is the only real climb. Plan tours so that you drive home with a tailwind. On the mainland opposite, the Baltic Sea cycle path continues towards Heiligenhafen and the Bay of Lübeck. Tips on rental bikes and luggage can be found in the guide [Bicycle on Vacation](/reisen/fahrrad-im-urlaub/).

**On site:** A spa tax is charged for the beach and promenade, usually through the accommodation. You can rent beach chairs on the managed beaches on a daily or weekly basis. Background information in the guide [beach chair and tourist tax] (/reisen/strandkorb-und-kurtaxe/).

## Travel time and weather

Fehmarn is considered one of the sunniest regions in Germany. However, the wind means that it often feels cooler than the thermometer shows.

- **June to August** is swimming season. During the summer holidays, the southern beach, campsites and holiday apartments are well filled.
- **May and September** are ideal for cycling and bird watching. The water is often even warmer in September than in June.
- **Spring and autumn** bring strong winds and therefore the best conditions for experienced surfers and kiters.
- **In winter** the island is very quiet. Many businesses are closing, but the beach and lighthouses are almost yours alone.

More about this in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

## Tips for windy and gray days

- **Take a windbreak with you.** A beach shelter or a windbreak makes a day at the beach on the open coast much more pleasant.
- **Choose the leeward side.** If the wind blows from land to sea, it is often quieter on the beach, but swimming animals drift away more quickly.
- **Explore the castle.** Cafes, small museums and shops fill a rainy day well.
- **Drive to the mainland.** Heiligenhafen with its pier and harbor is right behind the bridge.

You can find more ideas in the guide [Rainy days on vacation](/reisen/regentage-im-urlaub/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Underestimate the wind.** Sunburn occurs particularly quickly in cool winds because you can hardly feel the sun.
2. **Set off with the trailer in a storm.** Check beforehand whether the Sound Bridge is free for trailers.
3. **Start kiting without a course.** This is dangerous and forbidden on many beaches.
4. **Only know the south beach.** The natural beaches in the north and west show a completely different island.
5. **Forgot your ID for Denmark.** There are some checks at the border.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Fehmarn suitable for families?

Yes. The flat south beach, many playgrounds, holiday farms with animals and short distances make Fehmarn very family-friendly. A windbreak for the beach should be in your luggage. Further tips in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

### How long does it take to get to know Fehmarn?

Three to four days are enough for a first impression. If you want to learn to surf, go on bike tours and plan a trip to Denmark, it's better to stay for a week.

### Can you go on holiday on Fehmarn without a car?

Yes, if you live in Burg or on the south beach. You can get around easily by bike, bus and occasional taxi rides. If you're traveling by train, you should check the current connections due to the construction work. You can find more islands under [Islands](/themen/inseln/).
