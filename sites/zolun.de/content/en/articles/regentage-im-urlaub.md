Rain is part of a holiday in Germany, on the coast as well as in the mountains. But a rainy day doesn't have to be a wasted day. With a few ideas in hand, it can often turn into one of the best days on vacation: an afternoon in the thermal baths, a tour of a mine, a walk in the drizzle followed by hot chocolate or a day of games in the holiday apartment. This guide collects ideas for indoors and outdoors and shows you how to plan with the weather instead of against it.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Rain often only lasts for hours. Use the precipitation radar to find dry time slots for your walk.
> - Swimming pools, thermal baths, museums and aquariums are full when it rains. Come early in the morning or late afternoon.
> - Caves and mines are independent of the weather and have similar temperatures all year round.
> - With weatherproof clothing, many outdoor experiences are beautiful even in the rain, and some are even more beautiful.
> - Pack games, books, and crafts for rainy days, especially with children.

## Plan with the weather

Before you plan a whole day indoors, it's worth taking a look at the precipitation radar. Showers often pass quickly along the coast, while in the mountains it often rains in the afternoon. If you know when it will be dry, you can split up the day: go to the museum in the morning, go to the beach in the afternoon.

A few rules of thumb:

- **In the morning and evening** it is quieter in thermal baths, museums and aquariums. They are most crowded at midday when it rains.
- **Plan excursions flexibly depending on the weather.** Schedule boat tours, hikes and beach days on the dry days, and museums and baths on the wet ones.
- **Don't reserve anything outside too far in advance** if canceling would be expensive.

## Water from above, water from below: baths and thermal baths

If it's wet outside, you might as well get wet inside.

1. **Thermae and wellness pools** with warm pools, saunas and relaxation rooms. In Bavaria, the Erding thermal spa is one of the largest thermal spa areas in Europe.
2. **Fun pools** with slides and wave pool, ideal for children. In the Black Forest, the bathing paradise at Titisee is a well-known destination.
3. **Seawater wave pools** on the coast where you can experience the sea without wind and cold.
4. **Small indoor pools** in town, often cheaper and significantly emptier than the large adventure pools.

Tip: Many pools offer evening rates. If it rains in the afternoon, the evening is often the more relaxed time.

## Museums that are fun

Museums are the classic rainy day destination. Houses where you can try things out for yourself are particularly worthwhile:

5. **Technology and science museums** like the German Museum in Munich, where children and adults carry out experiments themselves. More in the guide [Munich](/reisen/muenchen-staedtereise/).
6. **Sea and climate houses** on the coast, such as the climate house in Bremerhaven, which stages a journey along a longitude around the world.
7. **National park centers** such as the Multimar Wattforum in Tönning, which explains the Wadden Sea with aquariums and hands-on stations.
8. **Shipping and harbor museums** in Hamburg, Bremerhaven or on the Baltic Sea.
9. **Open-air museums with covered houses** where ancient crafts are demonstrated.
10. **Model building and toys**, such as the Miniatur Wunderland in Hamburg. More in the guide [Discover Hamburg](/reisen/hamburg-entdecken/).
11. **Small local museums** in the holiday resorts, which are often surprisingly lovingly designed and tell stories that you don't hear otherwise.

## Underwater and underground

12. **Aquariums:** The Oceaneum in Stralsund shows the underwater world of the Baltic and North Seas in large tanks. There are also smaller marine aquariums in other coastal locations.
13. **Stalactite caves:** You can visit caves in many low mountain ranges, such as the Atta cave in the Sauerland, the Dechenhöhle near Iserlohn or the Baumannshöhle in the Harz. The temperature is a few degrees above zero all year round, so take a jacket with you.
14. **Visitor mines:** In the former Rammelsberg ore mine near Goslar and in many other mines, you can take a mine train or walk through tunnels and find out how hard the work underground was.
15. **Bunkers and casemates** in fortresses tell stories, even when it rains.

## Active indoors

16. **Climbing halls and bouldering halls** often have courses for beginners and children.
17. **Trampoline halls and indoor playgrounds** let children run around even when it rains.
18. **Bowling, mini golf in the hall and laser tag** for groups and families.
19. **Ice skating rinks** are also open outside of winter in many regions.

## Enjoyment and craftsmanship

20. **Brewery and winery tours** with tasting, especially in Franconia, on the Moselle and Rhine.
21. **Cooking courses and baking courses** in which you prepare regional specialties.
22. **Manufactories** show how porcelain, glass, chocolate or marzipan are made. In Meißen you can look over the shoulders of porcelain painters.
23. **Cafés with cake** are an institution in Germany. Drinking a long coffee in the afternoon saves many a gray day.

## Outside despite the rain

Many experiences are not worse when it rains, just different:

24. **Walk in the forest:** In the rain, the forest smells more intensely, mushrooms and snails appear, and the paths are empty.
25. **Wadden hike in the drizzle:** With rain gear and rubber boots, the mudflats are just as exciting when the sky is gray. However, thunderstorms and fog are a reason to cancel. More about this in the guide [Ebb and Flow](/reisen/ebbe-und-flut/).
26. **Storm by the sea:** Waves, spray and wind on the pier are a natural spectacle as long as you keep your distance from the water and breakwaters.
27. **Puddle jumping with children:** With mud pants and rubber boots, every rainy day becomes an adventure.
28. **Photography:** Reflections in wet streets, fog over lakes and dramatic clouds create special images.

**Important:** Beaches, water, mountain peaks and open areas are not part of the program during thunderstorms. Go into a building or into a car.

## Cozy in the holiday apartment

29. **Game Day:** Board games, card games, puzzles and riddles. Many holiday apartments have a shelf with games, ask.
30. **Get creative:** Paint shells and stones from the beach, write a vacation diary, design postcards, sort photos.

There's also reading, watching movies, baking and just sleeping in. A quiet day is good for everyone, especially on longer trips. Many holiday destinations also have a small library or bookcase where you can exchange reading material.

## Three sample plans for a rainy day

**On the North Sea with children:** Head to the national park center or seawater wave pool in the morning while it is still empty. Lunch in the holiday apartment. In the afternoon, when the shower has passed, go to the dike in rain gear to fly a kite or search for shells. Pancakes and a board game in the evening.

**In a big city for two:** A large museum in the morning, a market hall or a cozy restaurant at lunch, and a tour of a historical building or a brewery in the afternoon. In the evening there is a concert, theater or cinema. In many cities there are combination tickets for several museums.

**In the mountains:** In the morning, while the mountains are covered in clouds, go to the thermal baths. If it clears up in the afternoon, a short walk through a gorge or to a waterfall, which is particularly impressive after rain. Watch out for slippery paths and closed sections. A castle or monastery with a guided tour is the alternative if it continues to rain.

Such plans are easy to adapt. The only important thing is to think briefly in the morning instead of writing off the day.

## What belongs in your luggage

- **Rain jacket and rain pants** for everyone, plus waterproof shoes or rubber boots.
- **Small umbrella** for the city.
- **Change of clothes** in the backpack, especially for children.
- **Games, books and crafts** that take up little space.
- **Swimming clothes**, also for winter holidays, for indoor pools and thermal baths.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Plan to stay indoors all day.** Often it only rains for a few hours.
2. **Go to the thermal baths at lunchtime.** It's busiest when it rains.
3. **Without a jacket into the cave.** It is cool underground even in summer.
4. **In a thunderstorm, go to the beach or into the water.** This is life-threatening.
5. **Don't know any alternatives.** A short list of nearby rain destinations saves discussion.

## Frequently asked questions

### What can you do with children when it rains?

Fun pools, hands-on museums, aquariums, indoor playgrounds and caves are popular. With weatherproof clothing, jumping in puddles and walking in the forest are also great fun. More ideas in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

### Where does it rain the least in Germany?

Some regions in the east and in the lee of low mountain ranges are drier, while it rains more frequently on the coast and in the mountains. But there are no guarantees anywhere. More in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

### Is a boat tour worth it when it rains?

In light rain and calm seas, this is possible, as many excursion ships have heated saloons with large windows. However, during storms, thunderstorms or fog, trips are often canceled or uncomfortable. Ask at the dock whether the trip is taking place and whether there are seats below deck.

### What do I do with wet clothes in the holiday apartment?

When you arrive, ask if there is a drying room, clothes rack or dryer. Shoes dry faster with crumpled newspaper that you change regularly. After drying, ventilate well so that moisture does not collect in the rooms.

### Does a rainy day cost a lot of money?

Thermal baths and large leisure pools are not cheap, but museums, churches, walks and games days are hardly. There are often discounts with guest cards.
