Going on holiday by train has many advantages: you arrive relaxed, can read, play or sleep on the way, save yourself in traffic jams and looking for a parking space and travel is much more climate-friendly than by car or plane. At the same time, the train has its pitfalls: delays, full trains during the holidays and the question of how to get from the train station to the holiday apartment with suitcases, children and a bicycle. This guide explains how to plan a train trip so that it becomes the first nice part of your vacation.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Saver prices are cheap, but only apply to the booked train. Flex prices cost more and give you the choice.
> - Children up to 14 years of age travel free on long-distance transport when accompanied by parents or grandparents if they are listed on the ticket.
> - Plan generous transfer times. Delays are not uncommon in long-distance transport.
> - If you are late, you have rights: If your arrival is delayed by 60 minutes, part of the fare will be refunded.
> - For long-distance bicycles you need a parking space reservation. A dedicated bike map on local transport.

## Long-distance or local transport?

In Germany there are two types of trains that differ in terms of tickets and comfort:

- **Long-distance transport** with ICE, IC and EC quickly connects large cities with each other. The trains have seat reservations, an on-board bistro or dining car and WiFi.
- **Local transport** with regional express, regional train and S-Bahn runs shorter distances and stops more frequently. The Germany ticket is valid here, there are no reservations.

For long distances, long-distance transport is usually faster and more convenient. For excursions in the region and destinations away from the main routes, local transport is often the better choice. The guide [Going on vacation with the Germany ticket] (/reisen/deutschlandticket-im-urlaub/) explains how to travel with the Germany ticket.

## The right tickets

**Saver Price and Super Saver Price:** These tickets are significantly cheaper if you book early. However, they only apply to the trains you have booked. If it is foreseeable that you will reach your destination at least 20 minutes later due to a delay, you can take another train. Exchanges and refunds are limited or charge a fee depending on the plan.

**Flex price:** The flex price is more expensive, but flexible. You can take any train on the booked route on the day of travel and cancel it free of charge until shortly before the start of your journey.

**BahnCard:** If you travel by train several times a year, you can save 25 or 50 off many tickets with a BahnCard. Calculate whether the card is worth it for your travels, and remember that it will automatically renew if you don't cancel.

**Seat reservation:** Long-distance trains are often full during holidays and weekends. A reservation costs little and saves you standing in the aisle, especially with children and luggage.

**Tip:** Book saver prices as early as possible, as soon as the timetable for your travel period has been determined. The trains are busiest on Fridays and Sundays as well as at the start and end of the holidays and the cheap allotments quickly sell out.

## With children on the train

For families, the train is often more relaxing than the car: children can move around, play and go to the toilet without anyone having to stop.

- **Ride for free:** Children under six travel free. Children aged six to 14 travel free when accompanied by a parent or grandparent, but must be listed on the ticket.
- **Family area and toddler compartment:** Many long-distance trains have areas for families. Make a reservation there if you are traveling with small children.
- **Pack something to do:** Books, radio plays, art supplies and snacks make long journeys entertaining.
- **Stroller:** A foldable stroller is much more practical on the train than a large stroller.

Further tips in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

## Luggage: less is more

There is no weight limit on the train, but you must carry your luggage, lift it onto the train and stow it yourself. Stairs at train stations and full luggage racks quickly make large suitcases a burden.

- **One suitcase per person** is enough for most trips. Backpacks are often more practical on stairs.
- **Send luggage ahead:** With a luggage service you can pick up suitcases, strollers or bicycles from home and have them delivered to your accommodation. Plan a few days in advance for this.
- **Plan for a washing machine:** If your vacation rental has one, you'll only need a week's worth of clothes.
- **Valuables on your body:** Laptop, ID and money belong in your hand luggage at your seat.

## On the way: food, space and peace

A long train journey will be more pleasant if you prepare a few things:

- **Take provisions with you.** On-board bistro and dining car are practical, but are not available on every train and are sometimes closed. Water and snacks belong in your hand luggage.
- **Choose a quiet area** if you want to read or sleep. In many long-distance trains there are carriages in which telephone calls are made quietly and music is only listened to through headphones.
- **Sockets and batteries:** There are sockets in many places, but not in all. A power bank is good insurance.
- **Check carriage sequence:** The app shows where your carriage stops on the platform. This means you'll be in the right place when you pull in and won't have to walk through the train with your luggage.

## By bike

If you want to cycle on holiday, you can take your bike on the train, but there are rules:

- **For long-distance transport** you need a bicycle card and a parking space reservation. The parking spaces are limited, booked up early in the season and are not available on every train.
- **On local transport** you usually need your own bike card; the Germany ticket is not valid for bikes in most regions. At rush hour, you may be restricted in taking your bike with you.
- **Alternatives:** Many holiday resorts have good rental bikes and e-bikes. This is often easier than taking your own bike.

More about this in the guide [Bicycle on Vacation](/reisen/fahrrad-im-urlaub/).

## With a dog on the train

Small dogs in a transport box usually travel free of charge. Larger dogs need their own ticket, often half price, and must be on a leash and wear a muzzle. For longer journeys, you should plan water, a bowl and breaks when changing trains. Tips for traveling with four-legged friends in the guide [Holidays with dogs in Germany] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-hund/).

## Delays and passenger rights

Unfortunately, delays are part of everyday life in German long-distance transport. With a little planning, they remain a nuisance rather than a disaster.

**How to plan wisely:**

- Choose connections with a transfer time of at least 20 to 30 minutes, or more for important connections.
- Don't take the last train on the day of arrival so that you still have alternatives in case of cancellations.
- Use the train app to see delays and track changes in real time.

**Your rights if you are late:**

- If you arrive at your destination at least 60 minutes later, you will receive 25 percent of the fare back.
- From 120 minutes it is 50 percent.
- If you miss your last connection due to a delay and can no longer get on, the cost of an overnight stay or a taxi can be reimbursed. Keep receipts and find out beforehand what conditions apply.
- You can apply for compensation online or using a form.

## From the train station to the accommodation

The biggest challenge is often the last stretch: from the train station to the holiday home, farm or campsite.

- **Ask your hosts.** Many will pick guests up from the train station or know the best connection.
- **Use guest cards:** In some holiday regions, local transport is free for overnight guests with the guest card, for example in the Black Forest or the Bavarian Forest. Ask when booking.
- **Dial buses and taxis:** In rural areas, some buses only run on request. Register on time.
- **Rental bike or car sharing:** In many holiday resorts you can rent bikes, e-bikes or cars on site.

When booking accommodation, it is worth checking whether it is accessible by bus and train. What else you should pay attention to can be found in the guide [Hotel, guesthouse, holiday apartment or farm?](/reisen/hotel-pension-ferienwohnung/).

## Barrier-free rail travel

Anyone traveling with a wheelchair, walker or limited mobility can request assistance from the train when boarding, alighting and changing trains. Report your need as early as possible before the trip. Many stations have elevators and ramps, but not all. The train app shows which train stations have no steps and whether elevators are in operation.

## Which destinations can be reached particularly easily by train

Some holiday destinations are even easier to reach without a car than with:

- **Big cities** like Berlin, Hamburg or Munich, where parking is expensive and local transport is good.
- **Baltic Sea resorts** like Binz on Rügen or the seaside resorts in the Bay of Lübeck.
- **Lakes in the foothills of the Alps**, which can be reached from Munich in one to two hours. More in the guide [Bavarian Lakes](/reisen/bayerische-seen/).
- **Car-free islands** like Juist or Spiekeroog, where the car stays on the mainland anyway. More in the guide [Ferry and everyday life on the island] (/reisen/faehre-und-inselalltag/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Change times are too tight.** Five minutes isn't enough if you're late.
2. **Booked a saver fare and missed the train.** If the train is delayed, the ticket expires.
3. **Travel with three suitcases per person.** Stairs and full luggage racks make the journey tiring.
4. **Bike without a reservation on the ICE.** Without a parking space, you may not be allowed to take it with you.
5. **Forget the last mile.** Before you travel, figure out how you will get from the train station to your accommodation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is traveling by train cheaper than driving?

This depends on the route, time and number of people. The train is often cheaper for solo travelers and couples who book saver fares early. Families with children benefit from taking children with them free of charge. In addition to fuel, take into account tolls, parking and wear and tear on your car. Tips in the guide [Plan your vacation budget honestly] (/reisen/urlaubsbudget-planen/).

### How early should I book?

Saver prices are usually available a few months in advance. It is worth booking as early as possible for the start of holidays and public holidays.

### What happens if a train is canceled?

If there is a cancellation, you can usually take another train, even with a cheaper price. Find out about alternatives in the app or at the counter and keep receipts for additional costs. You can find further topics about arrival under [Arrival & Accommodation](/themen/anreise-und-unterkunft/).
