Off the coast of East Frisia lies a chain of seven inhabited islands, lined up like pearls between the Ems and Jade. From a distance they look similar: dunes, a long sandy beach facing the open sea, an island village and the mudflats at the back. But once you get there, you quickly notice that each island has its own character. On one side, cars drive through a small town with a spa house and promenade, on the other you only hear wind and seagulls in the evening. Brochures are of little help when choosing because every island describes itself as the most beautiful. This comparison ranks the seven islands according to what really counts on vacation: size, hustle and bustle or silence, how to get there and the question of who feels comfortable where.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - From west to east: Borkum, Juist, Norderney, Baltrum, Langeoog, Spiekeroog and Wangerooge.
> - There are restrictions on cars on Borkum and Norderney, while the other five islands are car-free for holiday guests.
> - At Juist, Baltrum, Spiekeroog and Wangerooge the ferries depend on the tide, so the departure times change daily.
> - Norderney and Borkum are suitable for a lot of infrastructure and variety, Baltrum and Spiekeroog are suitable for peace and quiet, Langeoog and Juist are suitable for families who need space on the beach.
> - Each island and each shipping company determines its own timetables, baggage rules and tourist fees. Check before booking.

## What all seven have in common

All East Frisian Islands are located in the Lower Saxony Wadden Sea National Park, which is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Large parts of the dunes, salt marshes and beaches are protected areas. Stricter rules apply in quiet zones, such as a ban on walking. Stay on the paths in the dunes as the plants hold the sand in place, protecting the island itself.

Each island has its own mainland port. If you remember the names, you will quickly understand how the journey works:

| island | Mainland port | Cars on the island | Character in a sentence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Borkum | Emden | yes, with restrictions | Largest island, far out, with island railway and lots of space |
| Juist | North dike | no | Long and narrow, horse-drawn carts instead of cars |
| Norderney | North dike | yes, with restrictions | Small town by the sea with a spa house and spa architecture |
| Baltrum | Neßmersiel | no | Smallest inhabited island, very quiet |
| Langeoog | Bensersiel | no | Family island with island railway and wide beach |
| Spiekeroog | Neuharlingersiel | no | Quiet, green and with a historic village center |
| Wangerooge | Harlesiel | no | Easternmost island with island railway and wide beach |

A tried and tested mnemonic for the order from east to west is: “Which sailor is in bed with Nanni?” The first letters stand for Wangerooge, Spiekeroog, Langeoog, Baltrum, Norderney, Juist and Borkum.

Between the islands there are sea gates through which the water flows at low and high tide. Swimming there is life-threatening, and mudflat hikes between the mainland and the island are only possible with a local guide. More about this can be found in [Understanding ebb and flow](/reisen/ebbe-und-flut/).

## The seven islands in portrait

### Borkum: big, far out, with space for everything

Borkum is the largest and westernmost of the seven islands and is the furthest away from the mainland. The crossing from Emden takes longer than to the other islands. Borkum has a lot of space for this: long beaches, an extensive network of cycle paths, an island railway that takes guests from the harbor to the town, and a town with a promenade, shops and restaurants. Cars are allowed, but not everywhere and not at all times. Many guests still leave their cars on the mainland because the island can be easily explored by bike, bus and train. Borkum suits families who want variety and rainy weather options, and everyone who likes cycling.

### Juist: long, narrow and no cars

Juist stretches long and narrow between the mudflats and the open sea and is nicknamed “Töwerland”, magic land. There are no cars for guests; loads are moved using horse-drawn carts and electric carts. The clatter of hooves characterizes the atmosphere of the place. The beach stretches almost across the entire island and offers space even in high season. If you like taking long walks on the beach or cycling along the mudflats, this is the right place for you. The ferries from Norddeich depend on the tide, so your arrival and departure times change with the tide calendar.

### Norderney: the most urban island

Norderney is considered the oldest North Sea spa in Germany and still looks like a small town by the sea today. White spa architecture, a spa building, cafés, shops, culture and gastronomy ensure that there is no boredom even on gray days. Cars are allowed with restrictions, but the best way to get around town is on foot, by bike or on the island bus. Things quickly become quiet in the east of the island: there are wide dunes and beaches where you hardly meet anyone. Norderney suits couples and families who value comfort and choice, and anyone who doesn't want to give up city life entirely.

### Baltrum: the smallest and quietest

Baltrum is the smallest inhabited East Frisian island and is often referred to as the “Sleeping Beauty of the North Sea”. There are no cars here, little hustle and bustle and short distances: you can reach almost everything on foot. It gets quiet early in the evening. Baltrum is ideal for people who really want to switch off, read and go for a walk. Anyone who expects a lot of variety, shopping opportunities or a wide range of leisure activities will be disappointed. The ferry from Neßmersiel runs depending on the tide.

### Langeoog: the classic family island

On Langeoog, the island train takes you from the harbor to the village, which is the first highlight for children. The island is car-free, the place is manageable and the beach is wide and long. The landmark is an old water tower in the middle of the village. Behind the beach, high dunes and paths run through the landscape, which can be explored by bike or on foot. Langeoog combines peace and infrastructure well and is therefore popular with families. The crossing from Bensersiel is easier to plan compared to the neighboring islands, which are dependent on the tide.

### Spiekeroog: quiet, green and original

With its historic village center, old trees and gardens, Spiekeroog appears greener than many neighboring islands. The old island church is one of the oldest buildings on the East Frisian Islands. There are no cars and life moves at a slow pace. In summer, a historic horse-drawn tram operates as a museum railway. Spiekeroog suits people who are looking for peace and quiet, but don't want to completely miss out on a lively village. The ferry from Neuharlingersiel depends on the tide.

### Wangerooge: far in the east, far on the beach

Wangerooge is the easternmost of the seven islands. Here, too, an island train runs from the harbor to the town, an old lighthouse characterizes the townscape, and the beach is long and wide. The island is car-free and suitable for families as well as couples who want to combine peace and quiet with some infrastructure. The ferry from Harlesiel depends on the tide.

## Why some ferries depend on the tide

The flat mud flats lie between the mainland and the islands. The shipping channels to some ports are only navigable when the water level is sufficient. That's why the ships to Juist, Baltrum, Spiekeroog and Wangerooge do not sail at fixed times, but according to a timetable that changes daily. Anyone traveling to Juist can travel in the morning one day and only in the afternoon a week later. Borkum, Norderney and Langeoog are largely served according to a fixed timetable.

For your planning this means:

1. **First the timetable, then the train connection.** First look at the shipping company's timetable for your arrival day and plan the train or car journey backwards from the departure time.
2. **Plan buffer.** If you miss a tide-dependent ferry, the next one won't be able to leave until many hours later.
3. **Clarify parking.** There are parking lots and garages at the mainland ports that can be full during peak season. Find out in advance.
4. **Please note the weather.** Ferry services may be canceled during storms or in severe winters when there is ice.

Many islands can also be reached by small island planes from the mainland. We explain in detail how tickets, luggage containers and everyday life work without a car in [Taking the ferry to the island](/reisen/faehre-und-inselalltag/).

## Which island is right for you?

| What you're looking for | Good choice | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Family with small children | Langeoog, Juist, Wangerooge | Car-free, wide beaches, manageable locations |
| Family with a need for variety | Norderney, Borkum | Many offers even when it rains, large selection of accommodation |
| Real peace | Baltrum, Spiekeroog | Small places, little hustle and bustle, short distances |
| Active by bike | Borkum, Norderney, Juist | Long distances, lots of space to drive |
| Easy to plan arrival | Norderney, Borkum, Langeoog | Largely fixed timetable |
| Culture, cafes, shopping | Norderney | Urban character with resort architecture |
| Short vacation with little travel | Norderney, Baltrum | Short crossing from the mainland |

An example from everyday life: A family with two children of daycare age wants above all a beach, sandcastles and peace and quiet when going for a walk, without cars in town. Langeoog or Wangerooge do this well, the island railway becomes a permanent excursion destination. For a couple who just wants to read and sleep after a busy year, Baltrum or Spiekeroog is the right place for them. A group of friends who like to eat out in the evening and cycle during the day will feel at home in Norderney or Borkum.

## Travel time: when which island is worth it

The main season is the summer holidays, plus Easter, Pentecost and the autumn holidays. Then accommodation and ferries are in demand, and there is a lot going on on Norderney and Borkum. In spring and autumn it becomes quieter, the beaches are emptier and many migratory birds rest in the Wadden Sea. In winter the islands are very quiet, some restaurants and shops close temporarily and ferries run less frequently. If you are looking for peace and quiet, you will find it on all seven islands. Before a winter vacation, check which offers are open.

A tourist tax is charged on all islands and varies depending on the island, season and age. How it works and what it covers can be found in [beach chair, tourist tax and beach sections](/reisen/strandkorb-und-kurtaxe/).

## Typical mistakes when choosing an island

1. **Decide based on photos only.** The beaches are similar, but the character of the places is not. Think about what you want your evening to look like.
2. **Check the ferry schedule too late.** On islands that depend on the tide, the desired connection may be inconvenient on the day of arrival.
3. **Taking too much luggage.** On car-free islands, you carry or push everything from the luggage container to the accommodation.
4. **Take the car with you without needing it.** Allowed on Borkum and Norderney, but often unnecessary and expensive.
5. **Forget the North Frisian Islands.** If you don't like an East Frisian island, it's worth taking a look at Schleswig-Holstein, see [Assessing the North Frisian Islands correctly](/reisen/nordfriesische-inseln/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which East Frisian island is the quietest?

Baltrum and Spiekeroog are considered the quietest islands. On both there are no cars, small towns and hardly any evening entertainment. Juist and Wangerooge are also very quiet outside of the high season.

### Can I drive to the East Frisian Islands?

Only to Borkum and Norderney, and there with restrictions too. On all other islands, holiday guests do not have their own car. The car remains in a parking lot at the mainland port.

### Can I visit multiple islands in one vacation?

Yes, there are excursions to neighboring islands in season, and some islands can easily be combined with the mainland. However, plan such trips generously, especially for connections that depend on the tide. The shipping companies and tourist information centers know the current offers.

### Do I need a bike on the islands?

A bike is practical on Borkum, Norderney, Juist and Langeoog; you can usually rent it on site. On Baltrum you can do almost everything on foot. If you would like to take your own bike with you, clarify this with the shipping company in advance.
