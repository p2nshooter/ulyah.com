For many people, camping is the best way to spend their vacation: waking up in the morning with a view of a lake, coffee in front of the tent, children making friends on the site from day one, and the smell of barbecue and campfire in the evening. Germany has around three thousand campsites, on coasts, lakes, rivers, in the mountains and on the outskirts of cities. To make your vacation as relaxing as it sounds, it's worth a little preparation. This guide explains the most important rules, costs and tips.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Wild camping is generally prohibited in Germany. Stay overnight at campsites, RV parks or official trekking sites.
> - In high season, popular places by the sea and lakes are fully booked early. Reserve early.
> - The price usually consists of parking space, people, electricity, spa tax and extras. Compare the total price.
> - Lunch and night quiet times apply at almost all sites.
> - For electricity you usually need a blue CEE plug, for gas you often need an adapter or the usual gray exchange bottles.

## Tent, caravan or motorhome?

**Tent:** The cheapest and most natural option. Ideal for short tours, cycling trips and canoe hikes. Comfort is limited in rain and wind, and on the coast you need long pegs and good bracing.

**Caravan:** Comfortable, with a bed, kitchen and often its own toilet. Good for long stays in one place. To tow you need a suitable car and, depending on your weight, an appropriate driving license.

**Motorhome:** Maximum flexibility for round trips. You can be somewhere else every day without having to set up and take down. Motorhomes can also be rented, but at high prices in the high season.

**Rental accommodation on the site:** If you don't have your own equipment, you can book mobile homes, wooden cabins, sleeping barrels or fully furnished tents. These offers are often referred to as glamping and are a good introduction to camping.

## Camping site, parking space or trekking site?

**Campsites** offer pitches for tents, caravans and mobile homes, as well as sanitary buildings with showers and toilets, often also a kiosk, restaurant, playground and swimming area. Large family pitches have entertainment, pools and sports facilities.

**RV sites** are easier. They are often located on the outskirts of towns, at harbors or at wineries and offer space for overnight parking, sometimes electricity, water and waste disposal. Tents are usually not allowed there, and camping with chairs and awnings is often not welcome.

**Trekking sites** are simple campsites in the forest that you can only reach on foot. They are found in several low mountain ranges, such as the Palatinate Forest, the Eifel, the Black Forest and the Sauerland. They usually only have a wooden platform for the tent, sometimes a composting toilet. You book it online for one night. For hikers they are the legal alternative to wild camping.

## Why wild camping is forbidden

In Germany you are not allowed to just pitch your tent anywhere in nature. The rules differ between the federal states, but it is forbidden almost everywhere in the forest, in protected areas, on beaches and on foreign land. On private property you need the owner's permission.

There is a gray area for motorhomes: A single overnight stay in a public parking lot to get back on the road is tolerated in many places as long as you don't camp, i.e. don't set up any chairs, tables or awnings. However, many communities have signs that expressly prohibit overnight stays. You are on the safe side at RV parks.

There is a good reason for the rules: wild camping disturbs animals, leaves behind trash and increases the risk of fires in dry summers.

## Find the right place

A few questions will help you compare:

- **Location:** Right on the water or would you rather be quiet in the hinterland? Front row seats are more expensive and book up quicker.
- **Size and style:** Large family sites have a lot to do, small sites have more peace and quiet. Some places are aimed at long-term campers, others at transients.
- **Facilities:** Cleanliness of the sanitary facilities, playground, swimming area, shopping facilities, WiFi.
- **Ratings and classification:** Many places have stars according to a nationwide classification system, as well as reviews in camping guides and portals.
- **Dogs:** Not every site allows dogs, some have their own dog areas.

**Reservation:** During the summer holidays, places on the North and Baltic Seas, on Bavarian lakes and on the Mecklenburg Lake District are often fully booked months in advance. Some places do not accept reservations and allocate spaces upon arrival. Then it's worth arriving in the morning.

## How much does camping cost?

Camping is not automatically cheap. The price usually consists of several items:

| Post | What's behind it |
| --- | --- |
| parking space | Basic price per night, depending on size and location |
| people | Price per adult and child |
| Electricity | Flat rate per night or billing based on consumption |
| Spa tax | In many holiday destinations per person per night |
| Showers | Sometimes included, sometimes with a coin or chip |
| Dog | Fee per night |
| Extras | Car, second tent, visitors, garbage |

In high season, families often pay not much less for popular seaside spots than for a simple holiday apartment. Camping is significantly cheaper in the off-season and in the hinterland. Tips for budget planning in the guide [Plan your holiday budget honestly] (/reisen/urlaubsbudget-planen/).

## Rules at the campsite

There are rules for each pitch. The most important points are the same almost everywhere:

- **Lunchtime** usually in the early afternoon for one to two hours.
- **Night quiet** usually from 10 p.m. until morning. The barrier is then closed and cars stay outside.
- **Barbecuing** is usually permitted, open fires only in designated areas. In dry summers, all fires may be prohibited.
- **Separate waste** according to the site's requirements.
- **Empty wastewater and chemical toilets** only at the disposal stations.

## Technology: electricity, gas and water

- **Electricity:** Blue CEE sockets are standard on German sites. You need a sufficiently long cable with a suitable plug that is suitable for outdoor use. Always unroll the cable completely when using it, otherwise it can get hot.
- **Gas:** In Germany, gray steel bottles are common in the barter system. Bottles from abroad often cannot be exchanged. An adapter or a German exchange bottle solves the problem. Check hoses and regulators before every trip.
- **Water:** You can tap fresh water at supply and disposal stations. Having your own hose with drinking water quality is practical for motorhomes.

## Camping with children

A campsite is often the perfect vacation spot for children: playing outside, making friends, riding a bike and sitting around the campfire in the evening.

- **Family pitches** have playgrounds, a children's program and often a swimming area. Ask whether the program takes place during your travel period.
- **Do not choose a pitch directly on the water** if there are small children, or agree on clear rules.
- **Pack warm clothes for the evenings**, even in summer.
- **Flashlight or headlamp** for each child, for going to the toilet in the dark.

Further ideas in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

## Packing list for your first camping holiday

When you go camping for the first time, you tend to forget little things that will be missing later. In addition to the tent, sleeping bag and sleeping pad, these things should be in your luggage:

- **Hammer or rubber mallet** for the pegs and a few spare pegs.
- **Tarpaulin or tent pad** that protects the tent from moisture and stones.
- **Headlamp** and a lantern for the awning.
- **Camping stove** with matching cartridge, lighter and a pot with lid.
- **Dishwashing bowl, dishwashing liquid and tea towel.** Washing up is done in the dishwashing house, not at the lake.
- **clothesline and pegs** for towels and bathing suits.
- **Small first aid kit**, mosquito repellent and sunscreen.
- **Swimming shoes** for the showers and sandals for the way there.
- **Multiple socket and CEE adapter** if you want to use electricity.

A trial weekend at a place nearby will quickly show you what's missing before the big journey begins.

## Weather and safety

- **Thunderstorms:** A tent is not a safe place during a thunderstorm. Go to the sanitary block, a permanent building or the car.
- **Storm:** Strong winds may occur along the coast and large lakes. Tension the tent well, use long pegs and take down awnings in good time if there is a storm warning.
- **Heat:** A pitch with shade makes a big difference in midsummer.
- **Valuables:** Don't leave anything valuable open in the tent. Many places have lockers at reception.

## Beautiful regions for camping

- **Baltic Sea** with places behind the dunes, for example on Fehmarn or Usedom. More in the guides [Fehmarn](/reisen/fehmarn/) and [Usedom](/reisen/usedom/).
- **Mecklenburg Lake District** with places right on the water and canoe tours from lake to lake. More in the guide [Mecklenburg Lake District](/reisen/mecklenburgische-seenplatte/).
- **Bavarian lakes and foothills of the Alps** with mountain views.
- **Rivers such as the Moselle, Rhine and Danube** with cycle paths in front of the square.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Leave without a reservation during the summer holidays.** Popular places are then full.
2. **Wild camping because the site is fully booked.** This is prohibited and can be expensive.
3. **Short pegs on the coast.** They don't hold in the sand.
4. **No CEE adapter included.** There is no power without a suitable plug.
5. **Only compare the basic price.** Electricity, people and spa tax sometimes double it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is camping cheaper than a holiday apartment?

In the low season and away from the coast, usually yes. In high season in popular locations, the difference for families is often smaller than expected. The guide [Hotel, guesthouse, holiday apartment or farm?] (/reisen/hotel-pension-ferienwohnung/) offers a comparison of all types of accommodation.

### What is a permanent camper?

Long-term campers rent a pitch for the whole season or the whole year and set up a caravan with an awning, often with a small garden. In some places they make up the majority of the guests. This is not a disadvantage for holidaymakers passing through, but the atmosphere is quieter and more familiar. When choosing, pay attention to how many places are reserved for tourists.

### Can you camp in Germany in winter?

Yes. Many places in the Alps, the Black Forest and low mountain ranges are open all year round, some offer heated sanitary facilities and drying rooms for ski clothes.

### Can I leave my motorhome on a lake overnight?

Only where it is allowed. Many riverside parking lots expressly prohibit overnight stays. Use RV parks or campsites. You can find further topics about travel and accommodation under [Travel & Accommodation](/themen/anreise-und-unterkunft/).
