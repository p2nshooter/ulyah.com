The idea is tempting: a ticket for buses and trains throughout the country and you're off to the Baltic Sea, Lake Constance or the Spreewald. Many people have the Germany ticket in their pocket anyway for the way to work or university and wonder whether it is also enough for their vacation. The answer: often yes, but not everywhere. Anyone who knows the limits will experience a cheap and relaxed journey. Anyone who doesn't know them stands at the ferry pier and still has to buy a ticket, or they sit on the ICE and explain to the train attendant why they thought the ticket was also valid here. This guide explains the basic rules, shows you how to realistically plan long journeys on regional trains, and tells you which special cases you should check before traveling.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The Deutschlandticket is generally valid on all local transport in Germany: regional trains, S-Bahn, subway, tram and bus, all in 2nd class.
> - Long-distance trains such as ICE, IC and EC, long-distance buses and most ferries to holiday islands are not included.
> - The ticket is a personal subscription, is valid for one calendar month and can be canceled monthly with a notice period from your provider.
> - Bicycles, dogs, children and exceptions are sometimes regulated differently by countries and transport associations.
> - Rules and price have already changed since launch. Before each trip, check your provider's current conditions.

## What the Deutschlandticket basically covers

The Deutschlandticket is a monthly ticket for local public transport that is valid nationwide. You can use it to board regional trains and regional express trains, regardless of which company operates them, as well as S-Bahn, subway, trams and buses. It applies in 2nd class and also when a regional train is replaced by a replacement bus.

For your holiday this means: the entire route from your front door to the coast can often be covered by local trains, and at your holiday destination you can travel by bus and train without an additional ticket. This is particularly convenient in cities: you can get on and off as often as you like throughout the day in Hamburg, Berlin or Munich. In Hamburg, the harbor ferries are even part of local transport, so you can travel across the Elbe with a ticket.

The ticket is always valid for one calendar month, not for thirty days from purchase. You get it as a mobile phone ticket in an app or on a chip card, depending on which transport company you take out it with.

## Which it basically doesn't cover

The most important border is long-distance traffic. ICE, Intercity and Eurocity are not included, nor are long-distance buses and private long-distance trains. On individual sections, countries release long-distance trains for local transport tickets. Such exceptions are agreed regionally, change occasionally and should always be checked in the timetable information or with the transport association before relying on them.

The second major limit is the ships to the holiday islands. The ferries to the East Frisian Islands, to Föhr and Amrum, to Hiddensee or Helgoland are operated by shipping companies with their own tariffs. You need your own ticket for them. We explain how the crossing, luggage transport and everyday life on a car-free island work in [Taking the ferry to the island](/reisen/faehre-und-inselalltag/).

Other things that usually cost extra or follow their own rules:

- **Bicycle transport:** It is not included in the ticket. Depending on the country or association, you will need a bicycle card; sometimes it is free to take along, sometimes it is restricted at certain times.
- **1. Class:** not included. Some associations offer surcharges.
- **Tourist railways and ships:** Historic narrow-gauge railways, museum railways, island railways, mountain railways and excursion ships often have their own tariffs.
- **Night trains:** They are long-distance trains.
- **Travel abroad:** The ticket is only valid on individual cross-border sections. Check carefully which train station it takes.

## Realistically plan long routes only with regional trains

If you travel across Germany, you can travel for a whole day on local trains. A family from northern Hesse who wants to go to the Baltic Sea spends significantly longer on the train with regional trains than with a long-distance train and changes several times. This can be a beautiful travel day if it is well planned and a stressful one if not.

This is how you do it:

1. **Only search for local transport.** In the usual timetable information you can limit the connection search to local transport. Compare the result with the fastest connection so you know how much time you're saving.
2. **Count transfers and plan buffers.** A connection with a few minutes of transition time often works in everyday life, but not always in holiday traffic with suitcases, strollers and platform changes. It's better to choose a connection with generous connection times, even if it takes longer overall.
3. **Share the trip.** If it's a very long journey, it's worth staying overnight on the way. A town halfway through becomes a little bonus excursion and you arrive rested the next day.
4. **Start early.** Early in the morning, regional trains heading to the coast are usually emptier than at midday, and there is more leeway in the event of delays.
5. **Don't max out the last connection.** Don't plan on taking the last bus of the day or the last ferry. If a train is canceled, you'll be left with no way to continue your journey in the evening.
6. **Reduce luggage.** There is little space for large suitcases on regional trains. Backpacks and smaller bags are easier to handle. If you need to take a lot with you, you can send your luggage to your accommodation in advance using a parcel or luggage service.

Also, expect to the fullest. During the summer holidays, on long weekends and when the weather is nice, routes to the coast and popular lakes are in high demand. Seats cannot be reserved on most regional trains and the bicycle compartments fill up quickly. If you drive on a Tuesday morning instead of a Saturday lunchtime, things are often much easier. You can find many more tips for reservations, luggage and delays in [Go on vacation by train](/reisen/mit-der-bahn-in-den-urlaub/).

### Combine instead of doing without

You don't have to choose between long-distance trains and Germany tickets. A sensible solution is often to cover the long distance with a long-distance train and use the Germany ticket for the last part and the local excursions. Please remember: If you combine separate tickets and a delayed long-distance train cancels the connection, the same claims do not automatically apply as with a one-way ticket. Read your provider's passenger rights before planning this and plan the transition generously.

## Understand the subscription logic

The Deutschlandticket is only available as a subscription. It automatically renews month to month until you cancel. Cancellation takes place with a deadline set by your provider. Anyone who misses the deadline will pay for another month. Many people only use the ticket for the month of travel and cancel it immediately after completing it. Check beforehand whether your provider allows this and by when you have to cancel.

The ticket is personal and non-transferable. Official identification may be required during checks. So you can't pass it on to your sister while you're on vacation yourself.

Pay attention to the calendar month: a vacation from July 25th to August 8th is two months apart. Then you need the ticket for July and August. In such a case, calculate whether it is worth it or whether individual tickets are cheaper.

A cell phone ticket requires a charged battery. Open the ticket in the app before departure, take a power bank with you and consider whether a chip card would be more practical for you. Especially in dead spots on the coast or in the country, it is reassuring if the ticket has already been saved.

## On site: guest card, bus and on-call bus

The Germany ticket is often the most valuable at the holiday destination. You take the bus to the nearest seaside resort, take the regional train to a trip into the city or take the tram to the museum. Some holiday regions also offer guest or spa cards, which allow you to use buses and trains in the region without an additional ticket. When booking, ask what your guest card contains. Sometimes you only need the Germany ticket for arrival and departure.

In the countryside and on the coast during the off-season, buses run less frequently. Check the timetable for the week of your stay, not just the day of arrival, as holiday and school timetables differ. In many places, on-demand buses or on-demand services supplement the network. You have to order them in advance via phone or app, often with a certain lead time. The Germany ticket is usually valid there, but sometimes there is a comfort surcharge.

## Special cases that you should check before traveling

- **Children:** Children under six years of age generally travel free on local transport. Older children generally need their own ticket. Some associations have their own transport rules, but these do not apply everywhere.
- **Dog:** Small dogs in a transport box travel free of charge in many places, larger dogs need their own ticket depending on the association. More about traveling with your four-legged friend can be found in [Holidays with dogs in Germany](/reisen/urlaub-mit-hund/).
- **Bike:** Check the rules of each country you ride through. If you would like to take your bike with you, you will find information in [Bicycle on vacation](/reisen/fahrrad-im-urlaub/).
- **Long-distance train approvals:** Only use where they expressly apply.
- **Border places:** If you are planning a day trip to a neighboring country, check where the ticket lasts.
- **Delays:** Your rights are governed by your passenger rights and the conditions of your provider. Some countries also offer a mobility guarantee in local transport. Don't rely on being able to take a long-distance train without your own ticket.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Just get on the ICE.** If you don't have a long-distance ticket, you'll travel there without a valid ticket. Pay attention to the type of train in the information.
2. **Forget the ferry.** Buy the crossing from the shipping company in good time, preferably in advance in the high season.
3. **Overlook the calendar month.** A vacation over the turn of the month requires a two-month subscription.
4. **Postpone the cancellation.** Put the deadline in your calendar immediately after graduation.
5. **Choose connections that are too tight.** It's better to arrive an hour later than to be stuck at a train station for an evening.
6. **Assume that everyone is allowed to ride.** Children aged six and over, bicycles and dogs need their own tickets depending on the region.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Germany ticket valid on the ferry to the island?

Not on most ferries to holiday islands because they are operated by shipping companies with their own tariffs. Ferries that are part of a city or regional local transport tariff, such as the port ferries in Hamburg, are usually included.

### Can I get to Sylt with the Deutschlandticket?

Sylt can be reached via the Hindenburgdamm with regional trains, and these are part of local transport. The car train to the island, on the other hand, is a separate offer with its own ticket. Check the current timetable before traveling, as the route is in high demand during the season.

### Is the Germany ticket worth it for just one vacation?

This depends on the route, travel duration and excursions. Calculate how much it would cost to travel with single tickets and add the local journeys. The more you travel around the holiday destination by bus and train, the more it's worth it. If you get a guest card with local transport, you may not even need it on site.

### Where can I find the binding rules?

With the transport company with which you take out the subscription and with the transport associations in your travel region. There you will find the tariff regulations, notice periods and regional special rules.
