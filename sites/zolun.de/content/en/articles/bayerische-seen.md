Bavaria has lakes for almost every taste: large bodies of water with islands and castles, crystal-clear mountain lakes between steep rock faces, swimming lakes just outside Munich and warm, shallow lakes in Franconia where children can play in the water for hours. At the same time, they differ greatly in water temperature, hustle and bustle and how to get there. This guide introduces the most famous lakes, compares them honestly and shows how you can reach many of them without a car.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Most of the large lakes are located in the foothills of the Alps between Munich and the mountains. Many can be reached by train from Munich in one or two hours.
> - The Chiemsee is the largest lake in Bavaria, with Herrenchiemsee Castle and the Fraueninsel.
> - The Königssee in the Berchtesgaden National Park is the most spectacular, but not a classic bathing lake.
> - Alpine lakes stay cool even in summer. The lakes of the Franconian Lake District are warmer.
> - On the large lakes, flashing orange lights warn of storms. You should know the signals before you go on the water.

## Overview: four types of lakes

- **The large Alpine foothill lakes** such as Chiemsee, Starnberger See and Ammersee are wide, with excursion boats, sailing boats, beaches and places on the shore.
- **The Alpine lakes** such as Königssee, Walchensee, Eibsee or Tegernsee lie between mountains, often have strikingly clear, green or turquoise shimmering water and are cooler.
- **The lakes in front of Munich**, for example in the Fünfseenland, are the city's local recreation area on summer weekends and are accordingly full.
- **The Franconian Lake District** in northern Bavaria consists of younger, artificially created lakes with flat banks, warm water and plenty of space for families.

## Chiemsee: the Bavarian Sea

The **Chiemsee** is the largest lake in Bavaria and is often called the Bavarian Sea. From the bank you can see the Alps in their entire width on clear days.

The main attractions are on the islands:

- **The Herreninsel** with **Herrenchiemsee Palace**, which King Ludwig II had built based on the model of Versailles. The Hall of Mirrors is one of the most magnificent rooms in Bavaria. You can only visit the castle with a guided tour. The island also has a large park with waterfront paths.
- **The Fraueninsel** with a Benedictine monastery, a small fishing village, gardens and fish smokehouses. It can be walked around in an hour and is particularly beautiful in the late afternoon when the day trippers are gone.

The ships to the islands mainly depart from **Prien** and **Gstadt**. In Prien, a historic steam tram takes you from the train station to the harbor during the season. For both islands in one day, you should start early.

The beaches around the lake, for example in Chieming, Seebruck or Übersee, are suitable for swimming. The water becomes pleasant in midsummer, but remains fresher than an outdoor pool.

## Königssee: Fjord in the Alps

The **Königssee** in the **Berchtesgaden National Park** is wedged between steep rock faces and almost looks like a fjord. Its water is exceptionally clear.

Electric boats operate on the lake to keep the water clean. On the way, the boat stops at a rock face where a trumpet signal traditionally echoes. The destination of most trips is **St. Bartholomä** with the pilgrimage church and its red onion domes. If you continue driving, you will reach **Salet** at the southern end and from there it is a short walk to the quiet **Obersee**.

Important to know:

- In high season there are long queues at the jetty. Take one of the first boats in the morning.
- The Königssee is not a classic bathing lake. The water is very cold, private boats and stand-up paddleboards are severely restricted.
- The national park offers excellent hiking trails, from easy riverside paths to challenging mountain tours. Stay on the marked paths.

## Tegernsee and Schliersee

The **Tegernsee** is located about an hour south of Munich and combines mountain scenery with well-kept places such as Tegernsee, Bad Wiessee, Rottach-Egern and Gmund. A circular route around the lake is easy to do on foot or by bike, and ships connect the places. A classic is a snack in the Bräustüberl on the bank.

The smaller **Schliersee** in the neighborhood is quieter and more familiar. Both lakes can be reached by regional train from Munich and are a good starting point for mountain hikes.

## Starnberg Lake and Ammersee

The **Starnberger See** can be reached quickly by S-Bahn from Munich and is in demand on warm weekends. There are villas, parks and bathing spots on its banks. In Berg, a cross in the water commemorates the spot where King Ludwig II died in 1886.

The **Ammersee** west of it is a little more rural. The **Andechs Monastery**, a popular excursion destination with a pilgrimage church and monastery brewery, towers high above the eastern bank. From Herrsching a path leads up through the Kiental. The Marienmünster in Dießen on the west bank is one of the most beautiful rococo churches in the region.

Together with Wörthsee, Pilsensee and Weßlinger See, the two form the **Five Lake District**. If you come during the week, you will find quiet swimming spots here even in summer.

## Walchensee, Kochelsee and Eibsee

The lakes further south in the mountains are among the most beautiful photo motifs in Bavaria.

- **The Walchensee** glows turquoise when the sun is shining and is one of the deepest Alpine lakes in Germany. It is popular with surfers and the water stays cool. From the Herzogstand you have a wide view over the lake.
- **The Kochelsee** is only a few kilometers north, deeper and on the edge of the Alpine foothills. It can be reached by train from Munich.
- **The Eibsee** at the foot of the Zugspitze near Grainau is famous for its clear, shimmering green water and small islands. A circular route of a few hours takes you around the lake.

Parking spaces at all of these lakes fill up early on summer weekends. Come early in the morning or use the train and bus.

## Franconian Lake District: warm water for families

In the north of Bavaria, south of Nuremberg, lie the lakes of the **Franconian Lake District**, including Altmühlsee and Großer Brombachsee. They were created as part of a large hydraulic engineering project and have flat sandy banks, playgrounds, cycle paths and sailing areas.

The big advantage: the water is significantly warmer in summer than in the Alpine lakes, and there is space on the wide banks even on nice days. For families with small children, the Franconian Lake District is often a more relaxed choice.

## Arrival without a car

Many lakes can be easily reached from Munich by regional train or S-Bahn, usually in one to two hours:

- **Chiemsee:** Prien am Chiemsee train station on the Munich–Salzburg route.
- **Starnberger See:** S-Bahn to Starnberg or Tutzing.
- **Ammersee:** S-Bahn to Herrsching.
- **Tegernsee and Schliersee:** Regional train from Munich directly to the lake.
- **Kochelsee:** Regional train to Kochel, then take the bus to Walchensee.
- **Eibsee:** Train to Garmisch-Partenkirchen, continue by bus or cog railway.
- **Königssee:** Train to Berchtesgaden, continue by bus.

The Deutschlandticket is valid for regional transport. You need your own tickets for excursion boats, cable cars and some buses. How you plan this can be found in the guide [Go on vacation with the Germany ticket](/reisen/deutschlandticket-im-urlaub/).

## Bathing and safety

- **Take cold water seriously.** In alpine lakes the water can be well below 20 degrees even in summer. Go in slowly and don't swim far out if you're not used to cold water.
- **Know storm warnings.** There are orange warning lights on many large lakes in Bavaria. If they flash around 40 times per minute, an advance warning applies. About 90 times per minute, a storm warning means: Leave the water or head for the nearest shore immediately.
- **Thunderstorms in the mountains** often occur quickly in the afternoon in summer. Plan boat trips and long swims in the morning.
- **Swim only in permitted places**. In nature reserves and on reed banks, the bank is taboo.

More about safe swimming in inland waters in the guide [Swimming in lakes and rivers](/reisen/baden-in-seen-und-fluessen/).

## Travel time

- **June to August** are the months for swimming. On weekends and during the holidays, the lakes near Munich are very full.
- **May and September** are ideal for hiking, cycling and boating with less activity.
- **Autumn** brings clear mountain views and golden forests, often the most beautiful time for photos.
- **Winter** is quiet. Some ships have restricted sailing and some locks are open.

More about this in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **On summer Saturdays by car to Königssee or Eibsee.** Start early or use the train and bus.
2. **Think of alpine lakes as swimming lakes.** The water is often cold and children cool down quickly.
3. **Storm warning missed.** The flashing lights also apply to stand-up paddlers and swimmers.
4. **Plan both Chiemsee islands in the afternoon.** You need a whole day for the castle and Fraueninsel.
5. **Only head for the well-known lakes.** Smaller lakes and the Franconian Lake District are often more relaxed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which Bavarian lake is warmest?

The lakes of the Franconian Lake District and many smaller, shallow lakes in the foothills of the Alps warm up the most in summer. The mountain lakes such as Königssee, Eibsee or Walchensee remain the coolest.

### Can you visit the Bavarian lakes as a day trip from Munich?

Yes, most of them. Starnberger See, Ammersee, Tegernsee and Chiemsee can be reached by train in one to two hours. Tips for the city itself can be found in the guide [Munich](/reisen/muenchen-staedtereise/).

### Where is it less crowded on the weekend?

The further away from Munich and the smaller the lake, the quieter it is. During the week, early in the morning and in the evening you can also find quiet places at well-known lakes.
