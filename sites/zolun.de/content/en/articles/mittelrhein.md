Hardly any other river landscape in Germany is as famous as the Middle Rhine Valley. The Rhine has dug deep into the slate mountains over around 65 kilometers between Bingen and Rüdesheim in the south and Koblenz in the north. Steep vineyards, cliffs, small half-timbered towns on the banks and a castle on almost every hill: this backdrop has thrilled poets, painters and travelers since the Romantic era and is now a UNESCO World Heritage Site. This guide will help you understand the valley and plan your trip, whether by boat, bike, on foot or by train.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The Upper Middle Rhine Valley between Bingen, Rüdesheim and Koblenz is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
> - The most beautiful section for a boat trip is between Bingen or Rüdesheim and St. Goar, past the Loreley.
> - There is no bridge over the Rhine between Koblenz and Mainz. Car ferries connect the banks.
> - Regional trains run on both banks, and cycle paths and two long-distance hiking trails run along the river.
> - Choose your accommodation carefully: there are railway lines on both sides where freight trains also run at night.

## Orientation: two banks, many places

The Rhine flows here from south to north. The **left bank** belongs to Rhineland-Palatinate, as does most of the **right bank**. Only in the very south, opposite Bingen, is a piece of Hesse, Rüdesheim and Assmannshausen.

The most important places from south to north:

- **Rüdesheim and Bingen** at the southern end, with cable car, Niederwald monument and the Mouse Tower on an island in the river.
- **Assmannshausen and Trechtingshausen** with castles like Rheinstein and Reichenstein.
- **Bacharach**, one of the most beautiful half-timbered towns on the Rhine, with city walls and Stahleck Castle.
- **Kaub** with Pfalzgrafenstein Castle on an island in the middle of the Rhine.
- **Oberwesel** with an almost completely preserved city wall and many towers.
- **St. Goar and St. Goarshausen** at the foot of the Loreley, with the Rheinfels ruins and the Katz and Maus castles.
- **Boppard** on a large bend in the Rhine, with vineyards and a chairlift to the top.
- **Braubach** with the Marksburg.
- **Koblenz** at the Deutsches Eck, where the Moselle flows into the Rhine.

## The Loreley and the castles

The **Loreley** is a slate rock over 130 meters high on the right bank, in a place where the Rhine is narrow and deep. It used to be dangerous for boatmen, and this gave rise to the legend of the woman on the rock, whose singing distracted the boatmen. Heinrich Heine made her world famous with his poem. On the plateau there is a visitor area with views, paths and restaurants.

Some castles you shouldn't miss:

- **Marksburg** above Braubach: It is considered the only hilltop castle on the Middle Rhine that was never destroyed. During guided tours you will see the armory, castle kitchen and battlements.
- **Pfalzgrafenstein Castle** near Kaub: A customs castle on a small island in the river that you can reach with a small ferry. She looks like a stone ship in the river.
- **Rheinfels Castle** near St. Goar: A large ruin with underground passages where you can explore with a flashlight.
- **Schloss Stolzenfels** near Koblenz: A castle in the Rhine Romantic style with gardens and richly furnished rooms.

Many castles are only open to a limited extent in winter. Check the opening times before visiting.

## By boat through the valley

A boat trip is the classic way to experience the Middle Rhine Valley. During the season, excursion boats travel between Mainz, Rüdesheim, Bingen, St. Goar, Boppard and Koblenz. The most spectacular section is between **Bingen or Rüdesheim and St. Goar**: Here you pass most of the castles and the Loreley.

A few tips:

- **Downstream is faster.** Traveling with the current takes significantly less time than against it. Many people go down by boat and return by train.
- Choose **upper deck or window seat** and pay attention to both banks. The castles are spread out to the left and right.
- **Combined tickets** for ship and train or ship and cable car are offered by some providers.
- **During low water** in late summer, trips may be restricted.

## Ferries instead of bridges

There is not a single Rhine bridge between Koblenz and the Mainz and Wiesbaden area. If you want to change sides, use one of the **car ferries**, for example between Bingen and Rüdesheim, near Kaub, between St. Goar and St. Goarshausen or near Boppard. The ferries commute at short intervals during the day, but some do not run in the evenings or at night. They are a nice little crossing for cyclists and pedestrians. A permanent bridge in the middle part of the valley has long been planned.

## Hiking: Rheinsteig and RheinBurgenWeg

Two long-distance hiking trails accompany the Rhine on both sides up on the slopes:

- **The Rheinsteig** on the right bank leads through vineyards, forests and past viewpoints with many meters in altitude. He is considered demanding.
- **The RheinBurgenWeg** on the left bank connects many castles and viewpoints and is a little quieter.

Both can be done in daily stages, with the train returning. There are also shorter circular routes known as dream loops. Good footwear is important because many paths are steep and slippery when wet.

## Cycling on the Rhine

The **Rhine Cycle Path**, which is part of a European cycle route from the Alps to the North Sea, runs on both banks. In the Middle Rhine Valley it runs flatly along the river, often directly on the bank, sometimes next to the road and railway. Beautiful circular tours can be built with ferries and trains. For trips to the heights you need fitness or an e-bike. More tips in the guide [Bicycle on Vacation](/reisen/fahrrad-im-urlaub/).

## Wine and enjoyment

The Middle Rhine Valley is a small but traditional wine-growing region, with the Rheingau bordering it to the south. **Riesling** in particular grows on the steep slate slopes. Many wineries invite you to try them, and in the season there are small restaurants run by winemakers that serve their own wine and simple dishes.

In **Rüdesheim**, the Drosselgasse with wine bars and music is a well-known destination that can be very crowded during the day. The wine towns in the middle part of the valley are quieter.

## Koblenz and Rüdesheim: the gates of the valley

**Koblenz** at the north end is worth a day of its own. The Moselle and Rhine flow together at the **Deutsches Eck**, overlooked by a large equestrian monument. A cable car floats from the banks of the Rhine over the river up to the **Ehrenbreitstein Fortress**, one of the largest preserved fortresses in Europe, with museums and a wide view over the city and both rivers. The old town with its squares, churches and wine bars is easy to explore on foot.

**Rüdesheim** at the southern end is the most famous place on the Rhine and is correspondingly lively. You take a cable car up over the vineyards to the **Niederwalddenkmal**, from where you have a view over the Rheingau and towards Bingen. Hiking trails begin at the top and lead through the forest to the Niederwald hunting lodge and down to Assmannshausen. You can return by chairlift or boat.

Both cities are good starting points if you prefer to live in a larger city with lots of restaurants and explore the valley on day trips.

## Rhine in flames

**Rhine in Flames** takes place on several evenings in summer and early fall: castles and banks are illuminated in Bengali style, and large fireworks displays are reflected in the river as illuminated ships pass by in convoys. The events take place at various locations, including near Rüdesheim and Bingen, in Koblenz and near St. Goar and Oberwesel. On these days, accommodation and ships are fully booked early.

## Arrival and on the way

**By train:** Regional trains run on both banks and connect almost all towns in the valley. Koblenz and Mainz are well connected to long-distance transport. The Deutschlandticket is valid for regional transport. More about this in the guide [Going on vacation by train](/reisen/mit-der-bahn-in-den-urlaub/).

**By Car:** Federal highways run along the river on both sides. Parking spaces in small towns are scarce during the season.

**Accommodation:** Hotels, guesthouses and holiday apartments are available in all towns, often in historic houses. Freight trains run through the valley on both banks, even at night. When booking, ask for a room on the quiet side or choose accommodation a little above the bank. Tips for choosing accommodation in the guide [Hotel, guesthouse, holiday apartment or farm?] (/reisen/hotel-pension-ferienwohnung/).

## Travel time

- **Spring** brings blooming fruit trees and fresh greenery in the vineyards, ideal for hiking.
- **Summer** is peak season for boat trips and festivals.
- **September and October** are harvest time with wine festivals and golden slopes, for many the most beautiful time.
- **Winter** is quiet, many ships don't sail, some castles close.

More in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Only see Rüdesheim.** The most beautiful places are further north in the valley.
2. **Plan a long boat trip against the current.** Go downstream and take the train back.
3. **Look for a bridge.** There are only ferries between Koblenz and Mainz.
4. **Take the room facing the train.** Ask for a quiet location.
5. **Go to castles in winter without checking.** Many then have restricted opening hours.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do you need for the Middle Rhine Valley?

A long day is enough for a boat trip, a castle and a wine town. If you want to hike, cycle and visit several castles, plan three to five days.

### Can you swim in the Rhine?

This is strongly discouraged. The current is strong, the river is a busy waterway, and ships create suction and waves. More about safe swimming in the guide [Swimming in lakes and rivers] (/reisen/baden-in-seen-und-fluessen/).

### Can the Middle Rhine be connected to the Moselle?

Yes. From Koblenz the Moselle valley with wine towns like Cochem leads upstream. The two valleys complement each other well to form a round trip. For more waterfront destinations, see [Lakes & Rivers](/themen/seen-und-fluesse/).
