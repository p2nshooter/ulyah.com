Many vacations turn out to be more expensive than planned. Not because something unexpected happens, but because many small items are missing from the planning: the final cleaning of the holiday apartment, the spa tax, the beach chair, the parking garage in the city, the ice cream in the afternoon. If you plan your budget honestly, you will travel more relaxed because there will be no nasty surprises in your account at the end of your vacation. This guide will show you what costs will be incurred, how to estimate them and where you can save without sacrificing relaxation.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Plan all cost items: travel, accommodation, meals, mobility on site, excursions and additional costs.
> - Most of the surprises are in the additional costs: final cleaning, spa tax, parking, beach chair, fees for dogs.
> - Expect a buffer of around ten percent for unforeseen events.
> - The travel time has the greatest influence on the price. Low season and holiday edges often save more than any discount code.
> - Set a daily budget for food and excursions and keep track of it throughout the trip.

## The cost blocks

**1. Arrival**

- By car: fuel or electricity for the return journey, possibly tolls abroad, parking fees at the holiday destination.
- By train: tickets, seat reservations, possibly luggage service and bicycle maps.
- To the islands: ferry, parking on the mainland, luggage transport.

**2. Accommodation**

- Price per night or week.
- Final cleaning, bed linen and towels for holiday apartments.
- Fee for pets, crib or parking.
- Breakfast if not included.

**3. Catering**

- Shopping for breakfast and dinner in the holiday apartment.
- Visits to restaurants, snack bars, cafés and ice cream.
- Drinks, especially on hot days and on the go.

**4. Mobility on site**

- Local transport, day tickets or the Germany ticket.
- Rental bikes and e-bikes.
- Parking fees at excursion destinations and in cities.

**5. Excursions and experiences**

- Entrance fees to museums, zoos, swimming pools and amusement parks.
- Boat tours, cable cars and guided tours.
- Courses such as surfing, sailing or climbing.

**6. Additional costs**

- Spa tax or guest contribution per person per night.
- Beach chair rental on the North and Baltic Seas.
- Tips, souvenirs, postcards.
- Travel insurance.

## The example calculation: a family week on the Baltic Sea

To make the items tangible, here is an example of a family with two adults and two children who spend a week in a holiday apartment on the Baltic Sea during the high season and travel by car. The amounts are rounded example values. Depending on the region, equipment and season, they can be significantly higher or lower.

| Post | Sample amount |
| --- | --- |
| Holiday apartment, 7 nights | 1.100 € |
| Final cleaning | 80 € |
| Tourist tax for two adults | 40 € |
| Arrival by car, around 1,200 km | 160 € |
| Purchases for breakfast and dinner | 350 € |
| Three restaurant visits | 250 € |
| Ice cream, snacks, drinks on the go | 120 € |
| Beach chair for a week | 90 € |
| Excursions and entrance fees | 200 € |
| Buffer, about ten percent | 240 € |
| **Total** | **around €2,630** |

What is striking is that accommodation accounts for less than half of the cost. Food, excursions and additional costs combined are almost as high. Anyone who only compares the price of the holiday apartment is clearly underestimating the holiday.

## This is how you estimate your own costs

1. **Fixed costs first:** Accommodation with all fees, travel, spa tax. You will know these items almost exactly after booking.
2. **Daily food budget:** Calculate how much you spend on groceries at home each day and add a holiday allowance for restaurants, ice cream and snacks.
3. **Select Excursions:** List the excursions you are sure you want to take and check the prices. For spontaneous ideas, plan an amount per day.
4. **Additional costs:** Beach chair, parking, rental bikes, souvenirs.
5. **Add a buffer:** About ten percent for unforeseen events such as a rain jacket, a visit to the doctor or an additional evening at a restaurant.

A simple table on your cell phone or on paper is enough. It is important to fill it out before your trip and keep a rough track of it along the way.

## Where you can save without going without

**Travel time:** The biggest lever is timing. In the off-season, accommodation is often significantly cheaper, and there are also expensive and cheaper weeks during the holidays. Anyone who is bound to school holidays can choose when the holiday starts or ends when other federal states are still in school. More in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

**Accommodation:** A holiday apartment with a kitchen saves a lot of money, especially for families. Prices are often noticeably lower a few kilometers from the beach or city center. The guide [Hotel, guesthouse, holiday apartment or farm?] (/reisen/hotel-pension-ferienwohnung/) offers a comparison.

**Arrival:** Saver train fares booked early, taking children with you free of charge and the Germany ticket for excursions in the region can reduce mobility costs. More about this in the guide [Go on vacation with the Germany ticket](/reisen/deutschlandticket-im-urlaub/).

**Guest cards:** In many holiday regions you get a guest card with the spa tax, which offers free travel on local transport or reduced entrance fees. Ask for it and use it.

**Food:** Breakfast and dinner in the apartment, lunchtime a picnic on the beach or on the hike. Restaurants at lunchtime are often cheaper than in the evening. Regional markets offer fresh products at fair prices.

**Free Experiences:** Beach, forest, hiking trails, playgrounds, churches, city walks and viewpoints cost nothing. Many museums have free or reduced entry days.

**Bundle small items:** Renting a beach chair for the whole week instead of daily is usually cheaper. The same applies to weekly tickets for local transport and family tickets for leisure activities.

## Three travel styles in comparison

How much a vacation costs depends less on the destination than on the style of travel. Three examples show how different the same week can look:

**Economical:** Arrival with a saver price booked early or Germany ticket, campsite or youth hostel, cooking yourself, hiking, swimming and cycling as the main program. Visits to restaurants are the exception. A week often only costs a fraction of a hotel vacation.

**Balanced:** Holiday apartment in the low season or on the edge of the high season, breakfast and dinner at home, two to three restaurant evenings, selected excursions with entry. This is the style shown in the example calculation above.

**Comfortable:** Hotel with half board or wellness area in high season, a daily excursion, restaurants and cafés without having to look closely. Here the cost can easily double compared to the balanced style.

None of these styles are better than the other. It is important that you know beforehand which one you are choosing and plan your budget accordingly.

## Pay while on vacation

In Germany, card payments are now possible almost everywhere, but not everywhere. Small bakeries, farm shops, beach chair rental companies, fish stalls or inns in the countryside sometimes only take cash. So always have some cash with you. Fees may apply at ATMs outside of your own bank. Find out beforehand where you can withdraw for free.

**Tips:** In restaurants it is customary to tip around five to ten percent; the amount is often simply rounded up. When paying, tell the waitress the total amount you would like to pay.

## Short vacations and city trips: different weights

If you spend a weekend in a city, the costs shift. Accommodation is often more expensive per night than a holiday apartment by the sea because hotels in cities are in high demand on weekends and during trade fairs. Beach chairs and spa taxes are no longer necessary, while local transport, entrance fees and restaurant visits become more important. A day ticket for bus and train, a museum with a family ticket and lunch instead of an expensive dinner reduce the costs noticeably. In many cities there is also an accommodation tax that the hotel adds to the bill. Tips for planning in the guide [Plan a city weekend] (/reisen/staedtewochenende-planen/).

## Insurance: what makes sense

- **Travel cancellation insurance:** Useful for expensive bookings with strict cancellation conditions, for example for holiday homes that are paid for months in advance. Read carefully which reasons are covered.
- **Health insurance:** Within Germany you are covered by your statutory or private health insurance. International health insurance is important when traveling abroad.
- **Liability insurance:** Private liability insurance covers damage that you cause to others, even while on vacation, for example in a holiday apartment. Check whether rented items are included.

## Keep an overview on the go

- **Set a daily budget** and have in mind in the morning what the day may cost.
- **Write down expenses**, either in an app or briefly on paper in the evening.
- **Include children:** A fixed holiday pocket money for school children avoids discussions. More about this in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).
- **Not an expensive excursion every day.** A day at the beach between two excursion days is easy on the budget and on your nerves.
- **Collect receipts:** Receipts for major expenses will help if something needs to be complained about or submitted to insurance.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Only compare the price of the accommodation.** Additional costs and food often make up half of it.
2. **Forget the spa tax.** In many holiday resorts it is charged per person per night. More in the guide [beach chair and tourist tax] (/reisen/strandkorb-und-kurtaxe/).
3. **Don't plan a buffer.** Something always comes up.
4. **Book expensive excursions spontaneously.** It's better to choose in advance and leave the rest flexible.
5. **Travel with card only.** Some small businesses only take cash.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a week's vacation in Germany cost?

This depends heavily on the region, season, accommodation and travel style. A family week on the coast in high season, including all expenses, can easily cost several thousand euros. A couple in a guesthouse in the country in the fall often only spends a fraction of that. The example calculation above shows how the costs are distributed.

### Is vacation in Germany cheaper than abroad?

Not automatically. The journey is shorter and cheaper, but accommodation on popular coasts and lakes is expensive in high season. In the low season and in lesser-known regions, Germany is often an inexpensive travel destination.

### When should I book to save?

It's worth booking early for the summer holidays because cheap accommodation sells out quickly. You can also find good offers at short notice for the off-season. For more planning topics, see [Travel Planning](/themen/reiseplanung/).
