Between Berlin and the Baltic Sea lies a landscape in which water is almost everywhere. Large lakes with wide horizons, small forest lakes with water lilies, rivers and canals that connect everything. The Mecklenburg Lake District is one of the largest contiguous lake areas in Central Europe and a destination for people who are looking for nature, peace and exercise: in a canoe, on a bike, on a houseboat or simply on a jetty in the evening. This guide will show you how the region is structured and how to plan your vacation there.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The lake district is located in the south of Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and extends as far as Brandenburg. It includes well over a thousand lakes.
> - The Müritz is the largest lake that lies entirely in Germany. The Müritz National Park begins on the eastern bank.
> - Waren an der Müritz is the most famous place and is easily accessible by train.
> - Many lakes are connected by rivers and canals. This makes the region ideal for canoe tours and houseboat holidays; for many boats a charter certificate is sufficient instead of a driving license.
> - The best time to travel is May to September, the cranes come in autumn.

## Orientation: how the lake district is structured

The region can be roughly divided into three areas:

- **The large lakes in the west** with Müritz, Kölpinsee, Fleesensee and Plauer See. The water is wide here, there are sailing ports, excursion boats and the most famous places such as Waren, Röbel, Malchow and Plau am See.
- **The Müritz National Park** in the east of the Müritz with forests, moors, small lakes and a rich birdlife. Nature is protected here and motorboats are taboo.
- **The Kleinseenplatte** in the south around Mirow, Wesenberg, Neustrelitz and further to Rheinsberg and Fürstenberg in Brandenburg. Many small lakes here are connected by short stretches of rivers and canals. A paradise for canoeists.

The distances are manageable, but the roads often go around the lakes. What looks close on the map can take half an hour by car. It is sometimes shorter over the water.

## Waren and the Müritz

**Waren an der Müritz** is the tourist center of the region. The old town with brick churches and half-timbered houses is located directly at the harbor, where excursion ships, sailing boats and houseboats dock. In the evening, the harbor promenade is the city's meeting point.

A good introduction to the region is the **Müritzeum**, a nature experience center with large aquariums for native freshwater fish and exhibitions about the landscape of the lake district. When it rains it is one of the best addresses in the area.

The **Müritz** itself is so big that when it's windy it almost looks like a sea, with waves and whitecaps. It is therefore not always suitable for beginners in canoeing or stand-up paddleboarding. A tour on the excursion boat shows you the vastness of the lake without risk.

Other places around the Great Lakes:

- **Röbel** on the west bank of the Müritz, a small town with a harbor and a view over the lake.
- **Malchow**, the island town whose old town is located on an island in the lake and can be reached via a swing bridge.
- **Plau am See** with the lift bridge over the Elde and a long riverside path on Lake Plauer.

## The Müritz National Park

East of the Müritz lies the **Müritz National Park**, one of the most important nature reserves in northern Germany. Forests, moors, meadows and more than a hundred lakes form a landscape in which sea eagles, ospreys and cranes breed. Part of the national park, the old beech forests near Serrahn, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

This is the best way to experience the national park:

- **By bike** on the marked paths. The network is large, the paths are mostly flat, but partly sandy.
- **On foot** on circular paths with observation towers on lakes and moors.
- **On a guided tour** with rangers, for example to observe eagles or to see the cranes in autumn.
- **In a canoe** on the open waters. Not all lakes in the national park are allowed to be accessed. The permitted routes are signposted.

The following applies in the national park: stay on the paths, keep your dogs on a leash, don't take anything with you and don't leave any rubbish behind. Binoculars make the visit much more exciting.

## By canoe through the Kleinseenplatte

The southern lake district is one of the most popular canoeing areas in Germany. Small lakes, short stretches of rivers and canals are lined up so that you can paddle from lake to lake for several days without having to travel the same route twice.

What you should know:

- You can rent **canoes and equipment** in many places, often with return transport. Some providers also provide waterproof bins for luggage and cards.
- You can **stay overnight** at campsites on the water and at water touring rest areas that are specially designed for paddlers. Wild camping is not permitted.
- **Locks** exist on the larger waterways. Waiting times may occur in high season. Small boats are sometimes guided past boat tows or portage points.
- **Wind** plays a role on the larger lakes. In strong winds, paddle close to shore or wait until it calms down.
- **Wear a life jacket**, especially with children, even if the water looks calm.

For a first tour, two or three days on a circular route with fixed overnight stays are enough.

## Houseboat without a license

A houseboat is a popular way to vacation on the lake district: you live on the water, set off in the morning and anchor on a quiet shore or dock in a harbor in the evening.

On certain waterways in Mecklenburg and Brandenburg you are allowed to drive many charter boats without a recreational boat license. The prerequisite is a **charter certificate**, which you will receive after instruction from the landlord. The instruction lasts a few hours and includes theory and practice: navigation rules, locks, mooring and anchoring.

A few tips:

- **Go slower than you think.** Houseboats react sluggishly and wind pushes them sideways.
- **Plan lock times.** Popular locks can get crowded on summer weekends.
- **Read the area map.** It shows channels, shallows, moorings and areas where anchoring is not permitted.
- **Book early.** Houseboats book up quickly during the summer holidays.

## Cycling around the lakes

The lake district is flat to slightly hilly and has a dense network of cycle paths. Some well-known routes:

- **The Müritz circular route** leads around the large lake, past Waren, Röbel and through the national park. You need one to two days to complete the entire tour.
- **The Berlin-Copenhagen cycle path** crosses the region and connects many lakes and small towns.
- **The Mecklenburg Lakes Cycle Path** runs across the country and touches many of the most beautiful lakes.

Many paths lead over forest floor or sand. Wide tires or an e-bike make the ride more pleasant. Tips on rental bikes, luggage and taking them on the train can be found in the guide [Bicycle on Vacation](/reisen/fahrrad-im-urlaub/).

## Swimming in the lakes

There are countless bathing spots in the lake district, from large beaches with jetties, kiosks and lifeguards to small spots on the edge of the forest. The water in many lakes is clear and pleasantly warm in midsummer.

- **Official bathing areas** are regularly examined for water quality. The country publishes the results.
- **Blue-green algae** can occur in individual lakes in warm summers. If the water is greenish, cloudy and streaks, don't go in.
- **Reed belts** are important habitats. Only enter the water in open areas.
- **Be careful when jumping** from jetties: the water depth is often shallower than it looks.

Everything you need to know about safe swimming in inland waters can be found in the guide [Swimming in Lakes and Rivers](/reisen/baden-in-seen-und-fluessen/).

## Arrival and accommodation

**By train:** Waren and Neustrelitz are on the route between Berlin and Rostock and can be reached directly. From there there are buses to the surrounding towns, and in the season there are also bicycle buses. The Deutschlandticket is valid for regional transport. More about this in the guide [Going on vacation by train](/reisen/mit-der-bahn-in-den-urlaub/).

**By car:** The motorway between Berlin and Rostock runs through the middle of the region. A car is practical for trips to small lakes and the national park, but not essential.

**Accommodation:** There are holiday homes on the lake, guesthouses in the cities, estates, many campsites right on the water and of course houseboats. Holiday homes with their own jetty are quickly snapped up during the summer holidays. The guide [Hotel, guesthouse, holiday apartment or farm?](/reisen/hotel-pension-ferienwohnung/) explains how you can find the right type of accommodation.

## Travel time

- **May and June** are ideal for canoeing and cycling tours. Nature is fresh, the days are long, the lakes are still calm.
- **July and August** are bathing time and high season. Then campsites, houseboats and canoe rentals are well booked.
- **September and October** are the time of the cranes. Thousands of birds rest in fields and shallow water areas. Their call in the evening is one of the most impressive natural experiences in northern Germany.
- **In winter** the region is very quiet. Many businesses are closing, but walks on frozen lakes have their own charm.

More about holiday times and weather in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

## Mosquitoes, ticks and sun

Where there is a lot of water, there are also mosquitoes in summer, especially at dusk and on calm evenings. Pack mosquito repellent, long evening clothes, and a mosquito net if you're camping.

Ticks can sit in forests and tall grass. They can transmit diseases such as Lyme disease. Check yourself and your children thoroughly after excursions and remove ticks as early as possible.

The sun is often underestimated on the water. Sunscreen, a hat and enough drinking water are essential on every canoe and houseboat tour.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Underestimate the Müritz.** High waves occur when there is wind. Small boats then stay close to the shore.
2. **Paddle everywhere in the national park.** Not all waters are open.
3. **Wild camping.** Use campsites and water hiking rest areas.
4. **Without mosquito repellent in the evening.** It becomes uncomfortable, especially when there is no wind.
5. **Too many lakes in one day.** The region thrives on slowness. Less distance means more relaxation.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big is the Müritz?

At over one hundred square kilometers, the Müritz is the largest lake that lies entirely in Germany. Only Lake Constance is larger, but it is divided into three countries. More about this in the guide [Lake Constance](/reisen/bodensee/).

### Is the Lake District suitable for families?

Very. Shallow swimming areas, holiday homes with gardens, canoe tours with short stages and the Müritzeum make the region family-friendly. Tips for planning with children can be found in the guide [Holiday in Germany with Children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

### Do I need a license to own a houseboat?

Not for many charter boats on designated waterways in Mecklenburg and Brandenburg. After an instruction you will receive a charter certificate. Larger or more powerful boats and other areas may require a recreational boat license. For more waterfront destinations, see [Lakes & Rivers](/themen/seen-und-fluesse/).
