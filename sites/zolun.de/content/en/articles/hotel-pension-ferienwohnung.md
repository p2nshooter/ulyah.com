The accommodation determines how a vacation feels. In the hotel you are looked after, in the holiday apartment you are free, on the farm you wake up to the crow of cocks and fresh milk, and at the campsite you sleep almost under the open sky. In Germany there is a large selection of all of these options, often in the same place. This guide honestly compares the most important types of accommodation and helps you find the right one for your trip.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Hotels and guesthouses are suitable for short trips, city trips and anyone who doesn't want to cook.
> - Holiday apartments and holiday homes are worthwhile from a week onwards, for families and groups.
> - Farm holidays are ideal for families with children who want to experience animals and nature.
> - Camping is flexible and close to nature, but requires equipment and good planning in high season.
> - Compare not only the price per night, but also final cleaning, bed linen, spa tax, parking space and cancellation conditions.

## Hotel: comfort without hassle

A hotel is the most convenient way to travel. The room is cleaned, breakfast is available, you can get tips at reception and someone is there if you have any problems.

**Advantages:**

- Short stays are possible without any problems, often for just one night.
- No housework, no shopping, no washing up.
- Often centrally located in cities and seaside resorts.
- Additional offers such as a wellness area, restaurant or child care.

**Disadvantages:**

- For families and longer trips it quickly becomes expensive, especially with half board.
- Little space when several people share a room.
- Fixed breakfast times and no own kitchen.

**Good for:** City breaks, short breaks, couples, business travelers and anyone who wants to switch off. You can find tips for the city in the guide [Plan a city weekend] (/reisen/staedtewochenende-planen/).

## Guesthouse and inn: personal and often cheaper

Pensions and inns are smaller than hotels and are mostly family-run. You get a room with breakfast, and often dinner in your own restaurant.

**Advantages:**

- Personal atmosphere and first-hand tips from hosts who know the region.
- Often cheaper than hotels in a comparable location.
- In rural areas it is often the only alternative to a holiday apartment.

**Disadvantages:**

- Less service, sometimes no constantly staffed reception. Clarify the arrival time in advance.
- Facilities vary greatly, from newly renovated to simple.
- Some establishments prefer to accept cash rather than card.

**Good for:** Cycling and hiking trips with changing locations, quiet country holidays and travelers on a budget.

## Holiday apartments and holiday homes: freedom and space

In a holiday apartment you live like you do at home, just in a different place. You have a kitchen, often several rooms, sometimes a balcony or garden.

**Advantages:**

- Lots of space at a lower price per person, especially for families and groups.
- Your own kitchen: You decide when and what you eat. That saves money.
- Peace and quiet, no fixed times, many apartments have their own washing machine.

**Disadvantages:**

- Often a minimum stay, in the high season often a week with a fixed arrival and departure day.
- Final cleaning and sometimes bed linen and towels cost extra.
- Shopping, cooking and cleaning up are all part of the vacation.

**Good for:** Families, longer stays, groups and travelers with dogs. The guide [book holiday apartment] (/reisen/ferienwohnung-buchen/) explains what you need to pay attention to when booking.

## Farm holidays

Many farms rent out holiday apartments or rooms. Guests experience everyday life on the farm: feeding the animals, watching milking, collecting eggs, driving the tractor.

**Advantages:**

- Often the best holiday ever for children: animals, space to run around, nature on the doorstep.
- Fresh products straight from the farm.
- Quiet location, often with a playground, hayloft and barbecue area.

**Disadvantages:**

- Remote location, often difficult to reach without a car.
- Smells and noises are part of it, it can get loud early in the morning.
- The offers are very different: Some farms are active businesses, others are more like holiday resorts with a few animals.

**Good for:** Families with children of kindergarten and primary school age. Ask beforehand what the children are actually allowed to do on the farm. More tips in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

## Camping, RV and glamping

Germany has thousands of campsites, many in beautiful locations on lakes, rivers and coasts.

**Advantages:**

- Closeness to nature and flexibility, especially with a motorhome.
- In many places cheaper than permanent accommodation.
- Community, playgrounds and animation on large family pitches.

**Disadvantages:**

- Equipment is necessary and the weather plays a big role.
- In high season, popular places are booked up early. Reserve early.
- Sanitary facilities are shared.

If you don't have your own equipment, you can book rental tents, mobile homes or wooden cabins at many sites, often as glamping with beds and your own terrace. Wild camping is generally not allowed in Germany. More about this in the guide [Camping in Germany](/reisen/camping-in-deutschland/).

## Youth hostel and hostel

Youth hostels are no longer just for school classes. Many offer family rooms with private bathrooms, breakfast and programs for children. For adults, membership in the hostel association is usually required, which you can take out online. Hostels in big cities are aimed more at young travelers and have shared rooms and double rooms.

**Good for:** Families on a budget, groups, solo travelers and cyclists.

## Three examples: which accommodation suits which trip

**A weekend in Hamburg for two:** A hotel or a small guesthouse in a well-connected area is the best choice here. You're only staying for two nights, you want to go out for dinner in the evening and explore the city during the day. A holiday apartment is rarely worth it because of the final cleaning. Ideas for the city in the guide [Discover Hamburg](/reisen/hamburg-entdecken/).

**Two weeks on the Baltic Sea with two children:** A holiday apartment or holiday home near the beach offers space, a kitchen for breakfast and dinner and a washing machine for sandy clothes. Alternatively, a farm in the hinterland is a nice choice if you have a car.

**A cycle trip along a river:** Guesthouses and inns that cater to cyclists, with lockable storage for the bikes and a hearty breakfast. Can also be booked for one night and often includes tips for the next stage. More about this in the guide [Bicycle on Vacation](/reisen/fahrrad-im-urlaub/).

## Travel barrier-free

Anyone traveling with a wheelchair, walker or limited mobility should ask the accommodation particularly carefully. The term “barrier-free” is not used the same way everywhere. Ask specifically about steps at the entrance, door widths, elevator, walk-in shower and grab bars. Some regions and companies have their offers checked according to a nationwide labeling system that provides precise information on dimensions and features. New hotels are more often barrier-free than historic guesthouses and half-timbered houses.

## The hidden costs

The price per night is only part of the bill. When comparing, pay attention to:

- **Final cleaning** for holiday apartments, often a fixed sum per stay.
- **Bed linen and towels** which sometimes cost extra or need to be brought with you.
- **Spa tax or guest contribution**, which is charged per person per night in many holiday resorts. More about this in the guide [Beach chair and tourist tax] (/reisen/strandkorb-und-kurtaxe/).
- **Parking space**, especially in cities and seaside resorts.
- **Pets**, for which a fee per night is usually required.
- **Breakfast**, which is not always included in the room rate.
- **Cancellation**: Cheap fares are often non-refundable. Consider whether travel cancellation insurance makes sense.

An honest total price for the entire trip is the best basis for comparison. Tips on this can be found in the guide [Plan your holiday budget honestly] (/reisen/urlaubsbudget-planen/).

## Checklist before booking

1. **Check location.** How far is it to the beach, train station, supermarket? A look at the map saves surprises.
2. **Read reviews,** especially the most recent and critical ones. Pay attention to how the host responds to criticism.
3. **Look closely at the pictures.** Are there photos of the bathroom, kitchen and bedroom, or just the view?
4. **Clarify amenities:** WiFi, washing machine, cot, elevator, accessibility, parking space.
5. **Please note arrival and departure times**, especially for holiday apartments with key handover.
6. **Check payment conditions**: deposit, final payment, deposit.
7. **Ask the host directly** if anything is unclear. The answer shows you how reliable he is.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Just look at the price per night.** Additional costs can increase it significantly.
2. **Do not check the location on the map.** “Near the beach” can mean two kilometers.
3. **Book too late.** Good holiday apartments and campsites go away early during the summer holidays.
4. **Drive to a remote farm without a car.** Clarify in advance how you can shop and go on excursions.
5. **Ignore cancellation policy.** Non-refundable rates are cheap until something comes up.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a holiday apartment cheaper than a hotel?

For families and longer stays, usually yes, because you have space for several people and you cook yourself. For one or two nights for two, a hotel is often cheaper because there is no final cleaning.

### When should you book for the summer holidays?

For popular regions on the North and Baltic Seas, in the Alps and on large lakes, it is best to do it in the winter before. More about planning in the guide [Best travel time for a vacation in Germany] (/reisen/beste-reisezeit/).

### Is it worth booking directly with the host?

Often yes. Many guesthouses, holiday apartments and farms also rent directly via their own website or by telephone. Sometimes the price is a little cheaper or the cancellation is more flexible and you can clarify questions immediately. Booking portals are practical for comparison and often offer their own customer service. In both cases, check who your contractual partner is.

### Can I take my dog ​​with me?

This depends on the accommodation. Many holiday apartments, guesthouses and campsites allow dogs for a fee, hotels vary. Always ask beforehand.
