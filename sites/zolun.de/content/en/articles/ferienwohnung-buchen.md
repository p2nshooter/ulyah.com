The apartment looks great in the photos, the price per night seems fair, and you can see the sea from the balcony. Then comes the final bill: final cleaning, laundry packages, tourist tax, dog allowance and a deposit that was never mentioned before. Or worse: the apartment doesn't even exist and the deposit is gone. For many people, a holiday apartment is the most pleasant way to holiday in Germany, especially with children, a dog or in a small group. This guide will show you how to recognize the real final price, read advertisements correctly, classify cancellation conditions, see through scams and arrange handover and departure so that no one ends up arguing. We will clarify whether a holiday apartment is the right type of accommodation for you in [Hotel, guesthouse, holiday apartment or farm?](/reisen/hotel-pension-ferienwohnung/).

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Always compare the final price for your entire stay, not the price per night. Final cleaning, laundry, tourist tax and pet allowance are often included.
> - There is generally no right of withdrawal for accommodation for a fixed travel period, as is the case with online purchase of goods. The cancellation conditions that you agree to when booking apply.
> - Pay via the platform or to landlords whose identities you have verified. Prepayment on a private account after switching to another chat is a classic warning signal.
> - Take photos of any defects immediately upon arrival, report them in writing and clarify before departure what the final cleaning covers.

## Understand the real final price

Many advertisements first show a price per night, often with a small “from” in front of it. This price is sometimes only valid in the low season, only for two people or only for a certain minimum length of stay. During the summer holidays, many landlords on the coast require a minimum number of nights, sometimes also fixed arrival and departure days. Some charge a surcharge for short stays. What you pay in the end is made up of several components:

| Post | What it's about | What you clarify beforehand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Final cleaning | Flat rate for cleaning after departure, usually regardless of the length of stay | Is it included in the price shown? What do you still have to do yourself? |
| Bed linen and towels | Included, can be booked as a package per person or you can bring your own | What applies to cots and sofa beds? |
| Tourist tax or guest contribution | The community charge per person per night, which is usually collected by the landlord | Included in the price or due on site? Is there a guest card? |
| Deposit | Security for damages that will be paid back after departure | How to pay and when will it be returned? |
| pet | Flat rate or amount per night | Does it apply per animal? Are there size or number limits? |
| Energy | Usually included, sometimes according to consumption, such as firewood | Are there consumption-related items? |
| Extras | Parking space, cot, high chair, rental bikes, sauna | What is included, what costs extra, what needs to be reserved? |
| Service fee | Some portals charge a fee in addition to the landlord's price | Does it only appear in the last booking step? |

This is how you compare cleanly: Create a line in a note or table for each apartment on your shortlist and calculate the total amount for exactly your period, including all people, the dog, laundry and cleaning. Only this sum is comparable.

An example from everyday life: A family from Kassel hesitates between two apartments on the Baltic Sea. The first is cheaper per night, but charges extra for final cleaning and linen packages for four people; the second includes both. The second one may end up being cheaper for a long weekend, and the first one for two weeks.

Each municipality sets its own tourist tax, often staggered according to season and age. What it covers and what advantages a guest card can bring can be found in [beach chair, tourist tax and beach sections](/reisen/strandkorb-und-kurtaxe/). [Plan your vacation budget honestly](/reisen/urlaubsbudget-planen/) shows how you can keep an eye on all travel costs together.

## Read the description, photos and reviews critically

### The location

Terms like “near the beach” or “quiet location” are flexible. Look at the address on a map and measure for yourself how far it is to the beach, train station and supermarket, and whether there is a main road or train line directly in front of the house. “Sea view” can also mean a narrow strip of water between two houses. If in doubt, ask for a photo from the window.

### Rooms and sleeping places

“For six people” doesn’t necessarily mean three bedrooms. A sofa bed in the living room or a bunk bed in the hallway often count. This makes a big difference for families with children who go to bed earlier than their parents, or for two couples who are friends. Also check the floor: is there an elevator and how many stairs are there with suitcases or strollers? A floor plan often says more than ten photos.

### Photos and equipment

Wide-angle shots make rooms appear larger. Pay attention to what is missing: no picture of the bathroom, no picture of the kitchen, no picture of the view outside. This may be harmless, but it is a reason to ask. Also think about what is essential for you, such as a washing machine, dishwasher, a lockable room for bicycles, a children's bed or WiFi for the home office. You shouldn't assume anything that isn't explicitly mentioned.

### Reviews

Above all, read the latest reviews, because apartments are being renovated and landlords are changing. The middle ratings are often more insightful than the best and worst. Pay attention to recurring issues, such as noise, cleanliness or problems with handing over keys, and whether the landlord responds objectively to criticism.

A short message with three or four specific questions before booking helps doubly: you get answers and notice how clearly the landlord communicates.

## Cancellation conditions and travel cancellation insurance

Many people assume that they can cancel a holiday apartment booked online free of charge within fourteen days, just like an order in the online shop. As a rule, this right of cancellation does not apply to accommodation that is booked for a fixed period of time. The cancellation policy applies, so read it before booking.

Terms range from flexible to non-refundable. In between there are scales in which your share increases the closer you get to arrival. Also check when the deposit and final payment are due, whether a rebooking is possible and whether you can provide a replacement guest in the event of a cancellation.

Travel cancellation insurance can be useful if you book well in advance, have a large amount at stake, or are traveling with small children and older relatives who could be prevented from going on vacation due to a sudden illness. What is important is which reasons are covered, whether there is a deductible and how pre-existing illnesses are dealt with. Also check whether you already have protection through existing insurance or a credit card. The consumer advice centers offer neutral advice on insurance and your rights in the event of a dispute with a landlord.

If the landlord cancels himself, have this confirmed in writing and clarify quickly whether you will accept an equivalent replacement or demand a refund of the amounts already paid.

## Recognize fake offers

Fraudulent listings target times when accommodation is scarce, such as summer vacations on the coast. You should know these warning signs:

1. **Advance payment to a private account**, especially abroad, or a request to pay via cash transfer services or with voucher cards.
2. **Switch away from the platform.** “Better write to me via messenger or email, then we’ll save the fees.” This means you lose all protection that the platform offers.
3. **Time pressure.** “There are still three interested parties, I need the deposit by this evening.”
4. **A price well below comparable apartments**, in the middle of the high season and in a prime location.
5. **Payment links via email** that lead to pages that just look like a well-known booking platform. Instead of clicking on links, open the app or website yourself.
6. **Same photos in other listings**, with different name or location. A reverse image search in the browser often reveals this.
7. **No tangible landlord**: no landline number, no address, a commercial website without an imprint.

Here's how to check an offer: Find the address on a map and compare the house, surroundings and view with the photos. Call the landlord instead of just writing. Many tourist information centers keep lists of hosts and can tell you whether accommodation is known there. Pay via the platform or with a payment method that allows you to request a refund in the event of a dispute.

If it does happen, every hour counts: contact your bank immediately and ask whether the payment can be recovered. Secure chat histories, emails and account details, file a police report, in many federal states online, and report the ad to the platform.

## Arrival, key collection and house rules

A few days before your arrival, clarify when you can go into the apartment and what will happen if you come later. Many apartments work with key boxes and access codes. Write down the code, address and telephone number of a contact person on site on paper, as the battery is often empty on the day of arrival.

When you arrive, take ten minutes to unpack your bags. Go through each room and take photos of any existing damage, such as stains on the sofa or a crack in the sink. Check that dishes, bedding and towels are present as agreed and see where the fuse box and main water tap are.

You should report defects immediately, in writing and with photos, by message via the platform or by email, and ask for remedial action within a reasonable period of time. Anyone who only mentions a defective refrigerator upon departure or only in a review has bad luck. Whether you have any money back in the event of significant defects depends on the individual case.

The house rules are worth taking a look at on the first evening. Typical points are rest periods, a ban on smoking, grilling on the balcony, dogs on the sofa and beds and waste separation, which is regulated slightly differently by each municipality.

## The day of departure

In many apartments, the final cleaning does not include everything. You will often be expected to wash and put away dishes, empty the refrigerator, dispose of trash separately, and sometimes unfurl the beds. “Swept” means that coarse dirt has been removed, not that you clean like a cleaner. Ask at least the evening before what exactly is expected.

Your checklist for the last morning:

1. Dishes washed, refrigerator empty, garbage disposed of separately.
2. Windows closed, heating turned down, lights off.
3. Charging cable, stuffed animals and dog bowl packed up, a last look under the beds.
4. A few photos of the condition of the apartment.
5. Return the key as agreed and briefly confirm the return via message.

Ask when the deposit will be returned. If amounts are withheld, please provide a comprehensible statement. Your photos of arrival and departure will be worth a lot. Our [Travel Check](/reisecheck/) collects what else you should check before and after every trip.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Compare prices per night.** Calculate the total price for all people and the entire period.
2. **Overlook walk-through rooms.** Ask who sleeps where and if anyone has to go through a bedroom to get to the bathroom at night.
3. **Pay for an off-platform discount.** You give up all protection.
4. **Book non-refundable, although plans are shaky.** The low price becomes expensive if the child gets sick.
5. **Do not coordinate the arrival time.** Especially when traveling to the island with ferries in the evening, it should be clear how you get the key.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the tourist tax included in the rental price?

That is different. Some landlords include them in the total price, others calculate them upon arrival. Ask expressly so that you are not surprised when you arrive.

### Is it worth booking directly with the landlord?

This can be worthwhile if you already know the accommodation and landlord. If an offer is unknown, an established platform or the local tourist information office offers more security. It is crucial that you know who you are signing a contract with and where you are paying.

### What should you do if the apartment is completely different than described when you arrive?

Document everything with photos and videos and immediately inform the landlord and, if you booked via a platform, their customer service. Ask for a remedy or alternative accommodation. What you are entitled to afterwards depends on the individual case; the consumer advice centers will help you assess this.
