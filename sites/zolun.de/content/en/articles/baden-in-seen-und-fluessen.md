A lake in the late afternoon, the water soft and without salt, a jetty on the shore and a meadow for the towel: for many this is the best summer vacation. Lakes and rivers seem more peaceful than the sea, and that is precisely their pitfall. There are no waves to warn, often no lifeguards and no flags. There are currents under a smooth surface, cold water in the depths, edges where the bottom suddenly breaks away, and in midsummer sometimes algae or tiny larvae that irritate the skin. This guide will show you how to find good swimming spots, check the water quality and correctly assess the dangers on lakes, rivers and quarry ponds so that your day on the water ends as relaxed as it began.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Swim preferably at official bathing areas. Your water quality is regularly examined during the bathing season, and the federal states publish the results.
> - Greenish cloudy water with streaks can indicate blue-green algae. If you can no longer see your feet when you're knee-deep in the water, don't go swimming.
> - Itchy red dots after bathing are often bathing dermatitis. It's annoying, but mostly harmless.
> - In large rivers, currents, groynes and boat traffic are dangerous. Only swim in specifically designated areas.
> - Children should always be within sight of the water, and small children should be within easy reach. In an emergency, dial 112.

## Find and check official bathing spots

In Germany, the federal states designate bathing water according to the EU Bathing Water Directive. During the bathing season, these areas are regularly checked for certain bacteria that indicate contamination. Each bathing area is given a rating from “excellent” to “poor”. This is the most reliable basis for you to choose a swimming area.

| Where you look | What you find out there |
| --- | --- |
| Bathing water portal for your holiday country | Location of bathing areas, current measurements, classification, warnings |
| Overviews from the Federal Environment Agency and the European Environment Agency | National and European-wide maps with the classifications |
| Information board at the bathing area | Classification, possible exposures, responsible authority |
| Municipality or tourist information | Guarded beaches, closures, bathing bans |

A measurement is always a snapshot. After heavy rain, water quality can temporarily deteriorate because rainwater washes dirt from fields and roads into the water. Some bathing areas expressly indicate this. If you are unsure after a storm, it is better to wait a day.

An example from everyday life: A family has booked a holiday home in Brandenburg, a small lake is five minutes' walk away. The evening before the first day of bathing, the mother looks at the state's bathing water portal: Is the site designated as bathing water, how is it classified, is there any current information? At the lake itself she reads the information board and before going in she tests the water with the knee-deep test, which you can find below. Three short steps that bring a lot of security.

Also pay attention to differences: A beach with lifeguards from the DLRG or the water rescue service is different from an official but unguarded bathing area. And in some bodies of water, swimming is completely forbidden, for example at many dams that provide drinking water, in nature reserves, in harbors or on shipping routes. Signs on site apply even if other people still go into the water.

## Blue-green algae: when the water becomes green and cloudy

Blue-green algae are actually bacteria called cyanobacteria. They reproduce particularly in warm, nutrient-rich and calm water, often in mid and late summer. Typical signs include greenish to blue-green cloudy water, streaks or a colored film on the surface. The wind often pushes such accumulations towards the shore it is blowing toward, so one bay may be affected and another not.

Some blue-green algae form substances that irritate skin and mucous membranes. If you swallow a lot of water, you may experience nausea, vomiting or diarrhea. Small children are particularly at risk because they play in shallow shore water where algae collects and swallow water. Contaminated water is also dangerous for dogs, so don't let them swim or drink there.

A simple rule of thumb is: If you're knee-deep in water and can no longer see your feet, don't go further in. After a swim in suspicious water, you shower and wash your bathing suits. If you have any symptoms, especially in children, see a doctor and mention where you bathed. Authorities warn about high levels of algae on information boards and portals or temporarily close a bathing area.

## Bath dermatitis: itchy spots after bathing

Sometimes small red, very itchy dots appear on the skin a few hours after bathing. Bath dermatitis is often the cause. The trigger is cercariae, tiny larvae of flukes that actually attack waterfowl and develop in aquatic snails. They burrow into the skin, die there and trigger a reaction. Bath dermatitis is not contagious and usually goes away on its own.

This is how you prevent:

1. Avoid shallow, warm shore areas with lots of aquatic plants where snails hang out.
2. It's better to swim in deeper water instead of sitting in the shallows for a long time.
3. After bathing, rub yourself vigorously with the towel, take a shower if possible, and quickly take off your wet bathing suits.
4. Do not feed ducks and swans, as waterfowl keep the larval cycle going.

The pharmacy can help you with the itching. If you have a severe reaction, in small children or if the areas become inflamed, get it checked by a doctor.

## Cold water, thermocline and your own strength

Lakes warm up from above in summer. Below a warm surface layer lies colder water, separated by the so-called thermocline. When you swim, you feel it when your legs suddenly plunge into cooler water. In deep lakes and mountain lakes, the water stays fresh even in midsummer, and cold muscles tire more quickly.

A quick jump into cool water after a long sunbath is particularly risky. The cold shock can lead to involuntary inhalation, rapid heartbeat and panic. So go in slowly, wet your arms, neck and chest first and only jump where you know the depth and bottom.

Distances are deceiving over water. The other bank looks close and yet is far, and there is no break halfway. It's better to swim parallel to the shore, turn back while you're still feeling strong, and never go far out alone. A colored floating buoy makes you visible to boats and stand-up paddlers and gives you something to hold on to. Alcohol and bathing don't mix.

On large lakes the wind can pick up quickly. Air mattresses, swimming animals and paddleboards then quickly drift away from the shore. On large lakes such as Lake Constance or Lake Chiemsee there are storm warning lights on the banks. If they flash, you return to shore.

## Rivers: more dangerous than they look

A river can appear calm on the surface while there is a strong current underneath. How strong it is depends on the water level, and this can rise further upstream after rain, even if the sun is shining where you are. Stones, branches and flotsam often lie invisible beneath the surface.

There are additional dangers on large rivers such as the Rhine, Elbe and Danube:

- **Gynes**, the stone dams across the shore, form small sandy beaches that look like bathing bays. At their tips, however, whirlpools and back currents arise, and behind them the bottom often drops steeply down to the fairway.
- **Ships** create suction and surge. The water first retreats along the bank and then rises again as a wave. Children in shallow water can be knocked over and dragged along. Ships cannot brake quickly or take evasive action, and swimmers can hardly be seen on board.
- **Weirs, locks, bridges and jetties** are taboo. Rollers form below weirs and pull under the water. Swimming near such facilities is prohibited on federal waterways.

Many rescue organizations therefore generally advise against swimming in large rivers. Some cities and municipalities deliberately designate river bathing areas where the situation has been checked. If you bathe at all, only swim there.

## Quarry lakes and quarry lakes

Quarry lakes are created when gravel and sand are mined. Its water is often clear and inviting, but its banks are treacherous: there are steep cliffs under the water, where you are standing one moment and no longer have any bottom in the next step. Sandy banks can slip and the water is very cold at depth. Where mining is still taking place, excavators, floating pipelines and conveyor belts are involved. These areas are life-threatening and cordoned off.

Many quarry lakes are private property with bathing prohibited, others are designated as bathing waters and have a bathing beach. Follow the signs and only swim on approved sections of the bank. In quarry lakes with rock walls, the following also applies: Never jump into water if you don't know the depth and bottom.

## Bathing rules and children at the water

The rescue organization's bathing rules can be boiled down to a few points: Only go into the water when you feel comfortable, not with a completely full or empty stomach, and leave as soon as you feel cold. Don't jump into unknown waters. During a thunderstorm, leave the water immediately. Non-swimmers stay in the shallow area and inflatable flotation devices are no protection against drowning.

With children there is a clear division: one adult is the water supervisor and only looks at the water, not at the cell phone. If you change, say it out loud. Small children stay within reach. Drowning usually happens quietly, without shouting or waving. Even a child with a seahorse is not yet a safe swimmer. How you can plan your entire vacation in a child-friendly manner can be found in [Holiday in Germany with Children](/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

In an emergency, dial 112 and call loudly for help. Give someone in need an object to hold onto instead of swimming after themselves without training.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Swim in a popular spot just because others are doing it.** Check whether it is officially designated and whether there are any restrictions.
2. **Check the water quality once and then forget it.** It can change after heat or heavy rain. Look at the information board on site.
3. **Swim across the lake.** Stay close to the shore where you can get out at any time.
4. **Let's splash around on the groyne beach.** The current at the heads of the groynes and the suction of the ships are dangerous even in shallow water.
5. **If there is a thunderstorm, take a quick swim.** Get out of the water as soon as it thunders.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a lake cleaner than the sea?

This cannot be said in general terms. The classification of the bathing area tells you what the water quality has been in recent years, the current measurements show the most recent status. For swimming in the sea, with flags and currents, read [Swimming safely in the North and Baltic Seas](/reisen/sicher-baden-am-meer/).

### Where can I find beautiful lakes for a beach holiday?

Lake-rich regions such as the [Mecklenburg Lake District](/reisen/mecklenburgische-seenplatte/) or the [Bavarian Lakes](/reisen/bayerische-seen/) offer many designated bathing areas. You can find more ideas in the [Lakes & Rivers](/themen/seen-und-fluesse/) section.
