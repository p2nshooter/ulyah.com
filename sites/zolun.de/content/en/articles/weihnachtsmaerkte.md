When it gets dark early, fairy lights shine in the old towns and it smells of cinnamon, roasted almonds and mulled wine, the Christmas market season begins in Germany. Almost every town and many villages have their own market, from the huge sea of ​​stalls in the big city to the small market in the castle courtyard. For many visitors from home and abroad, an Advent trip is a highlight of the year. This guide introduces well-known and lesser-known markets and explains how to enjoy them without being in the crowd.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Most Christmas markets open at the end of November and close shortly before Christmas. Some remain as a winter market until the new year.
> - It is busiest on weekend evenings. During the weekday afternoons it is much more relaxed.
> - Drinks are often served in cups with a deposit. You give the cup back or keep it as a souvenir.
> - Accommodation in well-known Christmas market towns is fully booked early on Advent weekends.
> - Dress warmly: You'll be standing outside for long periods of time, often in damp, cold weather.

## The classics

**Nuremberg:** The Christkindlesmarkt on the main market is one of the most famous in the world. It is traditionally opened by the Christ child, who speaks a prologue from the gallery of the Frauenkirche. Typical are Nuremberg gingerbread, small grilled sausages in a bun and figures made from dried plums. There is a children's Christmas market right next door.

**Dresden:** The Striezelmarkt is one of the oldest Christmas markets in Germany. It is named after the Striezel, which is what Dresden Christmas Stollen used to be called. Huge Christmas pyramids, candle arches and wooden art from the Ore Mountains characterize the picture. In the old town there are other markets with their own character.

**Cologne:** In Cologne, there are several large markets spread across the city center, the most famous being at the foot of the cathedral. Others are in the old town, on the Neumarkt or on the banks of the Rhine. If you like, you can wander from market to market in one evening.

**Munich:** The Christmas market on Marienplatz is located in front of the town hall with its neo-Gothic facade. There are also smaller, more cozy markets, for example in the inner courtyard of the residence. Tips about the city in the guide [Munich](/reisen/muenchen-staedtereise/).

**Erfurt:** The Christmas market on Cathedral Square is framed by the wide stairs up to the cathedral and Severikirche. In the evening, when the churches are lit up, the backdrop is unique.

## Small markets with a special charm

- **Rothenburg ob der Tauber:** The Reiterlesmarkt in the medieval old town looks like a film set. Stay overnight in the city and experience the streets in the evening without day visitors. More about the city in the guide [Small Old Towns in Germany](/reisen/kleine-altstaedte/).
- **Gengenbach in the Black Forest:** The town hall is transformed into a huge Advent calendar during Advent. Every evening a new window opens with an image.
- **Monschau in the Eifel:** The small market in the narrow Rur valley with half-timbered buildings and slate roofs is particularly atmospheric in the dark.
- **Esslingen am Neckar:** A medieval market with jugglers, craftsmen and stalls in historical display.
- **The Ore Mountains:** In places like Seiffen, the toy village, you can see where smokers, nutcrackers and Christmas pyramids are created. Many workshops show their craft.

In addition, many castles, palaces and monasteries have small Advent markets on individual weekends. They are often less crowded and particularly atmospheric.

## When is it less crowded?

Markets in large cities are very crowded from late afternoon on Saturdays and Sundays. In some places it only progresses at walking pace. This is how you experience it more calmly:

- **Come during the week**, preferably Monday to Thursday.
- **In the early afternoon** it's relaxed almost everywhere. The lights only come into effect at dusk, but in December it gets dark early anyway.
- **The first few days** after opening are often quieter than the last weekends before Christmas.
- **Smaller markets on the edge** of the old town are also more pleasant on weekends.

At large markets, also allow time for security checks and barriers, which are now in place at many entrances.

## Food and drink

A Christmas market is also a large open-air buffet. Typical specialties:

- **Bratwurst** in regional variations, from Thuringian to small Nuremberg.
- **Rub cake** or potato pancakes with applesauce.
- **Roasted almonds** and other roasted nuts.
- **Chestnuts**, hot roasted chestnuts.
- **Lebkuchen, Stollen and Printen**, which are baked differently depending on the region.
- **flambée, cheese spaetzle or mushroom pan** for something hearty.

When it comes to drinks, **mulled wine** is the classic, plus Feuerzangenbowle, eggnog and hot apple cider. For children and everyone who doesn't drink alcohol, there is **children's punch** and hot chocolate almost everywhere.

**Cup deposit:** Hot drinks are usually served in ceramic cups, on which you pay a deposit. If you return the cup, you will get the deposit back. Many markets have a new motif every year, which is why some visitors collect the cups.

## Handicrafts and souvenirs

In addition to food and drinks, Christmas markets are places for crafts. Typical souvenirs are:

- **Wooden art from the Erzgebirge** such as smokers, nutcrackers, angels and candle arches. Real handcraft has its price, but it often lasts for generations.
- **Glass decorations** for the Christmas tree, mouth-blown and hand-painted, for example from the Thuringian Forest.
- **Beeswax candles**, knitted hats, felt crafts and ceramics.
- **Gingerbread, Stollen and Printen** in tins that are easy to transport.

Pay attention to references such as “from our own workshop” and feel free to ask where something was made. In large markets, there are real craftsmen as well as dealers with mass-produced goods. Anyone specifically looking for crafts can often find them at smaller markets or in the workshops themselves.

## How much does a visit cost?

Admission is free to almost all Christmas markets. Individual markets in castles, palaces or special settings charge admission, often with programs such as music or fire shows. Expect prices for drinks and food to be similar to those in a simple restaurant, plus the cup deposit. With kids, carousel rides and snacking can add up quickly. A rough daily budget helps to keep track. Tips for this can be found in the guide [Plan your holiday budget honestly] (/reisen/urlaubsbudget-planen/).

## Arrival and accommodation

**By train:** Most of the large markets are located in the middle of the old town, often just a few minutes' walk from the train station. By taking the train you save yourself having to look for a parking space and can drink a mulled wine without worrying. The Germany ticket is suitable for trips to smaller towns. More about this in the guide [Go on vacation with the Germany ticket](/reisen/deutschlandticket-im-urlaub/).

**By car:** Parking garages in the city center fill up quickly on Advent weekends. Many cities are setting up park-and-ride services with shuttle buses.

**Accommodation:** In Nuremberg, Dresden, Cologne and other well-known cities, hotels are often fully booked months in advance on Advent weekends and prices rise. Travel during the week if possible, or live in a neighboring town with good rail connections. Tips for choosing accommodation in the guide [Hotel, guesthouse, holiday apartment or farm?] (/reisen/hotel-pension-ferienwohnung/).

## What you should wear

At the Christmas market you stand for a long time, often on cold pavement and in humid air. This quickly becomes uncomfortable if the clothing is not right.

- **Multiple layers** instead of a thick jacket so you can adapt to warm indoor environments.
- **Warm, waterproof shoes** with thick soles. Cold feet quickly end any market visit.
- **Hat, scarf and gloves** that allow you to still hold cups.
- **A small pocket on the front of the body.** Pickpockets have an easy time in dense crowds.

## Christmas markets with children

For children, Christmas markets are a feast for all the senses: carousels, nativity scenes with live animals, fairy-tale characters, bakeries and a visit to Santa Claus. Some cities have their own children's Christmas markets with craft stalls and child-friendly programs.

A few tips:

- Come with small children in the afternoon before it gets busy in the evening.
- Arrange a meeting point in case you get lost in the crowd, and write your phone number on a piece of paper in your child's jacket pocket.
- A stroller is impractical in a crowd. A stretcher or buggy for short distances is better.

Further ideas in the guide [Holiday in Germany with children] (/reisen/urlaub-mit-kindern/).

## Plan an Advent trip

Many visitors combine several cities into one tour. Some examples:

- **Franconia:** Nuremberg with detours to Bamberg and Rothenburg.
- **Saxony:** Dresden, Meißen and a day in the Erzgebirge.
- **Rhineland:** Cologne, Aachen with its prints and a small market in Monschau.
- **North:** Hamburg and Lübeck with their Hanseatic markets. Tips about Hamburg in the guide [Discover Hamburg](/reisen/hamburg-entdecken/).

Two markets in one day are enough. If you plan too much, you'll spend more time on the train than under the lights. The guide [plan a city weekend] (/reisen/staedtewochenende-planen/) explains how you can generally plan a city weekend.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Go to the biggest market on Saturday evening.** It's much more pleasant during the week.
2. **Book too late.** Hotels in well-known cities fill up early on Advent weekends.
3. **By car to the city center.** Parking spaces are scarce, train or park and ride are better.
4. **Shoes that are too thin.** Cold feet are the most common reason for returning home early.
5. **Just take the cup with you.** The deposit is then paid, but return it if you don't want to keep it.

## Frequently asked questions

### When do the Christmas markets open?

Most open in the last week of November, some earlier. Many close on December 23rd or 24th. Check the exact dates on the city websites, they change slightly every year.

### Which Christmas market is the most beautiful?

That's a matter of taste. Nuremberg and Dresden are the most famous, smaller places like Rothenburg, Monschau or Gengenbach often have more atmosphere and less crowds.

### Are there Christmas markets without alcohol?

Soft drinks are available at every market, from children's punch to hot juice. Some markets have quieter areas for families. More ideas for the cold season in the guide [Rainy days on vacation](/reisen/regentage-im-urlaub/). You can find more cities under [City Trips](/themen/staedtereisen/).
