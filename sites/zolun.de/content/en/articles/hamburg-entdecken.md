Hamburg is a city that should be understood from the water. The harbor, the Elbe, the canals of the Speicherstadt and the Alster in the middle not only shape the cityscape, but also everyday life. At the same time, Hamburg is big, and if you only have one day, you quickly lose time between the districts. This guide shows you the most important places, reveals how to get on the water cheaply and suggests a plan for two to three days.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Most of the sights are located between the main train station, Alster, town hall, Speicherstadt, HafenCity and the Landungsbrücken. Many things are within walking distance.
> - The harbor ferries are part of local public transport. With a normal ticket or the Germany ticket you can go on your own little harbor tour.
> - The Elbphilharmonie viewing platform, the Plaza, is accessible with a timed ticket.
> - The fish market takes place early in the morning on Sundays.
> - Pack a rain jacket. Hamburg's weather changes quickly, and there are many beautiful places outside.

## Orientation: the districts at a glance

- **City center and Alster:** There are shopping streets, arcades and the Jungfernstieg around the town hall and the Binnenalster. To the north, the large Outer Alster opens up, surrounded by parks and villas.
- ** Speicherstadt and HafenCity:** The Speicherstadt with its brick warehouses on oak stilts is, together with the neighboring Kontorhausviertel, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. To the south of it, HafenCity is growing with modern architecture and the Elbphilharmonie.
- **Landungsbrücken and St. Pauli:** Excursion boats and ferries leave at the edge of the harbor. Behind it is St. Pauli with the Reeperbahn, clubs, theaters and pubs.
- **Altona and Ottensen:** West of St. Pauli, with lively streets, cafes, the fish market on the bank and the path along the Elbe.
- **Blankenese:** Far in the west, with the staircase district on the slopes of the Elbe and a sandy beach on the river.

## The harbor: from the water

The Port of Hamburg is one of the largest ports in Europe. Giant containers, shipyards and cranes are so close to the city that you can watch them from the shore.

**Classic harbor tour:** Numerous shipping companies offer tours at the Landungsbrücken, with barges that also sail into the canals of the Speicherstadt, or with larger ships to the container port. For the Speicherstadt you need the small barges, and the journey depends on the water level.

**The cheaper option:** The HADAG port ferries belong to the Hamburg transport association. With a normal local transport ticket or the Deutschlandticket you can, for example, take line 62 from the Landungsbrücken down the Elbe to Finkenwerder, past the fish market, Övelgönne and the container terminals. Sit on the upper deck if the weather permits. More about the ticket in the guide [Go on vacation with the Germany ticket](/reisen/deutschlandticket-im-urlaub/).

**The Old Elbe Tunnel:** A hundred-year-old tunnel leads from the Landungsbrücken under the Elbe. Pedestrians and cyclists use it free of charge. On the other side you have one of the most beautiful views of the jetties and the city. One of the two tubes may be closed due to renovation work.

## Speicherstadt, HafenCity and Elbphilharmonie

In the **speicherstadt**, red brick facades are reflected in the streets, particularly beautiful in the evening when the buildings are illuminated. The old warehouses now house museums, offices and exhibitions, including the **Miniatur Wunderland**, a huge model railway system for which you should book timed tickets in advance during the season.

The **HafenCity** shows modern Hamburg: residential and office buildings on the water, squares, promenades and museums such as the International Maritime Museum.

The **Elbphilharmonie** stands at the top of HafenCity, a concert hall with a glass shaft on an old quayside warehouse. The public **Plaza**, around 37 meters high, surrounds the building and offers a wide view over the harbor and city. Access is possible with a timed ticket, which you can get on site or online for a small fee. Tickets for concerts are often sold out long in advance. Occasionally there are short-term remaining tickets.

## Town hall, Alster and city center

The **Town Hall** on the Rathausmarkt is a magnificent building from the imperial era. Guided tours are offered in many parts. The Alster arcades begin right next to it.

On the **Binnenalster** is the Jungfernstieg, from which the **Alster steamers** depart. A tour of the Inner and Outer Alster is a relaxed way to see the city. In summer you can rent rowing, pedal and sailing boats on the Outer Alster or take a walk around the lake on the riverside path. The loop is around seven kilometers long.

The **Planten un Blomen** park between the city center and the trade fair is a green oasis with a rose garden, Japanese garden and playgrounds. On summer evenings, free water light concerts take place at the park lake.

For a view from above, climb the tower of the main church **St. Michaelis**, which the people of Hamburg affectionately call Michel.

## St. Pauli, fish market and banks of the Elbe

The **Reeperbahn** in St. Pauli is famous for nightlife, theater and music clubs. During the day it appears rather unspectacular, but it shows its character in the evening.

The **fish market** in Altona is a Hamburg institution. It takes place every Sunday early in the morning, starting at five in the summer. Barkers sell fruit, fish and plants, and live music plays in the historic fish auction hall. If you don't want to get up early, come towards the end when prices drop.

From there, a walk along the banks of the Elbe leads down to **Övelgönne** with its small museum harbor and sandy beach. Further to the west is **Blankenese** with the winding staircase district and a view of the ships.

**Important:** There is no swimming on the Elbe beach. Currents, suction and waves from large ships are dangerous, even close to the shore. Children should never play alone near water.

## Museums and rain program

Hamburg has a lot to offer for rainy days:

- **Hamburger Kunsthalle** at the main station with art from the Middle Ages to the present.
- **Museum of Arts and Crafts** with design, fashion and photography.
- **International Maritime Museum** in HafenCity on the history of seafaring.
- **Miniature Wonderland** in the Speicherstadt, popular with children and adults.
- **Emigration World BallinStadt** on the Veddel about the history of emigration via Hamburg.
- **Elbphilharmonie Plaza**, which is worth a look even when it rains.

You can find more ideas in the guide [Rainy days on vacation](/reisen/regentage-im-urlaub/).

## Eating in Hamburg

- **Fish rolls** are simply a must at the Landungsbrücken, with herring, Bismarck herring, crabs or fried fish.
- **Franzbrötchen** are a sweet pastry with cinnamon, perfect with coffee.
- **Labskaus** is a traditional sailor's dish made from potatoes, cured meat and beetroot, often with a fried egg and rollmops.
- **Panfisch** is fried fish with mustard sauce and fried potatoes.
- **Red jelly** with vanilla sauce is the classic dessert.

In Ottensen, in the Schanzenviertel and in St. Georg you will also find a large selection of international cuisine.

## Out and about in the city

Hamburg has a dense network of subway, S-Bahn, buses and ferries. For a city trip it is usually enough to live centrally and do everything else using local transport. The Deutschlandticket is valid throughout the entire transport network, including the port ferries. Alternatively, there are day tickets and a guest card with discounts for museums and tours.

Driving into the city center is rarely worth it. Parking is expensive, and many destinations are better reached by train and ferry anyway. The guide [On vacation by train](/reisen/mit-der-bahn-in-den-urlaub/) explains how to travel by train.

## A plan for two to three days

**Day 1: City center, Speicherstadt and Elbphilharmonie**

- Morning: Town hall, Alster arcades and Jungfernstieg, Alster tour.
- Lunch: Walk to Speicherstadt, lunch in the area.
- Afternoon: Miniatur Wunderland or Maritime Museum, then HafenCity.
- Evening: Plaza of the Elbphilharmonie at sunset, then the illuminated Speicherstadt.

**Day 2: Port, Elbe and St. Pauli**

- Morning: Landungsbrücken, Old Elbe Tunnel, harbor ferry line 62 to Finkenwerder and back.
- Lunch: fish sandwiches at the jetties.
- Afternoon: Walk via Altona to Övelgönne, if you feel like it, continue to Blankenese.
- Evening: St. Pauli and Reeperbahn.

**Day 3: as desired**

- Sunday morning: fish market.
- Museum day in the rain or Planten un Blomen and the Outer Alster in the sun.
- In spring: Trip to the **Alte Land** southwest of the city when the fruit trees are in bloom.

If you only have one weekend, choose day 1 and day 2. The guide [plan a city weekend] (/reisen/staedtewochenende-planen/) explains how you generally plan a city weekend.

## Travel time

Hamburg is a worthwhile destination all year round. From May to September the days are long and life takes place outside by the water. Museums and concerts are the highlights in autumn and winter, followed by the Christmas markets in December. More about this in the guide [Visit Christmas markets in Germany] (/reisen/weihnachtsmaerkte/).

Large events such as the harbor birthday in spring or trade fairs drive up hotel prices sharply. Check the events calendar before booking.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

1. **Plan too much for one day.** Choose one focus per day.
2. **Book an expensive tour, even though the ferry is enough.** For a first impression of the port, line 62 is often enough.
3. **Miniature Wonderland without a timed ticket.** You'll otherwise have to wait a long time during holidays and weekends.
4. **Swim in the Elbe beach.** Currents and ship waves are dangerous.
5. **Set off without a rain jacket.** The weather changes quickly.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do you need for Hamburg?

Two days are enough for the most important places. With three days there is time for museums, the fish market, Blankenese or a trip to the Altes Land.

### Is Hamburg expensive?

Hotels in the city center are not cheap, especially during trade fairs and large events. Many highlights cost little: the harbor ferry, the old Elbe tunnel, walks along the Alster and Elbe and the Elbphilharmonie plaza. Tips for cost planning can be found in the guide [Plan your holiday budget honestly] (/reisen/urlaubsbudget-planen/).

### Can you travel to the North Sea or Baltic Sea from Hamburg?

Yes. Baltic Sea resorts on the Bay of Lübeck and North Sea towns such as Cuxhaven can be reached by train in one to two hours. The guide [North Sea or Baltic Sea] (/reisen/nordsee-oder-ostsee/) explains which sea suits you. You can find more cities under [City Trips](/themen/staedtereisen/).
