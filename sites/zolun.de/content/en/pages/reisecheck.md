ZOLUN explains how holidays work on the coast, on islands, on lakes and in cities. One thing we consciously do not do is mention timetables, prices, ferry times, opening times or the amount of the tourist tax from memory. Such information changes from season to season, sometimes week to week, and an outdated number can cost you a connection, the last ferry of the day, or a quiet arrival.

The traveler's check therefore shows you **what** you should check before a trip, **when** the right time is and **who** will give you reliable information. You can print out the page or save it as a bookmark and read through it carefully before each trip. It does not replace official information, but shows you the shortest route there.

## Who is responsible for what

The most important rule for relaxed travel is: Whatever can change, rely on the authority that determines it. Forums, older blog posts and booking portals sometimes reflect a status that is no longer valid.

| What you want to know | Whoever says it authoritatively | What you pay attention to |
|---|---|---|
| Train connections, construction sites, replacement transport | the transport company, and in local transport also the region's transport association | Retrieve the connection again shortly before departure |
| Validity of the Germany ticket | the company you have your subscription with and the local transport company | Long-distance trains, many island ferries and special services can be excluded |
| Ferries to islands and Halligen | the shipping company | Departure times, reservations for cars, rules for luggage, bicycles and dogs |
| Tides, i.e. high and low water | the tidal forecasts from the Federal Maritime and Hydrographic Agency (BSH) and the notices on site | The times apply per location and change every day |
| Weather warnings | the German Weather Service (DWD) | Storm, thunderstorm, heat, smoothness |
| Water level and storm surge on the coast | the BSH | especially important in autumn and winter |
| Flooding on rivers and lakes | the flood services of the federal states | after continuous rain or melting snow |
| Bathing water quality and blue-green algae | the responsible state and health authorities, as well as the notices at the bathing area | Warning notices on lakes in midsummer |
| Tourist tax, guest card, beach rules, dog beaches | the municipality, spa administration or tourist information | Rules often only apply during the season or only during certain periods |
| Rules in national parks and protected areas | the national park administration or the nature conservation authority | Resting areas, breeding times, path requirements in the dunes |
| School holidays | the state ministries of culture | The summer holidays begin at different dates in each federal state |
| Opening times of museums, baths and excursion destinations | the facility itself | Season times, rest days, holidays |
| Services of your accommodation | your booking confirmation and your host | Final cleaning, bed linen, arrival time, pets |

## Three times for the check

Not everything has to be clarified immediately. Some things you decide when booking, others you only check shortly before departure because they can still change by then.

### When planning and booking

- **Travel time and holidays:** Check whether there are school holidays in many federal states on your desired date. Then accommodation, trains and ferries on the coast are usually in greater demand and short-term bookings become more difficult.
- **Getting to your front door:** Don't just plan to get to the train station or ferry dock, but to your accommodation. Is there another bus running on the evening of arrival? How far is the journey with suitcases and children? On car-free islands, ask your host how luggage gets from the port to the accommodation.
- **What's included in the price:** Especially with holiday apartments, some items are charged extra, such as final cleaning, bed linen and towels, tourist tax, parking space or a surcharge for the dog. Therefore, compare the total price for the entire stay, not the price per night.
- **Cancellation and rebooking:** Read the cancellation policy before you book, not just when you need it. When it comes to train and ferry tickets, there are often tariffs with very different levels of flexibility.
- **Special Needs:** Steps, Elevator, Walk-in Shower, Crib, Dog Allowed? Ask specific questions instead of relying on a small symbol in a list.
- **Car on the ferry:** If you want to take your car with you to an island, find out early on whether this is even possible and whether you need to reserve a space. On car-free islands, the car stays on the mainland, so you need a parking space near the harbor.

### About a week before departure

- Retrieve your train connection again. Construction sites, replacement bus services or changed transfers often only become visible at short notice.
- Check the ferry times for your travel day. For ferries that depend on the tides, departures are at a different time every day.
- Check the weather trend and adjust your packing list. On the North and Baltic Seas, a windproof jacket and warm layers are essential in your luggage, even in summer.
- Clarify your arrival with your host: latest arrival time, key collection and a telephone number in case you are late.
- Have the documents ready: booking confirmations, tickets, ID card, health card and, for the dog, its vaccination or pet card.
- Think about medications that you need regularly, in sufficient quantities for the entire trip and a few days more.

### On the day of departure and on the way

- Take another look at your connection just before you leave. If a train is canceled, an early alternative is more relaxing than a missed connection in the evening.
- Pay attention to severe weather warnings. During storms, ferries can be canceled and piers can be closed.
- If you're going to the North Sea, check the tides for beach days. When the tide is low, the sea is far out in some places and swimming only makes sense at certain times.

## Safe on and in the water

Water is the best part of many holidays in Germany, and it demands respect. These basic rules apply almost everywhere:

- **Choose guarded swimming areas:** During the season, lifeguards are on duty at many beaches and lakes, for example from the DLRG or the water rescue service. The guarded area is usually marked with flags. What the flags mean is usually explained on a board at the beach entrance. A red flag means: don't swim.
- **North Sea and mudflats:** The water returns with the tide and first fills the tidal creeks, the deeper channels in the mudflats. Therefore, only go to the mudflats with an expert guide who knows the tides, weather and paths. Fog and thunderstorms make the mudflats particularly dangerous.
- **Baltic Sea and cliffs:** Material can break off on cliffs at any time, even without warning. Stay away from the edge and base of the cliff and respect closed sections.
- **Lakes:** The water at depth is often much colder than at the surface. After a long period of sun, cool down before going inside, and look out for signs of blue-green algae in midsummer. You can only access ice surfaces in winter if the municipality has expressly approved them.
- **Rivers:** Currents, ship traffic and groynes make swimming in large rivers dangerous, even for good swimmers. Only swim in designated areas.
- **Thunderstorms:** If a thunderstorm approaches, leave the water and the open beach.
- **Children:** Swimming aids do not replace supervision. Small children should always be within reach of an adult when near the water.
- **Dogs:** Never leave your dog waiting in a warm car, take enough drinking water with you and find out which sections dogs are allowed.

## In case of emergency

- **112:** Fire and rescue services, free of charge and throughout Europe. In the event of an emergency on the beach, in the mudflats or on the water, you can also dial 112. The control center will send the appropriate help.
- **110:** Police.
- **116 117:** medical on-call service if you need medical help outside of office hours but it is not life-threatening.
- **Pharmacy:** Which pharmacy currently has emergency service is posted on a notice board at every pharmacy.
- **Emergency veterinary service:** Ask your host or the tourist information office upon arrival where the nearest emergency service is. In an emergency, you don't have to search.

A little tip: save the exact address of your accommodation in your cell phone so that you can find it even without a network. On the beach, the names or numbers of the beach entrances, which are found in many coastal towns, help rescue teams find you quickly.

## If something goes wrong along the way

- **The train is delayed or canceled:** As a passenger, you have rights, such as continuing your journey on another connection or receiving compensation. What you are entitled to in each individual case depends on the ticket and means of transport. The transport company states the conditions. Save tickets and receipts. If you get stuck with the company, you can contact a public transport arbitration board.
- **The ferry is not running:** Storms, particularly low water or ice can cause trips to be cancelled. The shipping company will provide information about replacement trips. Let your host know early if your arrival is postponed.
- **The accommodation does not correspond to the description:** Take photos, report any defects to the host immediately and give them the opportunity to correct them. For a package tour, the tour operator is your contact person. The consumer advice centers provide advice on legal questions.
- **You get sick:** Take your health card with you, even for a week at the sea. Outside office hours, 116 117 can help.
- **Something has been lost:** Check with the municipality's lost property office or the transport company whose train, bus or ferry you were on.

## The short version to print out

1. Travel time and school holidays checked
2. Arrival planned to the front door, including the last few kilometers
3. Compare the total price of the accommodation, read the cancellation conditions
4. Ferry and, if necessary, car parking space clarified
5. A week before: Connection, ferry times and weather trends checked again
6. Arrival and key handover agreed with the host
7. Packed ID, health card, tickets, medication and documents for the dog
8. On the day of departure: View the connection and warnings one last time
9. At your destination: guarded swimming area, dog rules and emergency services in the area
