Making cheese, kefir, bread or preserves at home is an ancient practice and, done right, safe. But some of these foods offer conditions in which, if something goes wrong, dangerous microorganisms can grow. These are the rules we apply in all our recipes.

## 1. Hygiene before anything

- Wash your hands with soap and water before you start and every time you change tasks.
- Clean surfaces, utensils and containers well. For cheeses and fermented foods, rinse them with boiling water or wash them in the dishwasher at high temperature.
- Use clean cloths and change them frequently.
- Do not work with food if you have symptoms of gastroenteritis.

## 2. Milk: always pasteurized

At home we cannot control the risks of raw milk like professional cheese factories do, which follow strict health controls and specific maturation processes. That's why all our recipes for fresh cheeses, cottage cheese, yogurt and kefir use **pasteurized milk**. UHT milk is useful for some preparations, such as yogurt and kefir, but it curdles worse for making cheese; We explain it in each recipe.

## 3. The temperature

- Fresh cheeses, cottage cheese and dairy creams should be stored in the refrigerator, below 5°C, and consumed within a few days.
- During the fermentation of yogurt and kefir, respect the indicated temperature ranges: a temperature that is too low delays acidification and favors other microorganisms.
- Don't leave cooked food at room temperature for hours.

## 4. Acidity in preserves

The bacteria *Clostridium botulinum* can produce a very dangerous toxin in low-acid foods packaged without oxygen. Therefore:

- Home canning in a bain-marie is only safe in the home for acidic foods, such as most fruits in jam, acidified tomato, or pickles in vinegar with the appropriate ratio.
- Low acid foods, such as natural vegetables, legumes, meats or fish, require pressure sterilization with specific equipment and protocols. We do not recommend them at home.
- **Never try a preserve with a bulging lid, which splashes when opened, with a strange smell or cloudy appearance.** Discard it carefully.

## 5. The fermented

- Use clean glass containers and respect the proportions of salt or sugar.
- In vegetable ferments, the vegetables must be submerged in the brine.
- A uniform white layer on the surface may be normal in some vegetable ferments, but colored (green, black, pink) or fuzzy mold indicates that everything should be discarded.
- Trust your nose: a clean, acidic smell is normal; a rotten or solvent smell, no.

## 6. People at higher risk

Pregnant people, babies and young children, the elderly, and the immunocompromised are more vulnerable to some foodborne infections. For them, we take extreme precautions: only pasteurized milk, freshly made and well-preserved cheeses, and consult with your doctor about the consumption of homemade fermented foods.

## 7. When in doubt

If you are not sure that a homemade food is in good condition, do not consume it. It is the most important rule of the workshop.
