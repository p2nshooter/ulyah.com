Water kefir is the dairy-free cousin of milk kefir. It is a slightly sweet, acidic and refreshing drink, which with a second fermentation becomes bubbly, like a homemade soft drink. It is made with translucent nodules, similar to small crystals, that ferment water with sugar.

It is a great option for those who do not drink dairy, for vegans or simply for those looking for a homemade alternative to industrial soft drinks, with less sugar and a more complex flavor. This guide explains how to do it, how to flavor it, and most of all, how to do it safely.

## What are water kefir nodules

Water kefir nodules, also called "tibicos" or "water crystals," are small, translucent, brittle grains formed by a community of lactic acid bacteria and yeast. These microorganisms produce a polysaccharide called dextran, which forms the structure of the nodules.

When fermenting, they consume the sugar in the water and produce organic acids, carbon dioxide, aromas and small amounts of alcohol. As in milk kefir, the nodules grow and can be shared.

## Ingredients for one liter

- **3 or 4 tablespoons of water kefir nodules**.
- **1 liter of water** without chlorine: mineral, spring or tap, boiled and cooled, or filtered.
- **50 to 60 grams of sugar**, about three or four tablespoons. White sugar works; Brown sugar or panela provide minerals that the nodules appreciate.
- **A dried fruit**: a dried fig, some dates or some raisins. They provide minerals and nutrients.
- **A slice of lemon** (optional), without the excess white part of the skin.

## Utensils

- A 1.5 liter glass jar.
- A plastic or stainless steel strainer.
- A wooden or plastic spoon.
- Glass bottles with hermetic closure, suitable for pressure, for the second fermentation.

## First fermentation

1. **Dissolve the sugar** in a little warm water and complete with the rest of the cold water. Make sure the mixture is at room temperature before adding the nodules: hot water damages them.
2. **Add the nodules, dried fruit and lemon** to the jar.
3. **Cover the jar** loosely with a cloth or lid.
4. **Let it ferment** at room temperature, away from the sun, between **24 and 48 hours**.
5. **Check point**: The liquid will be less sweet, slightly acidic and somewhat cloudy. The dried fruit will have risen to the surface, a common sign that fermentation is progressing.
6. **Strain**: remove the fruit and lemon, and separate the nodules with the strainer.
7. **Start a new batch** with the nodules and new sugar water.

If you let it ferment too much, the kefir will become very acidic and not sweet, and the nodules may weaken from lack of food.

## Second fermentation: flavor and bubbles

With the kefir already strained:

1. **Pour the kefir into bottles** suitable for pressure, leaving a few centimeters free.
2. **Add flavor**: fruit juice, fresh fruit pieces, ginger, herbs or spices.
3. **Close the bottles** and leave them at room temperature for **12 to 48 hours**.
4. **Put them in the refrigerator** to stop fermentation.

### Combinations that work

- Lemon and fresh ginger.
- Strawberries and basil.
- Orange and cinnamon.
- Grape or grape juice.
- Raspberries and mint.
- Pineapple and a touch of turmeric.

## Gas safety

During the second fermentation, carbon dioxide accumulates in the closed bottles. If too much builds up, the pressure can cause the bottle to burst or the contents to eject when opened. To avoid it:

- **Use bottles designed to withstand pressure**, such as mechanical seal bottles or soft drink bottles. Don't use jam jars or thin bottles.
- **Release the gas** by slightly opening the bottles once a day during the second fermentation, especially with heat or with very sugary fruits.
- **Do not leave bottles in the sun** or in hot places.
- **Always open bottles carefully**, over the sink, pointing away from your face, and preferably cold.
- **A plastic soda bottle** as a "witness" helps: if it is hard when you squeeze it, the glass ones also have pressure.

## Take care of the nodules

- **Feed them regularly**: every day or two, with new sugar water.
- **Avoid honey** as the main food: it has antibacterial properties that can weaken the nodules.
- **Provides minerals**: dried fruit, low-refined sugar or a pinch of baking soda or clean eggshell from time to time help them grow.
- **To rest**: in the refrigerator, in water with sugar, they can last one or two weeks.
- **If they become slimy** or stop fermenting, change the type of sugar or water and give them minerals; Sometimes they need a few rounds to recover.

## Warning signs

- **Mold** on surface: discard contents.
- **Unpleasant smell**, different from the usual acidic and fruity smell.
- **Nodules that fall apart** completely or become gelatinous and do not ferment.

When in doubt, do not consume it.

## Sugar and alcohol content

During fermentation, microorganisms consume much of the sugar, so the finished water kefir contains less sugar than the initial mixture, although it still has some. The amount depends on the fermentation time.

Like all yeast fermentations, it produces small amounts of alcohol, usually below one percent. With long second fermentations and a lot of fruit, the quantity can increase. Keep this in mind if you offer it to children or if you avoid alcohol completely.

## Water kefir and health

Like milk kefir, it is a fermented food that contains live microorganisms. It is considered a pleasant drink and an alternative to soft drinks, but there is no strong evidence that it has curative effects. Immunocompromised people should consult with their doctor before consuming homemade fermented foods.

## Flavor combinations for second fermentation

The second fermentation is time to play. The base is always the same, already strained water kefir, and what changes is what you add to the bottle. These combinations work well for a one liter bottle and are a good starting point.

| Combination | What to add per liter | Indicative time |
|---|---|---|
| Lemon and ginger | Juice of half a lemon and three thin slices of fresh ginger | 24 hours |
| Strawberry and basil | Five chopped strawberries and two basil leaves | 24 to 36 hours |
| Orange and cinnamon | Juice of one orange and half a cinnamon stick | 24 hours |
| Grape | A small glass of grape must without added sugar | 12 to 24 hours |
| Pineapple and mint | A handful of diced pineapple and some mint leaves | 24 hours |
| red fruits | A handful of raspberries or blackberries, fresh or frozen | 24 to 36 hours |

Juices and very sweet fruits, such as grapes or pineapple, ferment quickly and generate a lot of gas, so it is a good idea to monitor the bottle first. Aromatic herbs provide perfume but little sugar, so they are usually combined with some fruit. If a combination is too acidic, next time shorten the first fermentation or add a little more fruit in the second.

Strain the fruits and herbs when serving. Do not reuse them for another batch: they have already given their flavor and will spoil quickly.

## Common problems and how to solve them

**Kefir tastes like vinegar.** Fermentation has been too long or it is too hot. In summer, 24 hours may be enough; in winter, you may need 48. Strain before and taste each day until you find your point.

**Smells like yeast or bread.** A slight fermenty aroma is normal, but a strong yeast smell indicates that yeast is dominating. It usually happens with too much sugar or high temperatures. Reduce the sugar a little and change the water more frequently.

**The liquid remains thick or viscous.** Some nodules, when stressed due to lack of minerals or sudden changes in temperature, produce a somewhat gelatinous drink. Rinse the nodules with non-chlorinated water, give them a batch with brown sugar or a piece of clean eggshell, and they usually recover in two or three cycles.

**The nodules do not grow.** It is not essential that they grow for them to ferment well. If you want them to multiply, make sure they have enough minerals, avoid distilled water and maintain a stable temperature between 20 and 25 °C.

**A white, cottony layer appears on the surface.** This is not normal. Fuzzy, green, black, or pink spots are mold. Discard everything, including the nodules, wash the jar well, and start over with healthy nodules.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use tap water?

If your water has a lot of chlorine, let it sit uncovered for a few hours, boil it and cool it, or use filtered or mineral water.

### Can I convert milk nodules into water nodules?

No. They are different communities. You need water kefir nodules.

### Why doesn't my kefir have bubbles?

The second fermentation may have been short, the bottle may not seal properly, or the temperature may be low. Adding a little fruit or juice in the second fermentation helps.

## In summary

Water without chlorine, sugar, dried fruit and nodules: in one or two days, you will have an acidic and refreshing drink. With a second fermentation in resistant bottles, it gains flavor and bubbles. Control gas, take care of nodules with minerals and enjoy a different homemade soda every week.
