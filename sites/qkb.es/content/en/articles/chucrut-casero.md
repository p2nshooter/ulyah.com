Sauerkraut is cabbage fermented with salt. Nothing else. It does not contain vinegar, sugar, or purchased cultures. The entire transformation is carried out by lactic acid bacteria that live naturally in the leaves of cabbage, which convert their sugars into lactic acid. The result is an acidic, crunchy and aromatic vegetable, which is preserved for months and goes wonderfully with meats, legumes, sandwiches and salads.

It is also the best fermentation to learn how fermented vegetables work. If you master sauerkraut, you will master the principle of almost all the others.

## How lactic fermentation of cabbage works

When you cut cabbage and mix it with salt, two things happen. First, the salt extracts water from the cells of the cabbage by osmosis, forming a salty liquid, brine, which will eventually cover the vegetable. Second, salt stops many undesirable bacteria, but lactic acid bacteria tolerate it well.

Under the brine, without oxygen, lactic acid bacteria begin to eat the sugars in the cabbage and produce acid. At first, some species dominate, such as *Leuconostoc mesenteroides*, which produce gas and some acid. Then, as the medium becomes more acidic, more resistant ones take over, such as different species of *Lactobacillus*, which continue to lower the pH. When the medium is sufficiently acidic, almost no harmful microorganisms can grow, and the cabbage is preserved.

## Ingredients

- 1 kg of white cabbage, already cleaned and without the trunk.
- 20 g of non-iodine salt, that is, 2% of the weight of the cabbage.

Optional, to flavor:

- 1 teaspoon caraway seeds.
- 5 or 6 juniper berries.
- 1 bay leaf.

### Why 2%

Salt is the only control element in this recipe, so it is a good idea to weigh it. With less than 1.5%, the cabbage becomes soft and the risk of contamination increases. With more than 3%, fermentation becomes very slow and the sauerkraut becomes too salty. 2% is the classic balance of safety, texture and flavor.

Weigh the cabbage after cleaning it and calculate the salt. For 1.3 kg of cabbage, for example, you need 26 g of salt.

## material

- A 1.5-liter wide-mouth glass jar, or a ceramic fermentation vessel.
- A weight to keep the cabbage submerged: a glass fermentation weight, a small jar filled with water, or a freezer bag with brine.
- A large bowl.
- A sharp knife or mandolin.

Wash everything well with hot water and soap and rinse it. There is no need to sterilize: the bacteria you need come in cabbage.

## step by step

### 1. Prepare the cabbage

Remove the two or three outer leaves of the cabbage and save one whole, which will serve to cover. Cut the cabbage into quarters, remove the stem and cut it into thin strips, about 2 or 3 mm. The finer it is, the faster it will release its juice.

### 2. Salt and massage

Put the cabbage in the bowl, sprinkle with salt and mix with your hands. Then squeeze and squeeze the cabbage vigorously for five to ten minutes. At first it will seem like nothing is happening, but little by little the cabbage will soften, become shiny, and begin to release liquid. If you have time, let it sit covered for half an hour and massage it again: it will release even more juice.

If you are using caraway or juniper, add them now.

### 3. Press into the jar

Put the cabbage into the jar by handfuls and press it with your fist or a wooden mortar, pressing each layer well to expel the air. As you squeeze, the liquid will rise. Also pour the juice that is left in the bowl.

Leave at least 3 or 4 cm free at the top of the jar, because fermentation will cause the liquid to rise.

### 4. Cover and dip

Place the whole cabbage leaf on top, like a lid, and put the weight on it. The goal is for **all of the cabbage to remain under the liquid**. If there is not enough liquid after pressing, wait a few hours: the cabbage will continue to release juice. If it is still not covered the next day, prepare a 2% brine (20 g of salt per liter of water) and add just the right amount to cover.

### 5. Cover

Close the jar loosely so the gas can escape, or use a lid with a fermentation valve. You can also cover it with a cloth, as long as the cabbage is well submerged. Place the jar on a plate, because some liquid may overflow the first few days.

## How long to ferment

The ideal temperature is between 18 and 22 °C. At that temperature:

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| Days 1 to 3 | Bubbles appear, the liquid becomes cloudy and rises |
| Days 4 to 7 | Lots of activity, strong cabbage smell, still slightly acidic taste |
| Weeks 2 and 3 | The acidity increases, the color becomes paler and more translucent |
| Weeks 3 to 6 | Full flavor, stable acidity, rounder aroma |

You can start trying it from the first week, but sauerkraut reaches its best flavor and most stable acidity after three weeks. At higher temperatures, it ferments faster but tends to be softer and with less fine flavors.

For the first week, press the weight once a day to release trapped bubbles and make sure everything is still submerged.

## Surface Yeast vs. Mold

A white, smooth or somewhat wrinkled film may appear on the surface of the liquid. These are usually **surface yeasts**, sometimes called *kahm*. They are not dangerous, but if left to grow they can give an unpleasant taste. Remove it with a spoon, wipe the edge of the jar and check that the cabbage is still submerged.

Mold, on the other hand, is fluffy, hairy and raised, and can be white, green, blue, black or pink. If mold appears on the cabbage or the liquid smells rotten, discard the entire jar. If there is only a small stain on the weight or covering sheet and the cabbage underneath is submerged, firm and smells good, some people remove the top; When in doubt, the prudent thing to do is to throw it away.

A healthy sauerkraut smells acidic and cabbage, intense but clean. The texture should be firm and somewhat crunchy, never slimy or mushy like porridge.

## Conservation

When the sauerkraut is to your liking, close the jar tightly and store it in the refrigerator. The cold almost completely slows down fermentation, and the sauerkraut will remain good for several months, as long as it is still covered by its liquid. Always use clean utensils to serve it.

If you cook it, the flavor and texture remain well, although the live bacteria are killed by the heat. To take advantage of them, add sauerkraut at the end of cooking or eat it raw.

## Variants

- **Sauerkraut with apple:** add a grated acidic apple for every kilo of cabbage.
- **Purple sauerkraut:** uses red cabbage. It remains an intense magenta color.
- **Sauerkraut with carrot and ginger:** replace a quarter of the cabbage with grated carrot and add a tablespoon of grated fresh ginger.
- **Sauerkraut with beets:** a little grated beet gives it color and sweetness.

Remember to calculate 2% salt on the total weight of the vegetables.

## How to use sauerkraut

- As an accompaniment to sausages, knuckle or roast meat, Central European style.
- In sandwiches and sandwiches, to provide acidity.
- In salads, mixed with cooked potato, onion and oil.
- Sautéed briefly with onion and bacon.
- In legume stews, added at the end.

## Common problems

**Does not release liquid.** The cabbage may be somewhat dry, especially if it has been in the refrigerator for a while. Massage more, let sit, or top off with 2% brine.

**It is soft.** This may be due to little salt, high temperature or a very long fermentation. Always weigh the salt and ferment in a cool place.

**It smells like sulfur.** It is normal in the first few days: cabbage contains sulfur compounds. The smell softens over time.

**It's too salty.** You can rinse it with water before serving, although it will lose some flavor. Next time, check the weight of the salt.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use iodized salt?

Iodized salt does not prevent fermentation, but some anti-caking agents can cloud the brine. If it's the only one you have, it will work; If you have the choice, use non-iodine salt.

### Should cabbage be washed?

Simply remove the dirty or damaged outer leaves. Do not wash the cabbage excessively, because lactic bacteria live on its surface that will carry out the fermentation.

### Can I use a plastic container?

Food-safe glass or ceramic is preferable. Plastic scratches, can retain odors, and is more difficult to clean.

## In summary

Finely cut cabbage, 2% salt, a lot of massage and a jar where everything is under the liquid. Let it ferment for three to six weeks in a cool place, remove the white film if it appears and discard the jar if there is any mold. With these few steps, you'll transform a humble cabbage into a classic that keeps for months.
