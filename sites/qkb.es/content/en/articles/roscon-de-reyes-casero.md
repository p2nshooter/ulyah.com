The Three Kings roscón is the sweet that closes the Christmas festivities in Spain. It is eaten on January 6, the day of the Epiphany, at family breakfasts and snacks, and hides two surprises inside: a figurine, which brings luck to whoever finds it, and a dried bean, which forces whoever it is his turn to pay for the following year's roscón. Its crown shape is reminiscent of the Three Wise Men, and the colored candied fruits represent precious stones.

A good homemade roscón is aromatic, tender and spongy, with a crumb that falls apart into strands. It takes time, but the result is nothing like most industrial roscones. This recipe uses a preferment that improves flavor and preservation, and explains each step so that the dough, rich in egg, sugar and butter, rises as it should.

## a little history

The origin of the roscón is usually related to the Roman Saturnalia, a winter festival in which round cakes with a bean inside were distributed. Whoever found it was named "king of the party." With Christianity, the tradition moved to Epiphany. In France there is *galette des rois*, a puff pastry cake with almond cream; in Portugal, the *bolo rei*, very similar to the Spanish roscón.

## Ingredients for a large roscón, for 8 to 10 people

### Preferment (the night before or 2 hours before)

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| strength flour | 100g |
| warm milk | 65g |
| fresh yeast | 5g |

### Mass

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| All preference | 170g |
| strength flour | 400g |
| sugar | 100g |
| Eggs | 2 large |
| warm milk | 70g |
| orange blossom water | 25 g, about 2 tablespoons |
| fresh yeast | 15g |
| salt | 5g |
| orange zest | 1 orange |
| lemon zest | ½ lemon |
| soft butter | 90g |

### Decoration

- 1 beaten egg.
- Candied fruit: orange, cherry or sour cherry, pumpkin or melon.
- Laminated almond.
- 50 g of sugar moistened with a few drops of water.

### Filling, optional

- 500 ml of whipping cream with 35% fat, very cold, with 40 g of icing sugar.
- Or pastry cream, or truffle.

## orange blossom water

Orange blossom water, distilled from the bitter orange blossom, is the most characteristic aroma of the roscón. It is found in pharmacies, baking stores and many supermarkets. It is very perfumed: don't overdo it, because too much gives it a soapy taste.

## Step 1: the preferment

Dissolve the yeast in the warm milk, add the flour and mix until it forms a ball. Cover it and let it ferment until it doubles in volume, about two hours at room temperature. You can also prepare it the night before and store it in the refrigerator.

## Step 2: flavor the milk

If you have time, heat the milk for the dough with the orange and lemon zest, without boiling it, and let it infuse until it is lukewarm. It is an optional step that intensifies the aroma.

## Step 3: knead

1. In a large bowl or mixer bowl, put the flour, sugar and salt.
2. Add the chopped preferment, eggs, milk, orange blossom water, crumbled yeast and zest.
3. Knead for about 10 minutes, until the dough is smooth and elastic.
4. Add the softened butter in three or four times, without adding the next one until the previous one has been absorbed.
5. Continue kneading for another 8 to 10 minutes, until the dough is very elastic and passes the membrane test.

The roscón dough is soft and sticky because of the sugar and butter. It's normal. Resist the temptation to add flour: a roscón with too much flour will be dry. A mixer makes this step much easier, although it can be done by hand with patience.

## Step 4: first fermentation

Shape into a ball, place in a greased bowl, and cover. Let it ferment in a warm place until it doubles in volume. Due to the amount of sugar and fat, it can take between two and three hours.

A practical alternative is to leave the dough in the refrigerator overnight, between 10 and 14 hours. In addition to being better organized, cold dough is much easier to form and the flavor improves.

## Step 5: Form the thread

1. Pour the dough onto the lightly floured table and degas it.
2. Form a ball with tension.
3. Insert your fingers into the center and open a hole.
4. Stretch the dough with your hands, turning it, until it forms a donut about 30 cm in diameter. The central hole should be large, about 10 to 12 cm, because it will close when fermenting.
5. Place the donut on a tray lined with baking paper.

If you are going to include the surprises, insert them now through the bottom of the thread. Use only heat-resistant, ceramic or food-safe metal figures, and wrap them in baking paper or aluminum foil. Always warn diners that the roscón has surprises, especially children, to avoid choking and broken teeth.

A trick to keep the hole from closing is to place a greased plating ring or a ball of aluminum foil in the center.

## Step 6: second fermentation

Brush the roscón with beaten egg, cover it with plastic wrap without letting it touch it, and let it ferment until it has almost doubled in volume, between one and two hours.

## Step 7: decorate

Preheat the oven to 180°C with heat up and down.

Paint the roscón again with egg, being very careful not to deflate it. Arrange the candied fruit in strips or pieces, alternating colors, and distribute the flaked almonds and mounds of moistened sugar among the fruit.

## Step 8: Bake

Bake the roscón in the middle part of the oven for 20 to 25 minutes, until golden brown. If it browns too quickly, cover it with aluminum foil for the last few minutes. Inside it should reach about 90 °C.

Let it cool completely on a rack.

## Step 9: fill

If you are going to fill the roscón, whip the very cold cream with the icing sugar until it is firm. Cut the roscón in half horizontally with a serrated knife, fill with the cream using a pastry bag and place the lid on top.

Fill it shortly before serving, because the cream softens the dough and requires refrigeration.

## A calendar to have it ready on January 6

The roscón requires many hours, but almost all of them are waiting. This is a schedule that allows you to have a fresh breakfast without getting up too early:

| Moment | Task |
|---|---|
| January 5, afternoon | prepare the preferment and, two hours later, knead |
| January 5, at night | store the dough covered in the refrigerator |
| January 6, early in the morning | form the donut and let it ferment in a warm place |
| Hour and a half or two hours later | decorate and bake |
| Half an hour before breakfast | let it cool and, if you want, fill it |

If you prefer not to get up early, bake it on the 5th at night, store it well wrapped when it is cold and fill it in the morning.

## Homemade candied orange

The candied fruit from the store can be replaced by candied orange peel at home, which is much more aromatic:

1. Cut the peel of two oranges into strips, with part of the peel white.
2. Put them in cold water, bring to a boil and drain. Repeat the process two more times to remove the bitterness.
3. Prepare a syrup with 200 g of sugar and 200 g of water, add the strips and cook them over very low heat for about 45 minutes, until they are translucent.
4. Let them dry on a rack for a few hours.

Store them in a jar with a little of their syrup in the refrigerator: they are also useful for sweet breads, cakes or for dipping in chocolate.

## Conservation

- The unfilled roscón remains tender for two or three days, well wrapped in film or in a bag, at room temperature.
- Filled with cream or cream, it should be stored in the refrigerator and consumed within one or two days.
- It can be frozen without filling. Defrost it at room temperature, wrapped, and heat it for 3 minutes in the oven at 160°C.

## Common problems

**Dough does not rise.** Enriched doughs are slow. Find a warm place, between 24 and 27 ° C, and be patient. Check that the yeast is fresh.

**The roscón is dry.** Too much flour or excess baking. Respect the quantities and do not leave it in the oven more than necessary.

**The hole has closed.** Open a larger hole by forming or using a ring in the center.

**The fruit burns.** Cover the roscón with aluminum foil in the last minutes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I do it without a mixer?

Yes. Knead the dough base by hand for about 15 minutes and add the butter little by little. It will be sticky at first, but with kneading it will become elastic.

### Can I use dry yeast?

Yes. Use one third of the weight indicated for fresh yeast.

### Can it be made with sourdough?

Yes, although the times are very long. Replace the preferment with 150 g of active sourdough and reduce the yeast or eliminate it.

## In summary

A preference for flavor, a rich dough very well kneaded, butter added little by little, a patient fermentation, a shape with a generous hole and a decoration that shines like a crown. The homemade roscón takes time, but on Three Kings morning it is worth every minute.
