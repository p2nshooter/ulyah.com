Madeleines are part of the memory of almost all Spanish kitchens: the smell of lemon in the oven at home, the curled paper that is carefully peeled off, the crunchy sugar on the top and the moment of dipping them in milk or coffee. They are simple, economical and, well made, much better than industrial ones.

The Spanish madeleine is not the same as the French *madeleine*, in the shape of a shell. Ours is made in paper capsules, with oil instead of just butter and with a characteristic "top", that little mountain that rises in the center. Getting that high pompadour is the small challenge that we explain in this recipe.

## Ingredients for about 16 muffins

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Large eggs, at room temperature | 3 |
| sugar | 150g |
| Sunflower or mild olive oil | 150ml |
| whole milk | 75ml |
| Loose or all-purpose wheat flour | 200g |
| Chemical yeast (baking powder) | 8g |
| Zest of a lemon | 1 |
| salt | a pinch |
| Sugar to sprinkle | to taste |

### About the ingredients

- **Eggs at room temperature:** they set better and trap more air. Take them out of the refrigerator an hour beforehand or leave them in warm water for 5 minutes.
- **Oil:** The traditional recipe uses mild olive or sunflower oil, which keeps the muffins soft for days. Some recipes mix equal parts oil and melted butter for more flavor.
- **Loose flour:** with little protein, gives a tender crumb. A strength flour would make muffins hard and springy.
- **Chemical yeast:** It is not baker's yeast, but a mixture of baking soda and acids that produces gas when heated. We explain it in our guide on [yeasts](/recetas-y-guias/levaduras-tipos-y-equivalencias/).

## material

- An electric whisk mixer.
- A metal muffin or cupcake mold.
- Paper cupcake cases.
- A pastry bag or a large spoon.

The metal mold is important: it holds the paper capsules and makes the dough rise instead of spilling out.

## Step 1: beat the eggs with the sugar

In a large bowl, beat the eggs with the sugar and lemon zest with the electric whisk on high speed for **8 to 10 minutes**, until the mixture pales, triples in volume and forms a thick cream. When you lift the rods, the mixture should fall forming a ribbon that takes a few seconds to disappear.

This step is essential. The air you trap here is an important part of the fluffiness of the cupcake.

## Step 2: add the liquids

Without stopping beating, at low speed, add the oil in a thin stream and then the milk. The mixture will lose some volume; It's normal.

## Step 3: add the flour

Sift the flour with the baking powder and salt over the mixture. Incorporate it with a spatula, with enveloping movements, from bottom to top, just until there are no lumps or traces of flour. Do not overmix: you will develop gluten and the muffins will be tough.

## Step 4: cold rest

This is the secret of the pompadour. Cover the dough with film and keep it in the refrigerator **at least an hour**, and better if it is two or even overnight.

Why does it work? When very cold dough enters a very hot oven, a thermal contrast occurs. The edges of the muffin, in contact with the hot mold, set quickly, while the center, still cold and liquid, continues to expand and can only grow upwards. The result is a cupcake with a raised center. Additionally, resting allows the flour to hydrate and the dough to become thicker.

## Step 5: fill the capsules

Preheat the oven to **230°C** with heat up and down, at least 20 minutes beforehand.

Place the paper capsules in the mold. Take the dough out of the refrigerator, stir it gently with a spatula and fill the capsules to **three-quarters of their capacity**. A piping bag makes this step cleaner and faster.

Sprinkle a little sugar over each cupcake. When baked, the sugar will form a thin, crispy crust.

## Step 6: heat stroke baking

Put the mold in the oven and **immediately lower the temperature to 200 °C**. Bake for 15 to 18 minutes, without opening the door for the first 12 minutes, until golden brown and a toothpick inserted into the center comes out clean.

The very hot oven at the beginning causes the initial impulse that forms the pompadour; The drop in temperature allows them to cook inside without burning on the outside.

## Step 7: Cool

Remove the muffins from the mold and let them cool on a rack. If you leave them in the hot mold, the moisture will condense on the paper and they will soften on the bottom.

## Why doesn't the pompadour go up?

If your muffins come out flat, check these points:

| Cause | Solution |
|---|---|
| Dough at room temperature | Refrigerate for at least one hour |
| Little hot oven | Preheat at 230°C for at least 20 minutes |
| Capsules without metal mold | Always use a mold that holds them |
| Lightly beaten eggs | Beat 8 to 10 minutes until white |
| Underfilled capsules | Fill up to three quarters |
| Expired chemical yeast | Check the date |
| Open the oven door soon | Wait at least 12 minutes |

## Conservation

Once cool, store the muffins in a tin or airtight container. They stay tender for four or five days thanks to the oil. They also freeze very well; To defrost them, just leave them at room temperature for an hour or microwave them for 15 seconds.

## Variants

- **Orange muffins:** replace the lemon zest with orange zest and half the milk with orange juice.
- **Chocolate muffins:** replace 30 g of flour with unsweetened cocoa powder and add some chocolate chips to the dough.
- **Yogurt muffins:** replace the milk with natural yogurt.
- **Whole-grain muffins:** replace half of the flour with fine whole-wheat flour and add 20 ml more milk.
- **Muffins with anise:** add a teaspoon of anise grain or a splash of sweet anise, as is done in some areas of Castile.

## Organize a large batch

If you want to make muffins for many people or for the entire week, it is a good idea to organize yourself:

- **Prepare the dough the night before.** Resting for a long time in the refrigerator not only improves the crust, but also the texture. The dough holds well for up to 24 hours.
- **Bake in batches.** If you have a single pan, keep the dough in the refrigerator while each batch is baking, so that the thermal contrast is maintained.
- **Allow the mold to cool between batches.** If you fill the capsules in a hot mold, the dough begins to heat up before entering the oven and the peak rises less.
- **Do not double the recipe in a single bowl** if your mixer is underpowered: it is best to blend twice the original amount.

## How to serve them

Magdalenas are the classic breakfast and snack in many Spanish homes. Some ways to enjoy them:

- **Dipped in milk or coffee with milk**, as tradition dictates.
- **With hot chocolate**, instead of churros, on a winter afternoon.
- **Cut in half and toasted**, with a little butter and homemade jam, when they are a couple of days old.
- **As a dessert base**, crumbled at the bottom of a glass with cream, fruit and cream.

## Cupcakes, madeleines and muffins

They look similar, but they are not the same. The **Spanish muffin** is made with well-beaten eggs and oil, baked in paper cases and boasts a high crest and a light crumb. The **French madeleine** uses butter, is baked in a shell-shaped metal mold and develops a small "hump" on the smooth side thanks to the same thermal contrast. The Anglo-Saxon muffin, on the other hand, is mixed just enough, without beating the eggs until white, and usually has fruit, nuts or chocolate in abundance, with a denser and moist crumb. Knowing the difference helps when choosing a recipe: if you are looking for the richness and fluffiness of traditional ones, the long beating of the eggs does not allow for shortcuts.

## a little history

The magdalena is one of the most popular pieces of Spanish pastries of the 20th century. For decades, in many towns they were made in the communal oven or bakery, and the recipe was passed down from generation to generation with small family variations: more lemon, a touch of cinnamon, half oil and half butter. There are those who defend that the true madeleine should only contain olive oil, and those who cannot conceive a madeleine without butter. The truth is that both versions are delicious.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I reduce sugar?

You can reduce it up to 120 g without major changes. Underneath, the texture will be less spongy, because the sugar helps stabilize the egg foam and maintain moisture.

### Can I make them without an electric mixer?

It is possible with manual whisks, but beating eggs and sugar will take much more time and effort. It is the step that should not be shortened.

### Why are my cupcakes sticking to the paper?

It usually happens when they are undercooked or when they are stored while still hot. Let them cool completely before storing them.

## In summary

Well beaten eggs, soft oil, loose flour, cold rested dough and a very hot oven that is lowered as soon as the mold is inserted. These are the secrets of fluffy cupcakes with high crests, like the usual ones.
