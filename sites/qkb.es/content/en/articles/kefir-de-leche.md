Milk kefir is a fermented drink native to the Caucasus, slightly acidic, creamy and sometimes with a slight effervescent tingle. Unlike yogurt, which is made from a few strains of bacteria, kefir is made from **nodules** that contain a complex community of lactic acid bacteria and yeast that live together in a gelatinous matrix.

One of the most beautiful things about kefir is that it is shared. The nodules grow and multiply with use, and many people receive them from a friend or neighbor who had extra. With a few nodules and milk, you can have fresh kefir every day for years. This guide explains how to do it and how to care for the nodules so they stay healthy.

## What are kefir nodules

The nodules, sometimes called "grains" or "bulgars", look like small white or yellowish cauliflowers, with a gelatinous and elastic texture. They are made up of proteins and polysaccharides, including one called kefiran, produced by the microorganisms that live in them.

This community includes various species of lactic bacteria and yeasts. Bacteria transform the lactose in milk into lactic acid, which gives the milk its sour taste and thickens; Yeasts produce small amounts of carbon dioxide and aromas, and a minimal amount of alcohol.

Kefir nodules cannot be "created" from scratch at home: they always come from other nodules. They can be obtained from other people, in some specialized stores or online stores, sometimes dehydrated.

## What you need

- **Milk kefir nodules**, one or two tablespoons.
- **Milk**: whole, semi-skimmed, cow, goat or sheep. Pasteurized and UHT milk work well. The whole one gives a creamier kefir.
- **A clean glass jar**, half a liter or one liter.
- **A fine mesh plastic or stainless steel strainer**.
- **A plastic, wooden or stainless steel spoon.**
- **A cloth or lid** that allows the jar to breathe or closes loosely.

For a long time it was said that metal killed nodules. Stainless steel is no problem in the brief contact of straining and stirring; What you should avoid are reactive metals, such as uncoated aluminum or copper, and containers that can rust.

## The proportion

A common reference is **one tablespoon of nodules per 250 to 500 ml of milk**. More nodules or more heat accelerate fermentation; Fewer nodules or more cold slow it down. Over time, you will adjust the ratio to your taste and the temperature of your home.

## step by step

1. **Put the nodules in the jar.**
2. **Add the milk** at room temperature or cold, without heating it.
3. **Cover the jar** with a cloth secured with a rubber band or with the lid loosely attached.
4. **Allow to ferment at room temperature**, between approximately 18 and 26°C, away from direct sunlight, for **between 18 and 36 hours**.
5. **Check point**: the milk will have thickened and will smell slightly sour. There may be a slight separation of whey in some areas.
6. **Strain**: pour the contents of the jar over the strainer placed on top of a bowl or another jar. Stir gently so that the kefir passes through and the nodules remain in the strainer.
7. **Put the nodules back in the clean jar**, or in the same unwashed one, with new milk, and start the cycle again.
8. **Store the strained kefir in the refrigerator.**

It is not necessary to wash the nodules with water between one batch and another; in fact, chlorinated water can weaken them.

## Flavor depending on time and temperature

| Fermentation time | Texture and flavor |
|---|---|
| 12–18 hours | Smooth, low acidity, thick milk texture |
| 24 hours | Balanced, creamy, slightly acidic |
| 36–48 hours | Very acidic, thick, with whey separation |

In summer, with high temperatures, kefir can be ready in 12 hours or less. In winter, it may need more than a day. If the kefir separates into a solid part and a lot of whey, it is overfermented: it is not dangerous, but it tastes very acidic. Next time, reduce the time or the number of nodules.

## The second fermentation

After straining, you can leave the kefir in a closed jar at room temperature for a few more hours, often with an added ingredient:

- Lemon or orange peel.
- A cinnamon stick.
- Some strawberries or red fruits.
- A little vanilla.

This second fermentation softens the acidity for some people, provides aromas and slightly increases effervescence. Then, store the kefir in the refrigerator.

## Take care of the nodules

### In the day to day

As long as you make kefir regularly, the nodules will stay healthy and grow. When you have too many, you can give them away, eat them, or compost them.

### If you go away for a few days

Put the nodules in a jar with fresh milk, in a generous proportion of milk, and store them in the refrigerator. This way they can last one or two weeks. When you return, strain and start with new milk; The first batches may come out somewhat irregular.

### For long periods

Some people freeze the nodules or dehydrate them at room temperature on paper. When reactivated, they may require several batches to return to full performance.

## Warning signs

- **Mold** on the surface, green, black, pink or fuzzy: discard the kefir and, if the nodules are affected, also the nodules.
- **Unpleasant odor**, rotten or very different from the usual acidic and milky odor.
- **Nodules that fall apart or become slimy** and stop fermenting milk.

A kefir that is very acidic, separated, or has a slight yeasty smell is not a sign that it has gone bad. If you have any real doubt about its status, do not consume it.

## How to use kefir

- **Alone**, cold, as a drink.
- **In smoothies** with fruit.
- **At breakfast**, with cereals or oatmeal.
- **In sauces**, as a base for a salad dressing with herbs, lemon and oil.
- **In baking**, instead of yogurt or buttermilk, in cakes and pancakes.
- **To make kefir "cheese"**, drain it on a cloth such as labneh.

## Adapt the rhythm to your consumption

The nodules grow and production increases over time, so it is a good idea to adjust the amount to what you actually drink. If at home you drink a glass a day, a tablespoon of nodules in half a liter of milk is enough; The rest can be given as a gift or stored in the refrigerator as a reserve. If one day you don't feel like straining, put the jar in the refrigerator: the cold slows down the fermentation and you can strain it the next day without the kefir being overcooked. Some people always ferment at room temperature for about 12 hours and finish another 12 in the refrigerator, which gives a smoother and thicker kefir.

## Three simple recipes with kefir

**Kefir and herb dressing.** Mix 150 ml of kefir with the juice of half a lemon, a tablespoon of extra virgin olive oil, chopped dill or chives, salt and pepper. It is used for potato, cucumber or lettuce salads and to accompany roasted vegetables.

**Kefir pancakes.** Beat 250 ml of kefir with an egg, a tablespoon of sugar and a pinch of salt. Add 150 g of flour and a teaspoon of baking powder, mix without over-beating and let it rest for ten minutes. Cook small portions in a greased skillet over medium heat until bubbles appear, flip and brown the other side. The acidity of kefir makes them especially fluffy.

**Breakfast smoothie.** Blend a glass of kefir with a ripe banana, two tablespoons of rolled oats and a pinch of cinnamon. If the banana is very ripe, it does not need sugar.

## Kefir and health: what we know

Kefir is a fermented food that contains live microorganisms, as well as proteins, calcium and vitamins from milk. Its possible effects on digestion, intestinal microbiota and lactose tolerance have been studied, and some results are promising, but the evidence in people is still limited and does not allow it to be attributed curative properties. The most reasonable thing is to consider it a healthy food within a varied diet.

Immunocompromised people or people with serious illnesses should consult their doctor before consuming foods with live microorganisms prepared at home.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does kefir have alcohol?

It contains very small amounts of alcohol, usually less than one percent, due to the action of yeasts. With long fermentations in a closed jar, the quantity may increase somewhat.

### Is it suitable for lactose intolerant people?

Some lactose is fermented, so many people with mild intolerance tolerate it better than milk. But it contains lactose. There are kefir options made with lactose-free milk.

### Can it be made with vegetable drinks?

Milk nodules can ferment some plant-based drinks, but they weaken over time because they need lactose. If you want to try, alternate with milk to keep them healthy.

### Are milk kefir and water kefir the same thing?

No. They are different communities of microorganisms: water nodules ferment water with sugar and are translucent and crystalline. They have their own guide in this section.

## In summary

Nodules, milk, a jar and a day at room temperature: that's how simple it is to have homemade kefir. Strain, start again with new milk and store the kefir in the refrigerator. With minimal care, the nodules will accompany you for years, and you will surely end up sharing them with someone.
