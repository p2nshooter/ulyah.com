Palm trees are one of the classics of Spanish pastries: two spirals of puff pastry joined together in the shape of a heart, crunchy, with layers that melt and a surface of caramelized sugar. There are small ones, to accompany coffee, and giant ones, the size of a hand, sometimes dipped in chocolate. In France they are known as *palmiers* and in other countries as "elephant ears".

With purchased puff pastry they are one of the simplest recipes that exist. But making them with homemade butter puff pastry is another experience: more flavor, more layers and the satisfaction of having made everything from scratch. This guide explains a **quick puff pastry**, much easier than classic puff pastry, and how to turn it into perfect palm trees.

## Classic Puff Pastry vs. Quick Puff Pastry

- **Classic puff pastry** encases an entire block of butter inside a dough of flour and water, and is folded and stretched six times with cold rests. It is the pastry shop method and requires practice.
- **Quick puff pastry**, also called "quick inverted" puff pastry or *blitz*, mixes the butter in pieces directly with the flour, without integrating it, and then folds it several times. The layers are somewhat less regular, but the result is excellent for palm trees, homemade millefeuille or empanadas, and is prepared in much less time.

## Ingredients for about 20 medium palm trees

### Quick puff pastry

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| All-purpose or bread-making wheat flour | 250g |
| Very cold unsalted butter | 250g |
| very cold water | 120 to 130 ml |
| salt | 5g |

### to form

- 150 g of sugar.
- Optional: 100 g of dark chocolate for dipping.

## Step 1: the dough

1. Cut the very cold butter into 1 to 1.5 cm cubes. If the kitchen is hot, put the dice in the freezer for 10 minutes.
2. In a large bowl, mix the flour with the salt.
3. Add the butter cubes and mix them with the flour so that they are coated. **Do not undo them**: there should be whole pieces visible.
4. Add the cold water little by little, mixing with a spatula or your fingertips, until the dough comes together into an irregular ball, with chunks of butter.

The dough will look very unpromising, with streaks and chunks of butter. It's exactly what we're looking for.

Flatten the dough into a rectangle, wrap it in film and keep it in the refrigerator for 20 minutes.

## Step 2: the folds

In puff pastry, each fold multiplies the number of layers.

1. On a lightly floured table, roll out the dough into a long rectangle, about 20 × 50 cm, always in the same direction.
2. Fold it in three, like a letter: one third towards the center and the other third on top. This is a **simple lap**.
3. Turn the dough 90 degrees, so the fold is on one side.
4. Stretch again into a long rectangle and fold again into three.
5. Wrap the dough and keep it in the refrigerator for 20 or 30 minutes.

Repeat the process two more times, until completing **six simple laps** in total, always with cold rests every two laps.

As you progress, the dough will become smoother and more uniform, and the pieces of butter will become fine veins. If at any point the butter starts to soften or stick, put the dough in the refrigerator before continuing.

After the last round, let the dough rest in the refrigerator for at least an hour, preferably overnight.

## Step 3: form the palm trees with sugar

In palm trees, sugar replaces flour to stretch the dough. This way it embeds itself in the layers and, in the oven, caramelizes.

1. Generously sprinkle the table with sugar.
2. Place the puff pastry on top and sprinkle sugar on top as well.
3. Roll out the dough into a rectangle about 25 × 40 cm and 3 to 4 mm thick, adding sugar on both sides as needed.
4. Mark the center of the rectangle on its long side.
5. Fold the two long sides toward the center, so that each takes up half the space up to the center line.
6. Fold both sides toward the center again, so they meet on the center line.
7. Fold one half over the other, like a book that closes.

You now have an elongated cylinder with six layers on each side. Wrap it in film and keep it in the freezer for 30 minutes or in the refrigerator for an hour, until it is firm. This way the cut will be clean.

## Step 4: cut

Using a sharp knife, cut the cylinder into 1 to 1.5 cm thick slices. Place them on a tray with baking paper, with the cut facing up, leaving plenty of space between them, because they open and grow quite a bit in the oven.

Slightly open the two "wings" of each palm tree with your fingers to shape them into a heart shape.

## Step 5: Bake

Preheat the oven to **200°C** with heat up and down.

1. Bake the palms for 10 to 12 minutes, until the base is caramelized and golden.
2. Take them out, turn them carefully with a spatula, because the sugar burns.
3. Bake another 5 to 7 minutes, until the second side is also caramelized.

Keep an eye on them for the last few minutes: the sugar quickly goes from golden to burnt.

Let them cool on a rack. As it cools, the caramel hardens and the palms become crispy.

## chocolate palm trees

When the palms are completely cool, melt the dark chocolate in a double boiler or in the microwave at short intervals. Dip one of the sides of each palm tree, or half of each one, and leave them on baking paper until the chocolate solidifies.

For a shiny chocolate that doesn't melt in your hands, you can temper it: melt two-thirds of the chocolate at about 45–50°C, remove from the heat and add the remaining chopped third, stirring until the mixture drops to about 31–32°C.

## Why puff pastry rises

The puff pastry does not contain yeast: it rises thanks to the steam. Each fold creates alternating layers of dough and butter. In the hot oven, the water contained in the dough and butter turns into steam and pushes the layers of dough upward, while the fat prevents them from sticking together. That is why cold during preparation is so important, which keeps the butter in sheets without melting with the flour, and a very hot oven from the beginning, which generates steam at once before the butter melts and escapes.

## Choose butter

Use real butter, about 82% fat, and never spreadable margarines or mixtures with a lot of water, which soften quickly and give poor layers. Look for a butter that is firm and somewhat plastic when cold, that bends without breaking into pieces. Some bakeries use special butters for puff pastry, harder, but at home a good unsalted butter, very cold, gives an excellent result.

## Salty palm trees for the aperitif

The same technique works for an appetizer. Replace the sugar with a thin layer of grated cured cheese, such as manchego, and a pinch of paprika from La Vera, or with a thin layer of black olive pâté. Stretch the dough with a little flour instead of sugar, form the same cylinder and bake at 200°C until golden, without needing to turn them. They are perfect freshly made, with a vermouth or a beer.

## Other ways to use quick puff pastry

With the same puff pastry you can make:

- **Puff pastry ties** with sugar, twisting strips of dough.
- **Homemade millefeuille**, baking sheets pierced with a fork and assembling them with pastry cream.
- **Cream sticks**, rolling strips around conical molds.
- **Salty dumplings and puff pastries**, filled with tuna, spinach or cheese.

## Conservation

The palm trees keep crisp for four or five days in a can or airtight container, in a cool, dry place. Moisture is your enemy: if they become soft, a couple of minutes in the oven at 160°C will restore their crispiness.

Raw puff pastry will keep for three days in the refrigerator, well wrapped, or up to three months in the freezer. You can also freeze the already formed cylinder of palm trees and cut it directly frozen.

## Common problems

**The puff pastry does not rise.** The butter has melted with the flour and there are no layers. Always work with the dough very cold and rest.

**Butter leaks out in the oven.** This happens if the layers have broken while stretching or if the oven was not hot enough to begin with.

**Palm trees open too much and lose their shape.** The cylinder was not firm enough when cut or the slices were too thick.

**The sugar burns.** The oven heats up too much from below. Raise the tray one height or place a second tray underneath.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use purchased puff pastry?

Yes. Use a sheet of butter puff pastry and follow the steps from forming with sugar.

### How many turns are necessary?

Six simple turns give a well-developed puff pastry. With four, the layers are thicker and more irregular; with more than six, the layers become so thin that the puff pastry rises less.

## In summary

Very cold butter in cubes, an irregular dough, six simple turns with cold rests and one shaped with sugar instead of flour. Cut slices of one centimeter, bake on both sides, keeping an eye on the caramel and, if you feel like it, dip them in chocolate. This way you get crispy, shiny palm trees full of layers.
