Before launching into sourdough, high hydration or preferments, it is a good idea to learn how to make a simple bread, with baker's yeast, that comes out right the first time. It is the bread that allows you to understand how the dough behaves, what good fermentation looks like and how your kitchen oven reacts. With this recipe you can make bars, balls or muffins in one morning.

## Ingredients for two bars or one large ball

| Ingredient | Quantity | Baker percentage |
|---|---|---|
| Bread flour | 500g | 100 % |
| warm water | 330g | 66 % |
| salt | 10g | 2 % |
| fresh yeast | 10g | 2 % |

If you use dry baker's yeast, use 3 to 4 g instead of the 10 g of fresh yeast.

## Fresh, active dry and instant dry yeast

In the supermarket you will find various presentations of baker's yeast. They are all the same microorganism, *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*, but in different formats:

| Type | How it is presented | How to use | Equivalence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fresh or pressed | Wet cubes, in the refrigerated area | Crumbled in flour or dissolved in water | 10g |
| Active dry | Granules | It is usually hydrated first in warm water | 4 to 5g |
| Instant dry | Fine powder in sachets | Directly mixed with flour | 3 to 4g |

Do not confuse baker's yeast with "chemical yeast" or baking powder, which is used in cakes and is not used for making bread. We explain it in detail in our guide to [yeasts](/recetas-y-guias/levaduras-tipos-y-equivalencias/).

## Step 1: mix

In a large bowl, put the flour. Crumble fresh yeast over it or sprinkle dry yeast. Add the warm water, about 25–30°C, and mix with a spoon or by hand until no dry flour remains. Add the salt and continue mixing.

Let the dough rest, covered, for 15 minutes. This rest allows the flour to hydrate and makes kneading much easier.

## Step 2: knead

Pour the dough onto a clean, flour-free table and knead for 8 to 10 minutes. A simple technique:

1. Push the dough forward with the heel of your hand, stretching it.
2. Fold it over itself.
3. Turn it a quarter turn and repeat.

At first, the dough will be sticky and uneven. Little by little it will become smooth, elastic and less sticky. It is ready when, when you stretch a small piece between your fingers, it forms a thin membrane before breaking.

## Step 3: first fermentation

Shape into a ball, place in a lightly greased bowl, and cover. Let it ferment at room temperature until it doubles in volume. At about 24°C, it takes about an hour; With colder weather, it can take twice as long.

During this fermentation, the yeast consumes sugars and produces carbon dioxide and alcohol. The gas inflates the gluten network and the dough rises.

## Step 4: Split and Bowl

Pour the dough onto a lightly floured table and gently degas it. Divide it into two equal parts, about 420 g each, if you are making bars, or leave it whole for a ball.

Roll each piece: fold the edges toward the center, turn it over and rotate it on the table with cupped hands to tighten the surface. Let them rest covered for 15 minutes.

## Step 5: Form

### Bars

1. Gently flatten each ball into a rectangle.
2. Fold the top third toward the center and press with the heel of your hand.
3. Fold the bottom third toward the center and press again.
4. Fold the piece in half lengthwise and seal the edge with the heel of your hand.
5. Roll the piece under your palms, from the center to the ends, until you reach the desired length.

### ball

Repeat the rolling with more tension until you obtain a smooth and taut surface.

Place the pieces, seam side down, on a tray lined with baking paper or on a floured cloth with folds separating them.

## Step 6: second fermentation

Cover the pieces and let them ferment for 40 to 60 minutes, until they have clearly increased in volume and, when pressed with a finger, the gap slowly recovers.

Meanwhile, preheat the oven to 240°C with an empty tray on the bottom.

## Step 7: brush and bake

1. Make some cuts in the surface with a cleaver or a very sharp knife: several diagonal cuts in the bars, a cross or a square in the ball.
2. Put the bread in the oven.
3. Pour a glass of hot water into the bottom tray and close quickly to generate steam.
4. Bake for 10 minutes with steam at 240°C.
5. Remove the tray from the water, lower to 220°C and bake for an additional 15 to 20 minutes for the bars or 25 to 30 minutes for the ball.

The bread is done when the crust is golden brown and sounds hollow when you hit the base.

## Step 8: Cool

Let the bread cool on a rack. The bars need about 30 minutes; the ball, at least an hour.

## How to get more flavor without getting complicated

Bread with yeast and short fermentation has a simple flavor. To improve it without changing the recipe:

- **Reduces yeast and lengthens times.** With 3 g of fresh yeast, the first fermentation will last about three hours and the bread will have much more flavor.
- **Ferment cold.** After kneading, store the dough in the refrigerator for 12 to 24 hours. The next day, temper it for an hour and continue forming.
- **Use a small part of whole wheat or rye flour**, 10%, for color and aroma.

## The village bread

In many areas of Spain, village bread is called a round bread, with a thick crust and a dense but tender crumb, traditionally made with a piece of dough from the previous day, the "old dough", which acts as a preferment. To get closer to that style at home, keep 150 g of the dough from this recipe in the refrigerator and add it to the next batch. The bread will gain flavor and keep better.

## Bagels

With the same dough you can make between eight and ten rolls. Divide the dough into pieces of 80 to 100 g, roll each one tightly and place them on the tray, separated from each other. The second fermentation will be somewhat shorter, 30 to 45 minutes, and the baking will be 15 to 18 minutes at 220 °C, with steam at the beginning. You can paint them with water and sprinkle sesame or poppy seeds before baking.

## How to preserve homemade bread

Homemade bread does not contain preservatives and hardens faster than industrial bread. Hardening is not due to drying, but rather because the starch reorganizes and crystallizes, a process that is accelerated at refrigerator temperatures. Therefore:

- **Do not store bread in the refrigerator.** It hardens faster than at room temperature.
- **Store in a cloth or paper bag**, or wrapped in a cloth, so the crust stays reasonably crispy.
- **Freeze it if you won't eat it within a day or two.** The freezer stops the hardening. Cut it first into slices or halves to get only what you need.
- **Recover it in the oven.** A few minutes at 200°C restore the crispy crust to day-old bread. If it is a little dry, run the crust under the tap for a second before putting it in.

Hard bread is not thrown away either: it is used for torrijas, migas, garlic soups, gazpacho, salmorejo or breadcrumbs.

## The baker's percentage, explained

The percentage column of the ingredients table is the tool bakers use to scale recipes. Everything is expressed in relation to flour, which is always 100% worth. Thus, a dough at 66% hydration has 66 g of water per 100 g of flour, and salt at 2% is 2 g per 100 g of flour. If you want to make more bread, just multiply: with 750 g of flour you will need 495 g of water, 15 g of salt and 15 g of fresh yeast. And if you change flour and find the dough too firm or too sticky, adjust the hydration two points at a time and write it down for next time.

## Know your oven

Each domestic oven has its character. Preheat it for at least 30 minutes, because the walls take longer than the air to reach the temperature and are what give the bread the initial push. If you notice one side browning sooner, rotate the pan halfway through cooking. And if the recipe times never add up, check the actual temperature with an oven thermometer: many differences between what the control says and what happens inside are resolved in this way.

## Common problems

**Bread does not rise.** The yeast may be expired or damaged by water that is too hot. Check the date and use warm water, never hot.

**It rises a lot but sinks in the oven.** The second fermentation has been too long.

**The crust is soft.** Lack of steam at the beginning and baking at the end. Leave the bread for a couple of minutes in the turned off oven with the door ajar.

**The crumb is very dense.** There is no kneading or fermentation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I knead with a robot?

Yes. Mix at low speed for 3 minutes and knead at medium speed for 5 or 6 minutes.

### How long does this bread last?

The bars are at their best on the same day. The ball keeps well for two or three days wrapped in a cloth. Both freeze very well.

## In summary

Flour, water, salt and yeast, in simple proportions. A good kneading, two fermentations until the dough doubles, forming with tension and a hot oven with steam. With this basic bread you will learn the fundamentals that you will then apply to any recipe.
