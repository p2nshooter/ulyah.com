Creating sourdough is the exciting part; keeping it alive for years is the part that really makes the difference. A well-tended sourdough is predictable: you know how long it takes to rise, what it smells like at its best, and how it will behave in bread. A neglected sourdough, on the other hand, is slow, very acidic and capricious.

The good news is that care is simple and adapts to almost any lifestyle, whether you bake every day or make bread once a month. This guide explains how.

If you don't have sourdough yet, start with our guide to [creating sourdough from scratch](/recetas-y-guias/crear-masa-madre-desde-cero/).

## Understand proportions

The clearest way to describe a diet is with three numbers: sourdough, flour and water, by weight. For example:

- **1:1:1** means 20 g of sourdough, 20 g of flour and 20 g of water.
- **1:2:2** means 20 g of sourdough, 40 g of flour and 40 g of water.
- **1:5:5** means 10 g of sourdough, 50 g of flour and 50 g of water.

The more food you give it relative to the sourdough you keep, **the longer it takes to reach its peak**. This is very useful to organize yourself:

| Proportion | Time to peak at 24°C | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 1:1:1 | 3 to 5 hours | If you are going to knead soon |
| 1:2:2 | 5 to 7 hours | Regular morning or afternoon feeding |
| 1:5:5 | 8 to 12 hours | Feed at night and knead in the morning |
| 1:10:10 | 12 to 16 hours | One-day absences in summer |

With the heat, all times are shortened; With the cold, they lengthen.

## Sourdough hydration

The most common thing is to keep the sourdough at 100% hydration, that is, with the same weight of water as flour. It is easy to calculate and mix. Some bakeries work with firmer sourdoughs, between 50 and 60%, which ferment more slowly and produce more acetic flavors. If a recipe calls for a firm sourdough and you have a runny one, you can turn it into a couple of feedings by adjusting the water ratio.

## The peak: when to use sourdough

A fed sourdough follows a curve: it expands, reaches a maximum volume, and when it runs out of food, it begins to sink. The best time to use it in a bread is **at the peak or just before**, when it has the maximum population of active yeasts and has not yet become too acidic.

Signs that it is at its peak:

- It has doubled or tripled its volume.
- The surface is domed, not sunken.
- It has bubbles everywhere, including on the walls of the jar.
- It smells like yogurt and fruit, not strong vinegar or acetone.

If you use it soggy, when it has already sunk, the bread will rise more slowly and come out more acidic. If you use it too soon, it will lack strength.

## Routine for those who bake often

If you make bread several times a week, it is easiest to keep the sourdough at room temperature and feed it once or twice a day:

1. In the morning, remove all but about 20 g of the dough.
2. Add 40 g of flour and 40 g of water, mix and mark the level.
3. At night, repeat the process if it's hot, or use a higher ratio, like 1:5:5, if you want it to last until the next day.

Keeping the quantity small reduces waste. On the day you are going to bake, prepare the quantity called for in the recipe with a slightly larger feed.

## Routine for those who bake once a week

The refrigerator is your ally. At around 4°C, the activity of the sourdough slows down greatly and you can go a week without eating.

1. Feed the sourdough and leave it for one or two hours at room temperature, so that it can start working.
2. Store it in the refrigerator with the lid propped up.
3. A day before baking, take it out, remove all but 20g and feed. If she was very lazy, do two feedings in a row before using her.
4. Prepare your bread and, with what is left, feed again and store in the refrigerator.

A sourdough can live like this for years. It is possible that, after weeks of refrigeration, a dark liquid appears on the surface: it is alcohol and water, a sign of hunger. Remove and feed.

## Long absences: vacations and breaks

### Up to three weeks: refrigerator with firm feeding

Before you leave, feed the sourdough with a high ratio and little water, for example 10g sourdough, 50g flour and 30g water. A firmer dough ferments more slowly and lasts longer. Store it in the refrigerator. Upon returning, he will need two or three feedings to regain his strength.

### Several months: dehydrated sourdough

It is the best way to have a backup or to give sourdough to someone who lives far away:

1. Feed the sourdough and wait for it to reach its peak.
2. Spread it in a very thin layer on baking paper.
3. Let it dry at room temperature, in a ventilated place, for one or two days, until it is completely dry and brittle. Do not use a hot oven: the heat will kill the yeast.
4. Break it into flakes and store it in an airtight jar in a cool, dry place, or in the freezer.

To reactivate it, mix 10 g of flakes with 20 g of water, let them hydrate for an hour, add 20 g of flour and continue feeding as at the beginning. It usually recovers in three to seven days.

### freeze

You can also freeze a small amount of active sourdough. It works, although some people notice that it takes longer to recover than when dehydrated.

## How to revive a lazy sourdough starter

If your sourdough takes a long time to rise or barely rises, the cause is almost always one of three: cold, prolonged hunger, or poor flour.

- Move it to a warm place, 24 to 27 °C.
- Feed every 12 hours for two or three days in a row, with a 1:2:2 ratio.
- Add 10 to 20% whole wheat or rye flour to each feeding.
- Use chlorine-free water.

Within two or three days, most sourdoughs return to their usual pace.

## How to change the flavor of bread from sourdough

The sourdough greatly influences the flavor of the bread. You can guide it:

- **For a softer, milkier bread:** use the sourdough at its peak, keep it liquid and warm, and feed it frequently.
- **For a more acidic bread:** use a firmer, colder and slightly past its peak sourdough, and feed it with whole wheat flour.

The temperature of bread fermentation also influences: long cold fermentations develop more acetic acidity.

## What to do with discards

Each time you feed, you remove a portion of the sourdough. These discards do not have to go in the trash:

- Pancakes and crepes, mixed with egg, milk and a pinch of baking soda.
- Cracker-type salty cookies, spread very thinly with oil, salt and seeds.
- Biscuits and muffins, as part of the liquid.
- Pizza dough, combined with a little yeast.

Store the discards in a jar in the refrigerator for a week and use them whenever it suits you.

## Share your sourdough

A healthy sourdough is the best gift for someone who wants to start making bread. To share, feed it a few hours beforehand and separate 30-50g into a clean jar with a lid, leaving room for it to grow. If the person is going to take a while to use it, it is better to give it freshly fed and stored cold, or give them some flakes of dehydrated sourdough in a paper envelope, which last for weeks without care. Accompany the gift with a simple note: what flour you use, what proportion you feed it, and how long it takes to reach its peak in your kitchen. With this information, you will adapt to your new home in a few days.

## Warning signs

A healthy sourdough is very resistant, but it is important to know the signs that something is wrong:

- **Hairy, colored mold stains** on the surface or walls.
- **Pink or orange streaks**, which may indicate bacterial contamination.
- **Rotten smell** that does not go away after feeding.

In these cases, it is prudent to discard it, clean the jar well and reactivate a dehydrated copy or start again. That is why it is advisable to always have a dry copy saved.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to change jars?

It's a good idea to transfer it to a clean jar every one or two weeks. Dirty walls accumulate dry debris that can become moldy.

### Can I feed it spelled or rye flour?

Yes. Whole wheat and rye flours ferment faster. Changing the flour alters the behavior for a few days, while the crop adapts.

### How much sourdough do I need to have?

Just enough. Keeping 50 to 100 g is enough for most homemade breads. The day before baking, feed it to obtain the amount called for in the recipe.

## In summary

Understand the proportions and use them to organize your schedules, use the sourdough at its peak, keep it in the refrigerator if you bake little and always keep a dehydrated copy. With these habits, your sourdough will be strong, predictable and as long as you want.
