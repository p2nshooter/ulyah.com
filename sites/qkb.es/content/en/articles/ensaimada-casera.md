The ensaimada is the most emblematic piece of Mallorcan pastry: a light, flaky and tender spiral, covered in icing sugar, that melts in the mouth. Its name comes from *saïm*, the Mallorcan word for lard, the ingredient that gives it its unique texture and unmistakable aroma. The "Ensaimada de Mallorca" has a Protected Geographical Indication, which regulates how it is made on the island.

Making it at home takes time, especially waiting, but it is not as difficult as it seems. The most important thing is a very elastic dough that can be stretched until it is almost transparent and a long, patient fermentation, until each spiral triples its volume.

## a little history

The first written references to the ensaimada in Mallorca date back to the 16th and 17th centuries, although its origin is probably earlier. For generations it was a festive and Sunday sweet, which Mallorcan families bought at the neighborhood bakery. Today it is also a travel souvenir: at Palma airport it is common to see travelers carrying the characteristic octagonal boxes.

There are two main types: the **plain ensaimada**, without filling, and the ensaimadas filled with angel hair, cream, cream or chocolate. A special variant is the *tallades* ensaimada, covered with sobrasada and candied pumpkin, typical of the carnival.

## Ingredients for 8 individual or 2 medium ensaimadas

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| strength flour | 500g |
| sugar | 100g |
| Eggs | 2 medium |
| Water or warm milk | 180 to 200g |
| fresh yeast | 20 g (or 7 g dry yeast) |
| salt | a pinch, about 3 g |
| Mild olive oil | 15g |
| Lard at room temperature | 120 to 150g |
| icing sugar | to sprinkle |

### About the butter

Lard is what defines the ensaimada. It provides a flaky, melting texture and a very characteristic aroma. Look for a good quality, white, neutral-smelling butter. It should be at room temperature, creamy like an ointment, so that it spreads easily without breaking the dough.

If you do not consume pork products, you can substitute it with creamed butter. The result will be tasty, but it will not be a traditional ensaimada: the texture will be different and the flavor will be more similar to that of a butter bun.

## Step 1: the dough

1. Dissolve the yeast in the water or warm milk.
2. In a large bowl, mix the flour, sugar and salt.
3. Add the eggs and yeast mixture and mix.
4. Knead for 10 to 15 minutes by hand, or 8 to 10 minutes with a mixer, until you obtain a smooth, elastic and somewhat sticky dough.

The dough must be very well kneaded. When you stretch it between your fingers, it should form a thin, translucent membrane without breaking. This elasticity is what will allow it to be stretched later until it is almost transparent.

If the dough is too dry and hard, add a little more water; if it is too soft, a little more flour. It should be soft and manageable.

## Step 2: first rest

Form a ball, brush it with a little oil, place it in a bowl and cover it. Let it rest in a warm place for between an hour and an hour and a half, until it doubles in volume.

## Step 3: Split

Divide the dough into eight portions of about 110 g, for individual ensaimadas, or into two portions, for medium ensaimadas. Ball each portion, spread it with a little oil and let it rest, covered, for 15 minutes. This rest relaxes the gluten and facilitates stretching.

## Step 4: stretch

This is the most characteristic step of the ensaimada.

1. Grease the table with oil. Don't use flour.
2. Place a ball of dough and flatten it with your hands.
3. Stretch it with the rolling pin until it forms an elongated rectangle.
4. Then, stretch it with your hands, gently pulling from the edges, until it is very thin, almost transparent. If a small hole breaks, nothing happens.

Mallorcan professionals stretch the dough until you can read through it. At home, just leave it as thin as possible without breaking it excessively.

## Step 5: spread the butter

Using your hands, spread a thin, even layer of butter over the entire surface of the dough. The butter is what will separate the layers and give the flaky texture, so don't be stingy, but don't add too much: a thin layer is enough.

## Step 6: Roll

Roll the sheet onto itself, from the long side, forming a long, thin cord, like a churro. Do it without squeezing too much.

Gently stretch the cord with your hands to lengthen it a little more and let it sit for 10 minutes to relax.

## Step 7: form the spiral

Place one end of the cord in the center of a tray lined with baking paper and roll it onto itself to form a loose spiral, leaving a small space between the turns. The cord will grow a lot during fermentation, so do not tighten it.

Leave plenty of space between the ensaimadas on the tray. The large ones need one tray each.

## Step 8: the long fermentation

Here is the secret of the ensaimada. Cover the tray with plastic or a large box, without touching the pieces, and let it ferment in a warm place, between 22 and 26 ° C, for 8 to 12 hours, or overnight.

The ensaimada is ready when it has **tripled its volume**, the turns of the spiral touch and, when you move the tray, it trembles. If the kitchen is cold, it may take longer; do not bake it until it has risen enough.

## Step 9: Bake

1. Preheat the oven to 180°C with heat up and down.
2. If you want, spray the ensaimadas with a little water so that the surface does not dry out.
3. Bake the individual ones for 12 to 15 minutes and the medium ones for 18 to 22 minutes, until golden brown.

Keep an eye on the oven for the last few minutes: the sugar in the dough makes them brown quickly.

## Step 10: Cool and Sprinkle

Let the ensaimadas cool on the tray, because they are very fragile when hot. When they are warm or cold, sprinkle them generously with icing sugar.

## stuffed ensaimadas

To fill the ensaimadas, place the filling on the buttered dough sheet before rolling it:

- **Angel hair:** a thin strip of angel hair along the sheet. It is the most traditional filling.
- **Pastry cream:** thick and cold, in a thin strip. It can also be filled after baking with a sleeve.
- **Chocolate:** chocolate cream or chopped chocolate.
- **Cream:** is injected with sleeve after baking and cooling.

## A schedule for Sunday breakfast

The ensaimada lends itself to working in the afternoon and baking in the morning:

| Indicative time | Task |
|---|---|
| Saturday, 5:00 p.m. | knead and let the first rest |
| Saturday, 6:30 p.m. | divide, bowl and let rest for 15 minutes |
| Saturday, 7:00 p.m. | stretch, spread the butter, roll and form the spirals |
| during the night | slow fermentation, well covered, in a warm place |
| Sunday, 8:00 | check they have tripled and bake |
| Sunday, 8:30 | cool and sprinkle with icing sugar |

In winter, if the house gets cold at night, leave the trays in the unlit oven with a bowl of hot water.

## homemade angel hair

Angel hair is made with the fibrous pulp of the citron, a pumpkin with green, marbled skin. To make it at home, cook the whole citron, chopped and with skin, in water until tender; Remove the skin and seeds and separate the strands from the pulp. Wash them several times with cold water and drain them very well, squeezing with your hands. Weigh them and cook them over low heat with about 800 g of sugar per kilo of strands, a strip of lemon peel and a cinnamon stick, stirring occasionally, for about 30 or 40 minutes, until they are shiny and amber in color. Store it in clean jars in the refrigerator and use it cold to fill the ensaimadas.

## Conservation

The smooth ensaimada is kept for two or three days in a closed box or container, at room temperature. Those filled with cream or cream should be stored in the refrigerator and consumed within a day. They can be frozen without the icing sugar; To serve them, defrost them at room temperature and bake them in the oven for 3 minutes at 160°C.

## Common problems

**The dough breaks when stretched.** Lack of kneading or lack of rest. Knead until the membrane is proof and let the balls rest for at least 15 minutes before rolling out.

**The ensaimada is hard and compact.** The fermentation has been too short. Wait until it triples the volume.

**It opens and warps in the oven.** The cord was too tight when forming the spiral.

**Surface cracks.** Too dry environment during fermentation. Cover them well and spray them with water before baking.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do you use oil and not flour to stretch?

The flour would absorb the shortening and dry out the layers. The oil allows the dough to be stretched without sticking or altering its texture.

### Can I leave the ensaimadas fermenting in the refrigerator?

Yes, but the process will be much slower, 24 to 36 hours, and then they will need several hours at room temperature to finish growing.

## In summary

A very elastic dough, stretched almost transparent on an oiled table, a thin layer of butter, a loose spiral and a whole night of fermentation. With patience and a good icing sugar, you will have the taste of Majorcan ovens at home.
