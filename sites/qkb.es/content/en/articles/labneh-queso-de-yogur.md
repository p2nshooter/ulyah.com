If you want to make your first homemade cheese without a thermometer, without rennet and without any risk of it going wrong, labneh is the perfect choice. It is yogurt drained until it loses much of its whey, with a little salt. The result is a thick, acidic and smooth cream, halfway between cream cheese and Greek yogurt.

Labneh is very popular in the Middle East and the eastern Mediterranean, where it is served for breakfast with olive oil, herbs and bread. But it fits perfectly in Spanish cuisine: on toast with tomato, as a base for a sauce or as a substitute for cream cheese in a cake. You only need two ingredients and patience.

## Ingredients

- **1 kilo of natural yogurt**, whole milk, unsweetened. It can be homemade or purchased. Greek yogurt makes a creamier labneh and yields more.
- **Between half and a teaspoon of fine salt**, to taste.

Avoid yogurts with thickeners such as gelatin or starch, which make draining difficult. Read the list of ingredients: ideally it only contains milk and ferments.

## Utensils

- A large strainer.
- A clean fine cotton cloth, double gauze or cloth filter.
- A bowl to support the strainer.

## step by step

1. **Mix yogurt with salt** in a bowl.
2. **Line the strainer with the cloth** and place it over the bowl, leaving space underneath for the whey to fall.
3. **Pour the yogurt** onto the cloth.
4. **Cover with the ends of the cloth** or with a plate.
5. **Allow to drain in the refrigerator** between 12 and 48 hours, depending on the desired texture.
6. **Remove the labneh from the cloth** and store it in a closed container.

## Texture according to time

| Draining time | Texture | Ideal use |
|---|---|---|
| 6–8 hours | I eat thick Greek yogurt | Sauces, breakfasts with fruit |
| 12–24 hours | Spreadable cream, such as cream cheese | Toasts, dips, fillings |
| 36–48 hours | firm paste | Balls in oil |

One kilo of yogurt yields approximately 400 to 600 grams of labneh, depending on the draining time and the type of yogurt.

## Why it works

Yogurt is already fermented milk: lactic acid bacteria have transformed part of the lactose into lactic acid, and that acidity has coagulated the milk proteins into a soft gel. When draining, the only thing we do is eliminate the water, the whey, that is trapped in that gel. Salt helps extract whey, adds flavor and contributes to preservation.

Being an acidic product and made cold, labneh is one of the safest homemade dairy preparations, as long as you start with yogurt in good condition and work with clean utensils.

## Labneh balls in oil

A traditional way to preserve labneh for longer:

1. Drain the labneh for 36 to 48 hours, until firm.
2. With clean, lightly oiled hands, form into walnut-sized balls.
3. Leave them in the refrigerator, on a tray, for a few hours to set.
4. Place them in a clean glass jar and cover them completely with extra virgin olive oil.
5. You can add dried herbs, such as oregano, thyme or za'atar, or paprika.

Keep the jar **in the refrigerator** and consume the balls in about two weeks, always using a clean spoon to scoop them out. The oil can solidify when cold; returns to its liquid state at room temperature.

A safety warning: Do not add fresh garlic or fresh herbs to canned foods in oil without refrigeration, as they can encourage the growth of dangerous bacteria. Always keep the jar in the refrigerator.

## How to serve it

- **Traditional style**: spread on a plate, with a hole in the center, drizzled with olive oil and sprinkled with za'atar, dried mint or paprika. With pita bread or toast.
- **On toast**: labneh, grated tomato and a splash of oil.
- **With vegetables**: as a dip for carrot, cucumber or pepper sticks.
- **With fruit and honey**: at breakfast, with seasonal fruit and nuts.
- **In sauces**: mixed with grated cucumber, garlic and mint, tzatziki style.
- **In baking**: instead of cream cheese in some cakes or frostings, with a more acidic flavor.

## Three recipes with labneh

### Labneh, tomato and oregano toast

For a five-minute breakfast: toast a slice of loaf bread, spread it with a generous layer of labneh, add sliced or grated ripe tomato, flaked salt, oregano and a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil. The contrast between the acidity of the labneh and the sweetness of the ripe tomato is irresistible.

### Labneh cream with roasted peppers

A sauce for dipping or to accompany grilled meats and vegetables:

1. Roast two red peppers in the oven at 200°C until the skin blackens, let them sweat covered and peel them.
2. Grind the peppers with 200 grams of labneh, a pinch of cumin, salt, La Vera paprika and a splash of oil.
3. Serve cold, with some toasted almonds on top.

### Cold labneh and lemon cake

A no-bake dessert, lighter than a traditional cheesecake:

1. Crush 200 grams of Maria-type cookies and mix them with 80 grams of melted butter. Cover the base of a mold with it and refrigerate.
2. Beat 400 grams of well-drained labneh with 200 ml of whipping cream, 80 grams of icing sugar, the zest and juice of one lemon.
3. Hydrate two sheets of gelatin in cold water, dissolve them in two tablespoons of hot cream and add them to the cream.
4. Pour over the base and refrigerate for at least six hours.

Serve it with fresh fruit or a homemade red fruit jam.

## Take advantage of whey

The whey obtained by draining the yogurt is acidic and preserves part of the proteins and vitamins of the milk. You can use it:

- Instead of some of the water in a bread dough.
- In smoothies.
- To marinate meats, which it softens slightly.
- In cold soups.

Consume it in two or three days.

## Labneh, cottage cheese and cream cheese: how they are different

All three are white, creamy and spreadable, but they are made very differently. **labneh** is born from an already fermented yogurt from which only the whey is removed: there is no heat or rennet, and its acidity comes from the bacteria in the yogurt. **Cottage** is obtained by heating milk or whey and adding an acid, such as lemon juice or vinegar, so that the proteins flocculate; Its flavor is sweeter and its texture is grainy. We explain it step by step in the guide to [homemade cottage cheese](/recetas-y-guias/requeson-casero/). Commercial **cream cheese** is made with fermented milk and cream, it has much more fat and usually contains stabilizers that give it that smooth texture.

In practice, labneh replaces cream cheese well in sauces, fillings and cold cakes, with a more pronounced acid point and less fat. If a recipe calls for the very firm texture of cream cheese, drain the labneh for longer.

## Organize a kilo of yogurt for the entire week

A single batch of labneh can last for several days if you take advantage of its different textures:

1. **Day 1, at night**: mix the yogurt with the salt and put it to drain in the refrigerator.
2. **Day 2, in the morning**: remove a portion, still creamy, for breakfast with fruit or for a cucumber dip.
3. **Day 2, at night**: set aside another spreadable portion for toast and dips; Store it in a closed container.
4. **Day 3**: Let the rest drain until it is firm and form the balls in oil.
5. **Whey**: Use it on weekend bread or in a smoothie within three days.

Label each container with the date: it's the easiest way to know what to consume first.

## Frequent errors

1. **Use yogurt with thickeners**: it drains poorly.
2. **Drain at room temperature for many hours**: it is best to do this in the refrigerator.
3. **Use a cloth with a detergent smell**: it transmits the smell to the labneh. Rinse it well before using it.
4. **Press the cloth**: you can make yogurt pass through with the whey.

## Variants

- **Goat labneh**: with goat yogurt, with a more intense flavor.
- **Sweet labneh**: without salt, with a little honey or icing sugar and vanilla, as a cream for desserts.
- **Labneh with herbs**: Mix fresh chopped herbs, such as dill, chives or mint, at the last minute and consume within a few days.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use skim yogurt?

Yes, but the labneh will be less creamy and more acidic.

### How long does it last in the refrigerator?

Simple labneh, in a closed container, lasts about a week. The balls in oil, in the refrigerator, about two weeks.

### Is it suitable for lactose intolerant people?

It contains less lactose than milk, because part of it has been transformed during fermentation and another part goes with the whey, but it is not lactose-free.

## In summary

Plain yogurt, salt, a cloth, and time in the fridge: that's all labneh needs. It is the simplest, safest and most versatile homemade cheese, ideal to start in the workshop and to always have something creamy and tasty in the refrigerator with which to brighten up a breakfast or dinner.
