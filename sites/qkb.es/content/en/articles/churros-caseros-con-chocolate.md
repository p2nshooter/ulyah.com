Few things are as Spanish as a breakfast of churros with chocolate on a winter Sunday, or the last stop of a night of partying at a churrería. Churros are, in their most classic version, a very simple dough made of flour, water and salt, which is fried in very hot oil and sprinkled, if desired, with sugar. Its secret is not in the ingredients, but in the technique: the consistency of the dough, the shape of the nozzle and the temperature of the oil.

In this guide we explain how to make churros that are crispy on the outside and tender on the inside, how to do it safely, how to prepare porras and how to get a thick hot chocolate, the kind that sticks on a spoon.

## Churros and porras: how are they different?

- **Churros** are made with a dough of flour and boiling water, without yeast. They are thin, striated, crispy and are usually served in the shape of a bow or in short strips.
- **The porras**, typical of Madrid, are thicker and spongier. Its dough is more liquid and sometimes contains baking soda or a raising agent. They are fried in a large spiral which is then cut into strips.

This guide starts with churros, which are the easiest to make at home, and then explains porras.

## Ingredients for about 20 churros

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| water | 250ml |
| All-purpose wheat flour | 250g |
| salt | 5 g, a level teaspoon |
| Mild olive or sunflower oil for frying | abundant |
| Sugar for sprinkling, optional | to taste |

The classic proportion is the same amount of water as flour: 250 ml of water per 250 g of flour. Some recipes add a tablespoon of oil to the water, which gives a slightly softer dough.

## material

- A manual churrera, with a star-shaped nozzle.
- A deep frying pan or a wide saucepan.
- A kitchen thermometer, highly recommended.
- A slotted spoon and absorbent paper.

## Why the star nozzle is not optional

The fluted shape of churros is not just aesthetic. It has a very important security function.

Churros dough contains water. Upon entering the hot oil, that water turns into steam. If the churro is smooth and cylindrical, steam can get trapped inside and cause the churro to **explode**, splashing boiling oil. The grooves increase the surface area and allow steam to escape gradually.

Therefore, **never make churros with a smooth or round nozzle**, nor with a piping bag without a nozzle. Always use a star nozzle with well-marked tips.

Another risk factor is air pockets within the dough. When filling the churrera, press well to avoid leaving bubbles.

## Step 1: prepare the dough

1. Put the water and salt in a saucepan and bring it to a boil.
2. Remove the saucepan from the heat, add all the flour at once and stir vigorously with a wooden spoon.
3. Continue stirring until the dough comes away from the walls and forms a smooth, homogeneous ball, without lumps.

The dough should be firm, but soft enough to pass through the churrera. If it is too hard, add a tablespoon of hot water; If it is too soft, the churros will lose shape.

Let the dough rest for about 10 minutes, covered, so that it tempers and is easier to handle.

## Step 2: fill the churrera

Fill the churrera with the still warm dough, pressing with the spoon to eliminate the air. Place the star nozzle.

## Step 3: Heat the oil

Heat plenty of oil in the frying pan, at least 3 cm deep, to **180–190 °C**. If you don't have a thermometer, add a small piece of dough: it should rise to the surface immediately surrounded by bubbles.

- With the oil too cold, the churros absorb oil and become greasy and soft.
- With the oil too hot, they burn on the outside and remain raw on the inside.

## Step 4: fry

1. Press the churrera over the oil, at a low height, so that the dough does not splash when it falls.
2. Cut the dough with scissors or a knife to the desired length, about 10 to 12 cm. You can also form loops by joining the two ends together.
3. Fry the churros in batches, without filling the pan, turning them over so that they brown evenly. They will take 2 to 3 minutes.
4. Take them out with the slotted spoon and leave them on absorbent paper.

If you want, coat them in sugar while they are hot.

## Safety precautions when frying

Deep frying is one of the most dangerous tasks in the home kitchen. Some precautions:

- **Do not fill the pan** with oil more than a third of its height.
- **Keep children away** from the kitchen while frying.
- **Dry the utensils well**: water in contact with hot oil causes splashes.
- **Never put out an oil fire with water.** If the oil ignites, turn off the heat and cover the pan with a lid or a well-wrung damp cloth. Always have them on hand.
- **Do not leave the pan unattended.**

## Step 5: serve

Churros are eaten freshly made, hot and crispy. As the minutes go by, they soften, so it is best to fry them just before serving them.

## Chocolate in the cup

Spanish chocolate for dipping churros is thick, intense and creamy. Their secret is a little cornstarch.

### Ingredients for 4 cups

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| whole milk | 500ml |
| Dark chocolate with 50 to 70% cocoa | 125g |
| sugar | 30 to 50 g, to taste |
| Corn starch (cornstarch) | 15 g, one heaped tablespoon |
| salt | a pinch |

### Preparation

1. Dissolve the cornstarch in 100 ml of the cold milk.
2. Heat the rest of the milk with the sugar and a pinch of salt.
3. When it is hot, without boiling, add the chopped chocolate and stir until it melts.
4. Add the milk with the cornstarch, stirring constantly.
5. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, for 3 to 5 minutes, until thickened and coats the back of the spoon.

The chocolate will continue to thicken as it cools. If it is too thick, add a splash of milk.

## Batons

To make porras, the dough must be more liquid and fluffier.

### Ingredients

- 500 g of all-purpose flour.
- 650 ml of water.
- 1 teaspoon of salt.
- 1 teaspoon of baking soda, or an envelope of raising agent used in Spanish baking.

### Preparation

1. Mix the flour with the baking soda.
2. Boil the water with the salt and pour it over the flour, stirring vigorously until you obtain a smooth and somewhat sticky dough.
3. Let it rest for 10 minutes.
4. Fill the churrera with a large star tip.
5. Fry the dough in a spiral, from the center of the pan outwards, in oil at 180°C.
6. Turn it over with two long toothpicks when it is golden.
7. Take it out, let it drain and cut it into strips with scissors.

## Organize a churros breakfast

Churros don't wait, so order matters. Prepare the chocolate first and keep it warm over low heat, stirring occasionally, or in a thermos. Then make the dough, fill the churrera and heat the oil. Fry in small batches and serve each batch as soon as it comes out of the pan, instead of piling them up: ten minutes of waiting is enough for them to lose their crunch. If there are many of you, let one person be cold while the others have breakfast, like in traditional churrerías.

## What to do with the oil after

Let the oil cool completely in the pan, away from the edge of the counter. Filter it with a fine strainer or kitchen paper and store it in an opaque, closed bottle in a cool place. It can be reused some more for sweet frying, but throw it away when it is dark, thick, smells rancid, foams, or starts to smoke at a lower temperature than usual. Never flush it down the sink or toilet: it clogs the pipes and contaminates the water. Keep it in a bottle and take it to a clean point or to the used oil collection containers that many municipalities have.

## Common problems

**The churros are soft.** The oil was not hot enough or they were served late.

**The churros open or become deformed.** The dough was too soft. Add a little flour next time.

**They are raw inside.** The oil was too hot or the churros were too thick.

**The churrera jams.** The dough was too hard or cold. Prepare it again a little softer and use it warm.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I make churros without a churrera?

You can use a heavy-duty fabric piping bag with a large metal star nozzle. Thin plastic sleeves can break due to the hardness of the dough.

### Can I make churros in the oven?

There are baked versions, but the texture is very different, more similar to profiterole dough. Authentic churros are fried.

### Can they be frozen?

Yes. You can freeze the raw churros, already formed, on a tray and then store them in a bag. Fry them directly from frozen, with the oil slightly less hot, at about 170 °C.

## In summary

Water and flour in equal parts, firm and airless dough, always a star nozzle, oil at 180–190 °C and fry in batches with all precautions. Accompany it with a thick chocolate and you will have the most classic Sunday breakfast in Spain at home.
