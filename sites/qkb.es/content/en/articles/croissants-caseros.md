The croissant is one of the most demanding pieces of pastry and, at the same time, one of the most rewarding to make at home. Its secret is in the **laminate**: a dough fermented with yeast and a sheet of butter that is folded and stretched several times to form dozens of very thin layers. In the oven, the water in the dough and butter turns into steam, separating the layers and creating that characteristic honeycomb structure.

You don't need professional equipment, but you do need patience, a refrigerator and a golden rule: **the dough and butter must always be cold and with a similar consistency**. If the butter melts, the layers disappear; If it is too hard, it breaks into pieces.

## What happens inside a croissant in the oven

Understanding the process helps avoid mistakes. When the croissant goes into the hot oven, several things happen almost at once:

- The water contained in the dough and butter, which is about 16%, turns into steam.
- The steam pushes the layers of dough and separates them, as if inflating hundreds of very thin sheets.
- The fat in the butter prevents those layers from sticking together again.
- The yeast, which has worked during the final fermentation, provides a second oomph and makes each layer tender and alveolate, not dry like puff pastry.
- The proteins and starches are set by heat, and the croissant maintains its shape.

That is why the croissant is a mixture of two worlds: puff pastry, which rises only due to steam, and bread, which rises due to fermentation.

## How to choose butter

Butter is the ingredient that most influences the result. Look for these features:

- **At least 82% fat.** Butters with more water become soft and sticky when laminating.
- **A cold plastic texture**, which bends without breaking. Some butters become brittle in the refrigerator; If this is your case, work on it a little more when making the block.
- **Unsalted**, to control the salt in the recipe.

European-type butters, sometimes called "obrador" or "puff pastry" butters, are ideal, although a good supermarket butter with the right fat percentage works very well.

## Ingredients for about 12 croissants

### Base dough or détrempe

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| strength flour | 500g |
| sugar | 55g |
| salt | 10g |
| fresh yeast | 20 g (or 7 g dry) |
| cold milk | 140g |
| cold water | 140g |
| soft butter | 40g |

### To laminate

- 280 g good quality butter, with at least 82% fat, cold.

### To paint

- 1 egg beaten with a tablespoon of milk.

## Organization in two days

Making croissants in a single day is possible, but at home it is easier to spread out the work:

- **Day 1, afternoon:** prepare the base dough and butter block. Both spend the night in the refrigerator.
- **Day 2, in the morning:** laminate, form, ferment and bake.

## Step 1: the base dough

Mix the flour, sugar and yeast. Add the cold milk and water and mix. Add the salt and softened butter and knead for about 5 minutes, just until the dough is smooth. **Do not knead it excessively**: a dough with too much gluten shrinks when rolling and is difficult to stretch.

Form a flat rectangle about 2 cm thick, wrap it in film and leave it at room temperature for an hour. Then, keep it in the refrigerator overnight.

## Step 2: the butter block

Place the cold butter between two sheets of baking paper. Hit it with the rolling pin to soften it a little and then roll it out into a square of about 17 × 17 cm and a uniform thickness. Fold the paper to size so that the edges are straight. Store it in the refrigerator.

The butter should be flexible but cold: if you bend it, it should curl without breaking.

## Step 3: enclose the butter

The next day, take the butter out of the refrigerator 10 or 15 minutes beforehand, so that it is flexible. Roll out the dough into a square of about 25 × 25 cm. Place the block of butter rotated 45 degrees in the center, like a rhombus, and fold the four corners of the dough toward the center, like an envelope. Seal the joints well by pinching them.

## Step 4: the double turn

Stretch the package into a long rectangle, about 20 × 60 cm, always in the same direction and with gentle rolling strokes before stretching. Work fast.

For the double turn, also called "book turn":

1. Fold one end of the rectangle up to a quarter of the length.
2. Fold the other end until it meets the first, in the center.
3. Fold everything in half, like a book that closes.

You now have four layers of buttered dough. Wrap the dough in film and keep it in the refrigerator for 30 to 45 minutes.

## Step 5: the simple round

Turn the dough 90 degrees from the previous position, so that the "spine" of the book is on one side. Stretch again into a rectangle of about 20 × 60 cm. Fold it in three, like a letter: one third over the center and the other third on top. Wrap and refrigerate another 45 minutes to an hour.

With a double and a single turn, you get 12 layers of butter, an ideal structure for homemade croissants: well-defined layers and a honeycomb crumb.

## Step 6: Stretch and Cut

Roll out the dough into a rectangle of about 25 × 60 cm and 4 to 5 mm thick. If the dough shrinks a lot, let it rest for 10 minutes in the refrigerator and continue. Trim the edges with a sharp knife so the layers show.

Cut isosceles triangles with a base of about 9 cm and a height of 25 cm.

## Step 7: Form

1. Make a small 1cm cut in the center of the base of each triangle.
2. Gently stretch the triangle to make it slightly longer.
3. Roll from the base towards the tip, loosely, slightly separating the two corners of the cut.
4. Place each croissant on the tray with the tip underneath, so it doesn't unroll.

Leave plenty of space between them, because they will grow a lot.

## Step 8: final fermentation

This is the step where most homemade croissants go bad. Fermentation must be carried out at a temperature **not exceeding 27 °C**. On top, the butter begins to melt and escape from the layers.

Place the tray in the turned off oven with a bowl of hot water in the base to create a warm, humid environment. Let it rise for 2 to 3 hours, until the croissants have almost doubled in volume, the layers are visible and they shake slightly when you move the tray.

## Step 9: Bake

1. Take out the croissants and preheat the oven to 200°C.
2. Paint them with the beaten egg with a soft brush, without staining the cuts of the layers.
3. Bake at 190°C for 16 to 20 minutes, until very golden.

A pale croissant will be soft inside. Don't be afraid of color: a well-baked croissant is a deep golden brown.

## How to read the result

| what you see | probable cause |
|---|---|
| Puddle of butter on tray | Fermentation too hot or not enough |
| Caked layers, crumb like bread | Butter too soft when laminating |
| Butter in visible pieces | Butter too cold or hard when laminating |
| Small and dense croissant | Short final fermentation |
| Dough that shrinks when stretched | Excess kneading or lack of rest |

## Take advantage of cuts

When cutting the triangles there are always pieces of laminated dough left over, and it would be a shame to throw them away. You can twist the strips on themselves, roll them in sugar and cinnamon, and bake them as crispy bows, or cut them into pieces, press them gently into the wells of a muffin tin, and bake them as little, irregularly layered buns. They ferment and bake just like croissants, only in less time. Do not knead and stretch them again like a new sheet: you would lose the layers and get a dense bun.

## Conservation

Croissants are at their best the day they are baked. They can be reheated for 5 minutes at 160°C the next day.

The best way to have fresh croissants without getting up early is to freeze them **without fermenting**, right after forming them. When you take them out of the freezer, leave them in the refrigerator overnight and in the morning proof and bake as usual.

## Variants

- **Chocolate Neapolitans:** Cut 8 × 15 cm rectangles, place two chocolate bars and roll them up.
- **Almond croissants:** fill day-old croissants with almond cream, cover them with flaked almonds and bake them again.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use margarine?

You can, but the flavor and aroma will be very different. Professional puff pastry margarine is easy to sheet, but butter croissant is something else.

### What do I do if the butter breaks when stretching?

It's too cold. Leave the dough for 5 to 10 minutes at room temperature before continuing.

## In summary

Lightly kneaded dough, quality butter, cold and constant temperature during rolling, a double and a single turn, and a patient final fermentation below 27 °C. With these rules, homemade croissants can compete with those from a good pastry shop.
