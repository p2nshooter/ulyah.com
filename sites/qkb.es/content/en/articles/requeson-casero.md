Cottage cheese is one of the oldest and simplest cheeses in Spanish tradition. White, soft and grainy, it appears in breakfasts, in desserts such as the famous cottage cheese with honey and in fillings for cakes and dumplings. In many rural areas it was made with the whey left over from making cheese, a perfect example of wasteful cuisine.

At home it can be made in two ways: from whole milk, adding an acid, or using the whey from a batch of fresh cheese. The first is simpler and yields more; The second is traditional and allows nothing to be wasted. In this guide we explain both.

## What exactly is cottage cheese?

Technically, there are two products that are often called the same:

- **Whey cottage cheese**, the traditional one, is obtained by heating the whey that remains after making cheese. In this whey there are proteins that the rennet does not trap, the **whey proteins** (such as albumin and globulin), which coagulate with heat and a little acidity. It is a relative of the Italian ricotta, whose name means "overcooked."
- **Milk cottage cheese**, most common at home, is obtained by heating whole milk and adding an acid. Here, especially the caseins coagulate, due to the acidity and heat. The result is a white and tender curd, very similar in use.

Both are delicious. The serum one is lighter and more delicate; the milk one, creamier and with greater yield.

## Method 1: cottage cheese with whole milk

### Ingredients

- **1 liter of pasteurized whole milk.**
- **100 ml of liquid cream** (optional), for a creamier cottage cheese.
- **3 tablespoons of strained lemon juice or white vinegar**, or 250 ml of buttermilk.
- **A pinch of salt.**

### Utensils

- A thick-bottomed pot.
- A thermometer.
- A strainer lined with cheesecloth or a thin clean cotton cloth.
- A skimmer.

### step by step

1. **Heat the milk** with the cream and salt over medium heat, stirring frequently so it doesn't stick, until it reaches **between 85 and 90 °C**. You will see small bubbles at the edge, but it should not come to a boil.
2. **Remove from the heat and add the acid** little by little, stirring very gently.
3. **Wait a few minutes.** As soon as the acid acts, the milk will separate into white lumps and a yellowish, almost transparent whey. If the whey is still white and milky, add a little more acid.
4. **Let it sit for 10 to 15 minutes** without stirring, so that the lumps settle and come together.
5. **Pass the lumps to the strainer** with the slotted spoon, without pressing.
6. **Drain** for between 15 minutes and an hour, depending on the texture you are looking for: longer, drier.

### Performance

With one liter of whole milk you obtain approximately between 150 and 200 grams of cottage cheese, more if you add cream.

## Method 2: cottage cheese with fresh cheese whey

This is the traditional method. It is used to take advantage of the whey left over after making [homemade fresh cheese](/recetas-y-guias/queso-fresco-casero/).

### Ingredients

- **The newly obtained whey** from making cheese: ideally, that of 3 or 4 liters of milk, because the yield is low.
- **A splash of whole milk** (optional), between 100 and 200 ml per liter of whey, to increase performance.
- **One or two tablespoons of vinegar or lemon juice** for every two liters of whey.

### step by step

1. **Use the fresh whey**, within hours of making the cheese.
2. **Heat slowly** up to about **90°C**, stirring occasionally. If you add milk, do so before heating.
3. **Add the acid** when the temperature is reached. Very fine white flakes will appear and rise to the surface.
4. **Remove from heat and let rest** 10 minutes.
5. **Collect the flakes with a fine slotted spoon** and transfer them to a strainer with very fine cheesecloth, because they are delicate.
6. **Drain** until desired texture.

### Performance

The yield is low: a modest amount of cottage cheese is obtained from several liters of whey, often less than 100 grams. That is why it is a form of use, not production.

## Which acid to choose

- **Lemon juice:** the most common at home. Leaves a soft citrus aroma, pleasant in desserts. Strain it so that no pulp or seeds remain.
- **White vinegar:** more neutral in flavor once the cottage cheese is drained, and more regular in its acidity than lemon, which varies from one piece to another.
- **Buttermilk or kefir:** they give a more tender cottage cheese with a slight fermented milk flavor, but larger quantities are needed.
- **Citric acid:** half a teaspoon dissolved in a little water replaces lemon juice with a more constant acidity.

Whatever the acid, add it little by little and stop as soon as the whey becomes clear: the excess hardens the curd and leaves flavor behind.

## Cottage cheese with spinach for fillings

For cannelloni, dumplings or a vegetable lasagna, mix 250 grams of well-drained cottage cheese with 300 grams of spinach sautéed with garlic and very drained, finely chopped. Add an egg, a handful of grated aged cheese, salt, pepper and a pinch of nutmeg. The mixture should be firm: if it feels wet, add a little breadcrumbs. It is a light and tasty filling that takes great advantage of the texture of homemade cottage cheese.

## Can it be frozen?

Yes, but when it thaws it releases water and becomes more grainy. Freeze it well drained, in small portions and in closed containers, and use it later in cakes, fillings and sauces, not to eat naturally with honey. Thaw it in the refrigerator and consume it within a day.

## Conservation

Cottage cheese is a very perishable product. Store it in the refrigerator, in a closed container, and consume it in **two or three days**. If you notice a strong sour smell, mold, or a slimy appearance, throw it away.

## Frequent errors

| Problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| No lumps are formed | Low temperature or low acid | Heat to 85–90°C and add some more acid |
| Gummy cottage cheese | Milk boiled too long or too much acid | Control the temperature and add the acid little by little |
| Lemon or vinegar flavor | Excess acid | Use just the right amount or rinse the curd lightly. |
| very dry | Drained too long or pressed | Drains less and without pressing |
| Sticks to the pot | high fire | Use medium heat and a heavy-bottomed pot. |

## Uses in the kitchen

Cottage cheese is enormously versatile:

- **Cottage cheese with honey**: a traditional dessert in many regions of Spain. It is served with flower honey and sometimes nuts.
- **Breakfast**: on toasted bread, with tomato or fruit.
- **Cheesecakes**: Many traditional homemade recipes use cottage cheese.
- **Filled**: dumplings, crepes, cannelloni or pasta.
- **Sauces and creams**: shaken with herbs, garlic and olive oil, as a spread.
- **Pastry**: in cakes and fritters, it provides juiciness.

## Two simple recipes with cottage cheese

### Cottage cheese with honey and walnuts

It is the simplest dessert and one of the most traditional. For four people:

1. Divide about 300 grams of homemade cottage cheese, well drained and cold, into four bowls.
2. Drizzle each serving with a generous tablespoon of honey. Flower or rosemary honeys combine especially well.
3. Add a handful of chopped walnuts and, if you like, a pinch of cinnamon.

Serve immediately, so that the contrast between the cold cottage cheese and honey is more intense.

### Baked cottage cheese pie

A homemade cake with a soft texture, without a cookie base:

- 500 g of cottage cheese, well drained.
- 3 eggs.
- 120 g of sugar.
- 40 g of wheat flour or cornstarch.
- The zest of a lemon.
- 100 ml of cream or milk.

1. Preheat the oven to 180°C and line a 20 centimeter mold with baking paper.
2. Beat the cottage cheese with the sugar until you obtain a homogeneous cream.
3. Add the eggs one by one, then the sifted flour, the zest and the cream.
4. Pour the mixture into the mold and bake for 40 to 50 minutes, until the center is set but jiggly slightly.
5. Let it cool in the off oven with the door ajar, and then in the refrigerator for at least four hours.

It can be served with red fruit jam or honey.

## Cottage cheese and nutrition

Milk cottage cheese provides protein and calcium, and usually has a moderate fat content if made without cream. Whey cottage cheese is lighter. People with lactose intolerance should be aware that cottage cheese contains lactose.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use semi-skimmed milk?

Yes, although the cottage cheese will be less creamy and the yield will be lower.

### And UHT milk?

Unlike rennet cheese, heat and acid curd can be made with UHT milk, although the texture is usually somewhat grainier. Fresh pasteurized milk gives better results.

### What do I do with the leftover whey?

You can use it to make bread, instead of some of the water; to cook pasta or rice; or in smoothies. It was also traditionally used as animal feed.

### Is it the same as ricotta?

Traditional Italian ricotta is made with whey, like whey cottage cheese. Commercially, many ricottas and cottage cheeses are made with milk or mixtures. They are very similar products.

## In summary

For a rich, creamy cottage cheese, heat whole milk to 85–90°C, add an acid, wait for the curds to separate, and drain. For the traditional version, take advantage of the whey from freshly made fresh cheese, heat it to 90°C and collect the flakes. In both cases, in less than an hour you will have a perfect homemade cottage cheese for breakfast with honey.
