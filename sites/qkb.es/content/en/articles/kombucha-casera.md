Kombucha is a sweetened tea drink fermented by a colony of bacteria and yeast. The result is an acidic liquid, slightly sweet and, if bottled well, with fine and persistent bubbles. In recent years it has become very popular in shops and cafes, but its homemade preparation is old and simple, as long as a few rules of hygiene and acidity are respected.

In this guide we explain what a SCOBY is, how to prepare the first fermentation, when it is ready, how to add flavor and gas in the second fermentation and, above all, how to distinguish a healthy kombucha from a contaminated one.

## What is kombucha and what is SCOBY

The word SCOBY comes from English and means "symbiotic culture of bacteria and yeast." It is that gelatinous, cream or beige disk that floats on the surface of the kombucha. However, the SCOBY is not just the disk: most of the active microorganisms are in the liquid itself. Therefore, to start a kombucha, as important as the disc is the **starter liquid**, that is, already fermented and acidic kombucha.

Two groups participate in fermentation. Yeast transforms sugar into alcohol and carbon dioxide. Acetic bacteria, in turn, transform a good part of that alcohol into acetic acid and other organic acids, which give the characteristic flavor. That's why kombucha tastes like a mixture of tea, sour apple and a touch of vinegar.

## Ingredients for two liters

- 2 liters of chlorine-free water.
- 8 g of black or green tea, loose or in bags (about 4 or 5 bags).
- 160 g of white sugar.
- 250 ml of already fermented, unpasteurized kombucha, as a starter liquid.
- A healthy SCOBY.

## Necessary material

- A wide-mouth glass jar of at least three liters.
- A thick cotton cloth or kitchen paper and an elastic band.
- Glass bottles with airtight closure, designed for carbonated drinks, if you are going to do second fermentation.
- A plastic or stainless steel funnel and strainer.

Avoid reactive metal containers and decorative ceramics with glazes of dubious origin: the acidic medium of kombucha can dissolve metals, and some antique glazes contain lead. Glass is the safest and easiest option to clean.

## The importance of initial acidity

This is the most important point of the guide. A freshly brewed sweetened tea is an ideal medium for many microorganisms, not just those in kombucha. What protects fermentation from the first minute is the acidity provided by the starter liquid. That's why **you should never start a kombucha with just the puck and sweet tea**, without any acidic liquid.

As a general rule, the starter liquid should make up at least 10% of the total volume, and should be well-fermented, acidic kombucha. If you have pH strips, the tea with the starter should be below 4.5. If it is above, add more starter liquid.

## First fermentation step by step

1. Boil half a liter of water, remove it from the heat and infuse the tea for 10 to 15 minutes.
2. Strain the tea and dissolve the sugar in it, stirring well.
3. Add the rest of the cold water. This way the mixture cools faster.
4. Wait until the tea is at room temperature, below 30°C. The heat would damage the crop.
5. Pour the tea into the jar, add the starter liquid and place the SCOBY on top.
6. Cover the mouth with the cloth and secure it with the rubber band. Kombucha needs oxygen, but not flies.
7. Leave the jar in a stable place, between 20 and 27°C, away from direct sunlight and away from other fermentations, such as sourdough or kefir, to avoid cross-contamination.

## How long to ferment

The first fermentation normally lasts between 7 and 14 days. The time depends on the temperature, the amount of starter and your taste.

Starting on the seventh day, taste the kombucha every one or two days. Insert a clean straw over the edge of the jar, without going through the disc, cover the top end with your finger and scoop out a little liquid. At first it will be sweet like a sweetened tea; little by little it will become acidic. It is ready when you find a pleasant balance between sweetness and acidity.

During this time, it is normal for a new disc to form on the surface, thin at first and increasingly thicker. It is also normal to see brown strands hanging from the disc or on the bottom: they are yeast.

## Signs that something is wrong

Knowing the difference between a healthy kombucha and a contaminated one is essential.

| what you see | What does it mean | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| New white, cream or beige disc, smooth or somewhat irregular | normal growth | Follow |
| Brown strands, brown spots under the disc | Yeasts, normal | Follow |
| Bubbles trapped under the disc | Fermentation gas, normal | Follow |
| Dry, hairy, circular, green, blue, black, or white cottony spots on the surface | Mold | Discard everything, disk included |
| Rotten or rancid cheese smell | Pollution | Discard |
| Liquid that is still very sweet after two weeks | Weak or cold crop | Check temperature and starter |

Mold always appears on the surface, it is dry and has a velvety appearance, just like that which appears on bread. If you doubt, don't consume it. There is no negotiation with mold: removing the stain is not enough.

## Second fermentation: flavor and bubbles

When the first fermentation is to your liking, remove the SCOBY with clean hands and reserve it in a container with a little of the kombucha, which will be the starter for the next batch.

Bottle the rest of the liquid and add flavor:

- 50 to 100 ml of fruit juice per liter.
- Or a handful of chopped fruit.
- Or fresh sliced ​​ginger, herbs or spices.

Close the bottles and leave them at room temperature for two to four days. The sugar in the fruit feeds the yeasts, which produce carbon dioxide, and since the bottle is closed, the gas dissolves in the drink.

### Be careful with the pressure

The second fermentation generates pressure. Only use bottles designed for carbonated drinks, such as those with mechanical seals with rubber, and never decorative or thin glass bottles. Open one bottle a day to check the gas and, when it is to your liking, put them all in the refrigerator. The cold stops fermentation. Always open bottles over the sink, pointing away from your face.

A safer alternative to start is to use a plastic bottle as a "control": when you press it and it is hard, the glass ones also have enough gas.

## Combinations that work

- Ginger and lemon.
- Raspberry and mint leaves.
- Apple and cinnamon.
- Mango and a pinch of turmeric.
- black grape
- Pear and vanilla.

## Alcohol and sugar

As with any yeast fermentation, kombucha contains some alcohol. In regular home brewing it is usually below one percent, but long second fermentations, with a lot of fruit and at high temperatures can increase it. Keep this in mind if you are going to offer it to children, if you are pregnant or if you avoid alcohol for any reason.

As for sugar, bacteria and yeast consume a significant portion, but the finished kombucha still contains some. The longer the fermentation, the less sugar and more acidity.

## Who should avoid it

Homemade kombucha isn't right for everyone. You should avoid it or consult your doctor:

- Immunocompromised people, undergoing chemotherapy treatments or having transplants.
- Pregnant and nursing mothers.
- Little children.
- People with kidney or liver problems.

Due to its acidity, it should be taken in moderation, a small glass a day, and rinse your mouth with water afterwards to protect your tooth enamel. There is no solid evidence that kombucha cures diseases. It is a pleasant drink and an alternative to soft drinks, not a medicine.

## SCOBY Hotel and Breaks

If you won't be making kombucha for a while, store your discs in a jar of highly acidic kombucha, covered with a cloth, at room temperature. This is what is called a "SCOBY hotel". Every four to six weeks, remove some of the liquid and add new sweet tea. Do not store the SCOBY in the refrigerator for a long time: the cold weakens the culture and makes it more vulnerable to mold when you use it again.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use less sugar?

Sugar is the food of the crop, not a whim. If you reduce the quantity too much, the fermentation will be weak and the acidity will not develop well. It is preferable to ferment for more days so that it is less sweet.

### Can I use honey or sweeteners?

Zero-calorie sweeteners are useless, because the crop cannot feed on them. Raw honey contains its own microorganisms and gives irregular results. To start, use white sugar.

### Can I use infusions instead of tea?

The crop needs the nutrients from the tea, so it is best to use real tea. You can add a small part of the infusion, but not replace the entire tea. Avoid teas flavored with essential oils, which can damage the crop.

### What do I do with the leftover discs?

You can give them away with some starter fluid, compost them, or store them in your SCOBY hotel.

## In summary

Tea, sugar, a healthy SCOBY and, above all, enough acidic starter liquid. Ferment for 7 to 14 days at warm temperatures, keep an eye on the surface for mold and bottle with fruit if you want bubbles. With hygiene, proper bottles, and moderation, homemade kombucha is an easy and rewarding project.
