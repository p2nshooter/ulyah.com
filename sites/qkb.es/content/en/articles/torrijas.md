Torrijas are the sweet of Spanish Holy Week. They were born as a way to take advantage of stale bread, in times when nothing was thrown away, and they have become a small seasonal luxury: slices of bread soaked in milk flavored with cinnamon and lemon, dipped in egg, fried in olive oil and covered in sugar and cinnamon or dipped in syrup or honey.

Its ideal texture is that of a cream inside, almost like a flan, and a golden and slightly crunchy surface. Achieving it depends on three things: the right bread, the right soaking time and well-controlled frying.

## a little history

The first written references to torrijas in Spain date back to the 15th century, and recipes for soaked and fried bread exist in many European cuisines, such as French *pain perdu* or Portuguese *rabanadas*. Their link with Lent and Holy Week is explained because they were a nutritious and economical food in a time of abstinence from meat, and because they allowed us to take advantage of leftover bread.

## Bread: the most important decision

Not just any bread will do. The ideal is a bread with a dense and tight crumb, from the day before, that absorbs the milk without falling apart.

- **Torrijas bread:** many Spanish bakeries sell it in spring. It is a compact crumb bar, designed precisely for this.
- **Day-old loaf of bread:** works, but the crumb should be somewhat dense. Very aerated bars fall apart.
- **Thick sliced ​​bread or brioche:** makes more tender and sweet French toast, although less traditional.

Cut the bread into 2 to 3 cm thick slices, slightly on the diagonal. If the bread is fresh, leave the slices in the air for a few hours to dry a little.

## Ingredients for about 10 torrijas

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Torrijas bread or loaf from the previous day | 1 bar, about 300 g |
| whole milk | 750ml |
| sugar | 80g |
| cinnamon stick | 1 |
| Lemon peel, without the white part | 1 large strip |
| Orange peel, optional | 1 strip |
| Eggs | 3 |
| Mild olive oil for frying | abundant |

### to cover

- 100 g of sugar mixed with 1 tablespoon of ground cinnamon.
- Or a syrup, or diluted honey, as we explain below.

## Step 1: infuse the milk

Put the milk in a saucepan with the sugar, the cinnamon stick and the lemon and orange peels. Heat over medium heat until it starts to boil, remove from heat, cover and let infuse for at least 30 minutes. The longer the time, the more aroma. Strain the milk and let it cool.

The milk should be warm or cold when soaking the bread. If it is hot, the bread falls apart easily.

## Step 2: Soak the bread

Place the slices in a deep dish in a single layer. Pour the milk over them and let them soak for 5 to 10 minutes on each side, depending on how dense the bread is. They should be well soaked to the center, but without falling apart.

To check, gently press the center of a slice. If you feel the crumb soft and moist throughout its thickness, they are ready.

If you have time, you can soak the bread and leave it in the refrigerator for a few hours. The torrijas will be creamier.

## Step 3: drain

Remove the slices with a slotted spoon or wide spatula and place them on a wire rack for a few minutes to drain off any excess milk. This way they will splash less when frying and absorb less oil.

## Step 4: pass through egg

Beat the eggs in a deep plate. Dip each slice in the egg, on both sides, just before frying. The egg forms a layer that seals the torrija and prevents it from absorbing too much oil.

## Step 5: fry

Heat plenty of mild olive oil in a deep frying pan, at least 2 cm deep, to about **170–180 °C**. If you don't have a thermometer, throw in a piece of bread: it should bubble immediately and brown in about 30 seconds.

Fry the torrijas two at a time or three at a time, without filling the pan, so that the oil does not cool down. Flip them when they are golden brown on the bottom, after a minute or two, and brown the other side.

- If the oil is too cold, the torrijas absorb a lot of oil and become greasy.
- If it is too hot, they will brown on the outside and be cold on the inside.

Take them out with a slotted spoon and leave them on absorbent paper.

### Safety when frying

Slices soaked in milk contain a lot of liquid and may splatter. Drain them well, place them in the oil carefully, keeping them away from you, and never leave the pan unattended. Keep a lid handy in case the oil gets too hot.

## Step 6: Cover

### With sugar and cinnamon

It is the most classic way. Coat the still hot torrijas in the sugar and cinnamon mixture on both sides.

### With syrup

Put 200 ml of water, 150 g of sugar, a strip of lemon peel and a cinnamon stick in a saucepan. Boil for 5 minutes and let cool. Bathe the fried torrijas with the syrup.

### with honey

Dilute 4 tablespoons of honey in 150 ml of hot water. Bathe the torrijas with this mixture. It is the preferred version in many areas of Andalusia.

### with wine

In some regions bread is soaked in sweet wine instead of milk, or in a mixture of wine and syrup. They are wine torrijas, with a more intense flavor, suitable only for adults.

## Baked French toast

If you prefer to avoid frying, you can make them in the oven:

1. Preheat the oven to 200°C.
2. Soak the bread in milk and dip it in egg.
3. Place them on a tray with baking paper lightly greased with butter or oil.
4. Bake for 10 minutes, turn them over and bake another 8 to 10 minutes, until golden brown.
5. Coat them in sugar and cinnamon.

The result is less crunchy and somewhat drier on the outside, but equally tasty and lighter.

## Caramelized French toast

A modern version very popular in restaurants: soak the brioche bread in a mixture of milk, cream and yolk, leave it in the refrigerator for several hours, brown it in a pan with butter and caramelize the surface by sprinkling sugar and burning it with a blowtorch or in the same pan. It is served warm, sometimes with ice cream.

## Make torrija bread at home

If your bakery doesn't sell it, you can bake it yourself the day before. The key is a poorly hydrated dough, which gives a tight and resistant crumb:

1. Mix 500 g of bread-making wheat flour, 270 g of water, 10 g of salt and 5 g of dry baker's yeast, or 15 g of fresh yeast.
2. Knead until you obtain a smooth and firm dough, and let it ferment, covered, until it almost doubles in volume.
3. Divide into two pieces, form into two thick, tight bars and let them ferment again for about 45 minutes.
4. Bake at 220°C for about 25 to 30 minutes, until golden brown.
5. Let cool completely and store the bars wrapped in a cloth until the next day.

If you want to know more about yeast, consult the guide to [yeasts: types and equivalences](/recetas-y-guias/levaduras-tipos-y-equivalencias/).

## Egg-free and gluten-free versions

- **Without egg:** instead of dipping the slices in egg, add a tablespoon of cornstarch to the infused milk before heating it and drain the bread very well. They are a little more fragile, so handle them with a wide spatula.
- **Gluten-free:** use a gluten-free bread with a dense crumb and soak it for a short time, because it falls apart faster than wheat bread. Fry in clean oil that has not been used for gluten products.

## Nothing is thrown away

If there is leftover infused milk, use it in rice pudding or custard: it already has the aroma of cinnamon and lemon. The leftover beaten egg can go into an omelet or cake batter. And the frying oil, once cold, is filtered and stored in a closed container for another sweet frying.

## Conservation

The torrijas are tastier the same day, warm or at room temperature. They keep for one or two days in the refrigerator, covered, and those with syrup or honey even improve after a few hours of rest. Take them out of the refrigerator a while before serving them.

## Common problems

**They break when removed from the milk.** The bread was too airy or soaked too long. Use a denser bread or reduce the soaking time.

**They are greasy.** The oil was cold or the pan was overfilled. Fry in batches at 170–180°C.

**They are dry inside.** Lack of soaking. Soak longer or use more milk.

**The egg burns.** The oil was too hot. Lower the heat.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use plant milk?

Yes. Oatmeal and almond drinks work well. The torrijas will be somewhat less creamy.

### Which oil is better?

Mild olive oil is traditional and holds the frying temperature well. Some people prefer sunflower for its more neutral flavor.

### Can I prepare them the day before?

Yes, especially those with syrup or honey. The sugar and cinnamon ones lose some texture, but they are still very delicious.

## In summary

Dense bread from the previous day, milk infused with cinnamon and lemon, a patient soak, an egg bath and frying at the right temperature. Then, sugar and cinnamon, syrup or honey. Torrijas are proof that creative cuisine can become a small luxury.
