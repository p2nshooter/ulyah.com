The yogurt cake is probably the first pastry recipe that many people in Spain learned. Its great advantage is that it does not need a scale: the yogurt glass itself serves as a measure for the rest of the ingredients. It is fluffy, aromatic, prepared in ten minutes and is the perfect base for breakfasts, snacks and simple birthday cakes.

This recipe respects the traditional formula, but explains the small details that separate a correct cake from an extraordinary one: the order of the ingredients, the mixing point, the oven temperature and how to know when it is done.

## Ingredients with glass measurement

Use the clean, empty yogurt glass to measure everything else:

| Ingredient | Quantity | Approximate equivalence |
|---|---|---|
| Natural or lemon yogurt | 1 | 125g |
| medium eggs | 3 | — |
| sugar | 2 glasses | about 200g |
| Sunflower or mild olive oil | 1 glass | about 110 ml |
| All-purpose wheat flour | 3 glasses | about 210g |
| Chemical yeast (baking powder) | 1 sachet | 16g |
| lemon zest | 1 lemon | — |
| salt | a pinch | — |

The equivalents in grams are indicative, because a glass of flour weighs less than a glass of sugar. If you want consistent results, weigh the ingredients the first time and write down the grams.

### About yeast

In Spain, chemical yeast for sponge cakes is sold in 16 g sachets, which correspond to half a kilo of flour according to the manufacturer. For a cake of this size, a whole envelope makes a tall cake, although many people prefer to use two-thirds of the envelope to avoid a slight chemical taste. Taste and adjust to your liking.

## Scale the recipe with the scale

The measurement of the glass is convenient, but not exact: a glass of flour can weigh considerably more or less depending on how much it is filled. If you want the cake to always come out the same, weigh it once using the table's equivalences and write down your figures. With the grams in hand it is also easy to adapt the recipe to another mold:

| Mold | Factor | flour | sugar | Oil | Eggs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small, about 18 cm | × 0,5 | 105g | 100g | 55ml | 1 large or 2 small |
| Normal, 22 to 24 cm | × 1 | 210g | 200g | 110ml | 3 medium |
| Large, 28 cm | × 1,5 | 315g | 300g | 165ml | 4 or 5 medium |

Also multiply the yogurt, yeast and zest by the same factor, and adjust the baking time: small molds need a few minutes less and large ones a little more. Always check with a toothpick.

## What each ingredient does

Understanding the function of each ingredient will allow you to adapt the recipe judiciously:

- **Eggs:** provide structure, because their proteins curdle with heat, and trap air when beaten. They also add color and flavor.
- **Sugar:** sweetens, but also retains moisture, softens the crumb and helps the surface brown. That's why reducing it too much changes the texture.
- **Yogurt:** provides moisture, a slight acidity that makes the crumb more tender, and a mild flavor. Its acidity also reacts with the chemical yeast.
- **Oil:** coats the flour particles, limits gluten formation and keeps the cake tender for days. Unlike butter, it remains liquid at room temperature, so the cake does not harden as it cools.
- **Flour:** provides the starch and proteins that, together with the egg, form the structure of the cake.
- **Chemical yeast:** it releases carbon dioxide in contact with humidity and, above all, with the heat of the oven, causing the dough to rise.

## material

- A 22 to 24 cm round mold, or a 25 cm rectangular mold.
- Baking paper or butter and flour to grease.
- Some rods, manual or electric.
- A large bowl.

## Step 1: prepare the oven and mold

Preheat the oven to **180°C** with top and bottom heat, without fan. Grease the mold with butter and sprinkle flour, or line it with baking paper.

Preheating well is essential: an oven that has not reached the temperature will cause the cake to rise poorly.

## Step 2: beat the eggs with the sugar

In the bowl, beat the eggs with the sugar and lemon zest for 4 to 5 minutes with electric beaters, until the mixture whitens and increases in volume. If you do it by hand, beat vigorously for at least 5 minutes.

This step introduces air into the dough and contributes to fluffiness.

## Step 3: add the yogurt and oil

Add the yogurt and beat until integrated. Then, add the oil in a thin stream while continuing to beat on low speed.

## Step 4: add the flour

Sift the flour with the baking powder and a pinch of salt, and add it in two or three times. Mix with a spatula, with enveloping movements, just until there are no lumps. Do not overbeat: you will develop the gluten and the cake will be denser and with tunnels.

## Step 5: Bake

Pour the dough into the mold and gently tap it against the table to eliminate any large bubbles. Bake on the middle height of the oven for **35 to 40 minutes**.

**Do not open the oven for the first 25 minutes.** The draft of cold air can cause the cake to sink.

Check to see if it's done by poking the center with a toothpick or skewer: it should come out clean or with a few dry crumbs. If it comes out with wet dough, leave it for 5 more minutes and check again.

If the top browns too much before the inside is done, cover it with aluminum foil.

## Step 6: Cool

Let the cake rest for 10 minutes in the mold and then turn it out onto a rack. Let it cool completely before cutting or decorating.

## Why does a cake sink?

| Cause | Solution |
|---|---|
| Open the oven too soon | Wait at least 25 minutes |
| Little hot oven | Preheat well and use an oven thermometer |
| Excess baking powder | Adjust the quantity |
| Undercooked sponge cake | Check with a toothpick before taking it out |
| Dough too beaten after adding flour | Mix just until integrated |

## Why is it dry?

- Overbaking: Every oven is different, check before the indicated time.
- Too much flour: if you measure with the glass, do not squeeze the flour.
- Little oil or skimmed yogurt: use full-fat natural yogurt.

## Conservation

Once cool, wrap the cake in film or store it in an airtight container. It stays tender at room temperature for three or four days, thanks to the oil and yogurt. It can also be frozen whole or in portions, well wrapped, for up to three months.

## Variants

### Marble sponge cake

Separate a third of the dough and mix it with two tablespoons of unsweetened cocoa powder and a splash of milk. Pour the white and cocoa dough into the mold, alternating tablespoons, and draw spirals with a knife to create the marble effect.

### Chocolate cake

Replace half a glass of flour with half a glass of unsweetened cocoa powder and add some chocolate chips.

### orange cake

Use orange zest instead of lemon and replace half the oil with orange juice.

### apple cake

Place a peeled apple cut into thin slices on the dough and sprinkle a little sugar and cinnamon before baking.

### Cake with red berries

Lightly flour a handful of blueberries or raspberries and spread them over the dough. The flour prevents them from sinking to the bottom.

### Whole wheat sponge cake

Replace half the flour with fine whole wheat flour and add a couple of tablespoons of milk.

## Other formats

The same dough is used for different molds. Adjust the baking time:

| Format | Temperature | Indicative time |
|---|---|---|
| Round mold from 22 to 24 cm | 180°C | 35 to 40 minutes |
| 25 cm rectangular mold | 180°C | 40 to 45 minutes |
| Crown or savarin mold | 180°C | 30 to 35 minutes |
| Cupcakes or muffins | 200°C | 15 to 18 minutes |
| Flat gypsy arm tray, half quantity | 200°C | 8 to 10 minutes |

The crown molds, with the hole in the center, bake the cake more evenly and prevent the center from being raw. They are a good option if your oven heats unevenly.

## How to use the cake in other recipes

Yogurt cake is an excellent base for:

- **Simple birthday cakes**, cut into two layers and filled with cream, cream or jam.
- **Trifles and desserts in a glass**, chopped with cream, fruit and cream.
- **Sponge cake toast**, in slices toasted with butter, when it is a few days old.
- **Homemade drunken sponge cake**, soaked in a light syrup.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use Greek yogurt?

Yes. The cake will be somewhat denser and juicier.

### Can I use butter instead of oil?

Yes, melted and tempered. The flavor will be more intense, but the cake will harden faster than with oil.

### Can I reduce sugar?

You can use a glass and a half without big changes. Underneath, the cake will be less tender and will dry out sooner.

### Why does my cake have a crack in the center?

It is quite normal in tall cakes: the surface sets before the interior, which continues to grow and breaks it. Does not affect the flavor. If you want to avoid this, lower the temperature to 170°C and bake for a few more minutes.

## In summary

One yogurt, two glasses of sugar, one of oil, three of flour, three eggs and one envelope of yeast: the easiest formula to remember in Spanish pastries. Beat the eggs well, mix the flour without excess, do not open the oven prematurely and check with a toothpick. The rest is choosing your favorite variant.
