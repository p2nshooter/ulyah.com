Homemade sliced ​​bread is not very similar to what you buy packaged. It is more tender, more aromatic and, above all, it does not contain preservatives or an endless list of ingredients. It is perfect for toast, sandwiches, French toast or simply with olive oil, and is made in one morning with ingredients found in any pantry.

This recipe uses baker's yeast, milk and butter. The milk softens the crumb and the butter makes it more tender and durable. We explain each step and the little tricks that make the difference.

## Ingredients for a 25 × 11 cm mold

| Ingredient | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Strong or bread flour | 500g |
| Warm whole milk | 300ml |
| soft butter | 40g |
| sugar | 25g |
| salt | 9g |
| Fresh bakery yeast | 15 g (or 5 g dry baker's yeast) |

To paint, optional: an egg beaten with a tablespoon of milk, or a little melted butter when it comes out of the oven.

### About the ingredients

- **Flour:** the strong one gives a higher and spongier bread; bread-making bread, a slightly denser but equally delicious bread.
- **Milk:** can be replaced with water, or a mixture of water and milk. With water, the bread is lighter in flavor and the crust is crispier.
- **Sugar:** feeds the yeast, softens the flavor and helps brown the crust.
- **Butter:** makes the crumb more tender. It is added when the gluten has already begun to form.

## Step 1: mix

In a large bowl, mix the flour, sugar and yeast. If you use fresh yeast, crumble it with your fingers into the flour. Add the warm milk, at about 30°C, and mix until you obtain an irregular dough. Add the salt and continue mixing.

Avoid putting salt in direct contact with fresh yeast for a long time: in high concentrations, salt slows down its activity.

## Step 2: knead

Pour the dough onto the table and knead for about 10 minutes. At first it will be sticky; Resist the temptation to add too much flour. With kneading, it will become smoother and more elastic.

Then add the softened butter in pieces and continue kneading for another 5 to 8 minutes. At first the dough will become slippery and seem to fall apart, but little by little it will absorb the butter and become smooth and shiny.

If you use a mixer, knead for 5 minutes at low speed, add the butter and knead another 6 to 8 minutes at medium speed.

### The membrane test

To check if the dough is well kneaded, take a small piece and stretch it carefully between your fingers. If you can stretch it into a thin, translucent membrane without breaking it, the gluten is well developed. If it breaks right away, knead for a few more minutes.

## Step 3: first fermentation

Shape into a ball, place in a lightly greased bowl, and cover. Let it ferment at room temperature, ideally around 24–26°C, until it doubles in volume. It usually takes between an hour and an hour and a half.

To check if it's ready, press the dough with a floured finger. If the hole recovers slowly and only partially, it is ready. If you recover immediately, you need more time. If nothing is recovered, you have gone a little too far.

## Step 4: formed

Pour the dough onto the table and gently degas it with your palms. Stretch it with a rolling pin or with your hands until it forms a rectangle of about 25 × 35 cm, with the short side facing you.

Roll the dough from the short side, squeezing lightly with each turn so as not to trap air. Seal the seam tightly by pinching it and place the roll in the greased pan, seam side down.

### Formed into three buns

Another very common option is to divide the dough into three equal parts, form three small rolls and place them next to each other in the mold. The resulting bread has a very beautiful wavy shape and an especially tender crumb.

## Step 5: second fermentation

Cover the mold with a cloth or plastic and let it rise for 45 to 60 minutes, until the dough protrudes one or two centimeters from the edge of the mold. Meanwhile, preheat the oven to 180°C with top and bottom heat.

If you want a straight-sided, square-shaped loaf, use a mold with a lid and close it when the dough reaches one centimeter from the edge.

## Step 6: Bake

If you want a shiny crust, brush the surface with the beaten egg. Bake in the middle of the oven for 30 to 35 minutes, until the top is golden brown.

The bread is done when, when you unmold it and tap the base, it sounds hollow. The most reliable way is to measure the interior temperature with a thermometer: it should be between 90 and 93 °C. If the top browns too much too soon, cover it with aluminum foil.

## Step 7: Cool

Unmold the bread as soon as it comes out of the oven and let it cool on a rack. If you leave it in the mold, the steam will condense and the crust will soften. If you want a very tender crust, brush it with melted butter when you take it out of the oven.

Wait at least an hour before cutting it. For clean cuts, use a serrated knife.

## How to keep it tender

Homemade bread does not contain preservatives, so it dries faster than industrial bread. To make it last longer:

- Store it, once completely cool, in a tightly closed plastic bag or in an airtight container.
- Keep it at room temperature. The refrigerator speeds up the hardening of the bread.
- For long periods, cut it into slices and freeze it. The slices can be toasted directly from frozen.

It stays tender for three or four days.

### The blanching trick

If you want an even more tender and long-lasting sliced ​​bread, try blanching some of the flour. Mix 25 g of the flour with 125 ml of the recipe's milk, heat over low heat, stirring until it thickens like a cream, at about 65 °C, and let it cool. Add this paste to the rest of the ingredients. Gelatinized starch retains more water and the bread stays tender for longer days. It is the principle of the Asian *tangzhong* method.

## Variations

- **Whole wheat bread:** replace half of the flour with whole wheat flour and add 20 ml more milk.
- **Bread with seeds:** add 60 g of sunflower, sesame and flax seeds at the end of kneading.
- **Sourdough bread:** replaces the yeast with 150 g of active sourdough and lengthens fermentation times.
- **Sweet milk bread:** increase the sugar to 60 g and add an egg, reducing 50 ml of milk.

## Adapt the recipe to your mold

If your mold does not measure 25 × 11 cm, adjust all the ingredients in the same proportion, comparing the surface of the base. A 20 × 10 cm pan, for example, has about three-quarters of the surface: use about 375 g of flour and reduce the milk, butter, sugar, salt and yeast by that same 75%. As a visual reference, the dough formed should occupy more or less half the height of the mold before the second fermentation. An overfilled mold overflows in the oven; one that is too empty gives a low bread with a tight crumb.

## Ideas to make the most of it until the last slice

- **Breakfast toast**, with olive oil and grated tomato or with butter and homemade jam.
- **Mixed sandwich**, golden in the pan with a little butter.
- **Croutons** for soups and creams: cubes of day-old bread browned in the pan or oven.
- **Homemade breadcrumbs**, crushing dry slices; keep it in a closed jar.
- **Tender torrijas**, with thick slices of sliced ​​bread a couple of days old, as we explain in the [torrijas] guide (/recetas-y-guias/torrijas/).

## Common problems

**Dense and heavy crumb.** Lack of kneading or fermentation. Do the membrane test and wait for the dough to double in volume.

**The top part separates from the crumb.** This is usually due to air trapped when forming the roll. Roll up tightly.

**The bread sinks when baking.** The second fermentation has been excessive. Put it in the oven when it protrudes one or two centimeters from the mold.

**Hard crust.** Oven too strong or too long. Brush with butter when leaving and store in a bag once cool.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I do it without kneading?

You can reduce the kneading by making folds every 20 minutes for the first hour, as with loaves. The crumb will be a little less fine and regular, but the bread will turn out well.

### Can I prepare the dough the night before?

Yes. After kneading, store the dough in a covered container in the refrigerator overnight. In the morning, let it cool for an hour, form the bread and continue with the second fermentation, which will be somewhat longer. The flavor gains depth.

### Why does my bread have a wrinkled crust when it cools?

It usually happens when the bread is a little raw inside and the crumb contracts when it cools. Bake for a few more minutes or check that the interior reaches 90°C.

### Can I use oil instead of butter?

Yes. Use the same amount of mild olive or sunflower oil. The bread will be equally soft, with a slightly different flavor and a slightly moister crumb.

## In summary

Milk, butter, good kneading until the membrane test, patient fermentation and an oven at 180 °C. With these elements you will have a tender, aromatic and preservative-free sliced ​​bread, much tastier than purchased bread.
