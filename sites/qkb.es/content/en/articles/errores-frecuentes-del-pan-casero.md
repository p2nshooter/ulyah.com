Everyone who makes bread at home has ever pulled something out of the oven that didn't look like what they expected: a dense brick, a flat cake, a pale crust, or a moist crumb that sticks to the knife. The good thing is that bread is very expressive: each defect tells what happened during the process. Learning to read it is the fastest way to improve.

This guide is organized by symptoms. Find the one that looks like your bread, check for probable causes, and apply the solution in the next batch. Change one thing at a time: that way you'll know what worked.

## Before we begin: the four variables that explain almost everything

Most homemade bread problems are due to one of four variables:

1. **Fermentation:** too short or too long.
2. **Gluten development:** poorly kneaded dough or with few folds.
3. **Formed:** without sufficient surface tension.
4. **Baking:** temperature, steam and time.

If you have to guess, always start with fermentation: it is the most common cause.

## Symptom 1: bread is dense and heavy

The crumb is compact, with very small or almost no alveoli, and the bread weighs more than it seems.

**Probable causes:**

- Fermentation too short: the dough did not have time to generate enough gas.
- Yeast or weak sourdough.
- Cold cooking that slows down fermentation.
- Little gluten development: the dough does not retain gas.
- Too much whole wheat or rye flour without adjusting the water.

**Solutions:**

- Judge fermentation by the appearance of the dough, not by the clock. In the first fermentation with yeast, the dough must double in volume.
- Use sourdough at its peak, when it has doubled in volume after feeding.
- Look for a warmer place, between 24 and 26 °C.
- Knead until the membrane is proof or add one more series of folds.

## Symptom 2: a large hole under the bark

The crumb is dense on the bottom and there is a large cavity just below the top crust. It is sometimes known as "the baker's tunnel."

**Probable causes:**

- Insufficient block fermentation: this is the most common cause in sourdough breads.
- Air trapped when forming.
- Formed without tension.

**Solutions:**

- Extend the block fermentation until the dough has risen between 50 and 75%, has a domed surface and visible bubbles.
- When forming, press gently to eliminate large bubbles from the surface and roll tightly.

## Symptom 3: Bread comes out flat and spreads

When you pour the dough, it spreads out like a cake and in the oven it barely rises upwards.

**Probable causes:**

- Excessive fermentation: the gluten has been weakened and the dough lacks strength.
- Too high hydration for your flour.
- Formed without tension.
- Flour with little protein.

**Solutions:**

- Shorten fermentation or reduce temperature.
- Lower the hydration a few points, for example from 75 to 70%.
- Practice forming by tensioning the surface well.
- Use a bread-making or strong flour.
- A delay in the refrigerator after forming hardens the dough and makes it easier for it to hold its shape when baking.

## Symptom 4: The crumb is wet and rubbery

When you cut the bread, the crumb sticks to the knife and has a wet, almost raw appearance.

**Probable causes:**

- The bread was cut hot.
- Insufficient baking.
- In rye breads, lack of acidity or short baking.

**Solutions:**

- Wait at least an hour before cutting, two into large loaves.
- Bake until the internal temperature reaches 96°C for rustic breads or 90–93°C for sliced ​​and enriched breads.
- If the crust browns too much too soon, lower the temperature and lengthen baking.

## Symptom 5: the cortex becomes pale

The bread is done, but the crust is light and soft.

**Probable causes:**

- Little hot oven. Many home ovens heat less than they say.
- Excessive fermentation: the yeasts have consumed almost all the sugars that would caramelize in the crust.
- Too much steam throughout baking.

**Solutions:**

- Check the actual oven temperature with an oven thermometer.
- Preheat for at least 45 minutes, especially if you use a stone or iron pot.
- Remove the steam after 15 or 20 minutes.
- Check the fermentation times.

## Symptom 6: the base is burned

The bottom of the bread burns before the rest is browned.

**Probable causes:**

- Lower oven heating element too powerful or bread placed too low.
- Iron pot in direct contact with the base of the oven.
- Doughs with a lot of sugar or milk.

**Solutions:**

- Raise the tray or pot one height.
- Place an empty tray under the pot to act as a shield.
- In the last 10 minutes, place a tray or rack lined with baking paper under the bread.

## Symptom 7: The cut does not open

The grout remains like a flat scar or the bread breaks on the sides.

**Probable causes:**

- Lack of tension in the forming.
- Cutting too shallow or vertical.
- Lack of steam at the beginning of baking.
- Passed fermentation dough.

**Solutions:**

- Form with more tension.
- Cut with the blade inclined at about 30 degrees and decisively, one centimeter deep.
- Ensure steam for the first 15 to 20 minutes, with the pot covered or with water in a tray.

## Symptom 8: the bread is too sour

**Probable causes:**

- Sourdough past its peak.
- Very long fermentations, especially cold.
- Very firm sourdough or with a lot of whole wheat flour.

**Solutions:**

- Use the sourdough at its peak.
- Reduce the amount of sourdough or the delay time in the refrigerator.
- Keep the sourdough more liquid and feed it with white flour.

## Symptom 9: the dough is unmanageable

The dough sticks to everything and there is no way to shape it.

**Probable causes:**

- Hydration too high for flour.
- Little gluten development.
- Excessive fermentation.

**Solutions:**

- Reduce the water and increase it little by little when you master the technique.
- Make more folds.
- Work with wet hands and a scraper.
- Chill the dough in the refrigerator 30 minutes before forming it.

## Symptom 10: Bread becomes stale in a day

**Probable causes:**

- Excessive baking, which dries out the crumb.
- Very little hydrated dough.
- Conservation in the refrigerator.

**Solutions:**

- Adjust the baking time.
- Increase hydration a little.
- Store the bread at room temperature in a cloth bag or freeze it in slices.

## Symptom 11: bread tastes like yeast or alcohol

**Probable causes:**

- Excess yeast.
- Fermentation too fast at high temperature.
- Excessive fermentation, in which the alcohol produced is noticeable in the flavor.

**Solutions:**

- Reduce the yeast by half and lengthen the times.
- Ferment in a cooler location or use colder water in the mixture.
- Bake when the dough is ready and not when it is overcooked.

## Symptom 12: The bark cracks irregularly

The bread breaks at the sides or at the base instead of opening through the cut.

**Probable causes:**

- Second fermentation too short: the dough still had a lot of strength and breaks wherever it can.
- Too superficial brushing.
- Poorly sealed seam in the form.

**Solutions:**

- Leave the dough a little longer in the second fermentation.
- Make deeper, more purposeful cuts.
- Seal the seam well and always place it face down on the tray.

## Symptom 13: bread sticks to the basket or tray

When you dump the dough, it sticks to the banneton or cloth, tears and loses the accumulated gas; or the cooked bread does not come off the tray.

**Probable causes:**

- Lightly floured basket or cloth, or floured with wheat flour, which hydrates and acts as glue.
- Dough with excess fermentation, more moist and sticky on the surface.
- Tray without baking paper or grease.

**Solutions:**

- Sprinkle the basket with rice flour or semolina, which do not absorb water as easily.
- Monitor the second fermentation and pour out the dough as soon as it is ready.
- Bake on baking paper and, if you use an iron pot, place it under the bread when putting it in.

## A baker's notebook

The most useful tool for improvement is not a utensil, but a notebook. Write down in each round:

- Flour and its proportion.
- Hydration.
- The temperature of the kitchen and the water.
- The times of each phase.
- What the dough looked like at the end of fermentation.
- The result and a photo of the crumb.

After five or six loaves, you'll see clear patterns and know exactly what to adjust.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does the same bread come out different every week?

Because the temperature of the kitchen changes, the sourdough is at a different point or the flour absorbs more or less water. That is why it is better to observe the mass rather than follow fixed schedules.

### What is the most common mistake when starting out?

Cutting off bulk fermentation too early. Almost all dense breads with a hole under the crust have this cause.

### Is it worth buying a thermometer?

Yes. A cheap probe thermometer allows you to measure the temperature of the water, the dough and the inside of the bread. It is the most reliable way to know if the bread is done.

## In summary

If the bread comes out dense, check the fermentation; if it comes out flat, the strength of the dough and the forming; if the crumb is wet, bake and be patient before cutting; if the crust is not browned, the actual oven temperature. Change one variable at a time, write it all down and each loaf will teach you something.
