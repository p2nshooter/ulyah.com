Mozzarella is a fascinating cheese. Unlike most, its curd is heated and stretched into long, shiny fibers, a technique Italians call *pasta filata*, spun pasta. The result is that elastic, juicy and soft cheese that melts into the pizza and that, freshly made, has a dairy and fresh flavor that is impossible to match with the supermarket one.

Making mozzarella at home is faster than it seems: around an hour from when the milk starts to heat up until you have the balls in a bowl of cold water. The key is not in rare ingredients, but in controlling two variables: **acidity** and **temperature**. This guide explains the quick method with citric acid, the most reliable in a home kitchen.

## Why does mozzarella stretch?

For a curd to be spun, the caseins in the milk have to reach a specific point of acidity. With just enough acid, the protein network loses some of the calcium that keeps it rigid and becomes plastic when heated: it stretches rather than breaks. If the curd has little acidity, it breaks; If it has too much, it becomes grainy and releases fat.

In traditional cheese factories, this acidity is achieved with lactic ferments that work for hours. In the domestic quick method, it is achieved by adding **citric acid** to the milk from the beginning, which allows the curd to be spun on the same day.

## Ingredients

For about four or five medium balls, around 400 to 500 grams:

- **4 liters of pasteurized whole milk**, not UHT. Some milks pasteurized at very high temperatures also do not work well; If one brand fails, try another.
- **6 grams of citric acid** (approximately one and a half teaspoons), dissolved in 60 ml of chlorine-free water.
- **Liquid rennet**, the dose indicated by the manufacturer for 4 liters, diluted in 60 ml of chlorine-free water.
- **Salt** for the spinning water.

Citric acid is sold in baking stores, drugstores, and stores selling products for making preserves or cheese.

## Utensils

- Large stainless steel pot.
- Thermometer.
- Long knife and skimmer.
- A bowl resistant to heat and very hot water.
- Heat resistant kitchen gloves, highly recommended.

## step by step

### 1. Acidify the milk

Pour the cold milk into the pot and add the dissolved citric acid, stirring well. You will notice that the milk thickens slightly: it is the acidity at work.

### 2. Heat to 32°C

Heat the milk over medium-low heat, stirring, to **32°C**.

### 3. Add the rennet

Remove from the heat, add the diluted rennet and stir gently for about thirty seconds. Stop the movement and let it rest, covered, between **5 and 10 minutes**, until the curd is firm and gives a clean cut.

### 4. Cut the curd

Cut the curd into cubes of about 2 centimeters with a long knife, in a grid pattern.

### 5. Heat and stir

Put the pot back on the heat and heat gently to **about 40 to 42 °C**, stirring very gently. The cubes will shrink and release whey. Remove from heat and stir for a few more minutes.

### 6. Separate the curd

Transfer the curd to a strainer or a bowl with the slotted spoon, pressing gently to remove as much whey as possible. Reserve the whey.

### 7. Heat spinning water

Heat salted water, about 2 liters with two tablespoons of salt, to **80 to 85 °C**. Some people use the salty whey itself.

### 8. Spin

This is the magic moment:

1. Cut the curd into pieces and put it in a bowl.
2. Cover the curd with the hot water and wait a minute.
3. Using gloves or two wooden spoons, gather the curd and stretch it. If it's ready, it will stretch like shiny gum.
4. Fold the dough over itself and stretch again, several times, until it is smooth and shiny. If it cools and stops stretching, submerge it in the hot water again.
5. Don't overwork it: if it stretches too much, the mozzarella will be tough.

### 9. Form the balls

When the dough is smooth, form balls by stretching the surface down and closing it underneath, as if you were making a bread ball. Pinch the closure.

### 10. cool

Submerge the balls in very cold water, with ice, for a few minutes to keep their shape. Then, store them in the refrigerator in a container with lightly salted water or a little whey.

## How to know if curd is ready to spin

A simple test: take a small piece of curd and immerse it in the hot water for a few seconds. If when you pull it it stretches forming shiny threads, it is ready. If it breaks into lumps, it needs more acidity or more temperature.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | probable cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Milk does not form firm curds | UHT or ultra-pasteurized milk, weak rennet | Change milk; check the curd |
| Curd breaks when stretched | Low acidity or not very hot water | Heats the water more; In the next batch, check the acid dose |
| Grainy mass that releases fat | Excess acidity or too high temperature | Reduce the acid slightly in the next batch |
| Hard and rubbery mozzarella | Excessive spinning or overly drained curd | Spin just until smooth |
| Tasteless mozzarella | little salt | Salt the spinning water or preservation brine |

## Organize the spinning session

Spinning is done in a few minutes and does not allow breaks, so it is a good idea to have everything ready before separating the curd: hot salted water in a saucepan, a large bowl with water and ice, gloves, a clean board and the container where the balls will be kept. For the first time, a half recipe with two liters of milk is more manageable: there is less curd to work with while it is hot and you learn to recognize the point without rushing. When you master the gesture, you can return to the full amount.

## Take advantage of whey

More than three liters of slightly acidic whey come out of four liters of milk. No need to throw it away:

- **In bread:** replace part of the water in the dough with whey; The crumb is tender and the crust browns more.
- **To cook:** gives flavor to rice, pasta or potatoes.
- **In soups and vegetable creams**, instead of part of the broth.
- **To preserve the mozzarella**, with a little salt, as explained above.

With the whey from this quick method you get very little cottage cheese, because most of the protein has already remained in the curd. If you are interested in cottage cheese, the [homemade cottage cheese](/recetas-y-guias/requeson-casero/) guide explains how to make it with whole milk. Store the serum in the refrigerator and use it within two or three days.

## Security

Work with pasteurized milk, clean utensils and keep the mozzarella in the refrigerator. When spinning, very hot water is handled: use heat-resistant gloves and be careful with splashes, especially if there are children in the kitchen.

## Conservation and consumption

Homemade mozzarella is best freshly made. In the refrigerator, in its liquid, it keeps well for **two or three days**. Take the mozzarella out of the refrigerator a while before serving it: at room temperature its flavor is much more intense.

## Ideas to use it

- **Caprese salad**: with ripe tomato, fresh basil, olive oil and salt.
- **Homemade pizza**: drain the mozzarella well and cut it into pieces so that it does not release too much liquid.
- **Sandwiches and toasts**: with tomato, oil and oregano.
- **Baked pasta**: melts and gratinates perfectly.

## Variants

- **Burrata**: with a sheet of freshly spun mozzarella a bag is formed that is filled with pieces of mozzarella mixed with cream, the *stracciatella*. It requires practice.
- **Bocconcini**: small balls the size of a walnut.
- **Braid**: The dough is stretched into cords and braided.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can it be made with goat milk?

Yes, although goat mozzarella stretches less and has a more intense flavor. The traditional Italian buffalo uses buffalo milk, which is difficult to find in Spain.

### Can I use lemon juice instead of citric acid?

It is not recommended: the acid concentration of lemon varies and it is difficult to get the acidity necessary for spinning.

### Can you spin it in the microwave?

Some recipes do this, heating the curd at short intervals. It works, but hot water gives more uniform control and a finer result.

### Why is my mozzarella rubbery and hard?

Normally because too much work was done during spinning or because the water was too hot. Stretch the curd just until it is smooth and shiny, and immediately form the balls. It may also be due to excess rennet.

### Can it be frozen?

Yes, but the texture changes: when it thaws, it releases water and becomes more grainy. Frozen mozzarella is great for pizzas and gratins, but not for eating fresh in a salad.

### How much mozzarella comes out of a liter of milk?

With whole cow's milk, you get approximately 100 to 130 g of mozzarella per liter, depending on the milk and how drained the curd is.

## In summary

Pasteurized milk acidified with citric acid, rennet at 32 °C, cut, gently heated to 40–42 °C, drained and spun in water at 80–85 °C. With practice, in an hour you will have shiny, elastic homemade mozzarella. The first batches may not turn out perfect: each one will teach you to recognize the exact point of your milk.
