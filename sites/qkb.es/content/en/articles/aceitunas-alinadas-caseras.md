Anyone who has ever tasted an olive freshly picked from the tree knows that it is inedible: it has an intense bitterness that is nothing like the olives eaten with an aperitif. This bitterness is due to a natural compound called **oleuropein**, and transforming the olive into a tasty snack consists precisely of eliminating it, a process that in many towns in Spain is called "sweetening" the olives.

In autumn, when the green olives are at their peak, many families in the south and east of the peninsula prepare their own dressed olives, each one with its secret recipe of garlic, thyme, fennel or orange peel. This guide explains how to do it at home.

## The bitterness of the olive

Oleuropein is a phenolic compound that protects olives from microorganisms and animals. It is soluble in water, so it can be removed with time and patience by submerging the olives in frequently changed water or brine.

There are three main methods:

1. **Sweetened with water:** the most traditional at home. Slow, but simple and without chemicals.
2. **Sweetened in brine:** olives spend months in brine, where they slowly sweeten and, at the same time, ferment.
3. **Treatment with soda:** the industrial method of the "Sevillian style". It is very fast, but requires handling a dangerous caustic product and we do not recommend it in a home kitchen.

This guide focuses on the first two.

## Choose the olives

- Use **green, firm and healthy olives**, without bruises or insect bites. Damaged olives become soft and spoil the rest.
- The most common varieties for seasoning in Spain are manzanilla, gordal, hojiblanca, cornicabra or morona, depending on the area.
- Pick or buy olives in the fall, when they have reached their size but are still green or starting to turn yellowish.

## Prepare the olives: split or crushed

So that the water penetrates and the bitterness comes out faster, the olives are opened before sweetening them:

- **Split or cracked olives:** make one or two longitudinal cuts in each olive with a knife, until you reach the stone.
- **Crushed or "crushed" olives:** Each olive is hit with a flat stone, a wooden mallet or the bottom of a glass, just enough so that the flesh opens without breaking the stone.

Whole, unopened olives take much longer to sweeten and are reserved for long brines.

## Method 1: sweetened with water

1. Place the split or crushed olives in a large glass, ceramic or food-grade plastic container.
2. Cover them with fresh water.
3. Place a plate on top to keep them submerged.
4. **Change the water every day**, or twice a day if it's hot.
5. Starting on the eighth or tenth day, try an olive. When the bitterness is mild and pleasant, the sweetening is over.

The total time depends on the variety, size and cut: it usually ranges from 10 days to 3 weeks for split olives. Keep in mind that some bitterness is desirable: an olive without any trace of bitterness is bland.

## Method 2: sweetened in brine

It is a slower method, but produces olives with more flavor, because a natural lactic fermentation occurs during the process.

1. Prepare a brine with **80 to 100 g of salt per liter of water**, 8 to 10%.
2. Place the olives, whole or split, in jars or a large container.
3. Cover them with the brine and keep them submerged with a weight.
4. Cover loosely and store in a cool, dark place.
5. Change the brine every two to three weeks.
6. Try after two or three months. Whole olives may need four to six months.

During this process it is normal for a whitish film to appear on the surface, which is removed when the brine is changed. Hairy or colored mold on olives, on the other hand, is a warning sign.

## Preservation brine

When the olives are sweetened, it is time to season them and preserve them in a final brine:

- **1 liter of water.**
- **80 to 100 g of salt**, according to your taste.
- Optional: a splash of wine vinegar, about 50 ml per liter, which increases acidity and improves conservation.

Dissolve the salt in the water, boiling it if you want, and let it cool completely before using.

## The dressing

Each family has its seasoning. These are the most common ingredients in Spanish dressed olives, for each kilo of olives:

- **Garlic:** 6 to 8 cloves, crushed or sliced.
- **Thyme:** a few twigs.
- **Wild fennel:** a few stems, typical of Andalusia.
- **Oregano:** one tablespoon.
- **Laurel:** two or three leaves.
- **Red or bell pepper:** a few strips, which give color.
- **Orange or lemon peel:** a strip, without the white part.
- **Chili pepper:** one or two, if you like spicy.
- **Cumin:** half a teaspoon, in some areas of Andalusia and Murcia.

### Homemade Sevillian style dressing

Crushed olives with garlic, thyme, fennel, oregano, red pepper and a splash of vinegar in the brine.

### citrus dressing

Split olives with orange peel, bay leaf, garlic and a touch of chilli.

### Dressing for the "finger suckers"

A very popular recipe in Andalusia: crushed olives with plenty of garlic, paprika, cumin, oregano, vinegar and oil.

## Pack and rest

1. Drain the sweetened olives.
2. Place them in clean jars, alternating layers of olives and dressing.
3. Cover with the preservation brine until they are completely submerged.
4. Close the jars and let them rest in a cool, dark place **at least a week** before eating them, so that they take in the flavor of the dressing.

## A note about garlic and oil

Some recipes finish the olives with olive oil on top. The oil insulates from the air and, combined with garlic, can create an oxygen-free, low-acid environment. If the olives are in a well-salted brine and, better, with some vinegar, the risk is low; But if you want to add oil, do it in the portion you are going to serve, not in the preservation jar. And never store garlic submerged only in oil at room temperature.

## Calendar for a fall batch

| Moment | Task |
|---|---|
| October or early November | pick or buy firm green olives, split or crush them |
| The next two or three weeks | sweeten with water, changing it every day |
| When the bitterness is soft | season and pack in preservation brine |
| one week later | first olives for the aperitif |
| Until spring | consume, checking that they are always covered |

If you prefer the brine method, pick as early as possible: the whole olives will not be ready until late winter.

## How to serve them

Take the olives out of the brine a little early and serve them at room temperature, which is when the dressing is best appreciated. Drain them, pour them into the bowl with a drizzle of extra virgin olive oil and, if you want, add some fresh thyme leaves or a strip of freshly cut orange peel. Accompanied by picos, regañás or bread, they are the perfect aperitif along with a fine wine, a vermouth or a cold beer.

## Olives and salt

The dressed olives contain a lot of salt due to the brine in which they are preserved. If you follow a low-salt diet, rinse them or soak them in water for an hour before serving them, and eat them in moderation. What is not advisable is to reduce the salt in the preservation brine, because it is what protects the olives for months.

## Conservation

- In well-salted brine, in a cool, dark place, the dressed olives are preserved for several months.
- Once you open the jar, always keep them submerged and use clean utensils.
- If it's hot, store them in the refrigerator.
- Discard any olives that are soft, slimy, moldy, or have an unpleasant odor.

## Common problems

**They are still very bitter.** They need more days of sweetening. Large or whole olives take longer.

**They have softened.** This may be due to overripe, bruised olives or a brine that is not very salty. Use firm, healthy olives.

**They have lost their green color.** It is normal for them to turn a duller or yellowish green over time.

**They are too salty.** Put them in water a few hours before serving.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I season olives purchased already sweetened?

Yes, it is what is done in many homes. Buy green olives in brine without dressing, drain them, and add your dressing and a new brine.

### Can black olives be sweetened?

Ripe olives, purple or black, have less bitterness and are usually cured in dry salt or brine. The process is different and shorter.

### How long do dressed olives last?

In a well-salted brine, always submerged and in a cool place, they last several months. Over time, the dressing becomes more intense and the olives may soften slightly. Many families prepare a large quantity in the fall and consume it until spring.

### What do I do with the white film from the brine?

If it is a thin, smooth and whitish layer, it is usually surface yeast. Remove it, wipe the rim of the container, and if it reappears frequently, change the brine. If it is hairy or colored mold on the olives, discard the jar.

## In summary

Green olives, healthy and firm, opened with a cut or a blow, sweetened with water that is changed every day or in a patient brine. When the bitterness is mild, season with garlic, thyme, fennel and orange peel, cover with 8–10% brine and wait a week. This way you will have olives from your own house for the aperitif.
