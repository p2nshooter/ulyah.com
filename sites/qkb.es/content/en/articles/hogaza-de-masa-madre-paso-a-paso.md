The sourdough loaf is the bread that many people dream of making when they start baking at home: golden, crispy crust that sings as it cools, shiny, honeycombed crumb, slightly acidic flavor, and a shelf life of several days. It requires time, but very little active work. Most of the process is waiting and watching.

This recipe is designed for a first loaf that turns out well, with moderate hydration, easy to handle, and baking in an iron pot that imitates a bakery oven. You need [active sourdough](/recetas-y-guias/cuidar-y-alimentar-la-masa-madre/), at its peak.

## Ingredients for a loaf of about 850 g

| Ingredient | Quantity | Baker percentage |
|---|---|---|
| Bread flour | 450g | 90 % |
| Whole wheat flour | 50g | 10 % |
| Water at about 28 °C | 360g | 72 % |
| 100% active sourdough | 100g | 20 % |
| salt | 10g | 2 % |

### What is the baker's percentage?

In baking, all ingredients are expressed in relation to the total weight of flour, which is 100%. Thus, 360 g of water over 500 g of flour is 72% hydration. This system allows you to scale any recipe and easily compare doughs.

## material

- Digital scale.
- A large bowl.
- A baker's scraper.
- A banneton or basket lined with a floured cloth.
- A cast iron pot with lid, oven safe at 250°C.
- A very sharp blade or knife.
- Baking paper.

## indicative schedule

An example of a schedule to have fresh bread in the morning, with a kitchen at about 24 °C:

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| 08:00 | Feed the sourdough |
| 12:30 | Autolysis: mix flour and water |
| 13:30 | Add sourdough and salt |
| 2:00 p.m. to 3:30 p.m. | Four sets of folds every 30 minutes |
| 15:30 to 18:30 | Untouched block fermentation |
| 18:30 | Preformed and rest |
| 19:00 | Formed and banneton to the refrigerator |
| 07:00 the next day | Bake |

If your kitchen is colder, the block fermentation will take longer; If it's hot, shorter. What rules is the appearance of the dough, not the watch.

## Step 1: autolysis

In the large bowl, mix the two flours with 340 g of water, reserving 20 g. Stir by hand or with a spatula until no dry flour remains. No need to knead. Cover the bowl and let it rest for an hour.

During autolysis, the flour becomes completely hydrated and gluten begins to form on its own. The dough becomes more extensible and will be much easier to work with.

## Step 2: add sourdough and salt

Spread the sourdough over the dough and press it with wet fingers to incorporate it. Pinch the dough several times and fold it over itself until the sourdough is integrated. Next, sprinkle the salt, add the reserved 20g of water and repeat the pinching and folding. The dough will break at first and then come back together. Work like this for about five minutes.

## Step 3: pleats

Instead of intense kneading, this recipe develops the gluten with periodic folds. Every 30 minutes, for the first two hours, do one set:

1. Wet your hand.
2. Take one end of the dough, stretch it up without breaking it and fold it over the center.
3. Turn the bowl a quarter turn and repeat until all four corners are complete.

After four sets, you will notice that the dough is smoother, more elastic and able to maintain its shape.

## Step 4: block fermentation

Cover the bowl and let the dough ferment without touching it. This is the most important step of the entire recipe and the one that most depends on observation.

Block fermentation is finished when:

- The dough has increased by 50 to 75% in volume. A container with straight and transparent walls helps a lot when measuring it.
- The surface is domed and with some bubbles.
- Bubbles are seen on the sides and base of the container.
- When you move the bowl, the dough shakes like jelly.

A short fermentation produces a dense bread with large irregular holes under the crust. Excessive fermentation produces flat, sticky and very acidic bread.

## Step 5: preformed

Pour the dough carefully onto the table, without flouring it. Using the scraper, shape it into a ball, pushing the dough toward you and turning it to tighten the surface. Let it rest uncovered for 20 to 30 minutes. It will relax and spread out a little.

## Step 6: formed

Lightly flour the surface of the ball and turn it over, so that the floured side is on the bottom. Gently stretch the dough into a rectangle, fold the bottom third towards the center, then the sides, and finally roll from the top towards you, tightening the surface. Seal the seam with the palm of your hand.

Flour the banneton, preferably with rice flour, which does not stick, and place the dough with the seam facing up.

## Step 7: delay in refrigerator

Put the banneton in a plastic bag or cover it, and store it in the refrigerator for 10 to 16 hours. The cold slows down the fermentation, develops the flavor and also firms the dough, which makes kneading much easier.

## Step 8: Bake

1. One hour before baking, put the pot with the lid in the oven and preheat it to 250°C.
2. Take the dough out of the refrigerator, turn it onto baking paper and score the surface with a sharp cut, tilting the blade at about 30 degrees and with a depth of one centimeter.
3. Carefully take out the hot pot, place the dough in the paper, cover and put in the oven.
4. Bake for 20 minutes covered at 250°C. Steam from the dough itself is trapped and allows the bread to expand.
5. Remove the lid, lower to 230°C and bake for another 20 to 25 minutes, until the crust is nicely browned, even toasted on the edges of the cut.

The bread is done when it sounds hollow when tapped on the base or when the internal temperature reaches about 96°C.

## Step 9: Cool

Let the bread cool on a rack for at least an hour, preferably two. Although the temptation is great, the crumb continues to cook and set with the residual heat. If you cut it hot, it will be rubbery.

## How to read the result

| what you see | probable cause |
|---|---|
| Dense crumb with a large hole under the crust | Short block fermentation |
| Flat bread that spreads when turned over | Excessive fermentation or forming without tension |
| pale bark | Oven not too hot or time without lid short |
| burnt base | Pot too close to the bottom resistance; place a tray underneath |
| Wet and chewy crumb | Hot cutting or lack of baking |
| Very acidic taste | Stale sourdough or very long fermentation |

For more diagnostics, consult our guide to [common homemade bread mistakes](/recetas-y-guias/errores-frecuentes-del-pan-casero/).

## Conservation

The sourdough loaf is kept for three to five days at room temperature, wrapped in a cotton cloth or paper bag, with the cut side resting on the board. It can also be frozen in slices and toasted straight from the freezer.

## Variations

When you master the base recipe, you can:

- Increase the hydration to 75 or 78% for a more open crumb.
- Replace whole wheat with rye or spelled flour.
- Add sunflower, sesame or flax seeds, previously soaked.
- Add walnuts and raisins in the last fold.
- Add olives and rosemary, or diced cured cheese.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I bake without an iron pot?

Yes. Place an empty baking tray on the bottom while you preheat and a stone or thick tray in the center. When you put the bread in, pour a glass of hot water into the bottom tray to generate steam and close quickly. Remove the tray from the water after 20 minutes. The result is somewhat less spectacular, but very good.

### Can I bake the same day, without a refrigerator?

Yes. After forming, leave the dough in the banneton at room temperature for between one and three hours, until when you press it with a finger the hole slowly recovers. The flavor will be softer and the kneading will be somewhat more difficult, because the dough is softer.

### Why doesn't the cut open?

There may be a lack of tension in the forming, a lack of steam during the first minutes, or the cut may have been too shallow or too vertical. An inclined and determined cut, with the cold dough fresh from the refrigerator, opens much better.

### What do I do if my kitchen is too cold?

Use warmer water in the mixture, around 30°C, and find a warm place for the bulk fermentation, such as an off oven with the light on. Always keep an eye on the appearance of the dough and not just the watch.

## In summary

Autolysis, sourdough at its peak, soft folds, a block fermentation that can be judged by eye, formed with tension and a night in the refrigerator. Then, a very hot pot does the rest. The first loaf may not be perfect, but each one will teach you something about your flour, your sourdough, and your kitchen.
