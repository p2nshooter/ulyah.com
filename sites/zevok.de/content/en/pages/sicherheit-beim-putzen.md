Cleaning products are chemicals, even those that smell like lemon or are found in the kitchen as household remedies. When used correctly, they are helpful and safe. Most accidents in the household, on the other hand, arise from a few, always the same mistakes: agents are mixed, poured into other people's bottles, sprayed in small rooms without fresh air or left within reach of children. On this page we have collected the rules that apply to each of our guides.

## The most important rule: never mix

Use only one cleaning product at a time and rinse thoroughly with water before applying another product to the same area. These combinations are particularly dangerous:

| Combination | What can arise | Typical situation |
|---|---|---|
| Cleaner containing chlorine and acid (vinegar, citric acid, descaler, acidic toilet cleaner) | Chlorine gas, which severely irritates eyes and respiratory tract | First toilet cleaner, then a chlorine product in the same toilet |
| Cleaner containing chlorine and ammonia (found in some glass and all-purpose cleaners) | Chloramines, also highly irritating | Windows and tiles treated with two different sprays |
| Two different drain cleaners | violent reaction, heat, splashes | The first one didn't work, so a second one comes along |

Many bleach and disinfectant cleaners as well as some mold removers contain chlorine. Pay attention to the information on the packaging that the product must not be used together with other products because dangerous gases can be produced. If you're not sure what's in a product, don't combine it with anything.

Even seemingly harmless mixtures are of little use: baking soda and vinegar fizz impressively, but neutralize each other. What's left is essentially water with a little salt. This only becomes dangerous when you pour the mixture into a sealed bottle, because the resulting gas builds up pressure.

## Understand labels and warning signs

On many cleaners you will find red-bordered, pointed squares. These hazard pictograms are regulated uniformly in the EU. The most important ones in the household:

- **Corrosiveness** (liquid that attacks a hand and a surface): can cause serious damage to skin and eyes, typical of drain and some oven cleaners.
- **Exclamation mark:** irritates skin, eyes or respiratory tract or is harmful to health in large quantities.
- **Flame:** flammable, for example denatured alcohol. Do not use near open fire, hot stoves or cigarettes.

There are also the signal words “Caution” and the stronger “Danger”. Also read the instructions for use: They will tell you how long an agent can work, whether it needs to be diluted and which surfaces it should not be used on.

## Ventilate and protect yourself

- **Window open:** Open a window before cleaning, especially in small bathrooms without windows, leave the door open and the fan running.
- **Wear gloves:** Household gloves protect the skin from alkalis, acids and constant contact with water and surfactants.
- **Do not spray into the air:** It is better to spray cleaner onto a cloth rather than over a large area, then you will inhale less spray mist.
- **Protect your eyes:** When working overhead or with highly corrosive agents, simple safety glasses are useful.
- **Do not steam hot:** Do not heat vinegar essence or other acids. The fumes irritate the respiratory tract.

## Store properly

Always store cleaners in the original packaging to ensure that the label and warnings remain intact. Never pour them into beverage bottles, glasses or cups. Confusion with drinks is a known cause of poisoning accidents, especially among children. Store cleaning supplies high or behind a child-proof door, and close bottles immediately after each use. Liquid detergents in small pillows or capsules look like candy to children; they also belong out of reach.

## Home remedies also need respect

Vinegar, soda and citric acid are considered gentle, but are not harmless. Vinegar essence is significantly stronger than household vinegar and irritates skin and eyes. Soda is a strong alkali that dries out the skin and hurts the eyes. Citric acid powder tends to collect dust when measuring. Baking soda is the mildest of the home remedies, but it only has a mild effect. We explain what each remedy can do and where it causes harm in the [Home Remedies](/themen/hausmittel/) section.

## Protect surfaces

Try each product on an inconspicuous area first and let the area dry before judging. Acids attack marble, limestone and other calcareous natural stones, alkalis damage aluminum, and too much water causes wood and laminate to swell. The care label of textiles and the manufacturer's instructions for floors, appliances and furniture take precedence over any home remedy tip.

## If something does happen

- **Swallowed:** Keep calm, do not induce vomiting and rinse your mouth. Call the poison control center immediately and have the packaging ready so you can tell them what product it is.
- **If it gets into eyes:** Rinse immediately with plenty of running water with the lids open for at least ten minutes. Then have a medical examination, and if you use caustic agents, go straight ahead.
- **On the skin:** Remove contaminated clothing and rinse skin thoroughly with water.
- **Vapours inhaled:** Leave the room, go into fresh air and ventilate. Get medical help if you have a cough that doesn't stop or if you have difficulty breathing.

In Germany there are several poison information centers, each of which is responsible for specific federal states and provides advice around the clock. It is best to save the number of the center responsible for you in your phone now. If you lose consciousness, have cramps or are short of breath, you should always call the emergency number **112** immediately.

## When experts are needed

Some things are not your own. Large areas of mold on walls or ceilings should be assessed by a specialist company. A drain that remains blocked despite the suction cup is a case for the plumber, not for the third-party chemical cleaner. And it's better to take valuable textiles, leather, silk or carpets to a dry cleaner before trying them at home permanently damages them.
