A good household guide must be able to do two things: it must work when you are faced with the stain, and it must not harm you or your material. These principles guide the work of the ZEVOK editorial team in every text, from the cleaning plan to the instructions for the limestone edge on the shower wall.

## Written for people

Every guide begins with a question that really comes up in everyday life: Why do towels smell musty after washing? How do you get candle wax out of the tablecloth? Can vinegar be used on natural stone slabs? We write the answer so that you can follow it step by step and explain why a step is necessary. Anyone who understands the principle can act correctly even if their own case is a little different. Each guide is individually planned and checked before publication.

## How the texts are created

We also use AI tools when researching and writing. They do not replace testing: We compare every information about means, temperatures, materials and safety with the basics that are in the next section and with the sources that we cite under each guide. Where a source says something different than a common tip, we follow the source and write why.

## What we rely on

Our guides are based on understandable principles, not hearsay:

- **Care labeling:** the internationally standardized care symbols on textiles (ISO 3758) for washing, bleaching, drying, ironing and professional cleaning
- **Hazard labeling:** the Europe-wide uniform hazard pictograms and signal words according to the CLP regulation that appear on cleaning products
- **Manufacturer information:** Instructions for use of cleaning agents, devices, floor coverings and fittings
- **Consumer information from public authorities:** such as the safe handling of household chemicals, the prevention of poisoning accidents and environmentally conscious washing and cleaning
- **Basics of cleaning chemistry:** how acids dissolve limescale, alkalis attack fat, surfactants loosen dirt from the surface and enzymes break down protein and starch

The most important sources of a guide are listed at the end under “Sources and further information”, with a link and the date of the last check. We mainly link public bodies and specialist associations there: the Federal Environment Agency, the Federal Institute for Risk Assessment, the consumer advice centers, GINETEX Germany for care labeling and Forum Washing.

One idea runs through many of our texts: the interaction of chemistry, mechanics, temperature and time, known in cleaning technology as the Sinner circle. If you save on one factor, such as temperature, you compensate for it with another, such as longer exposure time. In this way, we explain why a method works instead of just giving a recipe.

## Safety first

The same rules apply in every guide that contains cleaning products: never mix cleaners, especially no chlorine-containing products with acids or ammonia, ensure fresh air, wear gloves, test in an inconspicuous area first, follow the care label and manufacturer's instructions and keep products out of the reach of children and pets. Where a task needs to be handled by specialists, such as large areas of mould, valuable textiles or a stubbornly clogged drain, we say this clearly. We have summarized the basic rules on the [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/) page.

## Home remedies honestly evaluated

Vinegar, baking soda, citric acid, soda and gall soap can do a lot, but not everything. We'll tell you what a home remedy is good for, where it reaches its limits and where it causes damage. If a popular tip doesn't deliver what it promises, we'll write about it, no matter how often it's shared. An example: baking soda and vinegar fizz impressively, but largely cancel each other out.

## No made up numbers

We do not make up statistics, studies, percentages or expert quotes. Where information depends on the product, such as the dosage of a detergent or the exposure time of a cleaner, we refer to the packaging instead of giving a number that is not correct for your product. Where rules or offers may change, we ask you to check the latest information from the manufacturer or the responsible authority.

## independence

ZEVOK does not name brands, sell products, take money for mentions, or publish paid posts or product reviews. We write about active ingredients, materials and methods, not about bestseller lists. The website is financed through automatic advertisements from Google AdSense. These ads are separate from our writing, and advertisers have no influence on what we write.

## Language and design

We speak to you directly, explain technical terms the first time they appear and avoid advertising language and promises such as “guaranteed stain-free”. Steps are in the order in which you carry them out, overviews are in tables. Our images and symbols are specially designed for ZEVOK.

## Corrections and updates

We revise guides when recommendations change, when a bug is reported to us, or when we find a better explanation ourselves. Above each guide you can see when it was published or was last revised. This data is real: we do not backdate any text. All publications and revisions are collected on the [Updates](/aktualisierungen/) page. We will quickly correct any legitimate information about errors. Feel free to write to us at [kontakt@zevok.de](mailto:kontakt@zevok.de) if you notice anything. You can find out more about us on the [About Us](/ueber-uns/) page.
