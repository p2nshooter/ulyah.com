There is a dosing table on every detergent package: rows for soft, medium and hard water, columns for lightly, normally and heavily soiled laundry. Two things are almost always missing there: how hard your water is and how much you use if your drum doesn't contain exactly the normal load. That's what this calculator is for.

## The three hardness ranges

In Germany, Section 9 of the Detergents and Cleaning Products Act defines three areas. Your water supplier must tell you at least once a year which applies to your water.

| Hardness range | Calcium carbonate per liter | corresponds approximately |
|---|---|---|
| soft | less than 1.5 mmol | below 8.4 °dH |
| medium | 1.5 to 2.5 mmol | 8.4 to 14 °dH |
| hard | more than 2.5 mmol | over 14 °dH |

## Convert units

| Corresponds to 1 mmol/l calcium carbonate | |
|---|---|
| Degree of German hardness | 5.6°dH |
| Degree of French hardness | 10 °fH |
| Milligrams of calcium carbonate per liter | 100 mg/l |

An example: 16.8 °dH is 3.0 mmol/l, 30 °fH or 300 mg/l. The water is hard.

## This is how you read the dosage table

Find the row for your hardness range and the column for the contamination level. **Lightly** soiled is laundry without visible stains that has only been worn for a short time. **Normal** is laundry with visible dirt or a few light stains. **Strong** is laundry with clearly visible stains, such as kitchen linen, work or children's clothing. For most household laundry, the amount is sufficient for lightly or normally soiled laundry. The number in the table applies to a normal load: for heavy-duty, universal and colored detergents usually 4.5 kg of dry laundry, for delicates and wool detergents usually 2.5 kg. Whatever it says on your package comes first.

## How the calculator takes the load into account

Some of the detergent is used by the water in the machine, no matter how much laundry is in it. That's why you don't just halve the amount when you have half the charge. Forum Washing recommends around two thirds of the specified amount for half a load. The calculator therefore takes a third of the pack quantity as a fixed proportion and allows two thirds to rise or fall with the laundry. With a full normal load this results in exactly the package quantity, with a half load it is two thirds. The result is rounded to 5 ml or 5 g.

| Pack: 75 ml for 4.5 kg | 2kg | 3kg | 4.5kg | 6kg | 7kg | 8kg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amount according to the calculator | 45ml | 60ml | 75ml | 90ml | 105ml | 115ml |

If you have a large, full drum, leave a hand's width of space above the laundry, otherwise it won't get clean. Caps and tabs cannot be divided; one applies per charge.

## Why more doesn't help

Too much detergent doesn't wash cleaner. It doesn't rinse well, can irritate the skin, pollutes wastewater and costs money. In the long run, too little leads to limescale in the machine and laundry and to gray textiles. Today's detergents contain softeners which, according to Forum Waschen, are sufficient up to around 30 °dH. A separate softener is usually unnecessary.
