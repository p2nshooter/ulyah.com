The glass of red wine at dinner with friends, the coffee that spills over the edge of the cup on the train, the tea on the tablecloth at Sunday breakfast, the blueberries on the child's T-shirt: these stains belong to a family. They contain vegetable tanning agents and dyes, and they have one property in common. They often turn out surprisingly well when fresh, but when dried or fixed with heat they become a test of patience.

In this guide you will find out why these stains react differently than grease or blood, what to do in the first few minutes, how to treat white cotton and colorful clothing differently, what to watch out for with coffee with milk and how to get dried stains and tablecloths clean again.

## Why tanning and dye stains react differently

Red wine, coffee, tea and many fruits contain tannins. The name comes from the fact that such plant substances were previously used to tan leather: they like to combine with protein and fibers. Something similar happens in tissue. As long as the stain is wet, the fabrics still sit loosely between the fibers. If it dries up, they stick more firmly, react with the oxygen in the air and darken. Heat accelerates this process. That's why hot water is the wrong choice in the first step, and a dryer makes leftovers permanent.

There are also intense colors, for example from red grapes, blueberries or cherries. Many of these fruit pigments change color depending on the acidity of their environment. This is why a red wine stain in the washing solution sometimes turns bluish or gray. This is not a new stain, but the same dye in a different environment and you can continue to treat it as normal.

The good news: Unlike grease, these stains initially dissolve in water. Clear, cold to lukewarm water is therefore your most important remedy as long as the stain is fresh. Unlike pure protein stains, you can wash them warmer later, as long as the care label allows it.

## Act immediately: the first few minutes

1. **Blot.** Press a clean, light-colored cloth or paper towel onto the stain and absorb as much liquid as possible. Don't rub, otherwise you'll push the dye deeper into the tissue.
2. **Create a base.** Place a folded cloth under the area so that the stain does not migrate into the second layer of fabric or onto the table.
3. **Rinse from the reverse side.** Run the fabric, stain side down, under cold to lukewarm water. This way you can rinse the stain out of the fibers in the shortest possible way.
4. **Keep moist.** If you cannot continue immediately, place a damp cloth on the area or soak the item in cold water. A stain that does not dry remains easier to remove.

A napkin and some water from the carafe are enough on the way. Dab until there is no more absorbent, then wait until you get home to do anything else. The only important thing is that you don't do anything that fixes the stain: no hot water, no hand dryer.

### And what about salt?

Salt on red wine is probably the most well-known household tip. Salt absorbs some of the liquid, similar to paper towels. But it doesn't dissolve the dye. If it stays there for a long time, the stain underneath dries up and if you rub it in, you also scrub the fibers. Our conclusion: Salt only helps to a limited extent; a clean cloth and water are better. White wine is diluting but adds its own sugar and acidity, and mineral water essentially acts like tap water. ## White cotton and linen

White shirts, blouses, napkins and tablecloths made of cotton or linen are robust and can usually withstand more vigorous treatment. Before you get started, look at the care label: If the triangle is crossed out, no bleach is allowed. A triangle with two stripes means only oxygen bleach can be used. The guide [Understanding care symbols] (/ratgeber/pflegesymbole/) explains how to read the remaining symbols.

1. Rinse the stain with cold water as described and gently squeeze the water out.
2. Apply oxygen bleach, for example a stain remover, as described on the package: as a paste on the stain or dissolved as a soaking solution. Wear gloves when doing this as the powder irritates skin and eyes.
3. Leave the bleach on for as long as the package directs, making sure to keep the area moist.
4. Wash the item with a heavy-duty detergent at the highest temperature the label allows. Oxygen bleach works better with heat. Heavy-duty detergents in powder form usually contain bleach, while color and liquid detergents usually do not.
5. Check the area when it is wet. If a shadow is still visible, repeat the treatment instead of drying the part.

An old trick for white cotton actually works: hang the damp, washed item in the sun. The light bleaches out any remaining color. This is not a solution for colorful items because the textile color also fades.

We do not recommend bleaching products containing chlorine. They attack many fibers, can yellow certain materials and must never come into contact with acids or agents containing ammonia. Also keep stain remover in its original packaging and out of the reach of children. Find out more on our page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## Colorful and delicate fabrics

With colored clothing, the greatest risk is that the stain will also affect the color of the fabric. Therefore, before using any product, do a test on a hidden area, such as the inside seam allowance: Apply the product, wait a few minutes, dab it with a white cloth and let it dry. If the cloth discolors or the area becomes lighter, leave the product out.

1. Rinse the stain from the back with cold water.
2. Apply moist gall soap and gently work it into the stain with your fingers. Leave on for a few minutes, but do not let it dry out.
3. Rinse thoroughly and check the result. It often takes two or three passes.
4. Wash the item with color detergent as warm as the label allows.

Stain remover is also available in colorful versions. They are only an option if the label allows bleach and the color test was unremarkable. You can read what gall soap can do and where it reaches its limits in the guide [How to use gall soap correctly] (/ratgeber/gallseife/).

For wool, silk and viscose, just dab carefully with cold water. Stain removers and many enzyme detergents are unsuitable for wool and silk. A red wine stain on a silk blouse or wool coat should be sent to the dry cleaner. Tell me what caused the stain and what you have already tried.

## Coffee with milk and tea with milk: the combination stain

Coffee with milk, cappuccino, latte or tea with milk leave a combination stain. In addition to tannins, it contains milk protein and some fat. This changes the order: protein coagulates when heated and then holds the dye in the tissue. That's why you only start cold here.

1. Rinse the back with cold water until no milk streak can be seen.
2. Dissolve the grease with a little gall soap or a drop of dishwashing liquid and rinse it out again.
3. Wash with a detergent that contains enzymes. Many heavy-duty and colored detergents do this, the information is on the package.
4. If a brownish shade remains on white cotton, oxygen bleaching only now follows.

Sugar in coffee makes the stain sticky, but it is easily soluble in water and comes out when you rinse thoroughly.

## Dried stains

You often only discover the red wine stain from the weekend when you're cleaning up on Monday. Even then, not everything is lost, it just takes more time.

1. **Soak.** Place the item in cold water with a little detergent and leave it for a few hours or overnight. The stain swells and is then easier to work on.
2. **Treat.** Then proceed as described above: gall soap for colored items, oxygen bleach for suitable white items.
3. **Repeat instead of aggravating.** Several gentle passes are more effective than one aggressive agent that ends up damaging the fiber.
4. **Check before drying.** Only when the stain has disappeared when wet and in daylight can the item be put in the dryer or under the iron.

To be honest: Very old stains that have been washed and dried several times cannot always be completely removed. For valuable items, such as an inherited damask tablecloth, textile cleaning is the better address.

## Tablecloths and napkins

Table linen is particularly susceptible to these stains. If possible, take it off as soon as possible after eating, or the same evening at the latest. If you don't have time, soak them in cold water overnight to prevent them from drying out.

If you have a large tablecloth, treat individual stains directly. If there are a lot of them, soak the entire blanket in the bathtub or a large tub with cold water and a little detergent. Then wash white table linen made of cotton or linen with heavy-duty detergent as hot as the label allows, and colored or patterned table linen with colored detergent. Easy-care blankets made from blended fabrics usually tolerate less heat. Washable, coated tablecloths can be wiped with water and a little detergent and only washed if the label allows it.

Only iron when all the stains have disappeared, because the heat of the iron fixes every residue. If the blanket is on a wooden table, wipe the table top dry before the liquid is absorbed.

If the red wine ends up on the carpet, sofa or mattress, the basic problem is different: too much water leaves marks there. The guide [Removing stains from carpets, sofas and mattresses](/ratgeber/flecken-teppich-polster/) shows you how to work with little liquid.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Hot water in the first step:** It fixes tannins and allows the protein to coagulate in coffee with milk. Start cold to lukewarm.
- **Strong rubbing:** The stain becomes larger and the surface becomes rough. Pat and rinse the back.
- **Chlorine bleach on colored items:** In addition to removing the stain, it also removes the color and damages fibers. Stick with oxygen bleach and only if the label allows it.
- **Several remedies at once:** Gall soap, stain remover and vinegar at the same time have no advantage. Take a remedy, rinse and then decide on the next step.
- **Too early in the dryer:** Heat makes residual stains permanent. First check, then dry.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why did my red wine stain turn blue after washing?

The dyes in red wine react to the slightly alkaline wash liquor and change their color. The stain remained the same. Treat it further with gall soap or, for suitable white materials, with oxygen bleach and only dry the part when nothing can be seen anymore.

### Does white wine help against red wine?

It dilutes the stain, nothing more. Plain water does the same thing without introducing additional sugar and acid into the fabric.

### How long do I have?

The sooner you act, the better. There is no fixed point in time from which nothing works anymore. As long as the stain is moist and hasn't been heated, the chances are good. ## Briefly summarized

Red wine, coffee, tea and berries contain tannins and dyes that are easily soluble in water when fresh and become firmer with drying and heat. Blot immediately, rinse the back with cold to lukewarm water and keep the stain moist. Treat white cotton and linen with oxygen bleach if the label allows it, and treat colored cotton with gall soap after a color test. Latte always starts cold. Soak dried stains and treat them in several gentle rounds. Wool, silk and valuable items belong in the textile cleaning department.
