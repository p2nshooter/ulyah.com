For many people, ironing is one of the least favorite household tasks. It takes a long time, you burn your fingers, and in the end the shirt still has new wrinkles on the sleeve. You can save a lot of time if you know the correct order, work at the right temperature and treat the laundry during drying in such a way that some things no longer need to be ironed.

In this guide you will find out what the ironing symbols mean, which tools are worth using, in which order you iron a shirt, how to protect dark fabrics, wool and silk, how to care for your iron and how to keep the pile of ironing smaller.

## The ironing symbols

On the care label, a small iron shows how hot you can iron:

| symbol | Temperature | Typical materials |
|---|---|---|
| Iron with a point | low, to about 110 degrees | Acrylic, polyamide, elastane, some blended fabrics, often without steam |
| Two point iron | medium, up to about 150 degrees | Wool, silk, polyester, viscose |
| Three point iron | high, up to around 200 degrees | Cotton, linen |
| Crossed out iron | do not iron | sensitive synthetic fibers, prints, coatings |

Most irons have the same dots on the temperature control. This means you can adopt the setting directly from the label. All other symbols are explained in the guide [Understanding care symbols] (/ratgeber/pflegesymbole/).

## The right tool

- **Steam iron:** Sufficient for most households. Look for a sole that glides well and a steam boost function for stubborn wrinkles.
- **Steam iron station:** A separate water tank produces significantly more steam. This is worth it if you iron a lot, such as shirts for the whole family or bed linen.
- **Ironing board:** Adjust it to hip height to protect your back and shoulders. A cover with good padding and vapor permeability makes a big difference.
- **Sleeve board:** A small additional board for sleeves and children's clothing.
- **Spray bottle:** To lightly moisten areas that are too dry.
- **Ironing Cloth:** A thin, clean cotton cloth that you place between the iron and delicate fabric.
- **Steam smoother:** A hand-held device that you can use to steam-smooth clothes on the hanger. Ideal for blouses, dresses and quick refreshes, but does not replace ironing shirt collars.

## Preparation: sort and keep moist

1. **Sort by temperature.** Start with the parts that need the lowest temperature and work your way up. An iron heats up quickly but cools down slowly. That way you don't have to wait.
2. **Iron while damp.** Cotton and linen are easiest to straighten when they are still slightly damp. If the laundry is already dry, spray it with water beforehand, roll it up and leave it there for a few minutes.
3. **Check beforehand:** Stains must be completely removed before ironing. Heat fixes them permanently.

## A shirt in six steps

The quickest way to iron a shirt is in a fixed order. This means that parts that have already been smoothed will not be creased again.

1. **Collar:** Lay the collar flat and iron the inside first, then the outside, from the tips to the middle. This means there are no wrinkles at the tips.
2. **Cuffs:** Open the buttons, iron inside and outside.
3. **Sleeves:** Lay the sleeve flat on the board along the seam and iron one side, then turn it over. If you don't want a crease in your sleeve, use the sleeve board.
4. **Shoulder yoke:** Pull the shoulder section over the narrow end of the board and iron on both sides.
5. **Front pieces:** Iron around the buttons, not over them. You can iron the button placket from the inside.
6. **Back:** Finally, do the back in one go.

Immediately hang the shirt on a hanger and fasten the top button and one middle button. Let it cool for a few minutes before hanging it in the closet, otherwise new wrinkles will appear.

## Pants, blouses and bed linen

**Pants:** Iron the pockets and waistband first, preferably from the inside. Then place the legs on top of each other so that the seams line up and iron along the crease. Use an ironing cloth for trousers made of wool or dark fabrics.

**Blouses:** Same as shirts but with lower temperature if made of viscose, polyester or silk. Ruffle and fold with the tip of the iron.

**Bed linen:** Many people don't iron bed linen anymore. If you like it smooth, double the covers and only iron the outside, which halves the work. When shaken out well and folded smoothly, bed linen looks neat even when unironed.

**T-shirts and underwear:** Typically not necessary if hung smoothly.

## Dark fabrics without shiny spots

When ironing dark cotton, wool and many synthetic fabrics, shiny spots can easily appear, especially on seams and pockets. Here's how to avoid them:

- **Iron from the left,** i.e. on the inside.
- **Place an ironing cloth** between the iron and the fabric.
- **Do not iron too hot** and do not press too hard.

Shiny spots that have already appeared can sometimes be softened with steam and a damp ironing cloth by briefly holding the iron over them without pressing.

## Wool, silk and prints

**Wool:** Wool is steamed and pressed, not pushed back and forth. Put on a damp ironing cloth, put the iron on, give it steam and lift it off again. Then the next place. This way the structure is preserved.

**Silk:** You iron silk on a low to medium setting, inside out and preferably with a dry ironing cloth. Drops of water can leave stains on silk, so do not wet it with a spray bottle.

**Prints, sequins and appliqués:** Always iron inside out or leave out completely. Direct heat can melt or peel prints.

More about caring for these materials in the guide [Washing wool, cashmere and silk] (/ratgeber/wolle-und-seide-waschen/).

## Linen, table linen and children's clothing

**Linen** wrinkles badly and requires high temperatures and lots of moisture. Iron it almost wet, from the left for light-colored fabrics and from the right for fabrics that you want to shine. If you like the casual, wrinkled look, simply let linen dry flat on the hanger.

**Tablecloths and napkins** are ironed damp and in large, even strips. Place large tablecloths folded on the board and iron layer by layer so that the fabric does not get creased again when hanging down. Napkins can be folded into shape immediately after ironing.

**Children's clothing** is often made of cotton jersey and rarely needs ironing. For festive occasions, a quick pass with the steamer is sufficient. When it comes to prints and applications, be careful not to touch them directly with the iron.

## Care for the iron

**Avoid limescale:** Limescale clogs the steam nozzles and can land on laundry as white flakes. Read the operating instructions to find out which water your device can tolerate. Many manufacturers recommend a mixture of tap water and distilled water for hard water; some devices are expressly designed for tap water. Many irons have a self-cleaning function or a removable limescale cartridge.

**Clean the soleplate:** Unplug the iron and let the iron cool down. Wipe the sole with a damp cloth and a little dishwashing liquid. For burnt-in residues, there are special cleaning pens for soleplates that you can use according to the instructions. Steel wool, scouring milk and knives scratch the coating.

**Pour out water:** Empty the tank after ironing if the instructions recommend it. This means less deposits form.

## Iron less: this is how it works

- **Spin correctly:** Only spin shirts and blouses at a low speed so they wrinkle less.
- **Hang up immediately:** Do not leave laundry lying in the machine.
- **Shake out and smooth** before hanging.
- **Dry shirts on irons** then a quick ironing of the collar and cuffs is often enough.
- **Open the dryer in good time:** Take out the laundry immediately after the end of the program and fold it flat.
- **Buy easy-iron fabrics** if you really hate ironing.

## Security

- When taking breaks, always place the iron on the surface, never with the soleplate on the board.
- Do not let the cord hang down when children or pets are nearby.
- After ironing, unplug the iron and let it cool down out of the reach of children.
- A damaged cable or a device that leaks water should no longer be used.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Start with high temperature:** Then you have to wait for the iron to cool down. Always from low to high.
- **Iron stains:** Heat fixes them permanently.
- **Iron dark fabrics from the right side:** Shiny spots will appear.
- **Push wool:** Steam and press instead of rubbing.
- **Spray silk with water:** Water stains remain.
- **Clean the sole with abrasive cleaners:** The coating will be scratched.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do white flakes come out of the iron?

This is mostly lime from the water. Descale the device according to the manufacturer's instructions and use the recommended water. If the water is very hard, a mixture with distilled water will help, if the instructions allow it.

### What do I do if I singe something?

Slight yellowish singe stains on white cotton can sometimes be reduced with a wash and a little oxygen bleach. If the fibers are burned or melted, the damage is permanent.

### Do I have to iron bed linen?

No. Ironed bed linen feels smoother, but it is not more hygienic if it has been washed properly. When hung smoothly and folded neatly, it looks neat even without ironing.

### How do I iron a shirt without an ironing board?

In an emergency, place it on a thick towel on the table that you have previously tested for heat resistance. A steam straightener or iron in the steamy bathroom is an alternative when you're on the go. You can find further instructions in the [Laundry & Care](/themen/waesche-und-pflege/) section.

## Briefly summarized

Read the ironing symbols and start with the items that need the lowest temperature. Iron cotton and linen slightly damp. The quickest way to make a shirt is in the order of collar, cuffs, sleeves, shoulder yoke, front, back. Iron dark fabrics inside out or with an ironing cloth, steam and press wool, dry silk and inside out. Only care for the sole with gentle products, avoid limescale with the recommended water and save yourself a lot of work by shaking out laundry well and hanging it up smoothly.
