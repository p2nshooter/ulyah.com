White powder, similar names, similar packages: baking soda, soda and baking powder are constantly confused. In many cleaning tips they appear as interchangeable, as if you could simply use one or the other depending on what you have on hand. In fact, they are significantly different. Baking soda is a mild home remedy, soda is a powerful degreaser that can irritate skin and eyes, and baking powder is used for baking and is usually just an expensive substitute for baking soda when cleaning.

In this guide you will find out what is behind the three powders, what each is suitable for in the household, how to dose it, which materials it is not compatible with and what dangerous mix-ups you should definitely avoid.

## The three powders at a glance

| | Baking soda | Soda | Baking powder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chemical | Sodium bicarbonate | Sodium carbonate | Baking soda plus acidulant and anti-caking agent |
| Strength | mildly basic | strongly basic | almost neutral, reacts when moistened |
| Typical use | gentle scrubbing, smells, burnt residue | Grease, detergent booster, water softener | Baking |
| Skin contact | mostly unproblematic | lovely, wear gloves | unproblematic |

You may know **baking soda** as baking soda or emperor baking soda. It is only slightly alkaline in solution and acts as a fine, mild abrasive as a powder or paste.

**Soda** is available as pure soda, washing soda or crystal soda. It is significantly more alkaline than baking soda. You will usually find a warning on the packaging because soda can cause serious eye irritation.

**Baking powder** consists of baking soda, an acidulant and starch. As soon as it gets wet, the baking soda and acid react with each other and form carbon dioxide to make the dough rise. There isn't much effect left for cleaning afterwards.

## Attention: Baking soda is not caustic soda

A dangerous confusion concerns **caustic soda**. Behind this is sodium hydroxide, a highly corrosive substance that is contained in some drain cleaners, for example. It can cause severe burns to the skin and eyes. It has nothing in common with baking soda other than the similarity in name. When purchasing, pay attention to the exact name and danger symbols on the packaging. Our page [Safety when cleaning] (/sicherheit-beim-putzen/) explains what the symbols mean.

## Baking soda: the gentle all-round remedy

### As a paste against deposits

A paste of about **three parts baking soda and one part water** is a mild abrasive. It is suitable for:

- **gray cement joints,** as described in the guide [Cleaning tile joints] (/ratgeber/fliesenfugen-reinigen/),
- **burnt-on leftovers in the oven** that soften under the paste overnight. The complete instructions can be found in the guide [Cleaning the oven] (/ratgeber/backofen-reinigen/),
- **Tea and coffee edges** in cups and pots,
- **light dirt** on ceramics, enamel and stainless steel.

Apply the paste, let it work, rub with a soft sponge and rinse thoroughly with clean water. Otherwise, baking soda residues will dry up as a white haze.

### Burnt pots

Cover the bottom of the pot with water, add two to three tablespoons of baking soda and bring to the boil briefly. Then let it cool and soak for a few hours. The residue is then usually removed with a wooden spatula or a soft sponge. This works well with stainless steel and enamel. Aluminum can turn dark due to alkaline agents, which is why you should only treat aluminum pots carefully and briefly.

### Against smells

Baking soda can dull some smells, especially sour ones. An open bowl in the fridge or some baking soda in trainers overnight are simple experiments. You shouldn't expect miracles. Eliminating the cause is always more effective, such as removing spoiled food or washing shoes.

**On carpets** you can sprinkle baking soda thinly, let it work for a few hours and then vacuum thoroughly. The carpet must be dry. First treat damp stains according to the instructions in the guide [Removing stains from carpet and upholstery] (/ratgeber/flecken-teppich-polster/).

### Tarnished silver

Line a bowl with aluminum foil, put the silver in it, sprinkle baking soda over it and pour in hot water. The dark coating is transformed back through a chemical reaction. Then rinse the silver and dry it well. The method is not suitable for silver with deliberately blackened decorations, with pearls, glued-in stones or sensitive antiques.

## Soda: the powerful grease remover

Soda is significantly stronger than baking soda. That's why stricter rules apply to them: **Wear gloves, avoid splashes in the eyes and store out of the reach of children.**

**Grease in the kitchen:** A solution of one to two tablespoons of soda in a liter of warm water will dissolve grease on tiles, stainless steel extractor filters and crusty baking sheets. Then wipe with clean water.

**Laundry:** Soda can serve as a **detergent booster**, for example for heavily soiled cotton work or kitchen linen. It also binds some of the hardness agents in the water. Stick to the dosage on the package and only use soda for robust, white or colorfast fabrics. The guide [Dosing detergent] (/ratgeber/waschmittel-dosieren/) explains how to use detergent correctly.

**Drains:** A hot soda solution can loosen light grease buildup in the kitchen drain. It doesn't help against hair and solid blockages. Never combine them with other drain cleaners.

### What soda doesn't tolerate

| material | What happens |
|---|---|
| Aluminum | Surface is attacked and discolored |
| wool and silk | Protein fibers are damaged |
| Painted, oiled or waxed surfaces | Protective layer is attacked |
| Linoleum and some plastics | can become dull or spotty |
| skin and eyes | Irritation, so wear gloves and be careful |

You can find out more about sensitive fibers in the guide [Washing wool and silk] (/ratgeber/wolle-und-seide-waschen/).

## Baking powder: better in the cake

Baking powder contains baking soda, but also acidulants and starch. When moistened, baking soda and acid react with each other, leaving less effective baking soda. When cleaning, baking powder does at most what baking soda can do, only weaker and more expensive. If a tip recommends baking powder, use baking soda instead.

## Why baking soda and vinegar aren't stronger together

Baking soda and vinegar fizz impressively when mixed. This looks like cleaning power, but it is above all a neutralization: the acid and the base cancel each other out. What's left is essentially water with some salt and carbon dioxide. The bubbling can loosen loose deposits somewhat, but it will not loosen a clogged pipe or stubborn limescale. The gas also builds up pressure in a sealed bottle.

If you want to remove limescale, use an acid such as vinegar or citric acid. If you want to remove grease, use dish soap, baking soda or soda. Both at the same time don't help. More about such tips can be found in the guide [Home Remedies Myths Checked](/ratgeber/hausmittel-mythen/). The acids are presented in the guides [Vinegar in the household](/ratgeber/essig-im-haushalt/) and [Citric acid in the household](/ratgeber/zitronensaeure/).

## Which powder for which task?

| task | Best choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Gray joints, tar edges, light crusts | Baking soda as a paste | mild, scrubs gently, protects surfaces |
| Burnt remains in the oven | Baking soda overnight, if there is a lot of fat soda | Paste softens crusts |
| Greasy tiles and stainless steel filters | soda in warm water | dissolves fat much more strongly |
| Heavily soiled cotton laundry | Soda as a reinforcer according to package instructions | supports the detergent |
| Limescale on fittings and glass | none of the three | Lime needs acid |
| Musty shoes or refrigerator | Baking soda | can reduce odors |

This overview is particularly helpful when a tip from the Internet simply mentions “baking soda or soda”. If in doubt, always start with baking soda and only use soda if grease is the main cause and the material can tolerate it.

## Store properly

- **Store in the original packaging**, tightly closed and dry. Moisture causes the powders to clump together.
- **Label** when transferring into cans. White powders look very similar.
- **Store separately from food** if it is cleaning soda.
- **Out of reach of children,** especially soda.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Confusing baking soda with caustic soda:** Dangerous to life for skin and eyes. Pay attention to the exact name.
- **Soda without gloves:** The skin becomes irritated and dries out.
- **Soda on aluminum, wool or painted wood:** Damage cannot be reversed.
- **Baking soda against lime:** Baking soda is basic and does not dissolve lime.
- **Mix baking soda and vinegar:** The effect largely cancels out.
- **Do not rinse off powder residue:** White haze and edges remain.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use baking soda from the baking department for cleaning?

Yes. Baking soda and household baking soda are chemically the same substance. For larger quantities, baking soda is also available in larger packs, which are usually cheaper per kilogram.

### Does baking soda help in the washing machine?

A tablespoon of baking soda in the detergent compartment can reduce slight odors. Soda is significantly more effective as a detergent booster. If the machine itself smells, the cause is usually in the seal, dispenser compartment or lint filter. The guide [Cleaning the washing machine] (/ratgeber/waschmaschine-reinigen/) explains how to clean it.

### Is soda bad for the environment?

Soda is a simple mineral substance and is well tolerated in wastewater in normal quantities. Nevertheless, as with all cleaners, only use as much as is necessary.

### How long do baking soda and soda last?

Both powders last a long time if stored dry and well sealed. Baking soda gradually loses its effectiveness due to moisture and clumps together. Clumpy soda can usually still be used if you dissolve it in warm water.

### Which is better for the oven: baking soda or soda?

For most ovens, baking soda as an overnight paste is sufficient. Soda dissolves thicker fatty crusts more quickly, but requires gloves and more caution. Both are suitable for enamel, but neither is suitable for aluminum parts.

## Briefly summarized

Baking soda is a mild, alkaline powder that gently scrubs as a paste, dissolves burnt-in residues and reduces some odors. Soda is significantly stronger, dissolves grease powerfully and supports laundry, but requires gloves and is not compatible with aluminum, wool, silk and painted surfaces. Baking powder belongs in the cake, not in the cleaning bucket. Never confuse baking soda with caustic soda, do not mix it with vinegar and always use an acid for lime.
