The toilet is one of those places you like to do quickly. A squirt of cleaner, once with the brush, rinse, done. That's enough for everyday life, but not long-term. Deposits form under the rim of the sink, a yellowish-brown ring appears at the water line, and at some point there is a smell even though cleaning is done regularly. The reason is almost always **urine stone**: a hard, rough deposit to which bacteria and odors adhere particularly well.

Urine stone can be removed if you know how it forms and what dissolves it. In this guide you will learn how to thoroughly clean the toilet step by step, how to remove urine scale and limescale with the right product and enough time to react, what the cistern, flush rim and toilet brush are all about and how to prevent new deposits.

## What is urine stone?

Urine contains dissolved minerals. When it comes into contact with lime-containing dishwashing water and dries up, solid deposits of lime and other mineral salts form. They prefer to settle where water stands or drains slowly: **under the sink rim, at the water line and at the bowl drain**.

Urine stone is initially barely visible, then becomes yellowish and finally brown. Its rough surface provides support for bacteria and retains odors. The older and thicker it is, the more difficult it will be to remove.

The good news: Like lime, urine scale dissolves with **acid**. Alkaline cleaners, scouring agents or chlorine products hardly help.

## Preparation and safety

- **Wear gloves.** Acidic toilet cleaners irritate the skin, and it makes hygienic sense anyway.
- **Ventilate.** Open the window or turn on the fan.
- **Use your own towels.** Use towels for the toilet that you don't use anywhere else in the household, for example in your own color.
- **Only one product at a time.** Acidic toilet cleaners must never be mixed with cleaners containing chlorine because this creates toxic chlorine gas. If you want to change, rinse thoroughly several times in between. The most important rules are on our page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).
- **Avoid splashes.** Brushing with acid may cause drops to get into your eyes. Work calmly and not too vigorously.

## Clean the toilet step by step

### 1. Add cleaner to bowl

First, put toilet cleaner under the rim and into the bowl so that it can work while you clean the rest.

### 2. From outside to inside and from top to bottom

Wipe with a damp cloth and some bathroom cleaner in this order:

1. **flush button and cistern** outside,
2. **lid** top and bottom,
3. **Glasses** above and below,
4. **Hinges** and the places in between,
5. **Outside of bowl** and base to floor.

This means you don't carry dirt from the lower, more heavily loaded areas to the upper ones.

**Tip:** Many toilet seats can be removed in one move. Then you can reach the hinges and the area underneath much better.

### 3. Brush the inside of the bowl

Brush the inside of the bowl thoroughly, especially under the rim. That's where most of the dirt sits and you don't see it. A small mirror or a look with your cell phone shows what it looks like there. Then rinse.

### 4. Floor and wall around the toilet

Fine splashes of urine land on the floor, on the wall and in the joints around the toilet. This is often the root cause of smell. Wipe these surfaces regularly with cleaner and fresh water.

## Remove urine stone

If urine stone is visible, daily brushing is not enough. Now you need acid, time and a little trick.

### Lower the water level

The water in the bowl will dilute any cleaner. So that the acid can work directly on the surface, lower the water level:

- **Pump with the toilet brush:** Push the brush firmly into the drain several times. This is how some of the water drains away.
- **Skip the water:** Using an old cup and gloves, scoop the remaining water into a bucket.
- **Close the inlet:** If there is an angle valve for the cistern, you can turn it off and flush once. Then the bowl won't fill up again.

### The right means

| Means | Effect | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Acid toilet cleaner (gel) | good, sticks to the edge | Pay attention to the label, never combine with chlorine |
| Citric acid | good for limescale and light urine scale | Apply as a concentrated solution or powder |
| Vinegar essence | good, smells strong | only diluted on sensitive surfaces, gloves |
| Chlorine cleaner | Hardly dissolves urine stone | bleaches and disinfects, but does not replace acid |

How to dose citric acid can be found in the guide [Citric acid in the household] (/ratgeber/zitronensaeure/). You can read more about vinegar and vinegar essence in [Vinegar in the Household](/ratgeber/essig-im-haushalt/).

### Let it take effect

1. **Apply the product directly onto the surface**. With citric acid, you can sprinkle the powder on the damp areas or use a highly concentrated solution.
2. **For areas above the water line** Soak kitchen paper with the solution and place on top. This way the acid stays where it is supposed to work.
3. **Let it take effect for several hours,** or overnight for thick layers. Do not use the toilet during this time, preferably with a note on the lid.
4. **Brush vigorously,** then open the valve again and rinse.
5. **Repeat** if there are any leftovers. Old urine stone often comes off in several passes, layer by layer.

**No metal or abrasive cleaners.** Wire brushes, steel wool, knives or coarse scouring pads will scratch the glaze. New dirt and urine scale settles in the scratches even more quickly. Pumice stones are also sometimes recommended, but they can also damage a glaze.

## Cistern and flush rim

**Cigarette:** If the water is hard, the valves and seals in the cistern become calcified. This is reflected in a flush that runs or is weak. Special cistern descalers are tailored to the components. Strong acids or chlorine products in the cistern can attack rubber seals. Therefore, stick to the manufacturer’s instructions.

**Flushing rim:** On toilets with a flushing rim, there are small openings under the rim from which the flushing water flows. If they are calcified, the bowl will no longer be washed properly. Treat the edge with gel or paper towels as described above. **Rimless toilets** have no hidden rim and are much easier to keep clean.

**Toilet stones and cistern tabs** provide scent and color. They do not replace cleaning, they pollute wastewater, and some contain chlorine, which you should not combine with acidic cleaners.

## Don't forget the toilet brush

The toilet brush is one of the dirtiest utensils in the household.

- **Drain after use:** Briefly clamp the wet brush between the glasses and the bowl before placing it in the holder.
- **Empty and clean the holder regularly:** Standing water in the holder quickly smells and becomes a source of germs.
- **Clean the brush from time to time:** Place it in the filled bowl with a little toilet cleaner, let it work, rinse and let it dry.
- **Replace in a timely manner:** If the bristles are bent or discolored, it's time for a new one.

## Does the toilet need to be disinfected?

Disinfection is usually not necessary in a normal household. Thorough cleaning with toilet cleaner and regular wiping of the contact surfaces are sufficient. Things can look different if someone in the household has a contagious gastrointestinal infection. Then it may make sense to temporarily treat the flush button, glasses and door handles with a suitable disinfectant according to the instructions. Follow the instructions provided by your doctor’s office.

## Prevent urine stone

- **Brush briefly daily.** Fresh deposits can be easily removed.
- **Once a week** add toilet cleaner under the rim and allow it to take effect.
- **Use while sitting.** This significantly reduces splashes on the edge, floor and wall.
- **Rinse after use.** Urine that sits for a long time leaves more deposits.
- **If the water is hard**, use an acidic cleaner more often.

## How often which task?

| task | How often |
|---|---|
| Brush the inside of the bowl | daily or as needed |
| Wipe glasses, lid and flush button | several times a week |
| Thorough cleaning with toilet cleaner | weekly |
| Mop the floor and wall around the toilet | weekly |
| Treat urine stone specifically | with visible deposits |
| Clean brush holder | weekly to every two weeks |

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Combine acid and chlorine:** Dangerous to life. Always use only one agent.
- **Rinse the cleaner away immediately:** No agent will work without exposure time.
- **Metal or scouring powder:** The glaze will be damaged and urine scale will settle more quickly.
- **Clean the bowl only:** Odors often come from the floor, walls and joints around the toilet.
- **Let acid drip onto natural stone floors:** Marble and limestone are attacked. Wipe off drips immediately with water.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Cola help against urine stone?

Cola contains some phosphoric acid, but far too little for solid urine stone. In addition, sugar remains. You can read what's behind such tips in the guide [Home Remedies Myths Checked](/ratgeber/hausmittel-mythen/).

### Why does the toilet smell even though it looks clean?

Urine stone often sits under the flush rim where it cannot be seen, or urine splashes are embedded in the joints and floor around the toilet. Check both locations and the brush holder.

### How do I get rid of limescale on the waterline?

Just like urine stone: lower the water level, apply acid, let it take effect and brush. You can find out more about limescale in the bathroom in the guide [Removing limescale in the shower] (/ratgeber/kalk-in-der-dusche/).

## Briefly summarized

Clean the toilet from the outside in and from top to bottom and don't forget the flush button, hinges, floor and wall. Urine scale consists of mineral deposits and only dissolves with acid. To do this, lower the water level, apply acidic toilet cleaner, citric acid or diluted vinegar essence, let it work for several hours and then brush vigorously. Avoid metal and coarse abrasives and never mix acid with chlorine. Daily brushing, weekly leave-in and sitting while urinating effectively prevent new deposits.
