In hardly any other room is hard water more evident than in the shower. After a few days, the glass wall is covered in white drip edges, the faucet loses its shine, and water suddenly sprays out of the shower head in all directions. What sticks there is usually a mixture of lime and soap residue, the so-called **lime soap**. It gets thicker with every shower and becomes increasingly difficult to remove.

Lime dissolves reliably with acid. The key is to dose the acid correctly, let it work long enough, but not too long, and protect sensitive materials. In this guide you will find out which products are suitable, how to descale glass walls, fittings, shower heads, tiles and shower trays, what you need to pay attention to with natural stone and coated surfaces and how you can hardly give lime a chance in everyday life.

## What helps against limescale?

Lime is a mineral that only dissolves with **acid**. Alkaline products such as baking soda or soda help against grease and soapy dirt, but not against limescale.

| Means | Typical application | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Acid bathroom cleaner or limescale cleaner | Glass, fittings, tiles | easy to dose, exposure time according to label |
| household vinegar | Glass, fittings, shower head | smells strong, use diluted or pure |
| Vinegar essence | stubborn lime | strongly acidic, always dilute, wear gloves |
| Citric acid | Shower head, fittings, glass | low odor, dissolve in warm, not hot water |

**Household vinegar** usually has around five percent acidity and can be diluted with water for light deposits or used pure for heavier deposits. **Vinegar essence** is significantly more concentrated and should only be diluted on surfaces. How to use vinegar safely can be found in the guide [Vinegar in the Household](/ratgeber/essig-im-haushalt/). For **citric acid**, about two tablespoons of powder in half a liter of warm water is enough. More about this in the guide [Citric acid in the household] (/ratgeber/zitronensaeure/).

**Important:** Acid must never come into contact with cleaners containing chlorine because dangerous chlorine gas can be formed. Only use one product at a time and rinse thoroughly with water before using another. You can find all the rules on our page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## First check: What materials do you have?

Before you apply acid, it's worth taking a quick look at the materials in your shower:

- **Natural stone** such as marble, travertine, limestone or Solnhofen slabs themselves contain lime. Acid attacks the surface and leaves dull, rough spots. Only pH-neutral or special natural stone cleaners are permitted here.
- **Cement joints** between tiles are also sensitive to acid. Short contact is usually unproblematic, but long exposure times and frequent use slowly dissolve the joints.
- **Coated Glass:** Many shower walls have a factory coating that makes water roll off better. Abrasives and some aggressive cleaners can damage them. The manufacturer's care instructions take precedence.
- **Fittings with a special surface:** Chrome-plated fittings tolerate acid with a short exposure time. Matt black, gold, brushed or painted surfaces are more delicate. The manufacturer’s information also applies here.
- **Enamelled tubs and shower trays** become dull if exposed to acid frequently and for a long time. **Acrylic** is sensitive to scratches and does not tolerate abrasive cleaners.

If you are unsure, test the product on an inconspicuous area.

## Descale the glass shower wall

1. **ventilate** and put on gloves.
2. **Apply agent:** Spray limescale cleaner or diluted vinegar evenly onto the glass, from bottom to top, so that there are no running marks that will take effect faster than the rest.
3. **Let it sit,** usually five to ten minutes, or as long as the label states. The product must not dry.
4. **Wipe the surface with a soft sponge or microfiber cloth**. Scratching sponges and scouring milk are taboo.
5. **Rinse thoroughly with clean water,** preferably with a hand shower.
6. **Pull off with the squeegee** and wipe the edges with a dry cloth.

**For thick layers** the compress method helps: Soak kitchen paper or an old cloth with descaling solution and place it on the affected areas. The paper keeps the acid where it is supposed to work. After about 30 minutes, remove, wipe and rinse. If necessary, it is better to repeat the process rather than extending the exposure time significantly.

**Profiles and seals:** Limescale and dirt collect in the metal rails and rubber seals of the shower door. An old toothbrush with a little cleaner can reach these areas. Rinse the rubber seals well afterwards so that no acid remains in them.

## Make fittings shine again

- **Apply the agent to a cloth,** do not spray directly onto the fitting. This means less acid runs down joints and silicone.
- **Let it take effect briefly** and then wipe off. With chrome, a few minutes are usually enough.
- **Treat the edges of the lever and the spout** with a soft toothbrush.
- **Rinse well and polish dry.** Only after drying will you see whether any lime remains.

Limescale is often found on the **aerator**, the sieve at the spout of the sink. It can usually be unscrewed and soaked in a cup of descaling solution.

## Descale the shower head

A calcified shower head sprays unevenly and the water pressure appears weaker. Many models have soft rubber knobs on the nozzles. If there is light limescale, all you need to do is rub your finger over the nubs and the limescale will crumble off.

**Removable shower head:**

1. Unscrew the shower head from the hose.
2. Place in a bowl with vinegar or citric acid solution so that the nozzle plate is covered.
3. Leave on for about 30 to 60 minutes.
4. Clean the nozzles with a brush and rinse the shower head thoroughly.

**Fixed overhead shower:** Pour the solution into a sturdy freezer bag, place it over the shower head and secure it with a rubber band. After the exposure time, remove, brush off the nozzles and let the water run for a minute.

For particularly coated or gold-plated shower heads, you should check the instructions before placing them in acid.

## Tiles and shower tray

**Tiles:** Glazed wall tiles tolerate acidic bathroom cleaners well. Moisten the **cement joints with water first**, then they will absorb less acid. Then wipe thoroughly. The guide [cleaning tile grouting] (/ratgeber/fliesenfugen-reinigen/) explains how you can get gray or stained grouting bright again.

**Shower tray and tub:** Limescale edges on ceramics and enamel can be removed with limescale cleaner and a soft sponge. With acrylic you also only use soft sponges. The guide [cleaning sinks and bathtubs] (/ratgeber/waschbecken-badewanne-reinigen/) tells you which agents can tolerate which material.

**Natural stone:** The only thing that helps here is prevention, regular stripping and a special natural stone cleaner. Limescale that has already set in should be taken into the hands of a specialist company if water and a soft cloth are not enough.

## This prevents limescale from forming in the first place

The most effective method against limescale only costs one minute after every shower:

- **Stripping:** Remove the glass wall and tiles with a squeegee after showering. If it doesn't dry, it won't form edges.
- **Wipe the faucet dry:** A cloth on a hook in the shower makes this a habit.
- **Ventilate:** Moisture has to get out, this also helps against mold. You can read how to prevent mold in the guide [Preventing and removing mold in the bathroom] (/ratgeber/schimmel-im-bad/).
- **Little shower gel and shampoo on the walls:** Soap residue combines with lime to form lime soap. Rinse off any splashes immediately with the hand shower.
- **Once a week** Wipe the glass and faucet with bathroom cleaner. Thin deposits then disappear in just a few minutes.

## How often which task?

| task | How often |
|---|---|
| Remove glass wall and tiles | after every shower |
| Wipe the fitting dry | after every shower or daily |
| Wipe the glass, faucet and shower tray with bathroom cleaner | weekly |
| Descale the shower head and aerator | every one to three months, more often if the water is hard |
| Clean the shower door profiles and seals | monthly |

If the water is very hard or a shower that several people use every day, the intervals are shortened. Go by what you see: As soon as a white veil appears, it is the right time.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Acid on natural stone:** The surface becomes permanently matt. Use a natural stone cleaner.
- **Allow agent to dry:** Dried acid leaves stains. Work in sections and keep the area moist.
- **Leave for too long:** More time does not mean more cleanliness, but rather more risk to joints and surfaces.
- **Scouring milk on glass and fittings:** It leaves fine scratches in which new limescale settles particularly well.
- **Combine acid and chlorine:** Dangerous to life. Only use one product at a time and rinse thoroughly in between.
- **Do not rinse:** Acid residues continue to attack seals, silicone and joints.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does baking powder or baking soda help against limescale?

Not really. Baking soda is alkaline and does not dissolve limescale. As a mild scouring agent it can loosen soapy dirt, but you need an acid to remove real limescale marks. You can read what else baking soda can do in the guide [soda, soda and baking powder] (/ratgeber/natron-soda-backpulver/).

### How do I get old, milky stains off glass?

Apply compresses with descaling solution several times and rinse in between. If the glass still remains milky, the surface may already be damaged. This is possible with very old lime or after frequent scrubbing and can no longer be completely remedied.

### Can descaler be used on silicone joints?

Short contact usually does not harm silicone. Nevertheless, rinse the joints thoroughly, as residues of acid and soap form a film on which mold tends to settle. If the silicone is already dark in color, descaler will not help.

### Is it worth impregnating the shower wall?

For uncoated glass, a glass sealant from a specialist retailer can improve beading. However, it does not replace daily removal and must be renewed according to the manufacturer's instructions. If the glass is already coated, it is better to ask the manufacturer.

## Briefly summarized

Limescale and limescale soap in the shower dissolve with acid, i.e. with acidic bathroom cleaner, vinegar or citric acid. Check the materials beforehand: Natural stone does not tolerate acid, cement joints and special fitting surfaces only tolerate it for a short time. Apply the product, let it work for a few minutes, wipe with a soft sponge and rinse thoroughly. Treat stubborn areas with compresses, place the shower head in solution or cover it with a bag. Never mix acid with chlorine cleaners. If you wipe off after every shower and wipe briefly once a week, you hardly have to descale anymore.
