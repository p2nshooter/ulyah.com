Saturday morning, the sun is shining outside and you've been standing in the apartment with the bucket for hours because the bathroom, kitchen and floors are due at the same time. Or it works the other way around: you're constantly cleaning a little, but you never know when the fridge was last cleaned. Both have the same cause: there is a lack of overview of what needs to be done and when.

A cleaning plan creates exactly this overview. He distributes the work so that nothing is neglected and nothing is done twice, and he relieves you of the constant thinking. In this guide, you first collect all the tasks room by room, arrange them according to frequency and use them to build a plan that suits your household, whether you live alone, with children or in a shared apartment. We explain how you can complete the individual tasks yourself quickly and in the most sensible order in the guide [Cleaning Efficiently](/ratgeber/putzen-reihenfolge/).

## Step 1: Collect all tasks room by room

Before you divide anything into days of the week, you need a complete list. With a piece of paper or a cell phone, go through the apartment room by room from the apartment door, from the hallway to the balcony and storage room, and write down what happens there.

Three questions will help you in every room:

- **What is used here every day and gets dirty?** In the kitchen it is the sink, work surface and stove, in the bathroom it is the sink, shower and toilet.
- **Where is something slowly accumulating?** Dust on shelves, grease on the cupboard doors next to the stove, limescale on the shower wall, fingerprints on light switches and door handles.
- **What is hardly noticeable but still needs to be done at some point?** Windows, curtains, the inside of cupboards, the top of wall cupboards, the floor behind the sofa or washing machine.

Formulate each task so specifically that you can check it off. “Cleaning the kitchen” is not a task, but a project. “Clear out the refrigerator and wipe it out” or “clean the grease filter on the extractor hood,” on the other hand, is clear. The more clearly a task is described, the easier it is to plan, estimate and delegate to other people.

## Step 2: Set frequencies

Not every task is equally urgent. The following overview is a suggestion for an average household, not a law. If you cook a lot, you need shorter distances in the kitchen, and a bathroom for two people requires less maintenance than one for five. For devices such as extractor hoods, dishwashers or refrigerators, the information in the operating instructions takes precedence.

| area | Daily | Weekly | Monthly | Semi-annually or annually |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kitchen | Wipe down the sink and work surface, do the dishes, and briefly clean the hob after cooking | Thoroughly clean the stove and fronts, clean the inside of the microwave, empty and wipe out the trash can, change the dishcloths | Clean out and wipe out the refrigerator, sieves in the dishwasher, grease filters depending on use, oven if necessary | Wipe inside of cupboards, top of wall cupboards, defrost freezer compartment if ice forms |
| bathroom | Ventilate, remove the shower screen, briefly wipe the sink | Change toilet, sink, shower or bathtub, mirror, floor, towels | Hair strainer, fan grille, wash out trash can, toothbrush cup, soap dispenser, shower curtain | Thoroughly clean joints, inside cabinets, check silicone joints for dark spots |
| Living and sleeping rooms | ventilate, put things back in their place | Dust, vacuum, linen every one to two weeks | Vacuum radiators, light switches and door handles, upholstery | Windows, curtains, behind and under furniture, lamps |
| hallway | Put shoes away | Vacuum and mop the floor, shake out the doormat | Inside apartment door, wardrobe, mirror | Wipe out the shoe cabinet and wash the doormat if the material allows it |
| Soils in general | Sweep away crumbs in the kitchen and dining area | Vacuum all rooms and wet mop to suit the type of floor | Skirting boards and corners | Care according to manufacturer's instructions, clean carpets thoroughly |

The daily tasks are short tasks, not cleaning tasks. If you remove the shower wall after showering and wipe the work surface after cooking, you don't have to scrub on the weekend. Semi-annual tasks, on the other hand, are easily overlooked. Put it in your calendar right away with a specific month instead of trying to do it “sometime in the spring.”

## Step 3: Distribute the tasks throughout the week

There are two proven models for weekly tasks. Both work, what matters is what suits your everyday life.

**Model A: one area per day.** A possible week looks like this:

1. **Monday:** Bathroom complete
2. **Tuesday:** Dust in living rooms and bedrooms
3. **Wednesday:** Kitchen thoroughly, wipe out trash can
4. **Thursday:** Wash bed linen and towels
5. **Friday:** vacuum and mop all floors
6. **Saturday:** a monthly task
7. **Sunday:** free

**Model B: a fixed appointment.** You complete all of the weekly tasks in one evening or morning in one go and only keep up with the daily tasks for the rest of the week. This suits small apartments and people who prefer to get started.

It's best to spread the monthly tasks over the four weeks of a month instead of putting them all on one day. For example: in the first week the refrigerator, in the second radiators, light switches and door handles, in the third the monthly list in the bathroom, in the fourth oven and dishwasher sieves. This means that there is only one manageable additional task per week.

## Variants for different households

### Single household

There is less dirt on your own, and no one is responsible for that. Two short appointments per week are often enough, for example bathroom and kitchen on Wednesday evening, dust and floors on Saturday morning. More important than a full weekly schedule is remembering the rare tasks, because they are the ones that are easiest to forget. A recurring calendar entry for every monthly and half-yearly task solves this.

### Family with children

With children, the work in the kitchen, hallway and bathroom increases, and there is also significantly more laundry. Divide the tasks visibly among the adults instead of silently assuming that “it will be sorted out.” Children can help depending on their age. The age information in the table is only a rough guide; you know best what your child can do.

| Dude | Appropriate tasks | What you pay attention to |
|---|---|---|
| Kindergarten age | Put toys in boxes, sort socks in pairs, wipe table with a damp cloth | just water, no cleaners; Plan with images instead of text |
| Primary school age | Set and clear the table, dust low surfaces, arrange shoes in the hallway, fold laundry | Do the task together once, then alone |
| From about ten years old | Vacuum, empty the dishwasher, separate the garbage and take it out, clean the sink with a mild cleaner | state clearly when a task is considered completed |
| teenagers | Completely own room, alternating bathroom, mopping floors, washing your own laundry according to the label | Explain cleaners and what you should never mix |

Strong products such as toilet cleaners, oven cleaners or descalers remain the responsibility of adults and are generally out of the reach of children. You can find out what else is important on our page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

### shared apartment

In a shared apartment, only the shared areas are included in the plan: kitchen, bathroom, hallway, garbage and, if necessary, living room or balcony. Each person arranges their own rooms. To ensure that the plan remains fair and does not become an ongoing dispute, a few rules have proven to be effective:

- **Rotation instead of fixed responsibility.** Every week each person moves up one area. This means no one has to take over the bathroom permanently.
- **Weigh effort.** The bathroom is more work than taking out the trash. Combine light tasks, such as trash and hallway, so the packages are roughly the same size.
- **Define “Done.”** Post a short checklist for each area. Then “bathroom cleaned” means the same thing for everyone, including the toilet, mirror and floor.
- **Set exchange rules.** Exchanges are permitted if noted on the plan. If you're on vacation, you can catch up on your week or swap beforehand.
- **Clarify basic equipment.** Determine which cleaners and cloths are available together and who will buy them. What is really necessary for this can be found in our guide to [basic cleaning supplies] (/ratgeber/putzmittel-grundausstattung/).

## Realistically estimate time requirements

A plan rarely fails due to a lack of tasks, usually due to too many tasks in too little time. So don't make a gut guess, but instead, the next time you go through it, count how long you actually need for the bathroom, kitchen and floors and write it down on your list.

Add up the times per week, compare them with the time you actually have, and plan a buffer for collecting materials and washing out cloths. An example: If your bath took almost half an hour to measure, don't plan it in a gap of twenty minutes before work. If the total doesn't add up, you have three options: spread tasks over more days, reduce frequencies where it's acceptable, or delegate tasks to other people in the household.

## Make the plan visible

A plan that no one sees will not be followed. Where it hangs depends on the household:

| shape | Fits well for | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Printed plan, for example on the refrigerator | Families, households with children | visible to everyone, even without a cell phone; Ticking off makes progress visible | must be reprinted if changes are made |
| Whiteboard or chalkboard in the hallway or kitchen | Shared apartments, changing responsibilities | Names and weeks can be changed quickly | is easily blurred; needs fixed space |
| Shared list or shared calendar on mobile | Couples, working people, single households | Reminders come automatically, even for rare tasks | Children without cell phones are left out; Notifications are often wiped away |

A combination has proven to work well: weekly tasks on paper in the kitchen, rare tasks as recurring appointments in the calendar. For a shared apartment with rotation, a cardboard turntable is suitable, on which names and areas are rotated one space each week.

## Schedule seasonal tasks

Some work depends on the season. If you enter them straight away in the appropriate month, you don't have to think about them every year.

- **Spring:** Clean windows and frames, wash curtains, clean balcony and balcony furniture, wash winter clothes according to the label before storing them.
- **Summer:** Clean the insect screens, dust the fans, check the refrigerator more often because food spoils more quickly in the heat.
- **Autumn:** Thoroughly dust radiators before the heating season, prepare floor mats for the wet season, wash and put away summer clothes.
- **Winter:** Wipe road salt and dirt edges in the hallway more often, clean the oven and extractor hood after the cookie season.

If you have a major project coming up, such as spring cleaning or handing over an apartment, our [Checklist for basic cleaning](/ratgeber/grundreinigung-wohnung/) will help you.

## If the plan doesn't work

Hardly any cleaning plan fits on the first try. After about four weeks, take a few minutes and look at what's left behind. The most common problems can be resolved specifically:

| problem | Probable cause | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Entire days are constantly being postponed | too many tasks at once | Spread tasks over more days, remeasure time requirements |
| A certain task remains undone | worded too vaguely or particularly unpopular | describe it more specifically, couple it with a popular task or swap it in rotation |
| However, an area quickly becomes dirty again | Frequency too low | Shorten the distance or add a daily activity |
| Other areas are still clean after the appointment | Frequency too high | Extend your distance and use the time you gain differently |
| Rare tasks are forgotten | no memory | Create a recurring calendar entry with a fixed month |
| Anger over unequal distribution | Effort not weighted, “done” not defined | Re-tie packages, hang up checklists |

A typical mistake is to start with the perfect plan: an hour every day, all in the shortest possible intervals. Hardly anyone can endure that. It's better to start slim and add later. And adjust the plan if your daily routine changes, such as a new job, a baby or a move.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to take over all the tasks from the table?

No. Delete what isn't in your household and add what's missing, like a balcony, a pet, or a second bathroom.

### How do I start when everything is already dirty?

First, do a one-time basic cleaning, room by room, spread over several days. Only then does the plan start, because it is intended to keep things clean, not to catch up on backlogs.

## Briefly summarized

Collect all the tasks room by room and formulate them so specifically that you can check them off. Assign a frequency to each task, distribute the weekly tasks over days or a fixed date and spread the monthly tasks throughout the month. Adapt the plan to your household: reminders for singles, age-appropriate tasks for children, fair rotation with clear rules for the shared apartment. Measure your time needs, make the plan visible and review it after a few weeks.
