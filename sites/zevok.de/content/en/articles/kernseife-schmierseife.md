Soap is one of the oldest cleaning products ever. Long before there were liquid detergents, bathroom cleaners and spray bottles, people washed with curd soap and wiped with soft soap. Both are now back in many households because they are simple, cheap and free of unnecessary additives. But they also have properties that you should be aware of, because soap behaves differently than modern cleaners, especially with hard water.

In this guide you will find out what the difference is between curd soap and soft soap, how to use them correctly for floors, laundry and small household tasks, why soap forms a deposit with hard water and when you should use another product.

## What's the difference?

Both soaps are created in the same basic way: fats or oils are boiled with a lye. The difference is in the lye.

| | Curd soap | soft soap |
|---|---|---|
| Lye | Caustic soda | Potassium lye |
| Consistency | solid, as a piece | soft, pasty or liquid |
| Typical use | Stains, hand wash, hands | Floors, stone, wood, plant care |
| Special feature | usually without fragrance or dyes | leaves a fine care film |

**Curd soap** is a solid, simple soap, often without perfume or dyes. **Soap** is soft, is often made from vegetable oils and can be easily distributed in water.

Both soaps are **basic**. In normal doses this is not a problem for the skin or for robust materials, but sensitive fibers and some surfaces react to it.

## Why soap and hard water go badly together

Tap water contains dissolved calcium and magnesium compounds. Soap reacts with them and forms an insoluble compound, **lime soap**. You know it as a gray edge in the bathtub or as a dull film on tiles.

This has two consequences:

- **Some of the soap is lost** before it even cleans. The harder the water, the more soap you need.
- **Lime soap builds up** on surfaces, in textile fibers and in the washing machine.

Modern detergents and cleaners therefore contain synthetic surfactants and softeners that hardly react with lime. This is one of the reasons why soap is particularly useful where a fine film is desired or where only a little water is involved.

## Soft soap for floors

Soft soap is a classic floor care product. It cleans and leaves a wafer-thin film that protects some floors and makes dirt less likely to stick.

**Suitable for:**

- **Oiled wooden floors,** for which there are special wooden floor soaps that work on the same principle,
- **unglazed tiles and natural stone floors** such as terracotta, cotto or slate, which are traditionally cared for with soap,
- **robust stoneware and clinker floors.**

**Here's how:**

1. **Sweep or vacuum** to avoid rubbing sand across the floor.
2. **Dosage:** Add about one to two tablespoons of soft soap to a bucket of warm water or follow the instructions on the package.
3. **Wipe with a damp cloth:** Wring out the mop well. Wood does not tolerate puddles.
4. **Do not rinse afterwards** if the care film is desired. However, if you use too much soap, the floor will become sticky and slippery.

**Less suitable for:**

- **Sealed parquet and laminate:** The soap film can form streaks. A pH-neutral cleaner for these coverings is better.
- **Glazed tiles:** Here the film remains visible and can make the floor smooth.
- **Linoleum and sensitive plastic coverings:** Only according to the manufacturer's instructions, because some coverings do not tolerate alkaline agents well.

## Curd soap for laundry and stains

**Pre-treat stains:** Curd soap can remove dirt edges on collars and cuffs and light stains on robust fabrics. Moisten the area, rub the soap in, work it in gently and then wash the item as usual. For greasy stains, **gall soap** is often more effective, more about this in the guide [How to use gall soap correctly] (/ratgeber/gallseife/).

**Hand washing robust items:** Socks, tea towels or work gloves made of cotton can be hand washed with soap. Rinse thoroughly to avoid leaving any soap residue in the fibers.

**Wool and silk:** Curd soap is not a good choice for these fibers because of its alkaline effect. A wool or mild detergent will protect them better. More about this in the guide [Washing wool and silk](/ratgeber/wolle-und-seide-waschen/).

## Make your own detergent from curd soap?

There are many recipes circulating on the Internet for homemade liquid detergent made from grated soap, soda and water. It sounds cheap and natural, but it has disadvantages:

- **Lime soap for hard water:** The soap forms deposits in the laundry, which becomes gray and stiff, and in the washing machine.
- **No enzymes and no bleach:** Protein and dye stains are harder to remove.
- **Fluctuating quality:** Even mixed agents separate, gel or are dosed unevenly.
- **Soda is irritating to skin and eyes** and requires caution when mixing.

This may work for occasional experiments with soft water. For everyday use, a good detergent, correctly dosed, is a better and gentler solution. How to dose can be found in the guide [Dosing detergent] (/ratgeber/waschmittel-dosieren/).

## Little helpers in everyday life

**Sticking drawers and zippers:** Rub a dry bar of soap along the runners of old wooden drawers or along a stiff zipper. The soap acts like a lubricant.

**Brushes according to wall color:** Brushes with emulsion or acrylic paint can be washed with warm water and soap as long as the paint has not yet dried. For varnish or oil paints you need the solvent specified on the can.

**Hands after rough work:** Curd soap cleanses thoroughly, but dries out the skin. Applying cream afterwards helps.

**Plants against aphids:** A weak solution of soft soap and water is a well-known home remedy against aphids. Test it on a single leaf first, do not spray in full sun and, for sensitive species, rinse the plant later with clean water.

## Soapy water for surfaces outside and inside

A mild soap solution made from warm water and a little soft soap is suitable for many robust surfaces that do not need to be shiny:

- **Garden furniture made of wood and plastic:** Wash with a brush and soapy water, then rinse with clear water and allow to dry. Oiled wood then enjoys a new layer of oil.
- **Patio slabs and stone steps:** Green deposits and everyday dirt can be loosened with a scrubber and soapy water. Rinse thoroughly so that the surface does not remain slippery.
- **Window frames made of plastic or painted wood:** Wipe with a soft cloth and weak lye and wipe clean so that no film remains.
- **Garden tools and flower pots:** Soil and deposits dissolve particularly easily in warm soapy water.

Soap is less suitable on **glass, mirrors and high-gloss surfaces** because it easily leaves streaks.

## How much soap for what?

| task | Means | Guideline |
|---|---|---|
| Wood, stone and clinker floors | soft soap | about 1 to 2 tablespoons per bucket of warm water |
| Robust surfaces, garden furniture | soft soap | a strong splash in warm water, then rinse |
| Collar and cuffs | Curd soap | rub directly onto the moistened area |
| Aphids on plants | soft soap | about 1 tablespoon to 1 liter of water, test beforehand |

For finished products, always stick to the information on the packaging. The guidelines only help if you use pure soap without additives.

## Store properly

- **Store the soap in a dry place,** preferably on a soap dish with drip grooves. When wet it becomes soft and greasy.
- **Close the soft soap tightly** so that it does not dry out and protect it from frost.
- **Out of reach of children,** like all cleaning products. Soap in the eyes stings badly.
- **Do not pour into food containers**, even if soft soap looks harmless.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Too much soft soap in the mopping water:** The floor becomes sticky, slippery and attracts dirt.
- **Soap on sealed parquet or laminate:** Streaks will appear.
- **Soap detergent for hard water:** Gray, stiff laundry and deposits in the machine.
- **Curd soap for wool and silk:** The fibers can suffer.
- **Do not rinse out soap residue:** They attract new dirt to textiles.
- **Combine soap with vinegar:** The acid breaks down the soap and both lose their effectiveness.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is curd soap the same as gall soap?

Not quite. Gall soap is usually based on curd soap, but also contains beef bile or a vegetable substitute that dissolves fat particularly well. Gall soap is therefore usually the better choice for greasy stains.

### Why does my hardwood floor become dull after mopping with soap?

Most of the time the dosage was too high, or the floor was sealed and the soap film remained visible. Wipe once with clear, well-wrung out water and reduce the amount next time. If the floors are sealed, switch to a suitable floor cleaner.

### Can I grate curd soap for the washing machine and pour it straight in?

We advise against this. Grated soap dissolves poorly at low temperatures, and hard water creates limescale soap that builds up in the drum, hoses and dispenser drawer. If the machine already smells or shows greasy deposits, the guide [Cleaning the washing machine] (/ratgeber/waschmaschine-reinigen/) will help.

### Does soft soap help against mold?

No. Soap cleans, but does not reliably kill mold, and soap residue is even food for new fungi. The guide [Preventing and removing mold in the bathroom] (/ratgeber/schimmel-im-bad/) explains how you can safely treat mold.

### Are curd soap and soft soap environmentally friendly?

Soaps are easily biodegradable and often do not contain any fragrances or dyes. As with any cleaner, use sparingly. A comparison with other home remedies can be found in the guide [Home Remedies Myths Checked](/ratgeber/hausmittel-mythen/).

## Briefly summarized

Curd soap is a solid soap made from caustic soda, while soft soap is a soft soap made from caustic soda. Both are alkaline and form lime soap with hard water, which forms a deposit. Soft soap, used sparingly and wiped with a damp cloth, is suitable for oiled wooden floors, unglazed tiles and natural stone such as terracotta, but not for sealed parquet and laminate. Curd soap helps with collar edges, robust handwashing, jammed drawers and brushes with wall paint. For wool, silk and daily machine washing, use suitable detergents instead of homemade soapy water.
