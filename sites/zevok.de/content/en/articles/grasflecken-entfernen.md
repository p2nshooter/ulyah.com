Children coming from the playground, soccer players after a rainy game, gardeners after mowing the lawn, dog owners after walking through muddy fields: grass, soil and mud constantly end up on clothes, shoes and carpets. Many of these stains are considered hopeless, especially the green knees on light-colored pants. They can usually be easily removed if you know what they are made of and follow the correct order.

In this guide you will find out why grass stains are so stubborn, why you should let mud dry first, how to get sports clothing, children's trousers and sneakers clean again and what helps with stains on carpets and car seats.

## What the stains are made of

**Grass stains** are combination stains. They contain the green plant pigment chlorophyll, plant juices with protein and often some soil. The dye penetrates deep into the fiber and protein can seize at high heat. That's why grass stains need pre-treatment before they go into the machine.

**Earth and mud stains** are pigment spots. They consist of fine soil particles that get stuck between the fibers. When wet, they smear easily and penetrate deeper. However, most of them can be brushed out when dry.

You can find out more about the different types of stains in the guide [Removing stains: The basic rules](/ratgeber/flecken-entfernen-grundregeln/).

## Remove grass stains

1. **Do not wash with hot water.** Rinse the stain first with cold to lukewarm water to prevent the protein from setting.
2. **Work in gall soap.** Moisten the stain, rub gall soap on it and massage it in with a soft brush or your fingers. Leave it on for at least 15 minutes.
3. **Alternatively, detergent paste.** A heavy-duty detergent containing enzymes, mixed with a little water to form a paste, dissolves the protein components.
4. **Dissolve dye:** If a green shadow remains after pre-treatment, alcohol will help. Dab some denatured alcohol or isopropyl alcohol onto the stain with a white cloth and place a second cloth underneath to absorb the dissolved dye. First test in a hidden area whether the fabric is colorfast. Alcohol is highly flammable: use only with the window open and away from flames.
5. **Wash** at the highest temperature the label allows.
6. **Check** before the item goes into the dryer. If the stain is still visible, repeat the treatment.

For **white cotton**, oxygen bleach can help remove the last residue, provided the care label allows bleaching. More about gall soap and how to use it correctly can be found in the guide [How to use gall soap correctly] (/ratgeber/gallseife/).

## Remove soil and mud stains

1. **Let it dry.** Even if it's difficult: let mud dry completely. Wet mud smears as you wipe and is pushed deeper into the fabric.
2. **Brush and shake out.** Brush off the dried dirt with a firm brush, preferably outside. With pants, it helps to shake them vigorously or tap them against a wall.
3. **Vacuum** if there is still fine dust in the fiber.
4. **Pre-treat** with gall soap or detergent paste and let it take effect.
5. **Soak** heavily soiled items for a few hours in lukewarm water with detergent.
6. **Wash** as directed on label.

**Red clay court ash** from the tennis court consists of finely ground brick and has a particularly strong color. Brush them out dry, soak the item in cold water, treat the areas with gall soap and then wash it. For white tennis clothing, oxygen bleach helps against the reddish shade.

## Sportswear: jerseys, goalkeeper shorts and running clothing

After a game in the rain, jerseys, shorts and socks are often full of grass and mud. Here's how to get them clean:

- **Rinse roughly:** After drying and brushing mud, rinse it outside with a garden hose or in the shower to prevent soil from getting into the washing machine.
- **Soak overnight** in lukewarm water with a little detergent.
- **Pre-treat stains** with gall soap, especially on knees, hips and back.
- **Do not overload the machine:** Heavily soiled clothing needs space for water to flow through the fibers.
- **Please note the care label:** Many jerseys and functional fabrics can only tolerate 30 or 40 degrees and no fabric softener.
- **Do not tumble dry** unless label prohibits it. Printed numbers and logos come off due to heat.

The guide [Correct dosage of detergent] (/ratgeber/waschmittel-dosieren/) explains how to dose detergent correctly so that even heavily soiled laundry gets clean.

## Children's trousers with green and brown knees

Children's clothing is particularly stressed on the knees. A few tips:

- If possible, treat stains on the same day. The longer they sit, the harder they become.
- Gall soap in bar form is ideal: simply moisten it, rub it in, let it work and throw it in the wash.
- For trousers with coated or reinforced knees, brush carefully so that the coating does not suffer.
- For daycare and playgrounds, choose robust, darker clothing on which stains are less noticeable.

## Sneakers and sneakers

You clean **textile sneakers** like this:

1. Remove shoelaces and insoles.
2. Brush out dried dirt and clean the sole profiles with an old toothbrush.
3. Brush the fabric with lukewarm water and a little mild detergent or gall soap.
4. Wipe with a damp cloth and leave the shoes to air dry, stuffed with newspaper, not on the heater.

According to the manufacturer, some textile sneakers can be put in the washing machine, usually at 30 degrees in a laundry net. Check this beforehand because glue and leather can come loose.

**You can get white rubber soles bright again with a dirt eraser made of melamine foam or with a little detergent and a brush. You can wash shoelaces in a laundry net.

**Leather shoes** only wipe with a damp cloth, allow to dry and care for with suitable shoe polish. Stuff wet leather shoes with paper and dry slowly at room temperature.

## Garden clothing and work gloves

Anyone who works in the garden has to deal with all three types of stains at the same time: grass, soil and plant juices. It is therefore worth having your own clothing for gardening, which does not have to remain immaculate. Nevertheless, it will remain attractive for longer if you shake it out after work, let it dry, brush it roughly and only then wash it.

You can often wash fabric work gloves in the machine, preferably in a laundry net. Just tap out the leather gloves, wipe them with a damp cloth and let them air dry so that the leather does not become hard. Rinse rubber boots outside with the hose before they come into the house.

## Prevention instead of scrubbing

- **A mat and a brush in front of the door** help to keep the worst dirt from shoes and pants outside.
- **A box for dirty sports clothes** in the hallway prevents wet jerseys from being forgotten in the backpack and absorbing odors.
- **Pre-treat immediately:** A bar of gall soap next to the washing machine is a reminder to treat stains immediately after undressing.
- **Impregnated rain pants** for children keep mud at bay and are easy to wash off.

## Earth and mud on carpet and car seat

1. **Let dry.** Wet soil smeared in carpet.
2. **Suction,** thoroughly and from multiple directions.
3. **Treat the remainder:** Moisten a white cloth with lukewarm water and a few drops of dishwashing liquid and dab the stain from the outside in.
4. **Dab with clean water** and pat dry.
5. **Allow to dry thoroughly** in the car with the doors or windows open.

Similar rules apply to upholstery and mattresses. More about this in the guide [Removing stains from carpets, sofas and mattresses] (/ratgeber/flecken-teppich-polster/).

## Which doesn't help against grass stains

| Myth | Why it doesn't work |
|---|---|
| Hot water first | Protein components settle |
| Grate salt | roughens the fiber, does not dissolve the dye |
| Toothpaste | contains abrasives and leaves its own stains |
| Chlorine bleach on colored items | bleaches the fabric instead of just the stain |
| Mix several remedies | can be dangerous and damages the fiber |

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Wipe away wet mud:** It smears. Let it dry first, then brush it out.
- **Pre-wash grass stains with hot water:** First pre-treat with cold to lukewarm.
- **Without pre-treatment in the machine:** Most grass and earth stains need gall soap or detergent paste.
- **Too early in the dryer:** Heat fixes residual stains.
- **Alcohol without color test:** It can dissolve dyes from the fabric.
- **Muddy clothes directly into the machine:** Coarse dirt clogs the sieve and pump.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do old grass stains still come out?

Often yes, but they require multiple passes. Soak the item, treat it with gall soap or detergent paste and, for colorfast fabrics, also with alcohol. Oxygen bleach helps with white cotton.

### Does stain spray from the supermarket help?

Many stain sprays contain surfactants and enzymes and work just as well as gall soap on grass and soil. It is important to let them soak in before washing and to follow the instructions, especially with wool, silk and colored fabrics.

### Can I put muddy laundry in the washing machine?

Yes, but only after you have brushed out and rinsed out the coarse dirt. Otherwise sand and stones will get into the drum, sieve and pump. Check the lint filter afterwards. The guide [Cleaning the washing machine] (/ratgeber/waschmaschine-reinigen/) explains how to care for the machine.

### What helps with stains on leaves and flowers?

Leaf and flower spots behave similarly to grass spots. Treat them with gall soap and, for colorfast fabrics, with a little alcohol. You should not wipe pollen from lilies wet, but rather carefully dab it with adhesive tape. You can find further instructions in the [Remove stains](/themen/flecken-entfernen/) section.

## Briefly summarized

Grass stains are combination stains made from dye and protein. Treat them cold to lukewarm with gall soap or detergent paste and dissolve the green dye on colorfast fabrics with a little alcohol. Earth and mud are pigment spots: let them dry, brush them out and treat the rest with gall soap. Soak sportswear overnight, do not overload the machine and follow the care label. Brush out sneakers and clean them by hand, first vacuum carpets and then carefully dab them. Check every stain before the item goes into the dryer.
