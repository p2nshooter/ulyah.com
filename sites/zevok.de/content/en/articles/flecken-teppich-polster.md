A glass of red wine on the light carpet, cocoa on the sofa, the dog having a mishap in the hallway, a child who gets sick at night: stains on carpets, upholstered furniture and mattresses are particularly annoying because you can't just put these things in the washing machine. Many people then use a lot of water and vigorous scrubbing. The result is often larger stains, light edges, wet upholstery and odors that don't go away.

In this guide you will learn the basic rules for stains on textile surfaces, how to treat the most common stains, how to avoid water marks, what really helps with animal urine and when it is worth using a cleaning device or a specialist company.

## The basic rules for textile surfaces

1. **Act immediately.** Fresh stains are almost always easier to remove than dried ones.
2. **Dabbing instead of rubbing.** Rubbing pushes the stain deeper into the fibers, distributes it and roughens the pile.
3. **Work from the outside in.** This will make the stain smaller, not larger.
4. **As little water as possible.** Carpet backing, upholstery fillings and mattress cores dry slowly. Too much moisture leads to edges, odors and, in the worst case, mold.
5. **White cloths only.** Colored cloths may stain.
6. **Always test beforehand** in a hidden area, such as under the sofa or on the edge of the carpet.
7. **Dab cleanly.** Soap residue that remains in the fabric attracts new dirt. After each cleaning product, blot with clean water.
8. **Dry quickly.** Open window, use fan, do not enter area.

You can find out more about the different types of stains in the guide [Removing stains: The basic rules](/ratgeber/flecken-entfernen-grundregeln/).

## The basic equipment

- White cotton towels or kitchen paper.
- A spray bottle with cold water.
- Dishwashing liquid or gall soap.
- Baking soda for odors.
- An enzyme stain remover for carpets and upholstery, especially for pets and children.
- A soft brush and a vacuum cleaner.

## The most common stains on carpet and upholstery

**Red wine, coffee, tea and juice**

1. Immediately absorb as much liquid as possible with kitchen paper, by squeezing, not by rubbing.
2. Moisten slightly with cold water from the spray bottle and absorb again. This dilutes the dye.
3. Pour a solution of cold water and a few drops of dish soap onto a cloth and dab the stain from the outside in.
4. Blot with clear water and pat dry.

Sprinkling salt on red wine is a common tip, but it has little effect on carpets and can leave light edges. More about tannin stains in the guide [Removing red wine, coffee and tea stains] (/ratgeber/rotweinflecken-entfernen/).

**Fat, butter and sauces**

Remove excess fat, sprinkle on cornstarch and leave to work for a few hours, then vacuum. Dab the rest with dish soap solution and then blot clear. Explained in detail in the guide [Removing grease stains] (/ratgeber/fettflecken-entfernen/).

**Blood**

Always treat with cold water, never with warm water. More in the guide [Removing blood stains](/ratgeber/blutflecken-entfernen/).

**Chocolate and cocoa**

Carefully lift off solids, then dab cold with detergent solution because chocolate contains fat, protein and coloring. If there is any remaining paint, use a carpet cleaner according to the instructions.

**Mud and Earth**

Let it dry, brush it out and vacuum it, then treat the rest. More in the guide [Removing grass, soil and mud stains] (/ratgeber/grasflecken-entfernen/).

**Wax and chewing gum**

Let it harden with cold and lift it off, remove any wax residue with blotting paper and a lukewarm iron. More in the guide [Removing ballpoint pens, wax and chewing gum] (/ratgeber/kugelschreiber-wachs-kaugummi/).

## Animal urine: Permanently eliminate odors and stains

Urine from dogs and cats is a particular problem. In addition to the visible stain, uric acid crystals remain in the fibers, which always smell, especially in humid weather. Animals recognize the smell and often mark the spot again.

1. **Absorb as much urine as possible** with kitchen paper, pressing firmly.
2. **Lightly moisten with cold water** and absorb again.
3. **Apply an enzyme-based pet urine odor remover** generously so that it penetrates as deeply as the urine. The enzymes break down the odor substances. Allow it to take effect according to the instructions, often for several hours.
4. **Allow to dry** without entering the area.
5. **Repeat if necessary.**

**What to avoid:**

- **Cleaners containing ammonia:** They smell similar to urine to animals and may cause the area to be used again.
- **Steam cleaner and hot water:** Heat can fix protein components and odors.
- **Scented sprays:** They only mask the smell for a short time.

After drying, you can sprinkle baking soda on it, leave it to work overnight and vacuum it up. It binds residual odors.

## Vomit

1. Pick up solids with a spatula or card, wearing gloves.
2. Sprinkle baking soda or cornstarch thickly. It binds liquid and odor. Vacuum after about an hour.
3. Dab with an enzyme-containing cleaner or dishwashing liquid and then blot clear.
4. Dry thoroughly and ventilate.

If you have gastrointestinal illnesses, wear gloves, wash your hands thoroughly afterwards and wash used cloths at 60 degrees or higher.

## Avoid water marks

Light or dark edges occur when water transports loosened dirt to the edge of the damp surface during drying. Here's how to avoid them:

- **Use a little water** and use well-wrung cloths.
- **Hide the edges:** Finally, use a slightly damp cloth to dab outwards beyond the edge of the stain so that there is no sharp transition.
- **Dry quickly:** A hairdryer on a cold setting or a fan speeds up drying.
- **Distilled water** helps with hard tap water, which can leave streaks.

## Sofas: the material matters

- **Removable covers** made of cotton or linen can be washed often. Check the label, as many covers shrink, and wash all parts of a sofa together to keep the color consistent.
- **Microfiber and velor sofas** often react sensitively to water and quickly develop edges. Many manufacturers recommend cleaning with little moisture or special cleaners. Read the care instructions and test beforehand. After drying, straighten the pile with a soft brush.
- **Treat leather** only with leather cleaners and care products, no home remedies with lots of water or alcohol.
- **Velvet** is pressure sensitive. Dab instead of rubbing and brush the pile in the direction of the grain after it has dried.

## mattresses

You should never soak mattresses. This is how you do it:

1. Immediately absorb the liquid with towels and press firmly.
2. Dab the stain with a damp cloth and a little cleaner, depending on the type of stain.
3. Dab with clear water and press dry.
4. Set up the mattress, air it out and allow it to dry completely before making the bed again.

**Against odors** Sprinkle baking soda thinly, leave to work for several hours and vacuum thoroughly. **You can prevent** with a washable, waterproof mattress protector, especially in children's beds.

## Car seats and strollers

You treat fabric car seats like upholstery: absorb liquid immediately, dab with a little water and detergent solution, dab clear and leave to dry with the doors open. It dries more slowly in the car than in the apartment, which is why little moisture is particularly important here. Child seats usually have removable covers that can be washed according to the manufacturer's instructions. You must not put belts in the machine or treat them with sharp substances as this can reduce their strength. Just wipe them with lukewarm water and a little mild soap.

Covers for strollers and baby seats are often washable. Read the instructions before removing it and take photos of the assembly so you can reinstall it correctly later.

## Carpet Types

- **Wool carpets:** Wool is a protein fiber. Use mild cleaners that are suitable for wool and do not use highly alkaline or enzyme-containing agents without manufacturer approval.
- **Synthetic fiber carpets** made of polyamide or polypropylene are usually robust and tolerate dishwashing liquid and carpet cleaner.
- **Sisal, jute and seagrass:** These natural fibers change color when exposed to water. Dry cleaning powder and vacuuming can help here. Absorb liquids immediately.
- **Silk carpets and valuable oriental carpets** should be taken to specialist hands if they have stains.

## Cleaning equipment and specialist companies

For larger areas or a generally dirty carpet, it is worth using a **washer vacuum** or carpet cleaning device that sprays in cleaning solution and immediately vacuums it up again. You can rent such equipment at many drugstores and hardware stores. Make sure you use the correct dosage of the cleaner and then let the carpet dry thoroughly.

You should hire a **specialist company** for valuable carpets, sensitive upholstery fabrics, large or old stains and if an odor remains despite treatment.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Scrubbing:** Stain becomes larger, pile damaged. Always dab.
- **Too much water:** Edges, odors and mold in the upholstery.
- **Leave soap residue in the fabric:** The stain comes back as a dirty edge.
- **Ammonia in animal urine:** Animals mark the spot again.
- **Steam and heat for protein and urine:** Fixes stains and odors.
- **Work without testing:** Colors and materials react very differently.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get old stains out of carpet?

Old stains need multiple passes. Moisten it lightly, treat it with a suitable carpet cleaner and let it take effect. A vacuum cleaner can help with dried stains. With very old stains a shadow sometimes remains.

### Does baking soda help against odors in the sofa?

Yes. Sprinkle baking soda thinly, let it work for a few hours or overnight and vacuum thoroughly. It binds odors but does not remove stains. More about baking soda in the guide [baking soda, soda and baking powder] (/ratgeber/natron-soda-backpulver/).

### Can I use a steam cleaner on upholstery?

Only if the manufacturer expressly allows it. Steam can shrink fabrics, change colors and fix protein stains. You can find further instructions in the [Remove stains](/themen/flecken-entfernen/) section.

## Briefly summarized

The following applies to carpets, sofas and mattresses: act immediately, dab instead of rubbing, work from the outside in and use as little water as possible. First absorb the liquid with kitchen paper, then treat the stain depending on the type of stain and always blot with clear water. Animal urine needs an enzyme-based odor remover, no ammonia or heat. Avoid water marks by blending the edge outwards and drying quickly. Test each product beforehand and leave valuable carpets and sensitive upholstery to a specialist company.
