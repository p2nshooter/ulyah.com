An overturned yogurt cup that went under the vegetable drawer. A strange smell as soon as you open the door. A freezer compartment in which the layer of ice is so thick that the drawer only comes out by force. The refrigerator is the appliance in the kitchen that is opened most often and cleaned thoroughly the least.

Basic cleaning isn't a big deal if you do it in the right order. In this guide you will find out how you know when it's time, how to keep food cool during this time, how to clean the interior, compartments, seal and condensation drain, how to safely defrost a freezer compartment and how to prevent odors.

## How often and how you notice it

It is best to wipe up spills immediately. Then the actual work remains manageable. A good habit is to take a quick look before you do your weekly shopping: take out leftovers and expired items, and briefly wipe the compartments with a damp cloth. A basic cleaning is due if any of these signs appear:

- It smells unpleasant, although nothing spoiled can be seen.
- Compartments, drawers or door shelves are sticky or have dried edges.
- There is water at the bottom of the refrigerator compartment or under the vegetable drawer.
- A layer of ice has formed in the freezer compartment, drawers are freezing solid or the door no longer closes properly.

| task | When |
|---|---|
| Wipe up spills | immediately |
| Sift through supplies, briefly wipe compartments | weekly, preferably before shopping |
| Basic cleaning with all operations | every few months or if there are smells and stains |
| Check condensation water drain | with every basic cleaning |
| Defrost the freezer compartment | As soon as a layer of ice has formed, this is not necessary with no-frost devices |

## Store food safely

Plan the basic cleaning for a day when the refrigerator is almost empty, such as shortly before you go shopping. Then you have to outsource less.

- **Cool bag or cool box with ice packs:** Put the batteries in the freezer in good time. Delicate foods such as dairy products, meat, fish and opened foods go in first.
- **Pack freezer items tightly together:** If they are placed close together and wrapped in towels or a blanket, they will stay colder for longer. Still work quickly.
- **Sort out:** Anything that has exceeded the use-by date (“to be used by”) will be disposed of. The best-before date, on the other hand, is not a throwaway date: check such foods with your eyes, nose and a small sample. Anything that smells moldy or spoiled goes away.

## Step by step: clean the refrigerator

You don't need any special products for cleaning. Warm water with a little detergent, two clean cloths, a soft sponge and a soft brush or cotton swab for cracks are sufficient. Many operating instructions expressly advise against using abrasive, acidic or solvent-based agents in the interior because plastic and seals can suffer. Disinfectants are not necessary.

1. **Turn off the device.** For basic cleaning, turn off the refrigerator or unplug it as instructed in the instructions. This means you can work safely and the device will not cool against the open door.
2. **Clear out and remove inserts.** Glass plates, vegetable drawers and door shelves come out. Remember the order or take a photo to make insertion quicker.
3. **Clean inserts.** Wash plastic parts in the sink with warm water and dish soap. They only go in the dishwasher if the manufacturer allows it, because heat can deform plastic. Allow glass plates from the cold refrigerator to come to room temperature beforehand. Hot water on cold glass can cause tension and, in the worst case scenario, the plate will crack.
4. **Wipe the interior from top to bottom.** Start at the ceiling, then follow the side walls, back wall and floor. Wring out the cloth well so that no water gets on the lights, controls or sensors. Use a soft brush or cotton swab for cracks and corners.
5. **Clean the door and gasket.** Wipe the inside of the door and the shelf brackets, then clean the gasket as described in the next section.
6. **Wipe with clean water and dry.** Detergent residue can smell and stick. Then rub everything dry with a clean cloth.
7. **Insert, turn on, let cool.** Put the parts back in place, turn on the appliance and wait until it is cold again before putting the food back. How long this takes is stated in the instructions.

Take the outside with you straight away: handles are places that many hands touch and deserve special attention. Wipe stainless steel fronts with a damp cloth in the direction of the grinding and rub them dry.

| part | Here's how to clean it | Attention |
|---|---|---|
| Glass plates | warm rinse water, rub dry | let it come to room temperature first |
| Vegetable drawer and door shelves | in the sink with detergent and a soft sponge | Dishwasher only if the manufacturer allows it |
| Interior walls and floor | wrung out cloth, from top to bottom | no water for lights and electronics |
| Door seal | Rinse water, fold with a soft cloth, dry | do not pull, not with sharp objects |
| Condensation drain | Cleaning swabs or cotton swabs, a little warm water | do not poke it with force |
| Exterior and handles | Rinse water, stainless steel in grinding direction | no abrasive cleaners |

## Keep the door seal and condensation drain clear

### The door seal

Crumbs, dried drops and moisture collect in the folds of the rubber seal. Wipe it out with warm soapy water and a soft cloth, pulling the wrinkles apart slightly and wiping everything dry. Pointed objects and sharp substances have no place here.

You can check whether the seal still closes well with a simple test: clamp a piece of paper into the door and pull on it. If you feel significant resistance, the seal is in place. If the sheet slips out without resistance, check the area again at several points. A damaged or persistently leaking seal allows warm air in, promotes ice and condensation and should be replaced by customer service or according to instructions.

### The condensation drain

In many refrigerators, a small amount of frost always forms on the back wall and defrosts automatically. The water runs into a channel and through a small hole at the back, where it evaporates. If this hole becomes clogged with crumbs or debris, the water collects in the refrigerator compartment, usually under the vegetable drawer.

Some devices have a suitable cleaning stick that is inserted into the drain opening or is included. Otherwise, a cotton swab will help. Rinse with a small amount of warm water, for example with a pipette or a disposable syringe without a needle. Don't force it. And don't put food directly against the back wall: it can freeze and block the drain.

## Defrost the freezer compartment and freezer

Ice in the freezer acts like an insulating layer. The device has to work harder, requires more power, and it fits less. You do not have to defrost devices with no-frost technology. For everyone else it is necessary from time to time. Read the operating instructions beforehand because they will tell you how to switch off the device and which aids are permitted.

1. **Switch off.** Switch off the appliance or the freezer compartment as described in the instructions and open the door.
2. **Clear out.** Frozen food goes well packaged into the cool box.
3. **Collect water.** Place towels on the floor and in the compartment, placing a shallow dish underneath if necessary. Some freezers have a defrosting channel or a drain spout.
4. **Speed ​​up defrosting.** If the instructions allow it, place a bowl of hot water on a board or a folded cloth in the compartment and close the door. Renew the water regularly.
5. **Remove dissolved ice.** Remove pieces of ice as they become loose. A plastic scraper, as supplied with some devices, is permitted, but force is not.
6. **Dry and start.** Wipe out the compartment, rub it thoroughly dry, switch on the device and only put the frozen food back when the temperature has been reached again.

> **Never scratch with a knife, screwdriver or ice pick.** The refrigerant lines run behind the walls and panels of the freezer compartment. All it takes is one small hole and the device is irreparably damaged. In many modern devices, the refrigerant is also flammable. You also only use a hairdryer or fan heater if the instructions expressly allow it: water and electricity do not mix, and plastic parts can deform.

Allow a few hours for defrosting, depending on ice thickness and room temperature. If the ice always grows back very quickly, it could be due to a leaky seal or a door that has been opened too often and for too long.

## Prevent odors

Most smells have a specific cause: spilled liquid in a corner, a leftover item left behind in the back of the compartment, cheese or fish stored open, a dirty seal or standing water from a clogged drain. Only when these sources are eliminated will the smell really disappear.

- Store food covered or in sealed containers.
- Put a date on the leftovers so they don't go to the back unnoticed.
- Wipe up spills immediately, especially milk and meat juice.
- Only leave the door open as long as necessary.

And the famous bowl of baking soda? Baking soda can bind some odors and weaken a slight smell somewhat. You shouldn't expect miracles, and it doesn't help the cause at all. If you want to use it, keep it open in a compartment and replace it regularly. What else baking soda can and can't do can be found in the guide [baking soda, soda and baking powder] (/ratgeber/natron-soda-backpulver/).

## Check the temperature

Finally, it's worth taking a look at the temperature. The display on the controller is often just a level and not a number of degrees. It gets more precise with a refrigerator thermometer: place it in a glass of water on a middle shelf and read the value after a few hours. The water shows the same temperature as your food and does not fluctuate every time you open the door.

The value intended for your device can be found in the operating instructions. Freezers are usually set at minus 18 degrees or colder. If the refrigerator compartment is significantly warmer than specified, check the seal, load and the air circulation behind the device before setting the controller colder.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Remove ice with a sharp tool:** This puts the entire device at risk. Patience and warm water will safely loosen the ice.
- **Rinse cold glass with hot water:** Allow glass plates to reach room temperature first.
- **Sharp cleaners and scouring pads:** They attack plastic and seals and leave odors behind. Detergent is enough.
- **Covers that are too wet:** Water can run into lights and electronics. Wring out cloths well.
- **Putting food back too early:** Wait until the device has returned to temperature.
- **Forgetting the Drain:** Water under the vegetable drawer is almost always a sign of a clogged drain.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use vinegar to clean?

It is not necessary, dishwashing liquid and warm water remove the usual dirt in the refrigerator. Many manufacturers advise against using acidic cleaners in the interior. Follow your device's operating instructions. And never mix several products: the rules for this are on our page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

### What do I do if a smell remains despite cleaning?

Check the seal, condensation drain and the corners of the door shelves carefully again and leave the switched off device open for a while so that it can air out. If the smell persists, contact the manufacturer's customer service department.

## Briefly summarized

Wipe up spills immediately, inspect supplies weekly, and clean the refrigerator thoroughly every few months or if there is an odor. Store food in a cooler with ice packs. Clean the inserts and interior from top to bottom with warm soapy water, let cold glass plates reach room temperature first and dry everything. Keep the seal and condensation drain clean. Defrost the freezer compartment with the appliance switched off, towels and warm water, never with sharp tools. Finally, check the temperature with a thermometer.
