The new red sock that has smuggled itself between the white shirts. The favorite sweater that would fit a child after washing. The towels that were once bright white and now look gray. Almost every laundry mishap has its origins before the machine is started: when sorting, preparing or choosing the temperature and program.

With a few fixed rules, it becomes a routine that hardly takes any time. In this guide you will find out how to sort laundry according to color, material and dirt, what to do before washing in bags and on fasteners, which temperature is suitable for what, what the common programs do and how to load the drum correctly.

## Sort by color

Color is the first sorting criterion because new and brightly colored textiles can release dye when washed. White laundry readily absorbs this dye and turns gray over time if it is always mixed with colored items.

- **White:** Shirts, blouses, underwear, bed linen, towels and table linen in white. When washed with a heavy-duty detergent that contains bleach, whites stay white longer.
- **Light:** Pastel tones, light gray, beige and cream. These colors do not tolerate bleach, but quickly absorb foreign dyes.
- **Dark:** Black, dark blue, dark gray, brown and dark jeans. Dark fabrics fade at high temperatures and a lot of friction and then quickly look washed out.
- **Colorful:** Red, green, yellow, orange and bold patterns. If you have enough laundry, wash red and orange tones separately from blue and green tones.
- **New clothing:** Wash new items, especially dark jeans and bright red items, separately or with very similar colors for the first few times. You can see how much something bleeds by looking at the water after a quick hand wash in a light bowl.

A practical way to avoid sorting from the beginning every time: a laundry basket with two or three compartments into which everyone in the household throws their laundry in the same way.

## Sort by material and care symbol

Within a color pile, the material decides what can go into the machine together. The care label gives you the limit: The number in the wash tub is the maximum temperature, lines below it require a more gentle program, and one hand in the tub means hand washing. What each symbol means exactly can be found in the guide [Understanding care symbols] (/ratgeber/pflegesymbole/).

- **Cotton and linen:** sturdy, can withstand higher temperatures and vigorous spinning unless the label says otherwise.
- **Synthetics and blended fabrics:** easy to care for, but more sensitive to heat. They crease at high temperatures and strong spin.
- **Fine:** Viscose, lace, satin, delicate blouses. These materials require little movement and low temperatures.
- **Wool and silk:** in a separate program with suitable detergent or hand wash. Details can be found in the guide [Washing wool, cashmere and silk] (/ratgeber/wolle-und-seide-waschen/).
- **Functional clothing:** Sports and outdoor clothing according to the label, usually at low temperatures and without fabric softener.

Also watch out for lint. Terry towels and new cotton items release fibers, while fleece, corduroy and dark synthetics attract them. Anyone who washes black sports shirts with new towels spends a long time picking them afterwards.

## Sort by contamination

The garden trousers, the work clothes from the workshop and the football jersey after the rain game are not the office shirts that were only worn for one day. Heavily soiled laundry often needs a longer program and a higher dosage, while lightly soiled laundry needs a short program at a low temperature. If you separate the two, you won't wash any items for an unnecessarily long time and you won't spread dirt on clean laundry. Coarse dirt such as dried mud is brushed out beforehand and stains are pre-treated.

## Prepare laundry

A few simple steps before filling protect clothing and the machine:

1. **Empty your pockets.** A forgotten tissue will spread in flakes all over the load, coins and keys can damage the drum and pump, a lipstick or ballpoint pen will leave stains on everything.
2. **Close zippers.** Open teeth get caught in other parts and pull threads.
3. **Close Velcro fasteners.** Open Velcro areas collect lint and roughen other materials.
4. **Open buttons on shirts and blouses.** This protects buttonholes and seams. On the other hand, you button the duvet covers so that no small parts get caught in them.
5. **Turn inside out.** Wash jeans, dark clothing, shirts with prints, knitwear and functional clothing more gently inside out because the outside gets less friction.
6. **Use a laundry net.** Underwire bras, tights, delicate underwear and small items go in a laundry net. A strap that has slipped out can damage the machine. Also, close the hooks so they don't get caught on other parts.
7. **Remove belt and pins,** as well as loose decorations that may fall off.

## The right temperature

The temperature determines cleanliness, hygiene, energy consumption and the lifespan of your clothing. Higher is not automatically better: the machine needs most of the energy to heat the water, and heat puts a strain on fibers and colors.

| Temperature | Suitable for | Note |
|---|---|---|
| cold, 20°C, 30°C | slightly soiled everyday clothing, dark and strong colors, fine fabrics, wool and silk | Many modern detergents are designed for low temperatures |
| 40°C | most colored and everyday laundry, jeans, shirts, shirts, sportswear by label | the usual temperature for normally soiled clothing |
| 60°C | Towels, underwear, bed linen, tea towels, cleaning rags, washcloths | only if the label allows it, preferably with heavy-duty detergent |
| 90°C | robust white cotton for heavy soiling | rarely necessary, only if the label expressly allows it |

60 °C is the tried and tested temperature for laundry where hygiene plays a greater role, i.e. everything that comes into direct contact with skin, body fluids or kitchen dirt. A heavy-duty detergent with bleach will help with this. If someone in the household is sick, perhaps with a gastrointestinal infection, or if a family member's immune system is weakened, follow your doctor's recommendation on how the laundry should be washed.

You hardly need 90 °C in a normal household. Most textiles cannot tolerate this heat, and many cotton items shrink at high temperatures. If the label allows 90 °C and the laundry is very dirty, it may still make sense.

If you constantly wash at low temperatures, deposits and musty smells will easily develop in the machine. A regular wash cycle at 60 °C with towels or bed linen prevents this.

## Understand programs

The programs differ not only in the temperature, but above all in how hard the drum moves the laundry, how much water flows in and how high the spin speed is.

- **Cotton:** strong drum movement, lots of water, high spin speed. For robust laundry made from cotton and linen.
- **Easy care or synthetic:** less movement, lower spin speed, smaller load. Shirts and blouses wrinkle less this way.
- **Fine:** very gentle movement, lots of water as a cushion, low temperature and little spinning. For viscose, lace and delicate fabrics.
- **Wool or hand wash:** hardly any movement and low temperature to prevent wool from becoming felted. Only for items whose label allows machine washing.
- **Eco 40-60:** intended for normally soiled cotton laundry that, according to the label, can be washed at 40 or 60 °C. The program runs significantly longer, works with less heat and saves energy. The long running time is intentional and not a mistake. For laundry where hygiene is important to you, it is better to choose the regular 60 degree program.
- **Short or quick program:** for small amounts of lightly soiled clothing, for example a shirt that has been worn once. It's too short for full drums and real dirt.

Which programs your machine offers and which maximum load applies can be found in the operating instructions. The guide [How to properly dose detergent] (/ratgeber/waschmittel-dosieren/) explains how much detergent you should use and which type goes with which laundry.

## Load the drum correctly

A drum that is too full washes poorly: the laundry cannot move, the detergent is distributed unevenly, and residues and creases remain. A half-empty drum wastes water and energy.

- **Cotton program:** Fill the drum loosely so that about one hand still fits in at the top. Do not press in.
- **Easy care:** about half full.
- **Fine and wool:** significantly less, the exact amount is stated in the instructions.
- **Mix Large and Small Items:** A single heavy item such as a bath mat or wet robe will unbalance the drum while spinning. Combine it with smaller pieces.
- **Fill the bed linen loosely,** not in a tight ball, otherwise the inside of the cover will remain almost dry.

## Avoid discoloration

You have already completed the most important steps by sorting. These points are added:

- **Do not leave wet laundry lying around.** If damp items sit on top of each other in the drum or laundry basket for hours, color can migrate from one item to the other. Take out the laundry immediately after the end of the program and hang it up. You can read the best way to dry them in the guide [Drying laundry](/ratgeber/waesche-trocknen/).
- **Color detergent for colorful things.** It contains no bleach and is gentle on the colors.
- **Dye catcher cloths as a supplement.** They absorb dissolved dye from the washing water. They provide protection for mixed loads, but do not replace sorting.
- **Low temperature and not overloaded.** Both reduce color output.

If something is discolored, do not dry the affected parts under any circumstances. Wash them again immediately while they are wet. For white cotton, a heavy-duty detergent with bleach at the highest permitted temperature often helps. Special decolorizers are only an option if the label and package instructions allow it. Wear gloves and ventilate, more information on our page [Safety when cleaning] (/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Wash a new, strongly colored item with white:** Separate the first few washes consistently.
- **Everything at 40 °C in the cotton program:** Fine fabrics, wool and synthetics suffer from too much movement and too high a spin speed. Choose the program by material.
- **Drum too full:** Laundry is not clean and wrinkles. Better two loads.
- **Ignore care label:** Maximum temperature is a limit, not a suggestion. Fabrics above this shrink, become matted or lose color.
- **Don't check bags:** A single tissue is enough to cover an entire dark batch with white flakes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I wash white and light together?

Yes, if the light-colored parts are colorfast and do not require bleach. Then choose a detergent without bleach. However, white laundry is more likely to stay sparkling white in the long term if you wash it separately with heavy-duty detergent from time to time.

### Is 30 °C enough for everyday clothing?

For lightly soiled clothing that has been worn for a short time, usually yes. However, you can wash towels, bed linen, underwear and kitchen textiles at 60 °C if the label allows it.

## Briefly summarized

First sort by color into white, light, dark and colorful and initially wash new items separately. Separate the colors by material and care symbol and separate heavily soiled laundry from lightly soiled laundry. Empty pockets, close zippers and Velcro fasteners, open shirt buttons, turn delicate items inside out and use a laundry net. Choose 30 or 40°C for everyday use, 60°C for towels, bed linen and underwear if the label allows it. Load the drum according to the program and remove wet laundry immediately.
