White flakes in tea, a kettle that takes longer and longer, and a coffee machine that gurgles, drips and delivers lukewarm coffee: there is almost always limescale behind it. In many regions of Germany, tap water is hard, and every time it is heated, some of the dissolved minerals remain as a solid layer. This layer grows inconspicuously until it occupies heating elements, lines and valves to such an extent that devices work slower, louder and less economically.

The good news: Descaling isn't a big deal if you choose the right product and follow a few rules. In this guide you will find out how limescale is formed, which descalers are available, how you can descale kettles, filter coffee machines, fully automatic coffee machines and capsule machines step by step, how often this is necessary and how you can reduce limescale formation in everyday life.

## How limescale is formed and how hard your water is

Tap water contains dissolved calcium and magnesium compounds. The more of it in the water, the **harder** it is. When heated, some of it precipitates as lime and settles in the hottest places, i.e. exactly where the device heats.

In Germany, water hardness is often given in **degrees of German hardness (°dH)**. Roughly:

| Hardness range | Degree of German hardness | What that means for everyday life |
|---|---|---|
| soft | below about 8.4 °dH | little lime, descaling rarely |
| medium | about 8.4 to 14 °dH | noticeably limescale, descale regularly |
| hard | over about 14 °dH | lots of lime, decalcify often |

You can find out how hard your water is from your **water supplier**, usually on their website or on their annual bill. Many fully automatic coffee machines also come with a test strip that you can use to measure the hardness yourself. You will need this information later to set up the machine correctly.

Incidentally, lime is not harmful to health. Calcium and magnesium are minerals that are also found in foods. But it disturbs the taste, appearance and, above all, the technology.

## Which descalers are there?

Limescale can only be dissolved by **acid**. Alkaline products such as baking soda or soda help against grease, but not against limescale. These acids are particularly common in the household:

| Means | Suitable for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Citric acid (powder or liquid) | Kettle, simple appliances | low odor, do not use boiling |
| Vinegar or vinegar essence | Kettle, surfaces | strong smell, can attack seals |
| lactic acid | Coffee machines | Part of many coffee machine descalers |
| Amidosulfonic acid | Coffee machines, heavy coatings | in many ready-made descalers, wear gloves |
| Ready-made descaler from the device manufacturer | Fully automatic machines, capsule machines | Matched to the device, observe manufacturer's instructions |

**Citric acid** is the classic home remedy and is well suited for the kettle. It is important not to use them in boiling water: At high heat, poorly soluble calcium citrate can form and settle as a new layer. This is particularly unfavorable in narrow lines in a coffee machine. More about this in the guide [Citric acid in the household] (/ratgeber/zitronensaeure/).

**Vinegar** also dissolves limescale, but smells strongly when heated, and many manufacturers advise against using it in coffee machines because the acid can permanently attack rubber seals and hoses and leave a taste. You can read about where vinegar is useful and where it isn't in [Vinegar in the Household](/ratgeber/essig-im-haushalt/).

**Ready-made descalers** for coffee machines usually contain lactic acid, amidosulfonic acid or combinations thereof. They are tailored to materials and dosage. For expensive fully automatic machines, a ready-made descaler is the safe choice, also because some manufacturers exclude damage caused by unsuitable products from the guarantee.

## Descale the kettle

The kettle is the easiest device because you can see and reach the limescale layer directly.

1. **Unplug** and let the cooker cool down briefly if it has just been used.
2. **Fill in water** until the calcified areas are covered, usually about half is enough. The water should be warm but not boiling.
3. **Add descaler:** for example one to two tablespoons of citric acid powder per half liter of water, or a ready-made descaler according to the instructions on the package. Stir well.
4. **Let it take effect,** about 30 minutes to an hour, or longer for thick layers. The lime dissolves with a slight bubbling.
5. **Pour out** and remove any residue with a soft brush or sponge. No steel wool, it scratches stainless steel and glass.
6. **Rinse thoroughly** and then boil fresh water two to three times and pour away so that no taste remains.

**Don't forget the limescale filter:** Many kettles have a small sieve on the spout. It can usually be pulled out and soaked separately in a cup of descaling solution.

**Clean the outside:** Wipe the housing and base only with a damp cloth. Cookers and bases must never be immersed in water as they contain electrical contacts.

## Descale the filter coffee machine

With the classic filter machine, the limescale is in the heating pipe where you can't see it. Here the descaling solution runs through the machine.

1. **Check instructions:** Some machines have their own descaling program or specify a specific descaling program.
2. **Prepare the solution:** Mix the ready-made descaler with water according to the instructions on the package and fill it into the water tank.
3. **Insert an empty filter,** i.e. without coffee powder, and place the pot underneath.
4. **Let it run through about half way through,** then turn it off and leave it on for 15 to 30 minutes.
5. **Let the rest run through** and pour away the solution.
6. **Rinse at least two to three times with clean water** until the smell is no longer noticeable.

You can clean the glass jug at the same time: coffee edges come off with warm soapy water, limescale edges with a little descaling solution.

## Fully automatic coffee machine, portafilter and capsule machine

A simple rule applies to these devices: **Stick to the descaling program and the recommended product.** The pipes, valves and seals are more sensitive, and many machines measure the amount of water in order to display a message in a timely manner.

- **Start the program** as soon as the machine requires it and don't put it off for weeks. The thicker the lime, the harder it is to remove.
- **Remove the water filter** before descaling if there is one in the tank. The instructions will tell you if this is necessary.
- **Create enough space under the spout**, as descaling programs flush large amounts of water.
- **Let it run until the end,** including the rinse cycle. A canceled program can leave descaler residue in the pipes.
- **Set water hardness:** If you enter the hardness correctly in the menu, the machine will report at the appropriate interval.

**Water filters in the tank** significantly reduce limescale. However, they must be replaced according to the manufacturer's instructions, otherwise they will no longer be of any use or may even become contaminated. Many machines have their own setting for filter operation that you should activate.

With **portafilter machines**, descaling varies greatly depending on the type. It's worth taking a closer look at the instructions here, because with some devices only the water tank is filled with solution, with others the brewing group and steam lance have to be rinsed individually.

## How often do you need to descale?

The correct frequency depends on water hardness and usage. As a rough guide:

| device | soft water | hard water |
|---|---|---|
| kettle | every two to three months | approximately monthly or as soon as deposits are visible |
| Filter coffee machine | approximately quarterly | approximately every four to six weeks |
| Fully automatic, capsule machine | after displaying the machine | after displaying the machine |

Additionally, rely on your senses. Typical warning signs are:

- white flakes or a shimmer on tea and water,
- longer heating time or significantly louder bubbling,
- Coffee that is less hot or brews more slowly
- a machine that steams, hisses or drips more heavily.

## Slow down limescale in everyday life

You can't completely avoid limescale in hard water, but you can significantly reduce it.

- **Only heat as much water as you need.** Every liter that is boiled unnecessarily leaves behind limescale and costs electricity.
- **Pour out any remaining water.** Water that is left in the cooker or tank will evaporate and leave edges. Leave the lid open briefly after pouring to allow the interior to dry.
- **Fill the tank regularly** and occasionally wash out the water tank of the coffee machine with rinse water. This is also a question of hygiene, more about this in the guide [kitchen hygiene](/ratgeber/kuechenhygiene/).
- **Use a table water filter or filter cartridge** if you drink a lot of tea or coffee and change the cartridges on time.
- **Descale in a timely manner.** Thin layers come off in minutes, thick layers take hours and several passes.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Boil citric acid:** At high heat, new, difficult-to-dissolve deposits can form. Warm water is enough.
- **Vinegar in a fully automatic machine:** Many manufacturers advise against using it because seals suffer and the taste remains. Take the recommended remedy.
- **Rinse too little:** Acid residue tastes unpleasant. Better rinse again.
- **Mix descaler with other cleaners:** Acids must never come together with products containing chlorine. The rules for this can be found on our page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).
- **Dip the stove into the sink:** Water in contacts and electronics can destroy the device and become dangerous.
- **Splashes on natural stone:** Acid attacks marble and limestone. Wipe away drips on such countertops immediately with clean water.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I descale with baking soda or baking powder?

No, or just very bad. Baking soda is alkaline and does not dissolve limescale. Baking soda contains some acidulant, but far too little for a calcified heating plate. For lime you need an acid such as citric acid or a ready-made descaler. Which tasks baking soda performs well can be found in the guide [baking soda, soda and baking powder] (/ratgeber/natron-soda-backpulver/).

### Is the water immediately drinkable again after descaling?

Yes, if you rinse thoroughly. For a kettle, boil fresh water two to three times and pour it away; for coffee machines, run several tanks of clear water through them. If it still smells or tastes a bit sour, rinse again.

### Why is there a film forming on my tea?

With hard water, lime and the ingredients in the tea combine to form a thin skin on the surface. This is harmless, but unpleasant. Softer or filtered water and a freshly descaled kettle significantly reduce the effect.

### Can I use descaler in the dishwasher?

Descalers for coffee machines do not belong in the dishwasher. Correctly adjusted regenerating salt and rinse aid help against limescale in the dishwasher. We explain how this works in the guide [Cleaning the dishwasher] (/ratgeber/spuelmaschine-reinigen/). Treat limescale on fittings and shower walls as described in [Remove limescale in the shower](/ratgeber/kalk-in-der-dusche/).

You can find further instructions for appliances and surfaces in the [Cleaning the kitchen](/themen/kueche-reinigen/) section.

## Briefly summarized

Limescale is formed when hard water is heated and only dissolves with acid. Descale the kettle with citric acid in warm, not boiling water or with a ready-made descaler, remove any residue with a soft brush and rinse thoroughly. Filter coffee machines receive a descaling solution that runs through two stages and is rinsed with several passes of clear water. You always descale fully automatic machines and capsule machines using your own program using the recommended agent. How often this is necessary depends on the water hardness and usage. If you only heat as much water as necessary, pour out any remaining water and descale in a timely manner, you save time, electricity and hassle.
