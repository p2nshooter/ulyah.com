Many people pour detergent into the machine based on their feeling, usually too much, according to the motto “a lot helps a lot”. Others save and wonder why white towels turn gray and the machine smells musty. Both are a problem: too much detergent pollutes the skin and the environment, leaves residues in the laundry and costs money. Too little does not wash properly, leaves limescale in the machine and promotes odors.

The right amount depends on three things: the hardness of your water, how dirty the laundry is, and how full the drum is. In this guide you will find out how to dose correctly, which detergent is suitable for which laundry and when you can safely leave out fabric softener and hygiene conditioner.

## The three factors for the right amount

**1. The water hardness**

Hard water contains a lot of lime. The lime binds some of the detergent substances, which is why you need more detergent with hard water than with soft water. In Germany there are three hardness ranges:

| Hardness range | Calcium carbonate content | Approximately in degrees of German hardness |
|---|---|---|
| soft | less than 1.5 millimoles per liter | below 8.4 °dH |
| medium | 1.5 to 2.5 millimoles per liter | 8.4 to 14 °dH |
| hard | more than 2.5 millimoles per liter | over 14 °dH |

You can find out how hard your water is from your water supplier, usually on their website or on your water bill. The dosage information on the detergent pack is graded according to these hardness ranges.

**2. The degree of pollution**

- **Slightly soiled:** Clothing that has only been worn briefly, with no visible stains. Most everyday laundry is included.
- **Normally dirty:** Visible dirt or individual stains, towels, bed linen.
- **Heavily soiled:** Work clothing, sports clothing after training in the mud, kitchen towels, heavily soiled children's clothing.

Many people consider the dosage to be “normal” even though their laundry is only slightly soiled. Here you can often save a third of detergent without making the laundry any less clean.

**3. The loading**

The dosage information usually refers to a normal load, which is stated on the packaging. If you're half loaded you'll need less, and if you have a large drum that's fully loaded you'll need more. How full the drum should be depends on the program: for cotton, there is enough space above the laundry; for easy-care and delicate laundry, the drum should only be half full.

## This is how you dose correctly

1. **Find out water hardness** and remember.
2. **Sort laundry** according to color, material and temperature. More about this in the guide [Sorting laundry correctly] (/ratgeber/waesche-sortieren/).
3. **Assess the degree of pollution.**
4. **Read the appropriate amount on the package** and measure with the measuring cup, not by eye.
5. **Pre-treat for stains** instead of dosing the whole load higher.

**Tip:** Mark the amount you need most often on the measuring cup with a waterproof pen. Then you don't have to read it every time.

## Which detergent for what?

| detergent | Suitable for | Special features |
|---|---|---|
| Heavy-duty detergent (powder) | white and light linen, towels, bed linen | contains bleach and mostly optical brighteners and is particularly hygienic |
| Color detergent | colorful and dark laundry | without bleach or brighteners, protects colors |
| Mild detergent | Fine, synthetic, delicate fabrics | mild, for low temperatures |
| Wool detergent | Wool, cashmere, silk | without protein-splitting enzymes, protects protein fibers |
| Liquid detergent | colored laundry, greasy stains at low temperature | does not contain bleach |
| Special detergent | Black, sportswear, curtains | for special requirements |

**Powder or liquid?** Powdered heavy-duty detergents contain oxygen bleach, which lightens stains and kills germs. Liquid detergents cannot contain bleach because it is not stable in liquid. Powder is therefore the better choice for white laundry and for hygienic laundry at 60 degrees. Liquid detergents dissolve well at low temperatures and are suitable for colorful laundry and greasy dirt.

**Why color detergent for colorful items?** Optical brighteners make white laundry appear brighter, but change the color of colored and dark clothing. Bleach can fade colors. Color detergents do without this and often contain substances that prevent dissolved color from settling on other parts.

**Why wool detergent for wool?** Wool and silk are made of protein. Many heavy-duty detergents contain enzymes that break down protein stains such as blood or eggs. These enzymes also attack wool and silk. More about this in the guide [How to wash wool, cashmere and silk] (/ratgeber/wolle-und-seide-waschen/).

## Detergent caps and tabs

Detergent in pillows or tabs is convenient, but the amount is fixed. With soft water, small loads or lightly soiled laundry, you often use too much. However, with very hard water and large loads, a single cushion may not be enough.

**Important:** Liquid detergent pillows look like candy to children. Always keep them locked and out of the reach of children. If a child puts a pillow in their mouth, call poison control immediately.

## Fabric softener: usually unnecessary

Fabric softener makes laundry soft and fragrant, but has disadvantages:

- **Towels** absorb less water because the fabric softener coats the fibers.
- **Microfiber cloths** lose their effectiveness because the fine fibers stick together.
- **Functional clothing** breathes more poorly because the pores are closed.
- **The environment** is additionally burdened.
- **Sensitive skin** may react to fragrances.

You can get soft laundry even without fabric softener: with the right dosage, sufficient spin, shaking out before hanging and, for towels, a quick run in the dryer. The guide [Drying Laundry] (/ratgeber/waesche-trocknen/) explains how to dry laundry properly.

## Hygiene washer: only in exceptional cases

A hygienic dishwasher is not necessary for healthy households. Washing at 60 degrees with a heavy-duty detergent containing bleach is sufficient for towels, bed linen, underwear and kitchen towels. Hygienic dishwashers can be useful if infectious diseases occur in the household and laundry can only be washed at low temperatures. In such cases, talk to your doctor.

## Modular system for hard water

In regions with very hard water, a **modular system** can make sense. You combine a basic detergent without a softener with a separate softener, which you dose depending on the water hardness, and, if necessary, with a bleach for white laundry. This means you only need as much of each ingredient as necessary. This saves detergent and protects the environment.

## How you can recognize an incorrect dosage

**Too much detergent:**

- Laundry feels stiff or soapy, towels are scratchy.
- Lots of foam in the drum, foam oozing out of the dispenser compartment.
- Skin irritation and itching.
- Detergent residue in the folds.

**Too little detergent:**

- White laundry turns gray over time, this is called graying.
- Stains don't come out.
- Laundry smells musty after drying.
- The machine accumulates limescale and dirt and starts to smell.

If your machine smells, a wash cycle at 60 degrees or more with heavy-duty detergent in powder form will occasionally help. More about this in the guide [Cleaning the washing machine] (/ratgeber/waschmaschine-reinigen/).

## Wash at low temperatures

Modern detergents work well at 20 or 30 degrees, and low temperatures save a lot of energy. This is sufficient for most lightly soiled everyday clothing. There are still a few things you should keep in mind:

- **Towels, bed linen, underwear and kitchen towels** should be washed regularly at 60 degrees so that germs are reliably removed.
- **The machine occasionally needs heat:** If you only wash in cold water, you should wash it about once a month at 60 degrees or more with heavy-duty detergent in powder form to prevent deposits and odors from forming.
- **Grease dissolves better when warm:** For greasy stains, it is better to pre-treat them by washing cold.

## Allergies and sensitive skin

If you react to fragrances, you can find fragrance-free detergents for sensitive skin. Also make sure you use the dosage sparingly, because the less residue remains in the laundry, the less likely it is to irritate the skin. For baby laundry, a mild, fragrance-free detergent is sufficient; fabric softener is unnecessary.

## Storage

- Store detergent **dry and sealed** to prevent powder from clumping.
- **Keep out of the reach of children** and pets.
- **Do not transfer** into beverage bottles or other containers that may cause confusion.
- **Do not mix detergents** with each other or with cleaners. Which combinations are dangerous can be found on the page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Dose according to your feeling:** Take the measuring cup and the information on the package.
- **Don't know water hardness:** Ask your water supplier.
- **Dose lightly soiled laundry like normally soiled laundry:** You can save here.
- **Liquid detergent for whites:** Without bleach, white laundry will turn gray over time.
- **Fabric softener for towels and sportswear:** It impairs absorbency and breathability.
- **Drum overloaded:** Then the laundry won't get clean, no matter how much detergent is in it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much detergent do I need for half a load?

Approximately half to two thirds of the amount for a full load, depending on water hardness and contamination. Some packages give their own values ​​for this.

### Can I save on detergent without making my laundry dirtier?

Yes. Most everyday laundry is only slightly soiled. Dosing according to the instructions for light soiling and pre-treating individual stains. This often saves you a third of detergent.

### What do I do if there is too much foam?

Do not stop the wash cycle in the middle of the program. Let it run to the end and then start a washing program without detergent. Next time you dose less. You can find further instructions in the [Laundry & Care](/themen/waesche-und-pflege/) section.

## Briefly summarized

The correct amount of detergent depends on the hardness of the water, the degree of soiling and the load. Find out how hard your water is, honestly assess how dirty it is and measure it with a cup. Use heavy-duty detergent in powder form for whites and hygiene items, color detergent for colored items, delicate detergent for delicate items and wool detergent for wool and silk. Fabric softeners and hygiene conditioners are unnecessary in everyday life. Pre-treat stains instead of overdosing the whole load, and store detergent away from children.
