If you want to remove limescale but don't like the smell of vinegar, sooner or later you'll end up with citric acid. The white powder is almost odorless, inexpensive and easy to find in drugstores and supermarkets. Many commercially available limescale cleaners contain citric acid as an active ingredient. Nevertheless, it is not a tool that can be used everywhere without hesitation. Like any acid, it attacks sensitive materials and, if used incorrectly, can even cause new deposits.

In this guide you will find out what citric acid is and why it dissolves limescale, how to dose it correctly, why it shouldn't get too hot, where it is useful in the household, where it is harmful and why lemon juice from the fruit is not an equivalent substitute.

## What is Citric Acid?

Citric acid is an organic acid that occurs naturally in citrus fruits. Today it is mostly produced biotechnologically for household use. You can get it as a **powder** or as a **liquid concentrate**. As a food additive it has the designation E 330.

Your advantages when cleaning:

- **It dissolves limescale reliably,** because lime is a mineral that only dissolves with acid.
- **It hardly smells.** Unlike vinegar, it doesn't leave a pungent smell.
- **It is highly biodegradable.**
- **Powder has a long shelf life** and can be dosed precisely.

Nevertheless, it is an acid. Many packages state that it can cause serious eye irritation. Avoid splashes in the eyes, wear gloves when working for long periods of time and keep them out of the reach of children.

## Dose correctly

The right amount depends on the task. As a guide for powder:

| task | Dosage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Slight limescale on glass and fittings | about 1 tablespoon to 0.5 liters of warm water | Pour into a spray bottle and label |
| Shower head, aerator, kettle | about 1 to 2 tablespoons per 0.5 liters of warm water | Leave on for 30 to 60 minutes |
| Strong limescale and urine scale | Powder directly on the damp area or very concentrated solution | leave to act for several hours |

For **liquid concentrates**, the dosage on the bottle applies, as the strength varies depending on the product.

Dissolve the powder in **warm, not hot, water**. This will dissolve it faster than cold water, and you will avoid the problem explained in the next section.

## Step by step: prepare and apply the solution

1. **Check material:** Is the surface sensitive to acid, such as natural stone or aluminum? Then don't use it.
2. **Put on gloves** and open the window.
3. **Prepare warm water,** then stir in the powder until it is completely dissolved. Undissolved crystals can scratch sensitive surfaces.
4. **Apply targeted:** with a cloth, a spray bottle or as a bath for small parts.
5. **Let it take effect** without the solution drying. For vertical surfaces, a soaked kitchen paper towel helps.
6. **Wipe or brush** with a soft sponge or brush.
7. **Rinse thoroughly with clean water** and rub dry.

If limescale remains after the first pass, repeat the treatment. Several short passes protect joints and surfaces more than a single, very long one.

## Why citric acid shouldn't get too hot

When lime is dissolved, citric acid and lime form a compound called **calcium citrate**. It is highly soluble in cold and warm water, but much less soluble in very hot water. If citric acid is heated strongly, for example when boiled, calcium citrate can form a new, solid layer. This is annoying in an open kettle, but in the narrow pipes of a coffee machine it can lead to blockages.

Therefore:

- **Kettle:** Descale with warm water and citric acid, do not boil.
- **Coffee machines and fully automatic machines:** use the descaler recommended by the manufacturer. Many ready-made descalers are formulated in such a way that this problem does not occur.
- **Appliances with heating** such as washing machines and dishwashers should be cleaned according to the manufacturer's instructions instead of using self-mixed citric acid.

You can find the step-by-step instructions for the kettle and coffee machine in the guide [Descaling the kettle and coffee machine] (/ratgeber/entkalken-wasserkocher-kaffeemaschine/).

## Where citric acid helps

### In the bathroom

- **Shower screen and fittings:** Spray on the solution, allow it to take effect briefly, wipe with a soft sponge and rinse thoroughly. The complete instructions can be found in the guide [Removing limescale in the shower] (/ratgeber/kalk-in-der-dusche/).
- **Shower head:** Place in solution or place a bag with solution over the permanently mounted shower head.
- **Toilet:** Lower the water level, sprinkle powder on limestone edges and urine stone and leave to work for several hours. More about this in the guide [Cleaning the toilet and removing urine scale] (/ratgeber/toilette-reinigen-urinstein/).

### In the kitchen

- **Kettle:** Fill with warm solution, leave to work for 30 to 60 minutes, rinse and boil fresh water twice and pour away.
- **Sink and faucet:** Limescale edges on stainless steel and faucet can be removed with a cloth and a little solution. Then wipe clean and rub dry.
- **Glasses and vases:** Limescale deposits in vases and carafes will dissolve if you fill them with solution for a while.

### Around the house

- **Windows:** A little citric acid in window water helps with limescale stains on glass. More technology on this in the guide [Cleaning windows](/ratgeber/fenster-putzen/).
- **Watering cans and flower pot saucers:** White limescale edges can be removed with solution.

## Where citric acid is harmful

| material | What happens | More suitable |
|---|---|---|
| Marble, limestone, travertine | Surface becomes dull and rough | pH-neutral natural stone cleaner |
| Cement joints | are washed out with frequent use | Use briefly, moisten beforehand |
| Aluminum | can become discolored and attacked | mild dishwashing water |
| Enamel | becomes dull with frequent use | mild cleaners, acid only briefly |
| Coated or specially refined fittings | Surface can suffer | Manufacturer information |

Also be careful with **steam irons**: Many manufacturers advise against filling acid into the water tank and instead recommend a built-in self-cleaning or anti-limescale function. More about this in the [Ironing](/ratgeber/buegeln/) guide.

## Citric acid in finished cleaners

Many commercial bathroom and limescale cleaners contain citric acid, often together with surfactants, thickeners and fragrance. This has advantages: a gel adheres better to vertical surfaces and under the sink rim, and surfactants also dissolve soap residue. Homemade solution, on the other hand, is cheaper, odorless and without additives. The powder is sufficient for most household tasks, but a gel can be more practical for difficult areas.

## Never mix with chlorine

Citric acid is an acid. If it comes into contact with cleaners containing chlorine, such as some toilet cleaners, bleach or mold cleaners, toxic chlorine gas can be produced. Only use one product at a time and rinse thoroughly with water between two products. The most important rules are on our page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## Lemon juice is not a substitute

Many tips recommend removing limescale with a halved lemon or lemon juice. This only works with very light surfaces. Lemon juice contains significantly less acid than a powder solution, plus sugar, pulp and flavorings. These residues can stick, attract insects and leave stains. Lemon is the better choice for cooking and powder for cleaning. You can find more popular fact-checking tips in the guide [Home Remedy Myths Checked](/ratgeber/hausmittel-mythen/).

## Citric acid or vinegar?

| | Citric acid | Vinegar |
|---|---|---|
| smell | barely noticeable | clear, disappears after drying |
| Anti-limescale effect | good | good |
| Heating | do not use in boiling form | possible, but then it smells strong |
| Storage | Powder dry and long lasting | liquid, long lasting |
| Material limits | Natural stone, cement joints, aluminum | the same, plus seals in devices |

Both acids dissolve limescale. Citric acid scores points for its smell, vinegar for its availability. A mixture of both brings no advantage. You can read more about vinegar in the guide [Vinegar in the Household](/ratgeber/essig-im-haushalt/).

## Store properly

- **Dry and well sealed.** Powder attracts moisture and clumps.
- **In the original packaging** or in a clearly labeled container. White powders like citric acid, baking soda and sugar are easy to confuse.
- **Out of reach of children.**
- **Store finished solutions** in labeled spray bottles and use within a few weeks.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Boil citric acid:** New, difficult-to-dissolve deposits can form.
- **Acid on natural stone:** Marble and limestone are permanently damaged.
- **Combine with chlorine cleaner:** Dangerous to life.
- **Allow solution to dry:** Stains will remain. Work in sections and rinse.
- **Do not rinse:** Acid residues continue to attack joints and silicone.
- **Lemon juice instead of powder:** weaker and sticky.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is citric acid the same for food and cleaning?

Chemically, yes. When buying, pay attention to what a product is intended for. Cleaning products with citric acid often contain other substances such as surfactants and fragrance and do not belong in food.

### Can I use citric acid to remove rust stains?

Light rust stains on white cotton can sometimes be lightened with a citric acid solution. For colorful fabrics, test in a hidden area beforehand whether the color holds and then wash the item thoroughly.

### Can I use citric acid on silicone joints?

Short contact usually does not harm silicone. Then rinse the joints thoroughly, as a film of acid and soap residues promotes mold. Citric acid does not help with discolored silicone. How to proceed is described in the guide [Preventing and removing mold in the bathroom] (/ratgeber/schimmel-im-bad/).

### How long does citric acid have to work?

For light limescale, a few minutes are enough, for shower heads and kettles 30 to 60 minutes, and for urine scale several hours. It is better to repeat the treatment rather than prolong it.

## Briefly summarized

Citric acid is a low-odor, effective limescale remover for kettles, shower heads, fittings, glass and toilets. Dissolve the powder in warm, not hot, water, because poorly soluble calcium citrate can form at high heat, and use the manufacturer's products for coffee machines and devices with heating. Do not use citric acid on natural stone, aluminum and only briefly on cement joints and enamel. Never mix with chlorine cleaners, always rinse thoroughly and store the powder dry, labeled and out of the reach of children.
