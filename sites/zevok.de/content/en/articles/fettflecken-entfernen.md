A splash of frying grease on your T-shirt, a blob of salad dressing on your pants, the pizza that ended up on the sofa, or the black streak from bicycle chain oil on your trouser leg: grease stains are among the most common stains of all. They are tricky because they do not disappear with water alone. On the contrary, some grease stains are still there after washing, just invisible, and appear again weeks later as a yellowish edge.

The good news is: Grease can be removed using simple tools that almost everyone has at home. What is crucial is the correct order and a product that actually dissolves fat. In this guide you will learn how to remove fresh and old grease stains from clothing, upholstery and carpets and what helps with sunscreen, collar edges and chain oil.

## Why water alone is not enough

Fat and water don't mix. That's why water rolls off a grease stain and doesn't really reach the grease in the fiber. To dissolve fat, you need a substance that mediates between fat and water. This is what surfactants, which are contained in dishwashing liquid, laundry detergent, gall soap and shampoo, do. They surround the fat droplets so that the water can wash them away.

Unlike protein stains such as blood, **warmth** helps with fat: warm water softens fat and makes it easier to dissolve. Therefore, wash clothes with grease stains as warmly as the care label allows, but only after you have pre-treated the stain. The guide [Removing stains: The basic rules] (/ratgeber/flecken-entfernen-grundregeln/) provides an overview of all types of stains.

## Fresh grease stains on clothing

1. **Absorb excess grease.** Blot the stain with paper towels without rubbing. Rubbing pushes the fat deeper into the fibers and distributes it.
2. **Let the grease absorb.** Sprinkle some cornstarch on the stain and let it work for 15 to 30 minutes. The starch draws some of the fat out of the fabric. Then gently brush or shake off.
3. **Incorporate dishwashing liquid.** Pour a few drops of dishwashing liquid directly onto the stain and massage gently with your fingers or a soft brush. Dishwashing liquid is specifically designed to dissolve grease.
4. **Let it take effect.** Ten minutes is enough for fresh stains.
5. **Rinse.** Rinse the stain with warm water, as warm as the fabric can tolerate.
6. **Wash.** Wash the item at the highest temperature allowed. After washing, check whether the stain has disappeared before putting the item in the dryer.

**Galle soap** is a good alternative to dishwashing liquid. Wet the stain, rub the soap on it, let it work for a moment and rinse. On colored and delicate fabrics, you should test them in a hidden area beforehand. More about gall soap in the guide [How to use gall soap correctly] (/ratgeber/gallseife/).

## Old and dried grease stains

Fat that sits in the fiber for a long time changes: it oxidizes, becomes tough and turns yellow. Such stains require more patience.

1. **Pre-treat** with dishwashing liquid or gall soap as described above and leave on for longer, for example half an hour.
2. **Soak** in warm water with a little detergent for a few hours.
3. **Wash** at the highest temperature allowed.
4. **Repeat** if a shadow is still visible.

For **white, robust fabrics**, oxygen bleach according to the instructions on the package helps against yellowish edges, provided the care label allows bleaching.

## Collar edges and armpit areas

Gray or yellowish edges on shirt collars, cuffs and hats are caused by skin oil and sweat. They build up slowly and are often not completely removed with normal washing.

- **Apply shampoo or gall soap** to the edge with a soft toothbrush. Shampoo is designed to dissolve skin oil.
- **Let it soak in** and then wash as usual.
- **Pre-treat regularly,** then the edges won't build up at all.

Yellow spots under the armpits on white shirts are often caused by a mixture of sweat and deodorant. Treat with gall soap or detergent paste before washing and occasionally use oxygen bleach on white cotton.

## Sunscreen

Sunscreen contains oils and UV filters. On light-colored fabrics it often leaves yellowish edges that only become visible after washing. Sometimes yellow-orange stains appear after washing when certain UV filters react with minerals in tap water.

- **Treat before washing:** Apply dishwashing liquid or gall soap to the areas, let it work and wash it out.
- **No chlorine bleach:** It may increase discoloration.
- **For orange stains**, a stain remover against rust or minerals often helps. Read the instructions and test in a hidden area.
- **Prevention:** Allow sunscreen to fully absorb before putting on light-colored clothing.

## Bicycle chain oil, grease and car grease

Chain oil is particularly stubborn because, in addition to grease, it also contains fine metal abrasion, which turns the stain dark.

1. **Do not rub.** Carefully remove excess fat with kitchen paper.
2. Generously incorporate **dishwashing liquid or gall soap** and allow it to take effect for a long time.
3. **Wash out** with warm water and use a soft brush.
4. **Repeat** then wash at the highest temperature allowed.

On robust cotton fabrics, **white spirit** can help dissolve the grease. It is highly flammable: use it only in small quantities, with the window open and away from open flames and cigarettes. Test it in a hidden area first and then wash the item thoroughly before it comes near a heat source. For sensitive fabrics and functional clothing, a special stain remover or textile cleaning is a safer choice.

## Delicate fabrics: wool, silk and viscose

- **Wool and silk** are sensitive to rubbing and heat. Gently blot the fat with kitchen paper, sprinkle on cornstarch and let it work. Then dab with lukewarm water and a drop of wool detergent or very little dishwashing liquid. For valuable parts, it is better to take them to the dry cleaners.
- **Viscose** becomes very sensitive when wet. Do not rub, do not wring, handle with care.

The guide [washing wool, cashmere and silk] (/ratgeber/wolle-und-seide-waschen/) explains how you should generally care for these materials.

## Grease stains on upholstery and carpet

You can't just put upholstery and carpets into the machine. Therefore work with little water:

1. **Absorb fat** with kitchen paper.
2. Sprinkle **cornstarch** on the stain, leave to work for a few hours and vacuum.
3. **Make a dishwashing liquid** using warm water and a few drops of dishwashing liquid. Moisten a white cloth with it and dab the stain from the outside in.
4. **Dab with clear water** so that no soap residue remains. Soap residue attracts new dirt.
5. **Pat dry** and allow to dry thoroughly.

If there are large or old stains on light-colored upholstery, it is worth using an upholstery cleaner or professional cleaning. More about this in the guide [Removing stains from carpets, sofas and mattresses] (/ratgeber/flecken-teppich-polster/).

## Grease on wallpaper and paper

Grease splashes on the kitchen wall or a butter stain in a book can often be alleviated with an old trick: place absorbent blotting paper or kitchen paper on the stain and briefly go over it with a lukewarm iron without steam. The fat becomes soft and soaks into the paper. Repeat the process with fresh paper. Be careful with vinyl wallpapers and coated surfaces as heat can damage them. Therefore, test in an inconspicuous area and only use a low temperature.

## Grease on leather

Smooth leather absorbs grease quickly. Immediately blot fresh stains with kitchen paper and sprinkle on cornstarch, which you brush off after a few hours. Water and detergent can leave marks on leather. There are special leather cleaners for stubborn stains. For suede and valuable pieces, ask a specialist shop.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Treat with water only:** Water does not dissolve grease. Use dish soap or gall soap.
- **Rubbing:** The fat is pushed deeper into the fiber.
- **In the dryer before the stain is gone:** Heat sets grease stains and turns them yellow.
- **Too much detergent on upholstery:** Soap residue attracts dirt. Always blot clearly.
- **Naphtha near flames:** It is highly flammable.
- **Mixing agents:** Different cleaners never belong together. The rules are on the page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does the grease stain come back after washing?

Often it wasn't gone at all, just invisible in the wet fabric. As it dries or after some time, the fat oxidizes and becomes visible as a yellowish edge. Therefore, always treat grease stains before washing and check them before the item goes into the dryer.

### Does baby powder help with grease stains?

Yes, similar to cornstarch, powder absorbs some of the fat. Afterwards, you still have to treat the stain with dishwashing liquid or gall soap so that the rest disappears.

### What helps with grease stains on wood?

On painted wood, a damp cloth with a little detergent is usually sufficient. Grease absorbs into oiled or untreated wood. Sprinkle on cornstarch, let it work and then treat the area with the care oil intended for the wood. You can find further instructions in the [Remove stains](/themen/flecken-entfernen/) section.

## Briefly summarized

Grease does not dissolve in water, but rather with surfactants from dishwashing liquid, gall soap, detergent or shampoo. Blot off any excess fat, let the cornstarch absorb some of it and then work in dishwashing liquid or gall soap. Then wash as warmly as the label allows and check the stain before tumble drying. Pre-treat and soak old stains for longer, loosen collar edges with shampoo, patiently treat chain oil several times. Treat upholstery and carpets with a little water and always blot them clean.
