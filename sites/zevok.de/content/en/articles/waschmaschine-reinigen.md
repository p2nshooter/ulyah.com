One would think that a machine that cleans laundry should itself be clean. But exactly where water, detergent and dirt constantly come together, deposits form that you can't see: in the dispenser compartment, in the folds of the door seal, in the lint filter and on the outside of the drum. The consequences are a musty smell, gray streaks on the laundry and, in the worst case, water that no longer drains properly.

In this guide you will find out how to recognize a dirty washing machine, how to clean the individual parts step by step, how to descale safely and what habits you can use to prevent odors from occurring in the first place.

## How you can tell that the machine needs cleaning

- The machine smells musty, especially if the door has been closed for a long time.
- Freshly washed laundry doesn't smell fresh or even smells unpleasant.
- Black dots or slimy deposits can be seen in the door seal.
- Detergent residue and dark deposits have formed in the dispenser compartment.
- There are gray spots or lint on the laundry.
- The water drains slowly or the machine shows an error when pumping out.

## Why washing machines smell

The most common cause is a **biofilm**, a slimy layer of detergent residue, skin flakes, fibers and bacteria. It forms especially when

- **is almost only washed at low temperatures**,
- **only liquid detergent** is used that does not contain bleach,
- **too much detergent or fabric softener** is dosed, the residue of which is deposited,
- **the door is closed after washing** and the moisture cannot escape,
- **wet laundry** stays in the drum for hours.

The good news: With a thorough cleaning and a few habits, you can get the problem under control.

## Step 1: Clean the dispenser chamber

Remnants of detergent and fabric softener collect in the dispenser compartment, and mold often forms unnoticed in its housing.

1. **Remove the chamber.** With most machines, you pull the drawer out as far as it will go, press a release lever, usually in the fabric softener compartment, and pull it out all the way. The instructions show how to do it with your model.
2. **Rinse.** Rinse the chamber with warm water and remove any residue with a dishwashing brush or an old toothbrush. Many chambers have removable inserts for the fabric softener, which you can also clean.
3. **Clean the case.** Shine a flashlight into the opening from which you pulled the chamber. The nozzles from which the water comes are located at the top. Wipe off deposits with a brush and warm water with a little dishwashing liquid.
4. **Let dry** and reinsert the chamber.

## Step 2: Clean door seal

The rubber sleeve around the door has folds that trap water, lint, hair, and sometimes coins or hair clips.

1. **Open the folds.** Gently pull the seal forward so you can see into the folds.
2. **Remove foreign objects** such as hair, lint, coins and tissue residue.
3. **Wipe** with a cloth, warm water and a little dishwashing liquid or all-purpose cleaner. Work all around and also on the bottom, where most of the water collects.
4. **Wipe dry.**

**Black dots** in the seal are usually mold that has embedded itself in the rubber. For stubborn cases, there are special cleaners for seals that you can use exactly according to the instructions. However, some stains cannot be completely removed. If the seal is cracked or severely damaged, customer service can replace it. Important: Never use chlorine-containing and acidic products at the same time, as this creates toxic chlorine gas. You can find the rules on the page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## Step 3: Clean the lint filter

The lint filter protects the drain pump from foreign bodies. If it is clogged, the water drains poorly and can smell unpleasant.

1. **Turn off the device** and, if necessary, pull out the plug. If you just washed with hot water, wait until the water has cooled down.
2. **Open the flap.** The sieve is usually located behind a small flap at the bottom of the machine.
3. **Collect water.** Place a large towel on the floor and prepare a shallow dish. Many machines have a small emergency drain hose that you can use to drain the remaining water first.
4. **Slowly unscrew the strainer.** Let the water run into the bowl. There may be more than the bowl holds, so proceed gradually.
5. **Clean.** Remove lint, hair, coins and other foreign objects and rinse the sieve under running water. Look into the opening with a flashlight and remove whatever is there.
6. **Replace firmly** and close the flap. The next time you wash, check whether everything is tight.

Clean the lint filter about every two to three months and also if the water drains slowly or the machine reports an error while pumping out.

## Step 4: Clean drum and lines

To remove the biofilm in the drum, tub and hoses, a **hot empty wash cycle** is usually enough:

1. **Leave drum empty.**
2. **Pour heavy-duty detergent in powder form** into the dispenser compartment, approximately the amount for a normal load. The bleach it contains works against germs and odors.
3. **Start program at 60 to 90 degrees**, depending on what your machine offers. Many devices have their own drum cleaning program.

Alternatively, you can use a machine cleaner. Follow the instructions carefully and do not mix it with other products.

## Descaling: when and how

In regions with hard water, limescale builds up on the heating rod and drum. Many modern detergents already contain softeners, so additional descaling is not always necessary. Check what the manufacturer of your machine recommends.

- **Use citric acid or special descalers** for washing machines according to the instructions.
- **Vinegar is often recommended,** but many manufacturers advise against it because acid can permanently damage rubber seals and hoses.
- **Do not descale too hot** if you use citric acid. At high temperatures, poorly soluble calcium citrate can form. Stick to the temperature on the package.

You can find out more about citric acid and its limits in the guide [Citric acid in the household](/ratgeber/zitronensaeure/).

## This keeps the machine clean

- **Leave the door and dispenser compartment open** after washing so that the moisture can dry. Make sure children and pets do not climb in.
- **Take out laundry immediately** and hang it up.
- **Wipe seal dry** after the last wash of the day.
- **Wash in hot water once a month** at 60 degrees or more and use heavy-duty detergent in powder form, for example with towels and bed linen.
- **Dose correctly,** especially fabric softener, the residue of which tends to build up and forms an ideal breeding ground for germs. More in the guide [How to properly dose detergent] (/ratgeber/waschmittel-dosieren/).
- **Empty pockets** before washing. Coins, screws and hair clips damage the drum and pump.
- **Put underwired bras in the laundry net**. Loose brackets can become wedged between the drum and the tub.

## How often which task?

| task | How often |
|---|---|
| Leave the door and dispenser compartment open | after every wash |
| Wipe the door seal dry | weekly |
| Wash on a hot cycle with heavy-duty detergent | monthly |
| Thoroughly clean the dispenser compartment | every one to two months |
| Clean the lint filter | every two to three months |
| Descaling | according to the manufacturer's instructions and water hardness |

## Top loader

In top loaders, the drum lies horizontally and is filled from above. Cleaning works in a similar way: clean the dispenser compartment in the lid, let the drum run hot and clean the lint filter, which is often located in the drum or behind a flap, regularly. For top loaders, make sure the drum doors are closed and locked into place after washing before starting the machine again.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Wash cold only:** Without occasional hot wash, biofilm will form.
- **Close door immediately:** Moisture remains in the drum. Leave the door open.
- **Forgot the lint filter:** The water drains poorly and the pump is strained.
- **Too much fabric softener:** Residues build up in the chamber and pipes.
- **Mixing cleaners:** Chlorine-containing and acidic agents form toxic gas.
- **Open the sieve when removing without a towel:** The remaining water ends up on the floor.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does baking soda help against odors in the washing machine?

Baking soda can reduce odors, but is no substitute for thorough cleaning or a hot wash cycle with heavy-duty detergent. More about the limits of baking soda in the guide [Baking soda, soda and baking powder] (/ratgeber/natron-soda-backpulver/).

### Can I use dishwasher tablets to clean the washing machine?

This tip is often circulated on the Internet, but is not recommended. Dishwasher tablets are made for a different machine and different conditions, can foam heavily and put a strain on seals. Use heavy-duty detergent in powder form or a machine cleaner according to the instructions.

### What do I do with the washing machine when I travel?

Leave the door and dispenser compartment open so that nothing gets musty, and close the tap to which the machine is connected. This also prevents water from escaping if the hose is broken while no one is at home.

### Why is my laundry stained after washing?

Gray or greasy stains can come from biofilm in the machine, too much fabric softener, or an overloaded drum. Clean the machine, dose correctly and leave enough space in the drum.

### When should I call customer service?

If the water does not drain despite the lint filter being clean, the machine is leaking, makes unusual noises or displays error messages that cannot be remedied. Do not open the device yourself. You can find further instructions in the [Laundry & Care](/themen/waesche-und-pflege/) section.

## Briefly summarized

Musty smells and gray deposits are usually caused by biofilm from detergent residues, fibers and bacteria. Clean the dispenser compartment including the housing, wipe out the door seal, clean the lint filter with a towel and bowl and run the empty drum with heavy-duty powder detergent at 60 to 90 degrees. Descale according to the manufacturer's instructions. Leave the door and dispenser compartment open after each wash cycle, take out the laundry immediately, wash in hot water once a month and use sparingly so that the machine stays fresh for the long term.
