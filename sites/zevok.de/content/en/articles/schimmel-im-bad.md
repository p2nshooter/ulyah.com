Black dots in the shower corner, a dark edge on the silicone joint, gray spots on the ceiling above the bathtub: mold in the bathroom is one of the most common problems in apartments. No wonder, as there is hardly any other room in which so much moisture occurs in such a small area. After a hot shower, the air in the bathroom is saturated with water vapor, which settles on cool walls, tiles and windows. If this moisture regularly remains for too long, mold finds ideal conditions.

Mold is not just a visual problem. Its spores can cause allergies and strain the respiratory tract. That's why it's worth taking it seriously, understanding the cause and proceeding correctly. In this guide you will find out why mold occurs, how you can prevent it with ventilation and small habits, how you can safely remove small stains and when you should stay away from it and get help.

## Why mold occurs in the bathroom

Mold is everywhere in the air. But they only grow where they can find **moisture** and some **food** for a long time. In the bathroom they get both: water from the shower and bathtub and nutrients from soap residue, skin flakes and dust.

Typical causes are:

- **Not enough ventilation:** The water vapor after showering stays in the room and condenses.
- **Cold surfaces:** Exterior walls, corners and window reveals are cooler than the air in the room. Moisture condenses there first. Experts speak of **thermal bridges**.
- **Wet surfaces that never dry:** Tiles, grout and silicone remain wet unless the water is removed.
- **Dry laundry in the bathroom** without sufficient ventilation.
- **Structural defects:** Leaking pipes, damaged seals behind the tiles or water penetrating from outside.

A **hygrometer** helps to check your own feeling. High humidity is normal after showering. What is crucial is that it then falls quickly again. In everyday life, around 40 to 60 percent relative humidity is considered good.

## Ventilate properly: the most important measure

**With window:**

1. **After showering or bathing** open the window wide, i.e. ventilate it suddenly instead of tilting it.
2. Ventilate for about five to ten minutes, or longer in summer, until the mirror and tiles are free of fog.
3. **Keep the door to the rest of the apartment closed** so that the steam does not move into cooler rooms.
4. **Then close the window** and heat the bathroom normally so that the walls don't cool down.

A permanently tilted window provides little ventilation, but cools the wall around the window significantly. This is where mold is particularly likely to form.

**Without windows:** Many indoor bathrooms have an electric fan. Let it run for a sufficient amount of time after showering, often 15 to 30 minutes. Keep the ventilation grille clean, as a dusty grille reduces performance. To ensure that enough air can flow in, the bathroom door should have a gap at the bottom or a ventilation grille.

## Small habits to combat moisture

- **Stripping:** Remove the shower wall and tiles with a squeegee after showering. This removes a large portion of the water that would otherwise evaporate.
- **Wipe silicone joints dry,** especially in the corners of the shower.
- **Hang towels and bath mat** to dry and change regularly. How to dry laundry without moisture problems can be found in the guide [Drying laundry] (/ratgeber/waesche-trocknen/).
- **Unfold the shower curtain** so that it dries and wash it regularly in the washing machine according to the care label.
- **Keep your distance:** Cabinets and shelves on outside walls need a few centimeters of space to allow air to circulate.
- **Rinse off soap residue:** Shower gel and shampoo on tiles and grout lines are food for mold.

## Recognize and correctly classify mold

**Black, gray or greenish spots** on joints, silicone, ceiling or wall are usually mold. It often also smells musty.

**Pink or orange slime** on the drain, in shower corners or on the toilet is usually not mold, but a coating of bacteria. It disappears with normal cleaning and can be easily controlled by rinsing and drying.

**Size and depth** determine the procedure. The Federal Environment Agency makes a rough distinction between small, superficial infestations, which residents may be able to remove themselves, and larger or deeper damage, for which experts are responsible. The guideline for a small infestation is an area of ​​**less than about half a square meter** that is only superficially affected.

## When you shouldn't remove mold yourself

Get help instead of cleaning yourself if:

- **the area is larger** than approximately half a square meter,
- **the mold sits deep** in plaster, wallpaper or masonry,
- **he keeps coming back** even though you ventilate and clean,
- **Water from a pipe or from outside** could be the cause,
- **People in the household** have allergies, asthma, other respiratory diseases or a weakened immune system. In general, you should not remove mold yourself.

**In rented apartments** you should also report the mold to the landlord, preferably in writing and with photos. He must be able to clarify the cause and you can document the condition.

## Safely remove small stains

For small, superficial infestations on smooth surfaces such as tiles, joints or painted surfaces, proceed as follows:

1. **Protect:** Wear gloves, safety glasses and an FFP2 mask. This way you won't inhale any spores or get splashes in your eyes.
2. **Ventilation:** Open the window, close the door to the rest of the apartment.
3. **Do not brush or scrape dry:** This would throw spores into the air.
4. **Apply agent:** For smooth surfaces, **Alcohol with around 70 to 80 percent** is suitable, for example ethanol or isopropanol from the pharmacy. Apply it with a cloth. Alcohol is flammable: no open flames, no smoking and ventilate well.
5. **Wipe with a damp cloth:** Wipe the surface with a damp disposable cloth and use the cloth only once.
6. **Disposal:** Place used cloths immediately in a bag, close and dispose of with residual waste.
7. **Dry and observe:** Allow the area to dry thoroughly and check in the following weeks.

**Chlorine-containing mold removers** bleach the stains so much that they become invisible. However, they irritate the respiratory tract and skin and should never come into contact with acidic cleaners because this produces toxic chlorine gas. If you use them, do so only according to instructions, sparingly and with the window open. All the rules can be found on our page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

**Vinegar** is often recommended, but is not very suitable for plaster containing lime because the acid is neutralized by the lime. More about popular but ineffective tips can be found in the guide [Home Remedies Myths Checked](/ratgeber/hausmittel-mythen/).

## Mold in the silicone

Silicone joints are particularly vulnerable because they often remain damp and retain soap residue. If the mold is only superficial, a mold remover can lighten the stains. But often it has **grown into the silicone**. Then it comes back after a short while, no matter how thoroughly you clean.

In this case you have to renew the joint:

1. Cut out the old silicone completely with a cutter or joint knife.
2. Remove any residue with a silicone remover and a cloth.
3. Clean the joint thoroughly, wipe with alcohol and allow to **dry completely**.
4. Apply new sanitary silicone with fungicidal treatment and smooth it out.
5. Allow the joint to harden according to the manufacturer's instructions before using the shower again.

The guide [Cleaning tile joints] (/ratgeber/fliesenfugen-reinigen/) explains how to care for cement and silicone joints in everyday life.

## Mold on walls and ceiling

Stains on the ceiling above the shower or on the outside wall almost always indicate condensation. You can treat small, superficial areas of paint with alcohol as described above. **Simply painting over** doesn't help: the mold continues to grow under the paint and comes through again. Special paints against mold only make sense once the cause has been eliminated and the infestation has been removed.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Permanently tilted window:** The wall cools down and the air is hardly exchanged.
- **Brush off mold dry:** Spores are stirred up and distributed.
- **Combine acid and chlorine:** Dangerous to life. Always use only one agent.
- **Paint over or siliconize over mold:** It continues to grow underneath.
- **Treat only the stains:** If there are no changes in ventilation and humidity, the mold will come back.
- **Remove large infestations yourself:** This belongs in the hands of professionals.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should I ventilate after showering?

About five to ten minutes with the window wide open until the mirror and tiles are no longer steamed up. In summer, when the outside air is warm and humid, it takes longer. Without a window, the fan should run for 15 to 30 minutes.

### Is mold dangerous in the bathroom?

Mold spores can cause allergies and irritate the respiratory tract, especially in sensitive people. Small stains are no reason to panic, but they should be removed promptly and the cause remedied. If you have any health problems, speak to your doctor’s office.

### What do I do with a shower curtain full of mildew?

According to the care label, many shower curtains made of plastic fibers can be put in the washing machine. Wash it there and let it dry spread out. If the stains remain, a new curtain is often the better solution.

## Briefly summarized

Mold in the bathroom occurs when moisture stays on cool surfaces for too long. The most important prevention is proper ventilation: after showering, open it wide instead of tilting it, and without a window, let the fan run long enough. Remove tiles, wipe dry grout, and dry towels and shower curtain well. You can remove small, superficial stains with protective equipment and 70 to 80 percent alcohol without dry brushing. Mold in silicone usually requires a new joint. For large, deep or recurring stains, for people with sensitive health and in rented apartments, call in experts or the landlord.
