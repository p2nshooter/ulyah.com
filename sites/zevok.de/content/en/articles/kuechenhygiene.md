The kitchen is the place in the home where hygiene matters most. Raw meat, eggs, vegetables with soil, wet cloths and warm temperatures come together here. Many foodborne illnesses that people attribute to restaurants actually occur at home. The good news: It's not expensive disinfectants that help combat most risks, but rather a few simple habits that you quickly internalize.

In this guide you will find out where most germs collect in the kitchen, how often you should change dishcloths and sponges, why you need different cutting boards and why you shouldn't wash poultry, how to set up the refrigerator correctly and store leftovers safely.

## Where germs collect in the kitchen

| Location | Why | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Dishwashing sponge and dishcloth | damp, warm, full of leftover food | change frequently, wash hot, let dry |
| cutting boards | Meat juice and vegetable soil in grooves | separate boards, rinse with hot water, replace deeply scratched ones |
| sink and drain | Leftover food and standing water | Rinse daily and empty the sieve |
| Handles and switches | are touched with unwashed hands | wipe regularly |
| Refrigerator | Food with germs, dripping water | correct temperature, remove spills immediately |
| hands | transfer germs between foods | wash thoroughly and often |

## Wash your hands: the most important rule

Clean hands prevent more infections than any cleaning product. Wash your hands thoroughly with soap for at least 20 seconds:

- before you start cooking,
- after touching raw meat, poultry, fish or eggs,
- after going to the toilet and after changing diapers,
- after contact with pets and after emptying the trash can,
- after you blow your nose.

Dry hands with a clean towel or paper towel designed for hands only, not the tea towel.

## Dishwashing sponges and dishcloths

Damp sponges and rags are ideal breeding grounds for bacteria. If you wipe the work surface with it, you often spread the germs properly.

- **Change frequently.** You should replace dishwashing sponges approximately weekly, or more often if used heavily. Studies have shown that methods such as heating in the microwave do not permanently reduce the number of germs.
- **Washable dishcloths** are a good alternative. Wash them at at least 60 degrees with heavy-duty detergent and use a fresh cloth every day or every two days.
- **Allow to dry thoroughly.** Hang towels spread out after use, not crumpled up next to the sink.
- **Different towels for different tasks:** one for dishes, one for countertops, and a separate towel for hands. A color system helps with this. More about this in the guide [Cleaning Efficiently](/ratgeber/putzen-reihenfolge/).
- **After raw meat** clean the surface with kitchen paper and hot soapy water and throw the paper away immediately.

## cutting boards

**Use separate boards.** Use a different board for raw meat, poultry and fish than for vegetables, fruit, bread and cheese that are no longer heated. Colored boards or markers will help not to confuse them.

**Clean properly.** After each use, rinse boards with hot water and dish soap and brush out the grooves. Plastic boards can usually be put in the dishwasher, which is particularly hygienic. Allow all boards to dry completely while standing upright.

**Wood or plastic?** Both are fine if cleaned and dried well. Wooden boards do not belong in the dishwasher and are occasionally cleaned with food-safe oil. You should replace boards with deep notches and cracks because germs can settle there.

## Raw poultry and meat

Raw poultry often carries bacteria such as Campylobacter and Salmonella. To prevent them from spreading to other foods:

- **Do not wash poultry.** When rinsing under the tap, splashes spread germs to the sink, work surface and dishes. Heating kills the bacteria and washing is not necessary.
- **Dispose of packaging and defrosting water** immediately and do not allow them to come into contact with other food.
- **Use your own knives and boards** for raw meat or rinse them immediately in hot water after use.
- **Cook well.** Poultry and minced meat must be heated through completely. The guideline is a core temperature of at least 70 degrees for two minutes. A kitchen thermometer provides security.
- **Clean the work surface afterwards** with hot soapy water.

## Use the refrigerator correctly

- **Temperature:** In the fridge it should be a maximum of 7 degrees, better around 4 to 5 degrees in the middle. A refrigerator thermometer shows whether the setting is correct.
- **Use the cold zones:** It is usually coldest on the glass plate above the vegetable compartment. Raw meat and fish belong there, well packaged so that no juice drips onto other foods. Dairy products in the middle, drinks and butter in the door, vegetables in the vegetable compartment.
- **Do not overfill** to allow air to circulate.
- **Wipe up any spills immediately** and thoroughly clean the refrigerator regularly. Instructions in the guide [Cleaning the refrigerator] (/ratgeber/kuehlschrank-reinigen/).

## Store leftovers safely

- **Cool quickly:** You should not leave cooked food on the stove overnight. Place leftovers in shallow containers to cool more quickly and refrigerate within about two hours.
- **Store covered** in closed containers.
- **Use quickly:** If possible, eat cooked leftovers within one to two days.
- **Reheat Thoroughly:** Make leftovers hot through and through when reheating.
- **If in doubt, throw away:** If it smells or looks a little unusual, do not try. Mold on soft foods like yogurt, bread or jam means the whole product belongs in the trash.

Pregnant women, infants, the elderly and people with weakened immune systems should be particularly careful. Stricter recommendations apply to certain foods such as raw milk cheese, raw fish and raw eggs. If you have any questions, talk to your doctor.

## Fruits, vegetables and eggs

**Wash fruits and vegetables** thoroughly under running water before eating, even if you peel them, because when cutting, soil and germs from the peel get onto the flesh. Toss the lettuce and herbs in plenty of water so that the soil and sand sink down. Raw vegetables that are used to garnish raw dishes do not belong on the meat board.

Store **eggs** in the refrigerator and do not wash them, as water can damage the natural protective layer of the shell. For dishes with raw eggs such as tiramisu or homemade mayonnaise, only use very fresh eggs and chill them immediately. Dirty or damaged eggs do not belong in raw foods. Wash your hands after serving.

## Pets in the kitchen

Pets do not belong on work surfaces or dining tables. Feed them using your own bowls, which you wash separately from the family dishes, and wash your hands after feeding. You treat raw animal food like raw meat: on its own board, with your own cutlery and then cleaning the surface.

## Sink, drain and trash can

- **Rinse the sink daily** and wipe with dish soap. Do not leave food residue in the sieve.
- **Flush the drain** occasionally with hot water and clean the strainer.
- **Empty the trash can regularly,** especially with organic waste in summer, and wash the bucket out and let it dry if necessary.
- **Do not leave dishes out for days.** Dried residue is harder to remove and attracts fruit flies. The main thing that helps against fruit flies is to cover ripe fruit or put it in the refrigerator.

## Do I need disinfectant?

In a healthy household this is not necessary in everyday life. Hot water, dish soap, a clean cloth and thorough drying are all you need for the kitchen. The regular use of disinfectants has no demonstrable benefit, can irritate the skin and respiratory tract and pollutes the environment.

Exceptions can make sense if someone in the household suffers from a contagious gastrointestinal disease or is severely immunocompromised. Then talk to your doctor about what measures are necessary. Never mix disinfectants with other cleaners. The rules are on the page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **One sponge for everything, for weeks:** Change sponges often and use washable cloths.
- **A board for meat and salad:** Germs get on foods that are not heated.
- **Wash chicken under the tap:** Splashes spread bacteria.
- **Leftovers on the stove overnight:** Bacteria multiply quickly at room temperature.
- **Fridge too warm:** Check the temperature with a thermometer.
- **Tea towel for drying hands:** Keep the towel and tea towel separate.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I change the tea towel?

Ideally every one or two days and immediately after you have used it to dry something other than clean dishes. Wash tea towels at 60 degrees.

### Is the dishwasher more hygienic than washing by hand?

Generally yes, because it works with higher temperatures and stronger detergent than your hands can tolerate. But in order for it to stay clean itself, it needs care. More in the guide [Cleaning the dishwasher] (/ratgeber/spuelmaschine-reinigen/).

### Can I still eat food after the best-before date?

Often yes. The best before date indicates until when an unopened product will at least retain its properties if stored correctly. Then you can check whether it still looks, smells and tastes good. The situation is different when it comes to the use-by date, which is written on perishable foods such as raw minced meat: you should no longer eat them after this date.

### How do I clean wooden spoons and wooden boards?

Rinse thoroughly with hot water and detergent and allow to dry upright. Do not leave it in water and do not put it in the dishwasher. Occasionally care with food-safe oil. You can find further instructions in the [Cleaning the kitchen](/themen/kueche-reinigen/) section.

## Briefly summarized

Wash your hands thoroughly before cooking and after handling raw meat, poultry, and eggs. Change dishwashing sponges about weekly, wash dishcloths at 60 degrees and let them dry thoroughly. Use separate boards for raw foods and foods that will no longer be heated, and do not wash poultry. Keep the refrigerator at 7 degrees or less, store raw meat at the bottom of the vegetable drawer and refrigerate leftovers within two hours. Hot water, detergent and drying are sufficient for everyday use; disinfectants are only useful in exceptional cases.
