Many people clean by feeling: wipe a little here, remove dust there, the kitchen in between, then the bathroom comes to mind. In the end it took a long time, and yet there is still dust from the shelf on the freshly mopped floor. This is not due to a lack of hard work, but rather due to the order. If you follow a few simple basic rules, you will not only clean faster, but also more thoroughly, because no work has to be done twice.

In this guide you will learn the five basic rules for efficient cleaning, a tried and tested order for the entire apartment, how to use exposure times cleverly and why a color system for cleaning cloths belongs in every household.

## The five basic rules

**1. From top to bottom.** Dust and crumbs fall down. If you vacuum the floor first and then dust the shelf, the dust will end up back on the clean floor. Therefore, always start at the top, with lamps, cabinet tops and shelves, and work your way down. The floor comes last.

**2. From dry to wet.** First remove dry dust, then wipe with a damp cloth. Water turns loose dust into gunk that gets stuck in nooks and crannies. So vacuuming always comes before mopping.

**3. From clean to dirty.** In the bathroom, first wipe the mirror and sink, then the shower and bathtub, and finally the toilet. This means you don't carry germs from the toilet to surfaces you touch with your face or hands.

**4. Back to front.** In each room, work your way toward the exit from the corner furthest from the door. This means you don't have to walk over freshly mopped surfaces.

**5. Use the exposure time.** Cleaners need time to dissolve limescale and grease. Spray stubborn areas first and do something else in the meantime instead of scrubbing straight away.

## Before cleaning: ten minutes of preparation

Most of the time when cleaning is not spent on the actual cleaning, but on searching, running back and forth and tidying up. A short preparation saves a lot of time later:

- **Ventilation:** Open windows so that fresh air comes in and mopped floors dry faster.
- **Tidy up:** First put things that are lying around in their place. You clean twice as fast in open areas.
- **Pack cleaning basket:** Cleaner, cloths, sponge, gloves, and trash bags in a basket or bucket that you carry from room to room.
- **Start Laundry:** Remove bedding and run washer while you clean.

The few resources and tools you really need can be found in the guide [Basic cleaning supplies] (/ratgeber/putzmittel-grundausstattung/).

## The tried and tested order for the entire apartment

If you clean the entire apartment at once, this order works best:

1. **Spray the bathroom and kitchen.** Limescale cleaner on fittings and shower walls, cleaner in the toilet and on greasy areas on the stove. Now the means work while you move on.
2. **Wipe dust in all rooms**, from top to bottom: lamps, shelves, cupboards, picture frames, window sills, radiators, baseboards.
3. **Clean mirrors and glass surfaces**.
4. **Wipe surfaces with a damp cloth:** Tables, work surfaces, door handles, light switches.
5. **Finish cleaning the bathroom**, from clean to dirty.
6. **Finish cleaning of the kitchen:** Stove, sink, fronts, work surfaces.
7. **Vacuuming** throughout the apartment.
8. **Mop the floors**, from the back to the front of the apartment door.
9. **Take out the trash**, put cloths and mop covers in the washing machine.

This way you avoid double work: no dust falls on clean floors, no cleaner has to be applied again, and you don't constantly go back and forth between rooms. The guide [Wiping floors correctly] (/ratgeber/boden-wischen/) explains how to wipe floors depending on the material.

## Room by room or task by task?

There are two methods, and both work:

**Room by room:** You completely finish one room before moving on to the next. This is motivating because you see an immediate result, and works well if you only create one space each day.

**Task by task:** You complete an activity in all rooms, for example first dusting everywhere, then vacuuming everywhere. This is faster with a complete cleaning because you only take out devices and resources once.

Many people combine the two: bathroom and kitchen room by room, dusting, vacuuming and mopping task by task throughout the entire apartment.

## The color system for cleaning cloths

It is standard in cleaning companies, clinics and hotels, but unfortunately rare in households: there are cloths in a different color for each area. This means that the cloth from the toilet never ends up on the dining table.

| color | area |
|---|---|
| Red | Toilet and urine stone area |
| Yellow | Sink, shower, bathtub, tiles in the bathroom |
| Blue | Furniture, doors, surfaces in the living area |
| Green | Kitchen, work surfaces, dining table |

The colors themselves are not important, what is important is that everyone in the household knows the system. Wash cleaning cloths at 60 degrees without fabric softener and then let them dry completely. Damp cloths are an ideal breeding ground for germs. ## Use exposure time cleverly

Many people scrub too soon. Cleaners usually need a few minutes to remove limescale, grease or dried dirt. If you rub immediately, you need more strength and more resources.

- **Limescale on fittings and shower walls:** Spray on limescale cleaner, let it work for a few minutes, then wipe off and rinse with clear water. More in the guide [Removing limescale in the shower] (/ratgeber/kalk-in-der-dusche/).
- **Burned in the oven:** Treat with baking soda paste or oven cleaner and leave to work for several hours. More in the guide [Cleaning the oven] (/ratgeber/backofen-reinigen/).
- **Toilet:** Pour toilet cleaner under the edge and let it work while you clean the rest of the bathroom.
- **Stove and extractor hood:** Apply a degreasing cleaner, then wipe the other surfaces first.

Always remember: Never mix different cleaners, even one after the other on the same surface, without rinsing thoroughly. Chlorine-containing and acidic agents combine to form toxic chlorine gas. The rules are on the page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## The order in the bathroom and kitchen

**In the bathroom** you proceed as follows: Spray limescale cleaner on fittings and shower wall, put toilet cleaner under the edge. Then clean the mirror, empty and wipe down the shelves, clean the sink and faucet. Then shower or bathtub, then toilet from the outside to the inside: cistern, lid, seat, edge, and finally the basin with the brush. Finally the floor. The guide [cleaning the bathroom] (/ratgeber/bad-putzen/) explains it in detail.

**In the kitchen**, first clear the work surfaces and put dirty dishes in the dishwasher. Then from top to bottom: outside upper cabinets, extractor hood, tile backsplash, stove, work surfaces, sink and finally the fronts of the base cabinets and the floor. You don't clean the refrigerator and oven every week, but as needed.

## Save time in everyday life

- **Small portions instead of a cleaning marathon:** Ten to fifteen minutes a day keeps the apartment cleaner than a long day of cleaning on the weekend. A solid plan helps. More about this in the guide [Create a cleaning plan](/ratgeber/putzplan-erstellen/).
- **Wipe away immediately:** Spilled coffee, toothpaste in the sink and splashes on the stove are wiped away in seconds, dried up takes minutes.
- **Work with a timer:** Set an alarm for 15 minutes and see how far you get. This motivates you and prevents you from getting lost in details.
- **Music or Podcast:** Cleaning is easier when you have something going on at the same time.
- **Everyone helps:** Distribute tasks among all household members; children can also take on small tasks.

## An example: weekly cleaning in a two-room apartment

A thorough weekly cleaning in a two-room apartment with a bathroom and kitchen often only takes a good hour if done in the right order. The times are guidelines and depend on size and contamination.

| step | Approximate time |
|---|---|
| Ventilate, tidy up, pack the cleaning basket | 10 minutes |
| Spray bathroom and kitchen | 5 minutes |
| Dust in all rooms | 15 minutes |
| Finish cleaning the bathroom | 15 minutes |
| Finish cleaning the kitchen | 15 minutes |
| Vacuuming | 10 minutes |
| Mop floors | 10 minutes |

If you do this every week, you rarely need major cleaning. The guide [Checklist for basic cleaning] (/ratgeber/grundreinigung-wohnung/) explains when this is still due and what it entails.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **First the floor, then the shelves:** The dust ends up on the clean floor. Always from top to bottom.
- **Wipe before vacuuming:** Dust becomes grease. First dry, then wet.
- **One wipe for everything:** Germs migrate from the toilet to the kitchen. Use a color system.
- **Scrubbing immediately:** Allow the cleaner to take effect, this saves energy and resources.
- **Too much cleaner:** More product doesn't make it cleaner, it leaves behind residue.
- **Constant running back and forth:** A cleaning basket with everything you need saves a lot of trips.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which should I clean first: bathroom or kitchen?

This isn't crucial as long as you spray both of them first and use the exposure time. Many people start with the bathroom because limescale cleaner should last the longest.

### How often do I have to dust?

In most apartments, once a week is enough. If you have allergies, pets or are on busy roads, it may be necessary more often. A damp microfiber cloth picks up dust better than a feather duster that just stirs it up.

### Is a robot vacuum worth it?

A robot vacuum relieves you of the daily task of vacuuming crumbs and hair, especially with pets. You still have to take over corners, stairs and vacuuming under furniture with little clearance yourself from time to time. You can find further instructions in the [Systematic Cleaning](/themen/putzen-mit-system/) section.

## Briefly summarized

Clean from top to bottom, dry to wet, clean to dirty, and back to door. Clean up beforehand, pack a cleaning basket and spray the bathroom and kitchen first so that the cleaner works while you dust. Use separate cloths in solid colors for each area, wash them at 60 degrees without fabric softener and never mix different cleaners. Always vacuum before mopping and take out the trash at the end.
