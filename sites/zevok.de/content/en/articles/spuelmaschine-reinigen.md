A dishwasher cleans dishes every day, and yet many people think that it keeps itself clean. That's only partly true. Food residue ends up in the sieves, grease settles on seals and in corners, and hard water forms limescale. Most people are familiar with the consequences: glasses become milky, the dishes are no longer completely clean, and there is a musty smell when you open the door.

This can be avoided with a few firm moves. In this guide you will find out how to clean the sieves, spray arms, door seals and interior, what regenerating salt and rinse aid are really for, how to classify odors and deposits and what mistakes you can avoid when putting away things.

## Preparation and safety

Before you reach into the machine, you should consider a few points:

- **End the program and let it cool down.** After a hot program, the interior and heating elements are still warm.
- **Turn off the device.** When working on the pump or under the strainer, unplug or turn off the fuse.
- **Be careful with shards and knives.** Glass splinters sometimes end up in the sieve. Don't reach in blindly and wear gloves if necessary.
- **Have instructions ready.** Strainers, spray arms and pump covers are attached differently depending on the manufacturer.

## Cleaning sieves: the most important step

There is a sieve system at the bottom of the machine, usually consisting of a **coarse sieve**, a **fine sieve** and sometimes a **micro sieve**. It retains food residue so it doesn't get washed back onto the dishes or block the pump.

**How ​​often?** If used daily, approximately once a week, but at least every two weeks. If you wash up a lot of leftovers, more often.

1. **Pull out lower basket.**
2. **Unlocking the screens:** You usually turn the screen cylinder counterclockwise and lift it out. The flat fine sieve can then be removed.
3. **Put any coarse residue in the trash**, not in the sink, so that the drain doesn't get clogged.
4. **Brush under warm running water,** preferably with a dishwashing brush or an old toothbrush. Greasy sieves get a drop of detergent.
5. **Wipe the cavity underneath** with a cloth and check for foreign objects.
6. **Replace the sieves** and lock them properly. A crooked strainer allows dirt into the pump.

## Check spray arms

The spray arms rotate during the program and distribute the water via nozzles. If these are clogged, individual areas of the basket remain dirty.

- **Check that they rotate freely:** Rotate the arms by hand. If something hits something, there is usually a pot handle or a large plate in the way.
- **Removing:** Depending on the model, the arms are attached with a clip, a screw or a bayonet fitting. The instructions show how.
- **Cleaning the nozzles:** Rinse the arms under running water and pick any stuck residue out of the holes with a toothpick. Sharp metal objects can damage the openings.
- **Calcium on the nozzles** will come off if you place your arms in warm water with a little citric acid for a while and then rinse thoroughly.

## Door seal, door edge and interior

The rubber seal around the door and especially the **bottom edge of the door** get little water when flushed. This is exactly where fat, crumbs and a greasy coating collect.

- **Wipe the seal:** A warm cloth with a little detergent is sufficient. Also drive into the folds of the seal.
- **Clean the bottom edge of the door and side walls** outside the washing compartment with a damp cloth. An old toothbrush helps in nooks and crannies.
- **Wipe baskets and rails** occasionally, especially where residue remains.
- **Exterior:** Only clean the front and control panel with a damp cloth; for stainless steel, wipe in the direction of the grinding.

**Hot program against fat and odors:** If you almost exclusively use eco or short programs with low temperatures, you save energy, but promote fat deposits and biofilm. So run a **hot program** about once a month. You can use a **machine maintenance person** according to the manufacturer's instructions and let the machine run empty.

## Regenerating salt and rinse aid

These two remedies are often confused, but they have completely different tasks.

**Regeneration salt** belongs in the salt container at the bottom of the machine. It is intended for the built-in **softening system**. This removes limescale from the water and the salt regenerates it regularly. Without salt, the water is no longer softened and limescale builds up on dishes and in the machine.

- **Set water hardness:** Enter the hardness of your tap water into the machine, as specified by your water supplier. This is the only way to use the right amount of salt.
- **Refill** when the indicator lights up. When filling for the first time, according to the instructions, you usually add water to the container first.
- **Do not use table salt.** It is too fine and often contains additives that can disrupt the system.
- **Salt only in the salt container,** never in the detergent compartment. Wipe up spilled salt or rinse it away with a short program because salt water can attack stainless steel.

Even **multiple tabs** with salt content often do not replace regenerating salt for hard water. Please check the information on the packaging and in the instructions.

**Rinse aid** comes in its own compartment on the door and ensures that the water drains away better in the final rinse cycle. The dishes dry faster and without water stains. The dosage can usually be adjusted in stages:

| What you see | Possible cause | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Water drops and stains on glasses | too little rinse aid | Dosage one level higher |
| Streaks and sticky film | too much rinse aid | Dosage one level lower |
| white, wipeable limescale stains | Salt is missing or hardness is set incorrectly | Refill salt, check hardness |

## Arrange the toppings on the dishes

Not every white haze has the same cause. A simple test helps: **Rub a milky glass with a little vinegar.**

- **The veil disappears:** It is **lime**. Check salt, water hardness and rinse aid.
- **The haze remains:** It is probably **glass corrosion**. The glass surface itself is attacked. This cannot be undone. You can prevent this with lower temperatures for glasses, shorter programs and glasses that are marked as dishwasher safe.
- **Rust stains:** These often come from cutlery that is not rustproof or from damaged areas in the basket coating. Repair paint for baskets is available from specialist retailers.
- **Crumbly residue:** Most of the time the sieves are full, the spray arms are blocked or the machine is too full.

## Get rid of unpleasant smells

If the dishwasher smells musty or putrid when you open it, the cause can usually be found quickly:

- **Full sieve:** Food residue breaks down, especially in heat. Cleaning the sieve is the first step.
- **Dishes left for too long:** If you only wash every few days, run a short pre-wash program or scrape off any leftovers thoroughly.
- **Moisture after the program:** After clearing out, leave the door open slightly so that the interior dries. Pay attention to children and pets.
- **Standing water on the floor:** A little residual water in the pump sump is normal for many machines. If there is significantly more water in the dishwashing compartment, the process is disrupted.

**If the water doesn't drain:** Clean the strainers first. Then check whether the drain hose is kinked and whether the connection to the siphon is free. In many models there is a cover under the sieve behind which you can check the drain pump for foreign objects such as broken glass or cores. You only do this with the device switched off and according to the instructions. If that doesn't help, customer service is the right address.

## Stow away properly to keep the machine clean

- **Scrape off any coarse residue,** but do not pre-rinse under running water. This costs unnecessary water and energy.
- **Do not overload.** The water must reach every surface.
- **Large parts to the edge** so that they do not block the spray arms and do not stand in front of the detergent compartment.
- **Cups and bowls at an angle downwards** so that no water remains in them.
- **Delicate items are not included:** Wooden boards, cast iron pans, good knives and some plastics are damaged. The guide [Kitchen Hygiene](/ratgeber/kuechenhygiene/) tells you which utensils are better to wash by hand.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Never remove the sieves:** The cleaning performance decreases and it starts to smell.
- **Fill salt into the detergent compartment:** The salt only belongs in the container at the bottom.
- **Do not adjust the water hardness:** Then the water will be softened too little or too much.
- **Short programs only:** Grease and biofilm remain if a hot program is never running.
- **Vinegar as a permanent solution:** Many manufacturers advise against using it because acid can permanently attack rubber seals. The guide [Vinegar in the Household](/ratgeber/essig-im-haushalt/) explains when vinegar is useful.
- **Mixing cleaners:** Do not combine machine cleaners with other products, especially those containing chlorine. More about this on the page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I even have to clean the dishwasher if it washes?

Yes. The washing compartment is rinsed with every program, but the sieves, bottom edge of the door, seals and spray arms retain residue. Without care, the cleaning performance slowly decreases and odors develop.

### Can I use baking soda for odors?

A tablespoon of baking soda at the bottom of the empty machine, followed by a short cycle, can reduce slight odors. However, it does not eliminate the cause, usually a full sieve. What baking soda can do in the household can be found in the guide [baking soda, soda and baking powder] (/ratgeber/natron-soda-backpulver/).

### My glasses are milky despite salt and rinse aid. What now?

Do the vinegar test. If the veil remains, it is glass corrosion and the glass can no longer be made clear. Wash new glasses on a gentler program. If it disappears, check the hardness setting and whether the softening system is working properly. You can find out more about limescale in the guide [Descale kettle and coffee machine] (/ratgeber/entkalken-wasserkocher-kaffeemaschine/).

You can find further instructions in the [Cleaning the kitchen](/themen/kueche-reinigen/) section. Similar rules apply to the washing machine, which can be found in the guide [Cleaning the washing machine] (/ratgeber/waschmaschine-reinigen/).

## Briefly summarized

Clean the dishwasher filters every week or two, check the spray arms for clogged nozzles and wipe the door seal and bottom edge of the door regularly. Run a hot program about once a month, with machine maintenance if necessary. Set the water hardness correctly, only fill the salt container with regenerating salt and adjust the rinse aid dosage to the result. You can use the vinegar test to distinguish limescale from glass corrosion. Clean sieves, regular rinsing and a door that remains slightly open after the program help against odors.
