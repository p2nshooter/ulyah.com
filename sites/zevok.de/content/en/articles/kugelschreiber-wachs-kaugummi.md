Some stains do not follow any of the usual patterns. A ballpoint pen that has leaked in your trouser pocket, candle wax on the good tablecloth after Advent coffee, chewing gum that sticks to the sofa or children's trousers: neither cold water nor detergent alone can help here. Each of these messes needs its own method, and using the wrong one can often make things worse, such as wet wiping away wax or smearing chewing gum with warm water.

In this guide, you'll learn how to remove pen ink, candle wax and chewing gum from clothing, table linens, carpets and upholstery. There are also tips for gel pens, felt-tip pens and lipstick, which behave similarly.

## Ballpoint pen: Dissolve ink with alcohol

The paste in classic ballpoint pens consists of dyes dissolved in oily solvents. Water hardly dissolves them, but alcohol dissolves them well. That's why alcohol is the most important remedy for ballpoint pen stains.

**This is how you do it:**

1. **Do a color test.** Dab some alcohol on a hidden area, such as an inside seam. If the fabric discolors, do not use alcohol and rather take the item to the dry cleaner.
2. **Create a base.** Place a clean white cloth or several layers of paper towels under the stain. They absorb the dissolved ink instead of spreading it throughout the fabric.
3. **Dab alcohol.** Apply denatured alcohol or isopropanol to a white cloth and blot the stain from the outside to the inside. Do not rub or the stain will become larger.
4. **Change the underlay.** As soon as the underlay becomes discolored, use a fresh area. Repeat until no more ink transfers.
5. **Follow-up treatment.** Add some gall soap or liquid detergent to ensure that any remaining oily components disappear.
6. **Wash** according to care label.

**Safety:** Denatured alcohol and isopropanol are highly flammable. Work with the window open, away from candles, stoves and cigarettes, and keep bottles out of the reach of children.

**Hairspray?** Hairspray used to be a popular choice because it contained a lot of alcohol. Modern hairsprays often contain resins and care ingredients that leave behind sticky residues. Pure alcohol is a better choice.

### Ballpoint pen in the washing machine

If a pen has been washed in your pocket, the ink will often spread to several parts and into the drum. Treat the affected items of clothing individually as described above before putting them in the dryer. Wipe the drum with a cloth and a little alcohol and then run an empty wash cycle so that no ink transfers to the next wash. More about caring for the machine in the guide [Cleaning the washing machine] (/ratgeber/waschmaschine-reinigen/).

### Gel pens, felt-tip pens and highlighters

- **Gel pens** often contain pigments in a water-based paste. Rinse fresh stains with cold water and treat them with gall soap. Dried gel pen stains are difficult; oxygen bleach sometimes helps with light-colored fabrics.
- **Felt-tip pens for children** are usually washable. Rinse with cold water, work in gall soap and wash.
- **Permanent markers** can be removed from smooth surfaces with alcohol. A shadow often remains on textiles. Try using alcohol and a pad, like a ballpoint pen.
- **Ballpoint pens on the wall** can be carefully removed with a dirt eraser made of melamine foam. Test on an inconspicuous area first, as it has a slightly abrasive effect and can leave dull spots on shiny paint.

## Candle wax: first hard, then warm

Candle wax is a mixture of paraffin, stearin or beeswax and, for colored candles, additional dye. Liquid wax penetrates deep into the fibers, while solid wax can largely be lifted off. The method is therefore: first make it cold and hard, then get the rest out with heat.

**Step 1: Allow wax to harden.** Wait until the wax has completely cooled. You can put small textiles in a freezer bag in the freezer for an hour. For tablecloths and carpets, an ice cube in a freezer bag that you place on the wax helps.

**Step 2: Remove.** Gently break off the hard wax or scrape it off with a blunt object, such as the back of a knife, the handle of a spoon, or an old plastic card. Don't scratch or you'll damage the fibers.

**Step 3: Iron out any residue.** Place absorbent blotting paper or several layers of kitchen paper on and under the fabric. Run the iron on a low setting and without steam. The wax melts and soaks into the paper. Change the paper frequently to avoid pushing the wax back into the fabric. Repeat the process until no more wax transfers to the paper.

**Step 4: Treat paint residues and fat edges.** Often a slightly greasy shadow or a colored edge from colorful candles remains. Treat the grease edge with dishwashing liquid or gall soap, and carefully treat paint residues on colorfast fabrics with alcohol. Then wash, as warm as the label allows.

### Wax on carpet and upholstery

Proceed in the same way: let it harden, lift it off, then use paper and an iron on a low setting to pull out the remains. Be careful with synthetic carpets because some synthetic fibers melt when exposed to heat. Test the temperature in an inconspicuous area and never leave the iron in one place. Instead of an iron, you can also use a hairdryer and pick up the soft wax with kitchen paper.

### Wax on wood, glass and stone

- **Wooden table:** Allow wax to harden and carefully remove with a plastic card. Briefly warm up the residue with a hairdryer and wipe it off with a soft cloth. Then treat the area with the appropriate care product.
- **Glass or metal candle holders:** Place in the freezer for one hour. The wax contracts and can be easily removed. Alternatively, pour in hot water and pour off the melted wax, but not down the drain, but onto kitchen paper.
- **Natural stone and tiles:** Let it harden, lift it off with a plastic scraper, wipe off the rest with warm water and dishwashing liquid.

## Chewing Gum: Cold is key

Chewing gum is soft and sticky when warm. When it gets cold it becomes hard and brittle and can be broken off. Warm water and rubbing, on the other hand, only spread it.

**On clothing:**

1. **Freeze.** Place the garment in a freezer bag so that the gum is on the outside and not stuck to the bag, and place it in the freezer for one to two hours.
2. **Cancel.** Remove the part and immediately break off the hard gum. Bend the fabric slightly and the chewing gum will splinter off. Carefully scrape off any residue with a dull knife.
3. **Dissolve residues.** Small residues can be removed from robust fabrics with a little cooking oil. Massage in a few drops and scrape off the softened gum. Then you have to remove the oil with dishwashing liquid, otherwise a greasy stain will remain. How to do this can be found in the guide [Removing grease stains](/ratgeber/fettflecken-entfernen/).
4. **Wash** according to care label.

**On carpet, sofa and car seat:** Place an ice pack or ice cubes in a freezer bag on the gum for a few minutes until it is hard. Then carefully break off and scrape off. Dab the residue with a cloth and a little detergent solution, then dab with clean water and let it dry.

**Under tables and chairs:** Cold also works here. A cold spray from the hardware store can help, but follow the safety instructions and ventilate well.

## Lipstick and makeup

Lipstick, foundation and powder are combination stains made of grease, wax and color pigments.

1. Carefully remove excess with a blunt knife.
2. Work in dishwashing liquid or gall soap to dissolve grease and wax.
3. Dab off any remaining paint on colorfast fabrics with a little alcohol.
4. Wash and check before drying.

Mineral makeup and powder can often be brushed out dry before using water.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Rub ink with water:** Water does not dissolve the oily paste and only spreads it.
- **Alcohol without a base:** The dissolved ink migrates into the fabric instead of into the cloth.
- **Wipe away liquid wax:** It will then be absorbed even deeper. Let it get hard first.
- **Iron too hot:** Synthetic fibers can melt. Low setting, no steam.
- **Apply chewing gum with warm water:** It will become soft and smeared.
- **Forgot oil after chewing gum:** Without detergent, a grease stain will remain.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will ballpoint pens come out of leather?

You can sometimes remove fresh marks on smooth leather with a special leather cleaner. Alcohol can dry out leather and loosen the color. If you have valuable bags, jackets or sofas, it is better to ask a specialist company.

### How do I prevent wax stains on the tablecloth?

Always place candles on a plate or in a holder with a rim, shorten long wicks and do not place candles in a draft, as they will then drip more heavily. Candles that have been left in the refrigerator for a few hours drip less.

### What do I do if the wax has left a color in the fabric?

Dyes from colorful candles can often be dissolved with alcohol if the fabric is colorfast. Test this first in a hidden area. For white cotton, oxygen bleach also helps, provided the label allows bleach.

### Can I remove gum from hair without cutting?

Yes, often. Massage some cooking oil or greasy cream into the gum until it comes out of the hair, then wash the hair with shampoo. Cold helps here too. You can find further instructions in the [Remove stains](/themen/flecken-entfernen/) section.

## Briefly summarized

You can loosen ballpoint pens with alcohol: do a color test, place a white cloth underneath, dab from the outside to the inside and treat with gall soap. First let the candle wax harden, lift it off and remove the remains with blotting paper and a lukewarm iron, then treat with grease and paint. Freeze the chewing gum, break it off and loosen the residue with oil, which you then remove with dishwashing liquid. Only work with alcohol with the window open and away from flames and check every stain before the dryer.
