Vinegar is considered an all-purpose household weapon. It's cheap, is in the kitchen cupboard anyway and is supposed to help against limescale, streaks, odors and even bacteria. In fact, vinegar is an effective remedy, but not a harmless miracle cure. It is an acid, and acids not only dissolve limescale, but also attack natural stone, some metals and rubber. If you use vinegar everywhere, you can cause more harm than good.

In this guide you will find out what types of vinegar there are and how strong they are, how to properly dilute vinegar essence, where vinegar is really useful in the household, where you should definitely not use it and which myths persist.

## Which vinegar is which?

| Vinegar | acidity | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Household or table vinegar, brandy vinegar | usually around 5 percent | slight limescale edges, glass, window water |
| Vinegar essence | usually around 25 percent | stronger coatings, always diluted |
| Commercially available vinegar cleaner | depending on the product | Limescale and everyday dirt in the bathroom and kitchen |
| Wine, apple or balsamic vinegar | different | in the kitchen, not for cleaning |

Colorless brandy vinegar is best for cleaning. Wine or fruit vinegar contains colors and flavors that can leave stains or odors and are too good to clean.

**Vinegar essence** is approximately five times stronger than household vinegar. It can cause serious damage to the skin and eyes. Wear gloves when handling it, protect your eyes from splashes and keep the bottle out of the reach of children. Never pour them into drink bottles.

## Dilute vinegar essence properly

To make vinegar essence with about 25 percent acidity into a solution roughly equivalent to household vinegar, mix **one part vinegar essence with four parts water**. For heavier limescale deposits, you can use a little less water, for example one part essence to two parts water. The undiluted essence does not belong on surfaces.

When mixing, always add **the water** to the vessel first and then the essence. This way you avoid splashes of the concentrated acid.

## Where vinegar comes in handy

### Limescale on glass, tiles and fittings

Vinegar is in its element here. Lime is a mineral that only dissolves with acid. Diluted vinegar removes water stains on shower walls, limescale on fittings and the haze on glazed tiles. Leave it on for a few minutes, wipe with a soft sponge and rinse thoroughly with clean water. The guide [Removing limescale in the shower] (/ratgeber/kalk-in-der-dusche/) explains how to proceed with stubborn limescale.

### Shower head and aerator

Soak calcified shower heads and the small strainers on taps in vinegar for 30 to 60 minutes. Then brush off and rinse well.

### Windows and mirrors

A dash of vinegar in the cleaning water helps against limescale stains and streaks on glass, especially with hard water. You can find more technology for streak-free windows in the guide [Cleaning windows] (/ratgeber/fenster-putzen/).

### kettle

Light limescale in the kettle can be dissolved with vinegar water. However, the smell is noticeable when the solution warms up. Citric acid is often more pleasant here. The guide [descale kettle and coffee machine] (/ratgeber/entkalken-wasserkocher-kaffeemaschine/) describes both ways.

### Wipe out the refrigerator

Mild vinegar water is suitable for wiping the interior of the refrigerator after you have cleaned it with dishwater. It leaves no film and the smell disappears quickly. The complete instructions can be found in the guide [Cleaning the refrigerator] (/ratgeber/kuehlschrank-reinigen/).

### Pots, sink and glasses

**Calcium stains and rainbow shimmer in stainless steel pots** are caused by minerals in the cooking water and by heat. Add some diluted vinegar to the cooled pot, spread it around with a sponge, let it work for a moment and then rinse thoroughly. The spots are harmless, but usually disappear completely.

**Stainless steel sinks** get white edges when the water is hard. A cloth with vinegar water will dissolve it. Then wipe with clean water and rub the sink dry.

You can test **milky glasses** with vinegar: if the haze disappears, it was limescale. If it remains, the glass surface is affected by glass corrosion and vinegar will not help. More about this in the guide [Cleaning the dishwasher] (/ratgeber/spuelmaschine-reinigen/).

## This is how you use vinegar correctly

1. **Check material:** Is the surface sensitive to acid? If unsure, test in an inconspicuous area.
2. **Dilute:** Mix household vinegar with water for light coatings, always dilute vinegar essence.
3. **Apply in a targeted manner:** Using a cloth or spray bottle, do not pour over joints and silicone over large areas.
4. **Let it take effect briefly:** A few minutes are usually enough. The solution must not dry.
5. **Wipe and rinse:** Remove all acid residues with clear water.
6. **Drying:** Polish dry to avoid new water stains.

**Label spray bottle:** If you put vinegar water in a spray bottle, clearly write what is in it. This way no one will confuse the bottle with water or another cleaner.

## Where vinegar harms

The list of places where vinegar has no place is almost as important as the list of areas where it can be used.

| material | What happens | More suitable |
|---|---|---|
| Marble, limestone, travertine, Solnhofen slabs | Surface becomes matt and rough, stains remain | pH-neutral natural stone cleaner |
| Cement joints | Joints are washed out if used frequently | Baking soda paste, short exposure time for lime |
| Aluminum | Discoloration and attack of the surface | mild dishwashing water |
| Rubber seals in devices | become brittle over time | Manufacturer products and care programs |
| Oiled or waxed wooden surfaces | The protective layer is attacked and stains appear | Soapy water or wood care |
| Enamel | becomes dull with frequent use | mild cleaners, acid only briefly |
| Coated surfaces, screens | Coating can suffer | Manufacturer's information, soft cloth |

**Washing Machine and Dishwasher:** Vinegar is often recommended as a replacement for fabric softener or rinse aid. Many manufacturers advise against this because the acid can permanently damage seals and hoses. The guides [Cleaning the washing machine](/ratgeber/waschmaschine-reinigen/) and [Cleaning the dishwasher](/ratgeber/spuelmaschine-reinigen/) describe how to care for the devices.

**Fully automatic coffee machines:** Vinegar is almost always the wrong choice here. It can damage seals and leave a taste. Use the descaler recommended by the manufacturer.

## The most important safety rule: never use chlorine

Vinegar should **never** be mixed with cleaners that contain chlorine, including many bleach, disinfectant and mildew cleaners. This produces toxic chlorine gas that severely irritates the eyes and respiratory tract. This also applies if you put the products one after the other in the same toilet or on the same surface. Always rinse thoroughly with water between two products. You can find all the rules on our page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

Also, don't mix vinegar with baking soda hoping for a particularly strong effect. The two neutralize each other, and what's left is mostly water with a little salt. The resulting gas builds up pressure in a sealed bottle.

## Vinegar is not a disinfectant

Vinegar can reduce the number of some germs, but it is **not a reliable disinfectant**. Disinfection is rarely necessary for normal households anyway. Thorough cleaning with dishwashing liquid or all-purpose cleaner, drying and changing cloths regularly are usually sufficient. The guide [Kitchen Hygiene](/ratgeber/kuechenhygiene/) tells you how to work hygienically in the kitchen.

Vinegar is also not a good choice against **mold**, especially on calcareous plaster, where the acid is neutralized by the lime. The background information is explained in the guide [Preventing and removing mold in the bathroom] (/ratgeber/schimmel-im-bad/).

## Against odors and small pests

**Odours:** A bowl of vinegar is said to bind odors in the room. The effect is minimal and in the end it smells mainly of vinegar. Ventilating, eliminating sources and washing textiles help more.

**Fruit Flies:** A small bowl with a little vinegar, a splash of fruit juice and a drop of dishwashing liquid will attract fruit flies. The detergent relaxes the surface so that they can no longer fly away. It is even more important to cover ripe fruit and empty the organic waste frequently.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Use vinegar essence undiluted:** Danger for skin, eyes and surfaces. Always mix with water.
- **Vinegar on natural stone:** Marble and limestone will be permanently damaged.
- **Combine vinegar and chlorine:** Dangerous to life.
- **Vinegar as a permanent solution in devices:** Seals and hoses suffer.
- **Do not rinse:** Acid residues continue to attack joints and silicone.
- **Use colored vinegar for cleaning:** Dyes can leave stains.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the apartment smell for a long time after cleaning with vinegar?

No. The smell is noticeable when cleaning, but goes away as soon as the surfaces are dry and you ventilate them. If you don't like it at all, you can use citric acid instead of lime, which hardly smells. More about this in the guide [Citric acid in the household] (/ratgeber/zitronensaeure/).

### Is vinegar more environmentally friendly than a limescale cleaner?

Vinegar is readily biodegradable and requires little packaging. However, a limescale cleaner can have a more targeted effect because it adheres better to vertical surfaces and can be dosed more economically. The most important thing is to use every means sparingly and appropriately.

### How do I store vinegar and vinegar essence correctly?

Store both in the original bottle, tightly closed, cool and out of the reach of children. Do not place vinegar essence next to cooking oil and drinks to avoid confusion. Vinegar has a very long shelf life, even beyond the best-before date.

### Can I use vinegar and citric acid alternately?

Yes, both are acids and get along. However, a mixture does not bring any advantage. You can read about which remedies don't work together and which home remedy tips don't deliver what they promise in the guide [Home Remedy Myths Checked](/ratgeber/hausmittel-mythen/).

You can find more home remedies and their limitations in the [Home Remedies](/themen/hausmittel/) section, for example in the guide [Soda, Soda and Baking Powder](/ratgeber/natron-soda-backpulver/).

## Briefly summarized

Vinegar is an acid and reliably dissolves limescale on glass, tiles, fittings, shower heads and kettles. Household vinegar has about five percent acidity, vinegar essence about 25 percent and is diluted with water in a ratio of one to four, with gloves and water first. Do not use vinegar on natural stone, aluminum, oiled wooden surfaces and do not use vinegar permanently in washing machines, dishwashers or fully automatic coffee machines. Never mix it with chlorine cleaners and do not expect a disinfectant effect. Used correctly, vinegar is a cheap, effective helper against limescale.
