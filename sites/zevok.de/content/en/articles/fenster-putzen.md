Few things are as frustrating as a freshly cleaned window that, after drying, shows streaks, smudges and lint as soon as the sun hits it. Many people then resort to stronger cleaners, more water or other cloths, often making things worse. A streak-free result depends primarily on three things: the right time, a small amount of cleaner and clean technology.

In this guide you will find out which tools are best, in which order you should proceed, how to work with the puller and how to clean frames, seals, window sills and fly screens without damaging the material.

## The right time

Don't clean windows in full sun. The water then dries faster than you can remove it, leaving behind edges and streaks. An overcast, dry day or early morning or late afternoon when the sun does not shine directly on the pane is ideal.

Very cold days are also unfavorable: If there is frost, the water can freeze on the outer pane. A dash of spirit in the wiping water prevents this and also helps against streaks.

**How ​​often?** In most homes, it is enough to thoroughly clean windows two to four times a year. Good times are after the pollen season in spring and before winter. It may be necessary more often on busy roads or near construction sites. The guide [Create a cleaning plan](/ratgeber/putzplan-erstellen/) shows you how to put such tasks into a plan.

## The right tool

| tool | For what | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Bucket of lukewarm water | Basis for everything | just a few drops of dish soap |
| washer | Wet the windshield and loosen dirt | for large areas, saves time |
| Window squeegee with rubber lip | Remove water without streaks | Rubber lip must be smooth and undamaged |
| Microfiber cloths | Frames, corners, small panes | wash without fabric softener |
| Chamois leather or lint-free cloth | Dry the edges and corners | Use clean and well wrung out |
| Window vacuum | Suck off water instead of draining it | practical for many slices, not absolutely necessary |
| Telescopic rod | high windows from the floor | safer than the ladder |

For small windows and lattice windows, two microfiber cloths are sufficient: a damp one for cleaning and a dry one for polishing. For large windows, the washer and squeegee are significantly faster and leave fewer streaks.

## Little cleaner: the most important secret

Most streaks are caused by too much detergent. Surfactants that remain on the pane form a visible film when it dries.

- **A few drops of detergent** in a bucket of lukewarm water is enough for normally dirty windows.
- **Glass cleaner from a spray bottle** is practical for small areas and quick interim cleaning, but is expensive for large windows and tends to leave streaks if overdosed.
- **A dash of spirit** in the mopping water dissolves grease and allows the water to dry faster and more evenly.
- **If the water is very hard**, white spots may remain after drying. A small dash of vinegar in the water helps against this. Keep it away from natural stone window sills because acid attacks marble and limestone.

Never mix different cleaners. Which combinations are dangerous can be found on the page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## The correct order

1. **Remove curtains and decorations**, clear the windowsill and place a towel on the windowsill to absorb dripping water.
2. **Remove coarse dirt:** Remove cobwebs, dead insects and dust from the frame corners and running rails with a brush or vacuum cleaner.
3. **Clean the frame and seals.** This means that no more dirty water runs onto the freshly cleaned window.
4. **Clean the inside of the window.**
5. **Clean the outside of the window.**
6. **Dry the edges** and finally wipe the window sills.

If there is a lot of dust and pollen on the outside, start with the frame on the outside so that the dirt is not carried inside later.

## The technology with the puller

1. **Washing:** Dip the washer into the water, squeeze it lightly and wash the window vigorously, especially around the edges. Stubborn spots such as bird droppings or insect remains soak for a few minutes.
2. **Prepare the edge:** Use the squeegee to dry a narrow strip along the top of the frame. This way you have a clean starting point.
3. **Pulling:** Place the puller at the top and pull it downward in even strokes or in a continuous snaking line from top to bottom. The rubber lip should rest slightly at an angle and with even pressure.
4. **Wipe rubber lip:** Wipe the lip with a clean cloth after each stroke, otherwise you will redistribute the dirty water.
5. **Dry the edges:** Use a dry microfiber cloth or chamois to dab the edges and bottom edge where water collects.

**Tip:** If you see streaks, check the rubber lip first. Small nicks or dried residue leave a line on each strip. Replacement lips are cheap.

## Without a puller: the two-cloth method

Two cloths are sufficient for small panes, lattice windows and glass doors:

- Wipe the window with a damp, well-wrung microfiber cloth.
- Polish immediately with a dry microfiber cloth, in circular movements or in strips.
- Change the cloths as soon as they become damp or dirty.

This method is slower than the squeegee, but is completely sufficient and very thorough for small areas.

## Frames, seals and window sills

**Plastic frames** can be cleaned with warm water and a little dishwashing liquid or a mild all-purpose cleaner. Abrasives, scouring pads and solvents will scratch or discolor the surface. There are special plastic cleaners for yellowed frames that you can use according to the instructions.

**Wooden frames** are painted or glazed. Just wipe it damp with a mild cleaner and then dry it. Standing water can seep into cracks in the paint. You should have damaged areas repaired before moisture penetrates the wood.

**Aluminum frame** tolerates mild, pH-neutral cleaners. Acidic or strongly alkaline agents can attack the coating.

**Rubber seals** can be cleaned with water and a soft cloth. Once or twice a year, you'll enjoy a care product for rubber seals so that they stay supple and don't stick.

**Running rails and drainage holes** on the bottom of the frame keep rainwater out. Vacuum or brush them out and check whether the small openings are free.

**Window sills** made of plastic or wood should be wiped clean with a damp cloth. You can only clean window sills made of natural stone such as marble with pH-neutral products, never with vinegar or citric acid.

## Fly screens and roller blinds

Fly screens collect dust and pollen. If possible, remove them from the frame and rinse them with lukewarm water in the shower or in the garden. A soft brush and a drop of detergent help with stubborn dirt. Allow the grid to dry completely before replacing it.

You clean blinds and pleated blinds depending on the material. You can wipe fixed slats with a damp cloth or special slat brushes. Pleated fabric blinds often only tolerate careful vacuuming. Follow the manufacturer’s care instructions.

## Stubborn dirt

- **Paint Splashes and Grout:** Soak with water and gently loosen with a glass scraper with a new blade. Keep the scraper flat and only work on wet glass. Do not use scrapers or abrasive cleaners on coated or self-cleaning glass.
- **Decal residue:** Soak with warm soapy water and peel off. Stubborn adhesive residues can often be removed with a little cooking oil, which you can then remove with washing up water.
- **Bird droppings:** Soak with a damp cloth for a few minutes, then wipe off. Do not scrape dry, otherwise scratches will occur.
- **Calcium stains from sprinklers or gutters:** Soak with diluted vinegar and wipe off, then wipe with clean water.

## Safety comes first

- **Do not lean out of the window** to reach the outside window. Use a telescopic rod or open the tilt and turn window completely and clean the outside from the inside.
- **Use a sturdy ladder** or step, not a chair. Place the ladder on firm, level ground.
- **High windows and winter gardens** are better left to a specialist company.
- **Keep children away** as long as windows are open.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Cleaning in the sun:** The water dries too quickly and streaks appear.
- **Too much cleaner:** A few drops are enough. The rest remains as a film on the disc.
- **Reuse dirty water:** Change the water as soon as it becomes cloudy.
- **Fabric softener for microfiber cloths:** This causes the cloths to lose their effectiveness and leave streaks behind.
- **Clean the frame last:** Then dirty water runs onto the clean window.
- **Damaged rubber lip on the puller:** Each notch leaves a streak.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get windows clean without streaks?

Clean when the sky is overcast, use only a little detergent in lukewarm water, remove the water with a clean squeegee and dry the edges with a lint-free cloth.

### Is a window vacuum worth it?

If you have a lot of large windows or glass doors, a window vacuum saves time because it sucks up the water instead of letting it run onto the windowsill. A washer and squeegee are sufficient for a few normal windows.

### Is newspaper good for cleaning windows?

It used to be a popular trick, but today it is no longer necessary. Printer's ink can rub off on light-colored frames, and modern microfiber cloths produce better results.

### What do I do about fogged up windows from the inside?

Foggy windows are a sign of high humidity. Ventilate briefly and vigorously several times a day, especially after cooking and showering. ## Briefly summarized

Clean windows when the sky is overcast and not in the sun. First remove coarse dirt and clean the frame and seals, then the window. Use just a few drops of detergent in lukewarm water, wash the window and remove the water in even strips with a clean squeegee. Wipe the rubber lip after each strip and dry the edges. Only clean plastic and wooden frames mildly, never treat natural stone with acid and, for high windows, it is better to use a pole or a specialist company than to climb.
