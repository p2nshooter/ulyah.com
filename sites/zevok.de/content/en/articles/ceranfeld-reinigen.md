Milk that has boiled over, tomato sauce that splatters when stirring, burnt edges around the cooking zone and milky veils that remain after wiping: the hob is the surface in the kitchen that gets dirty most quickly. At the same time, it is more sensitive than many people think. The wrong sponge or a harsh agent leaves scratches on the smooth glass ceramic that will never disappear, and some dirt can even permanently damage the surface if left for too long.

In this guide you will find out how to clean ceramic hobs and induction hobs thoroughly every day, how to remove burnt-in items with a glass scraper, why sugar needs to be removed immediately, what helps against limescale and traces of metal and how to clean a gas stove with grates, burners and nozzles.

## Glass ceramic and induction: what the difference means for cleaning

A **ceramic hob** in the narrower sense is a hob made of glass ceramic, under which heating coils or halogen radiators heat the glass surface itself strongly. An **induction hob** has the same glass ceramic surface, but heats the bottom of the pot directly via a magnetic field. The glass surface only gets warm indirectly from the hot pot.

When it comes to cleaning, this means: Both surfaces are treated the same. However, dirt is less likely to burn on induction because the surface gets less hot.

## The daily cleaning

1. **Let the hob cool down.** The residual heat indicator shows you whether the zone is still hot.
2. **Wipe clean with a damp cloth and a little detergent**.
3. **Wipe with a dry cloth** to avoid water stains and streaks.

It's that easy if you wipe briefly after each cooking. Dried splashes from the previous day are much more difficult to remove.

**Important exception: sugar and plastic.** Foods containing sugar such as jam, caramel or overcooked syrup and melted plastic or aluminum foil must **be removed immediately while the hob is still hot.** As it cools, melted sugar can bond to the glass surface and tear small pieces out of the glass when released. Turn off the zone and carefully push the sugar out of the hot zone using a glass scraper. Protect your hand with an oven mitt because the surface is very hot.

## Remove burnt-in residue with the glass scraper

The **glass scraper**, also called a ceramic hob scraper, is the most important tool for glass ceramics. It has a sharp blade that lifts debris from smooth surfaces without scratching them if you use it correctly.

1. **Let the hob cool down** (except for sugar, see above).
2. **Soak crusts:** Place a damp cloth with a little dishwashing liquid on the area for ten to fifteen minutes. This loosens the crust.
3. **Place the scraper flat,** at an angle of approximately 30 degrees to the surface. Push the blade under the crust using light pressure. Never scratch with the corner, always with the whole blade.
4. **Wipe off any residue** with a damp cloth.
5. **Apply glass ceramic cleaner** if veils are still visible. It cleanses mildly and leaves a thin protective film that removes future dirt more easily.
6. **Re-polish dry.**

**Scraper Care:** Change the blade as soon as it becomes dull or has nicks. A damaged blade can cause scratches. Store the scraper with the blade retracted and out of the reach of children.

## What you shouldn't use on glass ceramics

| Means or tool | Why not |
|---|---|
| Scouring milk and scouring powder | fine scratches, the surface becomes matt |
| Green scrubbing side of sponges | scratches the glass surface |
| Steel wool and metal scourer | deep scratches |
| Oven spray | may attack decor and frame, not intended for this |
| Knife and spatula without scraper guide | Scratches and chips |
| Dirt eraser | has a grinding effect, many manufacturers advise against it |

If you are unsure, check the operating instructions for your hob. The manufacturers indicate which products are suitable.

## Limescale spots and haze

White or iridescent, rainbow-colored spots are often caused by limescale from boiled water or leftover food. They can be removed with a glass ceramic cleaner. Alternatively, a little diluted vinegar or citric acid on a cloth can help. Then wipe with clean water and dry the surface. Make sure that no acid drips onto natural stone worktops, as it attacks marble and limestone. More about this in the guide [Vinegar in the Household](/ratgeber/essig-im-haushalt/).

## Metal traces and discoloration

Silvery or gray streaks appear when pots with aluminum or copper bases are pushed over the hob. They look like scratches, but can usually be removed with a glass ceramic cleaner. Yellowish or brownish discoloration in the cooking zone often comes from burnt-on food residue. They disappear with a scraper and cleaner.

**Real scratches** and chips cannot be removed. They usually do not affect the function as long as the glass is not cracked.

## Avoid scratches

- **Lift pots, do not push them.** Pots tend to be moved, especially on induction cookers.
- **Keep pot bottoms clean and dry**. Crumbs and grains of salt under the pot act like sandpaper.
- **Do not place anything on the hob** that does not belong there, such as heavy objects or ceramics with a rough base.
- **Do not use the hob as a work surface.**

## Clean the frame and controls

Wipe stainless steel frames with a damp cloth in the direction of the cut and then dry them. You only clean touch control panels when the hob is switched off, otherwise you will accidentally switch zones on. Many hobs have a cleaning or locking function that briefly deactivates the control panel. You can clean removable knobs in warm soapy water.

## Clean the gas stove

A gas stove consists of grates, burner caps, burner heads and a bowl, usually made of stainless steel or enamel.

1. **Turn off the gas** and let everything cool down.
2. **Remove racks and burner caps.** Soak in hot water with dish soap. Whether they can be put in the dishwasher is stated in the operating instructions. Some coatings become dull there.
3. **Clean burner heads and nozzles.** The small openings from which the gas flows become clogged with overcooked food. Clean them carefully with a soft brush or a thin needle without enlarging the openings. Make sure that no water gets into the gas supply line.
4. **Clean the trough** with a damp cloth and detergent. Do not use abrasive cleaners on enamel; wipe in the direction of the grinding on stainless steel.
5. **Let everything dry thoroughly.** Damp burner parts ignite poorly.
6. **Reinstall parts correctly.** Burner caps must fit exactly, otherwise the flame will burn unevenly or the ignition will not work.

**Caution:** If you smell gas, do not turn on anything electrical, open the windows, leave the apartment and call your gas supplier's emergency service from outside.

## Old cast iron hobs and stainless steel troughs

**Cast hotplates** on older electric stoves can be cleaned when they have cooled down with a damp cloth and a little detergent. You can remove burnt-in residue with a soft brush. Then dry the plates by turning them on briefly to prevent rust. Occasionally you can treat them with a special stove top care product that protects against rust.

**Stainless steel troughs** around gas burners or old hotplates should be wiped in the direction of the cut, never in circles. You can remove limescale stains with a little diluted vinegar and then wipe thoroughly with clean water. A drop of cooking oil on a soft cloth makes stainless steel shine evenly again.

## Habits that save work

- **Lid on the pot** prevents splashes and saves energy.
- **Choose the right pot size** so that nothing boils over the edge.
- Place **splash guard** over the pan when frying.
- **Push spillage immediately** from the off zone before it burns in.
- **Wipe briefly after cooking** while the surface is still lukewarm. Then splashes come off without any effort.

## When a hob should no longer be used

If the glass ceramic is cracked or cracked, switch off the hob immediately and stop using it. Moisture can reach electrical parts through cracks. Disconnect the device from the power supply, for example via the fuse, and have it checked by a specialist.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Scrubbing with the green side of the sponge:** The glass surface will become dull and scratched.
- **Allow sugar to cool:** It can damage the surface. Remove immediately while hot.
- **Apply the scraper steeply or with the corner:** This leaves scratches.
- **Sliding pots:** Metal traces and scratches are the result.
- **Insert burner parts wet:** The gas stove does not ignite well.
- **Mixing agents:** Different cleaners never belong together. More on the page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use dishwashing liquid on the ceramic hob?

Yes, a damp cloth with a little detergent is sufficient for daily cleaning. Then wipe dry so that no streaks remain.

### Does baking soda help against burnt stains?

A paste made from baking soda or baking powder and water can dissolve incrustations. But don't rub it vigorously, as the fine grains can rub glass ceramic. Let the paste work, remove it with a damp cloth and use the glass scraper to do the rest. More in the guide [baking soda, soda and baking powder] (/ratgeber/natron-soda-backpulver/).

### Why do streaks remain after wiping?

Detergent residues or limescale from the water are usually the cause. Wipe with clear water and polish with a dry microfiber cloth, then the surface will shine streak-free.

### How often should I thoroughly clean the hob?

Wipe briefly after each cooking, thoroughly once a week with glass ceramic cleaner. ## Briefly summarized

After each cooking, wipe the glass ceramic and induction with washing-up liquid and dry. Remove sugar and melted plastic immediately while the surface is hot. Soak burnt-in items and remove them at a flat angle with the glass scraper, then use a glass ceramic cleaner to care for the surface. Avoid using abrasive cleaners, steel wool and oven spray, lift pots instead of pushing them and remove limescale with diluted acid. Soak the grates and burners on the gas stove, carefully clean the nozzles and put everything back in place, dry and with a perfect fit.
