Under the sink there is a bathroom cleaner, a kitchen spray, a degreaser, a stainless steel care product, a glass cleaner, two opened limescale removers and a scented cleaner that someone picked up on offer. Nevertheless, when cleaning the shower, the squeegee is missing, the sponge has been gray for weeks, and the only microfiber cloth leaves streaks. This is what it looks like in many households: a lot of resources are purchased and little is invested in good tools.

With four or at most five cleaners you can get through almost every task in the kitchen, bathroom and living room. It is crucial that you understand which agent works against which dirt. The principle behind it is simpler than the many special bottles in the supermarket suggest. Here you will find out which cleaners should be included in the basic equipment, what you can do without, which tools make the difference and how to care for and store everything safely.

## The pH principle: acidic, neutral, alkaline

Whether a cleaner helps against a specific dirt depends primarily on its pH value. The scale ranges from 0 to 14. 7 is the neutral range, below it it becomes acidic, above it it becomes alkaline, which is also called basic or alkaline. The further from the center a remedy is, the more powerful it is and the more attention you have to pay to skin, eyes and surfaces.

For the household, this can be boiled down to a simple rule of thumb: Every dirt has a suitable antidote.

| area | Works primarily against | Typical means | Be careful |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sour | Limescale, water stains, urine scale, lime soap | Limescale cleaner, acidic bathroom cleaner, toilet cleaner, vinegar, citric acid | Marble and other calcareous natural stones, cement-containing joints, sensitive coatings |
| Neutral | light everyday dirt, dust film, fingerprints | Neutral cleaners, many all-purpose cleaners, dishwashing liquid | hardly any restrictions, pay attention to the dosage |
| Alkaline | Fat, burnt-in food, protein-containing residues, soot | Degreaser, oven cleaner, soda | Aluminum, paint, wood, skin and eyes |

Lime is a mineral that dissolves in acid. If you put vinegar on a thick crust of lime, it sometimes bubbles slightly: carbon dioxide escapes and the lime dissolves. Fat, on the other hand, is attacked by alkalis and broken down into components that can be rinsed off with water. For normal everyday dirt you don't need one or the other, but above all surfactants. These are detergent substances that remove dirt from the surface and keep it suspended in the water.

An acidic cleaner therefore hardly helps against grease and a degreaser hardly helps against limescale. And a strong agent is not automatically better: the neutral range is sufficient for everyday dirt, you only use the strong agents for special tasks.

## The basic equipment of cleaners

Together, these cleaners cover almost all household tasks. The brand doesn't matter. Pay attention to the type of product, read the instructions on the label and stick to the specified dosage.

### Neutral or all-purpose cleaner

It is the workhorse for floors, doors, cabinet fronts, work surfaces, window sills and tiles in everyday life. Depending on the product, all-purpose cleaners are neutral to slightly alkaline. For sensitive surfaces such as painted wood, high-gloss fronts or certain floor coverings, a cleaner that is expressly designated as neutral is the safer choice. A small splash in a bucket of water is usually enough. More product leaves streaks and a sticky film on which new dust is particularly likely to settle.

### Dishwashing liquid

It's not just good for dishes. A few drops in warm water dissolve grease splashes on tiles, sticky handles and fresh grease stains on textiles. Dishwashing liquid is mild and safe for most surfaces. Use little, otherwise you'll end up wiping away foam for a long time.

### Limescale cleaner

An acidic cleaner belongs in the bathroom and in the kitchen: for shower walls, fittings, sinks, sinks and tiles. Depending on the product, it is based on citric acid, lactic acid or other acids. A toilet cleaner is basically a variant of this that sticks to the flush rim as a gel. Acid has limits: it attacks marble and limestone, washes out cement-containing joints if used frequently and can dull sensitive coatings. You only let it work on chrome for a short time and then rinse thoroughly.

### scouring milk

It contains fine abrasives in a creamy base and is intended for stubborn deposits on robust surfaces: ceramic sinks, glazed tiles, the outside of pots. On stainless steel you only work in the grinding direction. It is not suitable for acrylic, plastic, glass, mirrors, high gloss, lacquer, coated pans and hobs unless the manufacturer expressly allows it. Apply it to a damp sponge instead of directly onto the surface, do not press too hard and rinse thoroughly, otherwise a white haze will remain.

### Optional: glass cleaner

It is not a must for windows and mirrors. Water with a splash of detergent, a squeegee and a dry microfiber cloth are usually enough. A glass cleaner is convenient for the bathroom mirror every now and then. Some products contain alcohol or ammonia. Agents containing ammonia must never be mixed with cleaners containing chlorine. The guide [Cleaning windows without streaks] (/ratgeber/fenster-putzen/) shows you how to get windows clean without streaks.

Home remedies can replace individual bottles: vinegar and citric acid dissolve limescale, soda is a strong alkali against grease, baking soda has a mild effect. That doesn't make them harmless. The same rules apply to them as to purchased cleaners. The guide [baking soda, soda and baking powder] (/ratgeber/natron-soda-backpulver/) explains what differentiates the white powders.

## Which you usually don't need

Many bottles in the cleaning cupboard solve problems that wouldn't exist without them. You can usually save yourself these resources:

- **Special cleaner for each individual surface:** A separate agent for tiles, one for doors and one for the dining table makes nothing cleaner. The exception: If the manufacturer of a hob, an oiled wooden floor or a natural stone specifies a specific care product, this information takes precedence.
- **Fragrance additives, room sprays and scent beads:** A fresh smell does not mean something is clean. Fragrances mask odors rather than eliminating their cause, and some people react to scents with irritated airways or skin. Regular ventilation achieves more.
- **Disinfectants in normal everyday life:** Thorough cleaning with water and cleaner, regularly changed cloths and good drying are usually sufficient in an ordinary household. Disinfectants do not replace cleaning, because dirt has to come off first anyway. The situation is different if someone in the household has a weakened immune system, suffers from an infectious disease or is being cared for at home. Then you follow the recommendation of the doctor or nursing service and use the recommended remedy exactly according to the instructions.
- **Wet cleaning wipes for everything:** They are practical for on the go, but in everyday life they are expensive, create a lot of waste and definitely don't belong in the toilet because they can block pipes.

## The tool: this is where quality pays off

A good cloth and the right wiper often make more difference than the cleaning agent when cleaning. This equipment has proven itself:

| tool | For what | You pay attention to that |
|---|---|---|
| Microfiber cloths, several pieces | Dust, glass, fittings, fronts, damp wiping | different colors for kitchen, bathroom, toilet and living rooms |
| Sponges with and without scouring side | Sink, washbasins, tiles, pots | Scrub side only on robust surfaces and after testing |
| Brushing | Dishwashing brush, joint brush, small detail brush for cracks and fitting feet | Bristles not too hard for sensitive surfaces |
| bucket | Wiping water, soaking rags | with liter scale so that you can dose concentrates correctly |
| Wiping system | floors | Flat mop with washable covers or mop with spinner |
| puller | Shower screen, window, mirror | Rubber lip smooth and undamaged |
| Household gloves | Protection against cleaners and constant moisture | a separate pair for the toilet |
| Hand brush, dustpan, vacuum cleaner | Remove coarse dirt before wiping | Change bags and filters in a timely manner |

Microfiber cloths are made of very fine synthetic fibers that absorb dust and grease easily with just plain water. This means you no longer need a cleaner for many tasks. With different colors you can prevent the toilet cloth from ending up on the dining table at some point. The guide [Cleaning Efficiently] (/ratgeber/putzen-reihenfolge/) explains how such a color system works in everyday life.

A flat mop with removable covers reaches corners well and can be used when damp; a mop with a spinner absorbs more water. Which floor can tolerate how much moisture can be found in the guide [Wipe floors correctly] (/ratgeber/boden-wischen/).

A spray bottle is only worthwhile if you prepare a solution yourself, such as citric acid for the shower. Label them clearly with content and date.

## Care for tools properly

A musty cloth spreads odors and germs instead of removing them. With a few habits, your tools will remain usable for a long time:

1. **Rinse after each use.** Wash cloths and sponges in clean water, wring them out and hang them to dry. When they are crumpled up damp, they quickly start to smell.
2. **How ​​to wash microfiber properly.** Wash the cloths at the warm temperature specified on the label or packaging and avoid using fabric softener. It lies on the fine fibers so that they absorb less dirt and water. Do not wash microfiber with lint-prone terry towels, otherwise the lint will become trapped in the fibers. The cloths only go into the dryer if allowed.
3. **Replace sponges in a timely manner.** If a sponge smells, falls apart or can no longer be rinsed properly, it belongs in the trash. Special rules apply to the dishwashing sponge in the kitchen, more about this in the [Kitchen Hygiene] guide (/ratgeber/kuechenhygiene/).
4. **Allow brushes and buckets to dry.** Rinse brushes and allow them to dry so that no water remains in the brush head. After wiping, empty the bucket, rinse it and turn it upside down to dry.
5. **Check the puller and gloves.** A torn rubber lip leaves streaks and should be replaced. Rinse the outside of gloves before taking them off and then let them dry. One hole is enough for cleaner to reach the skin.

## Store safely

Cleaners are chemicals, even the mild ones. Always keep it in the original bottle to preserve the label, instructions and warnings. Place them where children and pets cannot get to them: high up or behind a child-proof door. Close each bottle immediately after use and do not store cleaning products next to food. Never pour them into drink bottles, as mix-ups are a known cause of accidents.

If possible, do not store chlorine-containing products and acidic cleaners right next to each other so that a leaky bottle does not come into contact with another product. Dispose of leftovers as directed on the packaging or ask your municipality's waste advisory service. You can find all the rules for mixing, ventilation and warning signs on the label on our page [Brushing Safety](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **A lot helps a lot:** Overdosed cleaners leave streaks and residues that attract new dirt. Stick to the dosage instructions and use the liter scale on the bucket.
- **The wrong cleaner for dirt:** Lime needs acid, grease needs surfactants or lye. Before you reach for the bottle, think about what you're dealing with for a moment.
- **Mix agents or apply one after the other to the same surface:** Cleaners containing chlorine and acids or ammonia form dangerous gases. Use only one product at a time and rinse thoroughly with water before using another.
- **One cloth for everything:** If you wipe the toilet and kitchen with the same cloth, you spread germs throughout the apartment. Separate colors solve the problem.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need separate cleaners for the kitchen and bathroom?

Separate means not necessarily, but separate cloths. A neutral cleaner works in both rooms. A limescale cleaner is used in the bathroom, and in the kitchen dishwashing liquid is usually sufficient for grease. You only use a strong degreaser for stubborn cases.

### How do I know if a cleaner is acidic or alkaline?

The intended use on the label usually reveals it: “limescale remover” or “against urine scale” indicates acid, “grease remover” or “oven cleaner” indicates lye. Strong means also carry danger pictograms and signal words. If in doubt, read the contents and instructions for use.

## Briefly summarized

For almost all tasks, a neutral or all-purpose cleaner, dishwashing liquid, a limescale cleaner and scouring milk are sufficient; a glass cleaner is optional. Remember the pH principle: acidic against lime, alkaline against fat, neutral for everyday life. It's better to invest in good microfiber cloths, suitable sponges and brushes, a wiping system, a squeegee and gloves. Wash microfiber without fabric softener, replace used sponges, and store all products in their original bottles out of the reach of children. And only take one remedy at a time.
