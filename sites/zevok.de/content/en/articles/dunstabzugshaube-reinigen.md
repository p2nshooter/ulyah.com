The extractor hood works silently during every cooking and is often forgotten. It accumulates an astonishing amount of fat in a short period of time. Those who take out the filters for the first time in months often find a sticky, brown layer that not only looks unappetizing. Clogged filters reduce suction power, leave odors in the kitchen and, in the worst case, can even catch fire if a flame from the stove ignites the grease.

In this guide you will find out which filters are in your hood, how to clean grease filters in the dishwasher or by hand, when the activated carbon filter needs to be changed, how to clean stainless steel, glass and paint housings and how to use the hood correctly.

## Exhaust or recirculated air: which hood do you have?

**Exhaust hoods** suck in the kitchen fumes and direct them outside via a shaft or wall box. They have a grease filter, but no activated carbon filter.

**Recirculation hoods** suck in the air, filter it and return it to the kitchen. They have a grease filter and an additional activated carbon filter that binds odors.

You can tell whether your hood has exhaust air or recirculated air by looking at the exhaust pipe that leads to the wall or ceiling or by looking at the operating instructions. Many hoods can be converted to both operating modes.

## Clean grease filter

The grease filter is located directly at the bottom of the hood, usually as a metal grid or as a cassette made of several layers of expanded metal. It catches the fat droplets from the steam.

**How ​​often?** Approximately every two to four weeks depending on your cooking habits. Many hoods have an indicator that tells you when the filter should be cleaned. If you fry and fry a lot, you clean more often.

**Removing:** The filters usually have a tab or a handle that you can use to unlock them and remove them downwards. Hold them tightly as they are often heavier and greasier than you think.

### In the dishwasher

Most metal grease filters can go in the dishwasher. Check the instruction manual.

1. **Place the filter upright or at an angle** in the lower basket so that the water can flow through easily.
2. **Wash alone or with few dishes**, as the dissolved fat can settle on glasses and dishes.
3. **Select a hot program**.
4. **Allow to dry thoroughly** before reinserting the filters.

**Good to know:** Aluminum filters can turn dark in the dishwasher. It doesn't look nice, but it doesn't affect functionality.

### By hand

1. Pour **hot water** into the sink or a tub and add **dishwashing liquid**.
2. **Soak the filter** for about 15 to 30 minutes to soften the fat.
3. **Brush with a dishwashing brush** from both sides without bending the grille.
4. **Rinse thoroughly with hot water**.
5. **Allow to dry completely** and reinsert.

A little baking soda in the soaking water helps with stubborn layers of fat. **Soda** dissolves grease even more strongly, but can discolor and attack aluminum. Therefore only use it with stainless steel filters and wear gloves. More about the differences in the guide [baking soda, soda and baking powder] (/ratgeber/natron-soda-backpulver/).

## Change activated carbon filter

The activated carbon filter in circulating air hoods binds odors. Over time it becomes saturated and no longer works.

- **Conventional activated carbon filters** are replaced according to the manufacturer's instructions, usually about every three to six months, depending on usage.
- **Renewable activated carbon filters** can be refreshed in the oven at a certain temperature according to the manufacturer's instructions and thus last significantly longer. A normal activated carbon filter is not allowed in the oven or in the water.
- **Buy the right filter:** Make a note of the model of your hood, as filters are usually device-specific.

You can recognize a saturated filter because smells linger in the kitchen for significantly longer after cooking.

## Clean the housing

**Before cleaning**, switch off the hood and, when wiping inside, disconnect it from the power, for example via the plug or fuse.

**Stainless steel:** Always wipe in the direction of the sanding with a damp microfiber cloth and a little dishwashing liquid, never in circles. Dry with a second cloth. Fingerprints disappear with a drop of dishwashing liquid or a stainless steel care product. Abrasives and steel wool will scratch the surface.

**Glass:** Clean glass surfaces with a damp cloth and a little dishwashing liquid or glass cleaner. If there is a greasy film, first degrease it with dishwashing liquid, then wipe it clean.

**Painted surfaces:** Only mild cleaners and soft cloths, no solvents.

**Inside and filter frame:** Wipe the area behind the filters with a well-wrung cloth and a little dishwashing liquid. Make sure that no water gets on the lamps, cables or motor.

**Control panel:** Only wipe lightly with a damp cloth, do not allow water to run into buttons or joints.

**Lighting:** Lamp covers can often be removed and rinsed. Let the lamps cool down first. You can replace defective bulbs according to the instructions.

## Backflow flap, exhaust pipe and wall box

With exhaust hoods, the air path does not end at the filter. Behind the hood, a pipe or flat duct leads to the outside wall, and there is usually a **wall box** with flaps. A **backflow flap** in the pipe or on the device prevents cold air from outside from drawing into the kitchen when the hood is switched off.

These flaps can stick over time if grease builds up that the filters didn't catch. A sign of this is a rattling noise, a noticeable draft when the hood is switched off or suction power that does not return despite the filter being clean.

- **Check the wall box from the outside:** When you can safely access it, check whether the slats move freely and whether there are leaves, cobwebs or nests blocking them.
- **Carefully clean accessible flaps:** With a cloth and a little washing up water, without bending the mechanism or springs.
- **Do not dismantle the pipe yourself:** If ducts are installed in ceilings or cupboards, opening them should be done by a specialist company.

## Get rid of smells in the kitchen permanently

Even a clean hood can't do everything. If you smell for a long time after cooking, it's worth taking a look at other sources:

- **Use a lid:** With a lid, less steam and grease mist are created and the hood has to work less.
- **Briefly and vigorously ventilate:** After roasting, open the window completely instead of tilting it for hours.
- **Check garbage and drain:** Often a stubborn smell does not come from the stove, but from organic waste or the sink siphon.
- **Change textiles:** Tea towels and dishcloths absorb the smell of cooking. Change them often, especially after fish or searing.

If none of this helps and the activated carbon filter is fresh, it may be that the hood is too small for the kitchen or is running at a setting that is too low.

## Use the hood correctly

- **Switch on before cooking.** This creates an air flow before the first steam rises.
- **Choose the appropriate level,** higher when frying, lower when simmering.
- **Let the hood run for another ten to fifteen minutes after cooking so that residual steam and odors are sucked out. Many devices have an automatic overrun function for this purpose.
- **Provide fresh air:** Exhaust hoods need air flowing in, otherwise they suck poorly. Tilt a window slightly.
- **No flambé flames** directly under the hood, the fat in the filters can ignite.

**Important for fireplaces or gas boilers:** If a room air-dependent fireplace or gas boiler is operated in the same apartment, an exhaust hood can create negative pressure and draw exhaust gases into the apartment. In such cases, safety devices such as a window contact switch are required. Have this checked by a specialist or a chimney sweep.

## How often which task?

| task | How often |
|---|---|
| Wipe the outside of the housing | weekly or after heavy frying |
| Clean grease filter | every two to four weeks |
| Change or regenerate activated carbon filter | according to the manufacturer's instructions, often every three to six months |
| Wipe out the interior behind the filters | Check briefly every time you clean the filter |
| Clean lamp covers | if necessary |

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Forgetting the filter for months:** The suction power drops and grease can ignite.
- **Soak aluminum filter in soda:** The metal is attacked and discolored.
- **Insert a damp filter:** Residual water drips and the grease attracts dust.
- **Wash out the activated carbon filter:** This makes normal activated carbon filters unusable.
- **Clean stainless steel in circles:** Streaks and scratches will occur.
- **Operate the hood without supply air:** It sucks poorly and can be dangerous in fireplaces.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my extractor hood no longer working properly?

The grease filters are usually clogged or the activated carbon filter is saturated. In the case of exhaust air hoods, the cause may be a lack of supply air, or the backflow flap in the exhaust air pipe may be stuck. Clean the filters and open a window while cooking. If that doesn't help, ask customer service.

### Can I treat the grease filters with oven cleaner?

Many manufacturers advise against this, especially with aluminum filters, which can be attacked. In most cases, dishwashing liquid, hot water and patience are enough.

### Can I operate the hood without a grease filter?

No. Without a grease filter, the grease goes directly into the fan, motor and exhaust pipe. From there it can hardly be removed, reduces performance and increases the risk of fire. After cleaning, always reinsert the filters before cooking.

### How do I get rid of the sticky layer on top of kitchen cabinets?

This layer is a mixture of grease and dust. A warm cloth with detergent and a little patience will solve it. Then place paper or a wipeable film on the cabinets that you replace regularly. ## Briefly summarized

Clean the grease filters every two to four weeks in the dishwasher or in hot soapy water, let them dry completely and do not use soda on aluminum. Change or regenerate the activated carbon filter of a recirculation hood according to the manufacturer's instructions. Wipe stainless steel in the direction of grinding, glass with mild detergent and paint and disconnect the hood from the power supply before wiping the inside. Switch on the hood before cooking, let it run, ensure supply of air and have the safety of your fireplace or gas boiler checked by a professional.
