Gall soap is one of the home remedies that many people swear by. The inconspicuous bar of soap lies next to the washing machine and is always used when a stain doesn't disappear on its own: a splash of grease on the shirt, a gray edge on the collar, grass stains on the children's trousers. In many cases, gall soap actually helps surprisingly well. But it is not a remedy for every stain and every material.

In this guide you will find out what gall soap actually is and why it works, which stains it is particularly good for, how to use it correctly step by step and which substances you should be careful with or avoid using completely.

## What is gall soap?

Classic bile soap is a **curd soap to which beef bile is added**. Bile contains bile salts that break down fat into fine droplets, similar to what happens during digestion in the body. Together with the soap, they remove grease and protein-containing dirt particularly well from textile fibers.

Today gall soap is available in different forms:

| shape | Advantages | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| bar of soap | cheap, long lasting, easy to dose | Collar, cuffs, individual stains |
| Liquid | absorbs quickly, good for larger areas | large stains, sports clothing |
| Spray | applied quickly | Pre-treatment directly before washing |
| Stain pen | handy for on the go | fresh stains, small spots |

There are also **variants without bovine bile** that work with plant surfactants and enzymes. They are often offered as vegan gall soap. A look at the contents shows what is in the product.

## Which stains gall soap helps with?

Gall soap is particularly strong on **fat** and on dirt in which fat and protein are mixed.

| stain | How well does gall soap help? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cooking oil, butter, sauces | very good | treat as early as possible |
| Collar and cuff edges | very good | Skin fat and sweat dissolve easily |
| Makeup, lipstick | good | Work in gently, do not rub |
| Grass | good | Do not treat the stain with hot water beforehand |
| Blood | good if fresh | always work cold |
| Chocolate | good | First scrape off any residue |
| Red wine, coffee, tea | moderate | other methods are often better |
| Ballpoint pen, wax, resin | low | Other means help here |

Gall soap works less reliably on types of stains that consist primarily of dyes, such as red wine or coffee. The guides [Removing red wine stains](/ratgeber/rotweinflecken-entfernen/) and [Removing ballpoint pens, wax and chewing gum](/ratgeber/kugelschreiber-wachs-kaugummi/) explain how to treat such stains.

## Apply gall soap step by step

1. **Check care label:** Can the garment be washed and at what temperature? The symbols are explained in the guide [Understanding care symbols] (/ratgeber/pflegesymbole/).
2. **Remove residues:** Carefully remove solid residues such as sauce, chocolate or soil with the back of a spoon or a dull knife without rubbing the stain.
3. **Wet:** Moisten the stain with **cold or lukewarm water**. Hot water can trap protein like blood in the fabric.
4. **Rubbing:** Rub the gall soap directly onto the stain; if using liquid soap, apply a few drops.
5. **Work in gently:** Work from the edge to the center with your fingers or a soft brush. This prevents the stain from spreading further.
6. **Leave on:** Approximately 10 to 30 minutes. The soap should not dry out completely.
7. **Washing:** Wash the garment as usual according to the care label.
8. **Check the result before putting it in the dryer:** If the stain is still visible, repeat the treatment. Heat in the dryer can permanently fix leftovers.

You can find basic rules that apply to every stain in the guide [Removing stains: the basic rules](/ratgeber/flecken-entfernen-grundregeln/).

## Typical everyday uses

**Collar and cuffs:** Gray edges on shirts and blouses are caused by skin oil and sweat. Moisten the area, rub in gall soap, brush gently with a soft brush and let it soak in briefly before washing. Used regularly, the edges don't even stick.

**Children's Clothing:** Grass, soil, food and chocolate are ideal candidates. If possible, treat the stains on the same day. The guide [Removing Grass Stains](/ratgeber/grasflecken-entfernen/) explains how to specifically remove grass stains.

**Grease splashes:** Oil and butter on clothes can be easily removed with gall soap, especially if you don't let them dry for too long. You can find out more about grease stains on various fabrics in the guide [Removing grease stains] (/ratgeber/fettflecken-entfernen/).

**Blood:** First rinse fresh blood with plenty of cold water, then treat with gall soap. Warm water is taboo here. You can find the complete instructions in the guide [Removing blood stains](/ratgeber/blutflecken-entfernen/).

**Sportswear:** Functional fabrics retain skin oil and odors. Gall soap on the armpits and collar can help. But check the care label, because some functional materials only tolerate special detergents.

**Cloth shoes and work clothes:** Canvas shoes can be freshened up with gall soap, lukewarm water and a soft brush, unless the manufacturer specifies otherwise. If your work clothing is smeared with oil, gall soap helps to loosen fresh smudges. Old, dried-out machine oils often need several passes.

## Gall soap, stain salt or stain spray?

There are many stain removers available in stores. They work differently and complement each other rather than replacing each other.

| Means | Operating principle | Strong at |
|---|---|---|
| Gall soap | Soap and bile salts dissolve fat | Bold, collar edges, makeup, grass |
| Stain remover with oxygen bleach | Oxygen bleaches dyes | Coffee, tea, red wine, fruit on light, colorfast fabrics |
| Enzyme-containing stain spray | Enzymes break down protein and starch | Blood, egg, milk, sauces with starch |
| Alcohol or spirit | dissolves dyes and resins | Ballpoint pen, felt-tip pen, resin |

The rule of thumb is: **Grease with bile soap, dyes with oxygen bleach, egg whites with enzymes and cold water.** If you don't know what a stain is made of, start with the mildest method, cold water and bile soap, and only increase if necessary. Always check stain removers and alcohol on a hidden area beforehand, as they can change colors. Alcohol is also flammable and should only be used in well-ventilated rooms without open flames.

## Where you should be careful

Gall soap is a soap and therefore **basic**. This is not a problem for robust fabrics such as cotton, linen and many blended fabrics. Sensitive materials, on the other hand, react faster.

- **Wool and silk:** These fibers are made of protein and do not like alkaline substances. Use gall soap only sparingly, selectively and briefly, rinse thoroughly and then wash with a wool or mild detergent. If you have valuable items, you're better off having them cleaned. More about this in the guide [Washing wool and silk](/ratgeber/wolle-und-seide-waschen/).
- **Colorful and dark fabrics:** First test whether the color holds in a hidden area, such as an inside seam. Vigorous rubbing can also create light spots.
- **Leather, suede and faux leather:** Gall soap is not intended for this. Special leather care products can help here.
- **Non-washable textiles:** Items that can only be dry-cleaned according to the label should be cleaned.
- **Carpets and upholstery:** Gall soap can help, but soap residue will attract new dirt later. Work with a little water, dab the soap thoroughly with clean water and let everything dry thoroughly. The exact technique is explained in the guide [Removing stains from carpets and upholstery] (/ratgeber/flecken-teppich-polster/).

**For the skin:** Gall soap is a detergent for textiles, not a care soap. If you have sensitive skin, wear gloves if you use it frequently.

## Store gall soap properly

- **Store the soap bar in a dry place:** It stays firmly on a soap dish with drip grooves and lasts a long time. If it is wet, it becomes soft and greasy.
- **Seal the liquid gall soap well** and protect it from frost.
- **Keep out of the reach of children,** like all detergents and cleaning products.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Start with hot water:** Protein stains such as blood will set. Start cold or lukewarm.
- **Rub the stain vigorously:** It will get bigger and the fibers will suffer. It's better to work gently from the outside in.
- **Allow soap to dry:** Dried soap residue is difficult to rinse out.
- **Without testing on colorful fabrics:** Color may come off or be rubbed light.
- **Soak wool and silk for a long time:** The fibers become brittle.
- **Too early in the dryer:** Remains of the stain are fixed by heat.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use gall soap for all laundry instead of detergent?

That doesn't make sense. Gall soap is intended for pre-treatment. A complete detergent also contains softeners, enzymes and other ingredients that are tailored to the entire load. How to dose detergent correctly can be found in the guide [Dosing detergent] (/ratgeber/waschmittel-dosieren/).

### Is gall soap the same as curd soap?

Not quite. Gall soap is usually based on curd soap, but also contains beef bile or a vegetable substitute that dissolves fat particularly well. The guide [Curd Soap and Soft Soap](/ratgeber/kernseife-schmierseife/) explains what else curd soap and soft soap do in the household.

### Does laundry smell like gall soap?

Gall soap has a typical, slightly soapy smell. After the normal wash cycle, it is usually no longer noticeable. If a smell remains, the amount was too large or the part was not rinsed thoroughly enough. An additional rinse helps.

### How long does a bar of gall soap last?

When used selectively, it often lasts for many months. It is crucial that the piece can dry after each use. A dried out, cracked piece can be briefly moistened and then works as usual again.

### Does gall soap also help with old stains?

Sometimes. Old stains that have already been washed or fixed in the dryer are much more stubborn. Several applications with a longer exposure time can lighten them. But there is no guarantee for this.

## Briefly summarized

Gall soap is a curd soap with bovine bile or a vegetable substitute and primarily removes stains containing fat and protein such as oil, collar edges, make-up, grass and fresh blood. Moisten the stain with cold or lukewarm water, rub in the soap, work it gently from the edge to the middle, let it work for 10 to 30 minutes and wash the item according to the care label. Check the result before putting it in the dryer. When it comes to wool, silk, colorful fabrics, leather and non-washable textiles, be careful or use other means.
