A freshly mopped floor that has gray streaks and sticky spots after it dries. A laminate that swells at the edges. A marble floor that has dull spots after cleaning. A surprising amount goes wrong when wiping, even though it looks so simple. The reason is almost always the same: too much water, too much cleaner or a product that doesn't suit the material.

In this guide you will find out how to prepare the floor, which tools are useful, how to treat every type of flooring correctly and why clear water is often more effective than a splash of cleaning agent.

## First vacuum, then wipe

Before water gets on the floor, dust, crumbs and especially sand have to go. Sand acts like sandpaper under the mop and leaves fine scratches on sensitive floors. Water also turns loose dust into gray gunk that collects in corners and crevices.

Therefore, always vacuum or sweep thoroughly first, including under furniture and on the skirting boards. The guide [Cleaning Efficiently](/ratgeber/putzen-reihenfolge/) shows you how to organize your work in the apartment sensibly.

## The right tool

| tool | Good for | To be noted |
|---|---|---|
| Flat mop with microfiber cover | almost all floors, especially laminate and parquet | Change and wash covers regularly |
| Mop with wringing bucket | Tiles, large areas | Wring it out well, otherwise it will be too wet |
| Sponge mop | smooth floors, small areas | Rinse and dry sponge thoroughly after use |
| Steam cleaner | Tiles and porcelain stoneware | not for laminate, parquet, cork and many vinyl floors |
| Cordless vacuum cleaner | smooth floors with lots of everyday dirt | Follow the manufacturer’s instructions on the floor type |

For most households, a **flat mop with multiple microfiber covers** is the best choice. You can control the humidity well and put on a fresh cover after each room. This way you don't spread the dirt from the kitchen into the hallway.

**Wash microfiber covers:** At 60 degrees with normal detergent and without fabric softener. Fabric softener sticks the fine fibers together, making them much less able to absorb dirt and water.

## Less cleaner, more results

Streaks and sticky floors are usually caused not by too little cleaning agent, but by too much cleaning agent. If you add too much to the water, it will remain as a film on the floor after it dries. New dirt settles in there particularly quickly.

- **Dosage according to the package instructions**, often one cap per bucket of water is enough.
- **Lukewarm water** dissolves dirt well, very hot water has no advantage and can damage sensitive floors.
- **Change the water** as soon as it becomes cloudy. With dirty water you only spread dirt.
- **If there are streaks:** Wipe the floor once with clear, lukewarm water. This dissolves cleaning residue.

## The right technology

1. **Open the window.** Fresh air allows the floor to dry faster.
2. **Work from back to front.** Start in the corner furthest from the door and wipe backwards out of the room. This means you don't have to walk over wet surfaces.
3. **Wipe in lanes or figure eights.** With the flat mop you can move in a horizontal figure eight in front of you without lifting the mop from the floor. This means that the dirt stays in front of the cover and is not spread.
4. **Edges and corners first.** Run along the baseboards before wiping the surface.
5. **Soak stubborn spots.** Briefly cover dried stains with a damp cloth and wipe after a few minutes instead of scrubbing.
6. **Let dry and do not walk on.** Keep children and pets away until the floor is dry. Wet floors are slippery.

## Tiles and porcelain stoneware

Glazed tiles and porcelain stoneware are robust. You can wipe them with a damp cloth, with an all-purpose cleaner or a special floor cleaner. In the bathroom, acidic cleaners occasionally help against limescale edges, but only on glazed tiles and not constantly, because acid attacks the cement-containing joints.

Unglazed porcelain stoneware with a rough surface absorbs dirt more easily. Regular wiping with an alkaline basic cleaner according to the manufacturer's instructions helps here. How to get the joints clean can be found in the guide [Cleaning tile joints] (/ratgeber/fliesenfugen-reinigen/).

## Laminate

Laminate consists of a baseboard made of wood fibers with a thin decorative layer. If water penetrates the edges, the panel swells and this cannot be reversed.

- Only wipe laminate with a damp cloth. This means: The cover is wrung out so well that the floor is dry within one to two minutes.
- Use a mild cleaner or a special laminate cleaner, no abrasive cleaners and no oil or wax.
- Absorb standing water immediately.
- No steam cleaner, even if the laminate is considered suitable for wet rooms, unless the manufacturer expressly allows it.

## Parquet and wooden floorboards

When it comes to wooden floors, the surface is important. Check the care instructions or ask during installation.

**Sealed parquet** has a protective layer of varnish. Wipe it damp with a pH-neutral wooden floor cleaner. Occasionally you can add a care product for sealed floors to the mopping water to fill in small scratches.

**Oiled or waxed parquet** is open-pored. Here you need special wooden floor soaps that have a moisturizing effect and care for the wood. Normal cleaners dry out the wood and make it stained. Oiled parquet needs to be re-oiled from time to time.

The following applies to all wooden floors: as little water as possible, no steam cleaner, no microfiber cloths with a strong abrasive effect on high-gloss paint.

## Vinyl, design flooring and linoleum

**Vinyl and design floors** are easy to care for and tolerate moisture better than laminate. Click vinyl on the carrier board should still not be wiped wet because water can run into the joints. Use mild cleaners. Solvent-containing agents and scouring milk can attack the surface.

**Linoleum** is a natural material made from linseed oil, resins and wood flour. It does not tolerate strong alkaline cleaners or standing water. Wipe it damp with a pH-neutral cleaner or a special cleaning care for linoleum.

## Natural stone

Marble, limestone, travertine and Solnhofen slabs contain lime and are sensitive to acid. **Vinegar, citric acid, limescale cleaner and acidic bathroom cleaner** dissolve the surface. This creates dull, matt stains that only a specialist company can sand off.

The following applies to natural stone:

- Only use pH-neutral cleaners or special stone soaps.
- Clean up spilled juices, wine and vinegar immediately.
- Granite and slate are less sensitive to acid, but should still be treated gently.

## Less dirt right from the start

The best floor is the one that doesn't get that much dirt in the first place. A few simple habits save a lot of wiping work:

- **Dirt trap mats** in front of and behind the apartment door keep out sand and moisture. A mat outside for the coarse dirt and one inside for the rest work better than just one.
- **Take off your shoes** in the entrance area to prevent sand, road salt and earth from being carried throughout the apartment.
- **Felt gliders** under chairs and tables protect laminate, parquet and vinyl from scratches. Check them regularly, because stones that have fallen in turn them into a grinding surface.
- **Remove road salt immediately:** In winter, shoes leave white edges. Wipe them away promptly with lukewarm water before the salt attacks surfaces and penetrates into joints.
- **Use a wipeable mat or rug under the sink and around the stove** where drips often occur.

## How often to wipe?

| area | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Kitchen | weekly, more often if necessary |
| Hallway and entrance | weekly, more often when wet and muddy |
| bathroom | weekly |
| Living and sleeping rooms | every one to two weeks |
| Basic cleaning with furniture backing | two to four times a year |

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Too much water on laminate and parquet:** The edges swell. Always wipe with a damp cloth.
- **Too much cleaner:** The floor becomes sticky and dirty faster. Dose according to instructions, wipe with clear water if there are streaks.
- **Vinegar on natural stone:** Acid leaves permanent dull stains.
- **Without vacuuming first:** Sand scratches the floor, dust turns into grease.
- **Fabric softener for microfiber cloths:** It makes the fibers ineffective.
- **Continue wiping with dirty water:** Change the water and cover after each room.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my floor sticky after mopping?

There was usually too much cleaner in the water or the cover was dirty. Wipe with clear, lukewarm water and reduce the amount next time.

### Can I add different cleaners to the mopping water?

No. Mixtures have no advantage and can be dangerous, for example when chlorine-containing and acidic agents come together. The most important rules are on the page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

### Is a steam cleaner more thorough than regular mopping?

On tiles and porcelain stoneware, hot steam dissolves stuck-on dirt well and does not require a cleaner. But it is not necessary for everyday use: a clean microfiber cover with lukewarm water also removes the usual dirt. However, steam can cause damage on laminate, parquet, cork, many vinyl floors and sealed natural stone. Before using it for the first time, read the floor manufacturer's care instructions.

### How do I get rid of stains on the carpet?

Different rules apply to carpets than to smooth floors. ## Briefly summarized

Always vacuum or sweep first. Use a flat mop with microfiber covers and change the cover after each room. Use cleaner sparingly and wipe with clean water if there are streaks. Only wipe laminate, parquet, cork and linoleum with a damp cloth; never treat natural stone with acid. Work from the far corner toward the door and let the floor dry before anyone walks over it.
