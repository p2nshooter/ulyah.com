At first glance, the sink and bathtub look similar, but they are made of completely different materials. A classic ceramic sink forgives almost everything. An acrylic bathtub, on the other hand, gets fine marks from just a scratchy sponge, and a mineral cast washbasin can become dull from a strong limescale cleaner. If you clean everything with the same products and the same technique, you risk causing damage that can no longer be repaired.

In this guide you will learn how to recognize the material of your pool, which cleaners and tools are suitable, how to keep the overflow, stopper and drain clean, how to treat edges, discoloration and light scratches and how to keep your bathroom well-maintained with little daily effort.

## What material do you have?

| material | How you recognize it | What it can handle |
|---|---|---|
| Sanitary ware | heavy, cool, hard shiny glaze, typical for sinks and toilets | most bathroom cleaners, mild scouring milk sparingly |
| Enamel | Tub made of steel or cast iron with a glass-like coating, sounds metallic when knocked | mild and acidic cleaners, no harsh abrasives |
| Sanitary acrylic | light, feels warmer, sounds hollow when tapped | only mild cleaners and soft cloths |
| Mineral casting | matt or silky glossy, often in modern wash basins | Mild cleaners, acids and scouring agents only according to the manufacturer's instructions |
| Natural stone | visible grain, marble, granite or limestone | pH-neutral special cleaners, no acid for marble and limestone |

If you are unsure, check your apartment's or the manufacturer's documentation, or test an agent on an inconspicuous area. If in doubt: **start gently**.

## Basic cleaning for every pool

You don't need much for weekly care:

- **a mild bathroom cleaner** or a little dishwashing liquid in warm water,
- **a soft sponge** or a microfiber cloth,
- **a small brush** for overflow and drain,
- **a dry cloth** for polishing.

This is how you do it:

1. **Clear the shelves:** Toothbrush cups, soap and bottles are put aside.
2. **Faucet First:** Clean the lever and spout before entering the pool.
3. **From top to bottom:** Edge, inside, bottom and finally the drain.
4. **Rinse thoroughly:** Cleaner residue attracts dirt and leaves streaks.
5. **Wipe dry:** This is the only way you can see whether any limescale remains and water stains don't stand a chance.

## Ceramic: robust, but not indestructible

Sanitary ceramics are very resistant. You can remove limescale marks with an acidic bathroom cleaner or citric acid solution, and soap residue with a mild cleaner. **You can use mild scouring milk** occasionally for stubborn areas, but not every day, because even a hard glaze becomes duller over time. You can also remove gray abrasion from metal objects, such as a ring or a pot, with a little scouring milk and a soft sponge.

## Enamel: Preserve shine

Enamel is a glass-like layer on steel or cast iron. It is hard but can lose its shine due to acids and abrasives.

- **Allow acidic cleaners to take effect for a short time** and rinse thoroughly.
- **No coarse abrasives** and no steel wool.
- **Repair chipped areas** quickly, as the metal underneath can rust. There are repair paints for enamel available from specialist retailers.
- **Rust stains** often come from such damaged areas or from metal objects that are in the tub. Don't place cans and razors on the edge of the tub.

## Acrylic: treat gently

Sanitary acrylic is warm, light and comfortable, but sensitive to scratches.

- **Only use soft cloths and sponges**, no scouring milk or scouring pads.
- **No solvents** such as acetone or nail polish remover. You can dissolve the surface.
- **Remove limescale with mild, diluted vinegar or citric acid** and rinse well. Follow the manufacturer's instructions.
- **Fine scratches** can often be repaired with a special acrylic polish from a specialist retailer. Deep scratches belong in the hands of a specialist company.

## Mineral casting and natural stone

**Mineral casting** consists of rock dust and resin, often with a smooth top layer. Many manufacturers recommend mild cleaners and advise against abrasives and strong acids. Read the care instructions, because the recommendations vary significantly depending on the surface.

**Natural stone** such as marble or limestone is sensitive to any acid. Vinegar, citric acid and limescale cleaner leave dull, rough stains. Only use pH-neutral cleaners or special natural stone care and wipe lime water dry after each use. Granite is more robust, but is also best cared for gently.

## Overflow, plug and drain

The **overflow** is the small opening under the edge of the pool. Soap residue and mucus collect there, which can smell unpleasant. Clean it regularly with a narrow bottle brush and some cleaner and rinse with hot water.

The **plug**, often as a drain fitting with a lever or push button, can be removed or unscrewed from most basins. There is hair and soap slime underneath. Using gloves, pull it out and clean the plug under running water.

**If the water drains slowly:**

1. **Remove Hair:** A majority of sink and tub clogs are hair and soap. A drain spiral or a narrow plastic hook gets them out.
2. **Use the suction cup:** Cover the overflow with a damp cloth so that the suction cup can build up pressure and pump vigorously.
3. **Cleaning the siphon:** Place a bucket under the basin, unscrew the siphon, empty and clean it and screw it back on tightly.
4. **Add hot but not boiling water**.

**Chemical drain cleaners** are very aggressive. They can damage pipes, seals and skin and react violently with other agents. Use them only as a last resort, exactly according to the instructions, and never use two different ones in a row. Do not use a suction cup after using a chemical drain cleaner because the caustic liquid may spray out. More about this on the page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

The popular mixture of baking soda and vinegar is not a miracle weapon either: it fizzes impressively, but largely neutralizes itself. The guide [Home Remedies Myths Checked] (/ratgeber/hausmittel-mythen/) explains why.

## Margins, stains and discoloration

**Bathtub edge:** After a bath, a gray ring of skin oil, soap and lime is often left behind. It's easiest to remove with warm water and a little dishwashing liquid while it's fresh. Therefore, rinse the tub immediately after bathing.

**Discoloration caused by hair dyes, bath products or tints:** Wipe off immediately with water and a mild detergent. The longer the paint works, the deeper it is absorbed, especially into acrylic and mineral casting.

**Yellowish spots under the tap:** Usually a mixture of lime and minerals from the water. Treat it like you would lime with an acidic cleaner if the material can tolerate it. The instructions for this can be found in the guide [Removing limescale in the shower] (/ratgeber/kalk-in-der-dusche/).

**Anti-slip mats with suction cups:** Take them out after bathing and let them dry. Otherwise, mold will quickly form on the underside. Clean them regularly with warm water and dish soap.

## Silicone joints on tub and basin

The elastic joint between the tub and the wall prevents water from getting behind the paneling. Keep them dry and clean to prevent mold from forming. If it is dark discolored, cracked or peeling off, it should be replaced. ## Small habits, big impact

You can save yourself most of the work with simple steps that only take seconds:

- **Rinse briefly after brushing your teeth** to prevent toothpaste from drying.
- **Wipe the basin and faucet dry in the evening,** especially if the water is hard.
- **Place the hair strainer in the drain,** then hair will end up in the strainer instead of in the pipe.
- **Place the soap on a shelf with drip grooves,** otherwise a greasy ring will result.

## How often which task?

| task | How often |
|---|---|
| Rinse out the basin and wipe dry | daily |
| Thorough cleaning with a mild bathroom cleaner | weekly |
| Clean overflow and stopper | every two to four weeks |
| Remove hair from the drain | if necessary, at the latest when the water drains more slowly |
| Check siphon | if there is an odor or constipation |

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Scouring milk on acrylic or mineral cast:** Dull spots and scratches occur.
- **Acid on natural stone:** Marble and limestone are attacked.
- **Do not rinse off the cleaner:** Remains form a film that attracts dirt.
- **Solvents on plastic:** Acetone and nail polish remover can dissolve the surface.
- **Combine several drain cleaners:** Dangerous reactions are possible.
- **Leave the tub standing after bathing:** The edges dry and are difficult to remove later.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I make a yellowed acrylic tub bright again?

Slight discoloration often resolves with a mild cleaner and a little patience. If the acrylic itself has yellowed, due to age or sunlight, for example, this cannot be completely reversed. An acrylic polish can freshen up the surface.

### Can I use a steam cleaner on the bathtub?

This is usually not a problem with ceramics and enamel. For acrylic and mineral casting, you should check the manufacturer's instructions beforehand because strong heat can damage the surface.

### How do I clean a glass sink?

You clean glass basins like you would a shower wall: with a mild or slightly acidic cleaner, a soft microfiber cloth and clear water. Then wipe dry, otherwise limescale stains will remain visible. Abrasives and hard sponges leave scratches on glass that cannot be removed.

### What helps against smell from the sink?

Mucus usually sits in the overflow or siphon. Clean both thoroughly. If it still smells, the siphon may have dried out because the basin hasn't been used for a long time. Then all you have to do is let the water run.

## Briefly summarized

First determine the material: Ceramic is robust, enamel loses its shine due to acids and abrasives, acrylic is sensitive to scratches, mineral casting and natural stone require mild, special care. Clean weekly with a mild bathroom cleaner and soft sponge from top to bottom, rinse clearly and wipe dry. Clean the overflow and stopper regularly, remove hair from the drain and use the suction cup and siphon before resorting to aggressive drain cleaners. After bathing, rinse the tub, wipe off paint stains immediately, and keep the silicone grout lines dry.
