As soon as a shelf is dusted, there is another layer of gray on it. Dust is a constant companion in every home, and it can never be completely gotten rid of. But how much of it goes around depends a lot on how you clean. A feather duster often just stirs up dust so that it settles somewhere else an hour later. A damp cloth and a good vacuum cleaner, on the other hand, will really pick it up.

In this guide you will find out what house dust is made of, which tools are best for removing it, how to gently dust sensitive surfaces, how to vacuum properly and which habits create less dust. There are additional tips for people with house dust allergies.

## What house dust is made of

House dust is a mixture of many small particles:

- **Dandruff** from people and pets.
- **Textile fibers** from clothing, bedding, carpets and upholstery.
- **Hair and animal hair.**
- **Pollen** and particles coming in from outside.
- **Earth and sand** from shoes.
- **Soot** from candles and traffic.

**House dust mites**, tiny arachnids that feed on skin flakes, live in this dust. It is not the mites themselves, but rather their droppings that trigger allergic reactions in many people. They like it warm and humid and particularly like living in mattresses, upholstery and carpets.

## The right tool

| tool | Rating | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Damp microfiber cloth | very good, binds dust | Wring out well, only slightly damp on wood |
| Dry microfiber cloth | good for electronics and screens | use clean and lint-free |
| Feather duster | unfavorable, stirs up dust | at most for hard-to-reach places |
| Electrostatic feather duster | usable, holds dust | Wash out or change cloths regularly |
| Vacuum cleaner with brush attachment | very good for books, lampshades, slats | Use a soft brush attachment |
| Vacuum cleaner with fine dust filter | important for allergies | Change filters regularly |

For most surfaces, a **slightly damp microfiber cloth** is the best tool. The fine fibers absorb dust and hold it instead of spreading it. Fold the cloth twice so you have eight clean sides that you can use one after the other.

You wash microfiber cloths at 60 degrees without fabric softener. Fabric softener sticks the fibers together and then they hardly absorb any dust.

## This is how you really dust off

1. **Open the window**, but not when there is a lot of pollen if you are allergic.
2. **Work from top to bottom**: lamps, cabinet tops, shelves, picture frames, window sills, radiators, baseboards.
3. **Lift objects** instead of swiping around them. A lot of dust collects under decorations.
4. **Turn the cloth regularly** and rinse if necessary.
5. **Finally, vacuum and mop** to remove any fallen dust.

## Sensitive surfaces

**Screens and Electronics:** Turn off devices beforehand. Wipe TVs, monitors, and laptops only with a dry, clean microfiber cloth or a special screen cloth. Never spray cleaner directly onto a device. For stubborn stains, lightly moisten the cloth with water. Glass cleaners and agents containing alcohol can damage screen coatings.

**Wooden furniture:** If you have painted furniture, wipe it with a damp cloth and then dry it. Oiled or waxed surfaces only need a dry or barely damp cloth. Standing water leaves light edges.

**High-gloss fronts:** Use a soft, lint-free cloth and a little water. Abrasive sponges and dry rubbing with dust particles scratch the surface.

**Books:** Vacuum the spines and top edges of books with the soft brush attachment. Once a year you can empty the shelves and wipe them with a damp cloth.

**Lampshades:** Clean fabric shades with a lint roller or the brush attachment, remove and rinse glass shades. Switch off the lamps beforehand and let them cool down.

**Plants:** Wipe large leaves with a damp cloth, supporting the leaf from below. You can rinse smaller plants lukewarm. Clean leaves absorb more light.

**Radiator:** A lot of dust collects between the slats and is stirred up when the heating is running. A narrow radiator brush or the crevice nozzle of the vacuum cleaner helps. A damp towel under the radiator catches falling dust.

**Ventilation grilles and fans:** Vacuum and wipe with a damp cloth. Unplug devices.

## Vacuum properly

A good vacuum cleaner is the most important weapon against dust. A few rules help:

- **Vacuum slowly.** Moving back and forth quickly picks up less dust than slow, overlapping paths.
- **Suck carpets in two directions**, once lengthwise and once across. This way you get dust from all sides of the fibers.
- **Choose the right nozzle**: Brush extended on hard floors, retracted on carpets. Crevice tool for corners and skirting boards, upholstery tool for sofas and mattresses.
- **Change the bag and filter in good time.** A full bag or clogged filter significantly reduces the suction power.
- **Cleaning the brush roller:** Hair and threads wrap around the roller and slow it down. Cut them off regularly.

How often you vacuum depends on your household. In many apartments, once a week is enough; if you have pets, small children or allergies, two to three times make sense. A vacuum robot can carry out basic daily cleaning, but it does not replace thorough vacuuming of corners and upholstery.

After vacuuming, wipe smooth floors with a damp cloth. How to do this depending on the material can be found in the guide [Wiping floors correctly] (/ratgeber/boden-wischen/).

## Which vacuum cleaner is suitable?

For most households there are three types of devices that complement each other well:

**Bagged or bagless canister vacuum cleaners** are the most thorough all-rounders. Bag devices retain dust better when emptying, which is an advantage for allergy sufferers. Bagless devices save follow-up costs, but a cloud of dust can easily arise when the container is emptied. It is therefore best to empty it outside over the garbage can.

**Cordless vacuum cleaners** are light, quick to hand and ideal for everyday life, for crumbs in the kitchen and for stairs. Pay attention to the battery life and that the battery is replaceable.

**Robot vacuums** keep floors clean between big cleaning days, especially in households with pets. They need clear floors and cables, socks or toys must be put away beforehand.

More important than the wattage are good suction power on the floor, a tight filter and suitable nozzles for upholstery, cracks and sensitive surfaces.

## Dust in the bedroom and children's room

You spend many hours at a time in the bedroom, and a lot of skin flakes and textile fibers collect there. Put the focus on the bed: change bed linen regularly, vacuum the mattress occasionally and vacuum under the bed. Stuffed animals, carpets and open toy shelves collect dust in the children's room. Boxes with lids and a few washed favorite cuddly toys make dusting much easier.

## Less dust in the apartment

- **Dirt trap mats** on the door keep sand and soil out.
- **Take off your shoes** in the entrance area.
- **Less open spaces:** Closed cabinets and display cases instead of lots of open shelves reduce the area where dust collects.
- **Wash textiles:** Wash curtains, pillowcases, blankets and bed linen regularly.
- **Ventilate properly:** Short, vigorous ventilation several times a day exchanges the air without pollen and fine dust constantly blowing in.
- **Use candles sparingly** because they produce soot, which forms a gray haze on walls and windows.
- **Keep an eye on humidity:** The air feels pleasant between around 40 and 60 percent. Very humid air promotes house dust mites and mold.

## Tips for house dust allergies

If you are allergic to house dust mites, additional measures are worthwhile. If you have any symptoms, talk to your doctor about what makes sense for you.

- **Use a vacuum cleaner with a high-quality fine dust filter** that retains allergens.
- **Change bags outside** or have someone else change them.
- **Wash bed linen frequently,** at least at 60 degrees if the material allows it.
- **Mite-proof covers** for mattresses, pillows and blankets, so-called encasings, keep allergens at bay.
- **Cuddly toys** occasionally wash or put in the freezer for a few hours and then wash.
- **Reduce carpets and heavy curtains** because they retain a lot of dust.
- **Wipe with a damp cloth instead of dusting dry,** so that no allergens are stirred up.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Working with the feather feather:** It distributes dust instead of removing it.
- **First vacuum, then dust:** The dust from above ends up on the clean floor.
- **Spraying cleaner onto screens:** Liquid can run into the device and damage coatings.
- **Wet cloth on wood:** Water leaves edges and causes wood to swell.
- **Continue to use the full vacuum cleaner bag:** The suction power drops significantly.
- **Fabric softener for microfiber cloths:** The cloths lose their effectiveness.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does the dust come back so quickly?

Dust is constantly created by people, textiles and air from outside. If you dry dust or use a frond, you'll just stir up some of it and it'll settle back down later. Wiping with a damp cloth and vacuuming thoroughly reduces it significantly.

### Do air purifiers help against dust?

Air purifiers with a good filter can filter fine particles and pollen from the air, especially in the bedrooms of allergy sufferers. They do not replace the dust on furniture and floors. Regular wiping and vacuuming is still necessary.

### What helps against animal hair on upholstery?

A rubber glove that you rub slightly damp over the upholstery collects hair into clumps, which you then vacuum up. Lint rollers and special animal hair brushes for the vacuum cleaner also help. Regular brushing of the animal reduces the amount of hair in the apartment.

### How often should I dust?

In most households once a week. If you have allergies, pets or are on busy roads, twice a week is better. ## Briefly summarized

House dust consists of skin flakes, fibers, hair, pollen and soil and is food for house dust mites. Instead of fluffing it up with a frond, pick it up with a slightly damp microfiber cloth and work from top to bottom. Only wipe screens dry or barely damp, wood only slightly damp. Vacuum slowly in overlapping paths, carpets in two directions, and change bags and filters in a timely manner. Dirt trapping mats, shoes off, regularly washed textiles and ventilation reduce dust permanently.
