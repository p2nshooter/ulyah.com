The bathroom is the room where you scrub the most and still often feel like you never get done. As soon as the sink is clean, glass cleaner drips into it while cleaning the mirror. The floor is mopped and then hair and foam falls on it while cleaning the shower. And anyone who first wipes the toilet and then the toothbrush cup with the same cloth spreads germs instead of removing them.

The solution is a fixed order. If you work from top to bottom, from clean to dirty and from the furthest corner to the door, you only do each area once and are finished faster. In this guide, we'll go through a complete bathroom cleaning step by step, show you which products belong where, and give you a little routine to keep the bathroom clean between two cleaning appointments.

## Preparation: air, space and material

**Ventilate.** Open the window before you start. In a bathroom without a window, leave the door open and the fan running. Many bathroom cleaners irritate the respiratory tract, and fresh air also helps with drying.

**Make space.** Remove toothbrushes, bottles, cosmetics and decorations from counters and sinks, remove bath mats and used towels and empty the trash can. You clean twice as fast in open areas.

**Provide materials.** Everything you need should be within easy reach so that you don't walk around the apartment with wet gloves:

- several microfiber cloths, a cloth for dry polishing, a sponge with a soft side
- a squeegee for shower walls and tiles, a small brush for grout edges and overflow
- a neutral or all-purpose cleaner, a bathroom cleaner against limescale, a toilet cleaner
- Household gloves, preferably a pair just for the toilet
- Bucket, mop or floor cloth

**Color system for cloths.** In professional cleaning, it is common to separate cloths by color. This is also worthwhile at home because germs from the toilet don't end up in the sink. You decide which color you use for what. The only important thing is that everyone in the household knows the same system.

| Color, for example | Area of application | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Red | Toilet, cistern, floor directly around the toilet | highest germ load, can't go anywhere else |
| Yellow | Sink, shower, bathtub, tiles | Sanitary areas in contact with water |
| Blue | Mirrors, shelves, cupboards, door handles, light switches | Surfaces that come into contact with hands and facial care |
| Green | just kitchen | Bath towels have no place in the kitchen |

## The order step by step

### 1. Allow the agents to take effect

First put the toilet cleaner under the flush rim and close the lid. Spray the limescale cleaner onto the shower wall and the fittings in the shower, provided the material can tolerate it. Both products will now work while you clean the other areas. Only use a single product in the toilet and never combine two different toilet products.

### 2. Dust from above

Wipe dry or just slightly damp over lamps, the fan grille, tops of shelves, the top edge of mirror cabinets and tiles as well as the windowsill. Any dust that falls ends up on surfaces that you are cleaning anyway.

### 3. Mirrors, shelves and cupboards

Wipe down counters and cabinet doors with all-purpose cleaner, then door handles and light switches. You clean the mirror at the end of this step: with a little cleaner on the cloth instead of on the glass, then polish with a dry cloth so that no streaks remain.

### 4. Sink and faucet

Clean the basin from top to bottom: first the fitting, then the edge, the inside of the basin and finally the overflow with a small brush. Don't forget the tiles directly behind the sink, where toothpaste splatters collect. Rinse with clean water and polish the fitting dry. This way you can see immediately whether there is still any limescale left. We explain in [Cleaning sinks and bathtubs](/ratgeber/waschbecken-badewanne-reinigen/) which agents ceramic, mineral cast and other materials can tolerate.

### 5. Shower and bathtub

The limescale cleaner has now worked long enough. Wipe the walls from top to bottom, then the faucet, exterior shower head, shelves, and shampoo bottles. This is followed by the shower tray or tub and finally the drain cover, from which you remove visible hair. Rinse everything thoroughly with clean water and remove the glass and tiles with the squeegee. Stubborn layers of lime require more time and technology; Everything about this can be found in [Remove limescale in the shower](/ratgeber/kalk-in-der-dusche/). If the grout lines are gray or stained, the guide [Cleaning tile grout lines] (/ratgeber/fliesenfugen-reinigen/) will help.

### 6. The toilet last

Now put on the toilet gloves and take the cloth in the toilet color. Work is carried out on the outside from top to bottom so that dirt from clean parts does not fall onto cleaned ones:

1. Cistern and push plate
2. Top and bottom lids
3. Glasses top and bottom, the hinges
4. Ceramic rim and outside of the bowl
5. Base and the floor directly around the toilet

Then clean the inside of the bowl with the brush, especially under the rim, and rinse. Clamp the wet brush between the glasses and the bowl to drain before placing it in the holder. Our guide [Cleaning the toilet and removing urine scale] (/ratgeber/toilette-reinigen-urinstein/) shows you how to remove urine scale and limescale rings.

### 7. The floor at the end

First, vacuum or sweep up hair and dust, including behind the toilet and under the radiator. Then you wipe with a damp cloth, starting in the farthest corner and working backwards towards the door. This way you don't step into the freshly mopped area. Use a separate cloth for the area around the toilet or wipe it last.

### 8. Clean up

Cloths and mop covers go into the wash as hot as their label allows. Rinse the gloves and let them dry. Only when the floor is dry do the bath mat, fresh towels and everything from the shelves come back.

## Which means belongs where

You don't need ten special cleaners for the bathroom. It is important to distinguish between the three basic types:

| Means | For what | Not for |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral or all-purpose cleaner | everyday dirt on tiles, shelves, cupboards, floor | stronger layers of lime |
| Acid bathroom or limescale cleaner | Limescale on glass, glazed tiles and chrome fittings | Marble and other calcareous natural stones, long exposure to joints, sensitive coated fittings |
| Toilet cleaner | the inside of the toilet | other surfaces unless the label specifically allows it |
| scouring milk | robust ceramic, economical | Acrylic, glass, mirrors, chrome, coated surfaces |

Most toilet and limescale cleaners are acidic. You don't need cleaners containing chlorine for normal bathroom cleaning. If you do use one, it should never be mixed with an acidic product, even in the same toilet one after the other. This can produce toxic chlorine gas. Read our [Cleaning Safety](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/) page and keep all cleaners out of the reach of children and pets.

## Small steps for every day

Thorough cleaning becomes much easier if limescale and moisture don't linger for long. Together, these actions hardly take longer than brushing your teeth:

- **Remove after showering:** Remove the glass wall and tiles from top to bottom with the squeegee. What doesn't dry out doesn't turn into lime.
- **Ventilation:** After showering or bathing, open the window wide or run the fan, leave the shower door or curtain open.
- **Spread out towels:** When they are crumpled up on the hook, they dry poorly and quickly smell musty. They dry faster when hung over a pole.
- **Wipe the sink briefly:** Remove toothpaste and hair with a cloth in the morning or evening.
- **Remove hair from the shower strainer:** Before flushing it down the drain.

## Weekly and monthly list

You do the complete cleaning above about once a week in a household with normal use. There are also a few tasks that occur less frequently but are often forgotten.

**Every week:**

- complete cleaning in the order described
- Change towels and bath mat
- Empty trash cans, change bags

**Every month:**

- **Hair strainer and drain cover:** Remove, remove hair and soap residue, rinse
- **Fan grille:** dust off; If it can be removed according to the instructions, wash it with soapy water
- **Trash can:** wash inside and outside and let dry thoroughly
- **Toothbrush cup:** Clean thoroughly, including in the dishwasher if it is suitable for this purpose
- **Soap dispenser:** Wipe the outside, remove soap residue from the pump and outlet
- **Toilet brush and holder:** clean with hot water and toilet cleaner, rinse holder and allow to dry
- **Silicone joints:** Check for dark spots and clean early

## Common mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Start with the floor:** Anything that falls or drips ends up on the freshly mopped floor. Bottom always last.
- **One cloth for everything:** This is how toilet germs get on sinks and toothbrush cups. Fixed cloths for each area, preferably with a color system.
- **Too much cleaner:** Large amounts leave a sticky residue that new dirt can adhere to. It is better to apply in doses and rinse thoroughly.
- **Spray cleaner into the air:** You breathe in the spray mist. Spray close to the surface or onto the cloth.
- **Acid on the wrong material:** Acidic cleaners attack marble and other calcareous stones and cause joints to age more quickly. Check beforehand and test in a hidden area.
- **Allow limescale cleaner to dry:** This causes stains on fittings. Observe the exposure time, then rinse and polish dry.
- **Wet toilet brush in the holder:** A greasy, foul-smelling coating quickly forms in standing water. Drain and rinse holder regularly.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to thoroughly clean a bathroom?

That depends on size, equipment and dirt. With a fixed order and material provided, it gets faster each time because you don't duplicate any area.

### Do I need disinfectant in the bathroom?

In a healthy household, thorough cleaning with the above-mentioned products is sufficient. It is best to discuss with your doctor whether more is necessary if you have an infection in the household.

## Briefly summarized

Ventilate, create space and provide all materials, then present toilet cleaner and limescale cleaner. Work from top to bottom and from clean to dirty: dust, mirrors and shelves, sink, shower or tub, toilet last and the floor backwards towards the door. Separate cloths by area, use a neutral cleaner for everyday use and an acidic cleaner for limescale, and never mix different cleaners. With cleaning, airing and a few monthly tasks, the bathroom will stay in good condition between cleaning appointments.
