The new sweater is out of the bag, the washing machine is already running, and then you look at the small label in the side seam: a tub with a hand, a crossed-out triangle, a square with a horizontal line, an iron with two dots and a circle with a letter. If you can't read these signs, you wash by feel, and that's exactly where the classic accidents happen: shrunken wool, faded colors, warped shirts and sweaters that are two sizes smaller after the dryer.

The system is simple once you understand the logic. There are only five basic symbols, and dots, dashes and crosses all mean roughly the same thing. In this guide, we'll go through each symbol, explain what it specifically means for your washer, dryer, and iron, and show you what to do if the label is missing or cut off.

## The five basic symbols and their fixed order

On labels in Germany and most European countries, the care symbols are always in the same order, just like care takes place in everyday life:

1. **Washing tub:** Washing
2. **Triangle:** Bleach
3. **Square:** Dry
4. **Iron:** Iron
5. **Circle:** professional textile care in the dry cleaners

Three basic rules apply to all symbols. A **cross** over a symbol means: This treatment is not permitted. **Points** represent temperature levels, the more points, the warmer. **Lines under a symbol** mean a more gentle treatment, two lines mean a particularly gentle treatment. It is also important: every specification is an upper limit. Washing colder, ironing lower or drying more gently is always allowed.

Next to the symbols, the fiber composition is usually stated on the label, for example “80% cotton, 20% polyester”. It is mandatory in the EU, but the care symbols are voluntary. Nevertheless, read both, because the fiber information will help you later if a symbol is missing or illegible.

## Washing: the tub

The washing tub is the most important symbol because it reveals three things: whether the item can be washed at all, how warmly and how vigorously.

- **Number in the tub:** the highest permitted washing temperature in degrees Celsius. 30, 40, 60 and 95 are common, more rarely 50 or 70. A tub with 60 without lines is suitable for towels or bed linen made of cotton, a tub with 30 is more suitable for printed shirts or delicately colored fabrics.
- **One line under the tub:** Gentle wash cycle with reduced mechanics, i.e. the program for easy-care textiles or synthetics. The machine moves the drum more gently and spins more slowly. Load them less than would be possible with the cooking and colored laundry program.
- **Two lines under the tub:** very gentle laundry, i.e. delicate or wool program. Typical for wool, silk and some viscose. Our guide [washing wool, cashmere and silk] (/ratgeber/wolle-und-seide-waschen/) shows you how to do this.
- **Hand in the tub:** Hand wash only, lukewarm at most. Do not rub, do not wring, but press gently and squeeze out rolled up in a towel. You only use a handwash or wool program on the machine if the operating instructions expressly specify it for such textiles.
- **Crossed out tub:** Do not wash, either by machine or by hand. The part belongs in the cleaning.

On clothing from other countries you sometimes see dots instead of a number in the vat. The same applies here: more points allow for warmer water. If you are unsure about the level, wash in cool water.

By the way, the fact that it only says 30 on a cotton shirt, even though cotton can tolerate hot water, is not a mistake. The label applies to the entire item with color, print, sewing thread, lining and applications. We explain in [Sort laundry correctly](/ratgeber/waesche-sortieren/) which temperature you should choose within the permitted limit and how you sort laundry accordingly.

## Bleach: the triangle

The triangle tells you whether and which bleaching agents can be used. This not only applies to bottled bleach, but also stain removers and detergents containing bleach.

- **Empty triangle:** Any bleach is permitted, including chlorine.
- **Triangle with two slanted lines:** only oxygen bleach, no chlorine bleach. Oxygen-based stain removers and heavy-duty detergents with bleach are fine.
- **Crossed out triangle:** no bleach. Then avoid stain removers with oxygen bleach and use a color or mild detergent, as many powdered heavy-duty detergents contain bleach.

On older labels you will sometimes find a triangle with the letters “Cl”. It means chlorine bleach is allowed, crossed out means it is banned. In a normal household, you hardly need chlorine bleach for textiles anyway. It attacks many fibers and agents containing chlorine must never be mixed with acids or cleaners containing ammonia. Find out more on our page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## Drying: the square

The square represents drying. A circle in a square means the tumble dryer, lines in a square mean air drying.

### In the tumble dryer

- **Circle in a square with two dots:** Dry at normal temperature.
- **Circle in a square with a dot:** only at reduced temperature, i.e. on the gentle program.
- **Crossed out:** do not tumble dry. This is not a recommendation, but a warning: wool can become felted, synthetic fibers can deform, cotton can shrink significantly.

A crossed out dryer only prohibits the dryer, not air drying.

### In the air

- **A vertical line:** dry on a line or hanger.
- **Two vertical lines:** Hang up dripping wet, i.e. without spinning. Typical for items that would otherwise crease heavily.
- **A horizontal line:** Dry lying flat, for example on a towel or a flat drying rack. Important for knits and wool, which stretch on the line due to their own weight.
- **Two horizontal lines:** Dry lying down while dripping wet.
- **Slanted mark in the top left corner:** Dry in the shade as the color may fade in the sun.

Labels from other countries sometimes show different variations, such as a bow at the top of a square for line drying. The basic idea remains the same. You can find practical tips for lines, clothes racks and dryers in the guide [Drying laundry] (/ratgeber/waesche-trocknen/).

## Ironing: the iron

In an iron, dots indicate the highest sole temperature. Most irons have the same dots on the temperature control, so you can transfer the setting directly.

| symbol | Maximum temperature, approximately | Typical materials | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Iron with a point | up to around 110 °C | Polyacrylic, polyamide, acetate, blends with elastane | Steam can permanently damage sensitive fibers |
| Two point iron | up to around 150 °C | Polyester, wool, silk, viscose | Wool and silk are best worn inside out or with a cloth in between |
| Three point iron | up to around 200 °C | Cotton, linen | It is best ironed slightly damp |
| Crossed out iron | do not iron | such as parts with coatings, prints or sequins | It's better to leave the creases hanging out |
| Iron with crossed out lines underneath | iron without steam | sensitive fibers and surfaces | Turn off the steam boost |

With mixed fabrics, you choose the most sensitive fiber, so you iron a shirt made of cotton and polyester with two points. It is best to iron dark and shiny fabrics from the left side to avoid shiny spots. How to iron shirts in a sensible order and which parts don't need ironing can be found in our guide [Ironing made easy](/ratgeber/buegeln/).

## Professional care: the circle

The circle is not aimed at you, but at the dry cleaners. He tells the dry cleaner which process they can use.

- **Circle with P:** dry cleaning with usual solvents permitted.
- **Circle with F:** dry cleaning only with certain, gentler solvents.
- **Circle with W:** professional wet cleaning permitted, a particularly gentle cleaning process using water.
- **Line under the circle:** gentle treatment, **two lines:** very gentle treatment.
- **Crossed out circle:** no dry cleaning. A crossed-out W means: no professional wet cleaning.

On older labels there is sometimes still a circle with an A. There is only one thing that is important for you at home: a circle does not mean that you cannot wash the item. The vat alone decides that. If it says a temperature, you can wash at home, even if there is a circle with letters next to it. Once the tub has been crossed out, take the part to the dry cleaner and show the label there.

## All symbols at a glance: What you have to do

This table translates the characters into handles. It is suitable for printing and hanging next to the washing machine.

| You see | You do |
|---|---|
| Tub with number, without line | Cotton or colored laundry up to the specified temperature |
| Vat with a dash | Program for easy care, drum not fully loaded |
| Two-line vat | Delicate or wool program, spin gently |
| Hand in the tub | by hand, lukewarm, pressing instead of rubbing |
| Crossed out vat | do not wash, take to the dry cleaners |
| Empty triangle | any bleaching allowed |
| Triangle with two lines | only oxygen bleach, no chlorine |
| Crossed out triangle | Do not use stain removers, color detergents or mild detergents |
| Circle in a square, one or two dots | Dryer with reduced or normal temperature |
| Crossed out dryer | air dry |
| Horizontal line in a square | dry lying flat |
| Iron with points | Set the slider to the same number of points |
| Circle with letter | Information for cleaning, does not change washing at home |

## If the label is missing or cut off

Many people cut out labels because they scratch. That's understandable, but it makes care a guessing game. This is how you do it:

1. **Look for more labels.** Often several pieces of paper hang on top of each other in the side seam. The fiber information is sometimes on a different sign than the symbols.
2. **Future photos.** For each new item of clothing, take a photo of the label before cutting it off and put the images in their own album. Cut close to the seam instead of tearing out the label, otherwise holes will easily appear.
3. **Deduce from the fiber.** If you know the material, choose the care according to the most sensitive fiber: wool and silk in the wool or hand wash program, viscose gently, robust cotton normally.
4. **If in doubt, the gentlest care.** Cold or maximum 30 °C in the delicate or hand wash program, no bleach, no tumble dryer, dry flat or on the hanger, iron with a point and test on a hidden area beforehand.
5. **Valuable items should be cleaned.** For coats, suits, silk dresses or favorite items without a label, dry cleaning is the safer route. Say what you know about the material there.

## Common mistakes

- **Read the number as a recommendation:** It is an upper limit. Lightly soiled clothing can often be cleaned in cooler temperatures.
- **Overlook lines:** A line under the tub is not decoration. The normal program spins too vigorously and easy-care items crease or warp.
- ** Heavy-duty detergent if bleaching is prohibited:** The bleach in the powder bleaches colors over time.
- **Despite the ban on putting it in the dryer:** Shrunken wool is practically impossible to save.
- **Stain remover without looking at the label:** Before treating a stain, check the bleach and wash symbol. You can find the basic rules in [Remove stains](/ratgeber/flecken-entfernen-grundregeln/).

## Frequently asked questions

### What do text instructions like “wash separately” or “wash before wearing” mean?

Such additions complement the symbols. “Wash separately” or “wash with similar colors” indicates that color may bleed, especially in the first few washes. “Wash inside out” protects prints and surfaces. Take these clues as seriously as the signs.

### Can I wash an item with 40 in the tub at 60 if it is very dirty?

No. Warmer than specified and you risk shrinkage, discoloration or damage to the print and lining. It is better to treat strong stains first and then wash at the permitted temperature.

## Briefly summarized

Care symbols always follow the same order: washing, bleaching, drying, ironing, professional care. A cross prohibits, dots show the temperature, lines require more care, and every indication is an upper limit. The circle is just an indication for cleaning; the tub decides whether to wash at home. If the label is missing, go for the most sensitive fiber or choose the gentlest care.
