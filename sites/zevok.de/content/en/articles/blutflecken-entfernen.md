A cut on your finger while chopping vegetables, a bruised knee, a nosebleed at night or a period that comes earlier than expected: blood stains end up on clothing, bed linen and mattresses more often than you think. The good news is that fresh blood can usually be completely removed. The bad news: A single mistake in the first minute, namely hot water, can permanently burn the stain into the fiber.

In this guide you will find out why blood is always treated cold, how you can remove fresh and dried stains from various materials and which remedies can help with stubborn cases without damaging the fabric.

## Why blood only tolerates cold water

Blood contains a lot of protein. Egg whites coagulate when heated, similar to how an egg solidifies in a pan. If you treat a blood stain with hot water, in the dryer or with an iron, the coagulated protein bonds firmly to the fiber. After that, the stain is often no longer removable.

Therefore, the following applies to blood without exception: **Treat with cold first and only when the stain has completely disappeared can heat be applied to the fabric.** More about the different types of stains and their treatment can be found in the guide [Removing stains: the basic rules] (/ratgeber/flecken-entfernen-grundregeln/).

## Fresh blood stains on clothing and bed linen

1. **Act immediately.** The fresher the stain, the easier it will come out.
2. **Rinse from the reverse side.** Hold the fabric, stain side down, under cold running water. This way you flush the blood out of the fiber instead of pushing it deeper in.
3. **Blot, do not rub.** Press a clean, white cloth onto the stain and change locations as soon as it changes color. Rubbing spreads the stain and roughens the fibers.
4. **Follow up with soap.** Carefully work some gall soap or a few drops of liquid detergent into the stain with cold water and let it work for a few minutes. Then rinse again with cold water.
5. **Check.** Hold the fabric up to the light. If a brownish shadow is still visible, repeat the last step or soak the fabric.
6. **Wash.** Only when no stain is visible can you wash the item normally according to the care label. Let it air dry and check again before putting it in the dryer.

**Tip:** Cold salt water can help loosen fresh blood. A teaspoon of salt in a glass of cold water is enough. Then rinse thoroughly with clean water.

## Dried blood stains

Old stains require patience, but can often be saved as long as they have not been treated with heat.

1. **Soak.** Place the item in cold water with a little detergent for several hours or overnight. Heavy-duty detergents usually contain enzymes that break down protein. This makes them particularly effective on blood.
2. **Pre-treat.** After soaking, apply gall soap or an enzyme-containing stain remover and allow to take effect.
3. **Rinse and check.** Repeat the process until the stain is gone.
4. **Wash** as described.

**For white, robust fabrics** such as cotton or linen, if there are residual shadows, you can use oxygen bleach, also known as stain remover, according to the instructions on the package. Note the bleach symbol on the care label.

**Hydrogen peroxide** in a three percent solution from the pharmacy can help on white fabrics. It foams when it comes into contact with blood. Be sure to test it on a hidden area as it can fade colors. Then rinse thoroughly with water. Do not use it on wool, silk or colored fabrics, and never mix it with other cleaners.

## Wool and silk: be careful with enzymes

Wool and silk are themselves made of protein. Enzymes that break down blood therefore also attack these fibers. The following applies to wool and silk:

- Use only cold water and gently rinse the stain.
- No heavy-duty detergents and no enzyme-based stain removers.
- Carefully work in a mild wool detergent or a drop of mild detergent with cold water.
- Do not rub, do not wring, let dry flat.
- For valuable items or large stains, it is better to hire a textile cleaner and tell them about the stain.

The guide [washing wool, cashmere and silk] (/ratgeber/wolle-und-seide-waschen/) explains how you generally wash wool and silk.

## Blood on the mattress

You can't put a mattress in the washing machine, and too much water shouldn't get into the core, otherwise there's a risk of mildew and mold. Therefore, work with as little moisture as possible.

1. **Blot up fresh blood** with a cold, damp cloth without rubbing. Always work from the outside in so that the stain doesn't get bigger.
2. **Pour cold water with a little detergent** or gall soap on a cloth, not directly on the mattress, and carefully dab the stain.
3. **Dab with clear water** so that no soap residue remains.
4. **Pat dry** with a clean towel and press firmly.
5. **Allow to dry well:** Open the window, set up the mattress or use a fan.

**For dried-on stains**, a paste made from cornstarch and cold water can help: brush on, let dry and then vacuum. For white mattress covers, you can carefully dab hydrogen peroxide on a concealed area after testing.

**Prevention:** A washable mattress protector protects the mattress from stains and can be easily washed at 60 degrees. Many mattress covers also have a zipper and can be washed according to the label.

## Blood on carpet and upholstery

1. **Absorb excess blood** with kitchen paper, just dab.
2. **Pour cold water with a drop of detergent** onto a white cloth and dab the stain from the outside in.
3. **Dab with clear cold water.**
4. **Pat dry** and allow the area to dry thoroughly.
5. For stubborn stains, use an enzyme-containing carpet or upholstery cleaner according to the instructions, testing on a hidden area first.

## Jeans, sportswear and functional fabrics

**Jeans** tolerate the same treatment as other cotton fabrics: rinse in cold water, pre-treat with gall soap, soak. Because denim is often not completely colorfast, you should avoid hydrogen peroxide and stain removers or only use them after testing on the inside of the waistband. Wash jeans inside out to protect the color.

**Sports and functional clothing** made of polyester or elastane absorbs blood less deeply than cotton, but can often only tolerate low temperatures. After training, immediately rinse stains in cold water, treat them with a little mild detergent and wash the clothing according to the care label. Fabric softener is taboo for functional fabrics because it closes the pores.

**Bed linen made of satin or silk** should be treated like silk: cold, without enzymes, with a lot of patience and, if necessary, by dry cleaning.

## Leather and delicate materials

You should only carefully dab blood on leather, suede or silk carpets with a damp cloth and ask a specialist afterwards. Many home remedies leave leather with marks or dry it out.

## Hygiene when handling someone else's blood

When cleaning blood from other people, wear disposable gloves. Wash your hands thoroughly afterwards. You can wash heavily soiled textiles at 60 degrees as soon as the stain has been pre-treated with cold water, if the material allows it.

## What you shouldn't do

| Error | Episode |
|---|---|
| Hot water | Protein coagulates and the stain becomes permanent |
| Dryer or iron before complete removal | Heat fixes the stain |
| Vigorous rubbing | The stain becomes larger and the fiber becomes rougher |
| Chlorine bleach on colored fabrics | light spots, fabric is damaged |
| Enzymes on wool and silk | Fibers are attacked |
| Mix different remedies | dangerous reactions possible |

Which means should never come together can be found on the page [Safety when cleaning](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Rinse with hot water:** Always cold, even if it feels wrong.
- **Press the stain into the fabric:** Rinse and blot from the reverse side.
- **Too early in the dryer:** First check whether the stain is really gone.
- **Too much water on the mattress:** Little moisture and thorough drying prevent mold.
- **Hydrogen peroxide without test:** It can fade colors.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does spit help against blood stains?

Saliva contains enzymes that can break down protein, and if you have a tiny fresh spot on your finger or shirt collar, this can actually help. For larger stains, cold water with soap is a more reliable choice.

### Will a blood stain still come out after washing?

If it was washed at a low temperature and not tumble dried, often yes. Soak the item in cold water with enzyme detergent and treat the stain again. If it was washed or dried on hot water, the chances are lower.

### Does vinegar or cola help against blood stains?

Both are often recommended in guides on the Internet, but have no real advantage over cold water with soap. Cola contains sugar and dyes that can stain themselves, and vinegar can change delicate colors. Stick with cold water, gall soap, and enzyme-based detergent. More about this in the guide [Home Remedy Myths Checked](/ratgeber/hausmittel-mythen/).

### How do I remove period blood from underwear?

Just like other blood stains: rinse immediately with cold water, treat with gall soap or detergent and then wash. For cotton underwear, you can use oxygen bleach for residual shadows if the label allows it. You can find further instructions in the [Remove stains](/themen/flecken-entfernen/) section.

## Briefly summarized

Blood is a protein stain and is only treated cold, otherwise the protein will coagulate and the stain will remain. Rinse fresh stains from the back with cold water, dab instead of rubbing and work in gall soap or detergent. Soak old stains in cold water with enzyme-based detergent. For white cotton, oxygen bleach or, after a test, hydrogen peroxide can help. Treat wool and silk only cold and without enzymes, moisten mattresses only slightly and dry well. Only put it in the dryer when the stain has completely disappeared.
