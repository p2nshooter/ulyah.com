A favorite sweater that is only a child's size after one wash. A silk scarf that is dull and stained after washing. A cashmere jacket full of little knots. Natural fibers like wool, cashmere and silk are some of the most beautiful and durable materials available, but they are less forgiving than cotton or polyester. If you know their characteristics, you can still easily care for them at home and rarely have to take them to the cleaners.

In this guide you will find out why wool becomes felted and how to prevent it, when airing is enough, how hand washing and the wool program work, how to wash silk, dry sweaters correctly, remove knots and keep clothes moths away.

## Why these fibers are special

Wool, cashmere, alpaca, angora and silk are **protein fibers**. They are made of similar building blocks to our hair. This results in three important rules:

- **No protein-splitting enzymes.** Many heavy-duty detergents contain enzymes against stains such as blood and egg. These enzymes also attack wool and silk. Therefore, use a wool detergent without these enzymes.
- **No aggressive agents.** Strongly alkaline cleaners, chlorine bleach and oxygen bleach damage the fibers.
- **No temperature shocks and no friction.** Wool becomes felted when warm water, temperature changes and movement come together.

## Why wool mats

Wool fibers are covered by tiny scales. These scales open in warm water. If the wool is then rubbed, kneaded or moved violently, the scales get caught together and contract. The tissue becomes dense, firm and smaller. This process cannot be reversed.

Therefore, the following applies to wool: **same temperature when washing and rinsing, little movement, no rubbing, no wringing.**

## Ventilate instead of washing

Wool is naturally dirt-repellent and absorbs odors less than many other fibers. A sweater that has only been worn briefly and has no stains does not need to be washed. Hang it in the fresh air overnight, preferably on a wide hanger or lying down in a shady, airy place. That's often enough to freshen it up.

Only wash wool clothing if it is visibly dirty or smells despite being aired. This protects the fibers and saves time.

## Hand wash: step by step

1. **Read care label.** A hand in a wash bowl indicates hand washing, a circle with a letter indicates professional cleaning. More in the guide [Understanding care symbols] (/ratgeber/pflegesymbole/).
2. **Pour lukewarm water** into a sink or bowl, about 20 to 30 degrees. It should barely feel warm on your hand.
3. Dissolve **wool detergent** in the water according to the instructions on the package before putting the clothes in.
4. **Insert clothing** and carefully submerge. Press lightly, do not rub or knead. Let sit for a few minutes.
5. **Soiled areas** such as cuffs and collars, gently press between your fingers.
6. **Rinse** in water at the same temperature. Drain water and add fresh water until no more foam is visible.
7. **Squeeze out water,** do not wring. Press the piece against the edge of the pool.
8. **Roll into a towel:** Lay the clothing flat on a large, dry towel, roll up and press firmly. The towel absorbs a lot of water.
9. **Dry lying flat** as described below.

## In the washing machine

Many modern wool items can be machine washed if the label allows it, often with the addition that the wool is treated to be machine washable.

- Select **wool program or hand wash program**. These programs only move the drum gently.
- **Low temperature,** usually cold or 30 degrees.
- **Low spin speed** so that the wool is not compressed.
- Use **wool detergent** and dose it correctly.
- **Small load:** Only fill the drum one third full.
- **Turn inside out** and place sensitive items in a laundry net.

If you are unsure whether an item can be machine washed, it is better to wash it by hand.

## Cashmere

Cashmere is finer and softer than normal sheep's wool and therefore more sensitive. Treat it like wool, only more gently: hand wash or a very gentle wool program, expressly only according to the label.

**Remove tangles:** Cashmere and fine wool form small tangles in areas with a lot of friction, such as under the arms and on the sleeves. This is not a quality defect, but normal for soft fibers. Remove them with a cashmere comb or lint razor, carefully and in one direction. After a few washes, fewer pills usually form.

## Silk

Silk is light, shiny and amazingly strong, but sensitive to heat, friction, light and aggressive agents.

- **Please note the label.** Some silk items may only be professionally cleaned. This is especially true for linings, evening dresses and silk with embossing.
- **Hand wash** in cold to lukewarm water using a wool and silk detergent or a mild, enzyme-free detergent.
- **Do not soak,** but wash and rinse quickly.
- **Don't wring,** but roll it in a towel.
- **Hang dry** on a padded hanger, in the shade. Unlike wool, silk does not wear out when hanging.
- **Iron** on low to medium setting, inside out, without water from the spray bottle as water droplets can leave stains.

**Stains on silk** are difficult. Perfume and deodorant can leave marks, so apply them before putting on silk and let them absorb. For larger stains, it is better to hire a textile cleaner.

## Dry properly

- **Dry lying flat:** Place wool and cashmere on a dry towel or drying rack. Return the item to its original shape and gently pull the sleeves and cuffs into place.
- **Do not hang:** Wet wool is heavy and will stretch under its weight.
- **No heat, no sun:** Heat and direct sunlight cause the fibers to shrink and fade.
- **Turn:** After a few hours, turn the item over and replace the towel if it gets soaked.
- **No dryer,** unless the label specifically allows it.

More about drying in the guide [Drying laundry] (/ratgeber/waesche-trocknen/).

## Hats, scarves, socks and blankets

**Wool hats and scarves have a lot of contact with the skin and need to be washed more often than sweaters. Hand wash and dry hats over an inverted bowl to keep their shape. Lay the scarves flat and smooth out the fringes.

**Wool socks** can often be put into the machine in the wool program. Turn them inside out to reduce pilling and dry flat. Socks with a high wool content quickly become felted if washed too warm.

**Wool blankets and throws** are large and become very heavy when wet. Air them regularly and wash them only when necessary, in a large tub or in a machine with a sufficiently large drum and wool program. Two drying racks next to each other on which the blanket lies flat are suitable for drying.

## Save shrunken wool

If a sweater is slightly shrunk, you can try to save it. Soak it in lukewarm water with a little hair conditioner to soften the fibers. After half an hour, carefully squeeze out the water, roll it in a towel and then gently pull it into the desired shape while lying down. Let it dry like this and reapply every now and then. However, heavily matted wool cannot be saved this way.

## Prevent clothes moths

Clothes moths lay their eggs in wool, cashmere and silk. The larvae eat holes in the fibers, especially in dirty areas with sweat and skin flakes.

- **Store cleanly only.** Wash or air woolen items before they go into the closet for the summer.
- **Pack airtight,** for example in lockable garment bags or boxes.
- **Cedarwood and Lavender** can deter moths but do not kill larvae. Refresh the scent regularly.
- **Check regularly:** Occasionally take out woolen items, shake them out and air them.
- **If infested:** Place the affected parts in a bag in the freezer for several days, then wash and vacuum and wipe the cupboard thoroughly. Pheromone traps help detect an infestation.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Wash with heavy-duty detergent:** Enzymes and bleach attack the fibers.
- **Wash warm and rinse cold:** The change in temperature causes wool to felt.
- **Wring and rub:** Fibers become matted or broken.
- **Hang up the wool:** It wears out.
- **Dry on the heater:** The fibers will shrink.
- **Store dirty woolen clothes:** Moths find them particularly attractive.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should you wash wool sweaters?

Rarely. Ventilation is often enough. Wash wool if it is visibly dirty or smells despite airing. More common with sweaters that are worn directly against the skin than with cardigans over a shirt.

### Can merino wool be put in the machine?

Many Merino items, especially functional underwear, are machine washable. Stick to the label, use the wool program with wool detergent and avoid fabric softener.

### Why does my wool sweater scratch after washing?

Often it is due to detergent residue or water that is too warm, which has roughened the surface of the fibers. Rinse thoroughly in water of the same temperature and use a wool detergent with moisturizing ingredients.

### Can I wash wool with shampoo?

In an emergency, yes, a mild shampoo without silicones is gentler than a heavy-duty detergent. However, a proper wool detergent is better suited to the fiber. Which detergents are suitable for what can be found in the guide [How to dose detergent correctly] (/ratgeber/waschmittel-dosieren/). You can find further instructions in the [Laundry & Care](/themen/waesche-und-pflege/) section.

## Briefly summarized

Wool, cashmere and silk are protein fibers and require a detergent without enzymes. Air wool often and wash it rarely. Wash by hand or on the wool program at a low, consistent temperature, without rubbing or wringing, and squeeze out the water in a towel. Dry wool lying in the shade, silk hanging on a padded hanger. Remove knots with a cashmere comb, and only store wool in a clean and airtight place to protect it from moths.
