The lasagne has overcooked, the roast has splattered violently, and the next time it is preheated the whole kitchen smells burnt. At the latest when it smokes while heating up, it is clear: the oven needs to be cleaned. Many people then reach for steel wool or the strongest spray from the supermarket and only afterwards notice that the enamel has scratches, the door seal has become brittle or the rough back wall no longer works properly after cleaning.

To prevent this from happening, good oven cleaning doesn't start with scrubbing, but with a question: What kind of oven do you have? Then you'll find out here how to prepare it, when a baking soda paste is enough and when an oven cleaner is necessary, how to clear the glass door and how to get trays and racks clean without dirtying half the kitchen.

## First: What does your oven allow?

Ovens are equipped differently inside, and what works for one will ruin the other. Therefore, first take a look at the operating instructions. If it is no longer there, you can usually find the model name on the nameplate on the frame, which is visible when the door is open. You can use this to look up the instructions from the manufacturer.

| Equipment | This is how you recognize them | Which is usually allowed | Which is usually forbidden |
|---|---|---|---|
| Enamel | smooth, shiny interior walls, often dark | Rinse water, baking soda paste, oven cleaner according to instructions, soft sponges | Steel wool, scouring powder, knife and metal spatula |
| Pyrolysis | own cleaning program in which the oven becomes very hot and burns residues into ash | Wipe out the ash with a damp cloth after the program and care for the door and seal according to the instructions | Leave accessories in the oven during the program unless they are specifically designed for this purpose; Oven spray is often not provided |
| Catalytic coating | rough, matt, slightly porous surfaces, often on the back wall, sides or ceiling | do nothing: The coating breaks down fat splashes when baking at high temperatures | Sponge, brush, baking soda paste and oven cleaner on these surfaces |
| Steam or water program | Cleaning program for which water is added to the cooking chamber | Wipe out any softened residue after the program | more water than specified, detergent in the water if the instructions do not allow it |

Many ovens combine several variants, such as an enamelled base with a catalytic back wall. Then you treat each area according to its own rules. If in doubt, it's better to start gently and repeat rather than going too hard once.

## Preparation: cool down, clear out, remove any coarse material

1. **Turn off and allow to cool.** The oven must be completely cold unless the cleaner instructions say otherwise. On hot surfaces, water evaporates immediately, cleaners dry out, and you burn yourself easily.
2. **Take out the accessories.** The trays, racks and drip pan come out, as do the side racks if they can be removed. You only remove telescopic extensions if the instructions describe it.
3. **Remove coarse items.** Remove crumbs and loose charred residue with a hand brush, a handheld vacuum cleaner or a plastic spatula. The less loose dirt there is in the oven, the better everything else works.
4. **Protect the environment.** Place an old towel or newspaper on the floor in front of the oven. Put on household gloves.
5. **Clear what needs to stay dry.** Heating elements, the fan behind the back wall, the lamp and the control panel must not get wet. You only treat the door seal with warm water and a soft cloth, without any cleaner and without pulling on it. Work with a well-wrung cloth, never dripping wet.

On some devices, the upper heating element can be folded down to clean the ceiling. Whether this is intended for your oven is stated in the instructions.

## Baking soda paste or oven cleaner?

For light to medium dirt on enamel, a paste made from baking soda and water is often sufficient. With thick crusts that have been burned in for years, it reaches its limits and an oven cleaner may be necessary. The difference lies primarily in the strength and the risk:

| | Baking soda paste | Oven cleaner |
|---|---|---|
| Effect | Mild, slowly softens fat and burnt food | Strongly alkaline, also dissolves thick crusts |
| exposure time | several hours or overnight | according to information on the packaging |
| Protection | Gloves | Gloves, safety glasses, open windows, keep children and pets away |
| Suitable for | Enamel, glass pane inside, metal sheets | Enamel if the oven manufacturer allows it |
| Not on | catalytic surfaces, heating elements, blower, seal | catalytic surfaces, heating elements, blower, seal, lamp, aluminum, external front and control panel |

### This is how you use the baking soda paste

1. Mix baking soda with a little water to form a spreadable paste, about as thick as toothpaste.
2. Lightly moisten the dirty areas with water and spread the paste over them with a sponge or brush. Skip heating rods, blower opening, gasket and rough catalytic surfaces.
3. Let the paste take effect for several hours, preferably overnight with the door closed. If it dries, spray some water over it.
4. Loosen the softened residue with a damp sponge or plastic spatula. Work in small strokes and rinse the sponge often.
5. Wipe several times with clean water until there are no more white streaks. Allow the oven to dry with the door open.

Stubborn areas often need a second pass. Make sure to use pure baking soda. We explain why baking powder is less suitable for this and what makes soda different in the guide [Baking soda, soda and baking powder] (/ratgeber/natron-soda-backpulver/).

### How to use oven cleaner safely

Oven cleaners are highly alkaline and often carry the danger sign of being corrosive. They can cause serious damage to the skin and eyes. Read the label completely before you begin and note these points:

- **Protect:** Wear gloves, safety glasses and long sleeves, open the kitchen window, keep children and pets out of the kitchen.
- **Apply in a targeted manner:** Gel or foam can be applied more precisely than a spray mist. Hold the nozzle close to the surface so you don't inhale anything, or apply the gel with a brush.
- **Avoid components at risk:** no splashes on the seal, heating elements, blower, lamp and catalytic surfaces, nothing on the external front or the control panel.
- **Observe the exposure time:** Longer does not work better, but in case of doubt it will attack the surface.
- **Wipe thoroughly:** Remove residue several times with clear water and a fresh cloth. Whatever is left in the oven smells and smokes the next time it is baked.
- **Never mix:** Do not use a second product in the same session without first wiping everything thoroughly with water.

Some manufacturers recommend heating up the empty oven briefly after cleaning so that it dries and any remaining odors disappear. Follow the oven and cleaner instructions and ventilate. What you should do if something gets into your eyes or on your skin is explained on our page [Safety when brushing](/sicherheit-beim-putzen/).

## The glass door inside and outside

The inner pane gets the most grease splashes. Treat it like the enamel with baking soda paste and let it take effect. You can then carefully remove burnt-in spots on the wet pane with a glass scraper, if the manufacturer allows this for the oven pane. Keep the blade very flat, like on a glass ceramic hob. Steel wool, scouring powder and rough sponge sides scratch the glass, and deep scratches can weaken the glass.

Dirt between the panes occurs when steam and grease enter the door through ventilation slots. You can only get to it if you remove the door or the windows. Do this only according to the instructions for your appliance: oven doors are heavy, the hinges are under spring tension, and the panes must return in the correct order and orientation. Before expanding, take photos of each step. If this is not provided for in the instructions or if you are unsure, leave the discs in.

Outside, warm water with a little detergent is usually sufficient. Then polish with a dry cloth. For stainless steel fronts, wipe in the direction of the cut. Do not spray anything directly onto the control panel or knob, but moisten the cloth so that no liquid runs into the electronics.

## Baking trays, racks and racks

**Enameled baking trays and drip pan:** Soak them in hot water with a little washing-up liquid, preferably for a few hours. For burnt-in items, sprinkle baking soda on the wet sheet metal, let it take effect and then clean with a soft sponge or plastic spatula.

**Coated sheets with a non-stick layer:** Steel wool, scouring powder and metal spatulas are taboo here because they scratch the coating. Soaking and a soft sponge are usually enough.

**Sheets and racks:** They are bulky, so you will need a large container, such as a plastic tub. If you're using the bathtub or shower base, place an old towel underneath to prevent metal edges from scratching the surface. Soak the parts in hot soapy water and then brush them with a dishwashing brush. They only go into the dishwasher if the instructions allow it and they fit in without blocking the spray arms.

**Telescopic extensions:** These slides often contain a lubricant. According to many manufacturers' instructions, they should neither be soaked nor put in the dishwasher, but should only be wiped with a damp cloth. The instructions for your device decide.

Allow all parts to dry completely before replacing them. Our guide [Cleaning ceramic hobs, induction hobs and gas stoves] (/ratgeber/ceranfeld-reinigen/) explains how you clean the hob above.

## Prevention: less burnt food

- **Remove spills promptly.** Wipe away fresh spills once the oven has cooled. Each time they are heated up, they burn in more firmly.
- **Cook covered.** A roasting pan with a lid or a casserole dish with a lid will keep any splashes of fat in the container.
- **Place a tray underneath.** For juicy fruit cakes and roasts, a tray on the rail underneath catches drips. Use baking paper on the tray, not on the oven floor.
- **No aluminum foil on the bottom of the oven.** Many manufacturers advise against this because heat can build up underneath and damage the enamel.
- **Choose suitable shapes.** A casserole dish that is too full will boil over. Leave some space to the edge.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Steel wool and scouring powder on enamel or glass:** They leave scratches in which even more will settle in the future. Use soft sponges and plastic spatulas.
- **Cleaner on catalytic surfaces:** The coating loses its effectiveness. Leave her alone.
- **Wet heating elements:** Clean around them and keep your distance with paste and spray.
- **Pyrolysis with accessories in the oven:** Trays and racks can discolor or warp if they are not specifically designed for this purpose.
- **Residue not wiped off:** They will smell and smoke the next time they are baked. It is better to wipe again with clear water.
- **Several products one after the other:** First wipe thoroughly with water before putting another product in the oven.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I clean the oven?

That depends on how much you bake and fry. If it smokes or smells when heating up, a thorough cleaning is necessary. If you remove splashes regularly after they have cooled down, you will need them much less often.

### Does vinegar help against burnt food?

Barely. Vinegar is an acid and works against limescale, not against grease and burnt-on substances. Mild alkalis such as baking soda are the better choice in the oven. Where vinegar really helps is in [Vinegar in the Household](/ratgeber/essig-im-haushalt/).

## Briefly summarized

First, clarify whether your oven has enamel, pyrolysis or catalytic surfaces and read the instructions. Allow it to cool, remove the accessories and keep the heating elements, fan and seal dry. For most dirt, a baking soda paste overnight is sufficient. Only use oven cleaner on permitted surfaces, with gloves, safety glasses and an open window. You clean the glass door with paste and a flat glass scraper; it can only be removed according to the instructions. You can soak trays and racks, and with the lid and drip tray, the oven stays clean for longer.
