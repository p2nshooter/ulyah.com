Whether a gear is easy or hard depends on the relationship between the front chainring and rear sprocket and how big the wheel is. The most obvious quantity for this is the **unfolding**: the distance that the wheel covers with one revolution of the crank. The speed results from this and the cadence.

## The formulas

**Unfolding (m) = wheel circumference (m) × chainring teeth ÷ sprocket teeth**

**Speed (km/h) = deployment × crank revolutions per minute × 60 ÷ 1,000**

The number of teeth is usually stamped on the chainring and sprocket. The wheel circumference is determined by the tire size that is written on the tire sidewall, for example 37-622 for a 28-inch tire with a width of 37 millimeters.

## An example

A trekking bike with a 38 chainring, 16 sprocket and 28 inch tires (37-622, circumference around 2,155 mm) covers 5.12 meters per crank revolution. At 70 revolutions per minute that's about 21.5 km/h. The calculator opens with these values.

| Cadence | 38 × 16, 28 inches |
|---|---|
| 60 rpm | 18.4 km/h |
| 70 rpm | 21.5 km/h |
| 80 rpm | 24.6 km/h |
| 90 rpm | 27.6 km/h |

## What you need the calculator for

- **Check uphill:** You can also climb steep ramps with less than 2 meters of deployment.
- **Compare gears:** The range is the ratio between the heaviest and lightest gears. Calculate both and divide the larger value by the smaller one.
- **Change the sprocket or chainring:** See beforehand how a larger sprocket affects hill climbing and speed.
- **Children's bike and folding bike:** Small balance bikes need larger chainrings for the same development.

60 to 80 crank revolutions per minute protect the knees and drive; In Pedelecs, the mid-engine works most efficiently in this area. The difference between derailleur gears and hub gears is explained in the guide [Derailleur gears or hub gears](/ratgeber/kettenschaltung-oder-nabenschaltung/), and how to adjust the gears is explained in the guide [Setting the derailleur gears](/ratgeber/kettenschaltung-einstellen/).
