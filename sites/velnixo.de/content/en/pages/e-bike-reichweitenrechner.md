Advertising claims such as “up to 150 kilometers” are not wrong, but only apply under ideal conditions. How far a Pedelec can really go depends primarily on how much energy it needs per kilometer, and this changes with level, terrain, weight, speed and temperature. The computer makes these connections visible.

## The bill

**Range = usable battery energy (Wh) ÷ consumption (Wh per kilometer)**

The capacity is written on the battery in watt hours. Common batteries have 400 to 750 watt hours. The computer deducts a 10% reserve so that you don't arrive with an empty battery, and a part of the capacity when it's cold because batteries then release less energy.

## Typical consumption values

The values are assumed, typical sizes from our guide to [e-bike range] (/ratgeber/e-bike-reichweite-verlaengern/), no manufacturer information:

| situation | consumption | Range with 500 Wh |
|---|---|---|
| Commuting on the flat, Eco level, about 20 km/h | about 5 Wh/km | around 100 km |
| Tour in the low mountain range, on the mountain level sport | about 11 Wh/km | around 45 km |
| Cargo bike with children, winter, city traffic | 15 Wh/km and more | 25 to 30 km |

The calculator opens with the values from the first example: 500 watt hours, Eco level, flat terrain and a commute distance of 15 kilometers. Around 75 watt hours are needed per route; one charge, with a reserve, is enough for six one-way trips.

## What changes the range the most

- **Support level:** the greatest leverage. One step lower on the plain, the high step only on the mountain.
- **Terrain and altitude:** A Pedelec needs a lot more going uphill; No energy is recovered going downhill.
- **Weight:** Driver, bike, luggage, child or trailer. Cargo bikes in particular consume significantly more.
- **Speed ​​and Wind:** Air resistance increases greatly with speed. 20 instead of 25 km/h saves noticeably.
- **Temperature:** The battery delivers less in winter. A warm battery and a thermal cover help.
- **Getting there:** Many traffic lights cost energy. Before stopping, downshift and start in easy gear.

## Which battery size fits

| Usage | sensible battery size |
|---|---|
| short city trips up to 15 km daily | 400-500 Wh |
| Commuting 20-40 km daily, little elevation gain | 500–625 Wh |
| Tours, hills, luggage | 625–750 Wh |
| Cargo bike, mountain region, long tours | 750 Wh and more or double battery |

How to charge and store the battery so that it lasts a long time can be found in the [battery care] guide (/ratgeber/e-bike-akku-laden-lagern-pflegen/).
