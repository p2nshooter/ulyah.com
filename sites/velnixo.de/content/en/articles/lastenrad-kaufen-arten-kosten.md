Cargo bikes have become an integral part of many cities. Parents use it to take their children to daycare, tradesmen take their tools to the construction site, and for many families they replace their second car for weekly shopping. However, a cargo bike is a larger purchase than a normal bike: it costs more, takes up more space and rides differently.

This guide will help you decide. He explains the most important types, what is important when transporting children, how much a cargo bike costs, what subsidies are available and what you should pay attention to when taking a test ride.

## The three most important types

| Construction type | loading area | driving experience | Suitable for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long John (two-wheeler with front loading area) | in front of the driver, low, often with a box | sporty, agile, takes some getting used to | Children, shopping, city traffic |
| Longtail (extended tail) | behind the driver on a long luggage rack | almost like a normal bike | one or two children, bags, commuting |
| tricycle | usually at the front between two wheels | stands by itself, doesn't tip over when standing, but doesn't lean when cornering | large loads, people with balance problems, delivery services |

### Long John

On the Long John, the loading area is low between the handlebars and the front wheel. The front wheel is steered via a linkage. The low center of gravity makes the bike stable and the children sit in the driver's field of vision. After a short period of getting used to it, a Long John is very safe and maneuverable. However, it is long, which can be a problem when parking and in narrow stairwells.

### Longtail

A longtail looks like a normal bicycle with a very long rear triangle. On the extended luggage rack, children sit on cushions with grab handles and footrests, and large bags hang on the sides. Because a Longtail rides almost like a normal bike, it takes little getting used to and is narrower than a Long John.

### Tricycle

Tricycles stand by themselves, which is convenient when loading and stopping. But they behave differently in curves: they don't lean and you have to drive much slower. They tip over more easily on sloping paths and curbs. Tricycles are wider and do not fit through every cycle path lock.

## With or without engine?

A loaded cargo bike can easily weigh 50 to 100 kilograms, more with children and shopping. A cargo bike without a motor is possible on flat stretches, but it becomes strenuous on inclines and on longer journeys. Most cargo bikes are therefore sold with an electric motor.

- **Mid-engine:** Good power transmission, especially on inclines, natural driving experience. Very common on cargo bikes.
- **Hub motor:** Often cheaper, good on flat routes, less powerful on steep climbs.
- **Battery capacity:** The range decreases with load. For daily use, a large battery or the option of a second battery is worthwhile.

A cargo bike with a motor up to 25 km/h is legally considered a pedelec and therefore a bicycle: no driving license, no insurance requirement, no helmet requirement. More about this in the guide to e-bikes, Pedelecs and law.

## Transport children safely

For many families, this is the main reason for a cargo bike. Pay attention to:

- **Manufacturer approved seats and belts:** Children sit on fixed bench seats with belts, small children in a suitable child seat or baby seat if approved by the manufacturer.
- **Rain cover:** A tarpaulin with windows protects against rain, wind and cold. But it also catches wind, which makes the bike restless in crosswinds.
- **Footwell and side walls:** Children's feet must not get into the wheels or steering.
- **Frame protection on the box:** In the event of a fall, a stable box protects children better than an open loading area.
- **Weight limits:** The permissible total weight and the maximum load of the loading area are in the instructions. Add up the driver, children and luggage.

In Germany, children are allowed to travel in a specially designed cargo bike if it is designed for this purpose and the seats are securely fastened. Follow the manufacturer's information about the age, number and weight of children.

## For whom a cargo bike is worthwhile

A cargo bike is not the right choice for everyone. It is particularly worthwhile if you transport children or larger loads several times a week, live in the city or a flat area and have a ground-level parking space. If you only occasionally do a large purchase, riding a normal bike with two panniers or a bike trailer is often cheaper and more flexible. And if you commute long distances across country every day, you can usually get around faster with a light Pedelec. Renting it from an initiative or a rental provider will show you honestly after a few days of everyday life whether the cargo bike suits your life.

## Parking and theft protection

A cargo bike needs space. Before you buy, clarify:

- **Where does the bike stay overnight?** A ground-level parking space in the yard, a garage or a bike house is ideal. A cargo bike usually cannot be carried into the basement.
- **Will it fit through the door and gate?** Measure the width of the yard gate, passage and bike room.
- **Is there enough space?** Normal bicycle racks are often too narrow in front of daycare centers, supermarkets and workplaces.

Cargo bikes are expensive and sought after. Always lock the bike to a solid object, use an additional frame lock and consider insurance. A GPS tracker can help find a stolen bike. More about this in the guide to theft protection.

## How much does a cargo bike cost?

| Category | Price range |
|---|---|
| Cargo bike without engine | 1,500 to 3,000 euros |
| Longtail with engine | 3,500 to 6,000 euros |
| Long John with engine | 4,000 to 8,000 euros |
| Tricycle with motor | 4,500 to 9,000 euros |

There are also accessories such as a rain cover, child seats, lock and possibly a second battery. The ongoing costs for maintenance and wear are higher than with a normal bike because the brakes, tires and chain are put under greater strain.

### Funding, leasing and lending

- **Funding programs:** Some federal states, cities and municipalities have subsidized the purchase of cargo bikes in recent years, often for families, businesses or clubs. The programs change frequently and are sometimes exhausted quickly. Check with your city or municipality to find out what currently applies.
- **Company bike leasing:** Many employers also offer cargo bikes for leasing. Find out more in the guide to company bike leasing.
- **Free cargo bikes:** In many cities there are initiatives that lend out cargo bikes free of charge or for a donation. Ideal to try before you buy.
- **Cargo bike rental:** Commercial providers rent cargo bikes by the hour or day.

## Driving: short getting used to

A cargo bike rides differently than a normal bike, especially a Long John:

1. **Start without a load:** Practice starting, braking, cornering and tight turning circles in an empty parking lot.
2. **Increase slowly:** First shopping, then children. The bike reacts more slowly under load and the braking distance becomes longer.
3. **Take curves wide:** A long bike needs more space to turn.
4. **Avoid curbs:** Approaching lowered curbs at an angle can cause tricycles to tip over. Drive up as straight as possible.
5. **Drive with foresight:** A cargo bike is wider than a normal bike. Be careful on narrow bike paths and bollards.

## Test drive checklist

- Is the seating position correct and can the saddle be adjusted for all riders?
- How does the bike ride empty and loaded, for example with crates of drinks as a replacement for children?
- How easy is it to push and maneuver?
- Is it securely on the stand, even when the children get in?
- How easy is the rain cover to put up and take down?
- Are the brakes and engine suitable for the terrain where you live?
- Is there a workshop nearby that services the model?

You can find out more about test drives in general in the test drive guide.

## Maintenance: what a cargo bike particularly needs

A cargo bike is subjected to greater stress than a normal bike because more weight is placed on the brakes, tires, chain and steering. Therefore, plan regular maintenance:

- **Brakes:** Hydraulic disc brakes are standard on cargo bikes. Check the pads more often than on a normal bike because they wear out more quickly under load.
- **Tires:** Cargo bikes often have smaller front wheels with special tire sizes. Maintain the recommended air pressure, too little pressure makes the wheel spongy and increases wear.
- **Steering linkage:** On the Long John, the linkage should be free of play. Rattling or play in the steering belongs in the workshop.
- **Chain and drive:** With motor support and load, the chain wears faster. A chain wear gauge shows when it needs to be replaced.
- **Inspection:** A workshop should inspect the entire bike once a year, or twice if used daily. When purchasing, ask whether the dealer will service your model.

## Commercial cargo bike

The cargo bike is also interesting for companies: delivery services, tradesmen, care services and janitorial services often reach their destination in the city more quickly than with a van, find a place to stop more easily and save fuel. For commercial use, robust models with a large payload, lockable boxes and a maintenance agreement make sense. Anyone purchasing a cargo bike for their business should seek tax advice and ask about commercial funding programs.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a driving license to ride a cargo bike?**
No, as long as it is a Pedelec with support up to 25 km/h or runs without a motor. The rules for bicycles apply.

**Can I ride my cargo bike on the bike path?**
Yes. A cargo bike is a bicycle and, like any bike, can use cycle paths. With wide models you should pay particular attention to other cyclists.

## Conclusion

A cargo bike can replace the car in everyday life: for children, shopping, tools and everything that doesn't fit in a pannier. A Long John carries low and securely at the front, a Longtail rides almost like a normal bike, a tricycle stands on its own but requires caution when cornering. With a motor, even inclines and long distances are possible. Before you buy, clarify where to park it, try out the bike with a load, find out about subsidies and rental options and secure the bike well against theft. Then the cargo bike quickly becomes the most important vehicle in the family.
