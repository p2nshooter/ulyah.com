There's something liberating about a bike trip with luggage: everything you need for the next few days is on your bike, and you arrive at a new place every evening. For this to feel good, the luggage has to be right. Too much weight makes every climb torture, incorrectly distributed weight makes the bike unsettled, and a wet sleeping bag can ruin a tour.

This guide explains which bags are suitable for which trip, how to distribute the weight correctly, what you really need to take with you and how to pack so that everything stays dry and is quickly at hand.

## How much luggage is sensible?

The most important rule for cycling holidays is: less is more. You feel every kilogram uphill, when starting off and when carrying the bike up stairs. As a rough guide:

| Travel type | Luggage weight without water and provisions |
|---|---|
| Hotel tour with light luggage | 5 to 8 kg |
| Hotel tour with a change of clothes for all weather conditions | 8 to 12 kg |
| Camping tour with tent and stove | 12 to 20 kg |
| Bikepacking with minimal equipment | 6 to 10 kg |

Many cyclists pack too much on their first tour and send a package home after a few days. Test packing before your trip, where you hold each item in your hand and ask yourself whether you really need it, saves weight and nerves.

## Which bags for which trip?

### Rear panniers

Two panniers on the luggage rack are the classic solution for cycling trips. Together they usually hold 30 to 50 liters and carry the majority of the luggage. Pay attention to:

- **Waterproofness:** Coated fabric bags with roll closure are truly waterproof. Zippered bags often need a rain cover.
- **Attachment:** A good hook system holds the bag firmly on the carrier and can still be released with one hand. It should match the diameter of the luggage rack struts.
- **Heel clearance:** The pockets must sit far back so that your heels don't hit when you pedal.

### Front panniers (lowrider)

For longer trips and camping trips, two smaller bags on a lowrider rack on the fork are worthwhile. They sit low, close to the wheel axle, and distribute the weight better between the front and rear wheels. This makes the bike quieter, especially if you have a lot of luggage.

### Handlebar bag

The handlebar bag is the glove compartment of your cycling trip: your wallet, cell phone, sunscreen, snacks and map are always within easy reach. Many models can be removed with one handle and taken with you as a shoulder bag when you park the bike for a short time. Don't pack it too heavy, otherwise the bike will steer sluggish, especially with a suspension fork.

### Frame bag, saddle bag and top tube bag

These bags come from bikepacking and are attached directly to the frame, saddle and top tube. They do not require a luggage rack and are suitable for light equipment and bikes without eyelets. Find out more in the bikepacking guide.

### Backpack?

A backpack is not a good idea for longer tours. It wets your back, puts strain on your shoulders and buttocks and shifts your center of gravity upwards. A small, light backpack for a stroll through town in the evening can be folded up and stored in a pannier.

## Distribute the weight correctly

How a loaded bike rides depends less on the total weight than on where it sits.

1. **Heavy things down:** The heaviest items, such as tools, stoves, provisions and water, belong deep in the panniers, as close as possible to the wheel axle.
2. **Heavy things in the front of the bag:** With rear wheel bags, the weight should be placed more at the front, i.e. towards the seat post, not all the way back over the axle.
3. **Left and right the same:** Both sides should be about the same weight, otherwise the wheel will pull to the heavier side. A simple luggage scale helps with balancing.
4. **Front and rear:** If you have a lot of luggage, distribute around 40 percent to the front panniers and 60 percent to the rear.
5. **Slightly up:** Sleeping mat, tent or sleeping bag can lie horizontally on the luggage rack, secured with tension straps. They are voluminous but light.

## Pack waterproof and organized

- **Package bags by theme:** Clothing, electronics, toiletry bags and tools each in their own colored packing bags. This means you can find everything quickly, and even a bag that isn't completely airtight keeps the contents dry.
- **Protect the sleeping bag twice:** Stow the sleeping bag in a waterproof pack sack. A wet sleeping bag is the worst thing that can happen on a camping trip.
- **Fixed places:** Each item always has the same place, for example rain jacket in the top left pocket, tools in the bottom right. After two days you will find everything blind.
- **Daily supplies above:** What you need during the day, rain gear, provisions, water, belongs upstairs or in the handlebar bag.

## The loaded bike rides differently

A bike with 15 kilograms of luggage behaves significantly differently than without:

- **Longer braking distance:** Brake earlier and evenly. Check the brake pads before traveling.
- **Sluggish steering:** The wheel responds more slowly to steering movements and can sway slightly at high speeds, especially downhill. Both hands on the handlebars and adjust your speed.
- **Starting:** Shift into a light gear before stopping so that you can start off on a hill or at a traffic light.
- **Parking:** A loaded bike can easily tip over. A sturdy two-legged stand or placing it against a wall helps.

Before you travel, take a test ride with a fully loaded bike. This way you can notice whether bags are rattling, heels are bumping or the bike is becoming unsteady, and you can make improvements in good time.

## Prepare the bike for luggage

- **Check luggage rack:** The permissible load is usually on the rack. Many everyday carriers are designed to hold 25 kilograms, travel carriers are often designed to hold more.
- **Tires:** Wider tires with puncture protection carry luggage more comfortably and are less prone to flats. Adjust the air pressure to the higher weight within the limits of the information on the tire.
- **Spokes:** On the rear wheel, luggage and the rider share their weight on the spokes. A well-centered wheel with consistent spoke tension is important. Have it checked before a long tour.
- **Translation:** With luggage, easy passages are worth their weight in gold. If you travel in hilly terrain, you should consider a gear ratio with a very low gear.

## Luggage with the Pedelec

With a Pedelec, climbing inclines with luggage is much easier, but there are a few special features you should keep in mind:

- **Total weight at a glance:** Manufacturers specify a permissible total weight for the bike, rider and luggage, often between 130 and 160 kilograms. A Pedelec itself weighs 25 kilograms or more, so for heavy riders there is less room for luggage than you might think.
- **Battery and charger:** The charger weighs about one kilogram and belongs in the pack bag, well padded. Plan stages so that you can charge in the evening and ask for a power socket for the bike when booking.
- **Range with luggage:** More weight and headwind shorten the range. On cycling trips, expect significantly fewer kilometers per battery charge than on the home route and use a low setting on flat sections.
- **Standing and pushing:** A fully loaded Pedelec is difficult to maneuver. A pushing aid helps on steep ramps and in the station elevator.

## Checklist in the morning of each travel day

1. Bags firmly attached, fasteners closed?
2. Are the tension straps on the luggage rack tightened?
3. Checked tire pressure with your thumb?
4. Brakes briefly tested?
5. Water filled up, provisions ready for the morning?
6. Cell phone and lights charged?
7. Rain jacket in the top of your pocket?

These two minutes in the morning prevent most problems along the way, from a lost pack bag to a flat tire after the first kilometer.

## Travel light: tips for saving weight

- **Wash clothes instead of taking them with you:** Functional clothing dries overnight. Two sets are usually enough.
- **Shrink toiletry bags:** Small travel sizes and solid soap instead of bottles.
- **Use together:** Share tools, pump and cooker between two people.
- **Shopping on the way:** In Germany there are usually enough shops along the route. Provisions for one day are enough.
- **Send home:** Anything unused after three days can be sent home in a package.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I really need waterproof bags?**
For tours over several days, yes. A single day of rain is enough to soak clothes and sleeping bags in non-tight bags. If you have cheap bags, you can also pack the contents in garbage bags or packing bags.

**Can I use a pendant instead of bags?**
A unicycle or two-wheel trailer takes the load off the bike and is suitable for a lot of luggage, for example with children or camping equipment for several people. But it makes the team longer, heavier and difficult on the track. For most trips, bags are the more practical solution.

**How do I secure my luggage during breaks?**
You always take the handlebar bag with valuables with you. Panniers can't really be secured; Therefore, park the bike within sight and take the most important bags with you on longer sightseeing or leave them in the accommodation.

## Conclusion

A good cycling trip begins with light, well-distributed luggage. Use waterproof panniers on the luggage rack, if you have a lot of luggage, also use lowriders on the front, and keep the handlebar bag light. Heavy things should be placed downwards and close to the axle; both sides should be equally heavy. Pack according to theme in colored stuff sacks, double protect your sleeping bag and electronics and take a test drive with full luggage before you leave. Then the bike stays quiet, you arrive relaxed, and the journey is all yours.
