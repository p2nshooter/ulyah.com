In autumn and winter you often drive to work and back in the dark. The lighting then determines whether you can see the road well and, more importantly, whether others can see you in time. In Germany, Section 67 of the Road Traffic Licensing Regulations (StVZO) regulates exactly which lights and reflectors a bicycle must have. Since the 2017 reform, the rules have become much more practical: battery-powered lamps are allowed and the lights can be removable.

This guide explains what is mandatory, how you can recognize approved lamps, which type of light suits which driver and how you can adjust the lamp so that it illuminates well without causing glare.

## The mandatory equipment at a glance

| part | regulation |
|---|---|
| Headlights | white, approved, fixed or removable |
| Front reflector | white, often integrated into the headlight |
| Rear light | red, approved |
| Large rear reflector | red, marked with "Z", often integrated into the rear light |
| Pedal reflector | yellow, two per pedal (front and rear) |
| Side reflectors | yellow spoke reflectors (two per wheel), reflective tire strips or spoke sticks on each spoke |

There are also the other mandatory parts that have nothing to do with light: two independent brakes and a bright-sounding bell.

## Rechargeable battery, battery or dynamo?

In the past, a bicycle had to have a dynamo. That's been over since 2017. Are permitted:

- **Hub dynamo:** It is located in the front wheel hub and generates electricity as soon as the wheel turns. It is almost wear-free, you never have to think about charging, and the light is always there. Ideal for everyday bikes and commuters.
- **Battery lamps:** They are charged via USB and can be removed with one hand. Good for road bikes, mountain bikes and bikes that rarely ride in the dark. Disadvantage: If the battery is empty, you are left without light.
- **Battery lamps:** They work like battery lamps, but use disposable batteries. Sufficient for occasional use, but less environmentally friendly in the long run.
- **Light on the e-bike battery:** On Pedelecs, the light is usually powered by the motor battery. Many systems switch off the engine when the charge level is low before the lights go out so that there is still a reserve of light.

Removable lamps don't have to be on the bike all the time. However, they must be installed and switched on when it is dark or visibility is poor, such as at dusk, in fog or in heavy rain. The previous exception for light racing bikes no longer exists: If you ride in the dark, you need light, regardless of the bike.

## How you can recognize approved lamps

Permitted bicycle lamps carry an official test mark: a **wavy line, the letter K and a number**. If this symbol is not on the lamp, it is not permitted on the road, even if it is bright.

This approval is often missing, especially for very bright lamps from the mountain bike sector. These lamps shine upwards in a round shape without any shielding; they blind oncoming traffic. They are practical off-road, but are forbidden on the road. Approved headlights have a **cut-off point**: They illuminate the road in front of you, but not into the eyes of oncoming people.

## How bright does the light have to be?

For bicycle headlights, the brightness is usually given in lux, i.e. the illuminance at a certain distance. As a rough guide:

| Lux | Suitable for |
|---|---|
| 10 to 30 | illuminated city streets to be seen |
| 30 to 70 | mixed paths, commuting with unlit sections |
| 70 to 150 | Country roads, unlit bike paths, faster driving |

More important than the highest value is uniform illumination: a wide carpet of light directly in front of the bike and a good reach to the front. A lamp with a well-shaped beam and 60 lux can be better in practice than one with 100 lux and a narrow light spot.

### High beam, daytime running lights and brake lights

Since the reform, additional functions have been expressly permitted:

- **High beam:** Some headlights have a second stage. It may only be used if no one is being blinded and must be switched off when there is oncoming traffic.
- **Daytime running lights:** Many lamps have an additional light that ensures better visibility during the day.
- **Brake light:** Rear lights with brake light function light up brighter when braking.
- **Flashing light:** Flashing front or rear lights are prohibited. An even light is easier for others to assess.

Turn signals are only intended for certain bikes, such as multi-lane cargo bikes, where a hand signal is difficult to see. On a normal bike, the hand signal remains mandatory.

## Adjust the lamp correctly

An incorrectly adjusted lamp is of little use or blinds you. Here's how to set it up correctly:

1. Place the bike on flat ground, about five meters from a wall.
2. Turn on the light and measure the height of the center of the lamp above the ground.
3. The upper edge of the bright light field on the wall should be about half as high as the lamp itself. If the lamp is 80 centimeters high, the cutoff point is about 40 centimeters.
4. During a short ride in the dark, check whether the beam of light reaches far enough and illuminates evenly.

A headlight that is set too high dazzles, while a headlight that is set too low only illuminates the area directly in front of the front wheel.

## The rear light: often underestimated

When it comes to safety, the rear light is just as important as the front light because many serious accidents happen because cars drive in from behind. Pay attention to:

- **Parking light function:** With dynamo lighting, the taillight should continue to shine for a few minutes after stopping, for example at traffic lights.
- **Good Position:** The taillight should not be obscured by luggage, bags or a child seat. If you have a trailer, it also needs a taillight and reflectors.
- **Cleanliness:** Dirt and slush significantly reduce the brightness. Wipe the taillight regularly.

## Become visible: more than just light

Light is mandatory, visibility is optional. With simple means you will be seen much sooner:

- **Reflective clothing:** High-visibility vest, jacket with reflective strips or reflective bands on trouser legs and arms. Moving reflectors on the feet and ankles are particularly easy to recognize.
- **Reflective tire strips:** They show the shape of a wheel from the side and make you visible at intersections.
- **Light-colored bags:** Bags with reflective elements on the luggage rack increase the visible area.

## Maintenance and troubleshooting

- **Battery lamps:** Charge before dark journeys. Many lamps show the charge level. A small spare lamp in your bag doesn't hurt.
- **Dynamo light:** If the light flickers or goes out, it is often due to a loose plug on the hub or a damaged cable. Check cable, clean plug and plug it back in tightly.
- **Check:** Check the front and rear lights once a week in the dark season, preferably before your first ride.

## How much does good lighting cost?

| Solution | Price range |
|---|---|
| Simple approved battery pack | 30 to 60 euros |
| Good battery pack with high brightness | 60 to 150 euros |
| Dynamo headlight and taillight | 60 to 200 euros, plus hub dynamo |
| Retrofitting a hub dynamo in the wheel | 100 to 250 euros including installation |

A fine for missing or defective lighting is around 20 euros and increases if someone is endangered. In an emergency, the question of joint liability after an accident if you were traveling without lights is more expensive.

## Light on the Pedelec and S-Pedelec

A **Pedelec** with support up to 25 km/h is legally considered a bicycle. The same rules as described above apply to its lighting. Most Pedelecs have factory-installed headlights and taillights that run on the motor battery. When buying, it's worth taking a look at the brightness, because the headlights are often weak, especially in cheap models. Replacing it with a brighter headlight suitable for e-bikes is usually possible, but should match the voltage of the system. If in doubt, ask the specialist dealer.

A **S-Pedelec** with support up to 45 km/h, on the other hand, is a moped. Its lighting is part of the vehicle registration and follows the regulations for motor vehicles. Modifications to lights and mirrors can jeopardize the operating license. The following also applies to motorcycles: They must also ride with lights on during the day.

## Retrofitting lighting: this is how you do it

If your bike has no or only weak lighting, you can retrofit it:

1. **Choose a system.** A hub dynamo is worth it for daily commuting, while a battery set is sufficient for occasional trips.
2. **Check the attachment.** Battery lamps are usually attached to the handlebars and seat post with rubber bands or clamp holders. Dynamo headlights are located on the fork head or fork, the rear light on the mudguard or luggage rack.
3. **Make sure you have a clear view.** The headlight must not be obscured by a handlebar bag or basket, and the rear light must not be obscured by luggage.
4. **Lay cables cleanly.** For dynamo lighting, use cable ties to guide the cables along the fork and frame without them being tensioned when steering.
5. **Adjust the lamp** as described above and take a test drive in the dark.

If you are unsure, have a hub dynamo installed in the workshop, as this requires the front wheel to be re-spoked or a new wheel to be purchased.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to have lights on my bike during the day?**
No, removable lamps only need to be installed in darkness and poor visibility. But reflectors must always be on the bike.

**Can I use a helmet lamp?**
A helmet lamp can also be helpful for seeing when cornering. However, it does not replace the mandatory headlight on the bike and must not blind anyone.

**Are spoke reflectors mandatory if my tires have reflective stripes?**
No. On the side, one of the permitted solutions is sufficient: spoke reflectors, reflective tire strips or spoke sticks on all spokes.

**How long do battery-powered lights last?**
That depends on the level. Many lamps last two to five hours on a medium setting. A lamp with a charge level indicator is worthwhile for daily commuting.

## Conclusion

A bicycle needs white light at the front and red at the back, reflectors at the front, back, on the pedals and on the sides. Since 2017, lamps have been allowed to be battery-operated and removable, but they must bear the K test mark and be switched on in the dark. A hub dynamo with parking light is the most convenient solution for commuters, and a good battery set for sporty bikes. Adjust the lamp to illuminate the path without causing glare, keep the taillight clean, and supplement the light with reflective clothing. This way you can see well and be seen well.
