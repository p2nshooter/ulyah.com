“How far can I go with one battery charge?” is the question that almost everyone asks before buying a Pedelec. The honest answer: It depends on many factors. The same battery can last for 120 kilometers one day and be empty after just 40 kilometers on another. If you understand what influences the range, you can plan it better and extend it significantly with a few simple habits.

In this guide we explain how to read the battery capacity, what factors determine the range, how to plan realistically and what tips you can use to get more kilometers from each charge.

## Watt-hours: the size of the tank

The capacity of a Pedelec battery is given in **Watt hours (Wh)**. It is approximately the size of a tank. Common batteries today have around 400 to 750 watt hours, some bikes have double batteries with over 1,000 watt hours.

The amount of energy a Pedelec uses per kilometer varies greatly. Roughly you can say:

- **Economical driving** on the flat with low support: around 4 to 7 watt hours per kilometer.
- **Medium ride** with mixed terrain and medium support: approximately 7 to 12 watt hours per kilometer.
- **Challenging ride** in the mountains, with high support, heavy luggage or headwind: 12 to 20 watt hours per kilometer and more.

A battery with 500 watt hours will roughly last from around 25 kilometers in very demanding conditions to over 100 kilometers with economical driving on the flat. These numbers are not promises, but orders of magnitude.

## The most important influencing factors

### Support level

The biggest lever. At the highest level, the motor produces multiples of its own power and consumes a corresponding amount of energy. At the lowest level it only helps a little and the battery lasts significantly longer. If you mostly ride at a medium level and only use the highest level on hills, you will gain a lot of kilometers.

### Own pedaling power

The more you pedal, the less the engine has to work. This has a direct effect on systems with a torque sensor: those who pedal harder receive more support, but also put in more power themselves.

### Cadence and gear selection

Engines work most efficiently in a certain speed range. If you push at a low cadence in a heavy gear, you force the motor and battery to work inefficiently. A cadence of around 60 to 80 revolutions per minute is favorable for many mid-engines. Therefore, downshift in good time, especially when starting off and on hills.

### Total weight

Driver, bike, luggage and possibly a child or trailer: every extra kilogram has to be accelerated and brought up the mountain. The range decreases significantly, especially with cargo bikes and when cycling with luggage.

### Terrain and elevation

Uphill, a Pedelec uses many times the energy it needs on the flat. A tour with many meters of altitude drains the battery much more quickly than the same route in flatland. No energy is recovered downhill; Pedelecs generally do not have recuperation.

### speed and wind

Air resistance increases sharply with speed. Anyone who constantly drives just under 25 km/h uses significantly more than someone who travels at 18 to 20 km/h. Headwind acts like a slight incline that never stops.

### Tires and tire pressure

Wide, coarse tires and too little air pressure increase rolling resistance. A bike with well-inflated, well-rolling tires will go further. Check the pressure regularly with a pressure gauge.

### Temperature

When it's cold, the battery delivers less energy. In winter the range can decrease noticeably. A warm battery and a thermal cover help.

### Frequent starting

City traffic with lots of traffic lights costs energy because every start requires a lot of power. This is where it helps to downshift in good time before stopping so that you start off in easy gear.

### Age and condition of the battery

An older battery has less capacity than a new one. You also have to take this into account when planning.

## Understand the range display correctly

Many displays show an estimated remaining range. This display is usually calculated from the consumption of the last few kilometers and the selected support level. If you change levels or drive into the mountains, the display jumps. It is therefore an indication, not a guarantee. If you're planning a long tour, you shouldn't rely on them alone.

Some manufacturers offer range calculators online where you can enter battery size, weight, terrain, support level and temperature. They provide good estimates for planning.

## Plan realistically

1. **Know the route and elevation:** Plan the route using a map or app that shows the elevation.
2. **Plan buffer:** Allow at least 20 to 30 percent reserve, especially for unknown routes, wind or cold.
3. **Know charging options:** Many cafes, inns and accommodations allow charging, sometimes for a small fee. Having your own charger in your luggage makes you flexible.
4. **Consider a second battery:** A second battery can make sense for long tours if your system allows it.
5. **Being able to drive without a motor:** Remember that a Pedelec with an empty battery is heavy. Plan so that in an emergency you can manage the last few kilometers without support.

## Ten tips for more reach

1. **Low level as standard:** Ride in Eco or a low level on the flat and use higher levels only on hills.
2. **Shift in good time:** Shift down before traffic lights and uphill gradients to keep the engine and legs in the efficient range.
3. **Drive evenly:** Constant acceleration and braking costs energy. Driving with foresight saves money.
4. **Slightly slower:** 20 instead of 25 km/h significantly reduces air resistance.
5. **Check tire pressure:** Tires roll more easily in the upper range of the permitted pressure.
6. **Reduce luggage:** Only take what you need.
7. **Care for the chain:** A clean, oiled chain runs easier.
8. **Check brakes:** A dragging brake costs a lot of energy without being noticed.
9. **Keep the battery warm:** In winter, only use it shortly before driving.
10. **Participate yourself:** Anyone who contributes a little more of their own strength will be rewarded with kilometers and also do something for their fitness.

## How much battery do I need?

| Usage | recommended capacity |
|---|---|
| short city trips up to 15 km daily | 400-500 Wh |
| Commuting 20-40 km daily, little elevation gain | 500–625 Wh |
| Tours, hills, luggage | 625–750 Wh |
| Cargo bike, mountain region, long tours | 750 Wh and more or double battery |

More capacity also means more weight and a higher price. A battery that is too large is not a problem, but it is not always necessary. It is important to have a reserve for winter and aging.

## Range and battery health

If you take care of your battery, you will maintain a long range for longer. Charging at room temperature, no constant heat and no long periods of lying with a full or empty charge slow down aging. Find out more in the battery care guide.

## Three example calculations

Three examples with the same Pedelec and a battery with 500 watt hours show how much the conditions change the range. The consumption values ​​are assumed, typical orders of magnitude.

**Example 1: Commuting on the flat.** A person weighing 75 kilograms travels 15 kilometers to work, usually in Eco mode, at around 20 km/h and with little luggage. With a consumption of around 5 watt hours per kilometer, it needs around 75 watt hours per route. The battery is mathematically enough for six one-way journeys, i.e. three working days, before it needs to be charged, with a reserve.

**Example 2: Weekend tour in low mountain ranges.** The same person rides a lap of 50 kilometers and 700 meters in altitude, on the mountain in the Sport level. Consumption increases to around 11 watt hours per kilometer, a total of around 550 watt hours. The battery wouldn't be enough. It helps here to consistently drive in Eco on the flat or to plan a break with charging options.

**Example 3: Cargo bike in winter.** A cargo bike with two children and shopping, total weight more than 150 kilograms, at zero degrees and frequent starts in the city. Consumption can rise to 15 watt hours per kilometer or more, and the cold further reduces usable capacity. Instead of over 60 kilometers, perhaps only 25 to 30 kilometers are realistic.

The examples show: The same battery size can be sufficient for very different routes. That's why a statement like "up to 150 kilometers" in advertising is not wrong, but only applies under ideal conditions.

## If the battery runs out on the go

It even happens to experienced Pedelec riders: the display suddenly only shows one bar and there are still ten kilometers to go home.

1. **Immediately switch to the lowest level** or switch off the support completely while it is flat. This leaves energy for the climbs.
2. **Choose an easy gear** and ride at a high cadence.
3. **Reduce speed.** Driving steadily and a little slower saves a surprising amount.
4. **Look for a charging option:** A café, restaurant or bike shop will often be happy to help if you ask nicely and have your charger with you.
5. **Continue driving without the motor:** A Pedelec can also be driven with an empty battery, it is just heavier. You can reach your destination with an easy walk.

By the way, many systems reserve some residual energy for the light so that you are still illuminated even when the drive battery is empty. But don't rely on it and plan your tours with reserves in mind.

## Summer versus winter: the temperature in numbers

At temperatures around freezing point, the usable capacity of a battery can decrease noticeably. Anyone who can cover 70 kilometers on one charge in summer should expect significantly less in winter, often 20 to 30 percent less, and even more in severe frost. In winter there are also wider tires, headwinds, heavier clothing and lights on. A warm battery, a thermal cover and a low support level get some of it back.

## Conclusion

The range of a Pedelec is not a fixed number, but the result of many factors: battery size, assistance level, weight, terrain, speed, wind, tires and temperature. Anyone who knows these factors will plan realistically and drive in a relaxed manner. With low support on the level, timely shifting, good tire pressure and a well-maintained drive, you get significantly more kilometers out of every battery charge.
