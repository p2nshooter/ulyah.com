The gears play a key role in how a bike rides, how much care it needs and how long the parts last. There are two basic systems in everyday life: the derailleur gear, in which the chain jumps over several sprockets on the outside, and the hub gear, in which the gears are located inside the rear wheel hub. There are also special forms such as gear shifts in the bottom bracket and continuously variable hubs.

This guide explains how the systems work, who is suitable for each one and what you should pay attention to when buying.

## This is how a derailleur works

With a derailleur gear, there are several sprockets of different sizes on the rear wheel, the so-called cassette. A rear derailleur guides the chain from one sprocket to the next when shifting gears. Many bikes also have two or three chainrings and a front derailleur at the front. Modern mountain bikes and gravel bikes often only have one chainring at the front and have a cassette at the back with a very large gear range.

The number of gears results from the combination: three chainrings times ten sprockets result in 30 gears. However, many of them overlap, and some combinations should be avoided because of the sloping chain path. What is important is not the number of gears, but the **bandwidth**, i.e. the difference between the easiest and the hardest gear.

### Advantages of derailleur gearing

- **High efficiency:** A clean, oiled derailleur transfers the power very efficiently. For sporty drivers, every watt counts.
- **Wide range:** Modern circuits in particular offer very easy mountain gears and fast gears for the flats.
- **Low weight:** Derailleur gears are lighter than comparable hub gears.
- **Cheap replacement parts:** Chains, cassettes and rear derailleurs are widely used and available in many price ranges.
- **Easy wheel removal:** The rear wheel can be quickly removed in the event of a breakdown.

### Disadvantages of derailleur gearing

- **Requires care:** The exposed chain and rear derailleur are exposed to dirt, water and salt. Without regular cleaning and lubrication, the drive will wear out quickly.
- **Delicate:** The rear derailleur protrudes to the side and can be bent if it falls over or is placed on a bicycle stand.
- **Only shifts when pedaling:** You cannot shift while stationary. If you stop at a traffic light in slow gear, you have to start with difficulty.
- **Wear:** Chain, sprockets and chainrings wear out and must be replaced regularly.

## This is how a hub gear works

With a hub gear, a planetary gear is located in the rear wheel hub. There is only one sprocket and one chainring on the outside, the chain always runs straight. Shifting is done via a cable that changes the gear ratio inside the hub. Three, five, seven, eight or eleven gears are common, and fourteen in high-quality models.

### Advantages of the hub gear

- **Little maintenance:** The gearbox is protected and only needs an oil change or greasing at longer intervals, depending on the model.
- **Shifting while stationary:** You can shift into easy gear at traffic lights. This is a huge increase in comfort in city traffic.
- **Robust:** There is no protruding rear derailleur that can be bent.
- **Full covering possible:** Because the chain runs straight, a closed chain case can be installed. Then the pants stay clean and the chain lasts longer.
- **Long-lasting chain:** Because the chain runs straight, the chain wears more slowly.

### Disadvantages of hub gears

- **Higher weight:** A hub gear weighs more than a comparable derailleur gear.
- **Slightly lower efficiency:** Slightly more power is lost in the transmission. You hardly notice this in everyday life, but sporty drivers notice it more.
- **Lower range for simple models:** A three-speed hub is sufficient in flat cities, but hardly sufficient in hilly areas.
- **More complex wheel removal:** If the rear wheel breaks down, the shift cable must be released. With a little practice this isn't a problem, but it's more complicated when you're on the move.
- **More expensive repair:** If the gearbox itself is defective, a repair is the responsibility of a specialist workshop.

## Special shapes

### Belt drive

Instead of a chain, a timing belt made of plastic with carbon fibers transmits the power. It is quiet, clean, requires no oil and lasts a very long time. A belt only works with a hub gear or a gear in the bottom bracket because it cannot jump sideways. In addition, the frame must be designed for this because the strap cannot be opened. Belt drives are found primarily on high-quality everyday and trekking bikes and on Pedelecs.

### Stepless hub

Continuously variable hubs change the gear ratio without fixed gear steps. You turn the handle and the translation adapts smoothly. This is particularly convenient for people who don't want to think about aisles. Some Pedelecs combine such hubs with automatic control.

### Gear in the bottom bracket

Some manufacturers do not build the gearbox in the rear wheel hub, but in the frame in the bottom bracket area. This shifts the weight to the center of the bike and keeps the rear wheel light. Such systems are robust but expensive and tied to special frameworks.

## The direct comparison

| criterion | Derailleur gears | Hub gears | Belt + hub |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maintenance effort | high | low | very low |
| Efficiency | very high | good | good |
| Weight | low | higher | higher |
| Bandwidth | very big | small to large | depending on the hub |
| Shifting while stationary | no | yes | yes |
| Sensitivity | higher | low | low |
| Acquisition costs | low to high | medium to high | high |

## Which circuit suits you?

### City trips and short commutes

For many stops at traffic lights, trips in everyday clothes and little desire for care, a hub gear with seven or eight gears is usually ideal. In flat cities, a three-speed hub is sufficient. With the chain box everything stays clean.

### Longer commutes and tours

Both systems make sense here. If you ride a lot of altitude and like to be sporty, you should choose a **derailleur** with a wide range. If you ride all year round in any weather and don't want to screw around much, you'll be well served with a **high-quality hub gear** and possibly a belt.

### Sports and terrain

For racing bikes, gravel bikes and mountain bikes there is hardly any way around the **derailleur gear**. It is lighter, more efficient and offers the necessary bandwidth.

### Pedelecs

In Pedelecs with a mid-engine, large forces act on the drive. This means that derailleur gears wear out more quickly, but they work well. Hub gears are more robust and comfortable because you can shift gears while stationary. Some combinations of motor and hub even automatically recognize the appropriate gear.

## Shift correctly: This means the gearshift lasts longer

- **Shift with foresight:** Shift into a lighter gear before going uphill or before traffic lights, not when you're almost standing still.
- **Relieve the strain briefly:** When using derailleur gears, briefly use some power from the pedals when changing gears. With a hub gear, it is best to shift without load or with little pressure.
- **Avoid extreme combinations:** For derailleur gears with multiple chainrings, a large chainring with the largest sprocket and a small chainring with the smallest sprocket are unfavorable. The chain then runs at a steep angle and wears out more quickly.
- **Maintain cadence:** It is better to pedal faster in an easier gear than to push in a harder gear. This protects your knees and material.

## Maintenance at a glance

- **Derailleur gears:** Clean and oil the chain regularly, more often every few hundred kilometers in wet conditions. Measure chain wear regularly. Check and adjust the rear derailleur and cables occasionally.
- **Hub gears:** Oil and tension the chain, occasionally adjust the gears using the markings. Depending on the model, have an oil change or maintenance done in the workshop after a certain mileage. The manufacturer's information states the intervals.
- **Belt:** Occasionally clean with water and check tension. Do not use oil.

## Understanding gears: range and development

Anyone who compares gears will come across two terms that say more than just the number of gears.

The **unfolding** is the distance the wheel travels in one full turn of the crank. It depends on the chainring, the sprocket and the circumference of the wheel. An easy mountain walk has a short development of perhaps two meters, a fast walk on the flats six to eight meters or more. The shorter the smallest deployment, the easier it is for you to get up a steep mountain, especially with luggage.

The **bandwidth** indicates by what percentage the heaviest gear is longer than the lightest. A simple three-speed hub achieves around 180 percent, many eight-speed hubs achieve around 300 percent, high-quality hubs and modern derailleur gears reach 400 to over 500 percent. For flat cities, 200 to 300 percent is sufficient. If you live in low mountain ranges or are traveling with luggage, you should aim for 400 percent or more.

Also important are the **aisle jumps**, i.e. the distance between two adjacent aisles. Jumps that are too big feel like you're constantly switching between "too easy" and "too hard." It's worth paying attention to this during the test drive, especially with gearshifts with few gears and a wide range.

## Typical problems and their causes

| Symptom | Common cause of derailleur gears | Common cause of hub gears |
|---|---|---|
| Gear jumps or slips | elongated chain, worn sprockets, incorrect tension | incorrectly adjusted shift cable |
| Switches delayed | dirty or rusted cables and casings | Train stiff, switch dirty |
| Clicking when idling | Rear derailleur not adjusted, derailleur hanger bent | mostly normal for some hubs, otherwise workshop |
| Chain falls off | Stop screws set incorrectly, chain too long | Chain tension too low |
| Heavy pedaling despite an easy gait | dry chain, dragging brake | Hub needs maintenance, brake is rubbing |

Many of these problems can be resolved with cleaning, fresh oil, and a small tension adjustment. If that doesn't help, a wearing part is often necessary.

## Questions for the dealer

When you buy a bike, ask specifically about:

- How wide is the bandwidth of the circuit, and is it sufficient for my routes?
- How often does the hub or gears need to be serviced and approximately how much does it cost?
- Are spare parts such as chain, sprockets or belts easy to obtain?
- Can a chain guard or chain box be installed later?
- How do you remove the rear wheel if it breaks down? Let me show you once.

## Conclusion

There is no circuit that is best for everyone. The derailleur is light, efficient and versatile, but requires care. The hub gear is robust, comfortable and low-maintenance, but heavier. Anyone who drives primarily in the city and doesn't like tinkering will be happy with a hub. Anyone who is sporty or rides high altitudes should use a derailleur gear. And if you use the bike as a car replacement in any weather, you should at least take a look at the belt drive and high-quality hubs.
