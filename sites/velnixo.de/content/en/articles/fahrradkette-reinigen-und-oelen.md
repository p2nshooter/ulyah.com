The chain is the most stressed component of a bicycle. It transfers all your power to the rear wheel with every pedal revolution, runs over gears, bends hundreds of times per minute and is exposed to rain, dust and road salt. A well-maintained chain runs quietly, shifts precisely and lasts thousands of kilometers. A neglected chain squeaks, rusts, wears out quickly and takes the sprockets and chainrings with it.

The good news: chain maintenance is easy, cheap and can be done in ten minutes. In this guide we will show you how to clean the chain properly, which oil you should use, how much of it and how often.

## Why chain care is so important

A bicycle chain consists of outer and inner plates, bolts and rollers. Friction occurs between the bolts and sleeves at every bend. Without lubrication, metal surfaces rub directly against each other, the chain wears out and “lengthens” because the joints wear out. If dirt is added, it forms an abrasive paste with the oil, which further accelerates wear.

The consequences of a poorly maintained chain:

- **More effort:** A dry or dirty chain requires noticeably more effort than a clean, well-lubricated one.
- **Noises:** Squeaking and grinding are typical warning signs.
- **Shifting problems:** The chain does not jump smoothly onto the next sprocket.
- **Expensive wear and tear:** An elongated chain also wears out sprockets and chainrings. Instead of a new chain for 20 to 50 euros, the entire drive often has to be replaced.

## How often should you care for the chain?

There is no fixed number of kilometers, but good guidelines:

| Conditions | Clean | Oiling |
|---|---|---|
| dry weather, asphalt | every 300-500 km or when dirt is visible | every 200-300 km |
| Rain, wet streets | Wipe clean after every wet ride | after every wet ride |
| winter, salt | thoroughly every week | after every trip with salt |
| Dust, gravel, dirt roads | after every dusty tour | after every cleaning |

A good indicator is the finger test: run your finger over the chain. If it is black and greasy, there is too much oil and dirt on it. If the chain feels dry and rough or you hear squeaking, it needs oil.

## What you need

- an old rag or kitchen paper,
- a brush, for example an old toothbrush or a special chain brush,
- Chain cleaner or a mild water-based degreaser,
- possibly a chain cleaning device for thorough cleaning,
- Chain oil, suitable for the conditions,
- a work stand or a place where you can lift the rear wheel,
- Disposable gloves if you want to keep your hands clean.

## Choose the right chain oil

Not all chain oil is the same. There are roughly three groups:

- **Wet lubricant (wet lube):** thick, sticks well, does not wash off so quickly in the rain. Ideal for autumn, winter and rain. Disadvantage: attracts more dirt.
- **Dry lubricant (dry lube):** thin, often with wax or dry additives. Attracts little dirt, ideal for dry, dusty conditions. Needs to be applied more frequently and washes out quickly when it rains.
- **All-weather oils:** a compromise for changing conditions and for those who don't want to constantly change.

There is also chain wax, which the chain is dipped into or treated with. Wax keeps the chain very clean, but requires thorough preparation and regular follow-up.

**Not suitable** are cooking oil that becomes resinous, motor oil that binds a lot of dirt, and universal penetrating sprays from the hardware store. Although these dissolve rust and expel moisture, they hardly lubricate and evaporate quickly.

## Step by step: quick cleaning

For regular care, a quick cleaning with a cloth is sufficient:

1. **Park the bike stably:** It is best to do so in a repair stand or upside down on the saddle and handlebars. When tilting your head upside down, be careful not to damage the display, bell or handlebar instruments.
2. **Put a rag around the chain:** Wrap the rag around the lower half of the chain.
3. **Turn the crank backwards:** Slowly turn the crank backwards so that the chain passes through the lobe. Repeat until there is hardly any dirt left on the rag.
4. **Wipe the gears:** Use a clean piece of cloth to roughly clean the chainrings and sprockets. A narrow strip of cloth that you pull back and forth between the sprockets helps.

Afterwards you can oil directly.

## Step by step: thorough cleaning

If the chain is very dirty or you have rarely cared for it, it is worth cleaning it thoroughly:

1. **Remove coarse dirt:** With a brush and cloth.
2. **Apply cleaner:** Spray a bicycle-safe chain cleaner onto the chain or pour it into a chain cleaning device. The device is clamped around the chain and as it is cranked backwards, rotating brushes scrub the chain in the cleaning bath.
3. **Let it take effect and brush:** Let the cleaner work briefly and brush the chain, sprockets and chainrings.
4. **Rinse:** If the cleaner is water-based, rinse with a little water. Do not use a hard jet of water or a high-pressure cleaner.
5. **Dry thoroughly:** Dry with a cloth and then let it dry for some time. Otherwise, residual moisture in the joints will displace the new oil. A quick blow dryer on a low setting or a few hours in the air will help.

Make sure that no cleaner gets on the brake discs, brake pads or rim sides. If necessary, cover the brakes with a rag.

## Step by step: oil properly

Oiling is the most important step and this is where most mistakes happen.

1. **Apply sparingly:** Put a small drop of oil on each chain link, on the inside of the chain, on the rollers. There the oil gets into the joints. With a chain with around 110 to 120 links, you slowly turn the crank backwards and drip as you turn.
2. **Let it take effect:** Turn the crank a few more revolutions and shift through several gears so that the oil is distributed.
3. **Wait a few minutes:** Give the oil time to penetrate the joints. Depending on the oil, a few minutes to an hour.
4. **Wipe off excess oil:** This is crucial. Grip the chain again with a clean rag and turn the crank backwards. There should hardly be any oil on the outside of the chain. Lubrication takes place on the inside; on the outside, the oil only attracts dirt.

A well-maintained chain has a slight shine, but feels almost dry on the outside.

## Chain care for hub gears and chain case

On bikes with hub gears and a closed chain case, the chain is well protected and requires much less care. Nevertheless, it should be oiled occasionally. With a full chain guard there is often a small opening or a removable cover. If the chain guard is open, care is as described above. The **chain tension** is also important here: The chain should have about one to two centimeters of play in the middle between the chainring and the sprocket. If it sags more, it can come off.

## Belt drive: no oil!

Bicycles with timing belts do not need oil. On the contrary: oil can damage the belt. A belt is only cleaned with water. A special silicone spray from the belt manufacturer can sometimes help with squeaks, otherwise the tension should be checked in the workshop.

## The most common mistakes

- **Too much oil:** A wet, dripping chain attracts sand and dust and will wear out faster than a slightly dry one.
- **Oil without cleaning:** New oil on a dirty chain flushes the dirt into the joints.
- **Wrong spot:** Oiling the outside of the tabs instead of the inside of the rollers doesn't help much.
- **Oil on the brakes:** A splash on the brake disc can massively reduce the braking effect. Then the pads often have to be replaced.
- **High-pressure cleaner:** It forces water into the chain joints and flushes out the lubricant.
- **Only spray, never wipe:** Spray oils spread everywhere, including on the rims, tires and brakes. A drip oil is usually more precise.

## When the chain needs to be changed

Even the best care can only slow down wear and tear. At some point every chain is stretched and needs to be replaced. You measure this with a chain wear gauge. Depending on the chain type, the wear limit is 0.5 to 0.75 percent elongation. We explain in detail how to measure and change the chain in the chain wear guide. As a rough guide, chains on everyday bikes last several thousand kilometers if well cared for, but on Pedelecs they often last less.

## Rust on the chain

Slight rust after a night of rain is nothing to worry about. Clean, dry and oil thoroughly and it will usually disappear quickly. On the other hand, if the chain is deeply rusted, stiff, or individual links can hardly be moved, replacing it makes more sense. A rusty chain can break, which can lead to a fall.

## Care routine for frequent travelers

If you commute every day, you can get used to a simple routine:

- **Every Friday:** Wipe the chain with a cloth and oil if necessary.
- **Once a month:** thorough cleaning with brush and cleaner.
- **Every few months:** Measure chain wear.
- **After every rain or salty ride:** Wipe the chain, dry it, oil it.

This means the drive stays clean, the gears remain precise, and the chain lasts significantly longer.

## Chain wax: an alternative for cleanliness lovers

More and more cyclists are lubricating their chains with wax instead of oil. The chain is either dipped in hot paraffin wax with additives or treated with a liquid wax that forms a solid layer after drying. The big advantage: wax hardly attracts dirt. The chain stays clean, as do your hands and trouser legs, and wear and tear can be significantly reduced.

The disadvantage is the effort at the beginning. A new chain must be completely degreased before it is waxed for the first time because wax is not compatible with factory grease. After that, depending on the product, it needs to be waxed every few hundred kilometers, and when it rains, wax wears off faster than a good wet oil. For commuters who drive in all weathers, a good oil is often more practical. Anyone who drives primarily in dry weather and likes to do crafts will love wax.

## Squeaking despite oil? Possible causes

If the chain squeaks or cracks despite care, it is not always the chain itself:

- **Stiff limbs:** Individual limbs move with difficulty. When you turn backwards you can see how the chain on the rear derailleur jumps briefly. Oil the limb specifically and move it carefully to the side.
- **Dry rear derailleur wheels:** The small wheels on the rear derailleur can also squeak. A drop of oil on the axle helps.
- **Pedals or bottom bracket:** Cracking when pedaling hard often comes from there and not from the chain.
- **Worn parts:** An elongated chain on worn sprockets makes noise that oil does not eliminate.

## Conclusion

Chain care is the simplest and most effective maintenance on a bicycle. Clean it, oil it sparingly, wipe the outside dry, measure regularly: that's all you need. Anyone who invests these few minutes will drive more quietly, more easily and will save a lot of money on spare parts over the years.
