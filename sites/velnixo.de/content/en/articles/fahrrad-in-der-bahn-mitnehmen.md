Train and bike are an ideal pair. You can take the train to the start of a bike tour in a relaxed manner, shorten a stage if the weather changes, or comfortably travel home after a long-distance tour. Many commuters also combine cycling and trains in everyday life to make longer journeys to work.

However, taking bicycles with you is not uniformly regulated in Germany. Different rules apply in long-distance transport than in regional transport, and in local transport the conditions differ from federal state to federal state and from transport association to transport association. This guide provides an overview and helps you plan your trip stress-free. Prices and conditions change occasionally, so please check with the provider before each trip.

## Long-distance transport: ICE, IC and EC

You need two things for your bike on Deutsche Bahn long-distance transport:

1. **A bicycle ticket for long-distance transport.** It is purchased in addition to your own ticket.
2. **A parking space reservation.** It is mandatory for long-distance transport. You are not allowed to take the bike with you without a reservation, even if there still seem to be places available.

You can make the reservation when booking online, in the app or at the counter. In high season, on weekends and at the start of holidays, seats on popular routes are booked out early. So book as early as possible, especially for Easter, Pentecost and the summer holidays.

### Not every train takes bikes

Not all long-distance trains have bicycle parking spaces. Newer ICE trains and most intercity trains offer seats, older ICE series often do not. In the timetable information you can set that only connections where you can take your bike with you are displayed. This is the most reliable way.

### Where the bike is on the train

The bicycle parking spaces are located in a specific carriage, which is marked with a bicycle symbol on the carriage stand display on the platform and on the train. Before the train arrives, check in which section of the platform this car stops. This means you don't have to rush along the platform on your bike.

## Regional transport: RE, RB and S-Bahn

It's usually easier to take regional transport, but the rules are different:

- **Bicycle day ticket:** There is a bicycle day ticket that is valid for one day for trips on Deutsche Bahn regional transport.
- **Regional tariffs:** Many transport associations have their own bicycle tickets, some allow you to take them free of charge at certain times, others require a child ticket or your own bicycle card.
- **Closed times:** In some regions, bicycle transport is restricted during rush hour on weekdays, for example between six and nine in the morning.
- **No reservation:** You generally cannot make a reservation on regional transport. The rule is: whoever comes first goes first. If the multi-purpose compartment is full, the train staff can refuse to take it on board.

### Germany ticket and bike

The Deutschlandticket is valid nationwide on local transport, but does not generally include taking bicycles with you. You need an additional ticket for the bike. Some federal states and transport associations have their own regulations where taking bikes is cheaper or free at certain times. Before you travel, find out about the rules in the region you are traveling in.

## Pedelecs, S-Pedelecs, folding bikes and cargo bikes

| vehicle | Takeaway |
|---|---|
| Normal bike | yes, according to the respective rules |
| Pedelec up to 25 km/h | yes, like a normal bike. Pay attention to the weight when loading and unloading |
| S-Pedelec with insurance license plate | excluded on many trains, especially on long-distance trains. Check conditions beforehand |
| Folding bike, folded up | is considered hand luggage and usually travels free of charge |
| Bicycle in a transport bag | disassembled and packed often allowed as luggage if it meets the dimensions |
| Tandem, recumbent bike, cargo bike, bike with trailer | Generally excluded in long-distance transport and only partially permitted in local transport |

If you often combine bike and train, a **folding bike** is the most flexible option. When folded it fits in the luggage rack or between the seats, no reservation is necessary.

## Getting on and off by bike

Getting in and out is the most strenuous part of the journey, especially with luggage and heavy Pedelecs. These tips will help:

1. **Get to the platform early:** Allow enough time for elevators, which are sometimes small, slow, or out of service.
2. **Remove bags:** Remove panniers before boarding if the bike is heavy or needs to be lifted over steps. With Pedelecs, it can help to remove the battery.
3. **Let others off first:** Wait for the passengers to get off, then get on quickly.
4. **Accept Help:** At high levels, a helping hand is welcome. Feel free to ask other passengers.
5. **Secure the bike:** Place the bike in the holder provided or fasten it with the strap. An unsecured wheel can fall over when cornering or braking.
6. **Prepare to get off:** Go to the bike in good time in front of the destination station and have your bags ready.

### Courtesy in the multi-purpose compartment

The multi-purpose compartment is also used by people with wheelchairs, walkers and strollers. You have priority. Position your bike so that no one gets trapped and the doors remain clear. If you get out earlier than other cyclists, put your bike forward. A short conversation about the destination stations helps everyone.

## Combine bike and train on a bike trip

Many cycle tours begin and end by train. To ensure that this goes smoothly, a little planning helps:

- **Arrival the day before or early in the morning:** Regional trains are often busier in the mornings on weekdays than on weekends. If you start early on Saturday, it is usually easier to find a place in the multi-purpose compartment.
- **Choose routes with regular service:** A destination station that is served every hour offers more security than a connection that only runs twice a day.
- **Transfers with a buffer:** Changing platforms takes longer with a bike and luggage. Allow at least ten minutes for a transfer, but more at large train stations.
- **Keep the return journey flexible:** On regional transport, you can spontaneously take an earlier or later train without a reservation if the weather turns bad or your legs are tired. This is more difficult in long-distance transport because of the parking space reservation.
- **Note train stations along the route:** They are your plan B if you need to shorten a stage.

## Prepare the bike for the train ride

A few simple steps before departure will make the journey easier for you and the other passengers:

1. **Secure loose parts:** Attach or remove the pump, drinking bottle and handlebar bag firmly so that nothing falls down.
2. **Take a tension strap with you:** In some trains there is no holder, just a rod. A short tension strap holds the bike securely.
3. **Packing bags ready to hand:** Quick-release bags can be removed in seconds if the bike needs to be lifted over steps.
4. **Note the chain and rear derailleur:** Park the bike so that the rear derailleur does not press against other wheels or walls. A bent derailleur hanger is a common result of a rough ride.
5. **Knock off dirt:** After a period of rain, remove the largest lumps of earth so that the compartment stays clean.

## Commuting by bike and train

Anyone who travels to work by bike or train every day needs a reliable solution, because a full train in the morning can ruin the whole day. Three ways have proven successful:

- **Two bikes:** A simple, sturdy bike at your place of residence and a second, inexpensive bike at your destination station. At the train station it is in a bicycle box or a guarded bicycle parking garage. This means you don't have to take it with you at all and blocking times don't matter.
- **Folding bike:** When folded, it fits on any train and usually travels free of charge. Ideal if you still have to drive some distance to your destination and there is no parking space available.
- **Seasonal ticket with bike transport:** Some transport associations offer monthly tickets that include the bike outside of the closed times. It's worth comparing the offers in your own region.

At the station itself, bicycle racks are often overcrowded and thefts are common. A good lock and, if possible, a place in a bicycle station are therefore particularly important. More about this in the guide to theft protection.

## Going abroad by bike

Many international trains take bicycles with them, usually with reservation requirements. This applies, for example, to connections to Austria, Switzerland, Denmark or the Netherlands. Some night trains also have bicycle spaces. The rules depend on the respective operator and places are limited. Book early and check whether the connection also takes bikes in the neighboring country.

## Alternatives to the train

- **Long-distance bus:** Some long-distance bus providers take bicycles on a rear rack on certain routes. Space is limited and must be booked in advance. Pedelecs, cargo bikes and very heavy bikes are usually excluded.
- **Bicycle buses:** In some holiday regions, buses with bicycle trailers run in summer, for example to the starting points of cycle paths or in mountain regions.
- **Luggage and bike transport:** Flat rate operators and specialized services transport bikes and luggage to the starting point.
- **Rental bike at your destination:** In many holiday regions, renting a bike on site is a convenient alternative to taking with you.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I take my bike with me on the ICE train without a reservation?**
No. Parking space reservations are mandatory for long-distance transport. Without it you may be refused entry.

**What happens if the train is canceled?**
If a train is canceled or delayed, you can switch to other trains according to passenger rights. However, it is not guaranteed whether there will be a free bicycle space there. Talk to the staff and plan a regional train as an alternative if necessary.

**Can I park the bike in the anteroom or hallway?**
No. Bicycles belong in the designated parking spaces. Hallways and door areas must remain clear for emergencies.

**Any tips for commuters?**
Anyone who combines cycling and trains every day should check season tickets with bicycle transport, as offered by some transport associations. An alternative is a second, inexpensive bike at the destination station or a folding bike.

## Checklist for traveling by bike and train

- Looking for a connection with bike transport
- Bought a bike card and reserved a parking space on long-distance transport
- Closing times in regional traffic checked
- Checked the position of the bicycle cart
- Allow enough time for elevators and transfers
- Pannier bags easily removable, strap or elastic band included

## Conclusion

With the train, the bicycle becomes a travel companion for long distances. For long-distance transport, you need a bicycle map and a parking space reservation, which you should book early in the season. On regional transport it is usually possible to take it without a reservation, but tariffs and blocking times vary depending on the region, and the Germany ticket generally does not include the bike. Pedelecs travel like normal bikes, folding bikes are considered luggage when folded, cargo bikes and tandems are usually excluded. With a little preparation, time buffer and consideration in the multi-purpose compartment, the journey is relaxed.
