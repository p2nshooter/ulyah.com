In Germany there is no requirement to wear a helmet for normal bicycles and Pedelecs. Nevertheless, a large proportion of cyclists now wear helmets, and for good reason: head injuries are common and often serious when falls. A well-fitting helmet can significantly reduce the impact of an impact. But it only protects if it fits, is adjusted correctly and is not too old.

This guide explains how to find the right size, what types of helmets there are, what standards and rotation protection mean, how to adjust the helmet and when it's time for a new one.

## How a bicycle helmet protects

A bicycle helmet usually consists of a hard, thin outer shell and a core made of hard foam, usually EPS. In the event of an impact, this foam deforms and absorbs energy that would otherwise affect the head. That's why a helmet is disposable protection: after a hard fall, the foam is compressed, even if you can't see anything from the outside.

The outer shell ensures that the helmet slides across the road instead of getting stuck and protects the foam from scratches and pressure points in everyday life. In high-quality helmets, the shell is welded to the foam; this is called in-mold construction. It is lighter and more stable than a shell that is just glued on.

## Standards: how you can recognize a tested helmet

In Europe, bicycle helmets must meet the **EN 1078** standard. It is written inside the helmet, usually on a sticker, together with the CE mark, the date of manufacture and the size. Don't buy a helmet without this information.

Helmets are required for **S-Pedelecs** that support up to 45 km/h. Helmets according to the Dutch standard **NTA 8776** are recommended here. They are tested for higher speeds, protect a larger area of ​​the head and are slightly heavier. A normal bicycle helmet is not a complete replacement at 45 km/h.

## Rotation protection: MIPS and similar systems

In many falls, the head does not hit vertically, but rather at an angle. This creates rotational forces that can put strain on the brain. Some manufacturers therefore install systems that allow a small movement between the head and the helmet and are intended to reduce these rotational forces. The best known is MIPS, a thin, sliding inner shell. Other manufacturers have their own solutions.

Whether such a system makes sense for you depends on your budget. A well-fitting helmet without rotation protection is better than a poorly fitting one with one. If the price difference is reasonable for you, a helmet with rotation protection is a sensible choice.

## Find the right size

The size of a helmet depends on the circumference of the head. This is how you measure it:

1. Take a flexible tape measure.
2. Place it about a finger's width above your eyebrows and across the widest part of the back of your head.
3. Read the circumference in centimeters and measure twice to be sure.

Manufacturers specify a range for each helmet size, approximately 54 to 58 centimeters. If your head circumference is on the border between two sizes, try both. The shape of the head varies from person to person: some helmets fit round heads, others fit oval heads. That's why it's worth setting up several models.

### The sitting test in the store

- **Without straps:** Put on the helmet and tilt your head forward. A helmet that fits will stay in place for a short time without you having to tighten the headband.
- **Pressure points:** Wear the helmet for a few minutes. If it presses on the forehead or temples, it is the wrong model.
- **With glasses:** If you wear glasses or sunglasses, try the helmet with them on.

## Helmet types at a glance

| Type | Properties | Suitable for |
|---|---|---|
| Urban helmet | closed, smooth shape, often with integrated light | City, commuting, everyday life |
| Trekking and all-round helmet | good ventilation, visor optional | Tours, everyday life, leisure |
| Road bike helmet | very light, lots of ventilation openings, aerodynamic | sporty driving on the road |
| MTB helmet | pulled deeper at the back of the head, visor | Terrain, trails |
| Full face helmet | with chin bar | Downhill, bike park |
| S-Pedelec helmet (NTA 8776) | larger protection area, tested for higher speed | S-Pedelecs |

An urban or all-round helmet is usually the best choice for everyday use. Make sure that pendulum helmets have good ventilation, otherwise it quickly gets warm in summer, and that they have an integrated rear light or reflective surfaces.

## Adjust the helmet correctly

A helmet only protects if it fits correctly. This is how you do it:

1. **Position:** The helmet sits horizontally on the head and covers the forehead. There should be space for about two fingers between the eyebrows and the edge of the helmet. A helmet that sits far back on the head does not protect the forehead.
2. **Head ring:** Adjust the wheel or the twist lock at the back of the head so that the helmet sits firmly but without pressure. If you shake your head, it shouldn't move.
3. **Side Straps:** The front and back straps form a Y under each ear. The spreader should rest just below the earlobe, leaving the ear exposed.
4. **Chin strap:** Close the chin strap so that approximately one finger fits between the strap and the chin. He must not press when swallowing, but the helmet must not be pulled away from his head.
5. **Test:** Try pushing the helmet back and forth with your hand. He should barely move and not expose his forehead.

Check the setting regularly, especially if you wear a hat under your helmet in winter. There are thin helmet caps that hardly change the fit. Thick winter hats are not a good idea.

## When a helmet needs to be replaced

- **After every head impact fall:** Even if nothing is visible, the foam may be compressed. The helmet has done its job and needs to be replaced.
- **After a hard fall:** If the helmet falls from a height onto a hard floor, it should also be replaced.
- **If there is visible damage:** Cracks, dents, peeled shell or damaged straps are a clear sign.
- **After a few years:** Many manufacturers recommend replacement after about five years because material ages due to sun, sweat and temperature changes. The date of manufacture is written on the helmet.

Some manufacturers offer a discount on a new helmet after a fall. It's worth asking about.

## Care and storage

- Clean the helmet with lukewarm water and mild soap. Solvents, cleaners containing alcohol and stickers with aggressive adhesives can damage the foam.
- The inner cushions can usually be removed and washed by hand.
- Don't leave the helmet in the car in the blazing sun; extreme heat damages the material.
- Don't hang the helmet on the handlebars if the bike could fall over, or carelessly throw it on the ground.

## Children and helmets

Children fall more often than adults and their heads are larger in relation to their bodies. A helmet is particularly important to them, and children are more likely to wear one if their parents do too. Pay attention to:

- **Appropriate size instead of growth reserve:** A helmet that is too large provides poor protection. Many children's helmets can be adjusted in one area using a head strap.
- **Light weight and good ventilation** so the helmet doesn't get in the way.
- **Remove helmet when playing:** The helmet must be removed on the playground and when climbing because otherwise the straps can get caught.

## How much does a good helmet cost?

| Category | Price range |

| Simple tested helmet | 30 to 60 euros |
| Good everyday or trekking helmet | 60 to 120 euros |
| Helmet with rotation protection and integrated light | 100 to 200 euros |

An expensive helmet is not automatically safer; all tested helmets meet the same basic standard. More money usually brings less weight, better ventilation, more comfort and additional functions. The most important thing remains the fit.

## Helmet and liability

People often ask whether you are partly to blame after an accident without a helmet. According to the previous case law of the Federal Court of Justice, riding without a helmet does not generally lead to a reduction in compensation for normal everyday cyclists because there is no obligation to wear a helmet. When it comes to sporty riding, such as racing bike training, courts have sometimes judged things differently. The legal situation can evolve. Regardless, the helmet protects your head, not your needs.

## Common Misconceptions

| Mistake | That's right |
|---|---|
| "A helmet only protects when driving fast." | Many head injuries occur from slow falls in city traffic, such as slipping on a curb. |
| "My helmet still looks good after the crash, so it's fine." | The foam can be compressed on the inside without being visible. |
| "If the helmet is too big, you can compensate for it with the headband." | The headband only helps in a small area. A helmet that is too large will slip on impact. |
| "The helmet can sit far back, it's more comfortable." | The forehead is then unprotected and is often the first to hit when falling forward. |
| "A used helmet is a good bargain." | You don't know if he's had a fall yet. It's better to buy new helmets. |

## Checklist before every trip

- Does the helmet sit horizontally and cover the forehead?
- Is the chin strap closed and not twisted?
- Are the padding and straps OK?
- Is the integrated taillight charged if your helmet has one?

It only takes a few seconds and over time it becomes a habit.

## Conclusion

A bicycle helmet is not required in Germany, but it can prevent serious head injuries in the event of a fall. Buy a helmet with EN-1078 marking that fits your head circumference and shape and adjust it carefully: horizontally, two fingers above the eyebrows, Y straps under the ears, chin strap with one finger space. Replace it after every fall and after a few years. Helmets are required for S-Pedelecs; a helmet according to NTA 8776 is the right choice.
