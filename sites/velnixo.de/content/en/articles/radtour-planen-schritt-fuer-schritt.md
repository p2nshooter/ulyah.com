A bike tour can be an afternoon on the river, a weekend from place to place or a trip lasting several weeks across Germany. As different as these tours are, the basics of planning are the same: How far can and want to go? Which route is suitable? What do you have to take with you? Where do I sleep? And what do I do if something goes wrong?

This guide will guide you through the planning step by step. It is aimed at beginners who are planning their first longer tour, but also contains tips for anyone who has already been on the road and would like to improve their planning.

## Step 1: Determine the type of tour

The first question is what type of tour you want to do. It determines almost everything else.

| Tour type | Typical duration | Special features |
|---|---|---|
| Day tour | a few hours to a day | light luggage, way back or train back |
| Rally | several days | fixed accommodation, a tour every day without luggage |
| Route tour | several days to weeks | a new place every day, luggage on the bike |
| Flat rate cycling trip | a week | Organizer organizes accommodation and luggage transport |
| Bikepacking | a weekend to several weeks | light luggage in frame bags, often off-road |

For the first multi-day tour, a **star trip** or a short **long-distance tour on a river cycle path** is ideal. River cycle paths are mostly flat, well signposted and have a dense infrastructure of accommodation, cafes and train stations.

## Step 2: Realistic daily stages

The most common mistake when planning is a daily route that is too long. If you drive at the limit every day, you don't have time for breaks, sightseeing or detours, and the tour quickly becomes torture.

As a rough guide for tours with luggage:

| Experience | Flat terrain | Hilly terrain |
|---|---|---|
| Beginners | 40 to 60 km | 30 to 45 km |
| Experienced recreational cyclists | 60 to 90 km | 45 to 70 km |
| Sporty touring riders | 90 to 130 km | 70 to 100 km |

Longer stages are possible with a Pedelec, but here too battery range and charging time are the limiting factors. For multi-day tours, plan a rest day or a short day every three to five days. The body needs rest, and the weather doesn't always cooperate.

### Don't underestimate the altitude

In addition to the route, the altitude counts. As a simple rule of thumb for planning your time, you can plan on an additional ten minutes for every 100 meters uphill with your luggage, or more for steep ramps and little training. A stage with 60 kilometers and 600 meters of altitude takes noticeably longer than 60 flat kilometers. Check the elevation profile of the route before you commit and, if possible, put the most strenuous sections at the beginning of the day, when you are still fresh.

## Step 3: Plan the route

### Use existing cycle routes

There is a dense network of signposted cycle paths in Germany, including many long-distance cycle paths on rivers, coasts and old railway lines. They offer many advantages for planning:

- Continuous signage with directions,
- often car-free or low-traffic paths,
- Accommodations that are geared towards cyclists
- Information about sights, workshops and train stations.

More about this in the guide to long-distance cycle paths in Germany.

### Planning with map and app

Cycling maps on a scale of 1:50,000 to 1:100,000, cycle route planners on the Internet and navigation apps are suitable for detailed planning. When planning digitally, pay attention to:

- **Path condition:** Asphalt, gravel or forest path? Long gravel sections are strenuous on a heavily loaded trekking bike, but often impossible to ride on a racing bike.
- **Traffic:** Many apps show whether a route takes place on busy roads. Prefer quiet paths.
- **Elevation profile:** Where are the steepest climbs? Can they be avoided?
- **Offline maps:** Download the maps for the region in advance, as reception is often poor in rural areas.
- **GPX file:** Export the route as a GPX file to use on a bike satnav or other app.

### Entry and exit points

Plan how you will get to the starting point and back from the finish. The train is ideal for cycling tours, but it is not possible to take bikes everywhere and must be reserved for long-distance transport. You can find out more about this in the guide to taking your bike on the train.

## Step 4: The best time to travel

In Germany the cycling season is roughly from April to October. Every season has its advantages:

- **Spring:** Blooming landscapes, pleasant temperatures, less activity. But often changeable weather and cool nights.
- **Summer:** Long days and warm weather, but popular cycle paths are busy, fully booked accommodation and sometimes very hot.
- **Autumn:** Quieter paths, beautiful colors and grape harvest in the wine regions. But the days are getting shorter.

If you are traveling during the summer holidays, you should reserve accommodation early.

## Step 5: The right bike and equipment

For most tours, a **trekking bike** or a well-equipped **pedelec** is ideal: luggage rack, mudguards, light, comfortable seating position and a wide range of gears. It is important that the bike is well maintained and fits. A tour is not the right time to try out a new bike or saddle for the first time.

### Before the tour: the check

- Check brakes, gears and chain, replace chain and brake pads if necessary.
- Install tires with good tread and puncture protection.
- Test the light and battery.
- Adjust the saddle and handlebars and test the settings on a longer test ride.
- If there are major defects, bring the bike to the workshop in good time, not just before you leave.

### The packing list

| area | What should with |
|---|---|
| Tools and repairs | Spare tube, repair kit, tire lever, pump, multi-tool, chain lock, cable ties |
| Clothing | Rain jacket, rain pants, functional shirts, cycling shorts, warm layer, clothing for the evening |
| Protection | Helmet, sunglasses, sunscreen, gloves |
| Electronics | Smartphone, charger, power bank, possibly bike navigation system, charger for the Pedelec |
| Hygiene and health | Toiletry bag, small first aid kit, seat cream, personal medication |
| Documents | ID card, bank card, health insurance card, reservations |
| Security | good lock, bike pass with frame number |
| Catering | Drinking bottles, snacks for on the go |

Pack heavy things low and as close to the center of the wheel as possible. More about this in the guide to cycling trips with luggage.

## Step 6: Accommodations

Depending on your budget and preferences, there are many options:

- **Bicycle-friendly guest establishments:** Many hotels, guesthouses and holiday apartments are geared towards cyclists, with lockable bike rooms, drying rooms, tools and a good breakfast. Pay attention to appropriate labels.
- **Youth hostels:** cheap, often with bicycle storage.
- **Campsites:** flexible and cheap, but more luggage.
- **Private hosts:** Networks for cyclists arrange overnight stays with private individuals.

In high season you should reserve in advance on popular routes. In the low season you can be more flexible and book spontaneously, but check whether the accommodation is open.

## Step 7: Food and drink

Cycling uses a lot of energy. On a long tour you can burn several thousand kilocalories a day.

- **Drink regularly:** About half a liter to a liter per hour, more on hot days. Don't drink until you're thirsty.
- **Eat regularly:** Small snacks every one to two hours prevent a drop in performance. Bananas, muesli bars, nuts and sandwiches are tried and tested.
- **Plan a stop:** In rural areas, cafes and shops are sometimes far apart or closed on certain days. Check where you can shop and take enough provisions with you.

## Step 8: Weather and Plan B

The weather is the biggest uncertainty factor. Check the forecast before each stage, especially for thunderstorms, storms and heat.

- **If it rains:** No problem with good rain gear, but plan shorter stages.
- **In the heat:** Start early, take a break at lunchtime, drink plenty of water, and don't forget sun protection.
- **During thunderstorms:** Do not drive, seek shelter.
- **Headwind:** Can make a stage significantly more strenuous. Sometimes it is worth choosing the direction of the tour based on the prevailing wind direction. In northern Germany the wind often blows from the west.

For each day, you should know where the nearest train station is in case you need to take a shortcut, for example due to a breakdown, injury or bad weather.

## Step 9: Safety and Emergencies

- **Leave route:** Share your route and planned stages with someone at home.
- **First Aid:** A small first aid kit belongs in your luggage.
- **Emergency call:** You can reach the emergency services throughout Europe on 112.
- **Breakdowns:** Practice changing a tube before the tour. More about this in the guide to breakdowns on the road.
- **Insurance:** Check whether your insurance covers theft and accidents while on holiday.

## Plan as a couple or in a group

Anyone traveling with a partner, family or friends plans for the weakest member of the group. This sounds obvious, but is often forgotten. Before the tour, talk openly about expectations: How many kilometers a day are comfortable for everyone? How important are sightseeing, swimming breaks or a good dinner? Who takes care of navigation, repairs and bookings?

- **Pace:** The slowest person drives in front or sets the pace so that the group doesn't break up.
- **Share equipment:** Not everyone has to take tools, pumps and first aid kits with them. Distributes the weight.
- **Meeting points:** If the group splits up, agree on a meeting point and time in advance.
- **Different bikes:** If one rides with a Pedelec and one without, the speed uphill is very similar. This can keep a group together that wouldn't otherwise be able to ride together.

## Example: Planning a four-day river tour

To make the steps tangible, here is an example of a beginner's tour:

1. **Type of tour:** Route tour on a river cycle path, four days, return journey by train.
2. **Stages:** 50 to 60 kilometers per day on flat terrain, total distance around 220 kilometers.
3. **Route:** Section of a signposted long-distance cycle path, GPX file from the Internet, saved offline.
4. **Accommodation:** Three nights in bike-friendly guesthouses, booked in advance.
5. **Arrival and return journey:** Regional trains with bicycles included, bicycle tickets purchased in advance.
6. **Equipment:** two panniers, handlebar bag, repair kit, rain gear.
7. **Plan B:** Stations noted along the route.

This planning leaves enough time for breaks, sightseeing and spontaneous detours.

## Conclusion

A good bike tour begins with realistic planning: choose a tour that suits your experience, plan daily stages that are too short rather than too long and take altitude and road conditions into account. Use signposted cycle routes, plan with a map and app and download the maps offline. Prepare your bike thoroughly, pack only what you need, book accommodation in advance in high season and keep an eye on the weather and train connections as a plan B. Then the tour remains what it should be: relaxation, adventure and the joy of cycling.
