The battery is the most expensive wearing part of a Pedelec. Depending on the manufacturer and capacity, a new battery often costs several hundred euros, or significantly more for large batteries. At the same time, its condition determines how far you get and how much fun the bike is. The good news: Modern lithium-ion batteries are robust, and with a few simple habits you can noticeably extend their lifespan.

In this guide we explain how a Pedelec battery ages, how to charge it correctly, how to store it in everyday life and over the winter, how to protect it from heat in summer and when a battery should be replaced. And we're talking about safety, because lithium-ion batteries require careful handling.

## How a battery ages

Pedelec batteries consist of many individual lithium-ion cells that are connected into a package and monitored by electronics, the battery management system. Over time, the cells lose capacity. This has two reasons:

- **Cyclic aging:** Each charge and discharge puts a little strain on the cells. Manufacturers often state that a battery still has a large portion of its original capacity after several hundred to over a thousand full charging cycles. A full cycle corresponds to being completely empty and full again; two half charges together make up one cycle.
- **Calendaral aging:** Even a battery that is rarely used ages over time. High temperatures and a permanently very full or very empty state of charge accelerate this.

So you can't stop the battery from aging, but you can slow it down significantly.

## Load correctly

### Use the original charger

Only charge the battery with the charger that the manufacturer provides for your system. Third-party chargers can deliver an incorrect voltage, disrupt communication with the battery management or, in the worst case, cause a fire. Replace a damaged charger or cable immediately.

### Charge at room temperature

Lithium-ion cells should be charged at moderate temperatures, around 10 to 25 degrees Celsius is ideal. If you have driven outside in the winter, let the battery warm up in the house for a while after the journey before charging it. Charging in frosty conditions can permanently damage the cells. Direct sunlight and proximity to heating should also be avoided when charging.

### Not always 100 percent

A battery that sits around fully charged ages faster. For everyday life this means:

- Fully charge the battery when you need the range the next day.
- If you only drive short distances, you don't have to charge it to the limit after every trip. A range between around 20 and 80 percent is particularly gentle on the cells.
- Don't leave a fully charged battery sitting for weeks.

Some systems offer a charging mode that stops at 80 or 90 percent. If your system can do that, it's worth using it in everyday life.

### Don't drive completely empty

A completely empty battery is stressful for the cells. The battery management protects against real deep discharge by switching off in a timely manner, but an empty battery should be recharged as soon as possible. Never leave it empty for a long time, especially in winter.

### Store with supervision and in a safe place

Charge the battery in a dry place on a non-flammable surface, preferably not unattended at night and not in the hallway, which serves as an escape route in an emergency. A smoke detector in the room where you are charging is a good idea. After charging, unplug the charger.

## Caring for everyday life

- **Keep contacts clean:** The contacts on the battery and holder should be dry and clean. Clean with a dry, soft cloth, do not use harsh cleaning agents.
- **Do not clean with water jet:** When washing the bike, remove the battery and do not spray the contacts directly.
- **Avoid Impact:** Do not drop the battery. Even if nothing is visible on the outside, cells can be damaged.
- **Lock:** Many batteries are secured with a lock on the frame. Keep the key and key number safe.
- **Keep software up to date:** During the inspection, the workshop can install updates for the engine and battery, which sometimes also improve charging management.

## heat in summer

Heat is particularly damaging to lithium-ion batteries. In summer the following applies:

- Do not leave the bike in the blazing sun for hours. A black battery in the midday sun can get very hot.
- Do not leave the battery in a hot car, for example while on a trip.
- Do not charge the battery immediately after a long mountain ride when it is warm, but let it cool down first.
- When transporting it on a bike rack in the car, remove the battery and transport it in the interior. This protects against heat, rain and wind and relieves the wearer.

## Cold in winter

Cold does less permanent damage to the battery than heat, but it does temporarily reduce the usable capacity. In sub-zero temperatures the range can decrease significantly. Therefore:

- Keep the battery indoors and only use it shortly before driving.
- Only charge it when it is at room temperature.
- There are insulating thermal covers for long winter journeys.
- Plan for a larger reserve in cold weather.

## Store correctly

If the Pedelec is not ridden for several weeks, for example over the winter:

1. **Remove the battery** and store it separately from the bike.
2. **Charge to approximately 30 to 60 percent.** Most manufacturers recommend a medium charge level. Some ads show two or three bars out of five.
3. **Store in a cool and dry place**, ideally at around 10 to 20 degrees Celsius, not in a frosty shed, not in a damp cellar and not next to the radiator.
4. **Check every few weeks.** If the charge level drops significantly, recharge it to the middle range.
5. **Charge fully before the season** and take a short test drive.

## When is the battery used up?

A battery is not suddenly broken, but rather gradually loses capacity. Signs are:

- The range drops noticeably under the same conditions.
- The charge indicator quickly jumps from full to half.
- The system suddenly switches off under heavy loads, such as on inclines, even though the remaining charge is still displayed.

Many workshops can read the battery using diagnostic software and display the health status, the number of charging cycles and the remaining capacity. This is also very helpful before buying a used Pedelec.

Whether an exchange is necessary depends on your routes. If the remaining range is still sufficient for your everyday life, you can continue driving with the battery. Only when he lets you down will a new one be due.

## Safety when handling lithium-ion batteries

Lithium-ion batteries are safe for normal use. However, if they are damaged, loaded incorrectly or exposed to high heat, they can catch fire and such fires are difficult to put out. Therefore note:

- **Do not use damaged batteries.** A battery that has been dropped, is cracked or deformed, is swelling, is unusually hot, hissing or smells should no longer be charged. Have it checked by a specialist workshop.
- **Never open or repair.** This also applies to “cell exchange” offers without manufacturer approval. Improperly repaired batteries pose a fire risk.
- **No cheap batteries of unknown origin.** Replica batteries without a traceable test and without a fit with the battery management of your system are a risk.
- **In an emergency:** If a battery smokes or burns, leave the room, close the door and call the fire department at 112. Do not try to pick up a burning battery.

## Dispose of battery

Old Pedelec batteries do not belong in household waste. Dealers who sell batteries take back old batteries, as do recycling centers. Before transport, cover the contacts with adhesive tape to avoid short circuits. Damaged batteries should only be transported after consulting the collection point.

## Myths about batteries

- **“You have to run new batteries completely empty several times.”** This applied to old nickel batteries. Lithium-ion batteries do not require “break-in”.
- **“A battery must always be full.”** A medium charge level is better for longevity, and it is worth charging fully before long journeys.
- **“Charging briefly in between is harmful.”** No. Lithium-ion batteries have no memory effect. Charging in between is no problem.
- **“A battery only lasts two years.”** If cared for properly, batteries often last significantly longer. The decisive factors are temperature, charging behavior and usage.

## Short care plan

| When | What |
|---|---|
| after every trip | Charge when necessary, but not when cold or hot |
| when washing | Remove battery, keep contacts dry |
| in summer | Don't leave your bike in the blazing sun and don't leave the battery in a hot car |
| in winter | Store the battery indoors and only charge it at room temperature |
| during a longer break | Charge to 30-60%, store in a cool, dry place, check monthly |
| during the inspection | Have the status read out, update the software |

## Charging times and chargers

How long charging takes depends on the battery size and the power of the charger. With a standard charger, a full charge takes approximately three to six hours, depending on the system. Fast chargers shorten the time, but heat the battery a little more. A normal charger is gentler for everyday use. The first 80 percent usually charge faster than the last 20 because the battery management charges more carefully at the end.

## Buying a replacement battery: what to pay attention to?

If a new battery is due, buy it as an original part from the manufacturer of your motor system or from a specialist dealer. Battery, motor and display communicate with each other, and only suitable batteries are recognized by the system and charged safely. For older bikes, it's worth checking whether original batteries are still available. Some manufacturers also offer professional overhaul. Dubious replicas without a test mark are not a good idea.

## Conclusion

A Pedelec battery ages, but it's up to you how quickly. Charge with the original charger at room temperature, avoid permanently full and empty states, protect the battery from heat and frost and store it half full in a cool, dry place during longer breaks. Handle damaged batteries carefully and, if in doubt, have the workshop check them. This means your tailwind will remain reliable for many years.
