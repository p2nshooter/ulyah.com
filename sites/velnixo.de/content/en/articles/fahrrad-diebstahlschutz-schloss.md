In Germany, around a quarter of a million bicycle thefts are reported every year, and only around one in ten cases are solved. The number of unreported cases is high because many thefts are not even reported. High-quality bikes and Pedelecs that are easy to resell are particularly popular. There is no such thing as 100% protection, but you can make it so difficult for thieves that they move on.

In this guide you will find out which locks are really useful, how to connect them correctly, which additional measures make sense and what you should do if the bike is still missing.

## How thieves operate

If you know how thieves work, you can protect your bike better. Most thefts follow a few patterns:

- **Occasion:** A bike that is unlocked or only secured with a thin cable lock is gone in seconds.
- **Tool:** Even stronger locks can be cracked with bolt cutters, saws or battery-operated cut-off machines, but this takes time and makes noise.
- **Carry away:** A bike that is only connected to itself is simply loaded into a transporter and later opened at leisure.
- **Parts:** Quick releases make it easy to take wheels or saddle with you.

The goal is therefore to force as much time, effort and attention as possible. The longer a theft lasts, the greater the risk for the thief.

## Lock types in comparison

| Lock type | Security | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cable lock | low | light, cheap, flexible | cut through in seconds with side cutters |
| Spiral cable lock | low | handy | hardly better than a simple cable lock |
| Frame lock (ring lock) | medium | firmly on the bike, quickly served | only secures the rear wheel, the wheel can be carried away |
| folding lock | medium to high | compact, good mounting on the frame | heavy depending on the model, joints are weak points |
| U-lock | high | very stable, good ratio between weight and safety | rigid, does not fit every object |
| Chain lock | high | flexible, good for connecting to lanterns and brackets | heavy, good chains weigh several kilograms |

For everyday use, many experts recommend a combination: a stable **U-lock, folding or chain lock** for connecting to a fixed object and a **frame lock** for short stops. Many frame locks can be expanded with a plug-in chain that uses the same lock.

### How you recognize a good lock

- **Material:** Hardened steel in bracket, chain or plates. For chains, the links should be at least eight to ten millimeters thick.
- **Locking cylinder:** A high-quality cylinder is protected against drilling and unlocking.
- **Test mark:** Manufacturers use their own security levels that are only comparable within one brand. Independent tests, such as locks recognized by insurers, provide better guidance.
- **Rule of thumb:** You should budget around ten percent of the bike's value for the lock, or more for very expensive bikes.

## Connect correctly

The best lock is of little use if it is used incorrectly. Follow these rules:

1. **Always attach to a solid object.** A firmly anchored bike bar, railing or lantern. Make sure the lock cannot easily be lifted over the object, such as short posts or loose signs.
2. **Connect the frame.** The lock must go through the frame, not just a wheel. A wheel with a quick release is quickly released and the rest of the wheel is then gone.
3. **Leave little room for maneuver.** The less space there is in the bracket or chain, the harder it is to attach tools. The lock should not be on the floor as it is easier to pry open or break.
4. **Park in a clearly visible place.** Busy, lit places with lots of passers-by are better than dark backyards.
5. **Two locks of different designs.** If you have to crack two different locks, you need two different tools and more time.

### Store safely at home

Many bikes are stolen from basements, hallways and garages. Here, too, you should connect the bike to a solid object. A wall or floor anchor is a sensible investment. A closed basement room is safer than a communal basement to which many people have access.

## Secure parts

- **Replace quick release:** Special safety axles or screws that can only be opened with a suitable key protect wheels and seat post.
- **Remove the battery:** If possible, take the battery with the Pedelec if the bike is stationary for a longer period of time. Without a battery, the bike is less attractive to thieves, and the battery itself is expensive.
- **Take the display with you:** Removable displays and control units should also not remain on the bike.
- **Accessories:** Take lamps, bags and navigation devices with you.

## Coding, bike pass and tracker

Most stolen bikes are never returned because the owners cannot be identified. Simple measures can help here:

- **Note the frame number:** Every bike has a number stamped on it, usually under the bottom bracket. Write them down, take photos of them and keep the purchase invoice.
- **Bicycle passport:** A bicycle passport contains frame number, make, model, color, special features and photos. It is available as a form or app, for example from the police.
- **Coding:** Many police departments and bicycle associations offer coding. A number is engraved into the frame or applied as a forgery-proof sticker from which the owner can be identified. A visible sticker is an additional deterrent.
- **Tracker:** GPS trackers or small Bluetooth trackers, well hidden in the frame or under the saddle, can help find a stolen bike. On some Pedelecs, a tracking module is already installed or can be retrofitted. A tracker does not replace a good lock, but it increases the chance of getting the bike back.

## Insurance: household contents or bicycle insurance

A stolen bicycle is only insured under household contents insurance if the contract includes bicycle theft. In many contracts, this must be agreed upon separately and the compensation is often limited to a percentage of the insured sum. Older contracts sometimes contain a night time clause, according to which a bike is only insured at night in locked rooms.

For expensive bikes and Pedelecs, it often makes more sense to have your own **bicycle insurance**. It usually covers the new value, but often also parts theft, vandalism and damage to the battery. Read the conditions carefully: Many insurers require a lock with a certain minimum price or security level and that the bike has been locked to a fixed object.

## What to do after a theft?

1. **Report it to the police.** Take the frame number, bike passport, photos and proof of purchase with you. Many federal states offer online monitoring.
2. **Inform insurance.** Report the theft quickly, often within a few days, and submit the notification confirmation. Keep the lock key; the insurer may request it.
3. **Check tracker.** If you have a tracker, check where the bike is and provide the information to the police. Don't get the bike back on your own.
4. **Watch online marketplaces.** Stolen bikes sometimes appear on classified ad portals. If you suspect anything, inform the police.
5. **Report on social media.** Local groups and theft registries help keep many people's eyes open for bikes.

## Don't become a fence when buying used

Anyone who buys a stolen bike runs the risk of having to give it back without compensation. Therefore, when buying a used bike, make sure you have an invoice, a traceable origin and a realistic price. A very cheap bike, a seller with no documentation or a worn area of ​​the frame are warning signs. Find out more in our checklist for buying used.

## At the train station, while traveling and at events

Train stations, city centers and events are places where a particularly large number of bikes are stolen. Additional precautions are worthwhile here:

- **Bicycle parking garages and boxes:** Many cities and train stations offer guarded bicycle stations or lockable bicycle boxes. They usually only cost a little per day or month and are the safest solution for commuters with expensive bikes.
- **Not always in the same place:** If you park at the same place at the same time every day, it makes it easy for thieves to watch the bike and wait for the right moment.
- **On bike trips:** Ask accommodations for a lockable room for bikes. At campsites, lock the wheels together and to a solid object.
- **For events:** Use guarded bicycle parking spaces if they are available. At large festivals, bikes on fences in side streets are particularly at risk.

## Maintain the castle

A lock that gets stuck is often not being used properly. A few simple steps keep it reliable:

- Spray the lock cylinder with a graphite or lock spray two to three times a year, especially before winter. Normal oil attracts dirt.
- Wipe down the bracket and chain after riding in the rain or with road salt.
- Keep a spare key in a safe place and write down the key number. Many manufacturers use this to make replacement keys.
- For combination locks, check regularly whether the wheels run smoothly.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a frame lock alone enough?**
It can be enough for short stops within sight. If the bike is standing for a long time, it should also be connected to a solid object with a sturdy lock, because a bike with a frame lock can easily be carried away.

**Are combination locks safe?**
Good combination locks with several wheels can be sufficient in everyday life. However, many inexpensive models can be opened by touching them. For expensive bikes, locks with high-quality locking cylinders are the better choice.

**Does an alarm system help?**
Locks or trackers with an alarm alert you to an attempted theft and deter opportunist thieves. They are not a replacement for a mechanically strong lock.

**How much should I spend on a lock?**
As a guide, around ten percent of the bike's value applies. For a bike for 1,000 euros, that's around 100 euros, for a Pedelec for 3,500 euros, a lock set costs 150 to 250 euros. Also check what requirements your insurance company has for the lock.

## Conclusion

There is one thing that helps against theft: making it as difficult as possible for thieves. Use a sturdy U-lock, folding lock or chain lock, always lock the frame of the bike to a solid object and park it in busy, well-lit places. Add a frame lock to the main lock, secure the wheels and saddle, and take the battery and display with you. Write down the frame number, have the bike coded and check your insurance coverage. And if the bike is missing: file a report immediately, inform your insurance company and keep an eye on marketplaces.
