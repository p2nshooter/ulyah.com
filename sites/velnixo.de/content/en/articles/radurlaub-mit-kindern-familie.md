A cycling holiday with children is different than a tour with adults. The daily stages are shorter, the breaks are longer, and the playground by the river can be more important than the attraction in the next town. That's exactly what makes it so appealing: children experience the landscape at their own pace, are in the fresh air, and the family spends the days together instead of in the car.

In order for the vacation to be enjoyable for everyone, it needs to be planned differently than an adult tour. This guide shows which stages are realistic at which age, how small children can travel, which routes are suitable and what should be packed in your luggage.

## How far do children get?

How far a family travels per day depends on the children's age, practice and, above all, their desire. As a rough guide for flat routes:

| Age and situation | Realistic daily route |
|---|---|
| Baby or toddler in a trailer or child seat | 20 to 40 km, with lots of breaks |
| Child on his own bike, 6 to 8 years | 10 to 20 km |
| Child on his own bike, 8 to 10 years | 20 to 35 km |
| Child from around 10 years old with practice | 30 to 50 km |
| teenagers | similar to adults when they feel like it |

These values are guidelines. Some children continue riding with enthusiasm, others have had enough after ten kilometers. What is crucial is that the stage can be completed without any time pressure. It's better to plan too little than too much, and leave room for a day without cycling.

## Riding along like little children

There are several options for children who are not yet driving themselves:

- **Bicycle trailer:** The child sits protected from wind, sun and rain, can sleep and has space for toys. Trailers are the most convenient solution for long tours, but they make the trailer longer and heavier.
- **Child seat:** Light and uncomplicated, good for short journeys. On long journeys it becomes uncomfortable for the child and it is difficult for him to sleep in the seat.
- **Cargo bike:** Offers space for one or two children and luggage, with motor support even for hilly routes.
- **Tandem pole or trailer:** For children aged four and over who want to ride along but can't ride alone for long. The children's bike or a trailing bike is connected to the adult bike.

## Choose the right route

Different criteria count for families than for sporty cyclists:

- **Car-free or low-traffic:** River cycle paths, railway cycle paths and paths through parks and fields are ideal. You should avoid long stretches on roads with car traffic.
- **Flat:** Inclines are strenuous for children on their own bikes and difficult for the team with trailer.
- **Good paths:** Asphalt or solid gravel paths. Coarse gravel and sandy paths are difficult with children's bikes.
- **Variety:** Playgrounds, swimming areas, zoos, castles, ice cream parlors and farms along the route make every stage an experience.
- **Train nearby:** Train stations along the route allow for a shortcut if your legs are tired or the weather turns bad.

Many river cycle paths in Germany are suitable, such as sections on the Elbe, Weser, Main or Moselle. More about this in the guide to long-distance cycle paths in Germany.

### Rally instead of a route tour

For families with small children, a **star trip** is often more relaxed: you stay in the same accommodation for several nights and go on a tour every day without luggage. This eliminates the need for daily packing and you can spontaneously decide whether today is cycling day or swimming day.

## Accommodation for families

- **Holiday apartments and farms:** lots of space, a kitchen and often animals to touch.
- **Youth hostels:** cheap, family-friendly, often with bicycle storage and play facilities.
- **Campsites:** Kids love them, but tent and gear mean a lot of luggage. Easily doable with a trailer or cargo bike.
- **Bicycle-friendly restaurants:** lockable room for bikes and trailers, drying room, often a good breakfast.

Book early during the holidays and ask if the trailer can be stored safely.

## Breaks: the most important part of the day

Children need regular breaks, much more often than adults:

- **A short break** approximately every 30 to 60 minutes, with a drink and a snack.
- **A long lunch break** with playing, bathing or sleeping, especially on hot days.
- **Plan breaks in exciting places:** a playground, a bridge to throw stones into the water, a lookout tower.
- **Set small goals:** “Until the next bridge” motivates more than “15 kilometers left”.

## Safety on the go

- **Helmet for everyone:** Children are more likely to wear them if adults do too.
- **Order in the group:** One adult rides in the front, one in the back, the children in between. With only one adult driving behind the children to keep an eye on them.
- **Clear commands:** Previously agreed upon words such as “Stop” or “Get to the right”.
- **Visibility:** Pennants on the children's bike and trailer, bright clothing and working lights.
- **Sun protection:** Children in the trailer sweat quickly in the sun. Sun roof, ventilation and enough to drink are important.
- **Check the bike:** The children's bike must be technically in order, especially the brakes.

## Packing list for the family tour

| area | What should with |
|---|---|
| For the children | A change of clothes, rain jacket and rain pants, hat and sun hat, favorite cuddly toy, small toy or book |
| Food and drink | Plenty of water, snacks such as fruit, cookies and sandwiches, the usual food for babies |
| Health | Sunscreen, plasters, disinfection, tick tweezers, personal medication, thermometer |
| tool | Replacement tubes for all wheel sizes, pump with matching valves, multitool |
| Comfort | Seat cushion for the trailer, blanket for breaks, wet wipes, garbage bags |
| Entertainment | Songs, puzzles, search games on the go |

Make sure that the replacement tubes fit all wheels: children's bikes and trailers often have different tire sizes and valves than adult bikes.

## Get the children's bike ready for travel

A children's bike is subjected to more wear and tear on a multi-day tour than on the way to school. It's worth checking before you travel:

- **Appropriate size:** The child must get on and off safely and reach the floor with the tips of their feet while sitting. A bike that is too big causes fatigue and insecurity.
- **Gear shifting:** A few gears are a big help for longer distances, especially on slight inclines and in headwinds.
- **Brakes:** The levers must be easy for children's hands to reach and pull firmly.
- **Luggage:** A light luggage rack or a small handlebar bag is sufficient. Parents carry heavy things.
- **Bell and light:** Both work, even if you only drive during the day.

## Involve children

Children enjoy it more when they are allowed to help plan:

- Look at the map together and select destinations, such as a zoo or a swimming area.
- Older children can take over the navigation or read the signposts.
- A small travel diary with photos, collected sheets or stamps from the trip.
- A separate small saddle bag for the children's bike in which the child can pack their belongings.

## Pedelecs and cargo bikes: tailwind for parents

A trailer with two children and luggage can easily weigh 50 kilograms. On flat stretches this can be done with practice, but with headwinds or slight inclines it becomes difficult. A Pedelec or an electric cargo bike takes a lot of the burden off parents, and the pace still remains suitable for children. Pay attention to the battery range with a full charge, which can be significantly less than the manufacturer's specifications, and plan charging options in the accommodation. For children on their own wheels, the parents' Pedelec doesn't change anything: they set the pace.

## When the mood changes

Even with the best planning, there are moments when a child no longer wants to. Then it helps:

- **Take a break immediately:** Hunger, thirst and tiredness are the most common reasons for a bad mood. A snack often works wonders.
- **Include something exciting:** A race to the next tree, a search game for red cars or cows in the pasture.
- **Relieve the child:** A route in the trailer or on the tandem bar, even if the child is already driving.
- **Short the stage:** Taking the train to your accommodation is not a failure. The holiday should be enjoyable for everyone.
- **Praise instead of pressure:** Children are proud of every kilometer they complete. Looking at the map together in the evening to see how far you have come motivates you for the next day.

## Example: a long weekend by the river

This is what a first family cycling holiday could look like with a child in the trailer and an eight-year-old child on their own bike:

1. **Friday:** Arrival by train in the afternoon, short walk of ten kilometers to the accommodation, dinner and early to bed.
2. **Saturday:** 25 kilometers on the river cycle path with a playground break in the morning and a swimming area in the afternoon.
3. **Sunday:** 20 kilometers to a train station, lunch on the way, return trip with the regional train.

With this experience you know what your children like and can do, and the next tour can be a few days longer.

## Frequently asked questions

**At what age can a child ride in a trailer?**
Babies can ride in trailers with special baby seats or hammocks approved by the manufacturer. Without such use, the child should be able to sit safely on their own. Follow the trailer manufacturer's instructions.

**How many children are allowed to ride in the trailer?**
In Germany, up to two children up to the age of seven can be transported in a trailer built for this purpose if the driver is at least 16 years old.

**What if it rains?**
Children in the trailer stay dry under the rain cover. Rain jackets and rain pants are important for children on their own bikes. When it rains constantly, a day at a museum or a day at the swimming pool is often a better idea.

## Conclusion

A cycling holiday with children is successful if the planning is based on the children: short, flat stages on paths with little traffic, lots of breaks in exciting places and a nearby train station as plan B. Small children ride in a trailer, in a child seat or on a cargo bike, older ones on their own bike with a helmet and in a clear order in the group. A star trip with permanent accommodation takes the pressure out of your vacation. And if the children are allowed to join in the planning, the bike tour becomes a shared adventure that the whole family will remember for a long time.
