Many parents want to take their children with them on their bikes from an early age: to daycare, shopping or on a weekend trip. There are several solutions for this, and each has its strengths. A child seat is easy and quick to install, a trailer offers more protection and space, a cargo bike carries children and shopping at the same time, and a trailer allows older children to ride along.

This guide compares the options, explains the rules in Germany and shows what you should pay attention to when buying, installing and driving.

## What is allowed?

The road traffic regulations allow people aged 16 and over to take children up to the age of seven on a bicycle if there is a special seat and the child's feet cannot get caught in the spokes. Up to two children up to the age of seven may be carried in bicycle trailers that are designed to transport people, and only those aged 16 and over. These age limits do not apply to older children with disabilities.

In addition, the manufacturer's information applies: maximum weight of the child, minimum age and the permissible load of the bike, luggage rack and trailer.

## The options at a glance

| Solution | Age (approximately) | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Front child seat | approx. 9 months to 3 years, up to around 15 kg | Child sits in view, good balance | little space for pedaling, handlebars become narrower |
| Rear child seat | approx. 9 months to 6 years, up to around 22 kg | inexpensive, quickly assembled, widespread | Center of gravity high, child sleeps poorly, no weather protection |
| Bicycle trailer | from baby age with use, up to around 6 years | Weather protection, two children, space for luggage, child can sleep | long, takes up space, heavier |
| Cargo bike | from baby age with use up to primary school age | Children in view, lots of space, often with a hood | expensive, needs parking space |
| Trailing or tandem rod | approx. 4 to 8 years | Child joins in, learns traffic | only for children with a sense of balance |

The age information is a guideline. It is crucial that the child can sit safely and upright before riding in a child seat, usually from around nine months. For younger babies, there are trailers and cargo bikes with special baby seats or hammocks if the manufacturer approves them.

## Rear child seat

The child seat on the luggage rack or on the seat tube is the most common solution.

- **Mounting:** Seats are mounted either on the luggage rack, which must be sufficiently strong, or with a bracket on the seat tube. Check whether your bike is suitable for this, especially if you have a carbon or aluminum frame with thin tubes.
- **Safety:** A good seat has a harness, footrests with straps, a high backrest and a spoke protector.
- **Cover springs:** If the saddle has springs, children's fingers can get into them. A spring guard or a saddle without open springs prevents this.
- **Stand:** A sturdy two-legged stand helps enormously when getting in and out. A bike with a child in the seat must never be left on the stand unattended.

## Front child seat

Front seats attach to the head tube or top tube. The child sits between the driver's arms, faces forward and can have a conversation. The weight is close to the center of gravity, which improves balance. However, it will be tight for the driver's knees and the seat is only suitable for small children. Front seats are very popular for short journeys in the city.

## Bicycle trailer

A trailer is the most comfortable solution for longer journeys and bad weather.

- **Weather protection:** A rain and insect hood keeps out rain, wind and mosquitoes. Make sure you have good ventilation, it can get warm underneath in summer.
- **Safety:** A roll bar, five-point harnesses, a sturdy hitch, and a high flag for visibility are standard on good models.
- **Suspension:** Suspension makes the ride more comfortable for children, especially on cobblestones and dirt roads.
- **Versatility:** Many trailers can be converted into a stroller with an additional wheel or into a sleigh with skis.
- **Space:** A trailer needs space to park. Many models can be folded.

With a trailer you can drive longer and wider. Plan curves further, pay attention to bollards and circulation barriers on cycle paths and drive more slowly downhill because the trailer is pushing.

## Cargo bike

A cargo bike with a children's bench in the box combines many advantages: the children sit in front of you in view, there is space for bags, and a canopy protects against rain. However, cargo bikes are expensive and need a good parking space. More about this in the guide to the cargo bike.

## Trailer and tandem rod

For children who are already cycling themselves but cannot yet manage longer distances:

- **Trailer:** A bike with a rear wheel, saddle, handlebars and pedals that attaches to the adult bike. The child joins in as much as he can.
- **Tandem bar:** A bar that connects the child's bike to the adult bike so that the front wheel of the child's bike hangs in the air. At the destination, the children's bike is uncoupled and can be ridden independently.

Both solutions are suitable for family trips where the child sometimes wants to drive themselves.

## Transporting children on a Pedelec

A Pedelec makes riding with a child seat or trailer much easier, especially on inclines and when starting off with the additional weight. However, please note:

- **Permissible total weight:** Bike, rider, child, seat and luggage count together. For many Pedelecs the limit is between 130 and 160 kilograms, which is quickly reached with a child seat.
- **Manufacturer approval:** Not every Pedelec is approved for child seats or trailers, especially for frames with an integrated battery in the luggage rack. A look at the operating instructions clarifies this.
- **Start with feeling:** A low level of support when starting prevents the bike with the child from shooting off suddenly.
- **Battery out of the way:** The seat must not prevent the battery from being removed, otherwise charging will be laborious.

## What to pay attention to when buying?

- **Testing standard:** Child seats should be tested according to the European standard for child bicycle seats, trailers according to the standard for bicycle trailers. The information is on the product or in the instructions.
- **Does it fit the bike?** Check the luggage rack load, frame shape, axle type for the trailer hitch (quick release, thru axle, hub gear) and location on the head tube.
- **Grow with you:** Adjustable straps, footrests and backrests last longer.
- **Buy used:** Trailers in particular are often available well used. Check the frame, clutch, belts, tires and covering and ask about accidents.

## Travel safely with children

1. **Helmet for child and driver:** Small children in particular are at high risk of head injuries if they fall. A suitable children's helmet is strongly recommended, even in the trailer if the manufacturer specifies it.
2. **Always close the belts:** Even on short journeys.
3. **Drive slower and brake earlier:** The additional weight increases the braking distance.
4. **Choose quiet routes:** Low-traffic paths and cycle paths are more relaxed.
5. **Visibility:** Pennants on the trailer, lights and reflectors, bright clothing.
6. **Practicing without a child:** A sack of potatoes or water bottles in the seat or trailer is a good way to test the driving experience.

## Comfort for the child

A child who feels comfortable will be happy to go along and let the parents drive in peace. A few things make a big difference:

- **Clothing according to the weather:** In the child seat, the child hardly moves and cools down more quickly than the adult who is pedaling. In spring and autumn children need an extra layer, in winter they need a footmuff, gloves and a hat under their helmet.
- **Sun protection:** A sun canopy helps in the trailer, and a helmet with an umbrella and sunscreen in the child seat.
- **Helmet and headrest:** Many child seats have a recess for the helmet so that the head is not pushed forward. Check whether the helmet and seat fit together.
- **Activity:** A small cuddly toy or a favorite book in the trailer, songs and conversations in the child seat in the front.
- **Breaks:** Young children need a break every 30 to 45 minutes to move, drink and eat.

## Adjust the seat correctly

A well-adjusted seat is safer and more comfortable. Adjust the footrests so that the child's thighs rest lightly and the feet are firmly in the shells. The shoulder straps sit at shoulder height or just above and are so tight that a flat hand can just fit between them. As the child grows, you should adjust the belts and footrests, preferably before the first ride after a long break, such as after the winter.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I take two children on one bike?**
This is generally possible with a child seat at the front and one at the back, provided both seats are suitable and the bike is approved for this. However, this makes the bike heavy and difficult to control. For two children, a trailer or cargo bike is usually the safer choice.

**When is a baby allowed to travel?**
Only use a child seat when the child can safely sit upright. Even earlier in trailers and cargo bikes with a baby seat or hammock approved by the manufacturer. Many pediatricians recommend waiting until the child can hold his head well before taking longer trips.

**Does every trailer fit every bike?**
Most trailers have adapters for different axles. A special clutch is often required for thru-axles, hub gears or Pedelecs with a rear wheel motor. Before you buy, check whether there is a suitable solution for your bike.

## Conclusion

For short journeys with small children, a child seat at the back or front is a simple and inexpensive solution. For longer journeys, bad weather or two children, a trailer is more comfortable and safer. A cargo bike is worthwhile for families who transport children and shopping every day, and a trailer or tandem bar accompanies the transition to their own bike. Pay attention to tested products that fit your bike, adhere to the legal age limits and manufacturer's instructions and ride with a child with particular foresight.
