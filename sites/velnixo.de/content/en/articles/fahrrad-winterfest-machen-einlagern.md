When the days get shorter and the first road salt is on the roads, cyclists are faced with a decision: keep riding or store their bikes until spring? Both are completely fine. The only important thing is that the bike is well prepared in one way or another. Cold, wet and especially salt take a toll on metal, storage and electronics. A bike that spends the winter unprotected in a damp yard or is left wet in the cellar after every salt trip often looks sad in March.

In this guide you will find both ways: how to get your bike ready for winter riding and how to store it properly when it takes a break. There is also a separate section on e-bike batteries, which needs special attention in winter.

## Way 1: Continue driving in winter

If you ride in the cold, you need a reliable, clearly visible and well-maintained bike. The following steps are best completed in late autumn, before the first frost comes.

### Thorough fall inspection

Go through the bike completely or have it inspected in the workshop. Particularly important are:

- **Brakes:** Pads with enough remaining strength, clean braking surfaces, strong pressure point. In winter, braking distances are longer on wet and slippery roads.
- **Light:** Headlights, taillights and reflectors must be working and clean. In winter you often drive in the dark.
- **Chain and gears:** Clean and well lubricated to prevent water and salt from entering the joints.
- **Tires:** Sufficient tread and intact sidewalls.
- **Mudguards:** They keep splashes of water and salt away from the drivetrain, frame and your clothing. If you don't have one, you should think about retrofitting it.

### The right chain oil

Switch to a **wet lubricant** for the winter. It sticks better and is not washed away as quickly by rain and slush. Oil the chain more often in winter, ideally after every ride in wet or salty conditions.

### Tires for slippery conditions

For normal winter conditions with wet roads, good all-weather tires with a little more tread are sufficient. If you often drive on icy or snowy roads, you can install **studded tires**. They have small metal pins in the tread that provide grip on ice. On dry asphalt they are loud and roll harder, so they are particularly worthwhile in areas with long periods of frost. A slightly lower tire pressure within the permitted range increases the contact area and improves grip on snow.

### Salt protection for frames and components

Road salt is the biggest enemy of winter wheels. It attacks chains, screws, spokes, rims, suspension forks and bare metal parts.

- **Apply a protective layer:** Before winter, treat bare metal parts, screws and the frame with corrosion protection or a care spray. Important: not on brake discs, rim sides or brake pads.
- **Wash regularly:** After riding on salted roads, rinse the bike with lukewarm water, especially the drive, brakes and underside. Then dry and oil the chain.
- **Not in a warm apartment:** A sharp change in temperature from frosty outside to warm inside causes condensation to form. A cool, dry basement or garage is better.

### Increase visibility

In winter it is often dark in the afternoon and drivers can see worse with fogged up windows and drifting snow. Supplement the prescribed lighting with reflective clothing, reflective tape on arms and legs and possibly additional reflectors on pockets. Check regularly whether the headlight and taillight are clean.

More about riding in slippery conditions, the right clothing and riding technique can be found in the guide to cycling in winter.

## Way 2: Store the bike

If you don't ride in winter, the bike shouldn't just sit in a corner gathering dust. With good preparation, it will survive the break without any damage and will be ready for use straight away in the spring.

### Step 1: clean thoroughly

Dirt, salt and moisture should not remain on the bike for months.

1. Remove coarse dirt with a soft jet of water and a brush; do not use a high-pressure cleaner.
2. Wash the frame, wheels and drive with bicycle cleaner.
3. Thoroughly degrease and clean the chain, sprockets and chainrings.
4. Dry everything carefully, preferably in a warm, dry place for a day.

### Step 2: lubricate and protect

- **Oil the chain:** After cleaning, oil the chain well. A dry chain will quickly rust in a damp basement. For storage, you can leave a little more oil on the chain and wipe it off in the spring.
- **Protect bare parts:** Protect screws, spoke nipples and bare metal parts with a thin film of care spray, excluding braking surfaces.
- **Check cables:** A few drops of thin oil on the cable entrances of the casings will help prevent them from rusting.

### Step 3: Tires and Wheels

- **Inflate tires:** Inflate the tires to normal pressure. If a wheel sits on the ground for months with little air, pressure points can form and the rubber can crack.
- **It's best to hang:** It is ideal to hang the bike on a wall hook or place it in a stand so that the tires are not subjected to permanent stress. If it stands, check the pressure every few weeks.

### Step 4: choose the right storage location

The ideal place is **dry, cool, protected from frost and dark**:

- A dry basement, storage room or garage are well suited.
- Damp basements are problematic because rust and mold can develop. A dehumidifier or occasional ventilation helps.
- Direct sun and extreme heat damage tires, saddles and plastic parts.
- Do not store near chemicals, solvents or heating oil.
- If the bike must remain outside, use a breathable cover that does not allow moisture to accumulate and secure the bike securely.

### Step 5: Don't forget theft protection

Unfortunately, communal cellars and garages are often the target of bicycle thieves, especially in winter when bicycles are left unattended for a long time. Also lock your bike in the basement, preferably to a solid object. Write down the frame number and take current photos in case it is stolen.

## The e-bike battery in winter

Lithium-ion batteries do not like cold or heat. In winter, the battery needs special attention, regardless of whether you continue driving or take a break.

### If you drive in winter

- **Charge at room temperature:** Never charge the battery in frosty conditions. Take it into the apartment and charge it at around room temperature.
- **Insert warm:** Only insert the battery shortly before driving. A warm battery delivers more power and range.
- **Plan for range:** When it's cold, the usable capacity drops noticeably. Plan a buffer for winter routes.
- **Thermal sleeves:** For long journeys in very cold conditions, there are neoprene sleeves that insulate the battery.
- **Keep contacts dry:** Dry contacts and holder after driving in slush.

### When the e-bike is stored

- **Removing the battery:** Store the battery separately from the bike in a dry room.
- **Ideal temperature:** Around 10 to 20 degrees Celsius is good. Not in the ice-cold shed and not right next to the heater.
- **Charge level:** Store the battery half full, approximately between 30 and 60 percent. A fully charged or completely empty battery ages more quickly when stored for a long time.
- **Check occasionally:** Check the charge level every few weeks and recharge to about half capacity if necessary. A battery must not be deeply discharged.
- **Note manufacturer information:** Some manufacturers have their own recommendations for charging status and storage.
- **Fire protection:** Do not store the battery on flammable surfaces or near heat sources. A damaged, swollen or hot battery must not be charged and must be checked or disposed of properly.

We explain more about battery care in the e-bike battery guide.

## Spring cleaning: getting the bike fit again

When spring comes, the bike will quickly be ready again after being stored well:

1. Wipe off excess oil from the chain and re-oil the chain if necessary.
2. Check and adjust tire pressure.
3. Test brakes and clean the braking surfaces.
4. Test shifting through all gears.
5. Check lights and bell.
6. Check all important screws.
7. For Pedelecs, fully charge the battery and ride a short test lap.
8. Take a short, quiet test drive before heading out into traffic.

Many workshops have long waiting times in spring. If you would like an inspection, it is best to book it in winter.

## The most common mistakes in winter

- **Do not wash off the salt:** Salt eats into the chain, screws and bearings. Regular rinsing is the most important winter care.
- **High-pressure cleaner:** It pushes salty water into bearings and flushes out grease.
- **Charging the battery in frost:** This can permanently damage the battery.
- **Store the bike wet:** Moisture plus time results in rust.
- **Leave tires flat:** This damages tires and tubes.
- **Don't be seen:** Missing or dirty lighting is particularly dangerous in winter.

## Checklist to check off

**Before winter, if you continue:**

- Inspection: brakes, lights, chain, tires, mudguards
- Wet oil on the chain
- Corrosion protection on bare parts, leave out braking surfaces
- Tires with tread or studs depending on the region
- Reflective clothing and clean lamps
- Take the battery into your apartment to charge

**Before storage:**

- Thorough cleaning and drying
- Lubricate chain and cables
- Pump the tires to operating pressure and hang the wheel up if possible
- Dry, cool, frost-free storage location
- Connect the wheel, note the frame number
- Remove the battery and store half charged at room temperature

If you check off these points once a year, you will significantly extend the lifespan of your bike and save yourself trouble and money in the spring.

## Conclusion

Whether you're a winter cyclist or a fair-weather cyclist: with a little preparation, your bike will get through the cold season well. If you continue driving, rely on good brakes, bright lights, wet oil, mudguards and regular rinsing off of the salt. Anyone who stores them cleans, lubricates, inflates the tires and chooses a dry, cool place. And in both cases the e-bike battery should be kept warm, half charged and checked regularly. So the bike starts the new season in spring without any unpleasant surprises.
