In everyday life, almost everyone says “e-bike” when they mean a bicycle with an electric motor. However, there are clear legal and technical differences, and they have consequences: for your driving license, insurance, the requirement to wear a helmet and the question of whether you are allowed to ride on the cycle path. Anyone buying a bike with a motor should know these differences before making a decision.

In this guide we explain the three most important classes, i.e. Pedelec, S-Pedelec and E-Bike in the narrower sense, and what applies to each in Germany. You will also find out how the motor support works, what you should pay attention to when buying and why “tuning” a Pedelec is not a good idea.

**Note:** Laws and fines are subject to change. This guide summarizes the legal situation in a generally understandable manner and does not replace legal advice. The current legal texts and information from the responsible authorities are binding.

## The Pedelec: a bicycle with a tailwind

The Pedelec is by far the most common form of electric bicycle. The name stands for **Pedal Electric Cycle**. Three characteristics are typical:

1. **The motor only provides support when you pedal.** If you stop pedaling, the support stops.
2. **The assistance ends at 25 km/h.** You can drive faster, for example downhill or under your own power, but the motor will no longer help.
3. **The rated continuous power of the motor is a maximum of 250 watts.**

In addition, a Pedelec may have a **pushing or starting aid** that moves the bike up to 6 km/h without pedaling. This is useful for pushing a heavy bike up a ramp.

### What applies to Pedelecs

A Pedelec that adheres to these limits is legally a **bicycle**. This means:

- **No driving license**, no moped test certificate.
- **No insurance plate** and no liability liability like with motor vehicles. Private liability insurance is still highly recommended.
- **No helmet requirement.** A helmet still makes sense, especially because you can travel faster on average with Pedelecs.
- **No legal age limit.** Many experts still advise children to exercise restraint because Pedelecs are heavy and fast. Some manufacturers recommend a minimum age.
- **The same traffic rules as for bicycles.** Cycle paths with blue signs must be used, approved forest paths and field paths are permitted as for bicycles.

The vast majority of “e-bikes” in bike shops, cargo bike shops and rental stations are Pedelecs.

## The S-Pedelec: faster, but no longer a bicycle

The S-Pedelec, the “S” stands for fast, also only supports pedaling, but **up to 45 km/h**. The engine power can be significantly higher. Legally, an S-Pedelec is **no longer a bicycle, but a moped** in the European vehicle class L1e-B. This has far-reaching consequences:

- **Driving license:** At least a class AM driving license is required. It is included in the class B car driving license. If you don't have a driver's license, you have to get the AM class.
- **Minimum age:** usually 16 years, depending on the driving license class.
- **Insurance license plate:** An S-Pedelec requires motor vehicle liability insurance and an insurance license plate that is renewed every year.
- **Operating license:** The vehicle requires type approval. Many components such as tires, brakes, mirrors and lamps must be approved for this. Anyone who exchanges parts must ensure that they only use approved parts.
- **Helmet requirement:** It is mandatory for S-Pedelecs to wear a suitable protective helmet. There are special helmets designed for these speeds.
- **Bike paths:** S-Pedelecs generally belong on the road. You may only use cycle paths if an additional sign expressly allows this. Forest and field paths that are only open to bicycles are also not permitted for S-Pedelecs.
- **Rearview mirrors and license plate lights** are mandatory.

S-Pedelecs are particularly interesting for commuters who travel longer distances on country roads and want to keep up with the traffic there. They are usually impractical for the city and for leisure trips on cycle paths.

## The e-bike in the narrower sense: riding without pedaling

In the legal sense, an e-bike is an electric vehicle that also drives **without pedaling**, for example using a throttle grip or a rotary knob. Such vehicles are considered motor vehicles, such as light mopeds or mopeds. Depending on the type and top speed, you need an insurance number, an operating license and a moped test certificate or a driving license. In Germany they are rare compared to Pedelecs.

Be careful when importing: Bicycles with throttle grips or powerful engines are sometimes offered on the Internet, which are permitted in other countries but are not considered bicycles in Germany. Anyone who drives such a vehicle on the road without registration and insurance risks significant legal consequences.

## This is how motor support works

A Pedelec uses sensors to measure how you pedal and provides appropriate motor power.

- **Torque sensor:** It measures how hard you pedal. The more force you use, the more support the motor provides. This feels natural, like a strong tailwind.
- **Motion sensor (cadence sensor):** It only measures whether you pedal. Then the motor provides firm support depending on the level. This is cheaper, but sometimes feels a bit “pushy” and less refined.

You can select the **support level**, such as Eco, Tour, Sport or Turbo, using a control panel on the handlebars. At low levels the range is great, at high levels you can climb any mountain effortlessly, but you use more energy.

## Motor position: middle, front or rear

Where the engine is located affects the driving experience:

- **Mid-engine:** on the bottom bracket, most common today. It ensures a low center of gravity, balanced driving behavior and uses the gear shift.
- **Front engine:** in the front wheel hub, cheap, but the front wheel can spin on slippery surfaces. It can be combined with hub gears and coaster brakes.
- **Rear engine:** in the rear wheel hub, powerful and quiet, often on sporty and urban bikes. The rear wheel becomes heavy and removing it in the event of a breakdown is more complicated.

We explain the differences in detail in the guide to drive types.

## Battery and range coming soon

The battery stores energy, measured in watt hours. Typical batteries today have around 400 to 750 watt hours. How far you can get depends heavily on the support level, terrain, weight, wind, tire pressure and temperature. Roughly you can expect 40 to over 120 kilometers. More about this in the guides on range and battery care.

## Who is which bike worth it for?

- **Pedelec:** for almost everyone. Commuting up to around 20 kilometers, hilly areas, cargo bikes, free time, getting back into work after a long break, people who want to arrive without sweating.
- **S-Pedelec:** for commuters with long journeys on country roads who want to ride with the traffic and can live with insurance, compulsory helmet use and the absence of cycle paths.
- **E-Bike with throttle grip:** for special applications and people who cannot or do not want to pedal, with the requirements of a motor vehicle.

## Tuning: why you should stay away from it

“Tuning kits” are offered on the Internet with which the switch-off limit of a Pedelec can be raised from 25 to 30, 40 or more km/h. This is tempting, but a bad idea for many reasons:

- **The Pedelec loses its status as a bicycle.** Legally it becomes a motor vehicle that is driven without registration, insurance number and driving license. This can be prosecuted as a criminal offense.
- **Insurance coverage may not apply.** In the event of an accident with a tuned bike, the insurance company may refuse coverage and you may be personally liable for high damages.
- **Guarantee and warranty generally expire**.
- **The technology is not designed for this.** The brakes, tires, frame and fork of a Pedelec are designed for 25 km/h support. Higher speeds significantly increase the risk of accidents.
- **Manufacturers often detect manipulations** via the software and can lock the system.

If you want to travel faster, you should buy an approved S-Pedelec. VELNIXO generally does not provide instructions for manipulating engine systems.

## What you should pay attention to when buying

1. **Clarify class:** Pedelec or S-Pedelec? For most people, the Pedelec is the right choice.
2. **Motor position and sensor:** Mid-engine with torque sensor for a natural driving feel, other systems if price or hub gears are more important.
3. **Battery size:** to suit your routes, with some reserve for winter and aging.
4. **Brakes:** Hydraulic disc brakes are highly recommended for Pedelecs.
5. **Frame shape:** A low entry or trapezoidal frame makes it easier to get on and off a heavy bike.
6. **Weight:** Can you carry the bike if you need to lift it into the basement or onto a bike rack?
7. **Service:** Is there a repair shop near you that can service your engine system? How long are batteries and spare parts available?
8. **Theft protection:** A Pedelec is a popular target. Plan to have a good lock and possibly insurance.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I tow a child trailer with a Pedelec?** Yes, like with a normal bicycle, as long as the trailer is approved and the coupling fits the bike. Pay attention to the permissible trailer load of the bike and good brakes.

**Do I have to register a Pedelec with my insurance company?** There is no compulsory insurance. But check whether your private liability insurance includes Pedelecs and whether your household contents insurance insures the bike against theft, if necessary with an additional module.

**Does the alcohol limit for cars apply to Pedelecs?** The same alcohol limits apply to Pedelecs as to bicycles. However, the limits for motor vehicles apply to S-Pedelecs. In any case, it is safest not to drive at all after drinking alcohol.

**Can I take my Pedelec with me on buses and trains?** Yes on many local trains, often with a bike card. Batteries are generally allowed as long as they are securely attached to the bike. The rules of the transport companies differ, more about this in the guide to taking bikes on the train.

## Conclusion

Anyone who says “e-bike” almost always means a pedelec: a bicycle with motor support up to 25 km/h that can be ridden without a driver’s license, insurance number and helmet requirement. The S-Pedelec is faster, but legally it is a moped with all the responsibilities that go with it. E-bikes with a throttle grip are motor vehicles. If you know these differences, you will buy the right bike, ride legally and insured, and enjoy your tailwind at the touch of a button for a long time.
