Anyone who rides a bicycle is a full-fledged road user. This means rights, such as having sufficient distance when overtaking, but also responsibilities. Many rules for cyclists are in the Road Traffic Regulations (StVO), some in the Road Traffic Licensing Regulations (StVZO), which regulate lighting, for example. A lot has changed since the major amendment to the StVO in 2020: There are bicycle zones, a green arrow just for cyclists and fixed overtaking distances.

In this guide we explain the rules that most often play a role in everyday life. It is not a substitute for legal advice and individual fines are subject to change. Where details are important, it is worth taking a look at the current legal text.

## Cycle path or road: the obligation to use it

The most important question first: Do I have to use the bike path? The answer depends on the shield.

| Signage | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Blue sign with bicycle (sign 237) | Cycle path, use is mandatory. |
| Blue sign with pedestrians and bicycles on top of each other (sign 240) | Shared footpath and cycle path, use is mandatory, consideration for pedestrians. |
| Blue sign with pedestrians and bicycles next to each other, separated by a line (sign 241) | Separate footpath and cycle path, use is mandatory, everyone stays on their own side. |
| No blue sign, but visible cycle path on the right | May be used, but does not have to. You can also drive on the road. |
| Sidewalk with additional sign “Cycling free” | Can be driven on at walking speed; pedestrians have priority. |

Even with a blue sign, the obligation does not apply if the cycle path is not usable, for example because it is parked, icy or blocked by construction sites. You can then move onto the road, but carefully and with a view to the rear.

**Important:** You may only use cycle paths on the left side of the street if this is expressly permitted by a sign. Driving on the left on a cycle path without this clearance is one of the most common causes of accidents because drivers do not expect cyclists coming from the right at junctions.

## Protective lanes and cycle lanes

On many streets, areas for cyclists are only painted:

- **Cycle lanes** are separated from the road with a solid, wide line and are usually marked with the blue cycle path sign. Cyclists are required to use it; cars are not allowed to use it.
- **Protection strips** are marked with a dashed line. Cars are only allowed to drive over them when necessary, for example when there is oncoming traffic, without endangering cyclists. Stopping is prohibited on protective strips. Cyclists should use them because the right-hand driving requirement applies.

Keep enough distance from parked cars on both lanes. A suddenly opened car door, popularly known as "dooring", is a serious danger. A distance of around one meter from parked vehicles makes sense, even if you use the edge of the strip for this.

## Right-hand driving and side-by-side driving

Cyclists must generally ride as far to the right as possible, but not in the gutter. A safe distance from the curb and parked cars is expressly permitted.

Since 2020, it has been clearly regulated: **Driving next to each other is permitted if it does not hinder traffic.** On a quiet side road you are allowed to drive next to each other with your companion. If there is traffic behind you and you can't overtake, you should drive behind each other. Riding side by side is generally permitted in cycle streets and cycle zones.

Groups of more than 15 cyclists are allowed to form a closed group and ride two abreast on the road. This is relevant for group trips with the club.

## Traffic lights and right of way

At intersections, the same right-of-way rules apply to cyclists as to everyone else: right before left, unless otherwise regulated, and traffic signs and traffic lights have priority.

This order applies to traffic lights:

1. If there is a separate **bicycle traffic light** or a combined traffic light with a bicycle and pedestrian symbol, this applies.
2. If there are none, the **traffic light for traffic** applies, even if you are riding on a cycle path.
3. The pedestrian-only traffic light does not apply to cyclists unless you are pushing your bike.

Red light violations are also expensive for cyclists. The fine starts at around 60 euros and one point in Flensburg, with a longer red phase or danger, it becomes significantly more. Regardless of the money, running a red light is one of the most dangerous mistakes in city traffic.

### The green arrow for cyclists

Since 2020 there has been a green arrow that only applies to cyclists. It shows a small bicycle symbol. At such traffic lights you are allowed to turn right on red after stopping and making sure that no one is obstructed or endangered. The general green arrow for cars also applies to you when you are driving on the road, with the same obligation to stop.

## Turning: hand signals and indirect left turns

You must announce your turn well in advance and clearly. On a bicycle this is done with a hand signal. Extend your arm in the direction you want to turn and put it back on the handlebars just before you turn so that you can brake safely.

When turning left you have two options:

- **Direct:** Look over your shoulder, signal your hand, move to the middle of the lane in good time and turn like a car. This is easily possible on quiet streets.
- **Indirect:** You drive straight across the intersection to the opposite corner, stop there, turn and cross the street if the traffic light or traffic allows it. This is often safer on large intersections, and sometimes it is required by markings.

## One-way streets, pedestrian zones and sidewalks

- You are only allowed to drive on **one-way streets** in the opposite direction if there is an additional sign with a bicycle and the word “free” underneath the sign. Many cities have now opened their one-way streets to cyclists.
- **Pedestrian zones** are taboo for cyclists unless an additional sign allows passage. Then walking speed applies and pedestrians always have priority.
- **Pedestrians** adults are only allowed to use “cycle traffic free”. There are exceptions for children: they have to ride on the sidewalk until their eighth birthday and they are allowed to do so until their tenth. A supervisor aged 16 or over may accompany the child on the sidewalk.
- **Zebra crossing:** If you drive through a zebra crossing, you do not have the right of way like a pedestrian. If you want to use it, get off and push.

## Bicycle streets and bicycle zones

In a **bicycle street** (blue sign with a bicycle and the word “bicycle street”), bicycle traffic has priority. Other vehicles are only allowed in if an additional sign allows it, and a maximum of 30 km/h applies to everyone. Cyclists are allowed to ride next to each other and cars have to adjust.

The **bicycle zone** applies these rules to an entire area, such as a residential area. At the beginning there is a corresponding zone sign, at the end there is one that cancels the zone.

Attention: Even on a bicycle street, the rule is right before left, unless signs state otherwise. Priority when driving through does not automatically mean right of way at every junction.

## Overtaking and being overtaken

Since 2020, the minimum distance when overtaking cyclists has been set by law: **1.5 meters in urban areas and 2 meters outside of urban areas.** If a car cannot maintain this distance, it must stay behind you. This also applies to protective strips.

You can overtake slow vehicles and cars stuck in traffic on the right if there is enough space, but only at a moderate speed and with particular caution. It gets dangerous at intersections: you have no place to the right of a truck or bus that could turn. More about this in the blind spot guide.

For trucks over 3.5 tonnes turning right, walking speed applies within town. However, don't rely on the driver seeing you.

## Cell phone, headphones and driving hands-free

- **Mobile phone:** Holding your mobile phone in your hand while riding is also prohibited on a bicycle. The fine is around 55 euros and increases if you are at risk. A fixed holder for navigation is permitted as long as you only need to read the device briefly.
- **Headphones:** They are not expressly prohibited, but hearing must not be impaired. You must be able to hear the siren, bell and engine noise. An earphone with a low volume is a compromise, loud music in both ears is not.
- **Hands-free:** Driving hands-free is prohibited, as is trailering to other vehicles. Your feet belong on the pedals.

## Alcohol and bicycles

Alcohol is also dangerous on the bike and can be expensive:

- From **1.6 per mille** you are considered absolutely unfit to drive. This is a criminal offense and the driving license authority can order a medical and psychological examination, even if you were only riding a bicycle.
- You can face penalties from just **0.3 per mille** if you show signs of failure, such as wavy lines, or cause an accident.

The safest way: after an evening of drinking, push the bike or leave it standing.

## Lighting and equipment

The StVZO regulates what equipment a bicycle needs in road traffic. These include two independent brakes, a bright-sounding bell, a white front light and a red rear light, reflectors on the front, rear, on the pedals and on the sides. Battery lights are now expressly permitted if they are approved. We explain the details in the bicycle lighting guide.

## Take children with you

People aged 16 and over are allowed to take children up to the age of seven on a bicycle if there is a suitable child seat and the child's feet cannot get caught in the spokes. Similar rules apply to bicycle trailers: up to two children up to the age of seven are allowed to ride in a trailer built for this purpose. With cargo bikes, what you can take with you depends on the design and approval by the manufacturer.

## The most common mistakes

| Mistake | That's right |
|---|---|
| "Cyclists must always use the cycle path." | Only with blue sign. The road is permitted without a sign. |
| "Driving next to each other is prohibited." | Permitted as long as traffic is not obstructed. |
| "The pedestrian traffic lights apply on the cycle path." | No, the bicycle traffic lights or the traffic lights apply. |
| "I have priority at the zebra crossing." | Only if you get off and push. |
| "When you're cycling, alcohol doesn't matter." | From 1.6 per mille it is a criminal offense and there are penalties even before that. |
| "One-way streets are always free for bikes." | Only with the corresponding additional symbol. |

## Conclusion

The StVO gives cyclists more rights than many people think: you can ride on the road without a blue sign, riding side by side may be allowed, and cars must keep a distance of 1.5 meters in urban areas and 2 meters outside of urban areas. At the same time, clear obligations apply: obey traffic lights, only drive on the left with permission, leave your cell phone in your pocket and drive sober. Anyone who knows these basic rules and drives with foresight will be safer on the road and can confidently assert their rights when in doubt.
