It happens to everyone at some point: in the middle of the tour the tire hisses, the chain comes off or the gears suddenly do what they want. The good news: With just a few tools and a little practice, most breakdowns can be repaired on the side of the road so that you can safely get to the nearest workshop, accommodation or home.

This guide shows the most common breakdowns on the road, how you can repair them temporarily, which tools should be in every bag and when it's better to leave the repair and get help.

## The minimal tool for on the go

For a day tour, a small amount of basic equipment that fits into a saddle bag or handlebar bag is sufficient:

| tool | For what |
|---|---|
| Replacement hose in the correct size | faster than roadside patches |
| Repair kit | when a second record comes |
| Two plastic tire levers | Remove the tire from the rim |
| Mini pump or CO₂ cartridge | Inflate the tire, note the appropriate valve |
| Multitool with Allen 2 to 8 mm, Torx T25 and chain riveter | almost every screw on the bike |
| Chain lock to match the chain | Close a broken chain in just a few minutes |
| Cable ties and duct tape | a surprising number of emergency solutions |
| Disposable gloves or a rag | clean hands after chain repair |

For longer tours, you will need a replacement derailleur hanger for your bike, a piece of old tire or a tire patch for cracks in the tire cover and a few spare screws.

## Flat plates: the most common breakdown

### Changing a hose on the side of the road

1. **Park safely:** Move the bike to a safe place away from traffic. Wear a high-visibility vest or light-colored clothing in the dark.
2. **Removing the wheel:** Release the brake if it is a rim brake. Open the quick release or quick release axle. Switch to the smallest sprocket on the rear wheel beforehand, this makes installation and removal easier.
3. **Lift off the tire:** Release any remaining air, position the tire lever opposite the valve and lift one side of the tire over the rim.
4. **Find cause:** This is the most important step. Carefully run your fingers over the inside of the tire cover and look for broken glass, thorns or pieces of wire. If the foreign body remains in the tire, the new tube will go flat again after a few meters.
5. **Insert new tube:** Inflate slightly so that it is round, insert valve through hole in rim and insert tube.
6. **Mount the tire:** Using your thumbs from the valve on both sides, press the tire back over the rim. Finally, if possible, work without a tire lever to avoid pinching the tube.
7. **Inflate and check:** Check that the tire is seated evenly all around, then inflate to the correct pressure.

You can find detailed instructions with patches in the guide to changing plates and hoses.

### Crack in the tire cover

A cut in the ceiling pushes the hose outwards and it bursts again. As an emergency solution, place a piece of old tire material, a special tire patch or, if necessary, a folded bill inside the crack before inserting the tube. This will take you to the next opportunity to buy a new tire. Drive carefully and with a little less pressure.

### Tubeless tires

In tubeless tires, the sealing milk usually seals small holes itself. Larger holes can be sealed with a tubeless patch set (rubber sausages that are pressed into the hole). As a final solution, a normal hose can be inserted when the valve is removed.

## Broken or broken chain

**Broken chain:** The chain usually jumps off the chainring when shifting under load. Place your hand back on the smallest chainring, slowly turn the crank backwards and it will run again. If it keeps falling off, the gears are out of adjustment or the chain is worn out.

**Broken chain:**

1. Remove the damaged link using the multitool's chain tool.
2. Connect the two ends with a suitable chain lock. Without a chain lock, you can rivet the chain again with the riveter, but this is fiddly.
3. The chain is now a little shorter. Avoid the combination of a large chainring and a large sprocket so that the rear derailleur is not over-tightened.
4. If possible, have the chain checked and replaced in the workshop.

## Bent derailleur hanger and circuit that no longer shifts

If the bike falls on its right side, the derailleur hanger, a small part between the frame and the rear derailleur, often bends. The result: The gear changes and the rear derailleur comes dangerously close to the spokes.

- **Check:** Look at the rear derailleur from behind. It should hang vertically under the sprockets.
- **Emergency solution:** A slightly bent derailleur hanger can be carefully bent back by hand. Afterwards, do not shift to the largest sprocket so that the rear derailleur does not get caught in the spokes.
- **Replacement:** If you're traveling, it's best to have a suitable replacement derailleur hanger with you. It's cheap, light and different for every bike.
- **Broken shift cable:** The rear derailleur jumps onto the smallest sprocket. With the limit screw or a small branch between the rear derailleur and the frame, you can set a middle gear and continue riding, with one gear instead of many.

## Brakes: don't grind, squeak or grip

- **Grinding disc brake after wheel installation:** Loosen the quick release axle or quick release, straighten the wheel, tighten it again. If that doesn't help, loosen the two fastening screws on the brake caliper, pull and hold the brake lever, then tighten the screws again.
- **Rim brake works too late:** Tighten the cable using the adjusting screw on the brake lever.
- **Brake doesn't work at all:** Don't drive any further. Check whether the cable is broken or a brake pad is missing. Pushing occurs without two working brakes.

## Loose parts: handlebars, saddle, luggage rack

- **Twisted handlebars or loose stem:** Tighten the stem screws evenly in a cross pattern using the Allen key, not too tight. For carbon parts, pay attention to the specified torque.
- **Slipping saddle:** Tighten the seat clamp. A mark with duct tape on the seat post helps to find the correct height.
- **Broken luggage rack strut:** Temporarily fix it with cable ties and duct tape, redistribute the luggage and continue driving carefully.
- **Rattling mudguard:** Tighten screws or secure with cable ties.

## Broken spoke

A broken spoke causes the wheel to seize. Wrap the broken spoke around a neighboring spoke or secure it with adhesive tape so that it does not get caught in the brakes or rear derailleur. With rim brakes, open the brake slightly if the wheel drags. Then drive carefully to the nearest workshop. Several broken spokes on the rear wheel are a sign that the wheel is overloaded or old.

## Breakdowns on the Pedelec

Pedelecs also have electrical problems:

- **Motor does not support:** Battery engaged correctly? Battery and contacts clean and dry? Switch the system off and on again.
- **Error message on the display:** Write down the error code. Many codes can be found in the manufacturer's instructions or in its app.
- **Battery empty:** A Pedelec can be ridden without assistance, but it is heavy. Shift into a light gear and plan the next charging opportunity, for example in a café.

You shouldn't open anything about the motor, battery or electrical system while you're on the move. That is a matter for the specialist workshop.

## Prevent breakdowns

Many breakdowns are on the horizon. A quick look before every longer tour prevents most:

- **Tires:** Check tread, cracks in the sidewalls and retracted splinters, check air pressure.
- **Chain:** clean, oiled and not worn. An elongated chain is more likely to break and fall off more often.
- **Brakes:** Pads with sufficient strength, levers grip firmly.
- **Screws:** Check the stem, seat clamp, luggage rack and bottle cage for tightness.

## When you shouldn't continue driving

Some damage cannot be reliably repaired temporarily:

- Cracks or deformation of the frame, fork or handlebars, especially with carbon
- Brake without effect
- Loose thru axle or quick release that cannot be closed securely
- Badly damaged rim where the tire no longer holds

In these cases, the only thing that helps is pushing, taking the train, a taxi or a breakdown service. Some automobile clubs and bicycle clubs offer bicycle breakdown service for members. Before going on a longer tour, check whether your membership covers this.

## Practice before it matters

The best preparation is a test run at home: remove and install the rear wheel, change a tube, open and close a chain lock. What you once did in peace, you can do in the rain on the side of the road.

## Find a workshop on the way

If a repair goes beyond temporary, a local bicycle repair shop can help. Here's how to quickly find one:

- **Map apps:** Searching for “bicycle workshop” or “bicycle shop” usually also shows opening times and telephone numbers. Call ahead and explain the problem; many workshops will take travelers on short notice if they know what is missing.
- **Ask accommodation:** Bicycle-friendly hosts know the nearest workshop and often have tools and a floor pump themselves.
- **Bicycle stations at train stations:** In many cities there are bicycle stations with repair services at large train stations.
- **Public repair stations:** There are freely accessible stations with pumps and chained tools on some bike paths and in cities.

For longer tours, have the most important data about your bike ready: tire size (it's on the tire sidewall), valve type, number of gears, type of brakes and, for Pedelecs, the motor manufacturer. This saves time on the phone.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it worth patching or getting a new hose?**
Changing tubes is quicker on the go and more reliable in the rain. You repair the broken hose in peace in the evening and then you have a replacement with you.

**Which pump is the right one?**
A mini pump with a hose that fits your valve is sufficient for most. CO₂ cartridges deplete quickly, but empty after one use. For trips with many kilometers, a slightly larger pump is more comfortable.

**Do I have to repair a breakdown on a new bike myself?**
A flat tire or a broken chain is not covered by the warranty; you can fix it yourself. If there are defects in components that occur shortly after purchase, you should complain to the dealer and not repair them yourself so as not to jeopardize your claims.

## Conclusion

Most breakdowns on the road can be fixed with a spare tube, tire levers, pump, multi-tool with chain tool, chain lock, cable ties and duct tape. In the case of a flat tire, finding the cause is the most important step; in the case of a broken chain, finding the right chain lock; in the case of a bent gear, be careful with the largest sprocket. Damage to the frame, fork or brakes, on the other hand, is not an issue for the side of the road. Practice the most important steps at home so that a breakdown won't upset you.
