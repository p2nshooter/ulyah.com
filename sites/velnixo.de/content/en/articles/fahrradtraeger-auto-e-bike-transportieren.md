For a vacation at the lake, a bike ride in a neighboring country or the way to the workshop, the bike sometimes has to go to or from the car. With light bikes this is usually uncomplicated. With Pedelecs, which often weigh 22 to 30 kilograms, transport becomes a challenge: not every carrier is approved for this, lifting is laborious and the permissible load of the car is quickly reached.

In this guide, we compare the common transport options, explain what you should pay attention to when buying a bike rack, and give tips for safe travel, from the battery to the lighting to changing the driving behavior of the car.

## The four options at a glance

| possibility | suitable for Pedelecs | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Towbar carrier | yes, if approved | low loading height, high payload, little air resistance | Trailer hitch required, tongue load limited, license plate and light required |
| Roof rack | mostly no | cheap, trunk remains free | high lifting height, roof load limited, more air resistance |
| Tailgate carrier | rare | possible without a trailer hitch | low payload, load on the tailgate, not for every car |
| Interior | yes, if space | protected, safe, no accessories required | Lots of space needed, dirt, security in the interior |

## Towbar carrier: the best choice for Pedelecs

A bicycle rack for the trailer hitch is almost always the most comfortable and safest solution for Pedelecs. The wheels stand at a low height on rails behind the car and are secured with clamping arms and straps.

### What you should pay attention to

- **Approval for e-bikes:** The carrier must be specifically designed for the weight of Pedelecs, often up to 30 kilograms per bike and 60 kilograms in total.
- **Car support load:** The support load is the maximum load that can press on the trailer hitch. It is stated in the vehicle registration document or in the operating instructions, often between 50 and 100 kilograms. The carrier and wheels must not exceed it. A carrier itself weighs 15 to 20 kilograms. With two Pedelecs weighing 25 kilograms each and a carrier weighing 18 kilograms, that's already 68 kilograms.
- **Folding function:** A folding carrier can be tilted backwards with loaded wheels so that you can access the trunk.
- **Access ramp:** For heavy Pedelecs, a ramp is very helpful, over which the bike can be pushed onto the carrier instead of having to lift it.
- **Distance between wheels:** The distance between the rails should be large enough so that the handlebars and pedals do not touch each other.
- **Lockable:** Carriers and wheels should be lockable.
- **Test mark:** Look for a tested product that states the standard and a valid operating permit.

### License plates and lighting

A rear rack usually covers the license plate and taillights. That's why it needs its own lighting with taillight, brake light and indicator as well as a holder for a third license plate. The third license plate can be requested from the registration office and has the same number as the car. Before each ride, check whether the lights on the carrier are working.

## Roof rack: only for light bikes

Roof racks are inexpensive and leave the trailer hitch and trunk free. However, they are usually unsuitable for Pedelecs:

- **Roof load:** The permissible roof load of many cars is 50 to 100 kilograms, including the base rack and bicycle holder.
- **Lifting height:** Lifting a heavy Pedelec onto a car roof is laborious and risky for your back and paint.
- **Air resistance:** Wheels on the roof significantly increase consumption and wind noise.
- **Headroom:** Garages, parking garages and car washes become a trap. Many people drive into their own garage with bikes on the roof.

For light bikes without a motor, a roof rack can be a good choice, especially if a trailer hitch rack isn't possible.

## Tailgate carrier: only with restrictions

Tailgate supports are attached to the tailgate with straps and hooks. They are relatively inexpensive and do not require a trailer hitch. However, they are often only approved for light wheels, put a strain on the tailgate and do not fit every vehicle. Many car manufacturers specify whether and with what load the tailgate can be loaded. In most cases, these carriers are not suitable for Pedelecs.

## Interior: if there is enough space

In station wagons, vans and minibuses, a bicycle often fits into the interior, possibly with the front wheel removed and the seats folded down. This protects the bike from rain, dirt and theft.

- **Backing:** A blanket or tarpaulin protects the interior from oil and dirt.
- **Securing:** The wheel must be secured so that it does not swing forward in the event of an emergency stop. Tension straps on the lashing eyes help.
- **Protect the rear derailleur:** Do not place the bike on the rear derailleur side.
- **Disc brakes:** When the wheel is removed, place a transport safety wedge between the brake pads to prevent them from accidentally being pressed together.

## The e-bike battery during transport

The following applies to Pedelecs for transport on a bicycle rack:

- **Removing the battery:** Almost all manufacturers recommend removing the battery before transport. This protects it from rain, heat, wind and stone chips and saves weight, often 3 to 4 kilograms per wheel. This leaves more reserve for the support load.
- **Transport in the interior:** Store the battery safely and protected in the car, not in the blazing sun and not loose in the trunk.
- **Removing the display:** If possible, also remove the display or the control unit so that it is not damaged by wind or falling stones.
- **Cover contacts:** If it rains, cover contacts on the bike with a protective cap or foil.

## This is how you load the carrier correctly

1. **Heaviest wheel on the inside:** The heaviest wheel is closest to the car so that the lever on the trailer hitch is smaller.
2. **Remove loose parts:** Bags, baskets, water bottles, air pumps, child seats and anything that could come loose.
3. **Align the wheels:** Alternately move the handlebars left and right so that they do not touch each other.
4. **Clamping arms and straps:** Place all clamping arms firmly on the frame and tighten the rim tapes tightly. Only clamp the carbon frame in designated areas.
5. **Complete:** Lock the wheels on the carrier and the carrier on the coupling.
6. **Check lighting:** Check taillight, brake light, turn signals and license plate.
7. **Check after the first few kilometers:** Belts can become loose. After a short drive, stop and tighten everything.

## The car drives differently

When the carrier is loaded, the driving behavior of the car changes:

- **Longer braking distance:** The additional weight increases the braking distance.
- **Different steering:** A lot of load behind the rear axle relieves the front axle. The car may feel different when cornering.
- **Length:** The car is significantly longer. Remember the carrier when reversing and parking. Parking sensors can give false warnings.
- **Speed:** Many carrier manufacturers specify a maximum speed, often 130 km/h. If there is a crosswind and a heavy load, it is better to go slower.
- **Consumption:** Wheels on the carrier increase air resistance and thus consumption.

## On the road: rain, dirt and theft

- **Covers:** There are special covers for wheels on rear racks. They protect against rain and insects, but must be secured very well so that they do not flutter.
- **Rest and overnight stay:** Do not leave bikes on the carrier unattended overnight. Pedelecs in particular are an attractive target. If possible, store the bikes in a lockable room.
- **After the ride:** If possible, wash off insects, salt and road dirt and oil the chain.

## Bicycle instead of car?

If you want to travel frequently by bike, you can think about alternatives: Many regions are easily accessible by train, and cycling trips by train and bike have their own appeal. You can find out more about this in the guide to taking your bike on the train. However, a car with a rear rack is often the most practical solution for transporting heavy Pedelecs.

## Legal details shortly

- The permissible vertical load and roof load of the car must not be exceeded.
- Taillights and license plates must be visible; a hidden license plate must be repeated on the vehicle.
- The load must not protrude excessively to the sides and must be securely fastened.
- Special rules apply to trips abroad, such as warning signs in some countries. Find out before you travel.

## Buying advice: Questions to ask before purchasing a carrier

Before you buy a carrier, it's worth taking an honest look at your habits. These questions will help you decide:

| Question | Why it is important |
|---|---|
| How many bikes do you regularly transport? | Two-wheel carriers are more compact, three-wheel carriers quickly become too heavy for the support load. |
| How heavy is the heaviest bike without a battery? | The permissible load per rail is often lower than the total load. |
| How wide are the tires? | Wide tires and fat bikes require suitable rails and longer straps. |
| How long is the wheelbase? | Long trekking bikes and cargo bikes do not fit every carrier. |
| Do you have space for storage? | A foldable carrier can be stored in the basement or trunk. |
| Do you have to go to the trunk? | A folding function that also works when loaded is then almost indispensable. |

Also look at the test mark. A carrier with tested safety (e.g. according to a recognized test seal) and a manufacturer that offers spare parts such as straps, keys and lights is a better choice in the long term than a cheap model without service.

## Install, maintain and store carriers correctly

- **Assembly:** Follow the manufacturer's instructions carefully. On trailer hitch carriers, the tensioning lever is usually readjusted with a knurled screw until it closes with significant resistance. A strap that is too loose will wobble and can come loose.
- **Coupling ball:** The ball should be clean and free of grease, as grease on the ball reduces the clamping force. After driving with a carrier, it can be lightly greased again if you are towing a trailer.
- **Care:** Rinse rails and joints with clean water after winter rides to remove road salt. Moving parts occasionally need a little care spray.
- **Electrical:** Check plugs and lights once per season. Corroded contacts are the most common cause when the lighting on the carrier fails.
- **Storage:** Keep the carrier dry and lock it if it is lockable. A carrier in the basement is safer than one that stays on the car for months.

## Common mistakes when transporting bikes

1. **Battery not removed:** This makes the bike heavier, puts strain on the mount and exposes the battery to wind and weather.
2. **Non-calculated load:** Carrier plus two Pedelecs quickly add up to more than the clutch can carry.
3. **Forget loose parts:** Drinking bottles, pumps, bags and displays fly away at highway speeds.
4. **Belt not tightened:** After the first few kilometers, the tires and belts settle, so it's worth a quick stop.
5. **Driving too fast:** Many carriers are designed for a maximum speed that you should not exceed.
6. **Forget the length when parking:** With a rack, the car is significantly longer, which is often overlooked when reversing and maneuvering.

## Conclusion

For Pedelecs, a trailer hitch carrier approved for e-bikes with a folding function and access ramp is usually the best solution. Pay attention to the load capacity of your car, remove batteries and loose parts, secure the wheels carefully and check the lights and belts before and during the journey. Roof racks and tailgate racks are more suitable for light bikes, and if you have space, the bike is best transported indoors. This means that the bikes and batteries arrive safely at their destination and the tour can begin.
