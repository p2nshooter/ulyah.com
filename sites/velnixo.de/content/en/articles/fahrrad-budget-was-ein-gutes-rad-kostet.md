“How much do I have to spend on a good bike?” is one of the most common questions in the bike shop, and the most honest answer is: it depends on what you need it for. Nevertheless, there are clear price ranges in which one can expect certain quality, and some limits under which a bike becomes more trouble than joy in everyday life.

In this guide we explain what you get in which price range, which components are worth saving on, what follow-up costs you will incur in the first year and how, with clever planning, you can still get a reliable bike cheaply.

## Why the price fluctuates so much

A bicycle consists of a frame, fork, wheels, drive, brakes, steering, saddle and often lights, mudguards and luggage rack. Each of these parts comes in very different quality. A cheap bike rarely differs from a more expensive one in just one place, but in many small ways: slightly heavier, brakes with less bite, gears that are adjusted more quickly, wheels that have to be re-aligned more often, bearings that wear out earlier.

With Pedelecs, the motor, battery and electronics also make up a large part of the price. That's why e-bikes with comparable equipment cost significantly more than bikes without a motor.

## Price ranges for bicycles without motors

The following areas are guideline values for new bikes in specialist shops. They vary depending on the brand, season and features.

| Price range | What you typically get | Suitable for whom |
|---|---|---|
| under €300 | Simple components, high weight, often hardware store or discount bikes | occasional short trips, with caution |
| 400–700 € | Solid everyday bikes, simple brand components, hub dynamo often possible | City trips, beginners, children's and youth bikes |
| 700–1.200 € | good trekking and city bikes, hydraulic brakes, durable wheels | daily commute, weekend tours |
| 1.200–2.000 € | High-quality gears, lighter frames, belt drive or good hub gears possible | Frequent cyclists, cyclists |
| over €2,000 | Lightweight construction, special bikes, racing bikes and gravel bikes with top equipment | sporty drivers, enthusiasts |

For a reliable everyday bike that rides every day in all weathers, the reasonable range for most people is between around 600 and 1,200 euros. Among these, it becomes difficult to find a bike that is safe, durable and comfortable at the same time. Above all, you pay for less weight, more comfort and less maintenance.

## Price ranges for Pedelecs

| Price range | What you typically get |
|---|---|
| under €1,500 | simple hub motors, smaller batteries, often heavy and with simple components |
| 1.500–2.500 € | Mid-engines from well-known manufacturers, batteries around 400-500 Wh, solid equipment |
| 2.500–4.000 € | powerful motors, large batteries, high-quality gears or belts, better suspension |
| over €4,000 | E-mountain bikes, cargo bikes, S-Pedelecs, premium equipment |

When it comes to Pedelecs, specialist retailers are often particularly important because the battery, motor and software require service and spare parts must be available. A very cheap Pedelec of unknown origin can become a problem if the battery weakens after three years and a replacement is no longer available.

## Where saving pays off

### Brakes

Brakes are the most important safety component. Good rim brakes or hydraulic disc brakes cost a little more, but they brake reliably even in wet conditions and can be finely adjusted. Cheap mechanical brakes with soft levers and weak bite are a risk, especially in city traffic.

### Wheels

Wheels carry all the weight and every impact. Cheap wheels with weak rims and poorly tensioned spokes quickly become out of round, spokes break and the rims wear out. Good wheels last for many years.

### tires

Good tires make a bike faster, more comfortable and more puncture-proof. A tire with a puncture protection insert costs more, but saves a lot of hassle along the way. Here the difference between cheap and good is immediately noticeable in everyday life.

### Light

A permanently installed lighting system with a hub dynamo and parking light is the best solution for everyday cyclists. It's always there, doesn't need batteries and isn't stolen. Anyone who saves money here and relies on cheap plug-in lights will end up driving in the dark more often in the fall than they would like.

### Castle

A good lock costs between 50 and 150 euros. That sounds like a lot, but it is insurance against the loss of a bike that cost many times more.

## Where you can save with peace of mind

- **Weight:** For everyday riding, it rarely matters much whether a bike weighs 14 or 16 kilograms, unless you have to carry it up stairs every day.
- **Number of gears:** More gears does not automatically mean a better bike. For flat cities, seven or eight gears are sufficient.
- **Suspension:** A cheap suspension fork is often worse than a good rigid fork with wide tires. A suspension seat post sometimes brings more comfort for less money in everyday life.
- **Brand name:** Many good bikes come from lesser-known manufacturers or as house brands from specialist retailers. The components installed and the service are crucial.
- **Previous year's model:** Bicycles get new colors and small changes every year. Previous year's models are often significantly cheaper and technically almost identical.

## The hidden costs in the first year

The purchase price rarely remains the same. Plan the following items from the start:

| Post | approximate costs |
|---|---|
| Lock (U-lock or folding lock) | 50–150 € |
| Helmet | 40–120 € |
| Light, if not installed | 30–80 € |
| Pump with pressure gauge | 25–50 € |
| Basic tools and repair kit | 20–60 € |
| Bicycle bags or basket | 30–150 € |
| Rainwear | 50–200 € |
| First inspection (often free from the dealer) | 0–60 € |
| Insurance (optional) | varies greatly |

Added up, it easily comes to 200 to 500 euros. If you don't plan for this, you're saving money in the wrong place when it comes to your bike.

## Ongoing costs per year

A bicycle is the cheapest means of transport after your own feet, but it is not free. Typical ongoing costs:

- **Wearing parts:** Chain, brake pads, tires and hoses. Anyone who rides a lot can roughly expect to spend 50 to 150 euros per year, with a Pedelec more because of the higher weight and power.
- **Inspection:** An annual inspection in the workshop costs around 50 to 120 euros plus materials, depending on the scope.
- **Care products:** Chain oil, cleaner and cleaning cloths cost little.
- **Battery on a Pedelec:** After a few years the capacity decreases. A new battery often costs several hundred euros. With good care, you can significantly postpone this point.

Even with all items combined, the annual costs are far less than what a car or a monthly public transport ticket costs.

## Saving cleverly: ways to get a good bike on a budget

### Buy used

A well-maintained, second-hand branded bike may be the best choice. It is important to carry out a thorough inspection so that expensive repairs do not have to be carried out afterwards. Our checklist for buying used will help.

### Use end of season

In late summer and autumn, many dealers clear out their warehouses for the new models. Then there are often noticeable discounts on the current model year.

### Company bike leasing

Many employers offer company bike leasing. The leasing rate is usually deducted from the gross wage via deferred compensation, which can bring tax advantages. Whether this is worth it for you depends on your salary, the term, the insurance and the residual value at the end. Calculate the offer carefully.

### Funding programs

Some cities, municipalities and federal states promote cargo bikes or Pedelecs, often for families, businesses or people with low incomes. The programs change frequently. It's worth checking your city or country's website before you buy, as the application often needs to be made before purchasing.

### Self-help workshops

In many cities there are bicycle self-help workshops where you can repair your own bike with tools and instructions. This saves workshop costs and makes you independent.

## When expensive makes sense

There are good reasons to spend more money:

- You ride long distances every day and want a bike that requires little maintenance, for example with a belt drive and hub gear.
- You are planning long cycling trips with heavy luggage and need robust wheels and a stable frame.
- You replace a car with a Pedelec or cargo bike. Then the comparison is not with a cheap bike, but with the cost of a car.
- You have physical limitations and need special geometry or equipment.

## Example calculation: What an everyday bike costs over five years

How expensive a bike really is only becomes apparent over the years. Let's take a solid trekking bike for 900 euros that is used on around 200 days a year for commuting and running errands, a total of around 3,000 kilometers per year.

| Post | over five years (rough) |
|---|---|
| Purchase price | 900 € |
| Accessories when purchasing (lock, helmet, pump, bags) | 250 € |
| Wear parts (two chains, one cassette, tires, pads) | 300 € |
| Inspections | 350 € |
| Small parts and care products | 100 € |
| **Total** | **around €1,900** |

That's the equivalent of around 32 euros per month or around 13 cents per kilometer, not including the residual value of the bike. A cheap bike for 300 euros seems cheaper at first glance, but it often needs expensive repairs sooner, is harder to ride and is often replaced after just a few years. In the end, the costs per kilometer are often not that far apart, only the joy of driving is different.

## Checklist before purchase

Before you commit, answer these questions for yourself. They help to distribute the budget sensibly:

1. Approximately how many kilometers will I drive per week?
2. Do I need lights, mudguards and luggage racks from the factory?
3. Does the bike need to be carried up stairs so weight is important?
4. Where is the bike at night and how well do I have to secure it?
5. Which parts would I service myself and what should the workshop do?
6. Have I already included accessories and ongoing costs?

Anyone who answers these questions honestly often realizes that a slightly higher budget for a robust everyday bike is worth it, while you can save on weight, number of gears and brand name.

## Conclusion

A good bike doesn't have to cost a fortune, but it shouldn't be the cheapest either. For most everyday cyclists, a budget of between 600 and 1,200 euros is a good frame for a bike without a motor; for a solid Pedelec, more like 2,000 to 3,000 euros. Plan for locks, helmets, lights and accessories right from the start, don't skimp on brakes, wheels and tires and take advantage of the used market, end of season and support programs. Then you get a bike that will accompany you reliably for many years, and that is ultimately the cheapest solution.
