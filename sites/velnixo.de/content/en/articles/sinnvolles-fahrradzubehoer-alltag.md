There are countless accessories for bikes, from simple bells to networked bike computers. Some things make everyday life noticeably easier, safer or more comfortable, others are in the drawer after a week. If you choose carefully, you will save money and have a bike that is exactly the right fit for your journey.

This guide sorts through the offerings: What is mandatory, what is worthwhile for almost everyone, what makes sense for certain purposes and what can you do without?

## Makes sense for almost everyone

### Mudguards

Without mudguards, every puddle causes water and dirt to land on your back, trousers and shoes. For everyday bikes, solid mudguards with splash flaps are the best solution; for sporty bikes, there are plug-in mudguards that can be quickly installed if necessary.

### Stand

A side stand is sufficient for light bikes. If you have a child seat, heavy luggage or a Pedelec, you should choose a two-legged stand that keeps the bike upright and stable.

### Air pump

Correct tire pressure is the easiest way to fewer punctures and easier driving. A floor pump with a pressure gauge for at home and a small mini pump for on the go are part of the basic equipment. Make sure the pump fits your valve.

### Castle

Without a good lock, the bike is quickly lost. A sturdy U-lock, folding lock or chain lock for connecting to fixed objects is mandatory for anyone who parks the bike in the city. More about this in the guide to theft protection.

### Luggage racks and bags

Instead of a backpack, the bike carries your luggage. A sturdy luggage rack and a matching bag make every shopping trip and every commute more pleasant. More about this in the guide to bags, baskets and luggage racks.

## For more security

- **Rearview Mirror:** A small mirror on the end of the handlebars or helmet shows what's happening behind you without you having to turn your head. It doesn't replace looking over your shoulder, but it complements it, especially in city traffic and on Pedelecs at higher speeds.
- **Additional rear light:** A small battery rear light on the backpack or helmet increases visibility to the rear.
- **Reflective elements:** Reflective tapes for trouser legs, spoke clips or reflective tire strips make you visible from the side.
- **Spacer:** A fold-out trowel on the luggage rack that extends to the left is intended to encourage cars to keep more distance. It must not endanger others and is controversial, but some commuters appreciate it.
- **Helmet:** Not required but recommended. Find out more in the bicycle helmet guide.

## For more comfort

- **Ergonomic handles:** Handles with wrist rests relieve strain on the wrists and prevent fingers from falling asleep on long journeys.
- **Right saddle:** A saddle that fits your sitting position and sit bones is more important than any padding. Many specialist retailers measure the distance between the sit bones.
- **Suspended seat post:** absorbs shocks on cobblestones and poor roads, especially on bikes without suspension forks.
- **Chain guard:** keeps pants clean and protects the chain from dirt. Full chain protection with hub gears makes the chain almost maintenance-free.
- **Drink bottle holder:** Indispensable on longer journeys and in summer.

## For navigation and technology

- **Mobile phone holder:** A stable holder on the handlebars or stem turns the mobile phone into a navigation system. Make sure it is firmly clamped and protected from rain and vibrations. If there is a lot of cobblestones, a holder with a damper protects the device.
- **Bicycle computer:** Practical for touring riders and sporty people because they run longer than a cell phone and are easy to read even in the sun.
- **USB charger on the hub dynamo:** Charges the cell phone while driving. Useful on long journeys, usually unnecessary in everyday life.

## Accessories and warranty

Some accessories interfere with the wheel. Anyone who clamps a luggage rack to a carbon frame, mounts a child seat to an unapproved bike or converts the light on a Pedelec risks damage and may lose warranty claims. Check the instructions for your bike to see what loads and attachments are permitted and ask the dealer if you are unsure.

## Accessories for children and families

If you are taking children or traveling with them, you will need a few additional things. A bicycle pennant on a long pole makes children's bikes and trailers visible to drivers early on. A two-legged stand keeps the bike securely upright when the child is placed in the seat. It is worth having an easily accessible bell, reflectors and a small battery-powered light for the children's bike, even if the child usually only rides during the day. A small handlebar bag on a children's bike makes children proud because they can transport their own things. And for longer trips, you should pack a spare tube the same size as your child's bike, as children's bikes almost always have different tire sizes than adult bikes.

## Prioritize accessories based on budget

If you don't want to buy everything at once, set your priorities. Everything that concerns safety comes first: working brakes, bright lights and a good lock. This is followed by parts that make everyday driving easier, such as mudguards, luggage racks and stands. Comfort and technology, such as ergonomic handles, a suspended seat post or a bike computer, come last. So you invest first where every euro has the most impact.

## Which you can often save yourself

- **Cheap battery-powered lamps without approval:** They dazzle, fail and are not permitted in traffic. It's better to buy a good lamp with a K number.
- **Very cheap locks:** A cable lock for a few euros can be cracked in seconds.
- **Gel saddle covers instead of a matching saddle:** They shift and do not solve the basic problem.
- **Accessories that constantly rattle:** Cheap brackets and baskets that come loose are more annoying than good.
- **Special gadgets without a clear purpose:** Before you buy, ask yourself what problem the item solves in everyday life.

## What to pay attention to when buying

1. **Does it fit the bike?** Check tube diameter, axle, eyelets on the frame, tire size and valve type.
2. **Is it approved?** Pay attention to the test mark for lights and reflectors.
3. **How ​​is it attached?** A solid hold prevents rattling and loss.
4. **Can it be removed?** Valuable accessories such as lamps, bags and bike computers should be easy to take with you when parked.
5. **Are there replacement parts?** For brackets, bags and lamps, it is worth it if the manufacturer offers replacement parts.

## Accessories for the Pedelec

Pedelecs are heavier and faster than normal bikes, which makes some accessories more important:

- **Two-leg stand:** A Pedelec weighing 25 kilograms or more can easily tip over on a side stand, especially with luggage.
- **Battery lock and screen protector:** Remove the display or control unit when the bike is stationary. The battery often has a lock that secures it to the frame.
- **Thermal cover for the battery:** In winter it keeps the battery warm and thus increases the range.
- **Rearview mirror:** At 25 km/h, other cyclists and cars are approaching faster than expected. A mirror helps with an overview.
- **Good tires:** Wide tires with puncture protection and approval for e-bikes can handle the higher weight and speed better.

## Accessories for autumn and winter

- **Handlebar cuffs or warm gloves:** Cold fingers cannot brake safely.
- **Studded tires:** A major safety benefit for commuters on icy roads. More about this in the guide to cycling in winter.
- **Helmet Cap:** A thin cap under the helmet keeps ears warm without altering the fit.
- **Strong lighting:** In the dark season, a bright headlight is more important than any other accessory.

## Install accessories correctly

Many parts can be attached yourself. A few rules prevent trouble:

1. **Use suitable screws:** For luggage racks and mudguards, use the manufacturer's screws or screws of a suitable length. Screws that are too long can protrude into the frame or spokes.
2. **Do not overtighten:** There are torque specifications, especially for carbon and aluminum. A small torque wrench is worth it if you do a lot of screwdriving yourself.
3. **Leave cables and cables free:** Brackets must not pinch or kink brake and shift cables.
4. **Tighten after the first ride:** New parts will settle. Check all screws after the first few kilometers.
5. **If in doubt, go to the workshop:** For child seats, luggage racks for heavy loads and anything that has to do with brakes, professional installation is the safe choice.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to have a bell when I ride a racing bike?**
Yes. The bell is required for all bicycles on the road, including racing bikes and mountain bikes. There are small, inconspicuous models that have hardly any weight.

**Is a bike computer better than a cell phone?**
For long tours, yes: it lasts longer, is easier to read in the sun and is more robust. A good cell phone holder is usually enough for the commute.

**Are expensive accessories worth it?**
For parts that have to do with security, yes: lock, light, brake pads and tires. When it comes to decorations and gadgets, a cheap option or none at all is often enough.

## Conclusion

Good accessories make the bike a reliable companion in everyday life. Brakes, bells, lights and reflectors are mandatory. For almost everyone, mudguards, a stable stand, a good pump, a secure lock and a luggage rack with a bag are worthwhile. Rear-view mirrors, additional reflectors and a helmet increase safety, while ergonomic handles and a suitable saddle increase comfort. Buy specifically and better once good than twice cheap, then the bike will suit your needs exactly.
