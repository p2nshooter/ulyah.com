A bicycle is surprisingly frugal. Nevertheless, parts wear out, screws become loose, tires lose air and chains become dry. If you regularly invest a few minutes, you will drive safer, easier and cheaper because small problems do not become major repairs in the first place. On the other hand, if you only repair your bike when something is broken, you usually end up paying more and ending up on the side of the road more often with a breakdown.

This guide provides an overview of all important care and maintenance work. It tells you what you should do and how often, what you can do yourself and when a specialist workshop is the better choice. You can find detailed step-by-step instructions for many points on VELNIXO.

## Why regular maintenance is worth it

- **Safety:** Brakes, tires, lights and steering must function reliably. Many accidents with technical causes can be avoided through simple checks.
- **Costs:** A chain that is regularly oiled and changed in a timely manner protects the sprockets and chainrings. If you run the chain for too long, you often have to replace the entire drive.
- **Riding experience:** A well-maintained bike runs quieter, lighter and shifts more precisely.
- **Retention of value:** A well-maintained bike retains its value if you want to sell it later.

## Before every trip: the ten-second check

Before you ride off, especially after a long period of standing or if the bike has been parked in public:

1. **Pull the brakes:** Do both brakes grip firmly before the lever touches the handlebars?
2. **Press the tires:** Do both tires feel bulging?
3. **Turn on the lights:** Do the headlights and taillights light up when you drive in the dark or in poor visibility?
4. **Check quick release:** If present, are the levers on the wheels and seat post closed?

This really only takes a few seconds and avoids most unpleasant surprises.

## Weekly or every few hundred kilometers

- **Measure tire pressure:** Using a floor pump with a pressure gauge. Bicycle tubes slowly lose air even without a hole. The permissible area is written on the tire sidewall. With the right pressure, the wheel rolls more easily and bottom-outs become less frequent.
- **Check the chain:** Is it dry, does it squeak, or is it dirty? Then clean and oil. More often in rain, snow and salt.
- **Visual inspection:** Loose mudguards, jammed stones in the tread, slipped bag holders, a rattling lock.

## Monthly

- **Check brake pads:** Rim brakes have grooves in the pads that serve as wear indicators. With disc brakes, look into the brake caliper from above or remove the pads. Keep braking surfaces clean.
- **Take a closer look at the tires:** Tread, cracks in the sidewalls, glass splinters or foreign bodies. Carefully remove any stuck splinters.
- **Check screws:** Stem, handlebars, seat clamp, luggage rack, bottle holder. Best with a torque wrench if a torque is specified.
- **Check light and cables:** Especially with dynamo lighting, cables sometimes come loose or are damaged when turned off.
- **Test the gearshift:** Shift through all gears while stationary with the rear wheel raised or while driving. If something jumps or gets stuck, this can usually be corrected with tension.

## Every 1,000 to 2,000 kilometers or every six months

- **Measure chain wear:** Using a chain gauge. Changing an elongated chain in a timely manner saves money because the sprockets and chainrings last longer.
- **Clean the bike thoroughly:** Frame, wheels, drive. Damage that is overlooked in everyday life becomes noticeable.
- **Check the wheels:** Do they rotate smoothly without running out? Are all spokes tensioned similarly? Do the hubs have play?
- **Check the headset:** Pull the front brake and push the wheel back and forth. If the handlebars click, the headset is loose.
- **Check bottom bracket and pedals:** Move cranks sideways, turn pedals. Play or crunching indicates wear.
- **View cables and casings:** Frayed or rusty cables and kinked casings will deteriorate brakes and gears.

## Once a year: inspection

A thorough review once a year, ideally before the main season in spring, includes all of the above plus:

- Check brake fluid or brake cables and replace if necessary,
- Maintain suspension fork and damper according to manufacturer's specifications,
- Check bearings of hubs, headset and bottom bracket and grease or replace if necessary,
- center the wheels,
- For hub gears, oil change or maintenance according to the manufacturer's instructions,
- For Pedelecs, update the software, have the motor and battery read out.

You can do a lot of this work yourself with experience. However, it is often worth going to a specialist workshop for an annual inspection, especially for Pedelecs, hydraulic brakes and suspension elements.

## The care plan at a glance

| interval | task | doable yourself? |
|---|---|---|
| before every trip | Brakes, tires, lights, quick releases | yes |
| weekly | Clean and oil tire pressure, chain | yes |
| monthly | Brake pads, tires, screws, gears | yes |
| 1,000-2,000km | Chain wear, thorough cleaning, wheels, bearings | predominantly yes |
| annually | Inspection, brake fluid, suspension, software | partly, otherwise workshop |

The intervals are guide values. If you drive in the rain, in winter or on dusty roads, you should care more often. If you only drive occasionally when the weather is nice, you drive less often.

## The right cleaning

A clean bike runs better and lasts longer. This is how you do it:

1. **Remove coarse dirt:** With a soft jet of water from a garden hose or watering can. Do not use a high-pressure cleaner, it forces water into bearings and removes grease.
2. **Wash frame and wheels:** With warm water, a little mild detergent or special bicycle cleaner and a soft sponge or brush.
3. **Cleaning the drive:** Clean the chain, sprockets and chainrings with a brush and chain cleaner. Then let it dry thoroughly.
4. **Omit braking surfaces:** Do not treat rims and brake discs with greasy products. If necessary, clean only with water or special brake cleaner.
5. **Dry and oil:** Dry the wheel, then re-oil the chain. Wipe off excess oil.

For Pedelecs, remove the battery first and do not spray the contacts, display and motor directly with water.

## Basic care tool

For most jobs, a small amount of basic equipment is sufficient:

- Floor pump with pressure gauge and matching valve head,
- Allen keys in sizes 2 to 8 millimeters, possibly Torx keys,
- tire levers and repair kit,
- chain oil and rags,
- chain wear gauge,
- Small torque wrench for stem and seat post, especially for carbon and aluminum parts.

We explain which other tools are useful in the guide to the workshop at home.

## What you would rather leave to the specialist workshop

Doing it yourself is nice, but it doesn't always make sense. These tasks usually belong in the workshop if you have no experience and no special tools:

- Bleeding hydraulic brakes,
- Maintenance of suspension forks and dampers inside,
- Working on the headset and bottom bracket with special tools,
- Spoking and strong centering of wheels,
- all work on the motor, battery and electronics of Pedelecs,
- Check after a fall, especially with carbon frames.

When it comes to safety, the following applies: If you are unsure, have it checked. An incorrectly installed brake or a stem that is tightened too loosely is dangerous.

## Seasonal maintenance

- **Spring:** Major inspection, check tires, replace or clean chain, test lights, adjust bike for the new season.
- **Summer:** Clean the chain more often if it is dusty, check the tire pressure in hot weather, and do not leave the battery in the blazing sun.
- **Autumn:** Check lights and reflectors, prepare brakes for wet leaves, possibly install tires with more tread.
- **Winter:** Wash off the salt regularly, oil the chain more often, consider winter tires when it is slippery, do not charge the battery in the cold. There is a separate guide on how to winterize your bike if you don't ride in the winter.

## Keep a maintenance record

Write down when you did what: chain changed at mileage X, brake pads replaced, inspection carried out. A simple notebook or note in your smartphone is enough. This way you know when the next work is due and when you sell the bike you can document the care.

## Wear parts and their typical service life

How long a part lasts depends heavily on care, weather, driving style, weight and terrain. The following values ​​are therefore only a rough guide for everyday bikes without a motor. In Pedelecs, the drive and brakes usually wear out more quickly due to the higher forces and weight.

| part | rough lifespan | How do you recognize wear and tear? |
|---|---|---|
| chain | 2,000-5,000km | Measurement with chain gauge, jumping gears |
| Cassette/sprocket | two to three chains | Chain jumps on new chains, sharp teeth |
| chainrings | several cassettes | shark fin like teeth |
| Brake pads rim brake | 1,000-3,000km | Grooves in the surface disappeared |
| Disc brake pads | 1,000-4,000km | Cover thickness less than about 0.5 mm |
| tires | 3,000-8,000km | Puncture protection visible, cracks, no profile |
| hoses | several years | porous rubber, frequent air loss |
| Brake and shift cables | 1-3 years | Stiff levers, frayed ends |
| Rims with rim brakes | 10,000 km and more | Wear indicator disappeared |

A maintenance booklet helps to keep track of these times. This means you can replace parts specifically before they damage other components.

## The most common care mistakes

- **Too much oil:** A dripping chain attracts dirt and turns into grinding paste. Always wipe off excess oil after oiling.
- **The wrong oil:** Universal sprays from the household penetrate well, but lubricate poorly and evaporate quickly. Use a chain oil, a thicker one for wet conditions and a thinner one for dry conditions.
- **High-pressure cleaner:** It forces water into bearings and washes out the grease.
- **Oil on the brakes:** Chain oil on brake discs or rims is dangerous. Carefully insert oil spray and cover the brake.
- **Tighten screws “according to feel”:** Tightening too tightly leads to cracks, especially on carbon and aluminum parts. Work with a torque wrench.
- **Tyre pressure by thumb:** It is difficult to reliably estimate the pressure using the thumb. A pump with a pressure gauge costs little.
- **Ignore warning signs:** New noises, a spongy brake lever or a clunking handlebar are signs that should be taken seriously.

## Pedelec care: what is also important

A Pedelec basically needs the same care as a normal bicycle, just at shorter intervals. The motor transfers additional power to the chain, sprockets and chainrings, and the increased weight places greater strain on the brakes and tires. Therefore, check the chain and brake pads more often. Keep the battery and holder contacts clean and dry, charge the battery at room temperature, and update the software during inspection. ## Conclusion

Bike care doesn’t have to be complicated. A quick check before the ride, regular tire inflation and chain oiling, a monthly check and an annual inspection will keep most bikes in reliable condition for many years. Anyone who adapts the intervals to their driving behavior and the weather and honestly knows their limits when it comes to safety-related work will drive safer, more cheaply and with more joy.
