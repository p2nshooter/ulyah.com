Anyone who sits on a Pedelec for the first time is usually thrilled: after the first pedal stroke, the bike picks up powerfully, inclines lose their fear, and the headwind feels like a gentle breeze. At the same time, the engine changes the driving behavior. A Pedelec is heavier, accelerates faster and reaches higher speeds more often in everyday life. Many accidents with Pedelecs happen during the familiarization phase, especially for people returning to the sport and older people, especially when starting up, dismounting and cornering.

This guide will show you how to safely get used to a Pedelec, which support level is appropriate in which situation and which riding technique will help you ride in a relaxed and confident manner.

## The first contact: practice in a quiet place

Before you ride a new Pedelec into traffic, take half an hour in a quiet, level place, such as an empty parking lot on the weekend or a wide, low-traffic path.

1. **Start without engine:** First do a few laps with the assistance turned off. This way you can feel the weight and the steering behavior.
2. **Lowest level:** Switch on the lowest assistance level and drive away. Note how quickly the engine starts.
3. **Try higher levels:** Level up slowly. At high levels the bike pulls much more strongly when starting off.
4. **Practice braking:** Brake from different speeds, first gently, then more forcefully, with both brakes.
5. **Drive and steer slowly:** Drive at walking speed, tight corners and a figure eight. This trains your balance with the heavier bike.
6. **Shoulder look and hand signal:** Practice looking over your shoulder and removing one hand from the handlebars without losing track.

Many bicycle associations, traffic guards and dealers offer Pedelec training. They are particularly recommended for beginners.

## The right level of support

The support level not only affects range, but also safety.

- **Low level (Eco):** For starting in tight situations, intersections, driving in a group and on slippery surfaces. The engine starts more smoothly.
- **Intermediate level (tour):** For most of the route in everyday life.
- **High level (Sport, Turbo):** For steep inclines, heavy luggage and strong headwinds. In the city with many stops it is often too rough.

A good habit: **Switch to a low gear and an easy gear before stopping.** Then you drive off in a controlled manner at the traffic lights.

## Drive safely

Starting off is one of the most common situations for falls because the motor pushes hard with the first pedal stroke.

1. **Keep the wheel straight**, both brakes slightly pulled.
2. **Place a pedal in position:** approximately two o'clock, i.e. slightly above the horizontal position.
3. **Looking forward and to the side:** Is the path clear?
4. **Release the brakes and start gently**, not with full force.
5. **First start, then sit down:** When starting off, many people sit on the saddle after pedaling one or two times. This is easier with a deeper entry.

When climbing on, be careful not to accidentally step on the pedal while the assistance is on. Many systems react to even small pressure.

## Stop and dismount safely

1. **Brake early** because the Pedelec is heavier and has more momentum.
2. **Shift down before coming to a standstill** and reduce assistance.
3. **Slide forward from the saddle** and place one foot firmly on the ground.
4. **Hold the handlebars firmly when dismounting:** A heavy bike quickly tips to the side.
5. **When stopping for a long time, switch off the engine** or set the level to zero so that an accidental pedal stroke does not push the bike forward.

## Drive curves

- **Brake before the curve**, not in the curve.
- **Look into the curve:** Look where you want to go, not at the obstacle.
- **Inner corner pedal at the top:** This prevents the pedal from touching the ground.
- **Pedal or Roll Steady:** A sudden engine jolt while cornering can cause the rear wheel to shift, especially on wet surfaces. It's better to roll briefly in tight corners.
- **In wet conditions, leaves and cobblestones** drive significantly slower and lean less.

## Braking with the Pedelec

A Pedelec with a driver can quickly weigh over 100 kilograms, and significantly more with luggage or a child. At 25 km/h it takes a correspondingly long time to stop.

- **Use both brakes:** The front brake can transfer most of the braking force. Pull them in a measured and even manner.
- **Move your weight backwards:** When braking hard, straighten your arms and push your body backwards.
- **Drive with foresight:** Keep a greater distance from cyclists in front and be aware of pedestrians, doors of parked cars and vehicles turning suddenly.
- **Care for brakes:** Pedelecs wear out brake pads more quickly. Check them regularly.

## Uphill and downhill

- **Uphill:** Shift to an easier gear in good time and keep your cadence high. A mid-engine then works more efficiently. On very steep climbs, it is better to choose a higher support level than to struggle with a low cadence.
- **Starting on a hill:** Shift to the lightest gear before stopping. If possible, position the bike slightly perpendicular to the slope when starting off.
- **Downhill:** The motor no longer supports speeds above 25 km/h, but the bike becomes fast due to its weight. Brake at intervals, not dragging, to avoid overheating.
- **Pushing aid:** On very steep places or on slippery surfaces, it is often safer to get off and use the pushing aid.

## In traffic

Pedelecs are often underestimated by other road users. Anyone who sees a bike expects a slower pace. Therefore:

- **Expect other people's mistakes:** Drivers turning or coming out of driveways often misjudge the speed of a Pedelec. Be ready to brake.
- **Be visible:** Light even during the day can help, bright clothing and reflectors increase visibility.
- **Adjust your pace:** Drive significantly slower on shared footpaths and cycle paths and in narrow places. 25 km/h is often too fast there.
- **Overtaking at a distance:** If you overtake other cyclists, announce yourself with the bell and keep enough distance to the sides.
- **Not in the blind spot:** At intersections, do not stop to the right of a truck that may be turning.

## Luggage and loads

Luggage changes driving behavior:

- **Pack heavy loads low and in the middle**, for example in side pockets on the luggage rack instead of in a high basket.
- **Note the permissible total weight:** It is stated in the instructions or on the frame. Many Pedelecs are designed for 130 to 150 kilograms, cargo bikes for more.
- **With a child seat or trailer** drive off and brake particularly carefully and select a lower support level.

## For those returning to work and older people

Pedelecs enable many people to cycle again who have not done so for a long time. This is wonderful, but requires some caution:

- **Do training:** A Pedelec course provides safety in situations such as starting, dismounting and avoiding.
- **Choose a bike with a low entry** so that getting on and off is easy.
- **Prefer a gentle motor** with a torque sensor that does not start jerkily.
- **Wear a helmet:** A helmet is not mandatory for Pedelecs, but it makes sense.
- **Practice with friends** and drive the first routes on quiet paths.
- **If you have any health questions**, speak to your doctor beforehand.

## On field, forest and gravel paths

A Pedelec behaves differently on loose surfaces than on asphalt. The increased weight pushes the wheel into soft ground, and a powerful engine surge can cause the rear wheel to spin.

- **Choose a lower level:** Gentle support provides better traction.
- **Step evenly:** Avoid jerky steps, especially on slopes with gravel or roots.
- **Adjust tire pressure:** In the lower permitted range, tires grip better on loose surfaces.
- **Be considerate:** There are also pedestrians, dogs and riders on forest paths and shared paths. Drive past slowly and ring the bell early.
- **Observe the rules:** Pedelecs are allowed to ride where bicycles are allowed to ride. Some federal states have their own rules for forest paths.

## Riding in a group

When people drive together with and without a motor, different speeds quickly arise. With the Pedelec at a low level, you stay at the same pace as the group and conserve the battery at the same time. Keep enough distance, as your braking distance may be longer, and announce overtaking maneuvers. Anyone driving in front should set their pace according to the slowest passengers.

## The most common mistakes

- **Starting at the highest level:** The wheel shoots forward and you easily lose control.
- **Getting on the bike with the engine on and your foot on the pedal:** The bike starts moving unintentionally.
- **Brake too late:** Weight and speed are underestimated.
- **Curves with engine thrust:** The rear wheel can slip in wet or sandy conditions.
- **Hard gear on hills:** The engine works inefficiently and the drive is put under a lot of strain.
- **Too fast on shared paths:** 25 km/h between walkers is dangerous and rude.

## A small exercise program for the first week

- **Day 1:** Driving in a quiet place without and with a motor, starting and stopping at all levels.
- **Day 2:** Braking exercises from different speeds, slalom and figure eight.
- **Day 3:** Short lap on quiet residential streets, practice looking over your shoulder and hand signals.
- **Day 4:** A route with an incline, practice changing gears in good time.
- **Day 5:** The first trip in everyday life, for example to the bakery, at a quiet time of day.

After a week, the Pedelec feels familiar to most people. If you still feel unsafe, treat yourself to driving safety training.

## In the rain and darkness

Wet roads, leaves, road markings, manhole covers and tram tracks quickly become slippery with a heavy Pedelec. When it rains, drive down a notch, brake earlier and more gently, and cross rails at a right angle if possible. In the dark, a bright headlight that also illuminates the road in front of you helps, because with a Pedelec you are faster and need more visibility.

## Conclusion

A Pedelec makes cycling easier, but not automatically safer. If you get used to the engine in a quiet place, adjust the assistance level to the situation, downshift before stopping, drive carefully in curves and brake early and carefully, you will be relaxed and safe on the road. And anyone who expects to be underestimated in traffic will avoid most dangerous situations in advance.
