Many bikes disappear into the basement in November and only come out again in March. Cycling in winter is often better than its reputation: the paths are emptier, the air is clear, and those who exercise are less cold than at the bus stop. In many cities, important cycle paths are now cleared even in winter. Nevertheless, the cold season places special demands on the rider, bike and riding technique.

This guide shows you how to prepare yourself and your bike for winter, how to ride safely on snow and slippery roads, and when it makes more sense to leave your bike parked. If, on the other hand, you want to store your bike over the winter, you will find a separate guide for this.

## The biggest danger: slippery conditions

In winter, the main problem is not the cold, but the slipperiness. The following are particularly treacherous:

- **Freezing wet:** In the morning after a clear night, bridges, shady areas and paths through the forest are often slippery, even though the road otherwise looks dry.
- **Lightning ice:** When rain falls on frozen ground, a continuous layer of ice forms in a short time. Cycling is practically impossible in lightning ice.
- **Blocked snow and ice ruts:** Ice forms in car ruts, causing the front wheel to slide sideways.
- **Slush:** It brakes unevenly and hides potholes and curbs.
- **Leaves under snow:** In early winter there may be wet leaves under a thin blanket of snow.

A thermometer alone is not enough to make an assessment. Even at temperatures just above zero degrees, the ground can be frozen.

## Make the bike winter-ready

### tires

Tires are the deciding factor between grip and fall in winter.

| tires | Suitable for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Normal tires with good tread | wet and cold but ice-free roads | slightly less air pressure improves grip |
| Winter tires with a soft compound and coarse tread | Snow, mud, occasional slippery conditions | stay softer in the cold and stick better |
| Studded tires | Ice, packed snow, icy paths | Significantly more grip on ice, loud on dry asphalt and with more rolling resistance |

Studded tires are permitted for bicycles in Germany. Anyone who regularly travels on icy paths, such as uncleared cycle paths or at higher altitudes, will benefit enormously from this. Many winter commuters fit a studded tire at the front because a slipping front wheel almost always leads to a fall, while a slipping rear wheel is easier to catch. The safest option is two studded tires.

The **tire pressure** should be slightly lower in winter than in summer so that the contact area is larger. But stay within the range specified by the manufacturer, otherwise the risk of a breakdown increases.

### Lights, mudguards and brakes

- **Light:** In winter you often drive in the dark. A bright headlight and a good taillight are essential. Batteries lose power more quickly in the cold, so charge them before every ride or use a hub dynamo.
- **Mudguards:** They protect you and the bike from slush and salt water.
- **Brakes:** Check the brake pads before winter. Rim brakes wear out more quickly due to dirt and salt.
- **Chain and gears:** A well-oiled chain runs even in cold conditions. At very low temperatures, a hub gear can have advantages because it is closed and less sensitive to dirt.

### Lower the saddle a little

Many winter commuters lower their saddles by one or two centimeters in winter. This will help you get your feet on the ground more quickly if the bike slips. This isn't ideal for power transmission, but it can prevent falls on slippery paths.

## Driving technique on snow and slippery surfaces

On slippery surfaces: everything is slower, gentler and with more distance.

1. **Slow down:** Allow more time. Anyone who is rushed makes mistakes.
2. **Straight ahead over slippery roads:** Do not steer, brake or pedal on icy areas, but roll straight and calmly over them.
3. **Brake gently:** Brake early and especially with the rear brake. A blocked front wheel will almost certainly lead to a fall on ice.
4. **Take curves carefully:** Drive through the curve with little lean and the bike as upright as possible, slow down beforehand.
5. **Pedal evenly:** Drive in a slightly heavier gear so that the rear wheel doesn't spin when starting off.
6. **Avoid ruts:** Do not drive in icy car tracks and cross them as steeply as possible.
7. **Keep your distance:** Leave a lot more distance between yourself and other cyclists and cars, because they can also slip.
8. **Dismounting is permitted:** Pushing a short distance across an icy bridge is not a sign of weakness, but of experience.

### Cycle path or road in winter?

A cycle path with a blue sign must always be used. However, if it is not cleared, is icy or blocked by piles of snow, it cannot be used. Then you can drive on the road, which is often better cleared. Drive there clearly visible and at a distance from the edge, where there is often slush.

## Stay warm: Clothing based on the onion principle

When cycling you get warm, but the wind cools you down a lot. The trick is not to freeze and still not arrive sweaty. The onion principle with several layers has proven itself:

- **Base layer:** Functional underwear made from merino wool or synthetic fiber that wicks moisture away from the body. Cotton stays wet and cools down.
- **Middle layer:** Fleece or a thin sweater that keeps you warm.
- **Outer Layer:** A windproof, water-repellent jacket with ventilation openings.
- **Legs:** A pair of windproof pants or overpants. The knees are particularly sensitive to cold.
- **Hands:** Warm, windproof gloves are mandatory because cold fingers can no longer brake safely. When it's very cold, mittens or handlebar cuffs can help.
- **Head and Neck:** A thin helmet cap under the helmet and a tube scarf for the neck and face.
- **Feet:** Warm socks, waterproof shoes or overshoes.

A good tip: If you feel a little shivering when you drive off, you are properly dressed. After a few minutes you will feel warm.

## The Pedelec in winter

Pedelecs are popular in winter because with support you don't sweat as quickly. However, a few special features apply:

- **Range:** When it's cold, the range drops significantly, often by twenty to thirty percent or more.
- **Keep the battery warm:** Charge and store the battery in the apartment at room temperature and only use it shortly before driving. There are insulating protective covers for the battery.
- **Do not charge cold:** A very cold battery should first reach room temperature before charging.
- **Assistance in slippery conditions:** Choose a low level so that the rear wheel does not spin when starting off.

## Road salt: the bike's biggest enemy

Salt attacks the chain, sprockets, screws and bearings. With a little care you can prevent rust:

- **Rinse regularly:** After riding on salted roads, rinse the bike with lukewarm water, not a high-pressure cleaner.
- **Care for chain:** After rinsing, allow to dry and re-oil. More common in winter than in summer.
- **Protective spray:** A corrosion protection spray on screws and bare metal parts helps. Leave out the brake discs and rim sides.
- **Do not let it thaw in a warm hallway:** If the bike comes from very cold temperatures into a warm room, condensation will form. A cool, dry storage location is better.

## When is it better not to drive?

There are days when the bike should stop, even for experienced winter cyclists:

- in lightning ice and freezing rain,
- in the event of black ice warnings from the weather service,
- in heavy snow with poor visibility,
- when you are tired or unsure.

On such days, the better choice is to take the train, bus or walk. It's worth knowing the connection beforehand.

## Winter service: which paths are cleared

Whether a cycle path is cleared depends on the city or municipality. Many municipalities clear main roads, bus routes and important cycle routes first, secondary routes later or not at all. Some cities publish maps with their winter service routes for cycling. It's worth looking for it and planning your own commute accordingly.

- **Prefer cleared main routes:** A small detour along a cleared cycle route is safer than the shortest route along an icy side road.
- **Sidewalks remain taboo:** Even if the sidewalk is cleared and the bike path is not, you as an adult are not allowed to ride on the sidewalk. Move onto the road.
- **Report defects:** Many cities have online portals that you can use to report uncleaned or icy bike paths. This is how you help others too.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are studded tires worth it in the city?**
In cities with good winter services, the streets are often only icy for a few days. If you still drive every day, you will gain a lot of safety with studded tires on those days. If you switch to the train or bus when it's slippery, you can usually get by with good winter tires.

**How cold is too cold for cycling?**
There is no fixed limit. With good clothing, many people ride even in minus ten degrees. Smoothness is more important than temperature. In extreme cold, especially protect your hands, feet and face.

**Do I have to service my bike more often in winter?**
Yes. Chain, brakes and gears suffer from moisture, dirt and salt. A short care per week and a check in spring significantly extend the lifespan.

**Can I drive on dry asphalt with studded tires?**
Yes, that is allowed. However, the spikes wear out more quickly, are loud and increase rolling resistance. That's why many drivers switch back to normal tires in the spring.

## Winter checklist

- Tires with good tread or spikes fitted, pressure adjusted
- Light bright and charged, taillight clean
- Mudguards installed
- Brake pads checked
- Chain oiled, protective spray on screws
- Warm gloves, helmet cap, tube scarf
- More time planned, weather warnings checked

## Conclusion

Cycling in winter is possible with the right preparation. The biggest danger is slippery roads, which is why winter or studded tires, a little less air pressure and a careful driving technique are worthwhile: brake slowly, gently, roll straight ahead over ice and avoid ruts. Dress according to the onion principle, protect your hands and head and keep the Pedelec battery warm. Rinse off road salt regularly and oil the chain more often. And in the event of lightning ice or a warning of black ice, the bike stops, which is the wisest decision.
