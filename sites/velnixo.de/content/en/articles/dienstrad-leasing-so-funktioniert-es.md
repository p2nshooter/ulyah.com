A high-quality bicycle or Pedelec can easily cost several thousand euros. When leasing a company bike, the employee does not pay this amount in one fell swoop, but rather the employer leases the bike and lets it be used, even privately. For many, this is a convenient way to get a good bike, often at a lower cost than buying one. But the model has rules, and not every offer is worth it for everyone.

This guide explains how company bike leasing basically works, what variants there are, what costs are incurred and what you should pay attention to before signing. Tax rules can change and every employer designs their offer differently. Therefore, always check the current conditions and, if in doubt, ask the human resources department or tax advice.

## The principle

1. **You choose a bike,** usually from a specialist dealer who works with your employer's leasing provider.
2. **The employer leases the bike** from a lessor, usually for 36 months.
3. **You use the bike** for commuting and privately, without restriction to business trips.
4. **The leasing rate is paid:** either by the employer in addition to your salary or via salary conversion from your gross salary.
5. **At the end of the term** you return the bike or, depending on the offer, you can take it over at a residual value.

## The two basic models

| model | Who pays the installment? | Taxation of the benefit (as of the last few years) |
|---|---|---|
| In addition to salary | the employer, without deduction from salary | usually tax-free |
| Salary conversion | you, from the gross salary | Monetary benefit, flat rate with a small portion of the list price per month |

### In addition to salary

If the employer provides the bike in addition to the wages they are already owed, this is particularly favorable for the employees: they do not pay any installments and the benefit is tax-free according to the rules that have been in force in recent years. This model is rarer because the employer pays the full rate.

### Salary conversion

Salary conversion is more common: the leasing rate is deducted from the gross salary. This reduces wage tax and social security contributions. At the same time, private use must be taxed as a monetary benefit, at a flat rate of a small percentage of the list price per month. For bicycles and Pedelecs up to 25 km/h, this rate has been significantly lower in recent years than for company cars. The bottom line is that there is often a saving compared to buying, the amount of which depends on income, tax class and the employer's subsidy.

Many employers provide a **subsidy** for salary conversion, for example by covering the insurance or part of the installment. This makes the offer more attractive.

## Which bikes are possible?

- **Bicycles and Pedelecs up to 25 km/h:** are the standard for company bike leasing.
- **Cargo bikes:** are also offered by many providers, often with a higher maximum value.
- **S-Pedelecs up to 45 km/h:** are considered motor vehicles and are treated like company cars for tax purposes. Not every provider offers them.
- **Accessories:** Lock, bags, child seat and helmet can often be leased if purchased with the bike.

Many employers set an upper and lower limit on the purchase price and allow one or two bikes per person employed.

## Which is usually included in the rate

| Performance | Commonly included? |
|---|---|
| Leasing rate for bike and accessories | yes |
| Insurance against theft and damage | often compulsory, sometimes paid for by the employer |
| Maintenance and inspection | depending on the package, often at an additional cost |
| Wear parts such as tires, chain, brake pads | partly in the service package |
| Roadside assistance | for some packages |

Read the offer carefully: A cheap leasing without insurance and maintenance can become more expensive if the bike is stolen or major repairs are needed.

## A calculation example for orientation

The exact savings depends on many factors. However, a simplified example shows how the components work together:

- Pedelec with a list price of 3,500 euros, 36 month term
- Monthly leasing rate including insurance: around 110 euros, via salary conversion
- The conversion reduces taxes and social security contributions, while at the same time a small monetary benefit is taxed
- The actual net burden is often significantly lower than the rate
- At the end of the term, a takeover fee may apply if you want to keep the bike

Calculate with your real numbers. Many providers provide calculators that take tax class, income and subsidies into account. Compare the result with the purchase price plus insurance and maintenance over three years.

## What you should pay attention to

### Before signing

- **Compare total costs:** Compare leasing rates plus takeover price against the purchase price plus insurance and maintenance.
- **Impact on social benefits:** The salary conversion reduces the gross salary on which some benefits are calculated, such as unemployment benefit, parental benefit or later pension entitlements. The effects are usually small, but not zero.
- **Takeover at the end:** Is a takeover planned and at what price? Can the employer or lessor promise them in advance?
- **Dealer selection:** Can you buy from your preferred dealer, and does they also take care of maintenance and repairs?

### During the term

- **Comply with insurance conditions:** A specific lock is often required and the bike must be locked to a fixed object.
- **Perform inspections:** For packages with maintenance, regular inspections are often a requirement.
- **Report damage:** Report accidents and thefts immediately to the insurer and employer.

### In case of a job change, parental leave or illness

This is the point that many miss. What happens if you change employer during the term, go on parental leave or are ill for a long time and no longer have any salary from which the installment can be converted? Many providers have regulations for this, such as the contract being taken over by the new employer, a transfer or a return. Have these rules explained to you and put in writing before you sign.

## Leasing, purchasing or financing?

In addition to the company bike, there are two other ways to get an expensive bike. When you buy it in cash, the bike is yours immediately, you can sell, convert or rent it at any time, and there are no commitments to terms or the employer. With **financing** through the dealer, you pay in installments, often with interest, and the bike is yours after the last installment. Company bike leasing is usually an advantage when tax savings and employer subsidies come together. If you want to ride your bike for a long time and use it freely or can foresee a job change, you can often be more flexible with purchasing or financing it.

## Who is leasing a company bike worthwhile for?

Leasing a company bike is particularly worthwhile if:

- you want a high-quality bike or Pedelec and use it regularly,
- your employer gives a subsidy or takes over the insurance,
- you don't want to pay the entire purchase price at once,
- your job appears secure in the long term.

It may be less attractive if you only need a cheap bike, are planning a job change or your income is so low that the tax savings are small.

## Address employers

If your employer doesn't yet offer company bike leasing, it's worth asking. The offer is usually easy for companies to implement: a leasing provider takes over a large part of the administration, and healthy, satisfied employees are a win-win for both sides. It often helps if several colleagues register interest together. You can also ask about parking spaces, showers and lockers; you can find out more about this in the guide to commuting by bike.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use the company bike privately?**
Yes, that is exactly the point of the model. Private use is permitted and will be taken into account when converting the salary into the monetary benefit.

**Do I have to bike to work?**
As a rule not. Even those who use the bike primarily in their free time can lease it if their employer allows it.

**Can I keep the bike at the end?**
Often yes, for a takeover price offered by the lessor. The conditions differ depending on the provider. A binding commitment before the start of the contract is tax sensitive. Ask how your provider regulates this.

## Public service and the self-employed

In the public sector, leasing company bikes was difficult for a long time because salary conversions were not provided for in collective agreements. There are now collective agreements in place for many public sector employees that allow bicycle leasing. For civil servants, it depends on the employer whether and how a company bike is possible. Ask your office for the current status.

Self-employed people and freelancers can lease or buy a bicycle through their company if it is used for business purposes. The tax treatment depends on the proportion of business use. Tax advice makes sense here.

## Company bike and flat rate travel allowance

Anyone who rides a company bike to work can still claim the flat rate for the commute to work in their tax return according to the previous rules, because it does not depend on the means of transport. Check the current requirements when you file your tax return.

## Pre-closing checklist

1. Does my employer offer a subsidy or does he cover the insurance?
2. How high is the net monthly burden, calculated using my real figures?
3. How much does the bike cost to buy outright, including insurance and maintenance for three years?
4. What services does the service package include and which dealers can I use?
5. What happens if you change jobs, take parental leave, have a long illness or are stolen?
6. Can I take over the bike at the end and under what conditions?
7. Does my lock meet the insurance requirements?

Once you have answered these questions, you will be able to decide whether a company bike is a better choice for your own situation than buying one.

## Conclusion

With company bike leasing, the employer leases a bike or Pedelec that you also use privately. If you get it in addition to your salary, it is particularly cheap. With the common salary conversion, you save taxes and duties, but you pay tax on a small monetary benefit, and a subsidy from the employer often makes the offer much more attractive. Compare the total costs with a purchase, pay attention to insurance and maintenance and let us explain what happens if you change jobs, take parental leave or become ill. Then a company bike can be a very good way to get a high-quality bike for everyday use.
