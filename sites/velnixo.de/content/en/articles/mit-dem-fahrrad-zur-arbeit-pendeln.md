Anyone who cycles to work begins and ends the working day on the move. Instead of standing in traffic or waiting for a late train, you roll past the queue, save money and do something for your health at the same time. Many who have tried the switch don't want to go back. Nevertheless, there are legitimate questions: Is the distance too far? What do I do when it rains? And how do I get there without sitting in the office drenched in sweat?

This guide answers these questions step by step, from choosing the route to the right bike and clothing to costs and support from the employer.

## Which routes are realistic?

How far you can cycle to work depends on your fitness, terrain and bike. As a rough guide:

| Easy route | Normal bike | Pedelec |
|---|---|---|
| up to 5 km | for almost everyone, 15 to 20 minutes | relaxed, hardly any effort |
| 5 to 10 km | easily doable, 20 to 40 minutes | very comfortable without sweating |
| 10 to 15 km | easy to do for experienced people | easy to do, even with gradients |
| 15 to 25 km | more sporty | realistic for regular commuting |
| over 25 km | sporty, for a few days per week | with S-Pedelec or combination with the train |

In cities, the bicycle is often the fastest means of transport for distances of up to five kilometers because there is no need to look for a parking space, traffic jams or waiting times. With a Pedelec, the useful radius approximately doubles.

You don't have to drive every day. Many people start with two or three days a week and increase as the routine settles in.

## Find the right route

The fastest car route is rarely the best bike route. When planning, pay attention to:

- **Low-traffic streets:** Side streets, bicycle streets, paths through parks and along rivers are more relaxed and often hardly longer.
- **Good cycle paths:** Wide, well-developed cycle paths with few junctions are safer than narrow paths behind parked cars.
- **Few traffic lights and intersections:** Every traffic light takes time, every large intersection requires attention.
- **Lighting:** In autumn and winter you ride in the dark. Illuminated paths are more pleasant and safer.
- **Altitude:** If you drive downhill to work, you arrive relaxed and only sweat on the way home.

Plan the route using a bike navigation app that favors low-traffic routes and ride it for the first time on the weekend, without time pressure. After a few weeks, you often find shortcuts and hidden routes that no app knows about.

## The right bike

Almost any roadworthy bike is suitable for commuting to work. However, some features have proven useful for daily commuting:

- **Mudguards:** Without them, every puddle will cause water to land on your back and pants.
- **Fixed light:** A hub dynamo or battery light that is always on the bike makes you independent of charging cycles.
- **Luggage rack:** For pannier, laptop and shopping to keep your back free.
- **Anti-puncture tires:** Getting a flat tire on the way to work is annoying. Tires with puncture protection significantly reduce the risk.
- **Low maintenance:** Hub gears or a robust derailleur gear, disc brakes or good rim brakes, possibly a chain guard.

A trekking bike or a well-equipped city bike does all of this. A Pedelec is worth it for longer distances, climbs or if you don't want to arrive sweaty. More about this in the guide “Which bike is right for me?”.

## Don't arrive sweaty

The fear of arriving at the office sweaty stops many people from cycling. You can get this under control with a few tricks:

- **Drive slower:** If you drive comfortably, you sweat significantly less. Deliberately roll out the last one or two kilometers in a relaxed manner.
- **Functional clothing:** A functional shirt under the jacket transports moisture to the outside and dries quickly. Cotton stays wet.
- **Onion principle:** It's better to start slightly freezing. After a few minutes you will feel warm.
- **Bags instead of a backpack:** A backpack leads to a wet back. Panniers on the luggage rack carry the weight.
- **Change of clothing:** Leave a shirt or blouse in the office, possibly a pair of shoes.
- **Freshen up quickly:** Wet wipes, deodorant and a towel are often enough. Some employers offer showers.
- **Pedelec:** With motor support, you can travel longer distances with almost no sweat.

## Clothing for all weathers

| Weather | What helps |
|---|---|
| Summer | Airy functional clothing, sunglasses, sunscreen, a change of shirt in the office |
| Spring and autumn | Windbreaker, thin gloves, arm warmers or a light vest |
| Rain | Rain jacket with ventilation, rain pants or rain cape, overshoes, waterproof bags |
| Winter | warm, windproof gloves, helmet cap, tube scarf, windproof jacket, warm shoes |

For everyday office life, there are now cycling clothes that don't look like sports: pants with stretch, jackets with reflective elements in a subtle design, rain capes that fit over everyday clothing. More about rain protection in the rainwear guide.

## Luggage: laptop, documents and lunch

- **Commuter bag for the luggage rack:** Many panniers have a padded laptop compartment and can be removed and carried like a briefcase.
- **Waterproof:** Documents and electronics must remain dry in the rain. Roll-top bags are reliable.
- **Front basket:** Practical for small purchases on the way home, but not for valuables.

You can find more selection help in the guide to bags, baskets and luggage racks.

## Safe on the way to work

- **Light and visibility:** In fall and winter you often ride at dusk. Bright lights and reflective elements are mandatory for your safety.
- **Drive with foresight:** Many people are stressed in rush hour traffic. Expect cars turning, doors opening and trucks in your blind spots.
- **Helmet:** Not required but recommended.
- **Regular Inspection:** Check brakes, lights and tire pressure once a week.

The guides on traffic rules and safe driving in the city explain the most important rules and danger spots.

## Good for head and body

Regular cycling is one of the easiest ways to incorporate exercise into your everyday life, without any additional appointments. Just 20 to 30 minutes of moderate cycling each way adds up to the amount of exercise health organizations recommend for adults over the course of a week. Many commuters also report that they arrive at the office more alert after the journey and are able to switch off on the way home from the workday. If you have health problems or have not done any sport for a long time, you can start with short distances or a Pedelec and seek medical advice if necessary.

## Park the bike at work

A safe parking space often determines whether bike commuting works in everyday life. A covered, lockable bicycle room or a bicycle garage on the company premises is ideal. Where this does not exist, a sturdy lock on a fixed bracket within sight of the entrance will help. Many employers are open to setting up parking facilities if employees ask. More about this in the guide to theft protection.

## How much does cycling commute cost?

Compared to a car, the bike is very cheap. A simple calculation for a ten kilometer commute to work:

| Post | Bicycle | Pedelec |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase (spread over years) | 100 to 200 euros per year | 300 to 600 euros per year |
| Maintenance and wear and tear | 100 to 200 euros per year | 150 to 300 euros per year |
| Electricity | — | a few euros per year |
| Insurance (optional) | 50 to 100 euros per year | 80 to 150 euros per year |

For a car, this means a multiple of fuel, wear and tear and parking costs for the same journey. In addition, the flat-rate travel allowance for income tax applies regardless of the means of transport, including cycling.

## Support from the employer

Many employers encourage cycling:

- **Company bike leasing:** The employer leases a bike or Pedelec, you also use it privately, the installment is often paid through salary conversion. Find out more in the guide to company bike leasing.
- **Storage facilities, showers, lockers:** Good infrastructure makes switching easy.
- **Campaigns:** Many companies take part in campaigns in which employees collect cycling kilometers together over several weeks.

If this doesn't exist at your workplace, it's worth asking. Often just the stimulus is missing.

## Combine bike and train

If the commute is too long, the bike can take you to the train station and from the train station to work. A folding bike fits on every train, and a second simple bike at the destination station saves you having to take it with you. You can find out more about this in the guide to taking your bike on the train.

## This is how you get started

1. **Test the route on the weekend** and measure the travel time.
2. **Start with two days per week,** on days with good weather.
3. **Leave a change of clothes in the office.**
4. **Make the bike suitable for commuting:** Lights, mudguards, luggage rack, good tires.
5. **Get rain gear** before fall comes.
6. **Approach colleagues:** Commuting together is more fun and you learn the best ways from each other.

## Frequently asked questions

**Am I insured when cycling to work?**
Anyone who is employed is generally covered by statutory accident insurance on their direct journey to work and back, regardless of the means of transport. Detours for private errands can interrupt protection. You report an accident on the way to work to your employer.

**What do I do on days with storms or black ice?**
Leave the bike behind and take the train, bus or car. Bike commuting doesn't mean having to ride in all weathers. A well-known alternative for such days takes the pressure off.

**Is a Pedelec worth going to work?**
For distances of more than ten kilometers, a lot of inclines or if you don't want to move around in the office, a Pedelec is often the key to regular commuting. It can be financed more cheaply with a company bike.

**How much time do I have to plan?**
In the city, expect an average speed of around 15 to 18 kilometers per hour for a normal bike and 18 to 22 for a Pedelec, including traffic lights and intersections. Allow some buffer time in the first few weeks until you know your route.

## Conclusion

Cycling is possible for almost everyone over distances of up to ten kilometers, and even much further with a Pedelec. Choose a low-traffic route and test it without time pressure. A bike with mudguards, fixed lights, luggage rack and puncture protection tires makes everyday life easy. Drive comfortably, wear functional clothing, use panniers instead of backpacks and leave a change of clothes in the office, then you'll arrive relaxed. Start with a few days per week and ask your employer about parking spaces and company bike leasing. After just a few weeks, many people notice that cycling to work has become the most relaxing part of the day.
