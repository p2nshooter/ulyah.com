A new Pedelec can easily cost several thousand euros. It's no wonder that many people are looking on the used market. There are actually good offers there: bikes from people who have moved, switched to a cargo bike or taken over a company bike after the end of the leasing period. At the same time, buying a used Pedelec is more demanding than buying a normal bike. The battery, motor and electronics are expensive, difficult to test and can have hidden defects.

This guide supplements our general checklist for buying used bikes with everything that is additionally important when it comes to Pedelecs. This way you can recognize a good offer and avoid expensive surprises.

## The most important question first: How is the battery doing?

The battery is the most expensive wearing part. Depending on the system, a replacement battery often costs several hundred euros, and large batteries can cost over a thousand. That's why you should know the condition as precisely as possible.

### Age and charging cycles

Question about:

- **When was the bike purchased?** Preferably with an invoice.
- **Is the battery original or has it already been replaced?**
- **How many kilometers were driven?** Many displays show the total mileage.
- **How ​​was the battery stored?** In the winter in a warm house or in a cold shed?

A battery ages through charging cycles and over time. A five-year-old battery that has been left in the sun a lot can be worse than an older, well-maintained one.

### Diagnosis at the specialist dealer

The most reliable method is to **read the battery using the manufacturer's diagnostic software**. Many bike repair shops can do this for a small fee. Among other things, the number of charging cycles, the remaining capacity and any errors are displayed. Ask the seller to take the bike to a workshop together or ask for a current diagnostic report. A reputable seller has no problem with this.

### Test without diagnosis

If a diagnosis is not possible, a practical test can help:

1. Show the battery fully charged.
2. Take a test ride of at least 15 to 20 minutes at a medium or high level of assistance, preferably with an incline.
3. Watch how quickly the charge bar drops. If it jumps from full to half after a short time, the battery has probably aged significantly.
4. Pay attention to whether the engine suddenly switches off under load even though charging is still displayed.

### External examination

Inspect the battery case for cracks, dents, deformation, and signs of drops. Check the contacts for corrosion and signs of burns. A bloated, damaged or unusually warm battery is a clear exclusion criterion. Also have the **charger** shown to you and check whether it is the original charger.

## Motor and electronics

### Test drive the engine

- **Use of assistance:** Does the motor kick in smoothly and evenly as you pedal?
- **Noise:** Clicking, grinding, grinding or a loud, irregular engine sound may indicate bearing damage or gear wear.
- **Shutdown at 25 km/h:** The assistance should end gently at around 25 km/h. If the motor supports significantly longer, the wheel could have been manipulated.
- **Test all levels:** Do all support levels and the pushing aid work?
- **Display and operation:** Does the display show everything correctly? Do all buttons work?

### Error memory

Many systems store errors in the engine or display. They are read out during the diagnosis in the workshop. Recurring errors may indicate problems with sensors, cables, or the motor.

### Cables and plugs

Check visible cables and connectors for chafing, kinks and moisture. Loose or damaged cables are often the cause of dropouts.

## Warning sign of manipulation

A tuned Pedelec for which the switch-off limit of 25 km/h has been lifted is no longer legally a bicycle and may not be ridden in traffic. Tuning also endangers insurance coverage and can overload the technology. Evidence of manipulation:

- Additional components on the speed sensor or on the cables.
- The spoke magnet is in an unusual position, is missing or there are two magnets.
- The speed shown obviously does not correspond to reality.
- The support ends well above 25 km/h.
- The seller mentions that the bike is “made faster.”

Only buy such a wheel if the manipulation has been professionally reversed and have it checked in the workshop.

## Frame, brakes and drive

Pedelecs are heavier and are often ridden faster than normal bicycles. This is why some parts wear out more quickly.

- **Frame:** Look for cracks on weld seams, especially around the engine, head tube and rear stays, as well as signs of falls.
- **Brakes:** Pedelecs need powerful brakes. Check the pressure point, pads and discs. Scoring and discolored panes indicate heavy use.
- **Drive:** Chain, sprocket and chainring wear out more quickly on mid-engines. Measure the chain wear and look at the teeth. A complete drive change can quickly cost a three-digit amount.
- **Wheels and tires:** The rear wheel in particular is subject to a lot of stress. Watch out for loose spokes, side hits and worn tires.
- **Suspension fork:** When was it last serviced?

## Documents and keys

A used Pedelec should be handed over with the following documents and parts:

- **Purchase invoice** with frame number, ideally in the seller's name.
- **Instruction manual** for wheel and motor system.
- **Inspection certificates** if available.
- **Battery key**, preferably two, with the **key number**. The battery often cannot be removed without a key, and a new lock is expensive.
- **Charger**.
- For some systems, access data or the option to re-register the bike to you in an app.

## Detect stolen Pedelecs

Pedelecs are a popular stolen item because of their value. The warning signs from our general used buying checklist apply particularly here:

- A very low price for a new bike.
- No bill, no keys, no charger.
- A filed or missing frame number.
- A salesman who knows little about the bike.
- A meeting point in public space and time pressure.

If possible, check the frame number against stolen bike databases. Some manufacturers can use the serial number of the motor or battery to check whether a bike has been reported stolen.

## What a used Pedelec can cost

The loss of value is high in the first few years, after which the price depends heavily on the condition of the battery. When negotiating the price you should consider:

- **Battery condition:** A weak battery significantly reduces the value because a replacement is due soon.
- **Wearing parts:** Drive, brakes and tires.
- **Maintenance due:** such as a suspension fork or engine inspection.
- **Availability of spare parts:** Older systems that no longer have batteries are worth significantly less.

## Alternatives to private purchase

- **Specialist dealers** often offer tested used bikes with a diagnostic report and warranty.
- **Returns from company bike leasing** are often processed and sold with a guarantee.
- **Specialized used bike dealers** check the battery and motor and sometimes give a guarantee.

These options are a little more expensive than buying privately, but offer significantly more security.

## Checklist: used Pedelec

1. Invoice, frame number, key with number, charger and instructions available?
2. Battery externally undamaged, contacts clean?
3. Battery diagnosis with charging cycles and remaining capacity, or detailed practical test?
4. Engine starts smoothly, no noticeable noises, switches off at around 25 km/h?
5. No indication of tuning?
6. Frame without cracks, brakes strong, drive not worn out?
7. Nearby workshop that can service the system and replacement batteries available?
8. Price takes battery condition and wear parts into account?
9. Written purchase contract with all data?

## What else belongs in the purchase contract

In addition to the usual information such as names, addresses, purchase price, date and frame number, the purchase contract for a Pedelec should contain:

- the **serial numbers of the motor and battery**, which are often found on a sticker,
- the **mileage** according to the display,
- the **result of the battery diagnosis**, if one was made,
- the assurance that the wheel has not been tampered with,
- the **keys, charger and documents** provided,
- Known defects, such as a weak battery or a chain that needs to be replaced.

Many associations and insurers offer templates that can be supplemented with these points.

## After purchase: the first steps

1. **Inspection:** Have the bike inspected at a workshop, update software and clear fault memory if not already done.
2. **Registration:** If possible, register the bike with the manufacturer or in the app. This is helpful for warranty questions and in the event of theft.
3. **Insurance:** Check whether your household contents or special bicycle insurance covers the Pedelec. Many insurance companies require an invoice or purchase contract and a specific lock.
4. **Coding:** Coding with owner identification makes resale by thieves more difficult.
5. **Battery care:** Charge the battery fully, get to know the display and get used to the care rules from our battery guide.

## The test drive with a used Pedelec

Plan more time for the test ride than with a normal bike, ideally half an hour. This is how you do it:

1. **Start cold:** Have the bike shown to you before the seller has “warmed it up”. Some problems are particularly noticeable when you switch it on for the first time.
2. **Roll without motor:** Drive a few meters without support. Does the bike run smoothly or does something brake noticeably?
3. **Switch through levels:** Drive a few hundred meters in each support level.
4. **Slope:** Ride an incline at medium and high levels. Pay attention to noises and whether the engine pulls smoothly.
5. **Emergency braking:** In a quiet place, test both brakes firmly.
6. **Shifting under load:** Shift through all gears under light load.
7. **Watch the display:** Note the battery level before and after the ride.

If the seller refuses a longer test drive, that is at least a reason to inquire more closely.

## Conclusion

A used Pedelec can be a very good investment if you check thoroughly. The battery determines the value: If at all possible, have it read out in a workshop. Check the engine, electronics, brakes and drive, make sure the documents and keys are complete and be consistent when there are indications of tuning or theft. It's better to miss out on a good deal than to buy an expensive problem.
