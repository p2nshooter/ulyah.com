Owning your first bike is a big step towards independence for children. To ensure that the step doesn't become a disappointment, the bike has to fit: it has to be light enough for the child to master, have the right size so that it can get on and off safely, and have equipment that is appropriate for the child's age and abilities.

Many parents buy a children's bike based on age or what is currently on sale. It is better to decide based on body measurements and level of development. In this guide we explain how to do this and what mistakes you should avoid.

## From balance bike to bicycle

Most children today first learn on a balance bike. It has no pedals, so the child pushes off with their feet and learns to keep their balance all by themselves. Many children then switch straight to a bike without training wheels because they have already mastered balancing.

**Training wheels** are viewed critically by many experts. They prevent the child from learning to lean into curves and can even cause them to tip over in curves. If your child is already riding a balance bike safely, they usually no longer need training wheels.

A good time for the first bike with pedals is when the child rolls long distances on the balance bike with his feet raised, steers safely and wants to brake. This is the case for many children between the ages of three and five, but every child is different.

## The wheel size: inches do not equal age

Children's bikes are classified according to the diameter of the wheels in inches. The following table gives rough guidelines. What is crucial, however, is the **inner leg length**, i.e. the child's stride length.

| Wheel size | approximate height | approximate inside leg length | typical age |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 inches | 90-105cm | 35-45cm | 2.5-4 years |
| 14 inches | 100-110cm | 40-48cm | 3.5-5 years |
| 16 inches | 105-120cm | 45-55cm | 4-6 years |
| 20 inches | 115-135cm | 53-63cm | 6-8 years |
| 24 inches | 130-150cm | 60-72cm | 8-11 years |
| 26 inch / XS frame | from around 145 cm | from around 68 cm | from 10-12 years |

The values vary depending on the manufacturer because frames and seat posts are constructed differently. Many manufacturers now specify a minimum and maximum inside leg length. This is the most reliable information.

### This is how you measure the inside leg length

Have your child stand barefoot with their back against a wall, feet slightly apart. Slide a book horizontally up between your legs until it rests lightly on your crotch. Then measure the distance from the top edge of the book to the floor.

### Check the fit

The following applies when testing:

- **For beginners:** The child should reach the floor with the balls of both feet while sitting. This way it feels safe and can stop at any time.
- **For experienced drivers:** It is enough for the child to reach the ground with their toes while sitting. The legs are then better stretched when pedaling.
- **Handlebars:** The child should reach the handlebars with their arms slightly bent, without having to stretch too far forward.
- **Brake lever:** The child's hand must be able to comfortably grip the lever and pull firmly.

## The most common mistake: buying too big

Children grow quickly, and it's understandable that parents want to buy a bike that will "last a while." But a bike that is too big is heavy for children, difficult to steer and difficult to brake. In an emergency, the child cannot get their feet on the floor quickly enough. You lose the joy of driving and the risk of accidents increases.

It is better to buy a suitable bike and pass it on or sell it later. Good children's bikes have a surprisingly stable resale value, especially if they come from well-known manufacturers.

## Weight: the underestimated factor

A children's bike should be as light as possible. Many cheap children's bikes weigh ten kilograms or more. For a child who weighs 20 kilograms, it is like an adult riding a bike weighing over 35 kilograms.

As a rule of thumb, the bike should not weigh more than 30 to 40 percent of the child's body weight, the lighter the better. Lightweight children's bikes often have an aluminum frame, do without unnecessary equipment and have light wheels. It costs a little more, but makes a huge difference when driving, starting and climbing hills.

## The brakes

Children must be able to brake safely. The important things are:

- **Child-friendly brake levers:** The distance between the lever and the handle must be adjustable for small hands. The brake must act with little force.
- **Two handbrakes:** Many experts recommend getting children used to two handbrakes early on because they will be driving with them later anyway.
- **Coaster brake:** It is often not required by law for small children's bikes, but is common. Some children cope well with this, others brake unintentionally when they turn the pedals back. If a coaster brake is installed, there should also be at least one good handbrake.

Practice braking with your child in a quiet place before driving into traffic.

## The corridors

- **Up to 16 inches:** Usually without gears. This is easier, simpler and completely sufficient.
- **20 inches:** Makes sense depending on where you live. For flat areas, one gear or a three-speed hub is often sufficient; in hilly areas, a simple derailleur gear can help.
- **24 inches and larger:** A gearshift with seven to nine gears makes longer distances and inclines easier, for example on the way to school.

It is important that the gear levers can be operated by children's hands. Twist grip switches are often easier for small hands than thumb levers.

## Equipment for road transport

As soon as your child rides in traffic, the bike must comply with the regulations of the StVZO. These include two independent brakes, a high-pitched bell, headlights and taillights, and reflectors at the front, rear, pedals and sides. We explain more about the exact regulations in the guide to bicycle lighting.

Also useful are:

- **mudguards** for rainy days,
- a **chain guard** so that the trousers don't get caught in the chain,
- a **stand** to prevent the bike from being knocked over,
- a **luggage rack** or basket for the school bag from primary school age onwards.

For very small children's bikes that are only ridden on the playground or sidewalk, full equipment is often omitted in order to keep the weight low.

## The helmet

In Germany there is no requirement to wear a helmet when cycling, not even for children. Nevertheless, many traffic experts strongly recommend that children wear a helmet because they fall more often and their heads are particularly in need of protection. The helmet must fit, must not wobble and should be replaced after a fall. Parents are a good role model when they wear a helmet themselves.

## New or used?

Children's bikes are often only used for two or three years and are therefore often in good condition when used. A used brand bike is usually a better choice than a cheap new bike. Check the brakes, tires, chain and bearings as you would an adult bike. Pay particular attention to ensuring that the brake levers are not bent and that the brakes are strong.

## Where to go with the child on the bike?

In Germany, children up to the age of eight must ride on the sidewalk if there is no structurally separated cycle path. Children up to the age of ten are allowed to use the sidewalk. A supervisor aged 16 or over may accompany a child under eight years of age on the sidewalk. Walking speed is required on the sidewalk and pedestrians have priority. Find out more in our guide to cycling safely with children.

## Checklist for buying a children's bike

1. Measure the inside leg length and compare with the manufacturer's instructions.
2. Test sitting: Feet reach the ground, handlebars are easily accessible.
3. Check the weight and choose as light as possible.
4. Brake lever is suitable for children and can be operated with little force.
5. Equipment suitable for use: playground, sidewalk or school route.
6. Don't buy too big, change later.
7. Consider used brand bikes as an alternative.

## What a good children's bike costs

Good, light children's bikes from specialized manufacturers cost around 250 to 600 euros, depending on the size. That's significantly more than a department store bike for 120 euros, but the difference when riding is enormous: less weight, better brakes, more durable bearings and child-friendly geometry. Because these bikes have a good resale value, the actual price after two or three years is often not that much higher. You should also plan a helmet, possibly a bell and light for the way to school, and a simple lock.

## Preparing the child for the bike

A good bike is only half the battle. With a few playful exercises in a traffic-free place, your child will quickly become confident:

1. **Starting and stopping:** Place one pedal forward, push hard, then stop specifically at a line.
2. **Practice braking:** Stopping from different speeds using both brakes. Explain that the front brake has a strong effect and must be applied carefully.
3. **Drive slowly:** If you can drive slowly, you have your balance under control. A race to see who can drive the slowest is fun for children.
4. **Slalom and curves:** Build a small course with pylons, chalk or branches.
5. **Driving with one hand and looking over your shoulder:** Important for the hand signal when turning and looking backwards, first briefly, then longer.
6. **Recognize obstacles:** Look for and discuss curbs, potholes and wet leaves together.

Praise and patience are more important than speed. Children learn at their own pace.

## Before every season: the short children's bike check

Children's bikes are often left outside, fall over and are not as cared for as adult bikes. So check in spring and before the summer holidays:

- Are the saddle and handlebars still at the right height? Children grow quickly.
- Are both brakes powerful and can the child still reach the levers easily?
- Do the tires have enough air and tread?
- Is the chain oiled and not too loose?
- Do the bell, lights and reflectors work?
- Are all screws tight, especially on the handlebars and pedals?

## Conclusion

The right children's bike is light, has the right size and brakes that the child can use safely. If you buy according to inner leg length instead of age, pay attention to weight and resist the temptation to buy too big, you are not only giving your child a bike, but also a bit of freedom, security and the joy of movement.
