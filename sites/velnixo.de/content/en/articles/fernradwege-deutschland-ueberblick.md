Germany is a country for cyclists. A dense network of long-distance cycle paths leads along large rivers, over old railway lines, past the North and Baltic Seas and through the low mountain ranges. Many of these routes are signposted throughout, are mostly away from car traffic and have a good infrastructure with accommodation, inns, workshops and train stations.

This guide introduces well-known long-distance cycle routes, explains how the routes are marked and how you can tell which route is right for you. The lengths given are guidelines, as many routes have variants, detours and sections abroad.

## How long-distance cycle paths are organized

### The cycling network in Germany

The Germany cycle network, formerly called D-Netz, connects twelve supra-regional routes that run through the entire country. These include the North Sea Coastal and Baltic Sea Coastal Routes, the Rhine Route, the Danube Route, the Elbe Cycle Path and the Oder-Neisse Cycle Path. The routes are marked with their own logo, which is displayed in addition to the regional signage.

### EuroVelo

Some routes are part of EuroVelo, the European long-distance cycle path network. For example, if you drive along the Rhine, you are on the EuroVelo 15, which leads from the source in the Swiss Alps to the mouth in the North Sea. EuroVelo is a good orientation for cross-border tours.

### ADFC quality cycle routes

The General German Bicycle Club awards cycle routes with one to five stars. Among other things, the condition of the route, signage, traffic volume, route guidance, tourism offers and connections to train stations are assessed. A route with four or five stars is usually well developed and well signposted. This is helpful guidance for beginners.

## River cycle paths: ideal for beginners

River cycle paths are the most popular long-distance cycle routes, and for good reason: they are mostly flat, orientation is easy, and there are many towns, villages and train stations along the rivers. If you travel downstream, there is a slight gradient, although often hardly noticeable.

### Elbe cycle path

The Elbe cycle path has been one of the most popular cycle paths in Germany for many years. In Germany alone it runs well over 800 kilometers from Saxon Switzerland on the Czech border to the North Sea near Cuxhaven, and with the Czech section it is even longer. On the way there are Dresden, Meißen, the Lutherstadt Wittenberg, Magdeburg and Hamburg, as well as the Elbe sandstone mountains, floodplain landscapes and the biosphere reserve on the middle Elbe. The route is mostly flat and well developed, in some places there are variants on both sides of the river.

### Weser cycle path

The Weser cycle path begins in Hann. Münden, where the Werra and Fulda flow together to form the Weser, and leads around 500 kilometers to the North Sea near Cuxhaven or Bremerhaven. It runs through the Weserbergland with its half-timbered towns, past the Pied Piper town of Hameln, through Minden and Bremen. A good route for a first longer tour of around a week.

### Danube cycle path

The Danube Cycle Path is one of the most famous river cycle paths in Europe. In Germany it runs from Donaueschingen in the Black Forest via Ulm, Ingolstadt and Regensburg to Passau, around 600 kilometers. Many cyclists continue on to Austria, where the section from Passau to Vienna is one of the busiest cycle paths in Europe. In the German part, the section through the Upper Danube Valley with its limestone cliffs is a particular highlight.

### Main cycle path

The Main cycle path connects the sources of the White and Red Main in Upper Franconia with the mouth of the Rhine near Mainz, around 600 kilometers. It leads through Franconia with its vineyards, past Bamberg, Würzburg and Aschaffenburg to Frankfurt. The Main cycle path was one of the first routes to be awarded five stars by the ADFC.

### Moselle cycle path

The Moselle cycle path follows the river through one of the most famous wine-growing regions in Germany. The German part runs around 250 kilometers from the border at Perl via Trier, Bernkastel-Kues and Cochem to the mouth of the Rhine at the Deutsches Eck in Koblenz. The route is flat, scenic and has many wine towns with accommodation. A good choice for leisure cyclists.

### Rhine cycle path

The Rhine cycle path is part of EuroVelo 15 and leads from the Swiss Alps to the North Sea in the Netherlands, a total of well over 1,000 kilometers. In Germany, the Middle Rhine Valley with its castles between Bingen and Koblenz, the route through Cologne and Düsseldorf and the Upper Rhine between Basel and Karlsruhe are among the well-known sections. The path is mostly flat, but in the Middle Rhine Valley it is narrow in places and close to roads and trains.

### More river cycle paths

- **Altmühltal cycle path:** through the Altmühltal Nature Park in Bavaria, scenic and manageable.
- **Ruhr Valley Cycle Path:** from the source in the Sauerland through the Ruhr area to the mouth of the Rhine near Duisburg, with many traces of industrial history.
- **Fulda and Werra cycle path:** through the middle of Germany to the beginning of the Weser, can be easily combined with the Weser cycle path.
- **Oder-Neiße cycle path:** along the German-Polish border from the source of the Neisse to the Baltic Sea, quiet and close to nature.
- **Saale cycle path and Neckartal cycle path:** with castles, vineyards and old university towns.

## Coastal cycle paths

### Baltic coast cycle path

The Baltic Sea cycle path in Germany runs over 1,000 kilometers from Flensburg to the island of Usedom on the Polish border. On the way there are Kiel, Lübeck, Wismar, Rostock, Stralsund, the island of Rügen and many seaside resorts. The route is mostly flat, but there are also hilly sections on the cliffs and in Schleswig-Holstein. There's a lot going on on the coast in summer, but it's quieter in early summer and early autumn.

### North Sea coast cycle path

The North Sea Coast Cycle Path is part of EuroVelo 12, which leads around the North Sea. In Germany it runs from the Dutch to the Danish border, often directly on or behind the dike. The landscape is flat, but the wind can make the stages very strenuous. Those who drive from west to east often have a tailwind because the wind on the North Sea blows predominantly from the west.

## Routes through low mountain ranges and regions

If you are looking for variety and more altitude, you will find beautiful routes in the low mountain ranges, for example in the Black Forest, the Harz, the Rhön, the Thuringian Forest or the Swabian Alb. Many of them use old railway lines that only have slight gradients. These routes are sportier and are well suited for Pedelecs.

### Lake Constance cycle path

The Lake Constance cycle path leads around the lake through Germany, Austria and Switzerland, a total of around 260 kilometers. It can be done in four to five days and offers views of the Alps, islands like Mainau and Reichenau, vineyards and orchards. Ferries across the lake allow for shortcuts.

### Berlin–Copenhagen cycle path

The Berlin–Copenhagen cycle path connects two capitals over around 630 kilometers. It leads through the lake landscape of Brandenburg and Mecklenburg, to the Baltic Sea and by ferry to Denmark. A varied route for two weeks that can also be driven in sections.

## Railway cycle paths: gentle gradients even in the mountains

A special feature are cycle paths on disused railway lines. Because railways could only handle small gradients, the cycle paths on their old routes also slope gently, even if they lead through low mountain ranges. The route often includes viaducts, tunnels and old train stations, which are now home to cafés or museums. Such paths have been created in many regions, such as the Eifel, the Sauerland, the Bergisches Land, the Rhön or the Erzgebirge. They are ideal for families and for anyone who wants to experience low mountain landscapes without steep climbs. Many railway cycle paths are asphalted, some have a water-bound surface that becomes a little softer when wet.

## Read the signs

You will find different types of signs on long-distance cycle paths:

- **Signposts** with red or green text on a white background show destinations and distances. They follow a uniform standard in most federal states.
- **Intermediate signposts** are smaller signs with arrows that confirm the direction at junctions without a large signpost.
- **Route logos** are small plaques that hang under the signposts. Each route has its own logo, such as a stylized wave or a symbol of the region. Follow the logo of your route.
- **Detour signs** in yellow indicate construction sites or closed sections.

If the routes are well signposted, you can hardly do without a map. A map or app as an addition is still useful for planning detours and checking the direction if there are no signs.

## Which long-distance cycle path is right for me?

| desire | Suitable route |
|---|---|
| First multi-day tour, flat and easy | Weser cycle path, Moselle cycle path, sections of the Elbe cycle path |
| Culture and cities | Elbe cycle path, Main cycle path, Danube cycle path |
| Wine and enjoyment | Moselle cycle path, Main cycle path through Franconia, Rhine cycle path in the Rheingau |
| Sea and beach | Baltic Sea Coast Cycle Path, North Sea Coast Cycle Path |
| Peace and nature | Oder-Neiße cycle path, Altmühltal cycle path |
| Cross-border | Lake Constance cycle path, Danube cycle path to Vienna, Berlin–Copenhagen |
| Something sportier | Routes in low mountain ranges, railway cycle paths |

## Tips for planning

- **Select sections:** You don't have to ride the entire long-distance cycle path. A stretch of three to seven days is the ideal length for many.
- **Going downstream:** The slight gradient and the often more favorable wind direction make the tour a little easier.
- **Check train connection:** There are many train stations on large river cycle paths. This makes it easy to get there and back and you can take shortcuts if necessary.
- **Book accommodation:** Accommodations on popular cycle paths are quickly booked up during the summer holidays.
- **Use official route information:** Many long-distance cycle routes have their own websites with maps, GPX files, information on detours and directories of cycle-friendly accommodation.
- **Note diversions:** Floods, construction work or closed sections may occur. Find out more before the tour.

## Conclusion

Germany offers long-distance cycle paths for every taste: flat river cycle paths on the Elbe, Weser, Danube, Main, Moselle and Rhine, coastal routes on the North and Baltic Seas and more sporty routes through the low mountain ranges. The routes of the Germany cycle network, the European EuroVelo routes and the ADFC stars help with the selection. Flat river cycle paths with good rail connections are ideal for beginners. Choose a section that suits your time and fitness, travel downstream and plan accommodation well in advance in high season. Then a trip awaits you that you won't soon forget.
