Nobody likes to think about accidents, serious illnesses or their own death. And yet it is precisely in moments like these that we see how much order is worth. If a person suddenly comes to the hospital, relatives often need to know within a few hours: Is there a living will? Who gets to make decisions? Which health insurance company is the person insured with? Which bills continue to run and where are the keys?

An emergency folder answers these questions before they are asked. It is a collection of the most important documents, information and wishes in a single, well-known place. It relieves the burden on the people who are close to you, and it relieves you because you know that in an emergency everything won't descend into chaos.

In this guide you will find out what belongs in an emergency folder, how to create it in manageable steps and how to keep it up to date. It does not replace legal advice: For powers of attorney, directives and wills, it is worth taking a look at the information from the Federal Ministry of Justice, speaking to your family doctor or making an appointment with a notary.

## What an emergency folder should do

A good emergency folder has three tasks:

1. **It makes you capable of acting.** Relatives will immediately find the powers and orders they need to speak and make decisions for you.
2. **It secures everyday life.** Anyone who has to take care of your apartment, your pets, your bills and your contracts knows where to start.
3. **He records your wishes.** Medical treatment, care, burial and personal messages do not rely on guesswork.

It is important: an emergency folder is not an archive. It contains what is needed in the first few days and weeks and hints about where the rest lies. The complete storage of your documents remains in your normal folder system.

## The contents at a glance

| area | What belongs in it | Original or copy? |
|---|---|---|
| Personal information | Name, date of birth, address, marital status, nationality | Overview sheet |
| ID cards | Copies of ID card, passport, driving license | Copy, original stays with you |
| Health | Health insurance, family doctor, medication plan, allergies, previous illnesses, blood type | Overview sheet |
| Precaution | Power of attorney, care directive, living will | Original or reference to storage location |
| Contacts | Relatives, close friends, employers, neighbors with keys | Overview sheet |
| Finance | Banks, accounts, credit cards, loans, ongoing standing orders | Overview sheet without PINs |
| Insurance | Policies with insurance numbers and contact persons | Summary sheet, policies in the main folder |
| Housing | Rental agreement or property, landlord, utility, key | Overview sheet |
| Contracts | Mobile phone, internet, subscriptions, memberships | Overview sheet |
| Digital | Reference to password managers, important accounts, wishes for digital inheritance | Note, no passwords in plain text |
| Last wishes | Will (location), funeral wishes, organ donation | Note or copy |

## Step by step to your own emergency folder

### 1. Obtain material

You'll need a sturdy binder with tabs numbered 1 to 12, a few folders for documents you don't want to punch, and a place where the binder can stay permanently. Many consumer advice centers and authorities offer free templates for emergency folders that you can download. They are a good starting point because they cover most areas.

### 2. Write the cover page

The first page is the most important. It summarizes at a glance what counts in the first few hours: your name, your date of birth, your health insurance company, your family doctor, the person who should be called first, and information about whether there is a health care power of attorney and a living will and where the originals are. Write the date on which you last checked the sheet.

### 3. Gather health information

Write down your regular medications with dosage, known allergies and intolerances, important previous illnesses, implants such as a pacemaker and previous operations. If you have a federal medication plan, include a copy. The contact details of your treating specialists also belong here.

### 4. Clarify pension documents

Here it is worthwhile not only to collect papers, but also to make decisions. Three documents are central:

- **Health care power of attorney:** It determines who can act for you if you can no longer do so yourself, for example in health matters, with authorities or in banking transactions.
- **Guardianship order:** It specifies who the court should appoint as guardian if guardianship becomes necessary and what wishes apply.
- **Living will:** It describes which medical measures you want or reject in certain situations.

Since 2023, there has also been a statutory right of emergency representation for spouses in health matters, which is limited in time. However, it does not replace a power of attorney and it does not apply to unmarried couples. Powers of attorney can be registered in the Federal Chamber of Notaries' central pension register so that courts can find them in an emergency. To formulate the living will, we recommend talking to your family doctor; The text modules from the Federal Ministry of Justice help with the formulation.

### 5. List finances and contracts

Create a list of all accounts with bank, account type and IBAN, your credit cards, loans and standing orders. Add current contracts: rent, electricity, gas, internet, cell phone, insurance, streaming, newspaper subscriptions, club memberships. Write the customer number and, if possible, the notice period for each contract. This list is also useful in everyday life to keep an eye on costs; more about this in the guide [Check and cancel subscriptions] (/ratgeber/abos-pruefen-und-kuendigen/).

**Important:** You do not write PINs, TANs and passwords in plain text in the folder. An emergency folder can get lost or fall into the wrong hands.

### 6. Regulate your digital estate

More and more of our lives are taking place online: email, photos, social networks, cloud storage, online banking, customer accounts. Write down which important accounts there are and what should happen to them in an emergency, such as deleting, archiving or memorializing them. For access, a password manager with emergency access or a sealed envelope with the master password that is kept separately from the folder, for example with a trusted person, is a good idea. Many large providers now have legacy contact functions. The guide [Clear out passwords and accounts] (/ratgeber/passwoerter-und-konten-ausmisten/) tells you how to sensibly thin out your access beforehand.

### 7. Record personal wishes

A sheet with personal wishes relieves relatives of difficult decisions: Who should be notified? What should the funeral look like? Is there an organ donor card or an entry in the organ donation register? Who looks after the pets? Where is the will? A handwritten will must be completely handwritten and signed. So that it can be found, it can be placed in official custody at the local court.

## Where the folder should be

The best emergency folder is of no use if no one can find it. Choose a place that is close to you and accessible to people you trust, such as your study or a specific closet. A bank safe deposit box is well suited for originals, but is often not quickly accessible in an acute emergency. Many people therefore combine the two: the emergency folder at home, valuable originals in the locker and a note about them in the folder.

Tell at least two people where the folder is. It helps to carry a small card in your wallet that says: “Please notify in case of emergency” with a telephone number and a note that you have a power of attorney.

## Keep the folder current

An emergency folder is never completely finished. He lives with you. Check him:

- **once a year**, for example on a fixed date like your birthday,
- **after major changes** such as moving, marriage, separation, birth of a child, new job or a new diagnosis,
- **if medications change.**

Write the date on the cover sheet for each exam. This way everyone can see how up-to-date the information is. The annual review can be easily combined with the paper lesson that we describe in the guide [Keep documents] (/ratgeber/unterlagen-aufbewahren/).

## Typical mistakes

**Only collect originals, but no overview.** A stack of documents without a cover sheet costs valuable time in an emergency. The overview sheets are the heart of the folder.

**Passwords in plain text.** They belong in a password manager or in a separate, secure location.

**Don't tell anyone.** A folder that no one knows about is not an emergency folder.

**Want everything at once.** You don't have to finish the folder in one evening. Start with the cover sheet and health information, that alone helps a lot.

**Power of attorney without conversation.** Talk to the people you are empowering. They should know that they are taking responsibility and know your wishes.

## A plan for a weekend

If you don't want to put off the folder, divide the work:

| point in time | task |
|---|---|
| Friday evening | Download template, prepare folders and registers |
| Saturday morning | Cover sheet, personal information, health, contacts |
| Saturday afternoon | List accounts, insurance, contracts |
| Sunday | Digital, personal wishes, determine location, inform trusted people |
| In the weeks afterwards | Fill out powers of attorney and directives, make an appointment with your family doctor or notary |

## Special features for families with children

Parents of underage children should also write down in the emergency folder who can temporarily look after the children in an emergency, with telephone numbers for grandparents, godparents or friends who know. Make a note of the child’s daycare or school, pediatrician, allergies and medications. In the worst case scenario, parents can specify in a will who the family court should consider as guardian. Talk to the person you intend to do this with beforehand.

## Don't forget pets

Anyone who lives alone with animals should note who can care for them in an emergency, where food and medication is available and which veterinary practice they know. A small notice on the door or in your wallet that animals are waiting at home can save a lot of suffering in an emergency.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need an emergency folder if I am young and healthy?

Yes. Accidents happen at any age, and young people in particular often do not have powers of attorney. Without a power of attorney, parents of adult children are not automatically allowed to make decisions for them.

### Is a digital version enough?

A digital copy is a good addition, for example encrypted in the cloud. In an emergency, however, a paper folder is often quicker to have at hand, and original powers of attorney are required.

### Who should know about my emergency folder?

At least the person you have authorized and a second person you trust. For families with children, it makes sense for both partners to know each other's folders.
