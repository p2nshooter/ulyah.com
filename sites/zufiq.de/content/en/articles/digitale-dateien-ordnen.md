Clutter isn't just a matter of drawers and closets. Things can get tight on the computer too: a desktop full of icons, a downloads folder with two thousand files, three versions of the same presentation with names like “final”, “final2” and “final_really”, photos on the phone, documents in the cloud, a USB stick in the drawer that could have something important on it. Digital clutter is invisible, but it costs time, nerves and sometimes data.

This guide will show you how to organize your files in manageable steps. It is not aimed at IT professionals, but at everyone who simply wants to find what they are looking for at home or in their home office. The goal is not a perfect system, but one that you maintain without effort.

## Why digital order is worth it

Digital files don't take up any visible space, which is why they pile up so easily. You can still feel the consequences:

- You search for documents longer than it would take to recreate them.
- You accidentally work on an old version.
- Storage space on your laptop or cell phone is becoming scarce.
- Backups take longer and contain a lot of baggage.
- Important files are lost because they are in a single, random location.

A tidy digital workplace also has the same effect as a free desk: your head has less to trip over.

## Inventory: Where are your files?

Before you sort, get an overview. Most people have files in more places than they realize:

| Location | Typical content |
|---|---|
| Desktop | Work started, screenshots, links |
| Downloads | PDFs, installation files, attachments from emails |
| Documents | older works, letters, forms |
| Cloud storage | shared folders, cell phone uploads, work files |
| cell phone | Photos, voice memos, scans, messenger files |
| External hard drives and USB sticks | Backups, old projects |
| Email inbox | Attachments that were never saved |

Write these places down. The goal is to end up with as few, clearly defined storage locations as possible: a main location for your files and one or two backups.

## Step 1: Set a simple folder structure

The best structure is one that you don't have to look up. A flat structure with a few main folders named after areas of life has proven successful:

1. **01 Personal**
2. **02 Household and finances**
3. **03 Work**
4. **04 projects**
5. **05 photos and videos**
6. **06 Media and books**
7. **09 Archive**

The numbers ensure that the order remains stable. Within the main folders, you create at most one or two additional levels. Deep folder trees with seven levels look neat, but are tedious in everyday use. When digitizing papers, use the same topics as in the [Digitize Documents](/ratgeber/dokumente-digitalisieren/) guide so that you only have one system to keep in mind.

### Separate active and archive

A helpful thought is to separate what you are currently working on and what has been completed. Current projects are located in “Projects”. When a project is finished, you move the entire folder to the “archive”. This keeps your active folders organized without having to delete anything.

## Step 2: Name files sensibly

File names are your most important search aid. A clear pattern saves a lot of time later:

- **Date at front, in year-month-day format:** `2026-09-02_Angebot-Kueche.pdf`
- **Descriptive words instead of abbreviations:** `Mietvertrag-Wohnung-Lindenstrasse.pdf` instead of `MV_LS.pdf`
- **Clearly mark versions:** `Bewerbung_v03.docx` instead of `Bewerbung_final_neu.docx`
- **No special characters and preferably no spaces** so that files work on all systems

You don't have to rename all old files. Start with new files and only rename old ones if you pick them up anyway.

## Step 3: Clear desktop and downloads

Desktop and Downloads are your computer's inboxes. They may fill up, but not permanently.

### The desktop

An empty desktop has a calming effect and often makes the computer faster. This is how you do it:

1. Create a Desktop Collection folder with today's date.
2. Move everything from the desktop into it except what you're actually working on that week.
3. Go through the folder over the next few days and sort the files into your new structure.
4. Anything you haven't missed after a month can go into the archive or trash.

### The Downloads folder

The Downloads folder is a transit location. Almost everything in it is either already stored somewhere else or is no longer necessary: ​​installation programs, invoices that you have long since stored, photos from messengers. Sort by size and delete the large, obviously unnecessary files first. Then by date: Anything older than a few months that you don't recognize is a good candidate for the trash.

Many operating systems provide features to automatically empty the Downloads folder or Trash after a certain period of time. Use it if you feel comfortable with it.

## Step 4: Duplicate files and memory hogs

Duplicate files are caused by multiple downloads, copies on different devices, or backups that were accidentally saved one inside the other. Most operating systems will show you which folders and file types take up the most space. Look there first:

- old videos and screen recordings
- Program installation files
- Backups of old cell phones
- duplicate photo libraries

There are special ways to create order for photos; They are in the guide [Arrange digital photos] (/ratgeber/digitale-fotos-ordnen/).

Be careful when deleting duplicates when using utilities. Check randomly whether the supposed duplicates are really identical and do not delete anything from program folders or system directories.

## Step 5: Back up before something happens

Order without security is deceptive. Hard drives fail, laptops are stolen, coffee ends up on the keyboard. The 3-2-1 rule has proven itself: three copies of your important data, on two different media, one of which is in a different location.

| Backup type | Advantages | What to pay attention to |
|---|---|---|
| External hard drive | cheap, fast, independent of the Internet | Connect regularly and store separately from the computer |
| Cloud backup | automatically, safe even in the event of fire or theft | Check provider and data protection, two-factor registration |
| Network storage at home | central for the whole family | needs facility and care |

Set the backup to run automatically. A fuse that you need to remember is forgotten.

## Keeping things organized: a weekly routine in ten minutes

Digital order lasts when it happens incidentally. A short routine is enough:

1. **Friday ten minutes:** Empty the desktop, look through downloads, sort new files.
2. **Project Completion:** Move folder to archive as soon as a project is finished.
3. **Once a month:** check whether the automatic backup is running.
4. **Once a year:** Look through the archive and delete anything unnecessary.

This routine fits well with the weekly planning described in the guide [Weekly Planning and Weekly Review](/ratgeber/wochenplanung-und-wochenrueckblick/).

## Typical mistakes

**Too many folders.** A system with thirty main folders is difficult to remember. Less is more.

**Want to do everything in one day.** Digital legacy issues are often huge. Work in small stages.

**Order without backup.** The most beautiful structure is worthless if the hard drive fails.

**Ignore search.** Your system's search function is a powerful tool. Good filenames make them even better.

## An example: This is what the finished structure can look like

So that you can better imagine the flat structure, here is an example from a household with a home office:

- **01 Personal:** Copies of ID cards, certificates, CV
- **02 Household and finances:** Bank, insurance, tax (one subfolder per year), housing
- **03 Work:** Contract, payroll, further training, current tasks
- **04 projects:** Bathroom renovation, vacation planning, club party
- **05 Photos and videos:** by year and within that by month
- **06 Media and books:** E-books, music, audio books
- **09 Archive:** completed projects, old employers, previous apartments

The important thing is not that your structure looks exactly the same, but that it fits your life and that you use it without thinking. If you notice that you are looking for a file twice, that is an indication that a folder name does not match. Then change it immediately.

## Order for several people

In families and shared apartments, there are files that several people need: rental contracts, insurance policies, vacation photos, daycare documents. A shared cloud folder with the same basic structure prevents the same file from being on three cell phones and two laptops. Determines who files new documents and uses the same rules for file names. Personal matters stay in your own areas.

## Devices you no longer use

Old laptops, smartphones and tablets are little digital basements. Before selling, giving away, or disposing of a device, back up the data you want to keep, log out of all accounts, and reset the device to factory settings. Resetting laptops with an encrypted hard drive is safer than unencrypted ones. Many electrical retailers take back old devices free of charge; However, the data on it remains your responsibility.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I store my files locally or in the cloud?

Both have advantages. Many people work locally and use the cloud for backup or to sync between devices. It is important that you set a main location and not edit the same file in multiple locations.

### What do I do with old USB sticks and CDs?

Sift through the content, transfer what you want to keep into your structure and save it. Old data carriers don't last forever. You can then safely dispose of them after deleting personal information.

### How do I handle email attachments?

Save important attachments directly into your structure and name them meaningfully. How you can relieve the load on your inbox yourself can be found in the guide [Cleaning up your email inbox](/ratgeber/e-mail-postfach-aufraeumen/).

### How often should I really delete old files?

Once a year is enough for most people. Then take a look at the archive and the big memory hogs. Don't delete anything you still need for taxes, warranty or documentation, and everything you're neither looking for nor missing. If you're unsure, first move files to a "To Delete" folder with a date and empty it after a few months.
