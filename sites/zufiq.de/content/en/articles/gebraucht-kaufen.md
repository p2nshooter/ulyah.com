The solid wood dining table has been in another apartment for years, has a few scratches and costs only a fraction of what a new one would cost. The winter jacket at the thrift store looks like it's barely been worn. And the smartphone from the refurbished dealer works like new. Buying used can be smart, cheap and conserves resources. Nevertheless, many people hesitate: What if the part is broken? What if the seller doesn't exist?

This guide will help you buy second-hand with a calm head. You'll find out what's particularly worthwhile used and what you'd rather buy new, what sources there are, how to check the condition on site and during shipping and how to recognize typical scams.

## Why buying used can be worthwhile

An item that already exists does not need to be manufactured, packaged and transported from scratch. If you buy used, you extend the lifespan of things and usually save money. There is also an advantage that many people don't initially think of: Older furniture or tools are often solidly built, and with used things you can already see how they age. A cabinet that is still standing upright after years will probably continue to do so.

But buying used also has a downside. Because everything seems so cheap, things that you weren't even looking for quickly end up in your shopping basket. A bargain that sits unused in the basement is not a win. So start with a list and ask yourself the same questions you would if you were making a new purchase. [Avoid impulse purchases](/ratgeber/spontankaeufe-vermeiden/) explains how to see through buying impulses.

## What is particularly worthwhile when used

Some things hardly lose any of their usefulness after the first owner, but they lose a lot of their price. For them, secondhand is almost always a good idea.

| area | Why it's worth it | What you pay attention to |
|---|---|---|
| Furniture made of solid wood or metal | durable, often easy to repair | Stability, connections, smell, dimensions for your own apartment |
| Books, games, music | Contents remain the same, condition is mostly good | Completeness, missing pages or parts |
| Clothing, especially jackets and occasion wear | often barely worn | Seams, zippers, stains, smell |
| Dishes, glasses, pots | robust, can be supplemented individually | Cracks, chips, damaged coatings |
| Bicycles | large selection, solid older models | Brakes, frame, proof of purchase and origin |
| Tools and sports equipment | often rarely used | Function, wearing parts, completeness |
| Smartphones, laptops, tablets | Refurbished available with warranty | Battery health, updates, account suspension |

If you only need something once or twice, buying used might be too much. It can often be borrowed. You can find ideas for this in [Borrow, share, exchange](/ratgeber/leihen-teilen-tauschen/).

## What you're better off buying new

For some things, safety and hygiene play such an important role that buying used is usually not worth it. The reason is almost always the same: you can't see their history.

- **Bicycle helmets and other protective equipment:** A helmet that has already cushioned a fall can appear intact on the outside and still no longer provide proper protection. Materials also age. What the manufacturer recommends for replacement is stated in the instructions.
- **Child seats for cars:** You can't tell from the outside whether a seat has been involved in an accident. There are also missing parts or instructions and changed specifications. If you still buy a used seat, you should know exactly where it comes from, for example from your own family.
- **Mattresses:** They are closely linked to hygiene, have adapted to the body of the previous owner and are difficult to inspect thoroughly.
- **Underwear, swimwear and opened care products:** Hygiene is crucial here. Exceptions are parts in their original packaging.
- **Devices with damaged cables or swollen batteries:** Such damage can be dangerous. Stay away from it, even if the price is tempting.

## Sources of supply at a glance

Each source has its own strengths. It is often worth looking in a different location depending on the item.

| Source | Advantages | Pay attention to this |
|---|---|---|
| Flea market | touch, compare, negotiate | be there early, cash in small bills, electrical items often cannot be tested |
| Social department store | cheap, proceeds often support social causes | Offer changes, check opening times and delivery beforehand |
| Thrift store or consignment shop | pre-sorted goods, often good quality | Ask about returns and exchanges before purchasing |
| Online classifieds | huge selection, also nearby | Fraud risk, prefer pickup, read platform rules |
| Refurbished equipment dealer | tested devices, rights like when purchasing from a dealer | Read condition classes carefully, battery, warranty period |
| Neighborhood and circle of friends | known origin, often given away | Make clear agreements and openly address expectations |

For rare books, it's worth going to an antiquarian bookshop; for bicycles, ask a workshop near you whether they offer used bikes.

## Check the condition on site

Take your time to view it and don't let yourself be rushed. An honest seller doesn't mind if you take a closer look.

### Furniture

1. **Shake carefully.** Are the legs or side walls wobbling? Do drawers and doors move smoothly?
2. **Look in corners and backs.** Damp spots, cracks and loose connections often show up there.
3. **Smell it.** Smoke, moisture or animal smell are stubborn, especially in upholstery.
4. **Check seams and cracks in upholstered furniture.** This is where dirt and sometimes unwanted little roommates get trapped.
5. **Check.** Does the piece fit through your stairwell, your door, on the planned wall?

### Clothing

Hold the item up to the light, check seams, buttons and zippers, and look under the arms and at the hems. Try on if possible. [Recognizing quality](/ratgeber/qualitaet-erkennen/) explains how you generally recognize good workmanship.

### Electronics

Have the device demonstrated to you, ideally with all the functions that are important to you. Ask about the age, the condition of the battery and the bill. For smartphones, tablets and laptops, make sure that the device is reset to factory settings and is no longer linked to the previous owner's account. Otherwise you may not be able to use it at all. Also check whether there are still security updates for the model. You can find this on the manufacturer's website.

### Bicycles

Test the brakes, gears and lights, check the frame for cracks and dents and take it for a quick spin. Ask for proof of purchase. A noticeably low price, a seller who knows nothing about the bike, or a worn-off frame number are warning signs that it may be stolen. Then you'd better leave it alone.

## Look carefully when purchasing mail order

If you can't check on site, you have to rely on the description and photos. This works better with a few habits:

- **Read the description twice.** If it says “handicraft goods”, “untested” or “without accessories”, you mean it seriously.
- **Please provide additional photos.** Such as the back, label, nameplate or a specific location. Anyone who actually owns the item can easily provide this.
- **Ask specific questions.** How old is the item? Are there any defects? Smoke-free household? Having the answers in writing on the platform helps with misunderstandings.
- **Pay attention to the seller profile.** Reviews, length of membership and previous advertisements give an idea but are not a guarantee.
- **Arrange trackable shipping.** Insured shipping is worthwhile for valuable items.

## Pay securely and detect fraud

Most classified ads are honest. But fraudsters always use the same patterns. Anyone who knows her will recognize her quickly.

| stitch | How you recognize them | This is how you protect yourself |
|---|---|---|
| Advance payment to unknown people | Goods far away, only bank transfer possible, collection “unfortunately not possible” | Pick up from strangers and pay in cash or use payment methods with buyer protection |
| Fake payment links | Link via SMS, email or messenger, supposedly from the platform | Do not open links from messages, only pay in the platform itself |
| Switch to a messenger | Seller urges to leave the platform quickly | Leave communication on the platform |
| Time pressure | “Many interested parties, you have to pay immediately” | Keep calm, an item is not worth cheating on |
| Too good an offer | Price significantly below all comparable offers | double check, reject if in doubt |
| Request for personal data | Ask for a copy of your ID, bank details or confirmation codes | never give out such data and don't tell anyone the codes |

When buying from private individuals, it is safest to pay upon delivery after you have inspected the item. For larger amounts, meet in a busy place or take someone with you. If you pay online, find out beforehand what protection the respective payment method offers and under what conditions it applies.

If you have fallen for a scam, contact your bank immediately, report the complaint to the platform and report it to the police. Back up chat histories, ad text and payment receipts beforehand.

## Private or dealer: the difference in broad strokes

Whether you buy from a private individual or a commercial dealer makes a legal difference. As a consumer, you usually have more rights with a retailer, for example if a defect becomes apparent, and when buying online you often also have the right to cancel. However, private individuals can usually exclude liability for defects, for example with phrases such as “purchased as seen”. How long claims are valid and what exceptions there are for used goods changes and depends on the individual case. You can get current and reliable information from the consumer advice center.

Also pay attention to whether a provider really sells privately. Anyone who offers a lot of similar things may be acting commercially.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Buy because it's cheap:** Ask yourself if you would have wanted the item at full price.
- **Forgotten dimensions:** The most beautiful shelf won't help if it's ten centimeters too wide. Measure beforehand and take a measuring tape with you.
- **Do not consider transport:** Clarify in advance how the sofa will get to the fourth floor.
- **Painting up defects:** “I’ll fix this quickly” often becomes a project that never happens. Be honest about the repairs. When it is worth it is stated in [Have it repaired instead of buying a new one](/ratgeber/reparieren-lassen-statt-neu-kaufen/).
- **Decide under pressure:** If you push you, you'll lose a buyer and you won't lose anything.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I do if an item shipped does not correspond to the description?

Contact the seller quickly and objectively, describe the problem and send photos. Keep your packaging, chat history, and payment receipt. If you paid via a payment method with buyer protection, check the deadlines and steps that apply there. If anything is unclear, the consumer advice center will advise you.

### Are refurbished smartphones worth it?

Often yes, if the model still receives security updates, the battery is in good condition and the dealer provides clear information about the condition, warranty and guarantee. Compare the conditions of several providers.

## Briefly summarized

Buying used saves money and resources if you go in with a clear need. Furniture, books, clothing, dishes, bicycles and refurbished technology are particularly worthwhile, while helmets, child car seats, mattresses and hygiene products are better bought new. Check the condition thoroughly on site, have additional photos sent to you when purchasing by mail order and stay on the platform with communication. If possible, pay when handed over to strangers, do not open payment links from messages and do not allow yourself to be pressured. If you have legal questions, the consumer advice center can help you.
