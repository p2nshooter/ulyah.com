Some weeks feel like they're just rolling over you. Monday morning begins with a surprising meeting, Wednesday realizes that her sister's birthday is on Thursday, and Friday evening the tax return that was supposed to be completed this week is still lying untouched on the desk. A short weekly planning can help here. It lasts about half an hour, and it changes one thing above all: you no longer go into the week blindly, but with an overview.

This guide explains how a weekly review and weekly planning work together, what you need and how to maintain the habit.

## Why a week is the right time period

A day is too short to accommodate larger tasks and a month is too long to keep a close eye on. The week, on the other hand, has a natural rhythm with working days and weekends, and most appointments, obligations and habits repeat themselves weekly. If you stop once a week, you can change course in time before small problems become big.

The review is just as important as the plan. Without it, you plan every week as if the last one hadn't happened and repeat the same mistakes: doing too much, not enough buffer, not enough breaks.

## What you need

- a fixed date, for example Sunday afternoon or Friday after work
- your calendar, on paper or digital
- a list of all open tasks, see below
- about 30 minutes of rest, a cup of tea or coffee

Whether you work with paper or an app is a matter of taste. What's important is that you have a single place for appointments and a single place for tasks. Scattered pieces of paper, three calendars and reminders in five apps are bound to cause something to get lost.

## Part 1: the weekly review, about 10 minutes

Look at the past week and answer a few questions in writing. Keywords are enough.

| Question | What it is used for |
|---|---|
| What went well? | See successes, even small ones |
| What was left behind and why? | Recognize patterns, such as over-planning |
| What did I not have enough time for? | Check priorities |
| How was my energy? | Recognize stress, plan breaks |
| What do I take with me into the new week? | Consciously transfer or delete unfinished business |

Be honest but friendly. Looking back is not a test, but a compass.

## Part 2: collect everything, about 5 minutes

Before you plan, collect everything that is floating around in your head and in your pockets: pieces of paper from your jacket, notes in your cell phone, letters that need an answer, emails with tasks, things that occurred to you when you looked back. Write everything on a list. Only when everything is on paper will your head clear. How you can keep paperwork under control permanently can be found in [filing system for papers](/ratgeber/ablagesystem-fuer-papiere/).

## Part 3: plan the week, about 15 minutes

### Enter fixed dates

First, enter everything that has a fixed time: work appointments, doctor's appointments, picking up children, sports, appointments, birthdays. Check whether you need a card or a gift for birthdays.

### Set three priorities

Choose a maximum of three things from your to-do list that you really want to get done this week. Not ten, not five. Three areas of focus force you to prioritize, and they are realistic. How to decide what's important is explained in [Setting Priorities](/ratgeber/prioritaeten-setzen/).

### Block time for the focal points

A focus without an appointment often remains undone. Enter a fixed block of time in the calendar for each focus, like an appointment with another person. Make sure to schedule challenging tasks during times when you have energy.

### Plan buffers and breaks

Consciously plan gaps. At least one evening without obligations, time to get things done, room for the unexpected. A week that is 100 percent planned falls apart at the first incident.

### Bundle up small things

You can complete many small tasks such as phone calls, transfers, returns or emails in a shared time block, such as 30 minutes each on Tuesdays and Thursdays. This is more efficient than spreading them out over the week.

## An example

Jonas plans his week on Sunday at 5 p.m. The review shows: The presentation was finished, but the sport was canceled three times and he was often exhausted in the evenings. In the new week there are two customer appointments, a dentist appointment and his father's birthday. His three main focuses are: writing an offer for the new customer, gathering documents for the tax, and going swimming twice. He blocks two hours on Tuesday and Wednesday mornings for the offer, one hour on Thursday evenings for the tax documents and Monday and Friday after work for swimming. Wednesday evening remains free. He writes a birthday card on Sunday.

## Plan family weeks

If you live with a partner or children, it's best to plan together. A quick appointment at the weekend where everyone looks at the family calendar prevents many misunderstandings: Who is taking whom where? Who cooks on which day? Which dates overlap? A clearly visible calendar in the kitchen or a shared digital calendar helps. Keep the meeting short and friendly so that no one feels like it's an obligation.

## Typical mistakes when planning your week

Many people try weekly planning and give up after a few weeks. This is usually not due to the method, but rather to some typical pitfalls that can be easily avoided.

**Doing too much.** The most common mistake. By Sunday the week feels long and the list just keeps getting longer. On Wednesday it becomes clear that half of it cannot be achieved. Therefore, consciously plan less than you are comfortable with. If you have time left, you can always add something.

**Tasks that do not require any time.** “Doing taxes” is not a task, but a project. Break down large projects into concrete steps with an estimated duration: “Sort the evidence for 2025, 45 minutes”. The guide [Plan your day realistically](/ratgeber/tag-realistisch-planen/) shows you how to estimate realistically.

**Forget paths and crossings.** Between two appointments there are travel times, finding a parking space, and short recovery phases. If you don't plan for these transitions, you'll be behind from the first appointment.

**Plan chores only.** A week of just work and getting things done feels heavy. Also include nice things: a walk with a friend, a movie night, an hour just for you. What's on the calendar is more likely to happen.

**Skip the review.** If you start planning right away, you won't learn anything from the past week. The ten minutes of review in particular make planning better from week to week.

## When the week goes differently than planned

No plan survives the week unchanged. A sick child, an urgent assignment, or simply a day without energy can throw everything into disarray. This is not failure, but everyday life. The plan is an orientation, not a contract.

It is helpful to take a quick look in the middle of the week, for example on Wednesday evening. Ask yourself: Which of my three priorities is still realistic? What do I have to move or delete? Who do I have to inform? Five minutes is enough to reset the rest of the week. And if a focus remains, it consciously moves into the next week instead of accompanying you as a guilty conscience.

## Beyond the week: the monthly look

It's worth taking a closer look once a month. During the first weekly review of each month, take an extra five minutes and look at the next four weeks: What birthdays, deadlines, trips or major projects are coming up? What needs to be prepared in good time, such as gifts, vacation applications or tax documents? This way you avoid important dates coming as a surprise. If you want to keep an eye on deadlines and contracts, you will find help in the guide [Keep documents] (/ratgeber/unterlagen-aufbewahren/).

## Maintain the habit

- **Fixed date:** Always at the same time, in the same place, if possible linked to something nice, such as Sunday tea.
- **Short minimum version:** If you don't have time, spend at least five minutes looking at the upcoming week's events.
- **Use template:** A list of questions and steps makes it easier to get started.

How to turn this into a lasting habit is found in [Building New Habits](/ratgeber/neue-gewohnheiten-aufbauen/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Sunday the best day for weekly planning?

For many, yes, because the new week is fresh in front of you. Others prefer to plan on Friday afternoon to enjoy the weekend without worrying about work. What is crucial is a fixed appointment that fits your rhythm.

### Paper or app?

Both work. Paper promotes conscious thought and is less distracting, and an app is useful if you share appointments with others or need reminders on the go. Many combine a digital calendar with a notebook for review and focus.

### What do I do with tasks that have been left undone for weeks?

Ask yourself three questions: Is the task really still important? Is it too big and needs to be broken down into smaller steps? Can someone else take it over? It often turns out that a task can honestly be eliminated or just needs a clearer first step.

### How long does it take for weekly planning to become a routine?

Most people notice after three to four weeks that the appointment becomes more natural and takes less time. The first few times often take longer because a lot of unfinished business has accumulated.

## Briefly summarized

Weekly planning takes about 30 minutes: ten minutes looking back, five minutes collecting everything, fifteen minutes planning. Enter fixed dates, choose a maximum of three focal points, block time for them, plan buffers and bundle small things. With a fixed appointment and a short minimum version, it becomes a habit that makes your weeks noticeably calmer.
