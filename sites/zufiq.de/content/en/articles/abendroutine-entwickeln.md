Many evenings don't end, they just stop. You scroll a little more, half tidy up the kitchen, think about the email that has to go out tomorrow morning, and then suddenly you're lying in bed without having mentally arrived at it. An evening routine starts right here. It is not a strict program with twelve points, but rather a small, reliable sequence of steps that tells your head and your body: The day is over, now it can be quieter.

In this guide you will find out what makes a good evening routine, how to build your own in seven steps and what you can do if it keeps falling apart in everyday life.

## Why an evening routine helps

Humans are creatures of habit. Recurring processes require fewer decisions and less willpower, and these are often used up in the evening. If the last thirty to sixty minutes before sleep are similar every day, a kind of internal transition occurs over time. The body gets used to the signal, similar to how you automatically become more tired after brushing your teeth.

An evening routine also has a very practical benefit: it prepares you for the next morning. If you pack your bag in the evening, get your clothes ready and know what's coming up tomorrow, you'll start the day calmer. The work is then done before the noise begins, just like with a house whose stones arrive at the construction site already finished.

Finally, a regular evening routine supports regular sleep. Consistent bedtime and wake-up times are one of the most well-known recommendations for good sleep, and a routine makes it easier to stick to.

## What makes a good evening routine

The best evening routine isn't the nicest one, but the one you can do even on a busy Tuesday. Three properties help:

- **It's short.** Twenty to forty minutes is enough. A routine that lasts two hours fails because of reality.
- **It is anchored.** Each step is tied to something that is already happening, such as dinner, brushing your teeth, or a fixed time.
- **It is flexible.** There is a full version for quiet evenings and a minimum version for days when nothing works.

Most evening routines consist of similar building blocks. You don't have to use them all, but the overview will help you choose:

| building block | What it is used for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| End the day | Park your thoughts, relieve your head | Write down three tasks for tomorrow |
| Reset the apartment | find order in the morning | Ten minutes to put everything in its place |
| Prepare the morning | prefer decisions | Pack your bag, get your clothes ready |
| Make screens quieter | Reduce stimuli | Charge your cell phone outside the bedroom |
| Shut down the body | Promote relaxation | Dim the lights, take a warm shower, stretch |
| A closing ritual | set a clear conclusion | read a few pages, drink tea |

## Step by step to your own evening routine

### 1. Take an honest look at what your evenings look like now

Before you make any changes, spend a week observing what actually happens between dinner and bedtime. Write down roughly when you finish work, when you eat, how long you are on your cell phone and when you turn off the lights. It's not about evaluation. Often, after just a few days, a pattern becomes apparent, such as the evening after dinner on the sofa simply fizzles out, or the last hour almost always disappears in front of the screen.

### 2. Choose a fixed end point

A routine needs a goal: the time you want to be in bed. To do this, work backwards from your wake-up time. How much sleep you need depends on the individual. Pay attention to how you feel after different nights instead of sticking to a number from the internet. Then plan your routine to start about half an hour before that end point. To do this, set a quiet alarm clock that doesn't wake you up, but rather reminds you.

### 3. End the day consciously

Many people lie awake because their heads are still working. Give open thoughts a place. Take five minutes and write down what's important tomorrow, what's on your mind, and what you accomplished today. A notebook next to the sofa is enough. Anyone who works from home also benefits from a small end-of-work ritual: close the laptop, clear the workplace, maybe walk around the block. This creates a boundary that would otherwise have been drawn on the way home.

### 4. Reset the apartment

It's not about cleaning, it's about resetting: everything that has moved during the day goes into its place. Dishes in the dishwasher or sink, jackets in the coat rack, mail in the permanent pile, toys in the box. Set a timer for ten minutes and stop when it goes off. Coming into an apartment the next morning that doesn't look like yesterday is a surprisingly big gift to yourself. You can take this opportunity to pack your bag and get your clothes ready for tomorrow. This is easier if the cupboard isn't overflowing; Our guide [cleaning out your closet] (/ratgeber/kleiderschrank-ausmisten/) tells you how to reduce the size in peace and quiet.

### 5. Turn down the screens

For many people, the cell phone is the biggest opponent in their evening routine. News, videos and social networks are designed to make you watch one more thing. Clear, simple rules are helpful:

- Charge your cell phone outside the bedroom and use a simple alarm clock.
- Set Do Not Disturb mode to turn on automatically every evening.
- Set a fixed time from which work emails will no longer be opened.
- If you like to watch TV in the evening, decide in advance how many episodes there will be and then switch off instead of letting the next one start automatically.

You can find more ideas for calmly using your cell phone and mailbox in the room [Digital Calm](/themen/digitale-ruhe/).

### 6. Shut down the body

Your body needs a transition from activity to rest. In the last hour, dim the lights, turn off bright ceiling lights and use a warm floor lamp. A warm shower, some quiet stretches, or a short walk after dinner can also help. Many people sleep better when the bedroom is cool, dark and quiet.

What you drink also plays a role. Caffeine from coffee, black and green tea, cola or energy drinks lasts for hours in many people. Although alcohol often makes you tired, it is generally known that it disrupts the quality of sleep in the second half of the night. If you find it difficult to switch off in the evening, it's worth taking an honest look at what's good for you.

### 7. Set a clear conclusion

The final element of your routine should be something you look forward to. A few pages of a paper book, a herbal tea, calm music or a quick look at three things that were good today. Over time, this ritual becomes the actual signal to your head: now it's time for work, even internally.

## Three examples of different lives

### For full working days

A quiet alarm clock reminds you at 9:30 p.m. Reset the apartment for ten minutes, pack your bag, get your clothes ready. Write down three points for tomorrow, cell phone in the hallway. Take a warm shower, dim the lights, read until you fall asleep. Duration: about 35 minutes.

### For parents of small children

Here, your evening often only begins when the children are asleep, and then you are exhausted. A shared routine is helpful: While the children brush their teeth, toys and shoes go into their place together. Then you allow yourself a conscious break of twenty minutes that is not reserved for household chores, and only then do you briefly conclude with notes and lights out.

### For shift work

If bedtimes are constantly changing, don't focus on the clock, but rather when you start sleeping. The routine is always the same, whether at 10 p.m. or 7 a.m.: darken, put your cell phone away, make a quick note of what has an impact, a quiet ritual. Blackout curtains and a sleep mask can help after night shifts.

## If it doesn't work: typical stumbling blocks

**Too much at once.** Anyone who introduces seven new habits overnight usually gives up after a few days. Start with one or two building blocks and only add them when they feel natural.

**The evening is the only free time.** Some people postpone going to bed because late evening is the only time that is theirs alone. That's understandable. Then it doesn't help to impose even more discipline on yourself, but rather to create small islands for yourself during the day so that the evening doesn't have to absorb everything.

**Exceptions become the rule.** An evening with friends or a late football game is not a failure. The only important thing is to get back in the next day. For days like this, a **minimum version** helps: cell phone away, lights off, a sentence in the notebook.

> A routine doesn't have to be successful every evening. It just has to be possible again every evening.

**Perfectionism.** Your evening routine doesn't have to look nice or please others. If an episode of your favorite series helps you switch off, it can be included as long as it stays just one episode.

## When a routine is not enough

A good evening routine can make many things easier, but it is not a treatment. If you have trouble falling asleep for several weeks or if you repeatedly lie awake for a long time at night, if you are very exhausted during the day despite having enough time in bed, if you snore loudly or if someone has noticed that you are having pauses in your breathing, please talk to your family doctor. This also applies if you are unable to let go of ruminations, worries or depression. ## Briefly summarized

An evening routine is a bridge between day and night. It consists of a few, always the same steps: end the day, reset the apartment, prepare for the morning, turn down the screens, shut down the body and set a clear conclusion. Start small, anchor each step to something that is already happening, and allow yourself a minimum version for hard days.
