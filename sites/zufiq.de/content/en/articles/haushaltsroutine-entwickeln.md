The household is never finished. As soon as the kitchen is clean, there are dishes in the sink again. As soon as the laundry is folded, the laundry basket fills up again. Many people therefore experience household chores as an endless struggle in which they are constantly running behind. At the weekend we tidy up and clean on a large scale, and on Wednesday it looks like it did before.

A good household routine changes this fundamentally. She divides the work into small, manageable portions that can be done almost at the same time. Instead of a strenuous major cleaning, there are a few minutes every day and a few fixed tasks every week. The result: an apartment that is usually in good condition and more free time on weekends. This guide will show you how to develop a routine that fits your life instead of chasing a perfect plan.

## Why routines work better than big cleaning

Large cleaning operations have one major disadvantage: they cost a lot of energy all at once. Anyone who has to clean for several hours on Saturday after a strenuous week tends to put off this task. The longer you wait, the bigger the mountain becomes and the harder it is to get started.

Small daily routines work differently. They last so short that internal resistance remains low. And because they take place regularly, there isn't a lot of clutter to accumulate. Five minutes of cooking in the evening prevents dishes from piling up for days and dried leftovers from having to be laboriously soaked.

There is also a psychological effect: a tidy environment has a calming effect. If you come into a clean kitchen in the morning, you start the day more relaxed. The guide [Building New Habits](/ratgeber/neue-gewohnheiten-aufbauen/) explains why small, regular steps are so effective.

## Step 1: Take an honest inventory

Before you plan a new routine, it's worth taking an honest look at your everyday life. These questions will help you:

- **What tasks do you have to do every day?** Dishes, cleaning the kitchen, putting away small items, trash.
- **What tasks need to be done weekly?** Cleaning the bathroom, vacuuming, bed linen, shopping.
- **What bothers you most?** Maybe it's the messy work surface in the kitchen or the pile of laundry on the armchair.
- **When do you realistically have time?** In the morning before work, in the evening after dinner, during your lunch break in the home office?
- **Who lives in the household with you?** Partner, children, roommates can and should help.

Write down the answers. It often becomes clear that a few places make the biggest difference. If you start there, the routine will work quickly.

## Step 2: The basic daily routine

The daily routine is the heart of it. It should be short, ideally ten to fifteen minutes, and should be attached to fixed points in the day. Here is an example that you can adapt:

| point in time | task | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| In the morning after getting up | Make the bed, tilt the window | 2 minutes |
| After breakfast | Put the dishes in the dishwasher, wipe the table | 3 minutes |
| When coming home | Shoes, jacket, bag in their place | 1 minute |
| After dinner | Clean up the kitchen, wipe the work surface, start the dishwasher | 7 minutes |
| Before sleeping | Reset the living room briefly, put away small items | 5 minutes |

Altogether, that's less than twenty minutes, spread out over the entire day. The trick is to stick to existing habits. You don’t clean up “eventually,” but always right after breakfast and after dinner. So the task becomes automatic over time.

A particularly effective component is resetting in the evening. You can bring the apartment into a basic state in just a few minutes: pillows on the sofa, cups in the kitchen, mail on the shelf, toys in the box. The next morning you start in an orderly environment. The guide [Develop an evening routine] (/ratgeber/abendroutine-entwickeln/) describes how you can make the evening hours quieter overall.

## Step 3: The weekly plan by room

Not everything has to be done every day. You distribute larger tasks throughout the week. A tried and tested principle is to assign a focus to each day. This means you never clean everything at once, but each area gets a regular cleaning.

| day | Focus | Example tasks |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | bathroom | Sink, toilet, shower, mirror, change towels |
| Tuesday | Laundry | Wash, hang, fold |
| Wednesday | Kitchen | Briefly check the refrigerator, stove, wipe the fronts |
| Thursday | floors | Vacuuming, mopping in main areas |
| Friday | Purchasing and planning | Check supplies, shopping list, weekly plan |
| Saturday | bedroom | Change bed linen, dust |
| Sunday | free | Relax, just the basic daily routine |

Each of these tasks usually takes twenty to thirty minutes. This is much more pleasant than a whole Saturday of cleaning. If a day is missed, it's no drama: the task will be made up for on the next day off or will simply be canceled this week.

## Step 4: Monthly and Seasonal Tasks

Some tasks are less common, but should not be forgotten: cleaning windows, cleaning the oven, wiping out the refrigerator thoroughly, turning the mattress, washing curtains, cleaning drains. For these tasks, it's worth making a simple list that you go through on the first weekend of the month, for example. Just focus on one or two points at a time.

Seasonal tasks such as rearranging clothes between summer and winter, tidying up the balcony or cleaning out the [basement and storage room](/ratgeber/keller-und-abstellraum-ordnen/) fit well into spring and autumn. Put them in your calendar as an appointment so that they don't get lost.

## Own less, clean less

A household routine is easier the fewer things are lying around. Every decorative figurine needs to be dusted, every pile on the countertop makes wiping difficult, and every item of clothing needs to be washed, dried and put away. If you [clean out](/ratgeber/ausmisten-anfangen/) regularly, you save a lot of work around the house.

Fixed places are just as important. When every thing has a home, tidying up is quick because you don't have to think about where something belongs. The guide [Fixed places and labeling](/ratgeber/feste-plaetze-und-beschriften/) shows how you can implement this throughout the apartment. The entrance area in particular benefits from this, as described in the guide [Order in the hallway] (/ratgeber/ordnung-im-flur/).

## Distribute tasks fairly

If you don't live alone, you shouldn't manage the household alone. A fair distribution relieves pressure and prevents disputes. For it to work, it needs clarity instead of silent expectations.

This is how a fair distribution can be achieved:

1. **Make all tasks visible.** Write down together what needs to be done in the household, including the invisible tasks such as writing shopping lists, keeping track of appointments or getting gifts.
2. **Consider preferences.** Some people like to cook, others prefer ironing. Those who take on tasks that are less difficult for them are more likely to stick with them.
3. **Responsibilities instead of help.** “You are responsible for the laundry” works better than “Will you help me with the laundry?” Responsibility also includes thinking for yourself.
4. **Include children in an age-appropriate manner.** Even small children can put away toys, set the table or sort socks. Older children take on fixed tasks such as taking out the trash or vacuuming.
5. **Readjust regularly.** Once a month, briefly discuss what is going well and what is not. Living circumstances change, and so can the division.

A clearly visible plan, for example on the refrigerator door, helps everyone keep track.

## Motivation: This is how you keep going

Even the best routine needs some discipline at the beginning. These tricks make it easier to get started:

- **The Timer.** Set a timer for ten minutes and vacate until it rings. After that you can stop. Often you continue voluntarily because the beginning has been made.
- **Music or podcast.** A favorite playlist or an exciting podcast makes housework more enjoyable. Some people reserve certain podcasts just for cleaning.
- **Visible progress.** A simple [habit tracker](/ratgeber/gewohnheits-tracker/) shows how often you've completed the routine. Not letting the chain break is motivating.
- **The two-minute rule.** Anything that takes less than two minutes is done immediately: hang up the jacket, put the plate in the dishwasher, open the letter and put it away.
- **Good tools.** A decent mop, a working vacuum cleaner and microfiber cloths make the job quicker and more pleasant. Store the bathroom cleaning supplies directly in the bathroom so that the journey is short.

## When life gets in the way

There will be weeks when nothing goes according to plan: illness, exams, moving, a new baby, a stressful project at work. Then it is important not to drop the routine completely, but to reduce it to a minimum.

Set up an emergency routine in advance, such as:

1. Dishes in the dishwasher every day.
2. Take out the trash when the bucket is full.
3. Secure clean laundry for the next few days.
4. Give the bathroom a quick wipe once a week.

Everything else can wait. This emergency routine prevents the apartment from descending into complete chaos and makes it easier to get back to work when the stressful phase is over. Be gentle with yourself during such times. A routine is a tool, not a yardstick to measure yourself against. The guide [Divide Energy](/ratgeber/energie-einteilen/) explains how to manage your energy well during strenuous phases.

## Adapt the routine to your life

There is no one correct household routine. Anyone who works shifts needs a different schedule than someone with fixed office hours. Families with small children have different needs than singles in a one-room apartment. Use the examples in this guide as a starting point and modify them until they suit you.

A good rhythm for checking is the [Weekly Review](/ratgeber/wochenplanung-und-wochenrueckblick/). Ask yourself briefly: What tasks did I complete this week? Where does it regularly get stuck? What could I simplify, give away or leave out? With each week your routine gets a little better and a little more natural.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take for a household routine to become a habit?

This varies from person to person. After a few weeks, many people notice that the daily tasks no longer require much effort. The important thing is not to give up if individual days are missed, but to just carry on the next day.

### What do I do if I'm too tired in the evening?

Reduce the evening routine to the most important things, such as putting away the dishes and starting the dishwasher. Some tasks can also be postponed until the morning if you have more energy then. The routine must match your energy flow, not the other way around.

### Is a cleaning assistant worth it?

If your budget allows it and you can use the time gained for things that are important to you, a household helper can be a great relief. Make sure you register them legally, for example via the mini-job center. The basic daily routine still makes sense.

### How do I get my family to join in?

By planning the tasks together, clearly distributing responsibilities and making successes visible. Accusations rarely help, but clear agreements and a common plan often help.
