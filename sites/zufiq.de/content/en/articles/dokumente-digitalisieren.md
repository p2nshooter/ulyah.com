Paper has one big advantage: you can touch it. But it also has disadvantages. It takes up space, it yellows, thermal paper fades, and if you need a quick invoice or contract on the go, the original is of course in the folder at home. Digitizing documents solves many of these problems. At the same time, it can create new ones: hundreds of files with names like “Scan_0047.pdf”, scattered across cell phones, laptops and the cloud, are hardly better than a box full of loose sheets of paper.

This guide shows you how to digitize papers so that you can actually find them again: with simple tools, clear file naming, a clear folder structure and a backup that can survive a hard drive failure. We also clarify which documents you should keep in their original form despite being scanned.

## Is digitizing even worth it?

Not every sheet needs to be scanned. Digitalization is particularly worthwhile for:

- **Receipts that you need for longer**, such as purchase receipts for warranty and guarantee, tradesman invoices and tax receipts
- **Documents that you often need when traveling**, such as proof of insurance, rental agreement or vaccination certificates
- **Papers that fade**, especially receipts on thermal paper
- **Documents that you need to share**, for example with tax advisors or family members

It makes less sense to digitize papers that you will soon be allowed to dispose of anyway, or advertising and information letters that have no evidentiary value. So before you scan, you sort out. How long which documents are important is stated in the guide [Keep documents] (/ratgeber/unterlagen-aufbewahren/).

## The right tool: What you really need

For most households, a current smartphone is sufficient. Modern scanning apps recognize page edges, correct pages photographed at an angle, improve contrast and save as PDF. Many operating systems already have such a function built in, for example in the notes or files app.

| device | Suitable for | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smartphone with scanning app | individual receipts, mail in everyday life | always there, free, fast | difficult with large quantities |
| Multifunction printer with feeder | Stacks of letters and contracts | several pages at once, good quality | needs space, drivers and setup |
| Document scanner | large archives, regular quantities | very fast, bilateral, reliable | Acquisition costs |
| Copy shop or scanning service | one-off major project | no purchase of your own | Check data protection, costs per page |

A cell phone is almost always enough to get started. Only when you notice that you are regularly scanning dozens of pages is it worth getting a device with a paper feeder.

### Settings that prove their worth

- **Format:** PDF, for multi-page documents all pages in one file
- **Resolution:** around 300 dpi is sufficient for texts; It can be more for photos and certificates with fine details
- **Color:** Grayscale for text-only documents, color for forms with stamps, signatures or colored markings
- **Text recognition (OCR):** switch on if available. Then you can later search for words in the document, such as a contract number.

## Naming files: the key to finding them again

A file name is like the label on a box. If you type it according to a fixed pattern, you can find any document in seconds. This order has proven successful:

**Date – Sender – Content**

For example:

- `2026-03-14_Stadtwerke_Jahresabrechnung-Strom.pdf`
- `2026-05-02_Elektrohandel_Rechnung-Waschmaschine.pdf`
- `2025-11-20_Krankenkasse_Bescheid-Zahnersatz.pdf`

The date is in year-month-day format. The files are automatically sorted chronologically. Avoid spaces and special characters, use hyphens and underscores. If you have a document with an expiration date, you can add something to it at the end, something like `…_Garantie-bis-2029-05.pdf`.

## A folder structure that grows with you

Create the same topics digitally that you use on paper. A simple structure could look like this:

1. **01 Personal** (ID cards, certificates, certificates)
2. **02 Health** (health insurance, findings, vaccinations)
3. **03 Housing** (rent or property, additional costs, utilities)
4. **04 Work** (contracts, pay slips, applications)
5. **05 Finance** (bank, loans, investments)
6. **06 Insurance**
7. **07 Tax** (one subfolder per year)
8. **08 Purchases and Warranties**
9. **09 vehicles**
10. **10 archives** (completed topics)

The numbers in front of the names ensure that the order always remains the same. Keep the structure flat: More than two levels of subfolders usually make searching harder instead of easier. If you keep the structure the same on paper and digitally, you only have to keep one system in mind. ## The process: from the mailbox to the file

The biggest hurdle is not technology, but habit. To ensure that digitization does not become the next unfinished project, a fixed process helps:

1. **Sort mail immediately.** Advertising directly into the waste paper, important things into the inbox.
2. **Scan once a week.** A fixed date, such as Sundays, for everything in the inbox.
3. **Name and file.** Immediately after scanning, while you still know what the document is.
4. **Decide original.** Does the paper have to stay? Then into the folder. If not, dispose of safely.

Plan your own appointments for the old stock. Target just one folder or topic per session, such as “Purchases and Warranties.” This keeps the project manageable.

## What you should keep in the original

In private life, one scan is sufficient for most everyday receipts. However, you should definitely keep some documents in their original form:

- Birth, marriage and death certificates
- Certificates and job references
- Notarial deeds, land register extracts, purchase contracts for real estate
- Will, power of attorney, living will
- Vehicle registration document (registration certificate part II)
- Documents for which an authority or a court expressly requests the original

In practice, easily readable copies are usually sufficient for tax receipts from private individuals. If in doubt, keep the original until the tax assessment is final.

## Security and privacy

Digitized documents often contain sensitive data. Treat it as carefully as you would a filing cabinet.

### The 3-2-1 rule for backups

A single hard drive can fail, a laptop can be stolen. The 3-2-1 rule has proven itself:

- **3 copies** of your data
- on **2 different storage media**, such as a laptop and an external hard drive
- of which **1 copy in another location**, such as in a cloud or with relatives

Check occasionally whether a backup can really be restored. A backup that doesn't work in an emergency isn't one.

### Encryption and access

- Protect your laptop and cell phone with a secure password or PIN and activate device encryption.
- If you use cloud storage, enable two-factor login.
- For particularly sensitive documents, you can use encrypted folders or containers.
- Only share documents specifically and with an expiration date for the link if your service offers this.

## Typical mistakes

**Scan without sorting out first.** Otherwise you will also digitize the ballast. First sort, then scan.

**Want to assign file names later.** “Later” rarely comes up. Name each file immediately.

**Throw everything into one folder.** The search function helps, but does not replace basic organization.

**Only have one copy.** Without backup, a digital archive is more vulnerable than a paper folder.

**Destroy originals too quickly.** This can be expensive for certificates and notarial documents.

## Less paper right from the start

The easiest scan is the one you don't have to do at all. Many senders now offer documents digitally:

- **Banks** provide account statements and notifications in online banking as PDFs. Check how long they remain available there and download them regularly.
- **Health funds and insurance companies** often have a customer portal or an app with a digital mailbox.
- **Energy suppliers, telephone and internet providers** will send invoices by email upon request.
- **The tax office** can provide notification data electronically via the tax portal.

Switch to digital mail for every contract you handle anyway, if that's okay with you. Then set a rule for where these documents end up: If you move your PDF invoices from your mailbox to the appropriate folder every month and name them correctly, you will build up your archive at the same time.

## An example: The old stock in four weeks

If you have three full folders and a shoebox full of receipts, you can get rid of the old inventory in one month with a simple plan:

| week | task | time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sort out everything that can be thrown away according to the deadlines | two evenings |
| 2 | Scan and name purchases, warranties and receipts | an evening |
| 3 | Scan contracts, insurance and housing | two evenings |
| 4 | Scan tax documents from the last few years, set up backups and check them | an evening |

After that, all that remains is the weekly appointment for new mail. It's important that you really test the backup in week four: Open some files from the backup before destroying any paper receipts.

## Together in the household

If several people live in a household, it is worth having a shared folder for documents that affect everyone, such as the rental agreement, insurance and additional costs. Personal documents such as medical reports remain in your own folders. Agree on who will do the weekly mail so that two people don't scan the same invoice or both think the other did it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a scan worth as much as the original in court?

That depends on the individual case. An ordinary scan usually has less evidentiary value than the original. Therefore, you keep the paper for important contracts and documents.

### What is the best way to scan receipts?

Immediately after purchase with your cell phone, as long as the writing is still legible. Name the file with the date, retailer and product and place it in the “Purchases and Warranties” folder.

### What do I do with old photos and letters?

Personal memoir is its own project, less about deadlines and more about meaning. The guide [Clearing out memorabilia](/ratgeber/erinnerungsstuecke-ausmisten/) describes how to do this carefully.

### How long do digital files last?

Files themselves do not age, but storage media and formats do. Hard drives, USB sticks and burned CDs can show errors after a few years. That's why important documents don't just belong on one medium, but rather in regular backups. PDF is a widely used, long-lasting format and a good choice for documents.
