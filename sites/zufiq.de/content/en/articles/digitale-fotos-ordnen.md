The cell phone reports that the memory is almost full. There are thousands of pictures in the gallery: the sunset from last summer in twenty almost identical versions, screenshots of timetables, photos of price tags and parking spaces, greeting pictures from the family chat. There are more photos waiting in the drawer of an old cell phone, even more on the camera's memory card, and somewhere there's a cloud account whose password you don't remember exactly. The most beautiful pictures of your life are somewhere in there, you just can't find them.

Organizing photos sounds like a huge task. It can be done well in stages. This guide will take you step by step: merge all storage locations, delete unnecessary items, create a simple folder structure, make favorite pictures visible and back up everything so that a lost cell phone is no longer a disaster.

## Before you start: first backup, then sort

The most important rule is right at the beginning: Before you delete or move larger quantities, you need a backup copy. A wrong click, a computer crashing or a synchronization that works differently than expected can otherwise destroy images that cannot be replaced.

Pay particular attention to synchronized storage. If your phone is synced with cloud storage, a picture you delete on your phone can also disappear in the cloud. Check the settings of your device or service to find out how deleting works there and whether deleted images end up in a trash can for a while.

Also clarify what you want to achieve. More storage on your phone? Find your favorite photos faster? A photo book for every year? Your goal will determine how thorough you need to be.

## Step 1: Merge all locations

Photos are hidden in more places than you think. Make a list before you start.

| Location | Which is often there | Pay attention to this |
|---|---|---|
| Current cell phone | most new photos, screenshots, videos | Check save status, don't forget the messenger folder |
| Old cell phones and tablets | Pictures from past years | Charge, unlock, have access data ready |
| Memory cards and digital cameras | Vacations, celebrations, older recordings | Get a reader and don't format the card too quickly |
| Computers and laptops | imported photos, downloads | also search download and desktop folders |
| External hard drives and USB sticks | old backups | check whether they are still readable |
| Cloud accounts, even old ones | automatically uploaded images | Clarify access data, track down forgotten accounts |
| Email inboxes | Photos that others have sent you | Save important attachments specifically |

This is how you do it:

1. **Create a folder.** Ideally on a computer or external hard drive with enough space. For example, call it “Photos Entrance”.
2. **Copy instead of moving.** The originals remain in place until you are sure that everything is complete and legible in the folder.
3. **Work through location by location.** Check off everyone on your list as soon as their images are copied.
4. **Keep originals.** Images sent via Messenger are often resized and sometimes no longer have the original date taken. If you have the original from the camera, keep that.

You only pass on or dispose of old devices and memory cards once your photos have been backed up and you have deleted the data on them.

## Step 2: Sort out in stages

Nobody has to look through thousands of images in one evening. Proceed in stages, from easy to difficult, and plan a manageable amount of time for each, about half an hour.

### Stage 1: Screenshots and note photos

Screenshots of order confirmations, directions, recipes or chat histories usually have a short expiration date. The same applies to note photos: the parking space in the parking garage, the price tag in the furniture store, the meter reading. Many galleries show screenshots in their own album. Delete what is done. You move important documents that you have photographed to your file storage, not to the photo collection.

### Stage 2: Messenger images

Greeting cards, joke pictures and forwarded photos accumulate quickly. Only keep what really means something to you. To prevent constant replenishment, you can set up many messengers so that received images are no longer automatically saved in your gallery.

### Stage 3: Series images and duplications

There are often ten variations of a motif. Put them next to each other, choose the most beautiful picture, maybe a second one, and delete the rest. Many photo programs help you find duplicate and very similar pictures. Still, check the suggestions yourself before deleting, and if there are true duplicates, keep the best quality file.

### Stage 4: Blurring and missteps

Blurry shots, pictures from inside the trouser pocket, closed eyes, thumb in the picture. You make an exception for blurry photos that are the only image of an important moment. They can stay.

### Stage 5: The rest after years

Now the actual memories remain. Work your way through year by year, preferably from newest to oldest. When looking at similar images, ask yourself: Which one best tells the moment? You don't have to decide more than that.

When sorting, images that hurt also appear, for example from a relationship that has ended or from people who are no longer there. You don't have to decide about them right away. Put it in its own folder called “Later” and review it when you’re ready.

## Step 3: A simple folder structure

A good structure is so simple that you will understand it years from now. An order by year and event, with the date at the front of the name, has proven successful. This means everything is automatically sorted in the correct order.

| level | Example | What it is used for |
|---|---|---|
| Main folder | Photos | a single place for all images |
| year | 2025 | quick overview, easy backup |
| event or month | 2025-07-12 Baltic Sea weekend | Pictures of an occasion together |
| Collection folder | 2025-00 everyday life | individual pictures without any special reason |
| Selection | 2025-99 favorites | the best pictures of the year |

A few rules help:

- **Date in year-month-day format.** This puts folders and files in the correct chronological order.
- **Short, clear keywords.** “Wedding Lisa and Tom” will be easier for you to find later than “celebration”.
- **Don't nest too deeply.** Year and event are sufficient for most collections.
- **Only change file names if it helps.** Many programs read the recording date from the image data. Renaming is practical, but not a must.

## Favorite albums and photo books as a goal

Order is not an end in itself. In the end, your pictures should bring joy again.

- **Mark favorites.** Most galleries offer a heart or star function. As you sort, mark the images that immediately resonate with you.
- **Make a yearly selection.** Choose a manageable number of favorite pictures from each year and put them in their own folder. You really look at this selection.
- **Create a photo book.** One book per year or per trip brings pictures from storage to the table. Plan a fixed date for this, such as January for the past year.
- **Print prints.** A few pictures on paper, on the wall or in a frame, are viewed much more often than a thousand on a cell phone.

Old paper photos and albums from the family are a task in themselves. How you decide carefully is described in [Clearing out memorabilia](/ratgeber/erinnerungsstuecke-ausmisten/).

## Save photos multiple times in different locations

A cell phone can be lost, a hard drive can break, an account can be blocked. Photos are only safe if they are in multiple locations.

- **At least two copies in addition to the original.** For example, on your computer, on an external hard drive and in cloud storage.
- **Various locations.** At least one copy should be kept outside of your home so that water damage or burglary doesn't affect everything at once. A hard drive at a relative's house can be sufficient.
- **Sync is not a backup.** If you delete an image, it often disappears everywhere when synchronized. A true backup is a copy that remains regardless.
- **Test recovery.** Open a few images from your backup every now and then. That's the only way you know it's working.
- **Check old storage media.** No storage medium lasts forever. Copy important collections to newer media in a timely manner.

## Data protection: Images of other people

In your photos there are not only landscapes, but also people: friends, colleagues, neighbors, children. The best way to deal with this is respectfully.

- **Ask before publishing or sharing.** There are legal rules surrounding publishing other people's pictures, and many people don't want their photos to end up on the internet or in large groups without being asked.
- **Be particularly cautious with photos of children.** Pictures of children do not belong in public profiles, even in harmless situations.
- **Delete on request.** If someone asks you to delete a photo, do it.
- **Check shared albums.** Regularly check who has access and stop sharing that you no longer need.
- **Pay attention to location data.** Many cell phones store the location in the image. You can remove this information before sharing publicly.

## The monthly routine: so that it doesn't grow again

A tidy collection only stays tidy if new images are sorted regularly. A quarter of an hour at the beginning of the month is usually enough.

1. **Copy new images into the collection folder.** From your cell phone and all other devices.
2. **Delete screenshots and note photos.** All that's done.
3. **Thin out series images.** Keep the best, delete the rest.
4. **Sort.** Move images into folders by year and event.
5. **Mark favorites.** Add the most beautiful pictures of the month to the annual selection.
6. **Check backup.** Briefly check whether the backup is current.

It's also helpful to have a habit when taking photos: it's better to take three deliberate shots than thirty hasty ones. And immediately after a trip in the evening, take a quick look through and delete the obvious mistakes. ## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Delete without backup:** Copy first, then sort.
- **Want everything at once:** Work in short stages, otherwise you'll give up after the first evening.
- **Too many folders:** A structure that you cannot remember will not be used.
- **Store on mobile only:** A single storage location is not an archive, it is a risk.
- **Forgotten Accounts:** Old cloud accounts and email addresses often contain photos that you no longer think about.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many photos should I keep?

There is no correct number for this. Keep as many as you enjoy looking at and can secure with reasonable effort. The year selection is the part that really counts.

### What do I do with videos?

Treat them like photos, only more strictly, because they take up a lot more storage space. Shorten long recordings to the most beautiful moments and delete shaky clips.

## Briefly summarized

Back up your photos before you begin. Collect all images from your cell phone, old devices, memory cards, computers and cloud accounts in one folder and copy instead of moving. Sort out in stages: first screenshots, messenger pictures and series, then blurred items and finally the rest year after year. Create a simple structure by year and event, mark favorites and treat yourself to photo books or prints. Save everything in multiple locations, be respectful of other people's pictures, and keep the collection organized with a short monthly routine.
