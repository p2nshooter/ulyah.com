The basement is often the most honest room in an apartment. Everything you don't want to decide about ends up here: the box from the last move that was never unpacked, the children's bike, the previous tenant's drill, three half-empty paint buckets, Christmas decorations, camping equipment, old electrical appliances "in case the new ones break". With each passing year the door becomes harder to open, literally and figuratively.

A storage room doesn't have to look perfect. It just needs to work reliably: you want to find the winter tires, the suitcases or the Christmas baubles without spending half an hour stacking boxes first. This guide will show you how to organize your basement, attic, storage room or garage in manageable stages and set it up so that order remains.

## Clarify beforehand: What should the room do?

Before you open a single box, answer one question: What do you need the space for? A basement can be a warehouse, workshop, pantry, bicycle garage or laundry room. If you give him two or three clear tasks, it will be easier to make decisions. Anything that doesn't fit any of these tasks is a candidate to be discarded.

Also check the conditions:

- **Humidity:** Is the room dry? Are there any mold stains or a musty smell? Paper, textiles, leather and electronics tolerate moisture poorly.
- **Temperature:** Items at risk of frost such as paints, glue or certain cleaning agents need a frost-free place.
- **Access:** How often do you have to enter? A cellar compartment in the communal cellar is suitable for rarely used things, not for everyday supplies.
- **Rules in the house:** In apartment buildings there are often fire protection requirements, such as that escape routes must remain clear or that no flammable liquids may be stored in large quantities. It's worth taking a look at the house rules.

## Stage 1: Clear out and sift through

For an average basement room, plan on a full day or two half days. Make sure you have good lighting, work gloves, trash bags, some empty boxes and labels.

Clear out the room as much as possible. Yes, really out. Only when the shelves are empty will you see how much space you actually have and whether the walls or floor are showing any damage. If clearing out can't be done all at once, work shelf by shelf.

## Stage 2: Sorting with five zones

Divide the free area in front of the room into five zones:

| zone | What goes in |
|---|---|
| Stays | is needed and has a place |
| Belongs somewhere else | for example to the apartment, to relatives, back to the neighbor |
| Give away or sell | well preserved, but unnecessary for you |
| Recyclable materials and hazardous waste | Electrical devices, batteries, paints, chemicals |
| Garbage | broken, moldy, unusable |

For each item, ask yourself simple questions:

- When was the last time I used this?
- Would I buy it again today?
- Would I have missed it if I hadn't known it was here?
- Does it still work and are all parts complete?

Particularly common basement dwellers and how to deal with them:

- **Moving boxes that have never been opened:** If you haven't missed anything from the box in years, that's a strong sign. Take a quick look inside anyway, because sometimes there are important papers there.
- **Spare parts and cables without device:** Without the right device, they are ballast.
- **Furniture “for later”:** If there is no concrete “later” in sight, give it away or sell it while someone can still use it.
- **Children's clothing and baby gear:** Only keep what you save for a specific sibling or person.
- **Hobby Equipment:** Be honest with yourself about what hobby is really still active.

## Stage 3: Dispose of properly

Especially in the basement, things accumulate that don't belong in the residual waste bin:

- **You can return old electrical devices** to a recycling center or retailer. Larger electrical retailers and many grocery stores with sufficient sales space take back small old devices under certain conditions.
- **Batteries and accumulators** belong in the collection boxes in stores.
- **Paints, varnishes, solvents, pesticides and chemicals** are hazardous waste. The municipalities offer pollutant collection points or mobile pollutants. In many municipalities, hardened, dried emulsion paint can be disposed of in the residual waste; Find out more from your local waste disposal company.
- **Energy-saving lamps and fluorescent tubes** contain mercury and are part of the collection point.
- You can register **bulky waste** online in many cities.

There are good ways to find everything that is still usable: neighborhood portals, classified ads, swap meets, social department stores or a “to give away” box in front of the house, if that is common in your area. You can find more ideas in the guide [Borrow, share, exchange](/ratgeber/leihen-teilen-tauschen/).

## Stage 4: Systematic tidying up

Now comes the satisfying part. To keep things in order, a few principles help.

### Define zones

Divide the room into areas that suit its tasks, for example:

1. **Seasonal:** Christmas decorations, garden furniture upholstery, winter items
2. **Leisure:** Camping, sports, suitcases
3. **Tools and DIY:** Toolbox, screws, paints
4. **Supplies:** Drinks, non-perishable foods, household supplies
5. **Archive:** Boxes with documents, memory boxes

What you often need comes forward and at eye level. Items that are rarely used can be moved to the back or top.

### Shelves instead of stacks

Open heavy-duty metal shelving is ideal for basements. They allow air to circulate, support a lot of weight and make the contents visible. Place shelves some distance away from the wall to prevent moisture from getting trapped behind them, and don't store anything directly on the floor. Even a pallet or the lowest shelf level protects against water if something leaks.

### The right containers

- **Transparent plastic boxes with lids** protect against dust, moisture and vermin and show what's inside.
- **Cardboard boxes** are only a good idea in the basement for a short time. They absorb moisture and become soft.
- **Same box sizes** stack better and look quieter.
- **Vacuum bags** save space for duvets and textiles, but should be filled dry.

### Label, always

Each box gets a label on the side you see when it's on the shelf. The label doesn't say "Miscellaneous", but specifically: "Christmas: balls and fairy lights" or "Camping: stove, dishes, lamp". The guide [Fixed Places and Labels](/ratgeber/feste-plaetze-und-beschriften/) explains the principle behind fixed places and labels.

### A simple inventory list

For larger warehouses, a list is worthwhile: box number, contents, shelf. This could be a note on the basement door or a note on your cell phone. If you store a lot, take a photo of the contents of each box and save the image under the box number.

## Keep order

A cellar fills up slowly and quietly. These habits will prevent it from becoming a junk room again:

- **One in, one out:** If something new comes into the basement, you check whether something can be used for it.
- **No temporary storage:** The basement is not a place for decisions that you put off. Don't put anything there without giving it a place.
- **Annual check:** Once a year, around spring, you walk through the room with a box and take with you what is no longer needed.
- **Take advantage of seasonal changes:** When you're putting away the Christmas decorations or putting the garden furniture away for the winter, take a quick look at the shelves next to them.

## The basement as a storage room for emergencies

A tidy basement is good for a small emergency stash. The Federal Office for Civil Protection and Disaster Relief recommends having food and drinks in the house for around ten days in case of emergencies such as longer power outages or storms. The guideline for drinking water is around two liters per person per day. That sounds like a lot, but it can be easily achieved with a fixed shelf and a little planning.

It is important that there is a supply alive. The principle of “first in, first out” has proven to be successful: you put new packs at the back, older ones at the front, and you gradually use up the supply in everyday life instead of leaving it untouched until the expiry date. Add a flashlight with batteries, a battery-operated or crank radio, candles and matches, a medicine cabinet and essential medications. The Federal Office provides a checklist for stockpiling on its website.

The stock belongs in the area with the best conditions: dry, cool, frost-free and easily accessible. Do not store food next to paints, solvents or cleaning products.

## An example: A cellar compartment of six square meters

A typical basement compartment in an apartment building shows what this all looks like in practice. Before, there were boxes up to the ceiling, two bicycles leaning against the door, and no one knew where the suitcases were. After organizing, there are three shelves on the back wall and one side:

- **Shelf on the left, eye level:** Supplies for emergencies and drinks, easily accessible
- **Shelf left, top:** Christmas decorations and Easter box, labeled
- **Rear shelf:** Toolbox, drill, screws in assortment boxes, paint residues only in small, well-sealed containers
- **Shelf on the right:** Camping equipment and suitcases, stacked inside each other
- **Wall hook:** Bicycles can be hung vertically to save space

There is a list of box numbers hanging on the door frame. Four moving boxes with unopened items, two defective electrical appliances, a box of cables without a device, old paint and a stroller that found a new owner within a day in a neighborhood group were cleared out. The effort: two Saturdays and a trip to the recycling center.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I prevent things in the basement from getting musty?

Ventilate properly: in summer on cool mornings or evenings, not in the midday heat, because warm, moist air condenses in the cool basement. Store textiles in closed boxes and keep them away from the wall. If there are persistent moisture problems, a hygrometer helps to assess the situation; If you have mold, get professional advice.

### Can I store petrol or gas bottles in the basement?

There are safety regulations for flammable liquids and gas bottles that often set strict limits. Clarify this with the property manager or landlord and follow the manufacturer's instructions.

### What do I do with things that belong to other people?

Give them a deadline. A friendly message with a date by which the items should be picked up usually works wonders.
