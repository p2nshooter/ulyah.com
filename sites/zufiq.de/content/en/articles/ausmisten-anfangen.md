You're standing in the hallway, looking into the apartment and don't know where to start. There is something everywhere: bags that have never been unpacked, cables without devices, boxes from the last move. Every room feels equally urgent, which is exactly why you don't start anywhere. This feeling is completely normal. A full apartment has grown over the years and cannot be emptied in one afternoon.

What helps is a timetable. This guide will show you in which order you can progress most easily, which time model suits your everyday life, what you can pay attention to in each room and where the things that have been sorted out go. There are separate, detailed instructions for individual areas such as the wardrobe, kitchen, books and memorabilia, which we refer to along the way.

## Before you get started: four basic rules

1. **First clear out, then organize.** Don't buy new boxes or shelves until you know what's left. Otherwise you will lovingly sort things that you don't want to keep.
2. **One area at a time.** It's better to have one drawer completely finished than five closets half-emptied. One finished area motivates, five open construction sites exhaust.
3. **Work with three containers.** One for things that go, one for trash, and one for anything that stays but belongs in another room. You only empty this third basket at the end of a unit, otherwise you'll constantly be running around the apartment and getting lost.
4. **A passage ends at the front door.** It is only locked when the things have left the apartment. Plan the removal from the start.

## The order: from easy to difficult

When decluttering, you make hundreds of small decisions. This takes strength, and the ability to make good decisions increases with practice. That's why the order is so important: you start where there are hardly any feelings involved and train before you get to the difficult things. Memorabilia, photos and heirlooms go at the very end.

| stage | areas | Why at this point? |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Obvious trash, empty packaging, expired items | Hardly any real decisions, quick visible success |
| 2 | Bathroom, pantry, hallway | clear criteria such as durability and use |
| 3 | Clothing and shoes | Fit and wearing habits make it easy |
| 4 | Kitchen | lots of duplication, little feeling |
| 5 | Living room, media, books | more personal connection, but easy to oversee |
| 6 | Work corner and papers | requires concentration and sometimes research |
| 7 | Storage room, cellar, attic | a lot of forgotten things, often heavy and bulky |
| 8 | Memorabilia | the hardest decisions when you're already practiced |

This is a suggestion, not a requirement. If a certain room bothers you every day, you can start there. Just remember this principle: If you get stuck in one place because it's getting too personal, put the matter aside and move on to something easier.

## Three time models to choose from

Not everyone has free weekends, and not everyone likes big promotions. Choose the model that suits your everyday life or combine them.

### Short sessions of 15 minutes

Set an alarm for 15 minutes and choose a small area: a drawer, a shelf, the compartment under the sink. When the alarm goes off, you stop, even in mid-swing, and put the containers in their place. This model is particularly suitable if you have little energy or large projects quickly become overwhelming. A small area per day adds up to a surprising amount after a month.

### The weekend promotion

For an entire room, such as the work corner or storage room, half or a full day makes sense. Plan breaks and a proper meal, get boxes and bags in advance and clarify when things will be picked up or taken away. It is helpful to have a second person who doesn't decide, but rather supports, asks questions and keeps you happy. Start in the morning and stop in good time so that everything isn't on the floor in the evening.

### The 30 day plan

A month with a fixed topic per day provides structure without being overwhelming. An example for 20 to 30 minutes each:

| Section | topics per day |
|---|---|
| Day 1 to 7 | Garbage and packaging, bathroom cabinet, cosmetics, medication, towels and bed linen, shoes in the hallway, jackets and bags |
| Day 8 to 14 | four days of wardrobe sorting by category, socks and underwear, accessories, taking away items that have been discarded |
| Day 15 to 21 | four days of kitchen cupboard for cupboard, supplies, living room cupboards, books and media |
| Day 22 to 27 | Desk, cables and technology, roughly sift through papers, storage room, basement or attic, hobby items |
| Day 28 to 30 | taking away everything that has been sorted out, the first souvenirs, looking back and taking next steps |

You don't make up for missed days, you just move on to the next topic. A plan is a help, not a contract.

## Short checklists room by room

These lists show where unnecessary things accumulate in most apartments. They don't replace detailed instructions, but they give you a starting point for each room.

### hallway

- Shoes that pinch or have not been worn for a whole season
- Jackets, scarves and hats in multiple versions
- Advertising, flyers and old mail on the dresser
- Keys without a known lock: first clarify what they belong to, then decide
- Cloth bags and bags stacked on the hook

### bathroom

- cosmetics that you no longer use or that have been left open for too long; Many products show with a small jar symbol how many months they can be used after opening
- Samples and travel bottles that have been lying around for a long time
- Old medicines: check the expiry date, never throw them away through the toilet or sink, and ask your local authority or pharmacy how to do this correctly
- Hairbrushes, hair ties and tools that double up

### Living room

- DVDs, CDs and games that you no longer play or can no longer play
- Remote controls and cables that no longer belong to a device
- Magazines and catalogs
- Decoration that you hardly notice anymore
- more pillows and blankets than you actually use

### bedroom

- everything on the bedside table that has no purpose there
- Bedding: Many people get along well with two sets per bed
- Things under the bed you don't remember
- the chair with the clothes on it: where does it actually belong?

There are separate instructions for the closet itself: [Clear out the closet] (/ratgeber/kleiderschrank-ausmisten/).

### Work corner

- empty pens, dried markers, office supplies in quantities for an entire office
- User manuals for devices you no longer own
- old cell phones, chargers, USB sticks and hard drives
- Stack of papers: only roughly pre-sort them here, as retention periods sometimes apply to contracts, tax and insurance documents

### Storage room and cellar

- Boxes of devices that have long since been unpacked unless you need them for a foreseeable move or shipping
- Furniture that needs to be repaired “at some point.”
- Paint residues, varnishes and old chemicals that are part of the pollutant collection
- Sports equipment, suitcases and tools that are duplicated or no longer used

### Areas with their own guidance

Some areas need more space than a checklist provides. For the kitchen with dishes, utensils and appliances, [Clear out the kitchen](/ratgeber/kueche-ausmisten/) will help you. If your shelf is bursting at the seams, you can find questions for each individual book in [Decluttering Books](/ratgeber/buecher-ausmisten/). And for letters, children's drawings and heirlooms that are your last concern, there's [Clearing Out Memorabilia](/ratgeber/erinnerungsstuecke-ausmisten/).

## Where should the discarded items go?

The best day of mucking out is of little use if the bags sit in the hallway for months afterwards. So when sorting, decide which way is right for which thing.

| way | Suitable for | Pay attention to this |
|---|---|---|
| Give away | well-preserved everyday things, furniture, toys | Offer it specifically to friends or in the neighborhood, arrange a pick-up appointment |
| Sell | Electronics in good condition, sought-after furniture, collectibles | Honestly assess the time required, flea market or online classifieds, set a deadline |
| Donations | Clothing, household goods, books, toys | Social department stores, clothing stores, public bookcases; ask beforehand what will be accepted |
| Bulky waste | large furniture, mattresses, carpets | Depending on the municipality, registration or fixed dates, processes and costs vary |
| Recycling center | Wood, metal, electrical appliances, pollutants | Check opening times and acceptance conditions beforehand |
| Dealer returns | Old electrical devices, batteries and accumulators | Delete data and remove batteries beforehand if possible |

### Electrical appliances and batteries

You can recognize electrical appliances by the crossed-out garbage can and they do not belong in the general waste. You can hand them in at the recycling center. Many retailers who sell electrical appliances also take back old devices under certain conditions, and sometimes even small devices without purchasing new ones. You can find out what applies in individual cases directly from the dealer. You throw batteries and accumulators into the collection boxes that are available in many stores. Before submitting, delete all personal data from cell phones, laptops, tablets and hard drives.

### The rules of your municipality

Bulky waste, recycling centers and pollutant collection are regulated differently from place to place. Whether you have to register bulky waste, how often it is free and what the recycling center accepts can be found on the website of your city or district. The waste advisory service also answers questions by telephone. Only leave things on the street to give away if you allow it, and bring them back in if no one takes them.

### A deadline for selling

Selling is especially worthwhile for a few valuable things. Give yourself a deadline of a few weeks. Anything that hasn't found a buyer by then is given away or donated. This means that the sales pile does not become new inventory.

## Motivation and setbacks

Decluttering is not a straight path. There are days full of momentum and days when a single drawer seems insurmountable. These ideas will help you stick with it:

- **Make progress visible.** Photograph an area before and after decluttering. On tough days, the picture reminds you of what you have already accomplished.
- **Enjoy finished areas.** Consciously open the tidy drawer and notice how easily you can now find something.
- **Find allies.** A friend who cleans out at the same time or a set date where you report to each other makes a lot of things easier.
- **Watch out for decision fatigue.** If everything suddenly seems important or doesn't matter, you've used up your energy for the day. Stop and continue tomorrow.

Setbacks are part of it. A strenuous period of work, an illness or a move, and things start piling up again. This doesn't erase your progress. Instead of waiting for the perfect moment to make a big fresh start, start fresh with a quick 15-minute session.

### Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Clear out everything at the same time:** In the end, the apartment is fuller than before. Work area by area.
- **Just reorganizing:** Moving things from one closet to another feels like work, but it doesn't change anything.
- **Temporary storage of discarded items:** What is waiting in the basement for the flea market often never gets there.
- **Deciding about other people's things:** Decide together when it comes to common things, never give away other people's things without asking.
- **Start straight away with the memories:** This almost always slows you down. They come to the end.

## And after that: order that remains

When a room is cleared out, give everything that remains a permanent place. Only then will the new calm last in everyday life. The guide [Fixed Places and Labels](/ratgeber/feste-plaetze-und-beschriften/) explains how this works.

## Briefly summarized

Start lightly and work your way up to the difficult things: first trash and expired items, then the bathroom, hallway, clothes and kitchen, later papers, the basement and at the very end, memorabilia. Choose a time model that suits you, whether 15 minutes a day, a weekend promotion or a 30-day plan. Work with three containers, lock each doorway at the front door and find out what disposal options your municipality offers. Setbacks are normal, a quick restart will get you back into the flow.
