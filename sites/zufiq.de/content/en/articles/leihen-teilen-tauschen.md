It is often said that a drill is only actually used for a few minutes in its lifespan. It's hard to say whether the exact number is correct, but everyone knows the principle from their own experience: the ladder is in the basement for eleven months, the raclette grill is on the table on New Year's Eve, the tent is waiting for the next summer, and the high-pressure cleaner cleans the terrace once a year. We don't need many of the things we own often, we only need their function occasionally.

Borrowing, sharing and exchanging starts right there. If you share things, you save money, space and resources, and you often get to know your neighbors at the same time. This guide will show you what options are available, what things are particularly worth borrowing for, how to make appointments fair and what you should know about insurance and liability.

## Why using is more important than owning

Owning has advantages: things are there when you need them and you don't have to ask anyone. But it also has costs that we rarely take into account. Every thing needs space, needs to be cared for, maintained, stored and eventually disposed of. A full basement, an overflowing tool shed or a closet full of rarely used kitchen appliances are the result of many individual, sensible-looking purchases.

When you borrow rather than buy things, you shift the question from “What do I want?” to “What do I really need and how often?” For things that you use daily or weekly, owning them usually makes sense. For everything you only need now and then, it's worth taking a look at the alternatives. This attitude fits well with a relaxed minimalism, as described in the guidebook [Minimalism without Dogma](/ratgeber/minimalismus-ohne-dogma/).

## Which is particularly suitable for borrowing

| Category | Examples | Why borrowing makes sense |
|---|---|---|
| Tools and machines | Hammer drill, jigsaw, tile cutter, carpet cleaner | expensive, bulky, rarely used |
| Garden | Scarifier, shredder, hedge trimmer, ladder | seasonal, requires a lot of storage space |
| Leisure and travel | Tent, sleeping bags, roof box, child trailer, snowshoes | only used a few weeks a year |
| Festivals and celebrations | Beer tent set, party tent, large pots, dishes for many guests | used for individual occasions |
| Children's equipment | Baby bouncer, travel cot, balance bike, children's skis | is only used for a short time, children grow quickly |
| Kitchen | Raclette, fondue, dehydrator, preserving pot | seasonal or for experiments |
| Technology | Projectors, camera lenses, measuring devices | expensive, often just for one project |

Less suitable are things that you need very often, items with high hygiene requirements and things whose value lies primarily in personal connection.

## The options at a glance

### Friends, family and neighbors

The easiest way is often the closest. Many people have exactly what you are looking for in their basement and are happy to lend it out. A short sentence in the family group or a note in the stairwell is enough. Anyone who offers things themselves quickly notices that a small network of give and take is formed.

### Neighborhood platforms

Digital neighborhood networks connect people in a neighborhood. There you can lend, borrow, give away things or offer help. The advantage: The distances are short and you meet again. Pay attention to the privacy settings on such platforms and what information you share publicly.

### Libraries of things

There are so-called libraries of things or rental shops in more and more cities. There you can rent tools, household appliances, games, musical instruments or camping equipment for a small fee or free of charge. Some city libraries have expanded their offerings to include such items. Ask your library or look for a rental store in your city.

### Swap meets and swap parties

At clothing swap parties, book swaps, plant swaps or toy swaps, things change hands without any money flowing. Open bookcases can be found in squares and parks in many cities. Those who sort things out will find a good way for things that can still bring joy; The guide [clearing out books](/ratgeber/buecher-ausmisten/) tells you how to do this.

### Commercial rental and sharing offers

Hardware stores rent machines on a daily basis, sports shops rent skis and bicycles, and there are sharing offers for cars, cargo bikes and vans. This costs money, but is often significantly cheaper than buying it yourself, especially when you factor in maintenance, storage and insurance.

### Buy together

For things that several households need regularly but not all the time, it can make sense to purchase them together: a lawnmower for the household, a trailer for three families, a workbench in the communal basement. Discuss in advance who will pay for what, where the device will be located, who will maintain it and what will happen if someone moves out.

## Good dates when borrowing

Borrowing only works in the long term if everyone involved feels comfortable. A few simple rules will help:

1. **Clearly agree on how long.** “Until Sunday evening” is better than “a few days”.
2. **Check the condition together.** A quick look or a photo when handing over avoids later misunderstandings.
3. **Return clean and complete.** With all parts, batteries charged, tools cleaned.
4. **Replace consumables.** Anyone who uses drills, saw blades or gasoline will replace them or offer compensation.
5. **Address damage openly.** If something breaks, say so immediately and offer a solution.
6. **Say thank you.** A small thank you, a piece of cake or something in return next time keeps the network alive.

## Insurance and liability

When borrowing from private individuals, the question arises at some point: Who pays if something breaks? When borrowing, the basic rule is that the borrower must handle the item carefully and be responsible for any damage caused. As a rule, it does not have to replace normal wear and tear caused by contractual use.

Whether private liability insurance covers damage to borrowed items depends on the contract. Many older tariffs exclude borrowed or rented items, while newer tariffs often include them, sometimes with maximum limits. A look at your own insurance conditions or a quick inquiry from the insurer will provide clarity, especially before you rent expensive equipment. For commercial lenders, the rental conditions regulate liability; read it before signing.

Special rules apply to vehicles and motorized equipment. Before renting a car, find out whether the insurance covers other drivers.

## Sharing in everyday life: small steps

You don't have to start a library of things right away. Even small steps make a difference:

- Make a list of things you like to lend out and share it with friends or neighbors.
- Before you next buy a rarely used device, ask if someone close to you already has it.
- The next time you clear out things, offer them for loan or give away before you throw them away.
- Organize a clothing, book, or game swap party with friends once a year.

## What you gain from it

Money and space are the obvious advantages. The encounters are at least as valuable. Anyone who borrows a ladder starts a conversation. Anyone who shares their lawnmower suddenly knows the people in the house. Trust grows from small acts of kindness, and this trust carries even in difficult times. Many people say that they feel more at home on their street since they share things.

## An example: The rental list of a household

An apartment building with twelve apartments shows what sharing can look like in everyday life. After a court party, a simple list is posted on the bulletin board and in the house group: Who has something they would lend? Within a week there was a drill, two ladders, a cordless screwdriver, a sewing machine, a fondue pot, a beer tent set, a children's travel cot, a cargo bike trailer, a tool box, a roof box and a Kärcher. Each party enters what it gives and how to achieve it.

The rules fit on half a page: Return on the agreed date, clean and complete, consumables are replaced, damage is reported immediately. After a year, several families decided not to buy anything of their own, and the basement has become noticeably emptier. But perhaps the biggest benefit is something else: the people in the house greet each other by name.

## Assess the benefits honestly

Borrowing is not automatically always the better choice. If you drive across town twice to get a drill, a short trip to a neighbor or a cheap device for your own use might make more sense. For commercial offers, calculate the rental costs against the purchase price if you need a device more often. And some things you just want to have for yourself, like a good mattress, hiking boots or your favorite knife in the kitchen. Conscious consumption does not mean no longer owning anything, but rather consciously deciding what you want to own and what you prefer to use. When buying, it helps to look at quality, as described in the guide [Identify Quality](/ratgeber/qualitaet-erkennen/).

## Typical mistakes

**Lending without prior agreement.** Unclear return times lead to trouble, even among friends.

**Lend things that you are very attached to.** Only lend what you could bear to lose in an emergency.

**Buy everything because borrowing seems cumbersome.** An in-house inquiry often takes five minutes, while a purchase with delivery and later storage takes much longer.

**Shared purchases without rules.** Shared ownership requires clear agreements on costs, care and exit.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I find a Library of Things near me?

Ask at your local library, environmental agency or local sustainability initiatives. Many rental shops are organized on a voluntary basis and are happy to receive support.

### What do I do if an item doesn't come back?

Remember kindly and specifically, with a date. If that doesn't help, be open about whether there is a problem. In the vast majority of cases it is forgetfulness, not malicious intent.

### Is it worth renting for expensive technology?

Often yes, for example for a special camera lens for a trip or a projector for a party. Then check the liability issue particularly carefully and document the condition upon handover.

### Can I pass on borrowed items to third parties?

Only with permission. Anyone who borrows something is generally not allowed to lend it to others without their consent. If a friend could also use the borrowed ladder, ask the owner briefly. This protects everyone involved if something happens.
