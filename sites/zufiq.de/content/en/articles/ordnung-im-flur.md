It's ten past seven, you have to go. The key is not in the bowl, but probably in yesterday's jacket, which is hanging on the hook under two other jackets. There are six pairs of shoes on the floor, none of them the ones you're looking for. There are piles of letters, advertising, a return and a hat from last winter on the dresser. And as you search, a quiet morning turns into a hectic one.

The hallway is the smallest room with the biggest task. This is where you arrive every day and where you leave every day. If it doesn't work, you'll notice it twice a day. This guide will show you how to understand the hallway as an arrival and departure zone, limit jackets and shoes, find fixed places for keys and bags, set up a station for mail and items to take with you, and keep order in just five minutes a day, even in narrow hallways and rented apartments.

## The hallway as an arrival and departure zone

Many small actions happen in a short space of time in the hallway. If you go through them consciously, you will quickly see where there is a missing space.

| Wait | What happens | What it takes for that |
|---|---|---|
| Arrive | Put down your keys, take off your shoes, hang up your jacket, put down your bag, bring in the mail | a bowl or hook near the door, a place for shoes, free hooks, a compartment for the mail |
| In between | Put down your purchases, accept packages, let wet umbrellas drain | a free storage space, an umbrella stand or hooks |
| Break up | Put on your shoes, grab your jacket, keys, bag, think about things to take with you | a place to sit, everything within reach, a visible place for things that need to be with you |

This leads to the most important rule for the hallway: Only what is needed when arriving or leaving belongs here. Everything else, such as the vacuum cleaner, the laundry basket or drinks crates, takes up space for these handles. If there is no other place for it in your apartment, at least it gets a clearly defined space that doesn't block the paths.

The basic principle behind it, i.e. giving every thing a permanent home close to its use, is explained in detail in [Fixed Places and Labels](/ratgeber/feste-plaetze-und-beschriften/). This is about how you actually implement it in the hallway.

## Inventory: What is actually in the hallway?

Before you assign new places, take a look at what lives in the hallway. The easiest way to do this is in half an hour with three baskets or bags.

1. **Clear everything from the floor, dresser and hooks.** Only what is standing or hanging in the hallway, not the entire apartment.
2. **Sort into three groups.** First: used daily or almost daily when coming and going. Second: belongs in the hallway, but not in the season. Third: belongs somewhere else.
3. **Take the third group away immediately.** Put the sports bag in the bedroom, the tools in the box, the deposit bottles in the shopping bag.
4. **Get rid of what no one wears anymore.** Squeezing shoes, too many cloth bags, scarves without an owner. You can find a short checklist for the hallway in [Start clearing out](/ratgeber/ausmisten-anfangen/).
5. **Only clear the first group.** The second group gets a place outside the hallway.

## Limit jackets and shoes

The wardrobe and shoe rack are the quickest to overflow because each person is constantly adding something and rarely taking anything away. It is helpful to have clear boundaries: Each person in the household has a fixed place in the hallway, and no more can fit there.

| What | In the hallway per person | Where the rest goes |
|---|---|---|
| Jackets and coats | the one or two jackets of the current season | Wardrobe, cupboard in the storage room |
| Shoes | the pairs that are currently being worn regularly | Shoebox or box in the closet, basement |
| Hats, scarves, gloves | one basket or compartment per person, only in cold weather | a labeled box for the warm season |
| Bags | the bag for every day | Closet, one bag stored in the other |
| Umbrellas | one umbrella per person, plus one spare | pass on additional umbrellas |

### Seasonal change

You replace them twice a year, for example when it gets warm in spring and cold in autumn. Winter jackets, boots and hats go into the closet or a box, light jackets and sandals go into the hallway. Take this opportunity to check what no longer fits or is broken and put it aside. If you are using space in the basement or storage room, a well-labeled box will help you. How you organize these rooms yourself can be found in [Organize the basement and storage room](/ratgeber/keller-und-abstellraum-ordnen/).

### A place for guests

Plan where visitors will put their jacket and shoes. A free hook or a few hangers on a rod are enough. Otherwise, guest jackets end up on the sofa or bed.

## Fixed places for keys, bags and things to go out with

Most of the searching in the hallway revolves around a few things. For them, a particularly well-thought-out space is worthwhile.

### key

The key needs a place that you can reach almost automatically when you come in, i.e. within easy reach of the door and on the same side as your free hand. A bowl, a hook or a small board is enough. It is important that you always put it in the same place, even if you only come in for a short time.

A few tips for security: The key should not be visible from outside, for example through a window next to the door. Don't keep spare keys right next to them, but in another, inconspicuous place in the apartment. If your car key has a keyless locking system, find out from the manufacturer or the police how you can protect it from theft with a radio extension. It is often recommended not to keep it directly at the front door.

### Bags

The everyday bag gets its own hook or a permanent place on a shelf. So that it doesn't become a second storage area, empty it completely once a week: receipts, packaging and stray pens come out, and essentials such as tissues and shopping bags are refilled. If you change your bag frequently, you will benefit from a small bag for the most important things that moves from bag to bag.

### Going out things

Sunglasses, headphones, bike lights, shopping bags, charging cables for when you're on the go: little things like these are often scattered around the chest of drawers, window sills and jacket pockets. Give them a common compartment or a small basket near the door, preferably one for each person.

## A post and takeaway station

The chest of drawers in the hallway is the place where everything that is on the move is collected. A small station with two directions brings order.

### Entrance: the post office

1. **Sort out advertising immediately.** A container for waste paper near the door ensures that brochures don't end up on the dresser in the first place.
2. **All other letters in one inbox.** A single compartment or tray, not multiple stacks.
3. **Empty the compartment regularly.** Once a week or at a fixed date, go through the mail and put it in your filing cabinet. How you can set up a simple system for this can be found in [filing system for papers](/ratgeber/ablagesystem-fuer-papiere/).

### Output: what has to be taken along

For things that need to leave the apartment, such as returns, borrowed books, letters to be thrown in or returnable bottles, set up a place right by the door that you can't miss when you go out. A hook on the door handle for a bag or a basket right next to the shoes has proven useful. For things that only need to be taken with you tomorrow, such as documents for an appointment, a simple rule helps: put them directly on or next to your bag in the evening.

## Ideas for narrow hallways and rental apartments

Not every hallway has space for closets. And in a rental apartment, you may not want to drill holes in the wall. These ideas usually don't require major intervention:

- **Freestanding wardrobes** do not require attachment to the wall. Make sure you have a secure footing.
- **Narrow shoe cabinets with folding compartments** take up little depth and hide the mess.
- **A bench with compartments or baskets** offers a place to put on shoes and storage space at the same time.
- **Hooks for hanging over the door** use the inside of doors without drilling.
- **Adhesive hooks or strips** are an option for light things. Check the stated load capacity and remember that some adhesives cannot be removed without leaving residue. If in doubt, ask your landlord first.
- **A mirror and good light** make a narrow hallway appear larger and friendlier.
- **Wall space instead of floor:** Anything hanging doesn't take up space on the floor. In any case, keep the path to the door clear.

In an apartment building, it is tempting to store shoes, umbrellas or strollers in the stairwell. The house rules and rental agreement regulate whether this is allowed, not least because escape routes must remain clear. If in doubt, ask the property manager.

## The five-minute hallway routine

A short daily round is enough to ensure that the hallway is ready every morning. It's best suited to the evening, for example as part of your [evening routine](/ratgeber/abendroutine-entwickeln/).

1. **Shoes back.** All couples standing around come to their places.
2. **Jackets on the hooks.** Whatever is no longer needed this season goes into the closet.
3. **Post in the inbox.** Advertising directly in the waste paper.
4. **Check the key.** Is it in the tray? If not, look now, not tomorrow morning.
5. **Check where you can take with you.** Do you need to take anything with you tomorrow? Then it is now ready.

Once a week you add a few minutes to the routine: empty your pockets, work on your in-box, work through your take-away basket and sort your things to go out.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Use the hallway as storage:** What is not needed when coming and going belongs somewhere else.
- **More hooks instead of fewer jackets:** If the wardrobe is overflowing, the inventory is too big, not the hook too small.
- **A common storage area for everyone:** Without designated areas for each person, no one knows who owns what. One basket per person creates clarity.
- **Spots too far from the door:** A key rack at the other end of the hallway is not used.
- **Forget the seasonal change:** In summer, boots don't need space in the hallway.

## Frequently asked questions

### My hallway is so small that I can't even fit a dresser. What now?

Then the wall space becomes important: a few hooks near the door, a narrow shelf for keys and mail, shoes on a flat shelf. Tighten the limit per person even further and consistently outsource everything that is seasonal.

### How do I get everyone in the household to participate?

Involve everyone in the planning and give each person their own space. Children do well with low hooks and baskets that they can reach themselves.

## Briefly summarized

The hallway is your arrival and departure zone, and only what you need when coming and going belongs there. Take inventory, limit jackets and shoes per person, and swap out seasonally twice a year. Give keys, bags and items a permanent place near the door and set up a station with an entry compartment for mail and an exit point for things to take with you. Use free-standing furniture and wall space in narrow hallways and rental apartments without blocking escape routes. And give the hallway five minutes every evening so that your morning starts calmly.
