Hardly anything is as unsettling when tidying up as paper. With an old sweater, you know if you're still wearing it. However, when it comes to a bank statement from 2017, a utility bill or a letter from an insurance company, the silent question remains: What if I need that again? This insecurity gives rise to folders, boxes and drawers full of papers that no one looks at anymore, but that no one dares to throw away.

This guide will help you bring calm to the pile of papers. You will find out which documents you should keep permanently, which can go after a few years and which you can dispose of almost immediately. The end result is a simple deadline list that will allow you to sort things out in half an hour once a year instead of losing a whole Saturday every few years.

An important note in advance: The information refers to private individuals in Germany and provides a general overview. Other and longer obligations sometimes apply to the self-employed, landlords, companies or special circumstances. If in doubt, ask your tax advisor, a tax assistance association or the responsible office.

## Why it's worth knowing deadlines

Most people keep too much, not too little. This is rarely due to negligence, but rather a lack of knowledge. If you don't know how long a document is important, you keep it forever. This has consequences: the really important papers get lost among the unimportant ones, and in an emergency you end up looking for the birth certificate among twenty years of cell phone bills.

Anyone who knows the rough deadlines can make a decision on the other hand. Many documents have a clear purpose, such as proof of tax returns or proof of a warranty. If this purpose is fulfilled, the paper can go. Other documents are permanently important because they prove your CV, your pension or your rights. These belong in a safe, secure place.

## The three stacks: forever, for a while, gone immediately

Before we get to individual documents, a simple mental model will help. Almost every paper belongs in one of three piles.

### Forever: Documents that prove your life

These documents are difficult or impossible to replace and can still be important decades later:

- Birth, marriage and death certificates, family register, divorce decree
- Certificates from school, training and studies as well as job references
- Proof of pension: pension information, insurance history, evidence of child-rearing periods, training periods and unemployment
- Real estate documents, such as purchase contracts, land register extracts and building plans
- Will, certificate of inheritance, power of attorney and living will (or an indication of where the originals are located)
- Vaccination certificate, important medical reports and documents on chronic illnesses
- Naturalization certificate, name changes and comparable evidence

Pension documents in particular are often underestimated. The statutory pension insurance will clarify the insurance account with you, but gaps can be closed most easily with your own evidence. Keep such receipts until your pension is finally determined and, if in doubt, beyond.

### For a while: documents with expiration dates

These papers have a purpose that will eventually be fulfilled:

- Documents for the tax return
- Proof of purchase for warranty and guarantee
- Invoices from craftsmen and service providers on the house
- Bank statements
- Documents for current contracts such as rent, insurance, electricity or cell phone
- Utility and heating cost bills

### Gone immediately: papers with no evidentiary value

Advertising, brochures, catalogs, expired vouchers, information letters without legal effect, duplicate printouts and receipts for small items such as groceries have no place in your filing system. Dispose of them as soon as you open the mail, preferably in the same place where the mail arrives.

## Deadlines at a glance

The following table summarizes the common orientation values for private individuals. It does not replace advice in individual cases, but it does help to classify most papers safely.

| Document | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Certificates, certificates, pension certificates | permanently | difficult to replace, important for pensions and authorities |
| Documents for tax returns | Until the tax assessment is final, a few years to be on the safe side | The tax office can request additional documents as long as the notice can be changed |
| Tax assessments | permanently or for a very long time | Proof of income, pension and applications |
| Invoices for tradesman services on the property | at least two years | Legal retention obligation for private individuals according to the VAT Act |
| Purchase receipts for larger purchases | at least for the duration of the warranty and guarantee | Evidence of defects |
| Bank statements | usually a few years | Proof of payments, the regular limitation period is three years |
| Rental agreement and handover protocols | until the end of the rental agreement and the settlement of the deposit | Damage and deposit disputes |
| Utility bills | a few years | Examination and comparison, possible reclaims |
| Insurance policies | as long as the contract runs, then for a while after that | Proof of protection against late damage |
| Pay slips | at least until the pension is determined, annual reports permanently | Proof of retirement account |

### A closer look at tax documents

For several years now, private individuals have generally no longer had to attach supporting documents to their tax returns. However, the tax office can request them. Therefore, the following applies: keep receipts as long as the tax assessment can still be changed. In practice, this usually means several years. If you want to be on the safe side, keep the receipts for a tax year until the decision is final and no change seems possible. Taxpayers with very high excess income are subject to statutory retention requirements of six years. The self-employed and landlords have their own, longer obligations.

You should keep the tax assessment itself for much longer. It proves your income and is helpful, for example, for applications, loans or pension questions.

### Proof of purchase, guarantee and warranty

You generally have a two-year warranty from the retailer for new goods. There are also voluntary guarantees from manufacturers, which often last longer. The easiest way to enforce both claims is with the proof of purchase. Therefore, keep receipts for electrical appliances, furniture, bicycles and other larger purchases for at least as long as the warranty and guarantee are valid. For expensive things such as jewelry, instruments or art, it is worth keeping the receipt permanently, for example as proof of value for household contents insurance.

A practical tip: thermal paper fades. Take photos or scan important receipts immediately after purchase. How you can do this without much effort can be found in the guide [Digitize documents] (/ratgeber/dokumente-digitalisieren/).

### Craftsman invoices

If you, as a private individual, commission construction work or other work on a property, such as roof repairs, new windows or work in the garden, you must keep the invoice for two years in accordance with the VAT Act. In addition, such invoices are often important for tax returns because tradesman services in the budget can sometimes be taken into account for tax purposes. And if there are defects in the construction, longer warranty periods apply than when purchasing movable items. For major work on the house, it therefore makes sense to keep the invoice, offer and acceptance together for several years.

## This is how you keep what you want to stay

Getting the deadline right is only half the battle. It is at least as important that you find the papers again. A division into three locations has proven successful:

1. **The document folder for the most important things.** Certificates, certificates, pension certificates and powers of attorney belong together in a folder or folder that is in a safe place. Many people use a fireproof document case for this. This folder is also the core of an [Emergency Folder](/ratgeber/notfallordner-anlegen/).
2. **The folder for ongoing life.** This is where contracts, insurance, tax, banking and housing are located, separated by topic. The guide [filing system for papers] (/ratgeber/ablagesystem-fuer-papiere/) explains how to set it up.
3. **The archive for completed years.** One box or file folder per year for tax receipts and bank statements, labeled with the year and destruction date. It can be kept in the basement or on the cupboard as long as it stays dry.

### Write down the date of destruction right away

The most important trick of all: Write not only the year on each archive box, but also the date from which it can be disposed of. Then you won't have to look anything up later. A sticker with the text “Tax 2025 – may go away from 2031” takes away any future uncertainty.

## Dispose of safely

Many documents contain personal information: account numbers, addresses, dates of birth, health information. They do not belong in the waste paper without being shredded.

- **Document shredder:** A device with particle cutting is usually sufficient for the household. Strip cut is easier to put back together.
- **Data protection bin:** Some municipalities, employers or waste disposal companies offer locked containers for confidential papers.
- **Blackening:** For individual sheets it is sufficient to make names, addresses and numbers unrecognizable before the paper goes into the waste paper.

Remove plastic wrappers, staples and paper clips beforehand so that the paper can be easily recycled.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

**Keep everything forever.** This feels safe, but it slows you down when you're looking for something. A document that you can't find is as good as non-existent.

**Want to clear out everything at once.** If you want to sort through fifteen years of paper in one afternoon, you'll give up after two hours. Only plan on one box or one year per appointment.

**Throw away originals because a scan exists.** A digital copy is sufficient for most everyday receipts. However, you should keep the originals of documents, notarial documents, certificates and wills.

**Don't take roommates into account.** Sort out papers that concern a partner or family together. This way everyone will later know where everything is.

**Damp storage locations.** A box in a damp basement can become unreadable after a few years. Store archive boxes elevated and dry.

## Your annual paper lesson

So that the pile doesn't build up again, plan a fixed paper hour once a year, for example in January, when the documents for the tax return come together anyway. The process:

1. Create the archive box for the past year and label it with the destruction date.
2. Look at the boxes that have reached their date and dispose of them safely.
3. Go through the current life folder: canceled contracts go into the archive, new contracts get a register.
4. Check the document folder: Is everything still up to date, such as copies of ID cards, powers of attorney and contact details?

This lesson combines well with a look at ongoing costs. If you already have account statements in hand, you can quickly see which debits you still need. The guide [Check and cancel subscriptions](/ratgeber/abos-pruefen-und-kuendigen/) shows you how to do this.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to keep bank statements as a private individual?

There is generally no general legal obligation for private individuals. However, bank statements are a convenient proof of payment. Since the regular statute of limitations for many claims is three years and only begins at the end of the year, it makes sense to keep statements for a few years. Many banks provide them digitally anyway; But check how long they remain available in online banking.

### Is a scan enough instead of the original?

For purchase receipts, invoices and many everyday documents in your private life, an easy-to-read digital copy is usually sufficient. When it comes to certificates, certificates, notarial documents and wills, the original remains decisive.

### What do I do with the documents of a deceased relative?

Separate considerations apply here, such as inheritance, taxes and current contracts. Don't sort anything out too quickly, but talk to the other heirs and, if necessary, to a notary or tax advisor.

### How do I start when the mountain of paper is huge?

With the smallest possible step: take a single folder or box and sort it into the three piles. Then you stop. This rhythm carries you further than a big resolution.
