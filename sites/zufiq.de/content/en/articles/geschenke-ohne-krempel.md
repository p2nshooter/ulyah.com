The birthday is just around the corner, Christmas is approaching, a friend's wedding is next month. And with every occasion comes the same question: What should I give as a gift? The search often ends with something that looks nice, fits somehow and ends up in a drawer. On the other hand, even after every celebration, you find yourself with a small pile of well-intentioned things that you neither need nor really like.

Giving is a wonderful thing. It shows affection, attention and gratitude. But a gift doesn't necessarily have to be an item that takes up shelf space. This guide will show you how to find gifts that bring real joy without filling other people's homes with clutter, and how to deal kindly with gifts that don't suit you.

## Why so many gifts become junk

Most bad gifts do not arise from indifference, but from time pressure and uncertainty. Shortly before the event, you can't think of anything, so you grab something general: a scented candle, a funny mug, a decorative object. The gift serves its purpose for the moment, but it has no place in the recipient's everyday life.

Added to this is the desire to have something “to unpack”. At first glance, a voucher or invitation seems less festive than a large package. The experience of many people shows that experiences and time spent together are remembered for a long time, while most objects are quickly forgotten.

And finally, there is the habit of always bringing something with you on certain occasions, even if everyone involved secretly wishes something different. An open word often helps here.

## The four types of clutter-free gifts

Almost all gifts that don't require clutter can be divided into four groups. If you can't think of anything, go through these groups in order.

### 1. Experiences

A concert visit, a theater ticket, a cooking class, a trip to a climbing park, a city tour or entry to the thermal baths. Experiences create memories and often shared time when you share them together. What is important is that the experience really suits the person. If you don't like crowds, you probably won't be happy about festival tickets.

**Tip:** Instead of a fixed date, you can offer a selection. “I invite you to an evening of your choice: cinema, concert or cooking class.” This way you avoid appointment stress and still meet the taste.

### 2. Consumables

Good olive oil, a special coffee, tea from a small roastery, regional specialties, high-quality soap, flowers or a bouquet from your own garden. Consumable gifts bring joy without taking up permanent space. They are ideal for people who “have everything”.

Pay attention to real quality instead of elaborate packaging. A simple jar with excellent honey from the beekeeper often seems more personal than a large gift basket full of filling material.

### 3. Time and help

A self-designed voucher for an evening of babysitting, help painting the apartment, a day hiking together, repairing the bike or a home-cooked meal. Especially for parents of small children, older people or friends in a stressful phase of life, gifted time is often more valuable than any object.

So that such vouchers are not forgotten, suggest a specific date right away or give a friendly reminder after a few weeks.

### 4. Targeted wishes

Sometimes an item is exactly the right gift, namely when it is really needed or wanted for a long time. A good kitchen knife for someone who likes to cook, a rain jacket for a hiking friend, a book that someone has wanted to read for a long time. The key is listening, not guessing.

## Listening as the best gift idea

The best gift ideas rarely come up just before the occasion, but rather in everyday life. When a friend mentions that her favorite pan is broken or your dad talks about a museum he'd like to visit, that's a gift idea.

To do this, create a simple note, perhaps on your cell phone or in a small notebook, with one page per person. Write down what you notice in the conversation: wishes, hobbies, favorite authors, clothing sizes, dislikes. When the next birthday comes around, you already have several suitable ideas. This way you also avoid the pressure to buy something at the last moment and the typical [impulse purchases](/ratgeber/spontankaeufe-vermeiden/) that arise from this.

## Wish lists: helpful instead of unromantic

Many people find wish lists to be impersonal. They can save givers and recipients a lot of stress. Especially in families with many gift-giving occasions, they help to avoid duplication and bad purchases.

A few ideas on how wish lists work well:

- **Rough directions instead of exact products.** “Books about garden design” or “something for my new camera” leave room for your own ideas.
- **Include experiences and consumables.** This way the list doesn't just become a shopping list.
- **Multiple price ranges.** So everyone can find something that fits their budget.
- **Enable shared gifts.** A larger wish can be fulfilled by several people together instead of everyone buying a small thing.

## Homemade things with meaning

Homemade gifts have a special value if they are created with love and consideration for the person receiving the gift. So that they don't become well-intentioned clutter, consumable or useful things are usually the better choice.

| idea | Why it is well received |
|---|---|
| Homemade cookies or bread | consumable, personal, almost everyone is happy |
| Preserves, jam, chutney | long lasting, beautifully packaged in a glass |
| Herbal salt or spice mixtures | small, useful, cheap |
| Photo book with shared memories | just an object, but with great emotional value |
| Family recipe collection | connects generations, especially beautiful at weddings |
| Knitted socks or hat | if the person likes and wears something like that |

The photo book in particular shows that an object does not have to be clutter. What matters is whether it has a permanent place and meaning in the life of the person receiving the gift. If you are sorting your pictures anyway, as described in the guide [Ordering digital photos] (/ratgeber/digitale-fotos-ordnen/), such a gift almost comes naturally.

## Gifts for children

Children often receive a flood of toys for birthdays, Christmas and other occasions, which quickly causes children's rooms to overflow. Many parents therefore want fewer but more thoughtful gifts.

Proven ideas are:

- **Shared excursions.** A day at the zoo, the swimming pool, the children's museum or on the farm, ideally together with the person giving the gift.
- **Courses and memberships.** Swimming course, library card, annual ticket for the zoo.
- **Crafts and consumables.** Paints, modeling clay, paper, kits that are used.
- **High quality, versatile toys.** Building blocks, wooden toys, books that will be used for years.
- **Borrow toys instead of buying them.** Some cities have toy libraries. More about this in the guide [Borrow, share, exchange] (/ratgeber/leihen-teilen-tauschen/).

Talk to your parents before buying. Most people are grateful for the question and often have specific wishes or needs that you don't think about, such as winter boots in the next size or a bicycle helmet.

## Agree on new rules with the family

In many families and circles of friends, a gift culture has developed over the years that no one is actually entirely happy with. Everyone gives gifts to everyone, budgets increase, and in the end there is a lot of stress. An open conversation helps here, ideally long before the next event.

Possible agreements:

- **Secret Secret Santa instead of giving gifts to everyone.** Everyone only gives a gift to one randomly drawn person, but with more care.
- **Set a budget.** A clear cap takes the pressure off of trying to outdo each other.
- **Only give gifts to the children.** The adults avoid giving gifts to each other and instead do something together.
- **Donate together.** Instead of giving each other gifts, the family supports a project that is close to everyone's hearts.

Such conversations are easier if you express your own wishes honestly and without blame: "I notice that all the gift-giving is stressing me out. Do we want to try something different this year?"

## Gifts at work

A lot of gifts are also given in the professional environment, for team birthdays, farewells or as thanks to business partners. Consumable gifts are almost always the best choice here: good chocolate, coffee, a shared breakfast for the team or a voucher for a nearby restaurant. When doing business, pay attention to the rules of your employer and the organization receiving the gift, as many companies and authorities have clear guidelines about what can be accepted.

## When you yourself receive clutter as a gift

Despite all your good intentions, you will always be given things that don't suit you. That's no reason to feel guilty. A gift is a gesture, and you can accept the joy of that gesture, even if the item itself doesn't make you happy.

Here's how to deal with it calmly:

1. **Thank you genuinely for the gesture.** “How nice that you thought of me” is always true.
2. **Keep only what you use or love.** A gift does not require you to keep it for life.
3. **Feel good about passing it on.** Giving away, donating, trading or selling are all legitimate ways. Just make sure that the person giving the gift isn't unhappy about it.
4. **Share your wishes next time.** If you know what makes you happy, you will give more accurately.

When [decluttering](/ratgeber/ausmisten-anfangen/) you come across old gifts that you keep only out of obligation, ask yourself: Would the person giving the gift want this thing to burden me? In the vast majority of cases the answer is no.

## Packaging without waste

The packaging can also be card-free. Cloth bags, tea towels, newspaper, wrapping paper with stamps, reusable cans or beautiful glasses replace glossy paper and plastic bows. The Japanese furoshiki technique of wrapping gifts in cloth is an elegant solution in which the wrapping itself becomes part of the gift. Many recipients are happy if they can continue to use the packaging.

## Frequently asked questions

### Isn't a voucher impersonal?

A generic department store coupon can actually seem impersonal. A voucher for a specific experience, a person's favorite store or time spent together, on the other hand, is very personal. What matters is how well it fits the interests of the person receiving the gift.

### What do I give someone who already has everything?

Experiences, consumables and time. A meal together, a concert, good food or a handwritten letter with memories are often more valuable than any other item.

### How do I say I don't want gifts?

Friendly and timely. For example: “I really don’t need anything, but I would really appreciate an afternoon together.” This way you give those around you an alternative instead of just rejecting it.

### Are monetary gifts OK?

In many situations yes, especially at weddings, for young adults or if there is a specific larger wish. A loving card or creative packaging turns money into a personal gift.
