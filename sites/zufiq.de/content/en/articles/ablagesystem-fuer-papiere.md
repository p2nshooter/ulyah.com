The property management wants a copy of the rental agreement, the health insurance company asks for a certificate, and the invoice from the tradesman is still missing for the tax return. You know that everything is somewhere. Maybe in a pile on your desk, maybe in a drawer in the hallway or in the box you packed “temporarily” the last time you moved. Half an hour later you found two of three papers and were in a bad mood.

A good filing system is not a hobby for particularly tidy people. It's a simple tool that saves you exactly that half hour, every time. This guide will show you what equipment you need, what a tried-and-tested category scheme looks like, why you should separate permanent and annual filing, and how a short weekly routine prevents new piles from forming.

## Why piles of paper are so stubborn

Every letter requires a small decision: Do I have to do something? Do I have to cancel this? Where to go with it? If there is no quick answer, the letter stays there. Things that still need to be done are mixed in the pile with things that just want to be put away and with advertising that has long since belonged in the waste paper. It is precisely this mixture that makes stacks so stressful. You never know exactly whether there is still a deadline in it.

A filing system neatly separates these three things: What arrives ends up in a fixed place. What needs to be done is clearly a matter of a second nature. What is done goes into a file where it has its permanent place.

## The basic equipment

You need less than stationery departments suggest. Don't buy until you know how many categories you have.

- **One Inbox:** a single location for all new mail, receipts and notes that need to be kept. Ideally near the door to your apartment or where you usually open mail.
- **A To Do folder:** for anything that requires you to do something, such as a transfer, a form, or a callback.
- **Folder or hanging file:** for the actual filing.
- **Tabs or dividers:** to allow you to create subcategories within a folder.
- **Hole punch, labels and an easy-to-read pen.**
- **A paper shredder or a container for papers with personal data:** Bank statements, old invoices with your address or customer number do not belong in the waste paper without being shredded.

### Folders or hanging files?

| | file folder | Hanging file |
|---|---|---|
| Discard | Hole punching and stapling, a little more effort | Just put the sheet in, very quickly |
| Search | Papers stay firmly in order | Papers can slip |
| place | needs shelf space | needs a drawer or hanging box |
| Good for | Permanent storage, annual folder, archive | current documents, frequently used categories |

Many households combine both: hanging files for the current year and frequently used documents, folders for what is left lying around for a long time. Both are fine. It is important that the same categories apply in both systems.

## A proven category scheme

Most private documents can be sorted into eight areas. You can customize the list, but don't start with twenty categories. Divisions that are too fine make it difficult to put things down, and then everything stays put again.

| Category | Typical documents |
|---|---|
| Personal | Certificates, certificates, proof of training, registration certificates |
| Housing | Rental agreement or purchase documents, utility bills, handover protocols, craftsman's invoices |
| Finance and tax | Account statements, credit agreements, tax assessments, receipts for tax returns |
| Insurance | Policies, premium invoices, changes, correspondence regarding claims |
| Health | Findings, doctor’s letters, certificates, letters from the health insurance company |
| work | Employment contract, pay slips, references, letters about pension insurance |
| vehicle | Purchase contract, workshop invoices, test reports, letters about vehicle insurance or taxes |
| Contracts | Cell phone, internet, electricity, memberships, subscriptions, warranty receipts for larger purchases |

### Here's how to set up the scheme

1. **Label a folder or hanging file for each category.** Categories for which you hardly have any papers can share a folder for the time being, separated by a register.
2. **Create subcategories with registers.** For example, under “Insurance” one register per insurance, under “Contracts” one per provider.
3. **Fill chronologically within a register.** The most recent goes to the front or top. This way you can find current letters immediately.
4. **Write down the categories.** A short list on the inside of the closet will help you and others file.

A trick helps with warranty receipts: file the receipt together with the operating instructions. If the device breaks, you have both in one place. Thermal paper fades over time, a copy or photo of the receipt provides security. The guide [Digitizing Documents] (/ratgeber/dokumente-digitalisieren/) explains how you can reliably scan paper and store it digitally.

## Separate permanent storage and annual storage

Not all documents age the same. A rental agreement remains valid until you move out. A bank statement from March will be just one receipt among many next year. If both are in the same folder, it will become thicker and more confusing every year. It is therefore worth distinguishing between two types of filing.

| | Permanent storage | Annual filing |
|---|---|---|
| What belongs in it | Documents that remain valid until replaced | Documents that belong to a specific year |
| Examples | Contracts, policies, certificates, certificates, loan agreements | Bank statements, pay slips, invoices, receipts, utility bills |
| What happens at the end of the year | remains standing, outdated items are sorted out | is closed, labeled with the year and placed in the archive |

In practice it looks like this: For permanent storage, you have your folders divided into categories. For the annual filing, you create one or two folders per year, also with registers by category. At the beginning of the year, you start a new annual folder and the old one goes into an archive compartment, such as the top shelf or the storage room. When you file your tax return, you have almost all of the year's receipts in one place.

How long you should keep individual documents depends on the type of document and your life situation. The guide [Keep documents] (/ratgeber/unterlagen-aufbewahren/) provides an overview. If in doubt, ask the office that issued the document or a tax advisor.

## The weekly post routine

The best system is of no use if the mail is left lying there. A fixed routine in two parts ensures that nothing grows anymore.

### Daily: collect only

When you take the mail out of the mailbox, immediately sort out anything that is clearly advertising and throw it in the recycling bin. Everything else goes in the inbox, including receipts that you want to keep and notes from school or the club. You don't have to do anything else that day.

One exception: you open letters from authorities, courts or with formal delivery, usually in a yellow envelope, immediately. There are often deadlines.

### Weekly: do, file, throw away

Set aside a fixed quarter to half an hour once a week, for example on Saturday mornings or as part of your [weekly planning](/ratgeber/wochenplanung-und-wochenrueckblick/). Empty the inbox completely:

1. **Open everything.** Even the letters you're avoiding. When unopened, they are usually more threatening than their contents.
2. **Do small things immediately.** What can be done in just a few minutes, a transfer, a short answer, a signature, you do straight away.
3. **Put bigger things in “To Do.”** Write the deadline prominently at the top of the page and put it on your calendar.
4. **File what is done.** Into the appropriate permanent or annual filing category.
5. **Throw away what you don't need.** Shred papers with personal information and put the rest in the recycling bin.
6. **Look at the “To Do” folder.** Is there anything in there with a near deadline? Then plan it for the coming week.

In the end the inbox is empty. That is the goal of each round.

### Mail that arrives digitally

Many invoices and certificates today come as PDFs via email or are stored in a customer account. Choose one path: Either print and file important items, or store them digitally in folders with the same eight categories. A mixed system, with half in the folder and the other half somewhere in the mailbox, leads to searching again.

## Storage for couples and households

When several adults live together, a few agreements are needed so that the system doesn't just exist in one person's head.

- **A common scheme:** Everyone uses the same categories, even if individual areas are managed separately.
- **Separate common and personal:** Rental agreement, household contents insurance and electricity contract belong in the shared file. Work, health and personal accounts have their own register or folder for each person, which can be easily identified by a color.
- **Clarify responsibilities:** Who empties the inbox, who handles which areas? What is important is not that everything is distributed fairly, but that each person knows what they are responsible for.
- **Both know the system:** Even if you don't usually bother with paperwork, you should know where the most important documents are. In the event of illness or an accident, it is also worth having your own [emergency folder](/ratgeber/notfallordner-anlegen/) with the most important documents and contact details.

In a shared apartment, a common register for rental agreements, additional costs and joint contracts is often sufficient. Each person does everything else for themselves.

## Annual thinning

The filing deserves a thorough once a year. A good time is the beginning of the year, when a new annual folder begins anyway, or the time when you are preparing your tax return.

1. **Close the previous year's year folder.** Label it with the year and put it in the archive.
2. **Go through the recurring file.** Canceled contracts, expired policies, and manuals for devices you no longer own will move from the active file. Please keep cancellation confirmations and final statements for a while.
3. **Check the archive.** Which old annual folders have exceeded their retention period? If you are unsure, check the retention period guide or ask before destroying something.
4. **Destruct securely.** Larger amounts of paper containing personal information can be disposed of using a document shredding service.
5. **Adjust the categories.** If one register is overflowing or one is left empty, change the classification.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Start shopping:** First sort, then know how many folders you need.
- **An “Other” category:** This will quickly become the largest folder of all. If something doesn't fit anywhere, a category is missing.
- **Everything in clear sleeves:** This makes folders thick and filing them slow. Covers are only worthwhile for originals that you don't want to punch, such as documents.
- **Use the in-basket as storage:** If the basket never gets empty, it's just a neater stack. Keep the fixed appointment.
- **Keep everything forever:** A shelf that is never thinned out will eventually be as full as the drawer that started it all.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much time do I need to set up the system?

That depends on your mountain. Divide the work: one day the equipment and the labels, then an hour each for one or two categories. You can first roughly sort old stacks into the eight areas and then file them away later.

### What do I do with documents that fit two categories?

Put them where you would look first. A craftsman's invoice for the apartment ends up under "living", even if you need it for taxes. For your tax return, you can put a copy in your annual folder or write down a note.

## Briefly summarized

A filing system for papers requires little: an in-basket, a folder for unfinished items, folders or hanging files and eight clear categories. Separate the permanent file for valid contracts and documents from the annual file for receipts and statements. Collect everything in the basket every day and empty it completely once a week: do it, put it down, throw it away. Agree in the household who will do what and thin out the shelves once a year. Then you can find any paper when someone asks for it.
