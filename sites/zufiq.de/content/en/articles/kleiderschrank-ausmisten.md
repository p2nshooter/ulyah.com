A full wardrobe and still nothing to wear: Many people know this feeling. In the morning you move the hangers back and forth, then put the same pants back on and the door can only be closed with force. Clearing out your closet is therefore one of the best ways to start a calmer everyday life. You see immediately what has changed and you feel it anew every morning.

This guide takes you step by step through decluttering, helps you with difficult decisions and shows where discarded clothing can usefully go. In the end you won't end up with an empty cupboard, but one in which you will find what you are looking for.

## Why the closet is a good place to start

Clothing is manageable. Unlike memorabilia or paperwork, most decisions are made quickly: Does it fit, do I wear it, do I feel comfortable in it? At the same time, you use your closet every day. Every improvement is immediately noticeable, and this quick success provides motivation for the next rooms.

A tidy closet also saves decisions. If you choose between a few pieces that can be easily combined in the morning, you'll be ready quicker and start the day more relaxed. This goes well with a quiet [evening routine](/ratgeber/abendroutine-entwickeln/) in which the clothes for tomorrow are already ready.

## The preparation: time, space and four stations

Plan a fixed time window for clearing out, ideally during daylight and without deadline pressure. Two to three hours are often enough for an average cabinet. If that's too much, try one category on several days. Have the following ready:

- a mirror in which you can see yourself completely
- a free area, such as the bed, where you can spread out clothes
- Bags or boxes for four stations, clearly labeled
- a note for things that you are missing or need to be replaced

The four stations are:

1. **Keep:** You'll love wearing this, it fits, and it's in good condition.
2. **Pass it on:** In good condition, but no longer yours. It goes to people who will wear it.
3. **Alter or have repaired:** A missing button, a hem that is too long. Only if you actually put it back on afterwards.
4. **Dispose of:** Worn, broken or so worn out that no one can use it.

There is also a fifth, small box: the **Maybe Box**. More on that later.

## Step by step through the closet

### 1. One category at a time

Don't look at the entire closet at the same time, but always one category: for example, first tops, then pants and skirts, then dresses, sweaters, jackets, underwear and socks, and finally shoes and accessories. Start with the category that is easiest for you. For many people these are socks and underwear because there are hardly any feelings involved.

### 2. Take everything out

Get all the items in the category out of the closet, including the laundry basket, the suitcase in the attic and the dresser in the hallway. Only when everything is in a pile do you realize how many black T-shirts or blue jeans you actually own. This moment is often the most important of the whole process.

### 3. Take every part in your hand

Pick up each item of clothing individually and ask yourself a few honest questions:

- When was the last time I wore this?
- Does it suit me now, not someday?
- Do I feel comfortable in it, or do I just wear it when everything else is in the wash?
- Would I buy it again today?
- Does it fit my everyday life as it really is?

### 4. If in doubt, try it on

For pieces you're thinking about, the only thing that helps is to try them on. Many decisions make themselves in front of the mirror: the sweater is itchy, the pants are pinching, the color doesn't suit you. This is not a judgment about you, but rather information about the garment.

### 5. Distribute consistently to the stations

Place each piece on a station immediately and don't delay the decision. If you find yourself debating on a part for longer than a minute, it goes into the maybe box.

### 6. Use the maybe box

Parts that you don't want to part with yet go into the maybe box. Write a date on the box, approximately three months in the future, and place it out of sight. If you don't miss anything from it and reach into it during this time, you can pass it on with a clear conscience. Often after three months you don't even know exactly what's inside.

### 7. Clean up systematically

Whatever remains, you put away so that you can easily find it and put it back. Things you wear every day should be at eye and reach height. Items that are rarely worn move up or down. Many people find it clearer to place T-shirts and sweaters upright next to each other in drawers instead of stacking them so you can see everything at a glance. Hang clothes by type and within the type by color, this looks calm and helps with combinations.

### 8. Act immediately

The most common mistake when clearing out happens afterwards: the bags of discarded clothing remain in the hallway for weeks and are returned piece by piece. Take away anything that is passed on within a few days. It's best to put the bags directly in the car or next to the front door and plan a specific date for this.

## Decision support at a glance

| Question | If yes | If no |
|---|---|---|
| Did I wear it last season? | check further | more likely to pass on |
| Does it fit me well now? | keep | pass it on or have it changed |
| Do I feel comfortable in it? | keep | pass on |
| Is it in good condition? | keep or pass on | Have it repaired or dispose of it |
| Would I buy it again today? | keep | honestly think about why not |

## Difficult cases

### Expensive bad purchases

The coat was expensive, so it has to stay? Unfortunately, the money has already been spent, whether the coat is hanging in the closet or not. If you don't wear it, it will only cost you space and a guilty conscience. Well-preserved, high-quality parts can often be sold used, and you'll learn something for your next purchase.

### Clothing in a different size

Many people save clothes for “when I lose weight again” or “when I gain weight.” Ask yourself honestly whether these parts motivate you or put you under pressure. Clothes should fit the body you have today. If you want to keep some favorite items, give them a limited space, such as a single box, rather than half the closet.

### Gifts and heirlooms

A gift did its job when you received it. You are not obligated to keep it forever. Things are different when it comes to clothing worn by deceased relatives, because this is about remembrance. Take your time and consciously keep one or two special pieces. Some also have something new sewn from favorite fabrics, such as a pillow.

### Clothing for special occasions

A formal suit, a dress for weddings, hiking clothes for your annual vacation: you rarely wear such items, but they have a purpose. Keep what you need for recurring occasions and store it safely away from everyday use so that it doesn't get in the way when you reach into the closet every day.

## Where should the discarded clothing go?

Well-preserved clothing deserves a second life. There are many options:

- **Friends and family:** Ask specifically whether someone can use certain items instead of handing out bags without asking.
- **Clothing chambers and social department stores:** Many institutions welcome donations. Ask in advance what is currently needed, because often certain sizes or seasons are missing, while others are in abundance.
- **Second-hand and sales:** High-quality or rarely worn items can be sold in second-hand shops or on online platforms. But only plan as much time as you really want to invest.
- **Clothing Swap:** Many cities have swap parties where you give away clothes and take others with you. Make sure you don't take more with you than you gave away.
- **Old clothing collection:** For clothing that is still wearable. Find out who runs the container and what happens to the donations.

Depending on where you live, what is really broken and worn belongs in a collection for used textiles or in the residual waste. The waste advisory service in your city or district will know which rules apply to you.

## This keeps the closet tidy

Decluttering is a start, not a one-time event. These habits will help prevent your closet from overflowing again in a year:

- **One in, one out:** For every new item, an old one leaves the closet.
- **The ironing trick:** Hang all the hangers upside down. After wearing, hang one part back the right way up. After a few months, you'll see exactly what you never wear.
- **A quick look per season:** When you swap summer and winter clothes, you immediately check what no longer fits or pleases.
- **A gap list:** Write down what you're really missing and buy specifically based on that rather than spontaneously.

## When cleaning out is more than order

Almost everyone finds it difficult to part with some things. But if letting go causes intense anxiety, if clothes and other things fill entire rooms and you withdraw as a result, there may be more to it than a lack of order. Then getting support is not a sign of weakness. You can find information about who can help on the [Help & Contact Points](/hilfe-und-anlaufstellen/) page.

## Briefly summarized

Clean out your closet category by category, take everything out, examine each item with honest questions and consistently divide it into four stations. Uncertain parts can be placed in a dated maybe box. Arrange what you keep so that you will enjoy using it, and get what you have discarded out of the house as quickly as possible. With small habits like “one in, one out” the new calm is maintained. You can find more ideas in the room [Decluttering & Minimalism](/themen/ausmisten-und-minimalismus/).
