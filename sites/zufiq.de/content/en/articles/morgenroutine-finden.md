A lot is written about morning routines, and a lot of it sounds strenuous: get up at five, meditate for an hour, exercise, journal, cold shower, green juice. For most people with work, kids, a commute, or simply a normal need for sleep, this isn't feasible. And it's not necessary. A good morning routine is nothing more than a small, reliable sequence of steps that takes you from waking up to a calm start instead of causing you to stumble into hectic and searching.

In this guide, you'll find out what your morning really needs, how to build a routine that lasts even on difficult days, and what you can do if the alarm clock still rings too early every day.

## Why morning has so much influence

The start of the day often sets the tone. Anyone who looks at their cell phone when they wake up reads messages, emails and appointments before they are fully awake and immediately thinks about the needs of others. If you look for your key in the morning, can't find a clean blouse and wolf down your breakfast standing up, you'll take this restlessness with you into the day.

A routine makes decisions for you in the morning. You don't have to think about what comes next because the order is fixed. This saves energy for the things that really need attention later. At the same time, it creates a small, protected space that is just yours, even if it's only for ten minutes.

## The most important part begins in the evening

A morning routine rarely fails in the morning itself, but almost always the evening before. If you go to bed too late, you will struggle against tiredness in the morning, and no routine in the world will help. Two things are therefore more important than any building block in the morning:

- **A realistic get-up time** that suits your sleep needs. Work out how many hours of sleep you need to feel rested based on your wake-up time and plan your bedtime accordingly.
- **An evening prepared.** Bag packed, clothes laid out, breakfast prepared, kitchen halfway set back. What is done in the evening is not missing in the morning. How to do this can be found in [Develop an evening routine](/ratgeber/abendroutine-entwickeln/).

## The building blocks of a morning routine

Not every building block fits every life. Choose two to four that are good for you and that you can realistically do.

| building block | What it is used for | Example for everyday life |
|---|---|---|
| Arrive without a screen | Collect your thoughts, don't react immediately | the first 20 minutes without a cell phone |
| Light and air | wake up | Open the curtains and open the windows briefly |
| Drink and breakfast | nourish the body | a glass of water, eat in peace |
| movement | stimulate circulation | Five minutes of stretching, a short walk |
| Organize the day | Gain clarity | Write down three most important tasks |
| Something just for you | Joy, calm | a cup of tea by the window, reading a few pages |
| Order when leaving | come back calmly | Make the bed, put the dishes in the sink |

## Step by step to your own morning routine

### 1. Watch your mornings for a week

For a week, write down when you get up, what you do in what order, when you leave the house and where there are problems. Maybe you waste ten minutes a day searching, maybe you look at your phone for twenty minutes in bed, maybe you don't eat breakfast at all. This observation shows you where a routine has the most benefit.

### 2. Determine the fixed end point

Every morning routine has an end that cannot be postponed: the bus, the start of work, kindergarten. Work backwards from this point to see how much time you realistically have. Allow a buffer of ten minutes. A morning that is timed to the minute is thrown into disarray at the slightest incident.

### 3. Choose a few building blocks

Start small. Two to three building blocks are enough to start with. Anyone who tries to introduce new habits for an hour usually gives up after a few days. How to build new habits sustainably is explained in [Building New Habits](/ratgeber/neue-gewohnheiten-aufbauen/).

### 4. Set a fixed order

The order is more important than the individual steps. If each step triggers the next, the routine soon runs almost by itself: get up, open the window, drink water, get dressed, have breakfast, write down three tasks, and get started. Write the order on a piece of paper at the beginning and hang it where you will see it in the morning.

### 5. Plan a minimum version

There will be days when you oversleep, a child is sick, or you feel bad. For these days you need a minimum version that fits in five minutes: for example, a glass of water, take a deep breath, write down the most important thing for the day. The minimum version keeps the habit alive even if the full version doesn't work.

### 6. Keep your cell phone at a distance

For many people, this step is the most effective. Charge your phone outside of the bedroom, use a simple alarm clock, and don't check messages until you're dressed and have had breakfast. If that's too hard, start with ten minutes. For more ideas, see [Tame Notifications](/ratgeber/benachrichtigungen-zaehmen/).

### 7. Check after two weeks

After two weeks you will know what works. Keep what works for you, cut out what feels like a chore, and maybe try a new building block.

## Three examples of different lives

### For commuters

Get up, turn on the lights, open the windows. A glass of water, dressed, breakfast in ten minutes at the table, not standing up. The bag has been packed at the door since the evening before. On the train: ten minutes of music or reading before using the cell phone for messages. The three most important tasks are noted along the way.

### For parents of small children

If you have children, you can only plan your morning to a limited extent. It is helpful to get up ten minutes before the children for a quiet coffee or tea. Everything else is prepared the night before: lunch boxes, clothes, bags. The children's routine is their own task, their own routine can then be very small.

### For the home office

When working from home, there is no natural transition to work. Create an artificial one: a quick walk around the block before sitting down at your desk, or a set routine like getting dressed, making coffee, opening the window, setting up your workspace. It is important not to move from bed to laptop in your pajamas.

## If it doesn't work: typical stumbling blocks

| stumbling block | What's behind it | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| You keep hitting the snooze button | not enough sleep or alarm clock within reach | Go to bed earlier, set an alarm clock across the room |
| The routine takes too long | too many building blocks | reduce to two or three |
| The cell phone pulls you in | habit, curiosity | Charge your cell phone outside the bedroom |
| You feel guilty when it doesn't work out | too high a demand | Make minimum version count |
| Every morning is different | Shift work, changing appointments | Link routine to getting up, not to the time |

## A weekend is not a working day

Many morning routines are tailored to workdays. The rhythm is different on the weekend, and that's okay. Nevertheless, it's worth keeping a few elements in place so that Monday doesn't feel like a new start. It has proven to be a good idea not to delay getting up by more than an hour on the weekend and to maintain one or two elements of the routine, such as a glass of water and airing out. The rest can be slower and more enjoyable: a hearty breakfast, a walk, time with the family.

This keeps your internal clock stable and at the same time you benefit from the freer weekend rhythm. The guide [Improving Sleep Habits] (/ratgeber/schlafgewohnheiten-verbessern/) explains how important regular times are for sleep.

## The morning routine as the seasons change

In summer it is light early in the morning, in winter the day often starts in darkness. This affects your mood and your ability to wake up. In winter, bright light as soon as you get up, a short walk outside as soon as it gets light and a warm, cozy atmosphere at breakfast help. In summer you can use the early light hours for a walk or a few minutes on the balcony.

It's okay to adjust your routine based on the season. Maybe in summer you'll want to take a quick ride on your bike, while in winter you'd prefer to do a few stretching exercises in the warm living room. It is important that the fixed framework is maintained: the same get-up time, the same order, the same end point.

## Small rituals with a big impact

Sometimes it's not the big building blocks that make a morning routine valuable, but rather small rituals that start the day on a friendly note:

- **Make the bed.** A task that can be completed in two minutes and creates an initial feeling of order.
- **A conscious first sip.** Don't drink the coffee or tea casually, but enjoy it for a moment by the window.
- **Three breaths before getting up.** A brief moment of calm before the day begins.
- **A look at the day.** Take a quick look at the three most important tasks so you can start with clarity. The guide [Plan your day realistically](/ratgeber/tag-realistisch-planen/) shows how to do this.
- **A kind word.** If you live with others, you can start the day with a quick conversation over breakfast instead of texting on your cell phone.

These rituals hardly take any time, but they noticeably change how the morning feels.

## For people who can't get going in the morning

Not everyone is a morning person, and that's okay. People have different internal clocks. Some people wake up straight away in the morning, others need an hour to really arrive. If you're the late type, don't schedule demanding tasks in the first hour after getting up, but keep your morning simple and mechanical. You can find out more about your own energy curve in [Divide energy throughout the day](/ratgeber/energie-einteilen/).

Anyone who wakes up feeling constantly exhausted despite getting enough sleep should have it checked by a doctor. You can find information about contact points on the [Help & Contact Points](/hilfe-und-anlaufstellen/) page.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to wake up early to have a good morning routine?

No. A morning routine works at any time. The important thing is that it fits your rhythm and your everyday life. Someone who doesn't get up until eight can have just as calm, reliable a routine as someone who gets up at five.

### How long should a morning routine be?

So short that you can do it even on hectic days. For many, ten to thirty minutes is realistic. A short routine that you do every day is more beneficial than a long one that you only do on good days.

### What do I do if my children decide the morning?

Then a routine for yourself is often only a few minutes long, and that's okay. Many parents get up five to ten minutes before the children to have a quiet coffee. It is also helpful to prepare a lot of things the evening before: clothes, lunch boxes, bags.

## Briefly summarized

A good morning routine is short, reliable and fits your life. It starts in the evening with a realistic bedtime and a prepared morning. Choose two to four building blocks, set a fixed order, initially keep the cell phone at a distance and plan a minimum version for difficult days. After two weeks you check what remains.
