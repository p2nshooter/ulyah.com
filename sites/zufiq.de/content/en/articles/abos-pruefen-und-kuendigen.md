You scroll through your bank statement and see lots of small debits. A streaming service you haven't opened since the last series. An app whose name you can't immediately identify. The cloud storage, the music service, the digital newspaper, the gym you last went to in the spring. Each amount on its own seems harmless. Together they add up to a sum that will surprise you once you calculate it.

Subscriptions are convenient, and that's exactly their purpose: you don't have to do anything to keep them running. This guide will show you how to find all current subscriptions in one afternoon, create a clear overview, honestly evaluate each position and cancel in a way that you can prove it.

## Why subscriptions are so easy to lose sight of

Subscriptions have a few features that make them inconspicuous. The amounts are small and are debited automatically. They go through different channels: direct debit from your checking account, credit card, payment service, app store or mobile phone bill. Annual subscriptions only appear once a year and are then quickly forgotten. And many start as a free trial that quietly turns into a paid subscription.

There is also a psychological effect: canceling a subscription feels like admitting that you have not implemented your good intentions. The language app, the gym, the cooking magazine. So it’s better to let it go, “just in case I start again.”

## Step 1: Find all current subscriptions

Allow one to two hours for the search and keep a list next to it. Write down everything that regularly costs money, even if you want to keep it.

### Bank statements from the last twelve months

A whole year is important so that you can also get annual and half-yearly subscriptions. In online banking you can often filter for direct debits or search for recurring recipients. Tag any recipient that appears more than once.

### Credit card and payment services

Many digital subscriptions are via credit card or an online payment service. Go through the statements there for twelve months as well. For some payment services, there is an overview of payment approvals granted to merchants in the account settings. There you can see who is allowed to make regular debits.

### Subscriptions in the App Store

Subscriptions that you have taken out via an app are usually not managed in the app itself, but in the app store account settings on your cell phone or tablet. There is a list of active and expired subscriptions. Check on all the devices you use to shop.

### Mail search

Search your inbox for terms like “subscription,” “subscription,” “membership,” “renewal,” “trial period,” “contract confirmation,” and “invoice.” You will also find subscriptions that are paid for via another account, such as your partner or an old credit card.

### Cell phone bill and paper folders

Check your cell phone bill for third-party items. And take a look at your contracts folder: memberships in clubs, paper magazines or a public transport subscription are often only there.

Typical candidates for your list:

- Streaming for films, series, music and audio books
- Newspapers and magazines, digital and printed
- Cloud storage, software and app subscriptions
- Gym, sports club, courses
- Delivery services, cooking boxes, shipping memberships to online shops
- Cell phone, internet, landline
- Memberships and regular donations
- Insurance, electricity and gas

## Step 2: An overview of monthly and annual costs

A list of names is not enough. Only when you see how much each subscription costs per year can you make a good decision. Create a table, on paper or on the computer, with these columns:

| Column | What belongs in it | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Subscription | Name of the service or association | clear assignment |
| Payment method | Account, credit card, payment service, app store, cell phone bill | shows where you cancel and where you control the debit |
| rhythm | monthly, quarterly, annually | Basis for conversion |
| Costs per month | Monthly amount or annual amount divided by twelve | make comparable |
| Costs per year | Monthly amount times twelve or annual amount | makes small amounts tangible |
| Next extension | Date according to contract or customer account | Basis for your calendar reminder |
| Last used | approximate date | honest basis for the evaluation |
| decision | keep, change, pause, cancel | your result |

At the end, sort the list by annual cost, with the most expensive at the top. Calculate the total. Many people experience a little shock when they see this number, and that is exactly what helps with the next step.

A note on security: Do not write passwords in this overview. However, customer numbers and the email address with which you are registered are useful because you need them to cancel.

## Step 3: Evaluate each position honestly

Go through the list line by line and ask yourself these questions about each subscription:

1. When was the last time I consciously used it?
2. Would I do it again today for the same price?
3. Do I use it because it gives me something or because I pay for it?
4. Is there something equivalent that I already have, such as a second service or what the city library offers?
5. Would I miss it if it was gone tomorrow?

The answers usually result in a quick decision:

| situation | decision |
|---|---|
| You use it regularly and would close it again in a heartbeat | keep |
| You use it, but only some of the functions | Switch to a smaller tariff, if one is available |
| You only use it at certain times, such as in winter | Cancel and re-sign if necessary, or pause if the provider offers this |
| Two services serve the same purpose | keep one, cancel the other |
| You keep it out of a guilty conscience | cancel, the intention will still remain with you |
| You don't know what the charge is for | clarify before you do anything else |

A switching principle helps many people with streaming services: only use one service at a time, and only when you have seen everything that interests you do you switch to the next one. [Avoid impulse purchases](/ratgeber/spontankaeufe-vermeiden/) explains how you can generally deal more calmly with the buying impulses that are behind some subscriptions.

## Step 4: Cancel verifiably

A termination is only completed when you can provide evidence of it. This is how you do it:

1. **Gather contract data.** Customer number, contract number, registered email address and payment method from your overview.
2. **Check the notice period and form.** Both are in the contract terms or in the customer account. If you were able to conclude a contract through a website, in many cases the provider must also allow you to easily cancel via a button on their website.
3. **Choose the appropriate method.** Cancellation button in the customer account, email or letter. For important contracts, a registered letter is one way to prove access.
4. **Formulate it clearly.** State your customer number, cancel as soon as possible and ask for confirmation with the exact end of the contract.
5. **Save confirmation.** Take a screenshot of the confirmation page, save the confirmation email as a PDF and put both in your contracts folder. [Filing system for papers](/ratgeber/ablagesystem-fuer-papiere/) shows how such a folder can be structured.
6. **Enter the end of the contract in the calendar.** After this date, check the next bank statement to make sure nothing more is being debited.
7. **Delete standing orders yourself.** If you pay by standing order, the provider does not stop anything. You have to do this yourself in your online banking.
8. **Follow up if nothing comes up.** If there is no confirmation, ask in writing. If debits continue despite cancellation, contact the consumer advice center and speak to your bank.

A short text for e-mail or letter is sufficient:

> I hereby terminate my contract with the customer number... at the earliest possible date. Please confirm receipt of the cancellation and the exact end of the contract.

Many providers contact you after a termination with a return offer. Decide on this based on your evaluation from step 3, not at the moment of the offer. If you accept it, note the new term and the price after the offer expires in your overview.

Once a subscription has ended, you often no longer need the associated customer account. [Clear out passwords and accounts](/ratgeber/passwoerter-und-konten-ausmisten/) explains how to delete old accounts and remove your data there.

## Keep an eye on trial subscriptions and automatic renewals

Free trial periods are handy for trying something out. However, they often automatically convert to a paid subscription if you do not cancel in time. Three habits protect you:

- **Immediately set a reminder.** As soon as you sign up, you put an appointment in your calendar a few days before the end of the test period: “Trial subscription X: keep or cancel?”
- **Cancel early where possible.** With many services you can cancel immediately after completing the trial and still use the trial period until the end. Whether this is the case with your provider is stated in the conditions.
- **Note renewal dates.** For annual subscriptions, enter the renewal date a little in advance so that you can decide in peace instead of noticing the debit until afterwards.

The deadlines for extension and termination depend on the contract and when it was concluded. The current rules for consumers are explained by the consumer advice center in your federal state.

## Insurance, electricity and gas: it's better to get advice

Not every ongoing contract is a subscription that you simply end. When it comes to insurance, canceling it can mean that you suddenly find yourself without important protection, and it is not always possible to take out a new policy at a later date under the same conditions. For electricity and gas, you should clarify before you cancel how the supply will continue afterwards.

So include these contracts in your overview to see the costs, but don't decide on them at the kitchen table. The consumer advice centers offer independent advice, including on energy issues. Only cancel an insurance contract once it is clear whether and how the protection is secured elsewhere.

## The annual subscription check

Once a year you repeat the review in brief form. Choose a fixed date, such as the beginning of the year or the weeks in which you are organizing your documents anyway.

1. Review bank and credit card statements from the last twelve months for new recurring items.
2. Open the subscription list in the App Store.
3. Update the overview: add new subscriptions, delete existing ones, enter changed prices.
4. Check each position with the five questions from Step 3.
5. Complete cancellations immediately and submit confirmations.

With a well-maintained overview, this is much quicker than the first round.

## Typical mistakes and how to avoid them

- **Delete the app instead of canceling it:** A subscription that runs through the App Store does not end when you remove the app from your cell phone. Cancel it in the store's subscription settings.
- **Return the direct debit instead of canceling:** This does not end the contract. The claim remains and reminders may follow. First cancel, then speak to the bank if there are unauthorized debits.
- **Cancellation without proof:** Without confirmation, it will be difficult for you to prove in the event of a dispute that and when you canceled.
- **Cancel everything at once:** If you cancel things that you like to use in a hurry, you will re-sign them a few weeks later. Rate first, then cancel.
- **Never look at the overview again:** Without an annual check, new subscriptions accumulate just as unnoticed as before.

## Frequently asked questions

### What do I do with a debit that I can't assign?

First, search your mailbox for the recipient's name. This is often a payment service provider who debits money for a well-known service. If you don't find anything, talk to your bank quickly. You can find further contact points, including for third-party items on your mobile phone bill, under [Help & Contact Points](/hilfe-und-anlaufstellen/).

### Is an annual subscription worth it instead of a monthly subscription?

An annual subscription is often cheaper per month, but ties you in for longer. It is especially worthwhile for services that you have been using regularly for a long time. For anything you're just trying out, the flexible option is usually the better choice.

## Briefly summarized

Search for all subscriptions in bank statements, credit card statements, in the app store, in the mailbox and on your cell phone bill, always for a whole year. Put them in an overview with costs per month and per year and evaluate each item with a few honest questions. Cancel verifiably, secure the confirmation and then check the account statement. Set a calendar reminder immediately for trial subscriptions and extensions, get advice on insurance and energy contracts and repeat the check once a year.
