Anyone who reads a guide about habits and order is looking for guidance, not more pressure. So that you can trust our texts, we adhere to these principles.

## Written specifically, not composed

Every text on ZUFIQ is written specifically for this website. We do not copy content from other websites, and when we rely on common knowledge, we state it in our own words. When structuring and formulating, we also use digital writing tools, including AI-supported aids. This doesn't change the rules on this page: They apply to every sentence, regardless of how it was created.

## No made-up numbers and experts

There are a lot of nice numbers floating around in the area of self-organization: how many days it supposedly takes to form a habit, how many things a household has, how many hours we spend on our cell phones. Many of them are inaccurate or unsubstantiated. We do not provide statistics, studies, or expert quotes that we cannot verify, and we do not invent testimonials. Where there is no reliable number, we say so.

## Where our knowledge comes from

- generally accepted knowledge about habits, motivation and sleep hygiene as described in textbooks and information from health organizations
- Information from consumer advice centers, authorities and advice centers, such as contracts, subscriptions and storing documents
- Proven everyday knowledge, always with the question in mind: What works in a small apartment, with children, in shift work, with little time?

## What every guide should do

Before a text appears, it must be able to answer these questions:

1. **Who is it for?** Every guide begins with a situation that really exists in everyday life, such as the overflowing stack of papers in the hallway or the cell phone that won't leave your hand in the evening.
2. **What can you do today?** Good advice doesn't end with theory. He names a first, small step that can also be done in a full day.
3. **Where are the limits?** He says openly when a suggestion isn't right and when it's better to ask experts.
4. **What is changing?** Where rules, deadlines, prices or settings in apps may change, we will say so and ask you to check the current information with the responsible authority or the provider.

## Calm instead of perfection

We don't show immaculate apartments or routines that only work without a job, children and fatigue. Our suggestions should fit into real life. We write without shame and without blame, even about messes, relapses into old habits and days when nothing works.

## Clear boundaries when it comes to health and money

We do not provide medical or psychotherapeutic advice or investment, tax or legal advice. Texts that touch on sleep, exhaustion, stress or money clearly state when professionals are needed and link to our [Help & Contact Points](/hilfe-und-anlaufstellen/) page. We don't promise quick transformations, wealth or happiness at the push of a button.

## independence

ZUFIQ is financed through advertisements that Google automatically delivers. We do not choose the ads and they have no influence on our texts. We do not take any money for articles, product tests or recommendations and do not name any brands as purchase recommendations in our guides.

## Sustainable, but without dogma

We encourage you to use things for longer, pass them on and buy consciously. But we don't judge anyone based on how much they own. For us, minimalism is a tool, not a competition.

## Updates and corrections

Each guide has a date. We revise texts if recommendations or rules change or if readers point out errors to us. We correct any factual errors quickly. You can send us information via the [Contact](/kontakt/) page.

## Language

We write in clear, friendly language and use the first-name terms of our readers. We explain technical terms when we use them. Every text should be easy to read even on a tired evening.
