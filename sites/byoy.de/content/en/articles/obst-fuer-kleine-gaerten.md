Many people believe that you need a large meadow with old high trunks to grow your own fruit. Apples, pears, cherries and berries can also be grown on a terrace, on a house wall or in a narrow terraced house garden. The key is the right tree shape and support: cultivated columnar trees, trellises pulled on wires and weakly growing spindles require hardly any more space than a shrub and often bear fruit in their second or third year. This guide presents the options and helps you find the right solution for your garden.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Columnar fruit grows narrowly upwards and only needs around 50 to 80 centimeters of space. It is suitable for beds and large containers.
> - Espaliered fruit on a sunny house wall uses the warmth of the wall and only needs a depth of 30 to 40 centimeters.
> - Spindle trees on weak rootstocks grow two to three meters tall and bear early.
> - A multivarietal tree bears several varieties on one trunk and solves the problem of fertilization.
> - Berry bushes as stems and self-fertile varieties produce a lot of yield in a small area.

## Columnar fruit

Columnar apples are special varieties that form almost no side branches. The fruits grow directly on the trunk on short fruit skewers. This creates a narrow, slender column that fits into every garden path and into every large pot. There are also columnar varieties of pears, cherries and plums, although these are usually kept in shape by cutting.

**Advantages:** very little space required, early yield, hardly any cutting.
**Disadvantages:** Lower yield per tree, some varieties tend to have variable yields, and columnar apples also need a pollinator variety, such as a second columnar.

Plant columns 60 to 80 centimeters apart, for example in a loose row along a path. They need at least 30 to 40 liters of soil in the bucket.

## espalier fruit

Espaliers are the classic solution for house walls and walls. The tree grows flat on a framework of wires or slats, with horizontal or diagonally drawn branches. A south wall stores heat during the day and releases it at night. This means that pears, apricots and peaches, which often do not produce reliably in the outdoor garden, even ripen there.

This is how you create a trellis:

1. Stretch three to four horizontal wires on the wall, 40 to 50 centimeters apart, with about ten centimeters from the wall to allow air to circulate.
2. Plant the tree about 20 to 30 centimeters in front of the wall, where the ground is not too dry due to the foundation and eaves.
3. On each floor, pull two side branches to the left and right along the wires and tie them loosely.
4. In summer, cut back all shoots growing forwards and backwards to just a few leaves. This creates short fruit shoots along the branches.

Espaliers need regular summer pruning. Anyone who likes it will be rewarded with a decorative and very productive tree. The basics of pruning explained [Fruit Tree Pruning](/gartenwissen/obstbaumschnitt/).

## spindle trees

A spindle is a slender tree with a continuous central shoot and short, horizontal side branches that become shorter from bottom to top, like a Christmas tree. On weakly growing rootstocks it remains two to three meters high and can be harvested from the ground. Spindles bear early and regularly. You need a permanent stake because the weak roots alone cannot hold the tree up. How to plant such a tree is described in [Planting a Fruit Tree](/gartenwissen/obstbaum-pflanzen/).

## Multi-variety tree

On a multi-variety tree, also known as a family tree, two to four different varieties of the same type of fruit are grafted, such as an early, a medium-early and a late apple. The varieties cross-pollinate and you harvest over a longer period of time. This is ideal if there is only room for one tree. When cutting, make sure that no variety overgrows the others. You should cut back vigorously growing varieties more vigorously.

## Berries in little space

Berry bushes produce the highest yield in the smallest area:

- **Currants and gooseberries as stems:** When grafted onto a short stem, they grow like small trees. There is space underneath for strawberries or herbs. More in [Currants and Gooseberries](/gartenwissen/johannisbeeren-und-stachelbeeren/).
- **Raspberries on wires:** A row on a wire frame only needs half a meter wide. Autumn raspberries are particularly easy to care for, as described in [Growing raspberries](/gartenwissen/himbeeren-anbauen/).
- **Blueberries in the pot:** Cultivated blueberries need acidic soil, which you can easily provide in the pot, see [Blueberries in the garden](/gartenwissen/heidelbeeren-im-garten/).
- **Strawberries:** in beds, hanging baskets or raised beds. How to plant them is described in [Planting Strawberries](/gartenwissen/erdbeeren-pflanzen/).
- **Mini kiwi:** The hardy kiwi berry grows as a climbing plant on pergola or fence and bears small, smooth-skinned fruits. For the yield you usually need a female and a male plant.

## Fruit in the bucket

Columnar fruit, dwarf trees, blueberries and figs grow in the pot. The important things are:

- a large bucket with drainage holes, at least 40 liters for trees,
- structurally stable pot plant soil, possibly mixed with a little compost,
- regular watering, often daily in summer,
- organic fertilizer from spring to July,
- Winter protection for the pot: The roots in the pot freeze faster than in the ground. Place the bucket on a protected wall of the house and wrap it with jute or fleece.

## Legal: Distance from neighbors

The neighboring laws of the federal states prescribe minimum distances for trees and bushes from the property line, depending on the type and height. Espaliers on your own house wall and columnar fruit are usually unproblematic, spindles and multi-variety trees on the border can be. Check your state's rules before planting.

## An example of a terraced house garden

A surprising amount of fruit can be accommodated in an area of around ten by six meters without the garden appearing overgrown:

- On the sunny house wall there is a pear trellis across the entire width,
- along the path there are three columnar apples with two different varieties for cross-fertilization,
- a row of autumn raspberries on wires at the property boundary,
- two currant stems with strawberries underneath,
- Blueberries in acidic soil in two large containers on the terrace.

Together, this results in fresh fruit from June to October, and the lawn in the middle remains free for play.

## Wild fruit and special fruits

In addition to apples, pears and cherries, there are a number of fruit trees that take up little space, are robust and also shine as ornamental plants. The rock pear produces sweet, dark berries in early summer and turns bright red in autumn. The cornelian cherry is one of the first bushes of the year to bloom yellow and produces tart red fruits for jam in late summer.

The Mayberry, also known as the Siberian blueberry, ripens in early summer as one of the first berries of the year. Aronia produces tart, dark berries that are good for juice and jam. And the frost-hardy mini kiwi grows on pergolas and fences; However, with most varieties it needs a male plant nearby so that the females bear fruit.

## Fruit for partially shaded places

Not every small garden offers full sun. Currants, gooseberries, raspberries and blackberries still produce well even in partial shade if they get a few hours of sun a day. Sour cherries are considered comparatively shade-tolerant and are even suitable for walls that do not face south. Apples, pears, sweet cherries and peaches, on the other hand, need a lot of sun to produce sweet fruits.

## Pollination in the small garden

Many types of fruit need a second, suitable variety as a pollinator. In densely built-up residential areas, such partner trees are often already in neighboring gardens, because bees fly long distances in search of food. Crabapples are good pollinators for many apple varieties and at the same time make pretty flowering trees. If you want to be on the safe side, choose a multi-variety tree or self-fertile varieties.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which types of fruit are self-fertile?

Many sour cherries, peaches, most damsons and plums as well as currants, gooseberries and raspberries produce no second variety. Apples, pears and many sweet cherries need a pollinator variety.

### Does columnar fruit really bear fruit?

Yes, often in the second year. The yield per tree is smaller than that of a large tree, but is surprisingly high based on the area.

### How much sun does fruit need on the balcony?

At least six hours. Currants and wild strawberries are more likely to thrive on north-facing balconies than apples.

### Which fruit trees need little pruning?

Quinces and medlars require little pruning, as do many wild fruit bushes such as serviceberry and cornelian cherry. Columnar fruit and spindle trees, on the other hand, need regular, albeit small, pruning so that they remain slim and fruitful.

### How many fruit trees can fit in a small garden?

More than many people think. There is space for one or two espalier trees on a few square meters of wall space, a row of columnar apples along a path, and a few berry bushes and strawberries in the bed. It is important not to plant the trees too closely and to give everyone enough sun.

### Can I also grow fruit trees in a container on the terrace?

Yes, columnar fruit, dwarf fruit and berry bushes in particular thrive in large containers. They need regular water and fertilizer and protection for the root ball in winter.

A large container with good drainage is more important than a special variety.

A bright, sheltered spot on the wall of the house is ideal.
