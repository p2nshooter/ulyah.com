If there is rustling, smacking and snorting in the bed on a warm summer night, there is often a hedgehog on the move. Hedgehogs are one of the most beloved garden creatures, and they are useful: they eat beetles, larvae, caterpillars, millipedes and also some snails. But many hedgehogs are not doing well. Tidy gardens without hiding places, thick fences, roads, robotic lawnmowers and a lack of insects make life difficult for them, and experts are observing a decline in populations in many regions. With little effort you can make your garden a safe area. This guide shows what hedgehogs need, what dangers you should avoid and when a hedgehog actually needs help.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Hedgehogs need hiding places: piles of leaves and brushwood, wild corners, dense hedges or a hedgehog house.
> - An opening of approximately 13 × 13 centimeters in the fence connects gardens. Hedgehogs travel long distances at night.
> - Only run the robotic lawnmower during the day, never at dusk or at night. Check before mowing high areas.
> - No snail pellet poisons, no open shafts, ponds with shallow exits, set nets away from the ground.
> - A hedgehog that runs around during the day in autumn, is emaciated or injured needs help from a hedgehog station or veterinary practice.

## How hedgehogs live

Hedgehogs are active at dusk and at night. During the day they sleep in nests made of leaves and grass under hedges, wood piles or in dense bushes. At night they roam in search of food, often covering a kilometer or more through many gardens. They mainly eat insects and their larvae, earthworms, millipedes and spiders. Snails are also on their menu, but only make up a smaller part. A hedgehog does not replace snail management, but supports it. More about this in [Nudibranchs without poison](/gartenwissen/schnecken-ohne-gift/).

Hedgehogs hibernate from around November until March or April, depending on the weather. To do this, they need a dry, frost-protected nest and enough fat reserves, which they consume in late summer and autumn.

## What hedgehogs need in the garden

### Hiding places and winter quarters

- **Pile of leaves:** A large pile of leaves in a quiet corner, preferably under a hedge or weighted with branches, is the ideal winter quarters. How to use leaves in the garden sensibly can be found in [Use leaves in the garden](/gartenwissen/laub-im-garten-nutzen/).
- **Piles of brushwood and dead wood:** Cut branches loosely piled up provide protection and food because many insects live in them.
- **Hedges made from native shrubs:** Hawthorn, hazel, blackthorn and elderberry provide cover and insect food.
- **Hedgehog house:** A wooden box with an entrance tunnel, filled with dry leaves and straw, in a shady, quiet place. The tunnel keeps cats and foxes away.

### Wilder corners

A garden where every square meter is mowed, raked and tidied up offers hedgehogs neither food nor shelter. Let a corner grow wild, with tall grass, nettles and leaves. Caterpillars, beetles and other insects live there, on which hedgehogs and many birds live. A flowering strip also helps, as described in [Creating a flowering meadow](/gartenwissen/bluehwiese-anlegen/).

### passages

Hedgehogs need lots of gardens to find enough food and mates. Dense fences and walls cut up their territory. An opening of around 13 × 13 centimeters on the ground in the fence or under the garden gate is sufficient. Talk to your neighbors to create a whole chain of connected gardens.

### water

In hot, dry summers, hedgehogs often cannot find water. A shallow bowl of fresh water, changed daily, helps them. Never give milk to hedgehogs: they cannot tolerate lactose and get diarrhea from it.

## Dangers in the garden

| Danger | What helps |
|---|---|
| Robotic lawnmower | Only operate during the day, never at dusk or at night, avoid areas with tall grass |
| Brush cutters and brush cutters | Before mowing embankments and tall grass, check with a stick |
| Leaf vacuums and leaf blowers | Use a rake and do not dig through piles of leaves |
| Garden ponds and pools | Flat bank area or a board to help you get out, cover pools |
| Light and cellar shafts | Cover with a close-meshed grille or insert an exit ramp |
| networks | Do not stretch berry nets and ball nets all the way to the ground; tie up football goals at night |
| Slug pellets and other poisons | Do not use in organic gardens |
| Fire in the pile of brushwood | Shift the heap before lighting it; it is better not to burn it at all |
| Holes, cans, plastic waste | Put it away, hedgehogs get stuck in it |

**Robot mowers** that run at night are particularly dangerous. When threatened, hedgehogs curl up instead of fleeing and are seriously injured. Many hedgehog shelters report such injuries. If you use a robotic lawnmower, program it exclusively for the bright hours of the day.

## Feeding: only in exceptions

A healthy hedgehog finds enough food in a natural garden. Feeding can actually be harmful if it concentrates hedgehogs in one place, spreads disease, or gives them the wrong food. In late autumn, supplementary feeding can be useful for small young hedgehogs that do not yet have enough weight before hibernation. Then give unseasoned wet cat food with a high meat content or special hedgehog food, plus water. Fruit, vegetables, bread, milk and leftover food are unsuitable.

## When does a hedgehog need help?

Hedgehogs are particularly protected under the Federal Nature Conservation Act. You can't take them from nature without a reason. However, you can temporarily take in hedgehogs in need of help to nurse them back to health and then release them again. Need help:

- Hedgehogs running around, staggering or lying in the sun during the day, especially in autumn,
- injured hedgehogs, for example after an accident with mowing equipment,
- emaciated hedgehogs with sunken flanks,
- orphaned hedgehog babies whose mother is proven to be dead,
- Hedgehogs that travel in frost and snow.

Place the hedgehog in a tall cardboard box with a towel, place it in a quiet, not too warm room and offer water. Then call a hedgehog sanctuary, a wildlife rescue center or a veterinary practice. Hedgehogs need expert care, and many diseases and parasites can only be recognized by those who are familiar with them. Leave hedgehogs that behave normally at night alone.

## A hedgehog-friendly garden in five steps

1. **Create a quiet zone:** A corner under hedges or bushes that is not tidied up, with leaves and branches.
2. **Open passage:** A small opening in the fence, coordinated with the neighbors.
3. **Eliminate dangers:** Cover shafts, pond with exit, tie up nets, robotic lawnmower only during the day.
4. **Promote food:** native shrubs, flower strips, dead wood and no poison. Where insects live, hedgehogs also find food.
5. **Provide water:** a shallow bowl in dry summers.

Anyone who implements these points not only helps hedgehogs, but also birds, toads, lizards and countless insects.

## Hedgehogs in winter: do not disturb

If a hedgehog hibernates in a pile of leaves or in a hedgehog house, it needs absolute peace. Every awakening costs him valuable energy reserves. Therefore, do not clear up piles of leaves, brushwood and compost between November and April, do not put compost in which a hedgehog could sleep, and do not check a hedgehog house. Only in late spring, when the nights are consistently mild, is it time for clean-up work, and even then carefully, with a rake instead of a fork.

## Danger from robotic lawnmowers

Robotic lawn mowers pose a particular danger to hedgehogs, especially when they drive at dusk or at night. Hedgehogs do not run away when threatened, but instead curl up. Many robotic lawnmowers do not recognize them as an obstacle, and research has shown that the devices can seriously injure hedgehogs, especially young animals.

Therefore, only operate a robotic lawnmower during the day and never at dusk or at night when hedgehogs are out and about. Before you start, briefly check hedges, edges and tall grass where hedgehogs hide during the day. Even with brush cutters and brush cutters, it is worth taking a close look at the undergrowth before mowing embankments and hedges.

## Hedgehogs in spring

When the hedgehogs wake up from hibernation in March or April, they are extremely emaciated and need to find food quickly. A natural garden with leaves, perennials and open areas in the ground then offers beetles, larvae and earthworms. Therefore, do not clear away piles of leaves and brushwood too early in the year, as there may still be sleeping hedgehogs in them.

A shallow bowl of fresh water helps the animals, especially in dry springs. Milk, on the other hand, is unsuitable because hedgehogs cannot tolerate it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a hedgehog in the garden a sign of a healthy garden?

Mostly yes. Hedgehogs can only find enough food where there are lots of insects and hiding places. If hedgehogs stay, it's a compliment to your natural garden.

### Can I build a hedgehog house myself?

Yes. A wooden box measuring around 30 × 40 centimeters with an entrance tunnel ten to twelve centimeters wide, without a bottom or with air holes, filled with dry leaves. Cover it with branches and leaves and place it in a quiet, shady place.

### Do hedgehogs drive away beneficial insects?

Hedgehogs also eat some ground beetles and earthworms, which we value as beneficial insects. This is kept in balance in a diverse garden. More about the helpers in the garden can be found in [Beneficial insects in the garden](/gartenwissen/nuetzlinge-im-garten/).

### Can I just take a hedgehog with me?

Hedgehogs are under special protection and are generally not allowed to be taken from the wild. Sick, injured or helpless animals can be taken in temporarily to be nursed back to health, but they must then be released again. In such a case, it is best to contact a hedgehog sanctuary, a wildlife rescue center or a veterinary practice, as proper care requires experience.

### What do I do if I see a hedgehog during the day?

Hedgehogs are nocturnal. A hedgehog that staggers around during the day, lies open in the sun, or appears very thin is often sick or weak. Observe him briefly closely, if he is in danger, put him in a tall box with a towel and contact a hedgehog station or veterinary practice. Young hedgehogs, which weigh well under 500 grams in late autumn, often cannot manage to hibernate on their own and need expert help.
