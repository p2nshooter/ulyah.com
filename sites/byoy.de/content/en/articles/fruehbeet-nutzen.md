A cold frame is the small greenhouse of the vegetable garden. A simple box with a clear lid captures the warmth of the spring sun, protects against wind, rain and light night frosts, and extends the gardening season by several weeks at either end. Radishes and lettuce grow in it as early as March, while there is still frost outside. In May you grow young plants for the bed, and in autumn and winter the cold frame provides fresh lamb's lettuce and winter purslane. If you garden without having space for a greenhouse, you can get a lot of harvest in a small space with a cold frame.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Place the cold frame in a sunny, wind-protected spot with the low side facing south.
> - Lettuce, radishes, spinach, kohlrabi and young plants grow in it from February to April, and lamb's lettuce and other winter salads grow in autumn and winter.
> - Ventilation is the most important work: on sunny days it quickly becomes too hot in a closed cold frame. From around 20 to 25 degrees open the lid.
> - On frosty nights, additional cover with fleece, mats or old blankets helps.
> - A manure bed with fresh horse manure underground generates natural heat for weeks.

## Types of construction

| Construction type | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Wooden box with glass windows | good insulation, durable, classic | heavy, glass fragile |
| Box with hollow polycarbonate panels | light, well insulating, shatterproof | Plates become cloudy over the years |
| Attachment for raised beds | Back-friendly, uses the warmth of the raised bed filling | only as big as the raised bed |
| Polytunnel over arches | inexpensive, flexible to relocate | less insulating, foil only lasts a few years |
| manure bed | natural warmth from below | needs fresh horse manure, more work |

A home-made cold frame made from untreated boards and old windows is a nice, inexpensive solution. Make sure that the lid closes tightly and can be easily locked so that it does not slam shut in the wind.

## The right location

- **Sun:** The cold frame needs as much light as possible, especially in spring and winter when the sun is low.
- **Orientation:** The lid is slanted and slopes towards the south. The high side is in the north.
- **Windbreak:** A hedge, wall or fence a few meters away protects against cold north and east winds without casting shadows.
- **Water:** A nearby watering hole makes frequent watering easier.
- **Soil:** The cold frame stands directly on good, humus-rich garden soil. Before using for the first time, incorporate plenty of mature compost.

How to create a raised bed with a cold frame attachment can be found in [Creating a raised bed](/gartenwissen/hochbeet-anlegen/).

## The cold frame throughout the year

| period | What you sow or plant | What you harvest |
|---|---|---|
| February to March | Lettuce, radishes, spinach, cress, rocket, early kohlrabi | Lamb's lettuce and winter purslane from last year |
| April | Grow lettuce, kohlrabi, leek and cabbage seedlings, harden off young plants | Radishes, lettuce, spinach |
| May to August | Cucumbers, peppers or melons in an open cold frame, or growing autumn vegetables | Salad, kohlrabi |
| September | Lamb's lettuce, winter purslane, Asian salads, spinach for the winter | Summer vegetables |
| October to December | little new, winter salads continue to grow slowly | Lamb's lettuce, Asian salads, winter purslane |

The cold frame is particularly valuable in spring as an intermediate station for young plants. Tomatoes, cabbage and lettuce that have been grown on the windowsill slowly get used to the sun, wind and cooler nights, as described in [Hardening off and planting out young plants](/gartenwissen/jungpflanzen-abhaerten-und-auspflanzen/).

## Ventilation: the most important rule

A closed cold frame heats up extremely on sunny days, even if it is only five degrees outside. It can reach 30 degrees or more within an hour. Seedlings and young plants then burn or grow soft and vulnerable.

- On sunny days, open the lid slightly in the morning and open it further from around 20 to 25 degrees inside.
- A simple thermometer in the cold frame shows you the temperature at a glance.
- Close the lid again in the afternoon so that the stored heat lasts through the night.
- **Automatic window openers** work with a wax cylinder that expands when heated and lifts the lid. They're worth it if you're not at home during the day.

Good ventilation also prevents fungal diseases such as gray mold, which spread quickly in moist, stagnant air. More about this in [Preventing fungal diseases](/gartenwissen/pilzkrankheiten-vorbeugen/).

## Pouring

It doesn't rain in the cold frame. Check the soil regularly and water in the morning so that the leaves dry by evening. If possible, use stale, not ice-cold water from the rain barrel. In winter, the slow-growing lettuces rarely need water.

## Frost protection

A cold frame keeps out light night frosts, but it also gets cold in severe frosts. On cold nights:

- also place a fleece directly over the plants,
- cover the lid with mats made of reeds, straw, old blankets or bubble wrap,
- pour leaves around the outside walls to insulate them,
- take the cover off again in the morning to let in light.

Lamb's lettuce and winter purslane tolerate frost well and recover after thawing. Only harvest them when the leaves are not frozen.

## Create a manure bed

Cold frames used to be heated with fresh horse manure. During decomposition, heat is created over several weeks, which rises into the earth from below. This is how radishes and lettuce turn out in February.

1. Dig the soil about 50 to 60 centimeters deep in the cold frame.
2. Fill a layer of 30 to 40 centimeters of fresh horse manure with straw and tamp it down.
3. Pour the manure if it is dry and close the lid. After a few days it starts to get warm.
4. As soon as the first heat has subsided, usually after a week, add 15 to 20 centimeters of good soil with compost on top.
5. Sow and plant when the soil is lukewarm.

In the fall, the manure has become valuable humus, which you spread around the garden. [Organically fertilize](/gartenwissen/organisch-duengen/) explains how you can use manure as fertilizer.

## Snails in the cold frame

The protected, moist air attracts snails. Check the cold frame in the evening and morning, especially around the edges, and collect snails. A tightly fitting frame and a screw plate on the outer edge help. More about this in [Nudibranchs without poison](/gartenwissen/schnecken-ohne-gift/).

## Build your own cold frame

You can build a simple cold frame in one afternoon:

1. Saw a box out of untreated boards with a back wall about 40 centimeters high and a front wall about 25 centimeters high. You cut the side parts diagonally.
2. Old wooden windows, double-wall panels or a film on a frame are suitable as a cover.
3. Attach the lid to the back wall with hinges and build a support with multiple levels so you can keep it open at different heights.
4. Place the box on the prepared ground, with the low side facing south.

It is important that old windows do not have lead-based paint that is flaking and that the glass is firmly seated in the frame.

## Which works particularly well in the cold frame

In early spring, the cold frame is ideal for quick, cold-tolerant crops: radishes, lettuce, rocket, spinach, kohlrabi and spring onions. They are often already harvested when the first seeds are sprouting outside. At the same time, the cold frame is a good place to slowly accustom young plants grown on the windowsill to the sun, wind and cooler nights.

In autumn and winter, lamb's lettuce, winter purslane, Asian salads and winter salads grow there, which can still be harvested under glass or foil even in frost. In cool regions, some gardeners use the cold frame in summer for heat-loving plants such as peppers or melons, which are more protected there than in the open bed.

## Keep an eye on the temperature

Even on sunny March days, the temperature under glass or foil rises well above thirty degrees within a short time, even if it is cool outside. Young plants then burn or become soft and vulnerable. A minimum-maximum thermometer in the cold frame shows you how warm it gets during the day and how cold it was at night.

If you are not at home during the day, you can use automatic window openers. They work without electricity with a cylinder whose filling expands when heated and lifts the lid. When it gets cold it closes again. This means the cold frame is ventilated even when no one thinks about it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How big should a cold frame be?

For starters, 1.2 × 0.8 meters is enough. It is important that you reach the center comfortably. You can divide larger cold frames into several windows.

### Can I grow tomatoes in the cold frame?

As young plants in April and May, yes. A cold frame is too low for fully grown tomatoes. In summer it is more suitable for cucumbers, peppers or melons if the lid remains open.

### What do I do with the cold frame in summer?

Remove the lid or set it wide open and use the area like a normal bed. In late summer you sow the winter lettuces, which continue to grow under the cover in autumn.

### Can I also use a cold frame on the balcony?

Yes, small cold frames and growing boxes with lids also fit on balconies and terraces. Place them in a bright, wind-protected place and secure them against storms, because light boxes offer a lot of surface for the wind to attack. Ventilation is just as important on the balcony as in the garden.

### Do I have to cover the cold frame in winter?

In mild winters the lid is sufficient. In severe frost, an additional layer of fleece over the plants, straw mats or bubble wrap on the lid and a border made of leaves help. Nevertheless, ventilate briefly on sunny days so that moisture does not build up and fungal diseases arise.

### How often do I have to replace the soil in the cold frame?

Replace the top layer of soil every year or so or incorporate mature compost. The cold frame is used for intensive cultivation, and over time pests accumulate there.
