For many gardeners, a raised bed is the gateway to growing their own vegetables. It's easy on your back because you sow, plant and harvest while standing. It warms up faster than the garden soil in spring, so lettuce and radishes grow earlier. Snails have a harder time, voles can't get in with a grid, and vegetables can also be grown on poor soil, on clay or even on paved surfaces. In order for a raised bed to produce good harvests for many years, it depends on the location, the material and, above all, the filling.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - A height of 80 to 90 centimeters, a maximum width of 1.20 meters and a length of around two meters are ideal. This way you can reach the middle from both sides.
> - Place the raised bed in a sunny location, if possible with the long side facing north-south.
> - A close-meshed wire mesh on the ground keeps voles away. A bubble wrap on the interior walls protects wood from rot.
> - Classic filling from bottom to top: branches and twigs, turned over sod, leaves and coarse compost, mature compost, garden soil.
> - In the first year, you plant heavy feeders such as tomatoes, zucchini and cabbage because the filling is very nutrient-rich.

## The right location

Vegetables need sun. Choose a spot with at least six hours of direct sun per day. If possible, align the raised bed so that the long sides face east and west, then both sides get light. Also pay attention to:

- a water connection or a rain barrel nearby, because raised beds dry out more quickly,
- enough space all around, at least 50 centimeters, better one meter for a wheelbarrow and watering can,
- a level surface so that the walls do not warp,
- Distance to large trees whose roots conquer the bed from below.

## Material and construction method

| material | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Wood (larch, Douglas fir, robinia) | of course, good insulation, easy to build yourself | Depending on the wood, it lasts about 5 to 15 years |
| Metal (galvanized steel, aluminum) | very durable, slim, modern | heats up strongly in the blazing sun, more expensive |
| Stone or gabions | almost indestructible, stores heat | complex, heavy, expensive |
| Plastic or recycled material | lightweight, weatherproof | less natural, quality very different |

When it comes to wood, you use untreated, naturally resistant wood. Wood with chemical wood protection or old railway sleepers do not belong in a vegetable patch. Line the inside with a bubble wrap, the knobs towards the wooden wall. This keeps the wood dry and ventilated. You leave the soil free so that water can drain away and soil life can migrate.

### Dimensions

- **Height:** 80 to 90 centimeters is comfortable for most people. If you garden in a wheelchair, you need a lower, wheelchair-accessible table bed.
- **Width:** a maximum of 1.20 meters, for beds that are accessible from one side on a wall, a maximum of 70 to 80 centimeters.
- **Length:** any, but wooden walls over two meters long need a cross brace in the middle to prevent them from bending outwards.

## Vole protection

Before filling, place a close-meshed, galvanized wire mesh with a mesh width of around 13 millimeters on the floor and run it up the walls a little. Plastic nets are bitten through by voles. Once a vole nest has nested under the raised bed, it is difficult to get rid of, so this precaution is worth it.

## The filling: layer by layer

The classic raised bed works like a lying compost heap. The lower layers slowly decompose, releasing nutrients and some heat and providing drainage. For a bed 90 centimeters high, this sequence has proven itself:

| layer (from below) | material | thickness |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coarse branches, twigs, shrub cuttings, shredded material | 30 to 40 cm |
| 2 | turned over sod or coarse green material | 10 to 15 cm |
| 3 | Leaves, half-rotted compost, possibly well-seasoned manure | 20 to 25 cm |
| 4 | mature compost | 10 to 15 cm |
| 5 | good garden soil or raised bed soil mixed with compost | 15 to 20 cm |

Lightly press and water each layer to prevent large voids from forming. Only use untreated, healthy material: no diseased plant residues, no seeding weeds, no coniferous wood in large quantities and no walnut or oak leaves in thick layers because they contain tannins and rot slowly. [Using leaves in the garden](/gartenwissen/laub-im-garten-nutzen/) explains where good leaves come from.

If you don't have room for garden waste, you can also fill a raised bed with purchased raised bed soil and compost. Then you need significantly more soil and the heating effect is eliminated, but the bed is ready for use straight away.

## Bagging and refilling

The filling decreases significantly in the first few years, often by 10 to 20 centimeters per year. Top up with ripe compost and soil every spring. After five to seven years the material has turned into soil. Then you can clear out the bed and re-layer it or continue it as a normal bed with good soil.

## Planting after years

The fresh stuffing is very rich in nutrients, especially nitrogen. That's why the planting changes over the years:

- **First year:** Heavy feeders such as tomatoes, zucchini, cucumbers, cabbage, celery and leeks. Leafy vegetables such as spinach and lettuce can store increased amounts of nitrate when the nitrogen supply is very high. Only plant it in small quantities in the first year.
- **Second year:** Medium feeders such as carrots, kohlrabi, fennel, chard, beetroot, lettuce and onions.
- **Third year and beyond:** Light eaters such as radishes, peas, beans, lamb's lettuce and herbs until you refill.

[Heavy eaters, medium eaters, weak eaters](/gartenwissen/starkzehrer-mittelzehrer-schwachzehrer/) explain what differentiates between heavy, medium and weak eaters. With mixed culture you make particularly good use of the space, see [Mixed culture: good and bad neighbors](/gartenwissen/mischkultur-gute-und-schlechte-nachbarn/).

## Pouring

Raised beds dry out more quickly than ground beds because they get air and sun from all sides. In summer, water thoroughly in the morning, preferably on the plants, not over the leaves. A mulch layer of grass clippings keeps moisture in the bed longer. A simple drip irrigation system with a timer is worthwhile if you are often on the move.

## Winter

After the last harvest, cover the raised bed with leaves, straw or green manure so that the soil does not become muddy and the soil life remains protected. With an attachment made of foil or glass, the raised bed becomes a cold frame in spring, as described in [Using the cold frame](/gartenwissen/fruehbeet-nutzen/).

## Common mistakes with raised beds

- **Built too wide:** If you don't reach the middle, you'll step on the ground or leave it unused.
- **No vole grid:** Retrofitting is hardly possible when the bed is full.
- **Just soil instead of layers:** Works, but costs a lot of money and heats up less.
- **Too much grass clippings:** Thick layers rot and smell.
- **Forgotten refill:** After two years the surface is 20 centimeters lower and the plants are in the shade of the walls.

## The right height for your back and wheelchair

A big advantage of the raised bed is the comfortable working height. For adults who garden while standing, a height of around 80 to 90 centimeters is comfortable, so that you can sow, plant and harvest without bending over. A lower bed makes more sense for children and people who work while sitting.

If you garden in a wheelchair, you need a raised bed that your knees can fit under. Such table raised beds have a shallow planting tray on legs and are only deep enough to allow salads, herbs, strawberries and other shallow-rooted crops to grow in them. The width should be chosen so that the middle can be reached from one side.

## Use a wooden raised bed for longer

Wooden raised beds are popular, but wood that is constantly in contact with moist soil will rot over time. Robust types of wood such as larch or Douglas fir last significantly longer than spruce. Also line the interior walls with a studded sheet of the type used in construction, with the studs facing the wood. This leaves a layer of air between the earth and the wood, and the wood dries again after it rains.

For vegetable beds, do not use wood that has been treated with chemical wood preservatives or old railway sleepers that are soaked in tar oils. These substances do not belong near food.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much material do I need?

A raised bed measuring 2 × 1 meter and 90 centimeters high holds around 1.8 cubic meters. That's a lot. Collect shrub cuttings, sod and leaves over a few weeks, or ask neighbors who are trimming hedges.

### Can I add grass clippings to the filling?

In thin layers, yes. Thick layers of fresh grass clippings rot, smell and attract slugs.

### Is a raised bed worth it even on good garden soil?

Because of the back and the earlier harvest, yes. On good soil, however, a ground-level bed with compost is just as productive and cheaper, as described in [Creating a vegetable bed](/gartenwissen/gemuesebeet-anlegen/).

### Does foil belong at the bottom of the raised bed?

No. The bottom of the raised bed remains open so that excess water can seep away and earthworms and other soil creatures can migrate from below. At the bottom there is only a close-meshed wire mesh to protect against voles. Foil may only be placed on the interior walls to protect the wood.

### Can I place a raised bed on pavement or on the balcony?

This is possible on pavements and terraces if the water can drain away at the bottom. Caution is advised on balconies and roof terraces: a raised bed with wet soil is very heavy. Clarify in advance the permissible burden and, as a tenant, the landlord's permission.

### How long does the filling of a raised bed last?

The layers inside decompose over several years, and the earth sinks a little each year. Therefore, top up every spring with mature compost or a mixture of compost and garden soil. After about five to seven years, the filling has usually completely turned into soil. Then you can clear out the bed, re-layer it and distribute the old soil throughout the garden.

This saves materials and closes the cycle in the garden.
