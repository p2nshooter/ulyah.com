In January, when nothing is growing outside, one of the most beautiful jobs begins in the vegetable garden: planning. With seed catalogs, squared paper and a cup of tea, the plan for the whole year is created. A good bed plan prevents there being twenty zucchini and not a single lettuce in the garden in July, tomatoes from depriving the beans of light, or cabbage ending up on the bed where it was the previous year. It doesn't have to be perfect. It's a map that you adjust throughout the year. This guide will guide you to your own plan in six steps.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Start with a wish list: What does your family really like to eat and eat often?
> - Calculate realistic quantities. One zucchini plant feeds two to four people, tomatoes two to three plants per person.
> - Draw the beds to scale on squared paper, for example a box measuring ten centimeters.
> - Tall crops such as runner beans, corn and tomatoes on the north side so that they do not cast any shade.
> - Plan subsequent sowings and subsequent crops and note everything for next year's crop rotation.

## Step 1: The wish list

Write down which vegetables are really needed in your kitchen. This sounds obvious, but is often overlooked. A bed full of chard is of little use if no one likes it. Divide the list into three columns:

- **Essential:** what you eat every week, for example lettuce, tomatoes, zucchini, carrots, herbs
- **Like:** what you eat regularly, such as beans, kohlrabi, beetroot, spinach
- **Try:** one or two new crops per year, perhaps an heirloom variety of tomato or parsnip

Vegetables that are cheap and readily available in the store and take up a lot of space, such as storage potatoes or white cabbage, can be left out in small gardens. Vegetables that taste much better freshly harvested belong first: lettuce, peas, sweet corn, tomatoes, beans, herbs.

## Step 2: Estimate quantities

| vegetables | Quantity per person for fresh consumption | Space required per plant |
|---|---|---|
| Zucchini | one plant for 2 to 4 people | about 1 m² |
| Tomatoes | 2 to 3 plants | 60×80cm |
| Salad | Replace 4 to 6 plants every 2 to 3 weeks | 25×25cm |
| French beans | 1 to 2 m row | Rows 40 cm apart |
| carrots | 2 to 3 m row | Rows 25 cm apart |
| Kohlrabi | 6 to 10 plants spread over the season | 30×30cm |
| Chard | 2 to 3 plants | 30×40cm |
| Pumpkin | one plant for 2 to 4 people | 2 to 3 m² |

The values are guidelines for a family that regularly cooks fresh food. If you want to cook or freeze, plan more.

## Step 3: Record the garden

Measure your beds and draw them to scale on graph paper. A scale of one box for ten centimeters is clear. Enter:

- the cardinal points and where shadows fall from trees, hedges or the house,
- paths, water connection, compost,
- solid elements such as rhubarb, strawberries or perennial herbs.

Once you have divided your area into four areas for crop rotation, mark them in color. [Planning crop rotation](/gartenwissen/fruchtfolge-planen/) explains how the system works.

## Step 4: Distribute the cultures

First assign the vegetables to their areas, i.e. heavy eaters, medium eaters and weak eaters, as described in [Heavy eaters, medium eaters, weak eaters](/gartenwissen/starkzehrer-mittelzehrer-schwachzehrer/). Then you distribute them within the areas according to these rules:

1. **Tall plants facing north:** Runner beans, corn, tomatoes and trailing cucumbers on the north side of the bed or garden. So they don't cast a shadow on lower cultures.
2. **Note space requirements:** Draw large plants such as squash and zucchini with their full space requirements, not the size of the young plant in May.
3. **Combine good neighbors:** Carrots and onions in alternating rows, tomatoes with basil, cabbage with celery. More about this in [Mixed culture: good and bad neighbors](/gartenwissen/mischkultur-gute-und-schlechte-nachbarn/).
4. **Separate bad neighbors:** Do not place beans next to onions and garlic, do not place tomatoes next to potatoes.
5. **Accessibility:** Commonly harvested crops such as lettuce and herbs close to the path or kitchen.
6. **Plan for flowers:** Marigolds, nasturtiums and marigolds on the edge of the bed or between the vegetables attract beneficial insects, as described in [Flowers in the vegetable patch](/gartenwissen/blumen-im-gemuesebeet/).

## Step 5: Schedule time

A bed often supports two or three crops in succession over the course of a year. Therefore, enter the time periods for each area:

| area | March to May | June to August | September to winter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heavy feeder | Fleece over early planted kohlrabi | Tomatoes, zucchini, cabbage | Green manure after harvest |
| middle eater | Spinach, radishes | Carrots, beetroot, chard | Lamb's lettuce, winter lettuce |
| weak eater | Peas | Bush beans after the peas | Phacelia |
| recovery | Incorporate green manure from the previous year | Strawberries or flowering plants | Compost for next year |

How the principle of pre- and post-culture works is explained in [pre-culture, main culture, post-culture](/gartenwissen/vor-und-nachkultur/). The [Sowing Calendar for Vegetables](/gartenwissen/aussaatkalender-gemuese/) helps you find the right sowing dates.

**Follow-up sowing:** You don't sow lettuce, radishes, bush beans and carrots all at once, but in several small batches two to four weeks apart. This way you harvest continuously instead of all at once.

## Step 6: Plan seeds and young plants

Derive a shopping list from the plan: How many bags of seeds, how many young plants, when does something have to be brought forward? Check your existing seeds with a germination sample before buying new ones, as described in [Store germination sample and seeds](/gartenwissen/keimprobe-und-saatgut-lagern/). You can grow tomatoes, peppers and cabbage on the windowsill or in the cold frame from February to April, or you can buy young plants from organic farming.

## An example of a bed measuring 1.2 × 4 meters

- **North side:** a row of pole beans on a scaffolding.
- **Middle:** three tomatoes with basil in between, next to two kohlrabi as a pre-culture that are harvested by June.
- **South side:** alternating rows of carrots and onions, with marigolds on the edge.
- **Post-culture:** After onions and kohlrabi comes lamb's lettuce in August.

## Use the plan over the course of the year

A plan is not a law. Later, when the peas germinate, the lettuce sprouts or a storm breaks tomatoes, you adjust it. Enter changes directly into the plan. At the end of the year, you have an accurate record of what was really where, and that is the most important basis for next year's plan.

## Evaluate the gardening season

Take an hour in the fall to compare your plan with reality: which cultures were worth it, which ones disappointed? What was there too much of, and too little of? Which varieties tasted particularly good? Write the answers right next to the plan. This evaluation is the most valuable part of planning because it makes the next plan better.

## Space-saving tricks for small gardens

You can harvest a lot even from just a few square meters if you plan ahead and in time. Runner beans, cucumbers, peas and small-fruited pumpkins grow upwards on trellises, fences or poles and only need a narrow strip on the ground. Fast-growing crops such as radishes or lettuce fit as intermediate seeds between slow crops such as carrots or parsnips and are harvested before they need the space.

A proven method for small beds is square gardening, made famous by the book “Square Foot Gardening” by the American Mel Bartholomew. The bed is divided into squares of about 30 by 30 centimeters, and each square receives a certain number of plants of the same type, for example one tomato, four heads of lettuce or sixteen carrots. This way you can keep track of everything and use every centimeter.

## Plan bed width and paths correctly

So that you can work on a bed without stepping on it, it should not be wider than about 1.20 meters. This means you can easily reach the center from both sides. Every step on the bed compacts the soil, and compacted soil hinders roots, soil life and water absorption.

Paths between the beds should be at least 30 to 40 centimeters wide, main paths on which you drive a wheelbarrow should be more like 60 centimeters or more. With wood chips, bark mulch or treads, the paths remain accessible even after it rains, and they should be marked on the plan right from the start, as they often subsequently disappear under overgrown crops.

## Frequently asked questions

### Paper or app?

Both work. Paper is quick, clear and does not require a battery. Garden apps remind you of appointments and save crop rotation automatically. The only important thing is that you write something down at all.

### How much space do I need for a family?

For a significant addition to the summer kitchen, 20 to 40 square meters are enough. To be largely self-sufficient with vegetables, you need significantly more space and a lot of time.

### When should the plan be ready?

Until the end of February. Then you can order seeds in good time and start growing tomatoes and peppers.

### Can I use the same plan every year?

The basic layout with beds and paths remains, but the crops should move every year. If the same family of vegetables is left in the same place for several years, diseases and pests accumulate in the soil. Therefore, plan a change according to the principle of crop rotation. The guide [Planning crop rotation] (/gartenwissen/fruchtfolge-planen/) explains how this works.

### What else do I include in the plan besides the plants?

Sowing and planting dates, the varieties, the spacing of the rows and notes on the harvest are helpful. If you record at the end of the season what grew well and what didn't, you have the best basis for planning for the next year.

### How do I plan space for flowers and herbs?

Reserve border strips or individual squares for accompanying flowers and herbs right from the start. They attract beneficial insects and make the bed more lively. You can find ideas for this in the guide [Flowers in the vegetable patch](/gartenwissen/blumen-im-gemuesebeet/).
