Beans are ideal starter plants: the large seeds are easy to sow, the seedlings are strong, and the plants hardly need any fertilizer because they get their own nitrogen from the air. Bush beans bear fruit just two months after sowing, pole beans provide fresh pods for many weeks in a small area. There are still a few things you should know so that the seeds don't rot in the cold ground and the harvest doesn't become woody.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Only sow beans when the soil is at least 10, preferably 12 °C, usually from mid-May.
> - Sow shallowly: 2 to 3 cm deep. “Beans want to hear the bells ring,” goes the old gardener’s adage.
> - You can sow bush beans in several batches until the beginning of July.
> - Runner beans need poles or strings that are 2 to 2.5 m high.
> - Raw beans are poisonous. Always cook the pods and seeds for at least 10 to 15 minutes.

## Bush, pole or runner bean?

French beans come in two growth forms. **Bush beans** grow 40 to 60 cm tall, require no support and produce almost simultaneously over about three to four weeks. **Runner beans** grow up to 3 m high, produce later, but over a much longer period until autumn, and produce significantly more per square meter of floor space.

The **fire bean** is a separate species. It is somewhat more cold tolerant, has strong vines and flowers bright red or white. Since its flowers have to be pollinated by bumblebees, it sometimes sets poorly in very hot summers. Their young pods and large, colorful seeds are both edible, cooked of course.

| Type | Height | Row spacing | Distance in the row | First harvest after sowing |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| French bean | 40 to 60 cm | 40 to 50 cm | 5 to 8 cm or clumps every 40 cm | about 8 to 10 weeks |
| Runner bean | 2 to 3 m | Rows of poles 60 to 80 cm | 6 to 8 seeds per stalk | about 10 to 12 weeks |
| Runner bean | 2 to 4 m | like runner beans | 4 to 6 seeds per stalk | about 11 to 13 weeks |

## The right time

Beans come from Central and South America and do not tolerate frost. But the ground temperature is more important than the air temperature. In cold, wet soil the seeds swell but do not germinate and rot. In addition, cold, moist soil attracts the bean fly, whose larvae eat the seedlings from the inside.

In most regions of Germany the ground is warm enough from mid-May. In cool locations you wait until the end of May. A simple soil thermometer, inserted 5 cm deep in the morning, takes away your uncertainty.

You can sow bush beans several times at intervals of three to four weeks, the last sowing in mild locations until the beginning of July, in cool regions until mid-July. This means you have fresh beans from July to October.

## Sowing step by step

1. **Prepare the bed:** Beans are weak eaters. A bed that had heavy feeders the previous year is ideal. Fresh manure or large amounts of fresh compost are not necessary and attract the bean fly.
2. **Pre-soak seeds:** If you want, soak the seeds in lukewarm water overnight. This will speed up germination, but then sow them immediately.
3. **Sowing:** Make grooves 2 to 3 cm deep or press holes for clumps. Place the seeds inside, cover them loosely with soil and press lightly.
4. **Keep moist:** Water, but then keep the soil moist, not wet.
5. **Protect from birds:** Pigeons and blackbirds like to peck at the seedlings. A fleece or net protects in the first few days.

The seedlings appear after one to two weeks in warm weather.

## The climbing aid for runner beans

Runner beans twine around their support in a counterclockwise direction. The following have proven themselves:

- **Rows of poles:** Insert 2.5 m long poles approximately 40 to 50 cm deep into the ground, in rows 60 to 80 cm apart. Two poles opposite each other are tied together at the top, creating a stable roof frame.
- **Bean tent (teepee):** Four to six poles set up in a circle and bundled at the top. Space-saving and popular in the garden with children.
- **Cords:** Cords attached to a sturdy crossbeam, anchored to a peg at the bottom.

Sow six to eight seeds in a circle around each pole, later leaving four to five vigorous plants. If the shoots don't find the pole on their own, gently wrap them around in a counterclockwise direction.

When placing them, consider the shade: rows of pole beans should be on the north side of the garden or in a north-south direction so that they do not shade low crops.

## Care

**Watering:** Beans need water especially during flowering and pod setting. In dry conditions they drop flowers. Then water thoroughly to the soil, not over the leaves, to prevent fungal diseases such as bean rust.

**Mulching:** A thin layer of mulch once the plants are about 6 inches tall will keep the soil evenly moist.

**Hill:** Bush beans will be a little more stable if you hill them lightly once they are 15 to 20 cm high.

**Black bean louse:** It sits in dense colonies at the tips of shoots, especially on fire beans and broad beans. It's usually worth waiting, because ladybirds and hoverflies will follow soon. If the infestation is severe, cut off the affected shoot tips.

## harvest

Harvest beans while the pods are crisp and the kernels are no more than small bulges. A good test: The sleeve breaks with an audible crack when bent. Pick every two to three days because the more regularly you harvest, the more new pods the plant will produce. When picking, hold the shoot with one hand so that you do not tear the plant.

For **dry beans**, let the pods ripen on the plant until they rustle like parchment. In damp autumn weather, pull out the whole plants and hang them in an airy, covered place to dry.

## Proven varieties

- **Bush beans:** 'Saxa' (early and robust), 'Maxi' (long pods that hang above the foliage), 'Purple Teepee' (purple pods that turn green when cooked), yellow wax beans like 'Rocdor'.
- **Runner beans:** 'Neckarkönigin' (long, stringless pods), 'Blauhilde' (purple), 'Cobra' (high-yielding).
- **Fire Beans:** 'Prize Winner' with red flowers.
- **Dry beans:** 'Borlotto' with red speckled kernels.

Many of these varieties are seed resistant. Beans are self-pollinating and are ideal for producing your own seeds.

## Beans and the soil

Beans form a partnership with nodule bacteria that sit on their roots and bind atmospheric nitrogen. After harvesting, cut the plants off at the ground and leave the roots in the bed. This means that some of the bound nitrogen is retained for the next crop. A break of three to four years for growing legumes on the same bed prevents diseases.

## Process and preserve the harvest

In good years, beans produce more than you can eat fresh. The most proven methods:

- **Freezing:** Clean the beans, cut into pieces, blanch in boiling water for three minutes, rinse in ice water, drain and freeze in portions. They last about a year like this.
- **Preserving:** Boil blanched beans in jars with lightly salted water. Beans are high in protein and low in acid, so canning must be done thoroughly and according to reliable instructions, often twice on consecutive days.
- **Make lactic sour:** The traditional Schnippel bean is fermented with salt like sauerkraut. Fermented beans also need to be cooked before eating.
- **Drying:** Store ripe dry bean kernels in a dark, dry and airtight place. A few days in the freezer before storing will kill any bean beetles that may be present.

| problem | Probable cause | What helps |

| Seeds do not germinate and rot | Floor too cold and wet | sow later, check soil temperature |
| Seedlings with eaten cotyledons | Bean fly | Wait for warm ground, not fresh manure |
| Flowers fall off | Dryness or extreme heat | Water evenly, mulch |
| Rusty brown pustules on leaves | Bean Rust | Keep leaves dry and remove plant debris |
| Colonies of black lice | Black bean louse | Wait for beneficial insects and remove shoot tips |

## Harvest dry beans yourself

Many types of beans are suitable not only for green pods, but also as dry beans for stews and salads. To do this, let the pods ripen on the plant until they are yellow, dry and parchment-like and the beans inside rattle. Harvest them on a dry day before persistent autumn rain causes the ripe pods to become moldy.

Then place the pods in an airy place until they are completely dry and peel the beans. Only when they are rock hard do they go into jars. Before cooking, soak dry beans overnight and then cook them thoroughly, because dried beans also contain the toxin phasin, which is only destroyed by sufficient cooking.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why are raw beans poisonous?

Beans contain the protein phasin, which makes raw pods and seeds toxic. It causes nausea, vomiting and stomach pain. Cooking for at least 10 to 15 minutes will destroy it. Children should not eat raw beans from the bush.

### Can I prefer beans?

Yes, in pots about two to three weeks before planting out. This protects against snails and bean flies. However, beans have delicate roots, so plant them before the pots are rooted.

### Why are my beans getting stringy?

Most modern varieties are stringless as long as they are harvested young. Older pods still form threads in many varieties. Harvesting regularly and early helps.

### Can I grow beans in the same place every year?

Better not. Beans should only be returned to the same area after three to four years at the earliest to prevent soil-borne diseases from accumulating. Plan to rotate them with other vegetable families.

### Why are my beans flowers falling off?

Common causes include extreme heat, drought during flowering or cold nights. During dry periods, water regularly and thoroughly during flowering, then the plants will produce pods again.
