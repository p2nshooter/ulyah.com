This calendar gives you an overview of what's happening in the organic kitchen garden in Germany month by month. The dates apply to average locations. In mild regions such as the Upper Rhine, the Palatinate or the Rhineland you can often start one to three weeks earlier, in low mountain ranges, in the foothills of the Alps or in cold locations in the east rather later. What is important is not calendar dates, but soil temperature and the risk of late frost.

> **How ​​to use the calendar:** Read it as a notepad. You will find detailed instructions for every point at BYOY. Write down the actual dates in your garden and your own calendar will become more accurate from year to year.

## January and February

- Create a cultivation plan for the year, check the crop rotation from the previous year ([Plan crop rotation](/gartenwissen/fruchtfolge-planen/)).
- Inspect seeds, take a germination test for older seeds ([germ test](/gartenwissen/keimprobe-und-saatgut-lagern/)).
- Prune fruit trees on frost-free days, especially pome fruit ([Fruit tree pruning](/gartenwissen/obstbaumschnitt/)).
- End of February: Prepare the cold frame, add early varieties of spinach, radishes and lettuce.

## March

- First direct sowing in open, dry soil: broad beans, peas, spinach, carrots from mid to late March in mild locations.
- Plant onion sets as soon as the soil is workable.
- Turn compost and bring mature compost to the beds for heavy feeders.
- Work in green manure from winter or cut it off and leave it as mulch.

## April

- Plant potatoes when the soil is about 8°C warm, from mid-April in many regions ([Growing potatoes](/gartenwissen/kartoffeln-anbauen/)).
- Sow or plant carrots, beetroot (from the end of April), chard, lettuce, kohlrabi and leek.
- Mulch berry bushes and remove old leaves from strawberries.
- Harden off young tomato and zucchini plants gradually.

## May

- Plant frost-sensitive crops after the Ice Saints (May 11th to 15th): tomatoes, zucchini, pumpkin, cucumbers, peppers.
- Sow bush beans and runner beans when the soil is at least 10 to 12 °C.
- Collect snails on wet nights and water the beds in the morning.
- Sow flower strips and offer nesting aids to wild bees.

## June

- Strip out the tomatoes and tie them up, pile up the potatoes.
- Second seeds of bush beans, subsequent seeds of lettuce and radishes.
- Harvest early potatoes, watch garlic: leaves turn yellow.
- Add a layer of mulch to keep the soil moist during hot periods.

## July

- Harvest garlic and winter onions, clear away early potatoes.
- Sow secondary crops: plant endive, sugar loaf, autumn turnips, kale.
- Plant strawberries or let cuttings root ([Plant Strawberries](/gartenwissen/erdbeeren-pflanzen/)).
- Summer pruning of apples and pears, summer raspberries after harvest.

## Aug

- Sow lamb's lettuce, spinach and winter purslane.
- Harvest seeds from beans, peas and tomatoes ([Gather seeds yourself](/gartenwissen/saatgut-selbst-gewinnen/)).
- Sow green manure on harvested beds.
- Thin out currants and gooseberries after harvest.

## September

- Plant garlic from mid-September to the end of October.
- Harvest and dry potatoes, pumpkins and storage onions.
- Make compost with the abundant material.
- Harvest autumn raspberries, mulch strawberries.

## October and November

- Plant fruit trees and berry bushes, bare root products from the end of October ([Plant fruit tree](/gartenwissen/obstbaum-pflanzen/)).
- Collect leaves and use them as mulch or for leaf compost ([Leaves in the Garden](/gartenwissen/laub-im-garten-nutzen/)).
- Cover the beds with mulch or green manure, do not leave them naked into the winter.
- Create deadwood hedges and piles of leaves as winter quarters for beneficial insects.

## December

- Clean garden tools, organize seed supplies.
- Evaluate the garden year: What worked well, which varieties will stay?
- Schedule a soil sample when it is due three to four years ago ([Determine Soil Type](/gartenwissen/bodenart-bestimmen/)).
