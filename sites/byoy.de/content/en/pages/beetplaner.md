The most common experience in the first year of gardening: too many zucchini, too few carrots. The planner above calculates for the number of people in the household how many plants are enough for fresh consumption and how much space they need.

## Quantities and space requirements

| vegetables | Quantity per person | Space requirements |
|---|---|---|
| Zucchini | one plant for 2 to 4 people | about 1 m² |
| Tomatoes | 2 to 3 plants | 60 × 80 cm per plant |
| Salad | 4 to 6 plants every 2 to 3 weeks | 25×25cm |
| French beans | 1 to 2 m row | Row spacing 40 cm |
| carrots | 2 to 3 m row | Row spacing 25 cm |
| Kohlrabi | 6 to 10 plants over the season | 30×30cm |
| Chard | 2 to 3 plants | 30×40cm |
| Pumpkin | one plant for 2 to 4 people | 2 to 3 m² |

An example for two people with zucchini, tomatoes, lettuce, bush beans and carrots: 1 zucchini, 5 tomatoes, 10 lettuce plants per set, 3 m beans and 5 m carrots - a total of around 6 m² of pure planting space, with paths around 9 m².

## Use the space twice

A bed often has two crops per year: after spinach or early lettuce there is room for beans, after peas for lamb's lettuce or green manure. What this looks like is shown in [Create bed plan](/gartenwissen/beetplan-erstellen/) and [Pre- and post-culture](/gartenwissen/vor-und-nachkultur/); If you have little space, read [Creating a raised bed](/gartenwissen/hochbeet-anlegen/).
