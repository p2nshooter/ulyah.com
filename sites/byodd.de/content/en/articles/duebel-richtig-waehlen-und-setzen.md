The dowel is the most inconspicuous component of any wall fastening and at the same time the most important. The wrong dowel in the wrong wall causes shelves to collapse, wall cabinets to tip or curtain rods to slowly move out of the ceiling. With a little basic knowledge about wall building materials and types of dowels, almost anything you want to fix in your home will hold.

These instructions explain which dowel belongs in which wall, which screw fits, how to set it correctly and what needs to be taken into account when carrying heavy loads.

## How a dowel works

Dowels hold in three ways:

- **Frictional engagement:** The dowel expands when the screw is screwed in and presses against the drill hole wall. Typical for concrete and solid brick.
- **Form fit:** The dowel gets knotted or gets caught behind a plate or in cavities. Typical for plasterboard and perforated bricks.
- **Material connection:** An adhesive mortar connects the anchor rod and the building material. Typical for injection systems in difficult substrates.

How it works depends on the wall building material. How you recognize it is written in [correct drilling into concrete, brick and plasterboard](/anleitungen/richtig-bohren-in-beton-ziegel-gipskarton/).

## The most important types of dowels

| dowel | Suitable for | Mode of action | Typical load |
|---|---|---|---|
| expansion dowel | Concrete, solid brick, sand-lime brick | Frictional engagement | Medium to high |
| Universal dowel | Almost all building materials, including perforated bricks and plasterboard | Spreading or knotting | Light to medium |
| Metal cavity dowels | Plasterboard, chipboard, cavity walls | Positive fit behind the plate | Light to medium |
| Toggle dowels, spring hinged dowels | Hollow ceilings, plasterboard | Flap behind the plate | Means |
| Aerated concrete dowels | Aerated concrete | Positive connection through ribs or spirals | Means |
| Long shaft dowels, frame dowels | Through-hole installation of windows, slats, cabinets | Frictional connection over long distances | High |
| Injection mortar with anchor rod | Perforated bricks, damaged drill holes, heavy loads | material connection | Very high |

## The right screw

A good dowel is only as good as the screw in it. Basic rules:

- **Screw diameter:** to match the dowel. Rule of thumb: dowel diameter minus 2 to 3 mm. For a size 8 dowel, a screw with 5 to 6 mm.
- **Screw length:** Dowel length plus thickness of the attachment plus approximately one screw diameter. The screw must extend all the way through the dowel so that it expands completely.
- **Screw Type:** Chipboard or wood screws with continuous thread. Follow the manufacturer's instructions on the packaging.

### Example

A shelf support made of 3 mm steel should be attached with an 8 × 40 mm dowel:

40 mm (dowel) + 3 mm (support) + 6 mm (screw diameter) ≈ 50 mm screw length, i.e. a 6 × 50 mm screw.

## Step by step: place the dowels correctly

1. **Mark the drilling location** and check for lines. See [Locating Pipes Before Drilling](/anleitungen/leitungen-orten-vor-dem-bohren/).
2. **Choose the right drill:** same diameter as the dowel.
3. **Drilling depth:** Dowel length plus 10 mm.
4. **Cleaning the drill hole:** Blow out or vacuum out the drilling dust. For injection mortar, brush out additionally.
5. **Insert the dowel:** flush with the wall or drive it slightly recessed. It must not protrude, otherwise the attachment will push it out again.
6. **Stop the attachment and screw in the screw:** until the dowel is firmly seated. Don't over-twist.

### Through-hole mounting

For battens, window frames or cupboards, drill through the attachment into the wall and insert the dowel. Advantage: The holes are guaranteed to fit together.

## Plasterboard: special rules

Plasterboard panels are 12.5 mm thick and have limited load-bearing capacity. Important:

- **Light loads** such as pictures and clocks: universal dowels or plasterboard dowels made of plastic or metal.
- **Medium loads** such as shelves with books: metal cavity dowels, several if possible, to distribute the load.
- **Heavy loads** such as wall cabinets, televisions or washbasins: only in the **metal or wooden supports** of the substructure or with built-in reinforcements, so-called trusses. You can find the stands with a locator or by knocking; they are usually 62.5 cm apart.

## Perforated bricks and aerated concrete

- **Perforated bricks:** drill without impact, use universal dowels with a long expansion path or injection mortar with a sieve sleeve.
- **Aerated concrete:** Drill without impact or with a cordless screwdriver, use special aerated concrete dowels. Some are driven in without pre-drilling.

## Heavy loads

The following applies to wall cabinets, TV mounts, sinks or wall shelves with books:

- **Observe manufacturer's information** regarding permissible loads.
- **Use multiple attachment points** and distribute the load.
- Consider **leverage**: A deep shelf or an extended TV arm creates a high pulling force on the upper dowel.
- **If in doubt, use injection mortar or heavy-duty anchors**.
- **If you are unsure:** ask a specialist company.

## Errors and their consequences

| Error | Episode | Remedy |
|---|---|---|
| Screw too short | Dowel does not expand, fastening loose | Longer screw |
| Screw too thin | No spreading pressure | Thickness as a rule of thumb |
| Drill dust in the hole | The dowel doesn't sit deep and doesn't hold well | Clean hole |
| Drilled into perforated bricks with impact | Hole too big | Injection mortar |
| Dowel sticks out | Attachment part is not in contact, dowel is pulled out | Drill deeper, sink dowels |
| Heavy load only in plasterboard | Plate breaks out | Attach to stand or traverse |

## Repair torn out dowel holes

If a dowel no longer holds:

1. Pull out the dowel and clean the hole.
2. **Minor damage:** Use a larger dowel or fill the hole with repair mortar and re-drill after it has cured.
3. **Major damage:** Injection mortar with sieve sleeve.
4. **Alternatively:** move the fastening a few centimeters.

How to close holes that are no longer needed can be found in [filling holes in the wall](/anleitungen/loecher-in-der-wand-spachteln/).

## Example: Wall cupboard in the kitchen on a perforated brick wall

A wall cupboard with a width of 60 cm weighs around 15 kg when empty, and when full with dishes it can easily weigh 40 kg or more. It hangs on a suspension rail and, thanks to its depth, pulls with leverage on the upper fastenings.

This is how you do it:

1. **Check wall:** Test drilling without impact. If the drill keeps falling through cavities, it is a perforated brick.
2. **Choose fastening:** For the suspension rail, either universal dowels with a long expansion path in sufficient numbers or, safer, injection mortar with sieve sleeve and threaded rods.
3. **Drilling without impact** at the depth and diameter specified by the dowel manufacturer.
4. **Clean drill holes:** For injection mortar, blow out, brush out, blow out again. This is the only way the mortar bonds to the stone.
5. **Insert the sieve sleeve, inject mortar, screw in the anchor rod** and wait for the curing time. It depends on the temperature and is written on the cartridge.
6. **Assemble the rail, hang the cabinet** and align it using the cabinet hangers.

Prepared in this way, the cupboard stays safe even when fully loaded. If you are unsure about the wall, it is worth asking the manufacturer of the dowel system or a specialist company.

## Insulated facades and adhesive dowels

Normal dowels cannot hold onto external walls with a thermal insulation composite system, i.e. insulation panels with thin plaster over them: the plaster is only a few millimeters thick and the insulation does not bear any load. For mailboxes, lamps, awnings or trellises, there are special mounting systems for insulated facades that transfer the load to the load-bearing masonry and do not form a thermal bridge. Drill holes in the facade must also be sealed tightly to prevent water from running into the insulation. In apartment buildings, the facade is also the responsibility of the landlord.

Where there is no need to drill at all, such as on tiles, there are adhesive dowels: A base plate is glued to the smooth surface with a special adhesive and, after it has hardened, supports towel holders or shelves. They hold surprisingly well, but only on solid surfaces and with fully cured adhesive.

## When safety comes first

For fastenings where failure would endanger people, such as canopies, heavy loads over passageways or hanging chairs on the ceiling, hardware store experience is not sufficient. Dowels and anchors with a building authority approval or a European Technical Assessment are used here, and if in doubt, the design is carried out by a specialist. The approval number can be found on the packaging of such products.

## Checklist

- Wall building material determined.
- Load and leverage estimated.
- Dowel type and diameter chosen appropriately.
- Drilling depth equals dowel length plus 10 mm.
- Drill hole cleaned.
- Screw long and thick enough.
- Heavy loads in drywall only in the substructure.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which dowel is the most versatile for beginners?

A universal dowel holds in most wall building materials and is a good basis. Special dowels are required for heavy loads or special walls.

### Can I reuse a dowel?

Plastic dowels do not, they deform. Metal cavity dowels usually remain in the wall.

### Do I always have to use a hammer drill for concrete?

For clean, fast holes, yes. It can be done with a hammer drill, but much slower.

### How much does a size 6 dowel last?

This depends heavily on the building material. The packaging gives guidelines. It holds significantly more in concrete than in plasterboard.

### Further questions from practice

**What is the difference between nylon and metal anchors?** Nylon plastic anchors are ideal for most residential applications. Metal dowels and heavy-duty anchors are used for very high loads, for fire protection requirements or on ceilings from which loads are suspended.

**Can I just use dowels on the ceiling?** For ceiling lights and light loads, yes. For heavy loads on ceilings, such as hanging chairs or punching bags, only use approved heavy-duty systems and strictly follow the manufacturer's instructions, because here a load has a permanent pulling effect.

**How ​​many dowels do I need for a wall cabinet?** At least two strong attachment points for the hanging rail, more for wide or heavy cabinets. If in doubt, add an additional attachment point.

## Conclusion

Choose the dowel that suits the wall: expansion dowels for concrete and solid bricks, universal and cavity dowels for plasterboard and perforated bricks, aerated concrete dowels for aerated concrete, injection mortar for heavy loads and difficult surfaces. Drill in the right diameter and deep enough, clean the hole, use a sufficiently long screw and fasten heavy loads in drywall only to the substructure.
