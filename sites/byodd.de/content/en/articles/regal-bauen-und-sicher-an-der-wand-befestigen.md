A wall shelf is often your first furniture project: It creates space for books, plants or spices and gives a wall character. At the same time, it is a project in which errors become visible: boards that bend under books, supports that pull out of the wall, or a shelf that hangs crooked.

These instructions show you how to plan a wall shelf, choose the right material and fastenings, assemble it step by step and secure tall furniture from tipping over.

## Step 1: Plan

### What should be on the shelf?

The load determines everything: a shelf for a few plants and picture frames has different requirements than one for a row of books.

| Content | Approximate weight per linear meter |
|---|---|
| Decoration, light objects | 5 to 10 kg |
| Paperbacks | 15 to 25 kg |
| Hardcover books, illustrated books | 25 to 40kg |
| Records | 30 to 45 kg |
| Dishes, canned goods | 20 to 40 kg |

### Check wall

What is the wall made of? Concrete, brick, aerated concrete and plasterboard require different dowels. See [correct drilling in concrete, brick and plasterboard](/anleitungen/richtig-bohren-in-beton-ziegel-gipskarton/) and [select and place dowels correctly](/anleitungen/duebel-richtig-waehlen-und-setzen/).

### Height and distance

- **Subject spacing:** For paperbacks 25 cm, for large books 30 to 35 cm.
- **Bottom shelf** above a chest of drawers or desk about 40 cm away.
- **Top shelf** at an accessible height, approximately up to 2 m.

## Step 2: Board and Span

A board will bend if the span between two beams is too large. Rules of thumb for an even load of books:

| material | Strength | Maximum span between beams |
|---|---|---|
| Chipboard coated | 16mm | 60cm |
| Chipboard coated | 19mm | 70cm |
| Solid wood pine/spruce (glued wood) | 18mm | 70 to 80 cm |
| Solid wood beech/oak (glued wood) | 20mm | 80 to 90 cm |
| Solid wood | 27 to 30mm | 100 to 120cm |
| Multiplex (birch) | 18mm | 80cm |

For heavy loads, such as records, reduce the span or choose thicker boards. A **overhang** at the ends of approximately one fifth of the span improves load distribution.

**Board depth:** For paperbacks 20 cm, for illustrated books 25 to 30 cm. Deep boards create more leverage at the attachment.

## Step 3: Select fastening type

| system | Optics | Resilience | Assembly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Angle supports (consoles) | Visible, many designs | High | Simple |
| Rail system with supports | Visible, flexibly adjustable | High | Simple, rails exactly vertical |
| Invisible Shelf Brackets (Bolts) | Brett appears to be floating | Medium, depending on board thickness and wall | Sophisticated, precise drilling into the board |
| Folding carrier | Visible | Means | Simple |

For beginners and heavy loads, **consoles or rail systems** are the safe choice. Floating shelves look elegant, but require a solid wall, a thick solid wood board and precise work.

## Step 4: Assembly with angle brackets

### material

- Board, cut to size (many hardware stores cut to size).
- Two or more consoles, capable of supporting the planned weight.
- Matching dowels and screws for the wall.
- Short wood screws for the console-board connection.

### Procedure

1. **Mark the height** and draw a horizontal line with a spirit level or laser. See [Measuring and Marking](/anleitungen/messen-und-anreissen-wasserwaage-laser/).
2. **Determine beam positions:** Distance according to span, overhang at the ends.
3. **Locate Pipes:** See [Locate Pipes Before Drilling](/anleitungen/leitungen-orten-vor-dem-bohren/).
4. **Stop the first console**, mark the drill holes, drill, dowel, screw on.
5. **Align the second console** with the spirit level at the same height as the first. Tip: Place the board, level on the board, align the second console like this.
6. **Place the board** and attach it to the consoles from below using short wood screws. Screws must not protrude through the board. See [Wood screws correctly: pre-drill and countersink](/anleitungen/holzschrauben-vorbohren-und-senken/).
7. **Stress test:** Gently press from above, then gradually put away.

## Step 5: Assembling a rail system

1. Align the first wall rail exactly vertically, drill the top hole and screw it on loosely.
2. Align the rail vertically using a spirit level, mark, drill and screw the remaining holes.
3. Attach second rail at the same height: Place a support in both rails and check with a spirit level.
4. Hang the supports, place the boards and secure them if necessary.

## Step 6: Floating Shelf with Invisible Holders

1. **Use a solid wood board** with a thickness of at least 30 to 40 mm.
2. **Mark holder positions** on the wall and mount the holder, perfectly horizontal.
3. **Holes in the board:** Drill exactly matching holes in the back edge, depth and spacing like the bolts. A drilling aid or a pillar drill helps to drill straight.
4. **Push the board on.** If there is a slight downward slope, align the holders slightly upwards or use compensating washers, depending on the system.

## Anti-tip protection for tall furniture

Bookshelves, wardrobes and chests of drawers can tip over if children climb on them or if several drawers are open at the same time. This happens more often than you think and can cause serious injuries.

- **Secure any high or tip-over furniture to the wall.** Most furniture comes with a tip protection device (angle or strap).
- **Use suitable dowels** for the wall; The dowels supplied do not fit every wall.
- **For plasterboard walls** attach to the substructure.
- **Put heavy items at the bottom**.

## Common mistakes

| Error | Episode |
|---|---|
| Too big a span | Bent boards |
| Wrong dowels | Carriers are tearing off |
| Not aligned horizontally | Objects slip, shelves appear crooked |
| Deep boards with small consoles | Leverage, breakaway |
| No anti-tip protection on high shelves | Danger of accidents |

## Prepare boards

A shelf only looks as good as its boards. Sand solid wood and glued wood boards before assembly, break the edges slightly and treat with oil, wax or varnish as described in [Oil, wax or varnish wood](/anleitungen/holz-oelen-wachsen-lackieren/). Treat both sides equally, otherwise the wood will absorb moisture on one side and can warp. Until assembly, store the boards flat and in the room where they will hang for a few days. When chipboard is cut to size, the cut edge remains raw; an ironed-on edge band protects it from moisture and looks cleaner.

## Shelves in niches and corners

A niche between the wall ledge and the cabinet or next to the chimney is ideal for shelves. Instead of consoles, wooden support strips that are screwed to the left and right of the side walls are often sufficient; the board lies on it and is supported almost invisibly. In the case of wide niches, a third strip is added to the back wall so that the board does not bend in the middle. Corner shelves are attached to both walls and are therefore particularly stable. Measure niches in several places, because old walls are rarely parallel; the board is cut or adjusted to the smallest dimension.

## Shelves in the kitchen and bathroom

In the kitchen, grease and steam build up on open shelves. Lacquered or well-oiled wood, metal or coated panels are easier to wipe clean than raw wood. Open shelves above the stove have no place because of heat and fat. Materials that can tolerate moisture are required in the bathroom: stainless steel, glass, plastic or wood with a moisture-resistant surface. Untreated chipboard quickly swells there.

## Plan for lights and cables

An LED strip under a shelf turns a bookshelf into an eye-catcher in the evening and illuminates the work surface below. Plan the path of the cable at the same time: A narrow cable channel on the wall or a groove in the back edge of the board prevents cables from hanging loose. Battery strips with motion detectors don't need a cable at all.

## Practical example: A record shelf above the sideboard

A music lover wants a 1.60 m wide shelf above his sideboard for around 150 records. That's a good 40 kg per shelf.

1. **Wall:** A test drilling shows sand-lime brick, a good base for expansion dowels.
2. **Board:** Oak glued wood 27 mm thick and 33 cm deep, suitable for the board format.
3. **Beams:** Three heavy steel brackets, span between the beams almost 70 cm, 10 cm overhang at each end.
4. **Fastening:** Two 10 mm expansion dowels with 8 × 80 mm screws per console.
5. **Alignment:** Using the cross line laser, project a line 45 cm above the sideboard and align the consoles with it.
6. **Stress test:** First load it with books and leave it overnight, then put the plates away.

The shelf supports the load without any visible deflection.

## Checklist

- Load estimated.
- Wall building material checked, cables located.
- Board thickness and span suitable.
- Fastening system suitable for load and wall.
- Aligned horizontally.
- Tall furniture with anti-tip protection.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does a wall shelf hold?

This depends on the brackets, dowels, wall and board. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for the carrier and distribute the load over several attachment points.

### Can I hang a shelf on a plasterboard wall?

Light shelves with cavity dowels, heavy bookshelves only on the supports of the substructure or with reinforcement.

### How do I stop boards from bowing?

Thicker boards, more beams, smaller spans or a steel strip under the front edge.

### Further questions from practice

**How do I prevent books from tipping forward?** Bookends or slightly tilted backwards mounting on rail systems; With floating shelves, there is a minimal inclination towards the wall.

**Can I mount shelves above the bed?** Only light items and with particularly secure fastenings. Heavy books do not belong over the headboard.

**How ​​long do the screws for the consoles have to be?** The rule of thumb is: dowel length plus the thickness of the console plus approximately one screw diameter. Only then will the screw reach the end of the dowel and spread it completely. The dowel package states the appropriate screw diameter.

## Conclusion

A safe wall shelf starts with planning: weight, wall, board thickness and span. Choose consoles or rail systems for heavy loads, floating holders only with solid board and solid wall. Align everything with a spirit level, use suitable dowels and always secure tall furniture from tipping over. Then the shelf will reliably carry what you entrust to it.
