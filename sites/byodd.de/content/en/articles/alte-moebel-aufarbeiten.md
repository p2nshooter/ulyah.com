The chest of drawers from the flea market, the cupboard from your grandparents' household, the kitchen chair from the bulky waste: old furniture is often made of solid wood, solidly glued and has a story. With a little work, they become favorite pieces that fit your home better than any new chipboard furniture. In addition, refinishing is sustainable and inexpensive.

This guide shows you how to assess, clean and repair an old piece of furniture, how to treat the old surface, which new surface fits and how to use small details to make a big impact.

## Step 1: Assess the piece of furniture

Before you get started, check:

| Question | How to recognize | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Solid wood or veneer? | View edges and cut surfaces: Veneer is a thin layer of wood on a substrate | Only sand the veneer carefully and finely, otherwise you will sand through it |
| Which surface? | Drop of spirit on a hidden area: Shellac becomes soft; Nitro lacquer reacts to nitro thinner; Oil shows no layer | Decide whether to sand or strip |
| Stable? | Shake corners, test drawers and doors | Repair loose connections first |
| Woodworm? | Small round holes with fresh drilling dust | Active infestations must be treated |
| Valuable? | Antique pieces, signatures, special craftsmanship | If in doubt, ask a restorer instead of painting over |

## Step 2: Clean

1. **Take out drawers,** unscrew fittings and collect in a labeled bowl.
2. **Vacuum dust,** including in corners and drawer runners.
3. **Clean** with warm water and a little soft soap or a mild furniture cleaner. Don't work too wet.
4. **Remove grease and old polish** with a mixture of spirit and water or a furniture degreaser.
5. **Let dry.**

Sometimes a thorough cleaning followed by freshening up with oil or wax is enough and the furniture looks almost like new.

### Musty smell in cupboards and drawers

Old cabinets often smell like a basement or attic. The smell is in the open-pored, untreated wood of the interior surfaces and drawers.

1. **Airing:** Open the doors, take out the drawers and leave the furniture in a dry, airy place for a few days. Avoid blazing sun as it bleaches wood and surfaces.
2. **Wipe** with water and a splash of vinegar, then allow to dry completely.
3. **Bind odors:** Place an open bowl with baking soda, coffee powder or activated charcoal in it for a few days and replace the contents regularly.
4. **Seal if all else fails:** A thin coat of shellac on the interior surfaces seals in any remaining odors and dries quickly.

If there is a clear smell of mold or if there are stains, it's more than just smell: check the area carefully and don't sand the affected wood in the apartment.

## Step 3: Repair

### Loose connections

- **Glued tenons and dowels:** Carefully take the connection apart, scrape off the old glue, re-glue with fresh wood glue and press with screw clamps until the glue is dry.
- **If disassembly is not possible:** Apply glue into the joint with a syringe and press it.
- More about wobbling chairs in [Fix Wobbling Chair](/anleitungen/wackelnden-stuhl-reparieren/).

### Stuck drawers

- Rub the running strips with candle wax or soap.
- Double up worn guides with a thin strip of wood.
- See also [Adjust Cabinet Door and Drawer](/anleitungen/schranktuer-und-schublade-einstellen/).

### Breakouts and holes

- **Fill small holes and scratches** with wood putty in a matching color.
- **Repair larger chippings on edges** with a glued piece of wood and sand flush.
- **Load the detached veneer** with wood glue and press it with a clamp and an allowance.

### Woodworm holes

Small round exit holes do not automatically mean that the woodworm is still active. Many old furniture bear traces of an infestation that has been extinct for decades.

- **Test:** Vacuum the furniture thoroughly and place dark paper under the affected areas for a few weeks. If fine, light-colored drilling dust trickles out again or new holes with light-colored edges appear, the infestation is active.
- **Old infestation:** Close holes with hard wax in a matching color, done.
- **Active Infestation:** Do not place the piece next to other wooden furniture. Specialist companies offer heat treatment that does not require chemicals. Only use chemical wood preservatives strictly according to the instructions for use, well ventilated and not on surfaces that come into contact with skin or food.
- **Severely eroded parts** such as feet or slats lose strength and may need to be replaced.

## Step 4: Edit old surface

You have two options:

### Method A: Sand and paint over

If the furniture is to be painted opaquely and the old surface adheres firmly:

1. Sand with P180 to P240 until the surface is matt.
2. Thoroughly dust off.
3. Apply primer, especially on smooth paintwork, plastic coatings or melamine.

### Way B: Remove completely

If you want to show or oil the wood grain:

- **Sanding** with the eccentric sander, P80 → P120 → P180. Be careful with veneer: start with P150 and work by hand. See [Sanding with Sandpaper](/anleitungen/schleifen-mit-schleifpapier-koernung/).
- **Stripping** of profiles, carvings and thick layers of paint. Apply paint stripper, allow to take effect, remove with spatula and brass brush, neutralize according to manufacturer's instructions. Work with gloves and well ventilated.

**Caution with very old paints:** Paints from before the 1970s may contain lead. If suspected, do not dry sand, but rather strip and wear respiratory protection.

## Step 5: New Surface

### Chalk paint

Chalk paints adhere to many surfaces without the need for extensive sanding and produce a matt, velvety surface in a country house style.

1. Clean the surface and sand it lightly.
2. Apply two coats of chalk paint.
3. For a shabby look, lightly sand the edges after drying.
4. **Be sure to seal** with wax or clear varnish, otherwise the surface is sensitive to stains.

### Lacquer

For robust, easy-care surfaces, such as children's and kitchen furniture:

1. Adhesive primer or base coat.
2. Two coats of varnish, with intermediate sanding P280 to P400.
3. Fine paint roller for surfaces, brush for profiles.

### oil or wax

For solid wood with a beautiful grain. Step by step in [Oil, wax or varnish wood](/anleitungen/holz-oelen-wachsen-lackieren/).

### Two-tone

A popular style: body painted, top plate and drawer fronts oiled in natural wood. This combines color and warmth.

## Step 6: Details that make a big difference

- **New handles and knobs:** Brass, leather, ceramic or black metal handles immediately change the character. Pay attention to the hole spacing of existing handles or close old holes with wood filler.
- **Line the inside of drawers** with paper or fabric.
- **Swap feet:** Tapered wooden feet or metal feet give a dresser a light, modern look.
- **Paint the back wall:** A shelf or open cupboard looks completely different with a colored back wall.

## Special case of chair: reupholster the seat

A worn or stained upholstery makes any chair look old, even if the frame is in perfect condition. For chairs with an attached seat, reupholstering is an afternoon project.

1. **Remove the seat plate:** Turn the chair over; The plate is usually attached from below with a few screws.
2. **Remove old cover:** Lever out staples with a staple lifter or flat screwdriver. Save the old fabric, it makes a good template.
3. **Check padding:** Any crumbly or flat foam will be replaced. Solid upholstery foam is suitable for seats; a thin layer of padding fleece on top softens the edges.
4. **Cut the fabric:** Seat plus about 5 to 8 cm all around for folding. Upholstery fabrics can withstand significantly more than decorative fabrics.
5. **Stack from the middle:** First place a staple in the middle of each side, tensioning the fabric evenly, then work towards the corners. Align the pattern and thread straight.
6. **Fold the corners** like a gift, cut off any excess fabric on the inside and staple it in place.
7. **Cover the bottom** with a piece of dustproof cloth, then screw the plate back on.

Braided seats made of cane or rushes and spring-loaded upholstery are a craft in their own right. It's worth asking an upholsterer or chair weaver.

## Example project: A chest of drawers from the 1970s

1. Bought a chest of drawers with dark, shiny lacquer and brass handles.
2. Clean, unscrew the handles, re-glue the loose corner of the drawer.
3. Sand the body with P180, primer, two coats of sage green varnish.
4. Sand the cover plate completely and treat it with hard wax oil.
5. Polish old brass handles and screw them back on.
6. Result: a unique piece for a fraction of the new price, over two weekends.

## Common mistakes

| Error | Episode |
|---|---|
| Sanded through veneer | Carrier material visible, complex repair |
| Painted on smooth varnish without sanding | Paint is flaking off |
| Chalk paint not sealed | Stains and abrasion |
| Loose connections not repaired | Furniture wobbles despite new surface |
| Dry sanded lead-based paint | Health risk |

## Time required and costs

| step | time | material |
|---|---|---|
| Clean and remove fittings | 1 to 2 hours | Cleaner, bowls for small parts |
| Repair and glue | 1 to 3 hours plus drying | Wood glue, clamps, wood putty |
| Sanding or sanding | 2 to 6 hours | Sandpaper, sander |
| Priming and painting | 2 to 4 hours plus drying | Adhesive primer, varnish or chalk paint |
| Oiling | 2 hours plus drying | Oil or hard wax oil |
| New handles and details | 1 hour | Handles, feet |

Depending on the color and handles, material for a chest of drawers usually costs a low to mid-double-digit amount, significantly less than new solid wood furniture.

## Checklist

- Type of wood, surface and condition assessed.
- Fittings kept labeled.
- Connections and damage repaired.
- Appropriate route chosen: sand or remove completely.
- Pay attention to lead in old paintwork.
- New interface suitable for use.
- Chalk paint sealed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I always have to sand?

No. A sanding is enough to paint over. You only have to sand it completely if there is oil or if the old layer doesn't hold.

### Can I refinish chipboard furniture?

Yes, with adhesive primer and varnish or chalk paint. Repair damaged edges with a spatula beforehand.

### How long does a project take?

A chest of drawers usually takes two weekends, mainly because of the drying times.

### Is refinishing worth it financially?

Almost always with solid wood furniture. With very cheap chipboard furniture, the effort is sometimes greater than the benefit.

### Can I refresh an old shellac surface instead of removing it?

Often yes. If the layer is only matt, dirty or slightly scratched, thorough cleaning and subsequent polishing with shellac polish is sufficient. This preserves the character and value of an old piece. The technique takes practice, so try it on a hidden area first and ask a restorer if you have valuable furniture.

### What to do with leftover paint stripper, varnish and thinner?

Liquid residues do not belong in the sink or in the household waste, but rather in the municipality's pollutant collection system. The local waste disposal company regulates how empty or completely dried cans are disposed of.

## Conclusion

Refurbishing an old piece of furniture begins with an honest assessment: type of wood, surface, stability. Clean thoroughly, repair joints and damage, sand or remove the old surface, then choose varnish, chalk paint, oil or wax. New handles, feet or a colored back wall bring the finishing touches. This is how a piece of furniture with character is created from a found object.
