Wooden furniture lives with its owners: a bunch of keys leaves a scratch on the dining table, a toy car leaves a dent in the parquet, a forgotten glass leaves a white ring on the dresser. Many of these marks can be repaired with home remedies and a few tools so that they are hardly visible anymore. It is crucial to correctly assess the type of damage and the surface of the wood.

This guide shows you how to repair scratches, nicks, dents and water stains in oiled, waxed and varnished wooden surfaces and when it makes more sense to re-treat the entire surface.

## Step 1: Recognize surface

The repair depends on how the wood is treated:

| surface | Recognize | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Oiled | Open-pored, natural, water droplets absorb after a while | Easy: sand and re-oil |
| Waxed | Silky shine, fingernail leaves a light mark | Easy: rewax and polish |
| Painted | Closed layer, water rolls off, smooth | More difficult: There are scratches in the paint |
| Veneered | Thin layer of wood over the carrier board | Careful: hardly any material to sand down |

More about surfaces in [oiling, waxing or painting wood](/anleitungen/holz-oelen-wachsen-lackieren/).

## Fine scratches

### On oiled wood

1. Lightly sand scratches with very fine sandpaper (P240) or an abrasive fleece **in the direction of the grain**.
2. Apply some of the same oil, let it absorb, remove any excess.
3. Repeat if necessary.

### On waxed wood

1. Put some wax on a soft cloth and work it into the scratch.
2. Polish after drying.

### On varnished wood

- **Hairline scratches in the paint:** Polish carefully with a furniture polish or a fine polishing paste.
- **Scratches with light wood on the base:** Color in a matching color with a retouching pen or furniture wax pen.

### Home remedies for small scratches

- **Walnut kernel:** On dark wood, rub the nut over the scratch; the oils darken the light scratch. Only for very fine scratches.
- **Furniture wax sticks:** available in many wood tones and the more reliable solution.

## Deep scratches and nicks

1. **Clean:** Remove dust and grease from the notch.
2. **Fill:**
   - **Hard wax** (melt wax) in a suitable color: melt it into the notch with a wax melter or a soldering iron-like device, and once it has cooled, remove it flush with a scraper or plastic spatula. Suitable for painted and oiled surfaces.
   - **Wood putty:** for notches in raw or oiled wood, sand flush after drying.
3. **Trace the grain:** Use a fine retouching pen to draw darker grain lines over the repair area so that it blends into the wood appearance.
4. **Adjust the surface:** Re-oil, wax or cover with touch-up pen.

## Hit the color

Most repairs are noticeable not because of their shape, but because of their color and shine. This order helps:

1. **Work in daylight.** Artificial light, especially very warm light, distorts wood tones.
2. **It's better to start a little lighter.** Wood consists of light and dark areas. Fill with the lighter base tone and then draw in the dark lines with a fine pencil. Darkening is easy, lightening is hardly possible.
3. **Mixing shades:** Hard wax in two adjacent colors can be mixed when melted until the tone matches.
4. **Adjust the level of gloss:** A spot with a perfect color will still be noticeable if it shines more or less than the surrounding area. For paint, rework with a fine pad or polishing paste; for oil, treat the entire surface thinly.
5. **Check from a distance:** Look from a distance of one meter and diagonally against the light, just as you see the furniture in everyday life.

## Dents: Allow the wood to swell

A dent is not a loss of material, but compressed wood fibers. They can often be straightened out again with moisture and warmth. This works best with raw, oiled or waxed solid wood; With paint, the moisture has to get through the layer first.

1. **Place a damp cotton cloth** on the dent.
2. Press the **iron** on the cloth briefly (for a few seconds) on a medium setting. The resulting steam penetrates the wood and causes the fibers to swell.
3. **Repeat** until the dent is gone or noticeably flatter. Check occasionally so that the wood does not burn.
4. **Allow to dry,** then sand lightly and treat the surface.

If the wood is varnished, you can pierce the dent several times with a fine needle to allow moisture to penetrate. Then touch up the paint.

## Water stains

### White spots (water rings)

White rings occur when moisture has penetrated the surface, usually wax or varnish, but not yet into the wood. They can often be removed:

- **Iron method:** Place a dry cotton cloth on the stain and briefly run the iron over it on a low setting without steam. The heat drives out the moisture. Check in between.
- **Hair Dryer:** Warm air on medium setting over the stain.
- **Furniture polish or a little oil:** Polishing with wax often helps with waxed surfaces.
- **Very fine polishing paste:** Carefully, only on painted surfaces.

### Dark spots

Dark spots mean that water has penetrated the wood and reacted with tannins or metals, especially in oak. They are harder to remove:

1. Sand the surface in the area of ​​the stain.
2. **Apply oxalic acid (wood bleach)** according to the manufacturer's instructions, allow to take effect and neutralize. Wear gloves and safety glasses.
3. Once dry, sand and rebuild the surface.

If you have large dark stains on veneered furniture, a specialist company is often the better choice.

## More traces from everyday life

| track | This is how you do it |
|---|---|
| Candle wax | Allow to harden completely, place an ice cube in the freezer bag to make the wax brittle, and lift it off with a plastic spatula. Remove any residue with furniture polish and apply a thin layer of oil to oiled areas. |
| White heat spot from a hot plate | It looks like a water ring and can often be treated in the same way: first with gentle heat, then with polish. |
| Cigarette or match burn mark | Remove superficial discoloration with fine sandpaper. If the wood is charred, carefully scrape out the black layer completely and fill the cavity like a notch. |
| Adhesive residue from labels | Heat it up with a hairdryer, remove it slowly, loosen any residue with a little furniture oil and wipe it off. |
| felt-tip pen or ballpoint pen | On paintwork, first try with a damp cloth and a little dishwashing liquid; Solvent only after testing on a hidden area because some paints dissolve. The paint is usually absorbed into oiled wood: sand lightly and re-oil. |

## Special case of veneer: bubbles and chipped corners

Veneer is often less than a millimeter thick, so sanding is the last choice. There are two typical damages you can easily repair yourself:

- **Bubble in the veneer:** Make a fine cut through the bubble in the direction of the grain with a sharp cutter knife. Squeeze some wood glue under both halves with a disposable syringe, place baking paper on top and press with a flat wooden tool and clamp or weight until the glue is dry. Wipe off any glue that comes out immediately.
- **Chipped corner or edge:** If the fragment is still there, reinsert it with wood glue and fix it with painter's tape. If it is missing, fill the area with hard wax; A carpentry workshop can close larger defects with a suitable piece of veneer.

If you have valuable or antique furniture, seek professional advice beforehand. Old furniture is often veneered with glutin glue, and inappropriate repairs using modern means are difficult to undo later.

## When should the entire area be renovated?

- Lots of scratches, stains and scuff marks all over the surface.
- Paint that is worn or peeling off in many places.
- Very gray or dried out wood.

Then it is usually easier to sand the surface and re-treat it. See [Refinishing Old Furniture](/anleitungen/alte-moebel-aufarbeiten/) and [Sanding with Sandpaper](/anleitungen/schleifen-mit-schleifpapier-koernung/).

## Floors: parquet and floorboards

- **Oiled floors:** Sand scratches locally and re-oil them, as with furniture.
- **Painted floors:** Small scratches with repair pens or hard wax, larger wear and tear by painting over after sanding or sanding by a specialist company.
- **Laminate and Vinyl:** Not real wood; Repair scratches with special repair kits and replace severely damaged floorboards.

## Prevention

- **Felt gliders** under chair and table legs, replace regularly.
- **Coasters** for glasses and pots.
- **Placemats** for meals with children.
- **Regular care** with oil or wax to keep the surface protected.
- **Doormats** at the entrance to prevent sand from getting on wooden floors.

## Practical example: The dining table after the child's birthday party

After a celebration, several white rings from glasses, a deep scratch from a fork and a dent from a dropped toy can be seen on an oiled oak table.

1. **White rings:** Place a dry cotton cloth on the rings and briefly run the iron over it on a low setting without steam. After three passes the rings disappeared.
2. **Dent:** Placed a damp cloth and pressed the iron on for a few seconds. The fibers swell and the dent is almost gone.
3. **Scratches:** Lightly sand along the grain with P240 until the edges are soft.
4. **Re-oiling:** The processed areas and then the entire table top are thinly re-oiled so that the shine is uniform.

Duration: about an hour plus overnight drying.

## Home repair kit

- Furniture wax sticks in two to three wood tones.
- Hard wax in matching colors and a small plastic spatula.
- Sandpaper P240 and abrasive fleece.
- The oil or wax that your furniture is treated with.
- Soft cotton towels.
- Felt gliders for retrofitting.

## Checklist

- Surface determined (oil, wax, varnish, veneer).
- Damage classified (scratches, nicks, dents, stains).
- Method tested on an inconspicuous area.
- Repair area aligned with the surrounding surface.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does toothpaste help against water rings?

Sometimes because it drags easily. But it can leave dull spots. Heat and polish are gentler.

### Can I repair scratches in high-gloss paint myself?

Fine scratches with polishing paste, yes. You can almost always see deep scratches in high gloss; A specialist company makes sense here.

### Why does the repair area become lighter or darker?

Because oil or wax colors fresh wood differently than aged wood. The color often evens out over time.

### Further questions from practice

**Does olive oil help with scratches?** In the short term it darkens light scratches, but does not harden and can go rancid. It is better to use the right furniture oil.

**Why doesn't my dent disappear with the iron?** Steam only straightens compressed fibers. If the fibers are torn or there is missing wood, there is a notch that needs to be filled. Use steam sparingly on veneer: heat and moisture can loosen the glue underneath and create a bubble.

**Can I undo a repair if the color doesn't match?** With wax, yes: The filling can be lifted out again with a plastic spatula and replaced with a more suitable tone. Wood filler, on the other hand, has to be scraped out. That's why it's worth testing in a hidden place.

**How ​​do I prevent scratches from pets?** Keep claws short, use runners in walkways on wooden floors, and choose hard wax oil or varnish with high abrasion resistance.

## Conclusion

First determine the surface, then the damage: fine scratches disappear with oil, wax or wax stick, deep notches with hard wax or wood filler, dents with a damp cloth and iron, white water rings with heat. Dark water stains require wood bleaching or a specialist company. You can prevent new damage with felt pads, coasters and regular care.
