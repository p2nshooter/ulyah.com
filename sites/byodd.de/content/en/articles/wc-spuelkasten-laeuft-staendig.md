A quiet trickle in the toilet bowl, a cistern that doesn't calm down after flushing, or a hissing sound that comes back every few minutes: a cistern that keeps running is not only annoying, but expensive. Even a thin trickle can waste several cubic meters of water a year and noticeably increase your water bill. The cause is usually a calcified or worn component that you can clean or replace yourself with little effort.

These instructions explain how a cistern works, how you can find the cause and fix it step by step, both for exposed and concealed cisterns.

## This is how a cistern works

A cistern has two main components:

| Component | task | Typical mistake |
|---|---|---|
| **Filling valve (float valve)** | Fills the box after rinsing and closes when the water level is reached | Does not close completely, water drains through the overflow |
| **Drain valve (siphon bell or flush valve)** | Releases the water when pressed and then seals the drain | Seal calcified or worn, water runs into the bowl |

There is also the **overflow:** a pipe in the drain valve that prevents the box from overflowing. If water constantly flows out over the overflow, the problem is usually with the filling valve.

## Step 1: Identify the cause

1. **Remove the cistern cover** (surface-mounted) or remove the actuator plate (flush-mounted, see below).
2. **Check the water level:** Is the water up to the top of the overflow pipe and draining over it? Then the **filling valve** does not close properly.
3. **If the water is below the overflow** and still flows into the bowl, the **drain valve** is leaking.
4. **Food coloring test:** Add a few drops of food coloring to the cistern, do not flush. If the water in the bowl changes color after 15 to 30 minutes, the drain valve is leaking.

## Step 2: Shut off water

There is an **angle valve underneath or next to the cistern.** Turn it clockwise. In concealed cisterns, the shut-off valve is located behind the actuation plate. Then rinse so that the box runs empty.

## Step 3a: Check and repair fill valve

### Adjust float height

Many filling valves can be adjusted in height using an adjusting screw or clip. If the float is set too high, the water will rise above the overflow.

- Adjust the float so that the water level ends approximately 2 cm below the top edge of the overflow pipe.

### Clean and descale

1. Remove the filling valve according to the manufacturer's instructions, usually by loosening a union nut or a bayonet lock.
2. Remove limescale and dirt from the seal and valve seat. Place small parts in warm citric acid solution. See [Descaling the shower head and aerator] (/anleitungen/duschkopf-und-perlator-entkalken/).
3. Check the seal: If it is hard, cracked or deformed, replace it.
4. Reinstall, turn on the water, test.

### Exchange

Filling valves are often available universally or manufacturer-specifically. Take the old valve with you or take a photo of the nameplate. The exchange usually only requires loosening the water connection and the fastening.

## Step 3b: Check and repair drain valve

### Clean seal

1. Remove the drain valve (siphon bell) according to the manufacturer's instructions. In many modern cisterns, it can be loosened by turning it and pulled out upwards.
2. **Check the seal at the lower end**: limescale, dirt or hair prevent it from sealing cleanly.
3. Clean and descale the seal and valve seat in the cistern. Do not use a sharp object that will scratch the seat.
4. Reinsert and test.

### Replace seal

If cleaning doesn't help, the seal is worn out. Replacement seals suitable for the cistern model are available from hardware stores or plumbing stores. Take the old part with you.

### Check linkage

Sometimes the linkage or cable gets stuck between the pusher and the valve, so that the valve does not close completely. Check mobility and adjust if necessary.

## Open the concealed cistern

For cisterns that are installed in the wall, the technology is accessed via the flush plate:

1. **Removing the actuation plate:** It is usually pushed up and then pulled forward or pressed in slightly at the bottom. Pay attention to manufacturer's instructions.
2. **Remove frame and cover,** often using screws or clips.
3. **Close shut-off valve** located inside.
4. **Removing components:** The filling and drain valves can be removed through the inspection opening; some systems have auxiliary tools.

The repair then follows the same steps. Many manufacturers offer videos and spare parts sets for their concealed cisterns.

## Assembly and testing

1. Insert components, check connections.
2. Open the shut-off valve slowly.
3. Observe the filling process: Does the water stop below the overflow?
4. Rinse several times and check that the water remains calm after filling.
5. Repeat food coloring test.

## What it costs if nothing happens

A constantly running cistern can waste between a few hundred liters and several thousand liters of water per day, depending on its strength. With usual water and wastewater prices of several euros per cubic meter, this quickly adds up to a double-digit or triple-digit amount per year. In rental apartments with water meters, the tenant also pays for this.

## When the specialist should come

- The angle valve cannot be closed or is dripping.
- Water comes out of the outside of the cistern or on the wall.
- The concealed cistern is leaking in the wall area.
- Components are no longer available.

In rented apartments, small repairs are often one of the minor repairs that the tenant is responsible for up to a certain amount according to the rental agreement. You should report any major damage to the landlord.

## Save flush volumes and water

Older toilets often flush at 9 liters or more. Modern cisterns require around 6 liters for a large flush, some even less, and have a second button for an economical flush of around 3 liters. In many cisterns, the amount can be adjusted at the drain valve. But don't reduce it arbitrarily: The wastewater must be reliably transported away in the pipes, and a flushing flow that is too small can lead to deposits in older pipe networks. Follow the manufacturer's setting ranges.

## What doesn't belong in the toilet

Many clogs and defects start with things that should never have gone into the toilet. Wet wipes do not break down like toilet paper, even if the package promises otherwise, and they become matted in pipes and pumps. Hygiene products, cotton swabs, cat litter, leftover food and frying fat belong in the trash. In many municipalities, old medications are disposed of with residual waste and never through the toilet because sewage treatment plants do not completely break down many active ingredients. What helps with a blockage can be found in [Drain clogged: what to do?](/anleitungen/abfluss-verstopft-was-tun/).

## The cistern is sweating

If drops collect on the outside of an exposed cistern in summer and a puddle forms on the floor, it is often not a leak but rather condensation: the cold water in the box cools the outer wall and moist room air condenses on it. Good ventilation after showering helps. For cisterns that regularly sweat heavily, there is condensation water insulation that is installed inside.

## Change toilet seat

A new toilet seat can be installed in just a few minutes. Beforehand, measure the distance between the two mounting holes in the ceramic, usually 155 millimeters, as well as the length and width of the basin, and pay attention to the shape, oval or rather square. Soft-close seats close quietly, and many can be removed for cleaning at the push of a button.

## Practical example: Concealed cistern that refills every 20 minutes

At night, a short hissing sound can be heard in the bathroom every 20 minutes. The food coloring test turns the water in the bowl blue after half an hour: the drain valve is leaking.

1. **Activation plate removed:** pushed up and pulled off to the front.
2. **Shut-off valve closed,** flushed once.
3. **Drain valve removed:** loosened by a quarter turn and pulled upwards.
4. **Seal checked:** There is a fine limestone edge on the rubber. Seal removed, placed in citric acid solution, seat in the cistern cleaned with a soft sponge.
5. **Replacement:** Because the seal was already slightly deformed, a new, suitable seal from the plumbing supply store was used.
6. **Assembly and Testing:** Valve inserted, water opened, color test repeated: Bowl remains clear.

Cost: a few euros for the seal. Time: 30 minutes.

## Checklist

- Cause determined with water level and color test.
- Water at angle valve or shut-off valve closed.
- Components photographed and model noted.
- Seals cleaned or replaced.
- Tested again after assembly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my cistern hiss every few minutes?

The drain valve is slightly leaking. The water level drops and the filling valve refills. Clean or replace seal.

### Can I put descaler in the cistern?

For targeted descaling of components, yes, according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Continuously used aggressive agents can attack seals.

### How do I find the right spare part?

The manufacturer's name and model are often found in the cistern or on the flush plate. Take the old part to the dealer.

### Further questions from practice

**The cistern fills very slowly. Why is this?** The sieve in the filling valve or on the angle valve is often clogged with dirt. Shut off water, clean sieve.

**The flush is too weak.** The flush volume can be adjusted in many cisterns. A water level that is too low can also be the cause.

**Can I retrofit a flush stop button?** Many systems have dual-flush buttons or conversion kits for economy and full flush.

**Can I put cleaning tablets in the cistern?** Many manufacturers advise against this because aggressive ingredients can attack the seals in the cistern. Flushing stones or cleaners that come directly into the basin are the gentler choice.

**The box has been running since the water was turned off.** After work on the water pipe, sand or rust particles often get into the network and get stuck in the filling valve or its strainer. Shut off the water, rinse the strainer and valve, and the problem is usually solved.

**How ​​do I find out how much water is lost?** Read the water meter in the evening, do not take any water overnight and read it again in the morning. If the meter moves even though all the taps are closed, water is running somewhere and the cistern is the most common suspect.

## Conclusion

A constantly running cistern almost always has one of two causes: the fill valve doesn't close and the water runs over the overflow, or the drain valve doesn't seal and the water runs into the bowl. You can find the cause by checking the water level and the food coloring test. By cleaning, descaling, adjusting the float or replacing the seal, you can usually fix it yourself and save water and money.
