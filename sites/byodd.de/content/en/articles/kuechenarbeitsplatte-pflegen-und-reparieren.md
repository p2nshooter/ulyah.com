The worktop is the most heavily used surface in the kitchen. People cut on it, knead it, place it on it, spill it and sometimes place a hot pot on it that doesn't belong there. Depending on the material, it reacts differently to these stresses, and the care and repair of damage also differ greatly.

These instructions explain how to care for worktops made of solid wood, laminate (coated chipboard) and natural stone or quartz composite, how to repair typical damage such as scratches, stains, burns and swollen edges and how to keep the connection joint to the wall tight.

## Which worktop do I have?

| material | Recognize | Typical weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| Solid wood (glued wood) | Real wood grain, rods visible, wooden edges | Water stains, scratches, needs regular care |
| Laminate | Decorative layer on chipboard, edge often as a edging | Swelling when exposed to moisture on edges and cutouts, burn marks |
| Natural stone (granite, marble) | Cold, heavy, natural pattern | Marble is sensitive to acid, staining occurs if the stone is not sealed |
| Quartz composite | Even pattern, very hard | More sensitive to heat than granite |
| Stainless steel | metal | Scratches, fingerprints |

## Solid wood: care

Wooden worktops are usually oiled. The oil protects the wood from the inside, but must be renewed regularly.

- **Daily:** Wipe with a damp, not wet, cloth. Wipe up puddles immediately, especially around the sink.
- **Regularly re-oil:** Depending on usage, every one to three months. Test: Add drops of water to the plate. If they no longer roll off, but instead absorb or leave dark spots, it's time.
- **Use food-safe oil**.

### Re-oiling

1. Clean the plate and let it dry.
2. If the surface is rough, sand lightly with P240.
3. Apply oil thinly and allow to absorb.
4. After 20 to 30 minutes, thoroughly remove excess oil.
5. Let dry overnight.

**Caution:** Oil-soaked rags can self-ignite. Allow to dry spread out or store in a sealed metal container with water. More about this in [Oil, wax or varnish wood](/anleitungen/holz-oelen-wachsen-lackieren/).

### Wood repairs

- **Scratches and cut marks:** Sand with P180 to P240 in the direction of the grain and re-oil.
- **Water stains (dark):** Sand the surface, use wood bleach if necessary, then re-oil. See [Repair Scratches in Wood](/anleitungen/kratzer-in-holz-reparieren/).
- **Burn marks:** Superficial burn marks can be sanded off. Carefully work out deep burn marks with a sharp chisel, sand and fill with wood filler.
- **Cracks at butt joints:** Fill with wood putty or a mixture of wood glue and sanding dust.

### If the wooden panel buckles or cracks

Solid wood reacts to changing moisture levels. Typical triggers in the kitchen are steam from the dishwasher, moisture around the sink, a plate that is only oiled at the top and a fastening that is too rigid.

- **Slight curvature:** Also oil the underside and observe the plate for a few weeks. If both sides are treated equally, a slight bulge sometimes disappears.
- **Check attachment:** Wooden worktops are held on the base cabinet from below through slotted holes or with mounting brackets so that they can work. Tightly screwed panels can crack.
- **Larger cracks:** If a crack continues to gape or runs through the entire thickness of the panel, a carpenter should inspect the panel.

## Laminate: care and repair

Laminate panels are easy to care for, but their chipboard core is sensitive to water.

### Care

- Wipe with a mild cleaner and a soft cloth, no abrasive cleaners.
- **Keep edges and cutouts** (sink, hob) dry.

### Puffy edges

If water has penetrated the chipboard along an edge or around the sink, it will swell. This cannot be undone, but it can be contained:

1. **Eliminate the cause:** Check and replace silicone on the sink, seal the edge.
2. **Let dry,** possibly with a hairdryer.
3. **Slightly swollen areas** can be carefully adjusted with a sanding block and protected with varnish or edge sealant.
4. **Severely damaged edges:** Replace edge banding or replace the panel in the affected area.

### Re-glue the edge

If a edging has come loose but the chipboard underneath is still dry and solid, you can replace it:

1. **Remove old edge:** Heat up with an iron, remove with a spatula and remove any remaining glue with sandpaper on a block.
2. **Check the substrate:** If the chipboard is swollen or crumbly, a new edge will not hold. Then only replacing the area will help.
3. **Iron on new edge:** Cut the edge band a little longer with hot melt adhesive, place it on top, place baking paper or a cotton cloth on top and slowly move the iron along. Immediately press firmly with a wooden block.
4. **Remove the excess:** After cooling, work it off flush with an edge cutter or a fine file. Move the file from the edge towards the surface so that the edge band does not lift off again.
5. **Break the edge slightly** and smooth it with fine sandpaper.

### Scratches and burn marks

- **Fine scratches:** Fill with repair paste or hard wax in a matching color.
- **Burn stains:** Cannot be removed from the decorative layer. Cover with a recessed cutting board or stainless steel guard, or replacement.

## Natural stone and quartz composite

- **Granite:** very robust. Care with a pH-neutral cleaner. Unsealed granite can absorb grease and oil; Impregnation protects.
- **Marble and limestone:** sensitive to acid. Lemon juice, vinegar and wine leave dull stains. Wipe away immediately, do not use acidic cleaners.
- **Quartz composite:** easy to care for, but sensitive to heat. Always use coasters.
- **Stains in natural stone:** Cover with a paste of baking soda and water overnight; For grease, special stain removers for stone.
- **Splinches** on edges are best repaired by a stonemason with resin in a matching color.

## Stainless steel

- **Cleaning in the direction of grinding:** Brushed stainless steel has fine lines. Always wipe along these lines with a soft cloth or microfiber cloth and mild detergent, then rub dry so that no limescale remains.
- **No steel wool or abrasive cleaners:** They leave scratches, and iron particles from steel wool can form rust spots on the surface.
- **Fine scratches** in brushed stainless steel can be carefully adjusted in the direction of grinding with a fine abrasive fleece. Test in a hidden area first; This doesn't work on polished surfaces.

## The wall connection joint

There is a joint between the worktop and the wall or tile backsplash that prevents water and dirt from running behind it. It is usually closed with silicone or a closing strip.

- **Check the silicone joint** and replace if there are cracks or mold. See [Renewing silicone joints](/anleitungen/silikonfugen-erneuern/).
- **End strips** made of plastic or aluminum with mounting adhesive or clips are a clean alternative, especially on uneven walls.

## Cutouts, bottom and sink cabinet

A lot of water damage starts where you don't look. It's worth taking a look into the sink cabinet with a flashlight every few months:

- **Feel the underside around the sink:** If the plate feels damp, soft or swollen from below, water is running somewhere. Common causes include a leaky sink seal, a leaky siphon, or a loose connection. How to remove the siphon and check its seals is described in [Drain clogged: What to do?](/anleitungen/abfluss-verstopft-was-tun/).
- **Cut edges of the cutouts:** For laminate panels, this is where the open chipboard is located. It should be sealed, depending on the manufacturer's instructions, with edge protection, varnish or silicone. If the seal is missing, you can make it up when the sink is removed anyway.
- **Oil the bottom and cutouts of wooden panels:** Untreated areas absorb steam and moisture and the panel swells unevenly.
- **Place a protective mat or flat tray** in the sink cabinet: It catches drips and makes small leaks visible early on.

## Cut, park, protect

- **Always cut on a cutting board,** even on stone: knives become dull and wood and laminate are scratched.
- **Trivets for hot pots** and baking trays.
- **Water around the sink** wipe away immediately.
- **Dishwasher Steam:** After the end of the program, do not fully open the door immediately if it is under a wooden worktop; the hot steam damages the wood. A steam shield helps.

## Practical example: Saving a neglected wooden worktop

A family takes over a kitchen with an oak countertop that hasn't been oiled in years. The wood around the sink is gray and rough, a dark water mark has formed in one place, and there are cut marks and a burn mark from the pot in front of the stove.

This is how they do it:

1. **Check the sink area:** The silicone joint between the sink and the countertop is cracked. It is removed first so that no more water runs under the plate.
2. **Sand the panel:** Use the eccentric sander P80 for the gray and dark areas, then P120 and P180 on the entire surface, always with suction.
3. **Treat Dark Water Spot:** It does not completely disappear after sanding. Using oxalic acid-based wood bleach makes it significantly lighter.
4. **Burn stain:** Carefully finished with a chisel and filled with wood filler.
5. **Oil:** Three applications of food-safe worktop oil, each removed with excess and allowed to dry for a day.
6. **New silicone joint** on the sink.

Result after a weekend: a record that looks almost like new. Since then it has been re-oiled briefly every two months.

## Daily care checklist

- Wipe wet areas immediately, especially around the sink.
- Use cutting board.
- Coasters for pots and baking trays.
- For wood: water drop test every few weeks.
- For stone: remove acidic stains immediately.
- Check silicone joints twice a year.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often do I have to oil a wooden worktop?

If used daily, every one to three months, more frequently in the first year.

### Can I cut on a wooden worktop?

Possible, but it leaves traces. With a cutting board, the plate stays beautiful for longer.

### What to do if you have a red wine stain on marble?

Record immediately. If discolored, use a natural stone stain paste. A stonemason can polish up acid-dull areas.

### Can a swollen laminate board be saved?

Minor damage can be stabilized. Severely swollen areas should not be replaced.

### Further questions from practice

**Which oil is suitable for worktops?** Special worktop oils or food-safe hard wax oils. Pure cooking oil such as olive oil does not harden and can go rancid.

**How ​​do I remove limescale stains from granite?** Using a pH-neutral stone cleaner and a soft cloth. Acidic limescale cleaners should only be used on acid-resistant stones and only according to the manufacturer's instructions.

**Can I disinfect the worktop?** For everyday use, hot water, dishwashing liquid and a clean cloth are sufficient, then wipe dry. Harsh cleaners and disinfectants can attack the oil or wax layer on wood and dull some surfaces. It is best to cut raw meat and fish on a separate board that can go in the dishwasher.

**Can I install a new worktop myself?** For laminate and wood, yes, with precise planning of the cutouts for the sink and hob. The connection of the sink and hob should be carried out by specialists if electricity or gas connections are affected.

## Conclusion

Wooden worktops need regular re-oiling and can be sanded and replaced if there are scratches or stains. Laminate is easy to clean but delicate around edges and around the sink; Moisture is its biggest enemy. Natural stone and quartz composite are robust, but marble is sensitive to acid and quartz is sensitive to heat. Cut on boards, place hot items on coasters and keep the wall joints tight.
