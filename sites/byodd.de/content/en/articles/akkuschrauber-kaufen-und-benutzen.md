The cordless screwdriver is the tool that is used in almost every DIY project: assembling furniture, screwing on shelves, adjusting hinges, drilling holes in wood and metal, loosening screws that won't move by hand. If you select and use it correctly, you will work faster, cleaner and protect screws, material and your wrist. Anyone who misses the point will be annoyed by slipping bits, frayed screw heads and batteries that fail after two years.

This guide explains the most important technical information, helps with purchase, shows the correct handling of the torque clutch, gears and bits and gives tips for a long battery life.

## Cordless screwdrivers, drills, impact drills, impact wrenches

The terms are not always clearly separated in trade. This distinction helps:

| device | Typical use | Special feature |
|---|---|---|
| Cordless screwdriver (compact) | Furniture assembly, small screws | Lightweight, often 12 V, little power |
| Cordless drill/driver | Screwing and drilling in wood and metal | Two gears, drill chuck, torque clutch |
| Cordless impact drill | Additionally occasionally holes in bricks | Switchable percussion mechanism, unsuitable for concrete |
| Cordless impact wrench | Long screws, wood construction, wheel nuts | Very high torque, hexagonal socket, loud |

For the household, a **cordless drill** is the best choice. For holes in concrete you need a hammer drill anyway; see [correct drilling in concrete, brick and plasterboard](/anleitungen/richtig-bohren-in-beton-ziegel-gipskarton/).

## Understand the technical information

### Voltage (volts)

The voltage says something about the performance class:

- **10.8 to 12 V:** Lightweight and handy, ideal for furniture assembly, cabinets, electrical work on covers and small drilling. Completely sufficient for most households.
- **18 V (some marketed as 20 V):** more power and endurance for long screws, large holes in wood and projects such as terraces or carports. Harder.

### Torque (newton meter)

The torque shows how much force the device applies when screwing in. Manufacturer information often refers to the “hard” maximum torque, which is rarely achieved in everyday life.

| Torque | Typical work |
|---|---|
| 20 to 30 Nm | Furniture assembly, screws up to 4 × 40 mm |
| 30 to 50 Nm | Shelves, screws up to 5 × 70 mm, holes in wood up to 20 mm |
| 50 to 80 Nm | Long wood screws, wood construction, large holes |

### Gears

A drill driver usually has two mechanical gears:

- **Gear 1 (slow, lots of power):** for screwing. The low speed gives you control and prevents slipping.
- **Gear 2 (fast):** for drilling in wood and metal.

### Brushless motors

Brushless motors are more efficient, require less maintenance and get more running time from the battery. They cost a little more, but are worth it if used frequently.

### Battery capacity (amp hours)

The capacity in ampere hours (Ah) determines the running time. For household use, 2.0 Ah at 12 V or 2.0 to 4.0 Ah at 18 V are common. Two batteries are better than one: while one is working, the other is charging.

## Buying advice in five questions

1. **What do I mainly do?** Furniture and small repairs: 12V is enough. Wooden projects in the garden: 18 V.
2. **Do I already have devices from a battery system?** Then stay within the system and share batteries. More about this in the [Basic tool equipment](/anleitungen/werkzeug-grundausstattung/).
3. **How ​​much weight is comfortable?** A 12 V device with battery often weighs less than a kilo, an 18 V device more like 1.5 kg.
4. **Drill chuck or bit holder?** A keyless chuck (10 or 13 mm) holds drills and bit holders and is the most versatile.
5. **What's included?** Charger and at least one, preferably two batteries, case, bit holder. So-called solo devices without batteries are only cheap if you already have batteries.

## Use the torque clutch correctly

The numbered ring behind the drill chuck is the torque coupling. It limits the force with which the screw is screwed in. Once the set force is reached, the clutch audibly disengages (“ratchets”) and the screw stops turning.

This is how you set it up:

1. **Start with a low setting,** about 3 to 5.
2. **Turn in the screw.** If the clutch disengages before the screw head is flush, increase the setting by one or two.
3. **Repeat** until the head sits flush without damaging the material.
4. **Drill symbol:** The clutch is deactivated in this position. It is only intended for drilling.

Why this is important: Without a coupling, screws in chipboard come apart, heads are turned off, and small screws in hinges or plastic become overtightened. If the clutch is correctly adjusted, all screws are at the same depth.

## Bits: the underestimated detail

The most common reason for frayed screw heads is not the screwdriver, but the wrong or worn bit.

| screw head | Recognizable | Bit |
|---|---|---|
| Phillips Phillips (PH) | Simple cross | PH1, PH2, PH3 |
| Phillips Pozidriv (PZ) | Cross with four fine additional lines | PZ1, PZ2, PZ3 |
| Torx (TX) | Six-pointed star | TX10 to TX40 |
| Hexagon socket (Allen) | Hexagon | 2 to 8mm |
| Slit | Simple line | Width to match slot |

**PH and PZ look similar, but they don't fit together properly.** A PH bit in a PZ screw slips easily. Chipboard screws from European production are usually PZ or Torx.

### Tips for Bits

- **Hold the bit in the screw axis** and apply sufficient pressure.
- **Prefer Torx:** Torx screws transfer force best and hardly slip.
- **Magnetic bit holder:** holds the screw when setting and makes overhead work easier.
- **Replace worn bits immediately.** They cost little, damaged screws are stressful.

## Screw correctly: step by step

1. **Select gear 1** and set the clutch to a low position.
2. **Pre-drilling if necessary:** In hardwood, close to edges and with thick screws, pre-drilling prevents splitting. Details in [Wood screws correctly: pre-drill and countersink](/anleitungen/holzschrauben-vorbohren-und-senken/).
3. **Place the screw,** Bit into the screw, hold the device as an extension of the screw.
4. **Let it start slowly:** The gas switch is stepless. First slowly until the screw takes hold, then quickly.
5. **Listen for the clutch** and set the head flush.

## Drill correctly with the drill/driver

- **Select gear 2 and drill symbol**.
- **Wood drill with center point** for clean holes in wood; **HSS drill** for metal, with low speed and some cutting oil.
- **Place a board underneath,** so that the wood doesn't fray when the drill comes out.
- **Large holes** in wood with Forstner bit or hole saw and low speed.

## Battery care for a long life

Lithium-ion batteries are long-lasting if you follow a few rules:

- **Do not charge in the cold:** charging below around 5°C will damage the battery. Allow tools to come to room temperature from the cold garage.
- **Do not store in the heat:** Avoid car in summer or directly by the heater.
- **For longer breaks, store half charged,** not full and not empty.
- **Do not deeply discharge:** Modern batteries switch off in a timely manner. A battery that is left empty for months can be damaged.
- **Use manufacturer's charger.**
- **Don't use damaged batteries any more** and hand them over to retailers or recycling centers, not with household waste.

## Loosen stuck screws

Old screws in furniture, door hinges or garden wood are often tighter than you think.

1. **Select left-hand rotation and gear 1**, place the clutch on the drill symbol so that full power is applied.
2. **Insert a new, exactly fitting bit** and press the device firmly into the screw axis. Start slowly at first; spins the bit, stop immediately before the head frays.
3. **Treat rusty screws** with penetrating oil beforehand and allow it to take effect.
4. **If the head is already damaged,** a screw extractor set can help: Depending on the set, one side drills a small recess in the head, the other reaches in with a left-hand thread and unscrews the screw. Work slowly and with a lot of pressure.

## Work safely

- **Clamp the workpiece:** When drilling in metal or with large drills, the drill can get caught. A loosely held sheet metal then rotates or the device hits the side of your hand. Use clamps and hold the device with both hands; Many 18 V devices have an additional handle for this.
- **No loose gloves or loose sleeves** on the rotating drill, tie long hair back.
- **Protective glasses** when drilling in metal, tiles and overhead.
- **When changing bits or drills**, move the rotation direction switch to the middle position (lock) or remove the battery.
- **Do not touch drill bits after drilling:** They get very hot, especially in metal.
- **Think about cables before every hole in the wall**; see [Locating pipes before drilling](/anleitungen/leitungen-orten-vor-dem-bohren/).

## Common mistakes

| Error | Episode | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Screwing at high speed | Bit slips, head frays | Gear 1 |
| Coupling always on drill symbol | Overtightened screws, torn chipboard | Adjust clutch |
| Wrong bit (PH instead of PZ) | Slipping | Look closely at the head |
| Too little pressure | Bit pops out | Press the device into the screw axis |
| Store the battery in the car | Loss of capacity | Store at room temperature |
| Drilling into concrete with a cordless screwdriver | Drill glows, device overloaded | Use hammer drill |

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a 12 V device enough for the apartment?

For furniture assembly, shelves, hinges and drilling holes in wood and metal, yes. For many long screws in horticulture, an 18V device is more convenient.

### How long does a battery last?

With good care, many years and several hundred charging cycles. Cold, heat and deep discharge shorten the lifespan the most.

### Can I drill dowel holes with a cordless screwdriver?

In plasterboard, aerated concrete and wood, yes. In brick only with impact function and laborious. Not in concrete.

### Why does my screw spin without tightening?

Either she has torn out the thread in the chipboard or the dowel rotates with it. Remedy: thicker screw, wood glue and toothpick in the hole or a new dowel.

### Do I need an impact wrench?

Only for a lot of long wood screws, for example when building a terrace. It is too loud and too powerful for household use.

### Do batteries from other manufacturers fit?

As a rule not, batteries are tied to a manufacturer's system. Although adapters and replica batteries are available, it is not always guaranteed whether they will work safely with the device and charger and the warranty may be void. Original system batteries are the safe choice.

## Purchase checklist

- Voltage class suitable for your projects.
- Two batteries and charger included or available.
- Two gears and adjustable torque clutch.
- Keyless drill chuck.
- Weight and grip tested in hand.
- Guarantee and spare parts availability checked.
- Bought a bit set with PZ, PH and Torx.

### A word about accessories

In addition to bits, a **magnetic bit holder with a guide sleeve** that keeps the screw straight, an **angle attachment** for tight spaces in cupboards and a small **set of wood and metal drills** are worth it. A **countersink** and a **Forstner drill** in 35 mm (for cup hinges) round off the equipment for furniture work.

## Conclusion

A cordless drill with 12 or 18 V, two gears and a torque clutch is the heart of every DIY equipment. Screw in slow gear, adjust the clutch appropriately, use the right bit and pre-drill where the wood could split. Care for the batteries by storing them at room temperature, and for concrete use a hammer drill. This means you can work cleanly, quickly and without damaged screws.
