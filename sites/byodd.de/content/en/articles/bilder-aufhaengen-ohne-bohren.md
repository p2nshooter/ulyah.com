Not everyone has a drill, not every landlord likes to see lots of holes, and in some apartments the walls are so hard that every picture becomes an effort. Luckily, there are many ways to hang pictures, posters and even lightweight mirrors without drilling. Every method has its limitations, and not every method works on every wall.

This guide introduces the main methods, states their load-bearing capacity and suitability, shows how to apply them correctly and explains how to remove them without damaging wallpaper or paint.

## Removable adhesive strips

These strips stick firmly and can be removed by slowly pulling on a tab without damaging the wall.

### Attach correctly

1. **Check wall:** Smooth, painted and load-bearing. They do not hold well on woodchip, wallpaper, rough plaster and freshly painted walls (less than a week) or tear off paint when removed.
2. **Cleaning:** Degrease the wall and back of the frame with rubbing alcohol. No household cleaners that leave residue.
3. **Note weight:** Read the manufacturer's instructions for each strip carefully and prefer to use one more strip.
4. Glue **strips to the frame** and press firmly for 30 seconds.
5. **Press firmly against the wall** for 30 seconds. Many manufacturers recommend that you then remove the picture again, let the strips attach to the wall for an hour without any load and then hang them up.
6. **Room temperature:** Many strips hold up worse in cold or high humidity, such as in bathrooms.

### Remove correctly

Pull the tab **slowly and flatly parallel to the wall** downwards, at least 30 cm long, never forwards. Not jerky.

## Picture nails and steel pins

Special picture nails made of hardened steel are hammered into the wall at an angle with a light hammer. They only leave tiny holes that can be easily closed with a little putty. See [Filling holes in the wall](/anleitungen/loecher-in-der-wand-spachteln/).

- **Systems with multiple pins** in one hook also support heavy frames on gypsum plaster.
- **They often don't penetrate concrete**; Adhesive solutions or drilling are necessary there.
- **There are special hooks on plasterboard**.

Strictly speaking, this is a “hole”, but it is so small that it is a problem in many rental apartments.

## Picture rails and gallery rails

A rail, once mounted, either on the ceiling or just below on the wall, allows pictures to be hung on ropes or rods with sliding hooks. Images can be changed in height and position at any time without having to create new holes.

- **Assembly:** The rail is attached with a few dowels. See [Select and place dowels correctly](/anleitungen/duebel-richtig-waehlen-und-setzen/).
- **Ideal for:** Tenants who redecorate often and for gallery walls that are allowed to change.
- **Old building:** Many old building apartments already have a picture rail under the stucco.

## Picture ledges to lean on

Narrow shelves with an edge on which pictures can be leaned. Although they require a few attachment points, they allow for arbitrary rearrangement and overlapping. See also [Design a gallery wall](/anleitungen/bilderwand-gestalten/).

## Lean: the drill-free design

- **Large format pictures** lean on a dresser, sideboard or the floor against the wall. Looks casual and modern.
- **Mirror** large and heavy leaning on the floor; Secure against tipping over with a discreet adhesive strip or a rubber stopper on the floor, especially with children and pets.

## Mirror without drilling

- **Lightweight mirrors** (up to a few kilos) with mirror tape or adhesive strips that are suitable for mirrors. Normal assembly adhesives can attack the mirror coating.
- **Heavy mirrors** are better leaned on or secured securely with a hole. A falling mirror is dangerous.

## The right height

Many pictures hang too high. A tried and tested rule of thumb from galleries and museums: The center of the picture is approximately at eye level, i.e. around 145 to 155 centimeters above the floor. Above a sofa or chest of drawers, the picture depends on the furniture: There is around 15 to 30 centimeters of space between the backrest or top edge of the furniture and the bottom edge of the picture so that the picture and furniture appear as a unit. You treat several pictures like one big one: the middle of the whole group is at eye level, not every single picture.

## Straighten images

A simple trick helps to ensure that the picture hangs straight on the first try: Stick a piece of painter's tape over the back of the frame, mark the position of the hanger or both hangers on it, remove the tape and stick it to the wall, aligned with a spirit level. Now you can see exactly where the nail or hook needs to be. With frames with two hangers, the picture will hardly slip later. Small felt or rubber dots on the bottom corners of the frame also keep the picture straight and protect the wall from abrasion.

## Posters and canvases

Posters appear to be of higher quality if they are not hung directly on the wall with adhesive tape. Wooden or aluminum poster strips, which hold onto the paper at the top and bottom magnetically or with a clip, are lightweight and can be hung on a single nail or adhesive hook. Clamp frames made of acrylic glass are a cheap alternative. Lightweight canvases on stretcher frames hang well on two picture nails or on adhesive strips if the wall is smooth.

## Security over bed and sofa

A picture falling on the bed at night can cause serious injury, especially with glass. It is therefore worth having two attachment points over beds and sofas, a generous weight reserve and acrylic glass instead of glass. Light frames without glass are the better choice in children's rooms. Also check once a year whether hangers, ropes and hooks are still secure, as cords and adhesive connections can give way over time.

## Practical example: A rental apartment with concrete walls

In a new apartment with reinforced concrete walls, picture nails cannot be inserted into the wall and the residents do not want to drill dozens of holes. Your solution:

- **Living room:** A gallery rail under the ceiling, once secured with four dowels. Five pictures of different sizes hang on nylon ropes and movable hooks. The images are rearranged every season.
- **Hallway:** Two narrow picture ledges on which framed photos and postcards stand leaning.
- **Bedroom:** A large print 70 × 100 cm in a lightweight frame, attached to the smooth painted wall with four pairs of removable adhesive strips. The wall was previously degreased with alcohol.
- **Bathroom:** A light mirror with mirror tape suitable for wet rooms on the tiles.

This meant that only eight drill holes were created in the entire apartment, all in areas that are easy to close when moving out.

## Correctly assess load capacity

Manufacturer's information applies under ideal conditions: smooth, clean, stable surface, room temperature, correct application. Plan a reserve:

- **Weigh the picture frame**, for example on the bathroom scale: first without a picture, then with it.
- **Double the weight** as a safety factor, especially with glass frames.
- **For heavy mirrors** and above beds, it is better to use a screwed attachment.

## Checklist

- Wall surface checked (smooth, wallpapered, plaster, tile).
- Weight of the image measured.
- Appropriate method chosen, reserve planned.
- Surface cleaned and degreased.
- When removing, slowly pull parallel to the wall.

## Frequently asked questions

### How heavy can a picture with adhesive strips be?

This is what it says on the package, often between 1 and 7 kg for a pack of strips. It's better to plan generously.

### Can I hammer nails in the rented apartment?

Hanging pictures is part of normal home use. Small holes in reasonable numbers are common; When moving out, you may be required to lock them. See [Beautify your rental apartment without the hassle](/anleitungen/mietwohnung-verschoenern-ohne-aerger/).

### Do adhesive hooks hold in the bathroom?

High humidity weakens many adhesives. There are special products suitable for wet rooms.

### Further questions from practice

**Do adhesive strips stick to glass and mirrors?** Many strips stick well to glass. Use special mirror adhesive tape for mirrors that does not attack the coating.

**How ​​long do adhesive strips last?** When used correctly and with the right load, they can often last for years. Heat, humidity and direct sun shorten the shelf life.

**What to do if the tab on an adhesive strip tears off?** Heat the strip with a hairdryer on low heat, then use dental floss or a thin fishing line to saw along behind the strip until it comes away. Glue residue can usually be carefully rubbed off with your fingers.

**Does magnetic paint work?** Magnetic paint contains iron particles and is applied in several layers under the normal wall paint. Strong magnets hold postcards, drawings and light posters, but the adhesion is usually not sufficient for picture frames.

## Conclusion

Pictures can be hung in many ways without a drill: with removable adhesive strips on smooth walls, with fine picture nails on plaster and woodchip, with gallery rails for flexible picture walls or simply leaning. Pay attention to the weight, the wall surface and a clean, grease-free surface, and always slowly pull adhesive strips parallel to the wall when removing.
