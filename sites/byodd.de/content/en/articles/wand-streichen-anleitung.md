A freshly painted wall transforms a room more than almost any other renovation, and it's one of those jobs that almost anyone can do themselves. Nevertheless, many self-painted walls look stained in grazing light, show roots, roller marks or old paint showing through. This is rarely due to a lack of talent, but rather to small errors in preparation, material and technology.

These instructions take you step by step through painting a wall or ceiling: from checking the surface to choosing paint and tools to the correct rolling technique that delivers an even, streak-free result.

## Material and tools

| What | For what | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Wall paint (emulsion paint) | Main coat | Coverage class 1 or 2, wet abrasion class 2 or 3 for living spaces |
| Deep ground | Absorbent or sandy surfaces | Only if necessary, see subsurface test |
| Paint roller (18 to 25 cm) | Areas | Medium pile (12 to 14 mm) for smooth walls and woodchip |
| Small roller or brush | Corners, edges, behind radiators | Flat brush 50 mm |
| Scraper grid and paint bucket | Apply paint evenly to the roll | Hang the grid in the bucket |
| Telescopic rod | Ceilings and high walls | Saves the ladder |
| Painter's tape | Masking edges | UV resistant if it sticks longer |
| Cover film and painter's fleece | Protect floors and furniture | Fleece on the floor does not slip |
| Spatula and putty | Holes and cracks | See own instructions |
| Head | Safe working height | Stable |

## How much paint do I need?

Most wall paints provide around 6 to 8 square meters per liter for one coat. The exact information is on the bucket.

**Example calculation:** A room measuring 4 × 3.5 m and 2.5 m high has wall areas of (4 + 3.5) × 2 × 2.5 = 37.5 m². Minus one door (2 m²) and one window (2 m²), approximately 33.5 m² remains. The ceiling is 14 m². For two coats: (33.5 + 14) × 2 = 95 m². At 7 m² per liter that's about 13.5 liters, so a 12.5 liter bucket plus a small reserve.

Woodchip and highly absorbent surfaces use more paint.

## Step 1: Check the surface

Preparation determines the result. Three simple tests:

| test | Implementation | Result and measure |
|---|---|---|
| Wipe test | Run your hand over the wall | White hand: chalky old paint, deep ground required |
| Tape test | Press the adhesive tape firmly and pull it off quickly | Color remains on the belt: remove or fix the old layer |
| Wetting test | Moisten the wall with a wet sponge | Immediately darkens and absorbs strongly: deep ground; Water rolls off: adhesive base or sanding |

Further points:

- **Fill and sand holes and cracks**. See [Filling holes in the wall](/anleitungen/loecher-in-der-wand-spachteln/).
- **Stains from nicotine, water or soot** will show through normal paint. You need an insulating paint.
- **Mold** must be professionally removed and the cause clarified before painting. See [Removing mold from the wall](/anleitungen/schimmel-an-der-wand-entfernen-und-vorbeugen/).
- **Wash off grease and dirt, for example in the kitchen, with diluted dishwashing liquid and allow to dry.

## Step 2: Prepare the room

1. **Remove furniture** or move it to the middle of the room and cover it.
2. **Protect the floor** with painter's fleece or foil, attach to the edges with crepe.
3. **Unscrew socket and switch covers:** Turn off fuse, remove covers, tape off boxes. Don't just mask it off, otherwise messy edges will remain around the cover.
4. **Tape edges:** on ceilings, window frames, door frames and skirting boards. Press the crepe firmly so that no color runs through.
5. **Air, but without draft:** Drying too quickly promotes roots.

## Step 3: Prepare Paint

- **Stir well,** preferably with a stirrer on the drill.
- **For multiple buckets:** Mix in a large container so that there are no differences in color between buckets.
- **Thin only according to manufacturer's instructions:** Many colors are ready to paint; too much water reduces coverage.

## Step 4: Pre-paint corners and edges

Using a brush or small roller, paint a strip about 5 to 8 cm wide on all edges, corners and around sockets. **Only apply as much as you can follow with the roller in the next few minutes** so that the edges are wet when the surface is rolled. Otherwise approaches will arise.

## Step 5: Roll the surface

### First the ceiling

Always start at the top: first the ceiling, then the walls. Drops from the ceiling land on walls that have not yet been painted.

### The technique: wet on wet

1. **Dip the roll** and wipe on the rack until evenly saturated but not dripping.
2. **Distribute the paint:** Apply the paint in a large W or M to an area of ​​approximately 1 × 1 m.
3. **Distribute crosswise:** Without adding new paint, roll out the surface crosswise.
4. **Unroll in one direction:** With light pressure, unroll all strips in the same direction, on the wall from top to bottom, on the ceiling away from the window into the room, i.e. in the direction of the incident light.
5. **Connect the next surface** while the previous one is still wet, and overlap a piece.

Work across a wall from corner to corner without stopping. A break in the middle of the surface almost always leaves a visible mark.

### Second coat

Apply the second coat only after the first has completely dried, usually after four to six hours. Follow the information on the bucket.

## Step 6: Remove crepe and clean up

- **Take off the crepe while the paint is still slightly damp,** at an acute angle and slowly. If the paint is completely dry, the edge will otherwise tear.
- **Cleaning tools:** If using emulsion paint, wash out rollers and brushes with water. Do not pour paint residue down the drain; Dried leftovers belong in the trash, liquid leftovers go to the recycling center.
- **For overnight breaks:** Wrap the roll in cling film or seal it airtight in a bag.

## Problems and solutions

| problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Stripes and approaches | Don't work wet in wet, breaks in the area | Paint the entire wall without stopping, second coat |
| Old paint shows through | Low coverage, applied too thinly | Second coat, opacity class 1 |
| Water stains show through | Water-soluble discolorations | Insulating paint before painting |
| Paint is peeling off | Chalky or smooth surface | Remove old layer, prime |
| Roll marks in grazing light | Incorrect unrolling, too little paint | Unroll in one direction, saturate the roll sufficiently |
| Splashes | Too much paint, rolled too fast | Strip the roll better and roll more smoothly |

## Radiators, reveals and other difficult places

You can hardly get behind radiators with a normal roller. There are narrow radiator rollers on long handles and curved radiator brushes for this. If possible, have the radiator removed beforehand; this is worth it if you are doing a complete renovation. Paint the window reveals with a small roller 10 centimeters wide and the edges with a brush. At the transition between wall and ceiling where masking is difficult, such as uneven plaster, an edge brush with diagonally cut bristles and a steady hand can help. Only paint stucco moldings and profiles with a brush and a little paint so that the details do not run together.

## Two colors on one wall

For a two-tone wall, for example dark at the bottom and light at the top, draw the dividing line with a cross-line laser or a chalk line and cover it with painter's tape. The trick for a razor-sharp edge: First coat the crepe edge thinly with the color that is already underneath. If paint runs under the crepe, it is this base color that is not noticeable and the edge is then sealed. Only then does the second color follow. Remove the crepe while the last coat is still slightly damp. How to combine colors is described in [Select wall color](/anleitungen/wandfarbe-auswaehlen/).

## Stroke healthily

Modern emulsion paints are solvent-free, but often contain preservatives that can cause allergies in sensitive people. Anyone affected should use preservative-free paints, such as those based on silicate or lime, and pay attention to environmental labels for low-emission products. Ventilate thoroughly during and after painting and, if possible, let bedrooms, children's rooms and rooms for pregnant women air out for a few days before using them again.

## Choose color

The choice of color determines the effect of the room. You can find the basics about colors and effects in [Select wall color](/anleitungen/wandfarbe-auswaehlen/). It is important for rental apartments: strong colors often have to be painted over in a neutral way when you move out. ## Time required and costs

| step | Time for a room of about 15 m² |
|---|---|
| Clear out, cover, tape off | 1 to 2 hours |
| Fill and prime | 1 to 3 hours plus drying |
| Paint the ceiling, two coats | 2 to 3 hours plus drying |
| Painting walls, two coats | 3 to 4 hours plus drying |

Good wall paint costs more than a cheap product, but often covers completely with the second coat, while cheap paint needs three coats. The bottom line is that the better color is usually cheaper and saves time.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does the paint have to dry?

Can usually be painted over after four to six hours, fully resilient after a few days. Cool and damp rooms extend the time.

### Do I always need deep ground?

No. Only on highly absorbent, sandy or chalky surfaces. It is more likely to cause damage on intact old paintwork because it can worsen adhesion.

### Can I paint over wallpaper?

Yes, via woodchip and paintable non-woven wallpaper. Glue down any loose areas beforehand.

### What room temperature is ideal?

Between 15 and 25 °C without drafts and without direct sun on the wall.

## Checklist

- Substrate tested with wipe, adhesive tape and wetting test.
- Holes filled, spots isolated.
- Floor and furniture covered, edges taped, covers unscrewed.
- Paint stirred well, bucket mixed.
- First the ceiling, then the walls.
- Edges pre-painted, surface rolled wet on wet without a break.
- Crepe removed while the paint is still wet.

## Conclusion

A streak-free coating relies on good preparation and the right technique. Check the surface, fill and insulate where necessary, cover and tape edges cleanly. Paint the ceiling first, then the walls, working wet on wet without a break from corner to corner and finally rolling in one direction. With two coats and the crepe removed early, the result is as good as the painter.
