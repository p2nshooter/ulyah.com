A dripping faucet is more than just an annoying noise at night. One drop per second adds up to many liters per day and several thousand liters per year, with warm water adding energy costs. The repair is usually simple: in older faucets with two handles it is a worn seal or a valve top part, in single-lever mixers it is the cartridge.

These instructions show you how to find the cause, open the valve safely, get the right replacement parts and complete the repair step by step.

## Which fitting do you have?

| fitting | Recognize | Typical cause of dripping |
|---|---|---|
| Two-handle fitting (cold and hot separately) | Two turning handles | Worn gasket or valve top |
| Single lever mixer | A lever for quantity and temperature | Worn or calcified cartridge |
| Thermostatic fitting (usually shower) | Rotary knob for temperature, separate knob for quantity | Cartridge or backflow preventer |

## Where is it dripping?

- **From the spout even though the tap is closed:** Seal, valve top or cartridge.
- **On the handle or lever:** Seal on the valve top or cartridge.
- **At the base of the fitting:** O-rings on the spout (for swivel spouts) or connection.
- **Under the sink:** Connection hoses or angle valves. Act quickly here, this can lead to water damage.

## Preparation

### tool

- Screwdriver (slotted and cross), Allen key.
- Open-end wrench or adjustable wrench, water pump pliers.
- Cloth to protect chrome surfaces.
- Descaler or citric acid.
- Fitting grease (silicone grease).
- Possibly cartridge key.

### Shut off water

1. **Turn corner valves** under the sink or sink (clockwise).
2. **Open the tap** to release the remaining pressure and check whether there really is no more water coming out.
3. **Close the drain** or place a cloth in it so that no small parts disappear.
4. If a corner valve is stuck or missing, close the main shut-off valve in the apartment.

**Photography:**Take a photo before disassembling each part. This makes assembly much easier.

## Two-handle fitting: Replace seal or valve top part

1. **Remove the handle cap:** Carefully lever out the colored cover (red/blue) with a small slotted screwdriver.
2. **Unscrew the handle:** There is a screw underneath. Pull off the handle.
3. **Unscrew the valve top:** Anti-clockwise with a suitable open-end wrench. Hold the fitting with your other hand to prevent it from twisting.
4. **Check the seal:** There is a rubber seal (cone seal) at the lower end of the upper part of the valve. If it is hard, cracked or dented, replace it. It is usually attached with a small screw or by clipping it on.
5. **Check valve seat:** The seat in the valve body should be smooth. If there is heavy limescale, clean with descaler.
6. **Alternatively, replace the entire valve top part:** Costs little, pay attention to thread size (usually 1/2 inch) and spindle length. Take the old part with you when purchasing.
7. **Assembly:** Insert the valve top part with a little fitting grease, hand tight and then tighten with feeling. Assemble the handle and cap.

## Single lever mixer: replace cartridge

1. **Remove the cover cap:** Often at the front or back of the lever; There is a screw underneath (Allen or cross).
2. **Remove the lever.**
3. **Unscrew the cover sleeve:** Often by hand or with pliers and a cloth.
4. **Loosen the cartridge:** Usually secured with a large union nut or two screws. Loosen and pull the cartridge upwards.
5. **Check cause:** Sometimes it is enough to clean and descale the seals on the underside of the cartridge. An exchange is often necessary.
6. **Suitable replacement cartridge:** Determine the manufacturer and model of the fitting (engraving, nameplate, proof of purchase). Cartridges differ in diameter (for example 35 or 40 mm), height and tenon shape. Take the original part with you.
7. **Install:** Align new cartridge exactly (guide pins), tighten nut or screws, install sleeve and lever.

## Drips at the spout of swivel fittings

If water is leaking from the base of a swivel spout, the O-rings are worn:

1. Remove the spout (often by loosening a screw or by pulling it out forcefully).
2. Remove old O-rings, insert new ones of the appropriate size, rub with fitting grease.
3. Put the spout back on.

## Turn on the water and test

1. Close tap.
2. Open corner valves slowly.
3. Check for leaks, including under the basin.
4. Open and close the tap several times and check the temperature.

## Don't forget the aerator

If the water jet sprays after the repair, dirt or lime got into the aerator when it was shut off. Unscrew and clean the aerator. See [Descaling the shower head and aerator](/anleitungen/duschkopf-und-perlator-entkalken/).

## When a new fitting makes sense

- The fitting is very old and spare parts are no longer available.
- The fitting body is damaged or corroded.
- Several parts are worn.

Installing a new faucet is possible for experienced DIY enthusiasts; Tight connections and functioning angle valves are important.

Whether the clause is effective depends on its wording. Report any major damage or leaks under the sink to the landlord immediately. ## Time required and costs

| Repair | time | Spare part (rough guide) |
|---|---|---|
| Replace the seal in the upper part of the valve | 20 to 30 minutes | very cheap |
| Replace valve top | 20 to 30 minutes | cheap |
| Replace the cartridge in the single-lever mixer | 30 to 60 minutes | medium, depending on the manufacturer |
| Replace O-rings on the spout | 20 minutes | very cheap |
| Install new fitting | 1 to 2 hours | depending on the fitting |

An installer charges additional travel and working hours. For simple repairs, it's worth doing it yourself.

## Keep an eye on angle valves and connection hoses

Corner valves that have not been moved for years become stuck with limescale and can no longer be closed in an emergency. Therefore, close and open it again once or twice a year. The flexible connecting hoses between the angle valve and the fitting also age: the rubber inside becomes brittle and the metal mesh can rust. Check them at every opportunity for kinks, rust and moisture, and replace them as a precaution if they have been in use for many years. A burst connection hose quickly floods an entire kitchen.

## Low pressure or high pressure fitting?

If there is a small under-sink hot water tank hanging under the washbasin or sink, caution is advised. Many of these devices are open, unpressurized storage tanks and require a special low-pressure fitting, recognizable by three connection hoses instead of two. Anyone who installs a normal high-pressure fitting here puts the accumulator under line pressure, which can damage it or cause it to leak. When replacing a faucet, always check which type is available and buy the same one again.

## Whistles, hums and knocks on the line

If the valve whistles or hums when you turn it on, a corner valve is often not fully open, a seal is loose or a valve top is worn out. A loud knock in the pipe when a single-lever mixer is closed quickly is called a pressure shock; it occurs when flowing water is abruptly stopped. Closing more slowly helps; if there are frequent impacts, an installer should check whether a pressure reducer or a shock absorber makes sense.

## Prevent water damage

If you know where the apartment's main shut-off valve is located, you won't waste a minute in an emergency. Small, inexpensive battery-operated water detectors that are placed under the sink or washing machine sound the alarm as soon as they get wet. For washing machines and dishwashers, there are hoses with a safety valve that automatically blocks the inlet in the event of a defect.

## Clean fittings gently

If you take care of your fittings, you will see limescale and damage sooner. Clean chrome surfaces with a soft cloth, warm water and a little dishwashing liquid and then rub dry to avoid limescale stains. Only allow lime-dissolving cleaners to take effect for a short time and then rinse thoroughly; scouring milk, steel wool and microfiber with abrasive content will scratch the surface. The manufacturer's own care instructions often apply to brushed, matt or colored-coated fittings because acidic cleaners can attack their surface.

## Practical example: Kitchen faucet drips after closing

A single lever kitchen faucet drips even when the lever is closed, and the lever is harder to move than it used to be. The diagnosis: calcified and worn cartridge.

1. Close the corner valves for cold and hot under the sink, open the tap to release the pressure.
2. Close the drain with a cloth.
3. Pry out the red cover cap on the lever, loosen the Allen screw, remove the lever.
4. Unscrew the chrome sleeve by hand and loosen the cartridge with the union nut.
5. Take a photo of the cartridge, measure the diameter (here 40 mm) and buy a suitable replacement cartridge from a specialist retailer, stating the model of the fitting.
6. Clean the seat in the fitting with a soft cloth, insert a new cartridge, pay attention to the guide pins.
7. Assemble everything, open the angle valves slowly, check for leaks.

The tap is tight, the lever runs smoothly again. Total time: about 45 minutes.

## Checklist

- Corner valves or main shut-off known and functional.
- Drain closed.
- Photos of every step.
- Original part taken with purchase.
- Chrome protected with cloth.
- After assembly, the tightness under the basin was checked.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much water does a dripping faucet lose?

One drop per second roughly results in 5 to 10 liters per day, depending on the drop size, i.e. several cubic meters per year.

### Can I just turn the tap tighter?

This further damages the seal. Better to fix the cause.

### How do I know which cartridge fits?

Determine the manufacturer and model of the fitting, remove the cartridge and take it with you or measure it carefully.

### Further questions from practice

**The corner valve cannot be closed. What to do?** Do not turn with force; old angle valves can break. Close the main shut-off valve in the apartment and have the angle valve replaced by the installer if possible.

**Why does it continue to drip after replacing the cartridge?** Often the cartridge is not inserted completely correctly, a seal under the cartridge is missing or the seat is dirty. Open again and check.

**Can I change the fitting myself?** With working angle valves and a little skill, yes. The correct connection hoses and tight connections are important.

**Do I have to clean the aerator after the repair?** It's worth it. After the line is shut off and opened, limescale and dirt particles often come loose and collect in the aerator. Unscrew the aerator, rinse and reinsert. More about this in [Descaling the shower head and aerator](/anleitungen/duschkopf-und-perlator-entkalken/).

## Conclusion

A dripping faucet can usually be repaired with little effort: turn off the water at the angle valves, open the faucet, replace the seal or valve top part of two-handle faucets, replace the cartridge of single-lever mixers, replace the O-rings if the spouts are leaking. Take photos of every step, take original parts with you when purchasing and finally check all connections for leaks.
