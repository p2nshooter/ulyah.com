Anyone standing in front of the wooden shelf in the hardware store is spoiled for choice: pine, spruce, beech, oak, glued wood, chipboard, MDF, multiplex, OSB. Each material has its strengths, weaknesses and price. The right choice determines whether a shelf will bend, whether a table top will become stained or whether garden furniture will rot after two winters.

This guide provides an overview of the most common solid woods and wood-based materials for do-it-yourselfers, their properties and typical areas of application, and gives tips for purchasing.

## Solid wood: softwood and hardwood

### Softwoods

| Wood | Properties | Typical use | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spruce | Bright, light, soft, easy to work with, resinous | Structural wood, slats, simple shelves | Cheap |
| Jaws | Yellowish to reddish, darkening, pronounced grain, slightly harder than spruce | Country house style furniture, shelves, glued wood panels | Cheap |
| Larch/Douglas fir | Reddish, rich in resin, more weather-resistant | Terraces, facades, garden furniture | Means |

Softwoods are soft: they get dents more easily, but they are easy to saw, screw and sand.

### Hardwoods

| Wood | Properties | Typical use | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beech | Hard, light to reddish, fine-pored, “works” strongly during changes in humidity | Countertops, furniture, toys, stairs | Means |
| Oak | Very hard, robust, pronounced grain, rich in tannins | Tables, floors, worktops, high-quality furniture | High |
| Ash | Hard, elastic, bright | Furniture, tool handles | Medium to high |
| Maple | Very bright, hard, fine-pored | Furniture, countertops, cutting boards | Medium to high |
| Walnut | Dark, noble | High quality furniture, veneers | Very high |

**Special feature of oak:** The tannic acid reacts with iron. Uncoated steel screws and nails leave black marks, especially when wet. Use stainless steel screws in oak. See [Use Wood Screws Correctly](/anleitungen/holzschrauben-vorbohren-und-senken/).

## glued wood

Glued wood panels consist of narrow strips of solid wood that are glued together. They are more dimensionally stable than a wide solid wood board and are available in many types of wood.

- **Advantages:** Solid wood look, warps less, available in standard sizes.
- **Application:** Shelves, table tops, worktops, furniture construction.
- **Variants:** Continuous slats (quieter look) or finger-jointed slats (cheaper, more restless look).

## How solid wood works and what that means for your project

Wood absorbs moisture from the air and releases it again. It swells and shrinks very unequally: in the direction of the grain, i.e. along the board, the length hardly changes, and across it, in the width, it changes significantly. This results in three practical rules:

1. **Do not clamp wide panels rigidly.** A table top that is screwed tightly onto the frame all around can crack or warp. Attach them through slots or with angles that allow for slight movement across the grain.
2. **Do not glue anything across the grain across the entire surface.** A strip that is glued across a wide solid wood panel hinders work. It's better to screw, also with elongated holes.
3. **Look at the annual rings.** On the end grain you can see how a board was cut from the trunk. Standing annual rings that run steeply through the thickness of the board indicate a quiet board. Rings that lie flat tend to curve transversely.

Glued wood warps less than a wide single board, but swells and shrinks in width like solid wood. With multiplex, the movements are largely canceled out by the cross-glued layers.

## Wood materials

| material | Construction | Properties | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| chipboard | Pressed wood chips with glue, often coated | Cheap, heavy, sensitive to moisture, screws hold up well | Furniture carcasses, shelves |
| MDF | Pressed wood fibers | Very smooth, ideal for painting and milling, heavy, swells when wet | Lacquered furniture fronts, shelves, wall paneling |
| Multiplex (plywood made of many layers) | Cross-glued veneer layers, e.g. B. Birch | Very stable, true to shape, nice edges, screws hold well | High quality furniture, shelves, worktops |
| Plywood (poplar) | Few layers of veneer, light | Light and cheap, less resilient | Back walls, light furniture |
| OSB | Coarse, aligned chips | Robust, inexpensive, rustic look | Substructures, workshop furniture, industrial style |
| Hardboard | Thin fiberboard | Cheap, thin | Back panels, drawer bottoms |

### Moisture

Chipboard and MDF swell significantly when wet. There are moisture-resistant versions (usually colored green) for bathrooms and kitchens. Edges must be sealed.

## Saw, screw and coat wood materials correctly

- **Coated chipboard:** The coating splinters easily when sawing. Apply painter's tape to the cut line and use a fine saw blade. Jigsaws and hand-held circular saws tear out at the top, so the nice side is at the bottom.
- **Screws in board edges:** In chipboard and MDF, screws split the edge slightly. Place it in the middle of the edge, with enough distance to the corners, and pre-drill.
- **MDF:** Cut edges absorb paint like a sponge and become rough. Prime the edges, let them dry, sand them finely and only then paint them. MDF dust is very fine: work with a vacuum cleaner and wear an FFP2 mask.
- **Multiplex:** The edges may remain visible and are only finely sanded. Saw across the grain of the top layer with a fine blade, otherwise the veneer layers will fray.
- **OSB:** The surface is rough and can splinter. For furniture, break the edges and sand the surfaces before oiling or painting.
- **Cover open chipboard edges** with edging, an iron-on edge strip. This means they look clean and absorb less moisture.

| Project | Recommendation |

| Large span bookcase | Beech or oak glued wood 20 to 27 mm or multiplex 18 mm |
| Lacquered furniture fronts | MDF |
| Dining table | Solid oak or beech or glued wood |
| Kitchen countertop | Oak, beech or maple as glued wood, well oiled |
| Workbench | Multiplex or beech glued wood for the top, spruce for the frame |
| Children's room furniture | Multiplex, beech or pine with a saliva-proof surface |
| Garden furniture, raised beds | Larch, Douglas fir, or pressure-treated wood |
| Terrace | Larch, Douglas fir, thermowood or hardwood from certified forestry |

## Wood for outside: Construction beats painting

How long a garden bench or planter lasts depends less on the paint and more on whether water can dry out quickly. These principles of constructive wood protection cost almost nothing:

- **Distance from the ground:** Do not place the wood directly on the ground or lawn, but rather on stones, feet or post bases.
- **Allow water to drain:** Make the tops slightly slanted, break the edges and leave narrow gaps between the slats to prevent puddles from forming.
- **Protect end grain:** End faces absorb water particularly quickly. Cut off the end grain on top at an angle or protect it with a cover strip.
- **Air on all sides:** Where wood borders walls or earth, ensure an air gap, for example with spacers or bubble wrap, so that moisture does not build up.

Naturally weather-resistant woods such as larch also benefit noticeably.

## What to look for when buying

- **Straight Boards:** Lift the board at one end and point along the edge. It should not be twisted, arched or bent.
- **Branches:** Solid, ingrown branches are not a problem. Loose or black branches may fall out.
- **Cracks:** Small end cracks are normal; long cracks weaken the board.
- **Wood moisture:** Use dry wood (8 to 12%) for interior furniture. Lumber can be wetter and will warp as it dries.
- **Certification:** FSC or PEFC seals show sustainable forestry.
- **Cutting service:** Many hardware stores cut panels to size. Long cuts in particular are more accurate there than with a jigsaw at home.

## Store wood properly

- **Lying down and level,** on several shims to prevent it from bending.
- **Allow to acclimate for a few days in the room** in which it is processed.
- **Not directly on the heater** or in damp basements.

## Practical example: Three projects, three wood decisions

### A bookshelf for the living room

A couple wants a 6-foot-wide shelf with four shelves for hardcover books. With 18 mm laminated pine timber, the floors would visibly bend under this load over a span of 90 cm. They decide on multiplex birch in 21 mm, divide the width into two fields of 88 cm each using a central wall and oil the panels. Multiplex holds screws in the edges much better than chipboard, and the layered edges become a design detail.

### A workbench in the basement

A do-it-yourselfer chooses spruce squared timber 70 × 70 mm for the frame because it is inexpensive and easy to process. The worktop is a 40 mm thick glued beech panel that can withstand blows and vices. In the damp basement, he stores the wood in the room for a week before construction and oils the board on all sides so that it doesn't warp.

### A raised bed for the balcony

Larch is used here: it is more weather-resistant than spruce and does not require chemical wood protection. The insides are lined with a bubble wrap so that the moist earth does not constantly touch the wood. The screws are made of stainless steel.

## How much does which wood cost approximately?

Prices vary greatly depending on region, dealer and market situation. As a rough guide for a 200 × 40 cm panel with a thickness of 18 to 20 mm:

| material | Rough price range |
|---|---|
| Chipboard coated | low |
| Spruce/pine glued wood | low to medium |
| MDF raw | low |
| Multiplex birch | medium to high |
| Beech glued wood | medium |
| Oak glued wood | high |

Cutting at the hardware store usually costs little per cut and saves a lot of work. Have leftover pieces given to you: They are suitable for test painting and small projects.

## Checklist for purchasing wood

- Project, load and span noted.
- Inside or outside, dry or damp?
- Appropriate type of wood or suitable material selected.
- Boards checked for warping, cracks and loose knots.
- Cutting list prepared with all dimensions.
- Suitable screws included (stainless steel for oak and outdoors).
- Storage area prepared for acclimatization.

## Even more questions from practice

### Can I use lumber from the hardware store for furniture?

Yes, if it is dry enough. Lumber is often wetter and rougher. For furniture, it is better to choose planed, kiln-dried wood or let lumber dry indoors for several weeks.

### Does OSB smell in the living room?

New OSB boards may initially smell of wood and glue. Choose low-emission panels and ventilate well.

### What do “rough sawn”, “planed” and “KVH” mean?

Rough sawn means: straight from the sawmill, with a rough surface and often inaccurate dimensions, good for hidden substructures. Planed wood has smooth surfaces and uniform dimensions and is suitable for furniture. KVH stands for solid construction wood: technically dried, usually planed construction wood that warps less than simple construction wood and is also suitable for visible frames.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is solid wood always better than chipboard?

Not necessarily. Chipboard is dimensionally stable and inexpensive, solid wood is repairable and more durable. For many pieces of furniture, multiplex is a good compromise.

### Why is my board warping?

Wood works with changes in humidity. Wide solid wood boards warp more than glued wood. Treating both sides equally helps.

### Which wood is easiest for beginners?

Spruce and pine: soft, cheap and easy to saw and screw.

### Can I use OSB in the living room?

Yes, for a rustic look. Pay attention to low-emission panels.

## Conclusion

Softwoods such as spruce and pine are cheap and easy to process, while hardwoods such as beech and oak are hard and noble. Glued wood combines a solid wood look with dimensional stability, multiplex is the stable all-rounder, MDF is the basis for painted fronts, chipboard is the economical solution for carcasses. Before purchasing, check boards for straightness and cracks, allow them to acclimatize and protect them with the appropriate surface.
