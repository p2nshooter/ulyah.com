Cold drafts at the window, fogged up corners, a whistling sound in the wind or a window sash that requires force to close: many of these problems have to do with poorly adjusted fittings or old seals, not a defective window. Modern tilt and turn windows can be adjusted in just a few minutes using an Allen key. This saves heating costs and increases living comfort.

This guide explains how turn-tilt fittings work, how to adjust the contact pressure, how to correct a sagging window, checking and replacing seals and caring for fittings.

## How a tilt-and-turn fitting works

When you turn the window handle, a rod moves inside the sash. There are **locking pins** (often mushroom head or roller pins) on it, which engage in **locking pieces** on the window frame and pull the sash against the seal.

Many pins are mounted **eccentrically**: turning them changes how strongly the sash is pressed against the frame. This is the **contact pressure.**

## Step 1: Check for leaks

- **Paper test:** Clamp a sheet of paper between the sash and frame, close the window and pull on the paper. If it can be pulled out easily, the contact pressure at this point is too low. Test all around in several places.
- **Candle test:** On a windy day, run a candle along the closed window (be careful with curtains). If the flame flickers, it pulls.
- **View gaskets:** Are they hard, cracked, flattened or missing pieces?

## Step 2: Adjust contact pressure

### Find cones

Open the window and look at the edge of the sash: This is where the locking pins are located, usually round or oval, often with a marking, a slot or an Allen key opening.

### Set

| Pivot position (marking) | contact pressure |
|---|---|
| Marking for the interior of the room | Stronger (winter) |
| Marking to the outside | Weaker (summer) |
| Marking in the middle | Normal |

Depending on the manufacturer, the marking varies; With oval cones, the long side shows the direction. If in doubt, check the manufacturer's information (name usually stamped on the fitting).

1. **Turn the pin with an Allen key, pliers or screwdriver,** in small steps.
2. **Set all pivots of a window the same.**
3. **Repeat paper test.**
4. **Window must still be able to close easily.** If the handle only turns with great force, the pressure is too high; this puts a strain on fittings and seals.

### Summer and winter position

Many guides recommend setting a higher contact pressure in winter and a lower one in summer. This protects the seals. It is not necessary for all windows; The most important thing is that the window is tight in winter.

## Step 3: Recreate hanging window

If the sash rubs against the bottom of the frame when closing or can only be closed by lifting it, it has sagged.

- **Corner bearing (at the bottom of the hinge side):** The height of the sash can usually be adjusted here. Remove the cover cap and turn the adjusting screw with an Allen key.
- **Scissor bearing (at the top of the hinge side):** The lateral position of the sash at the top can be adjusted here. Pulling the sash at the top towards the hinge side raises the opposite side.
- **Adjust in small steps** and check closing after each change.

The exact position of the adjusting screws depends on the manufacturer. If you have heavy window sashes, balcony doors or lift-and-slide doors, it is better to hire a specialist company.

## Step 4: Maintain and replace seals

### Nurture

- **Cleaning:** Once or twice a year with warm water and mild detergent.
- **Care:** Rub with seal care stick or silicone spray. This keeps the rubber supple.
- **Do not paint over:** Paint makes seals hard and leaky.

### Renew

Old, hardened seals can be replaced:

1. **Pull out the old seal** at one corner. It is usually in a groove.
2. **Determine the profile:** Take a piece of the old seal to the specialist dealer or measure it. There are many profiles.
3. **Clean groove.**
4. **Press in the new seal,** do not cut in the corners, but lead around the corner if the profile allows it. Don't compress or stretch.
5. **Butt** in a spot at the top center, not in a corner, and connect with sealant if necessary.

For older windows without a groove, there are self-adhesive window sealing tapes made of foam, rubber or silicone. It is important to have a clean, dry and grease-free surface: wipe the frame with spirit, apply the sealing tape without pulling and press it firmly. Choose the profile thickness so that the window still closes well.

## Step 5: Maintain fittings

- **Once a year** lubricate all moving hardware parts with an acid- and resin-free oil or grease, especially tenons, strikers and scissors.
- **Move the handle several times** to distribute the lubricant.
- **Wipe off excess.**
- **Tighten loose handles**: Turn the cover at the base of the handle and retighten the screws.

## Other causes of drafts

- **Roller shutter box:** Often leaky and not insulated. Insulate and provide the belt passage with a brush seal.
- **Connection joint between window frame and masonry:** Close leaky joints on the inside with acrylic. Have the outside professionally sealed.
- **Window sill:** Seal the gap under the window sill.
- **Mold on reveals** indicates thermal bridges and moisture. See [Removing and preventing mold from the wall](/anleitungen/schimmel-an-der-wand-entfernen-und-vorbeugen/).

## Ventilate properly despite tight windows

Tighter windows reduce uncontrolled air exchange. This makes it all the more important to ventilate the room several times a day, otherwise the humidity will increase and with it the risk of mold.

## Rental apartment

Adjusting and maintaining window fittings is part of normal care. Broken windows, broken fittings or leaky connections are the responsibility of the landlord and should be reported.

## Misted windows between the glasses

If an insulating glass pane fogs up not inside or outside, but between the two glasses, the edge seal has become leaky. Moisture has penetrated into the space between the panes and the insulation effect is weakening. Adjusting or sealing doesn't help here; the glass unit needs to be replaced. Often only the window can be replaced and the frame remains intact. If you have to replace it anyway, you should pay attention to the thermal insulation: older insulating glazing from the 1980s lets in many times the heat that modern thermal insulation glazing loses.

## Wooden windows need care

Wooden windows will last for decades if their paint remains intact. Once a year, check especially the lower edge of the frame and the weatherboard, where rainwater runs off. Small cracks in the paint allow water into the wood, which then swells, turns gray and ultimately rots. Lightly sand flaking areas and recoat with a suitable glaze or varnish before major damage occurs. How to properly prepare wood can be found in [Sanding with sandpaper](/anleitungen/schleifen-mit-schleifpapier-koernung/). Plastic windows are easier to care for, but also benefit from cleaned seals and lubricated fittings.

## Retrofit burglary protection

Many burglars simply pry open windows. Simple fittings with roller pins, on the other hand, offer little resistance. Mushroom head pins, which engage in suitable security strikers and make it much more difficult to pry open, are more effective. Lockable window handles prevent the handle from being turned from the outside through a small hole in the glass. For older windows, there are additional locks that are screwed onto the frame and sash. The police advice centers provide free advice on which measures make sense for your apartment. The following applies to rented apartments: discuss retrofitting with the landlord in advance.

## Space for the heating air

There is a radiator under many of the windows. It warms the cold window surface and prevents condensation from forming there. Long curtains that fall over the radiator or wide window sills that trap warm air weaken this effect. Let the warm air rise freely through the window.

## Windows and small children

Lockable window handles not only provide protection against burglary, but also child protection. In addition, there are opening restrictors that allow a window to be opened just a crack, and locks that allow it to be turned, but only in the tilt position. Also, do not place furniture, boxes, or beds directly under windows that children could climb on, especially on upper floors. For floor-to-ceiling windows and balcony doors, the same applies to balcony furniture that can serve as a climbing aid.

## Practical example: drafts in the living room window

In winter there is a draft in the large living room window and there is condensation on the windowsill in the morning. The paper test shows that the sheet can be easily pulled out from the bottom of the handle side.

1. **Pin checked:** All five locking pins are in the middle position.
2. **Contact pressure increased:** Use the Allen key to turn all pins one step towards the inside of the room.
3. **Paper test repeated:** Now the paper sticks everywhere.
4. **Seal checked:** The seal is flattened at the bottom of the handle side. Treated with seal care pen; It will be replaced in the spring.
5. **Roller shutter box:** The belt passage has a brush seal.
6. **Ventilation adjusted:** Instead of tilting, the room is ventilated for five minutes in the morning.

The condensation occurs less frequently and the drafts have disappeared.

## Checklist

- Paper test done all around.
- All pins set the same.
- Window can still be closed easily.
- Seals cleaned and maintained.
- Fittings lubricated.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much heating energy does a tight window save?

That depends on the condition. Significant drafts can noticeably increase heat loss; readjustment is one of the cheapest measures.

### My window is difficult to close. What to do?

Reduce contact pressure, lubricate fittings, check whether the sash has sagged.

### Can I seal old wooden windows?

Yes, with self-adhesive seals, if necessary after refurbishment. There are special solutions for box windows.

### Further questions from practice

**The window gets stuck when tilted. What to do?** The top of the scissors is often dirty or dry. Clean and lubricate, check contact pressure.

**My window handle is loose.** Turn the cover under the handle 90 degrees, the two screws become visible and can be tightened.

**Does film help against cold windows?** Insulating films for windows can reduce some heat loss from old single glazing. With modern windows, adjusting and sealing brings more.

## Conclusion

Drafts on the window can often be remedied with an Allen key: increase the contact pressure using the eccentric pins, adjust sagging sashes on the corner and scissor bearings, clean, maintain or replace seals and lubricate fittings annually. Also check the roller shutter box and connection joints and ventilate regularly despite the tight windows.
