Wallpapering is considered the supreme discipline of renovation. With modern non-woven wallpaper and a little planning, it can be easily achieved, even for beginners. Things usually get difficult in the same places: the first panel hangs crooked, the corners curl, cracks appear at sockets, and the seams gape after drying.

This guide explains the main types of wallpaper, the materials required, how to prepare the surface and how to wallpaper step by step, including corners, sockets, windows and radiators. At the end you will find out the easiest way to remove old wallpaper.

## Which wallpaper for what?

| Wallpaper | Properties | Glue | For whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| paper wallpaper | Cheap, lots of patterns, stretches when soaked | Paste the track, wait for the soaking time | Experienced people who can handle soft times |
| woodchip | Paper with wood fibers is painted over | Paste the track, soak time | Classic in rental apartments, conceals small imperfections |
| Non-woven wallpaper | Dimensionally stable, does not stretch, dry strippable | Paste the wall, lay the membrane dry | Beginner, quick work |
| vinyl wallpaper | Durable, washable | Mostly pasted onto a non-woven backing or wall | Kitchens, hallways, children's rooms |
| Fiberglass fabric | Very robust, can be painted over | Paste the wall | Heavily stressed walls, bridging cracks |

**Recommendation for beginners:** Non-woven wallpaper. It is not soaked, the strips remain the same length and width, and the next time you renovate it can be removed dry in whole strips.

## Material and tools

- Wallpaper with the same **batch number** on all rolls (otherwise color differences possible).
- **Paste** suitable for the wallpaper: non-woven paste for non-woven wallpaper, normal paste for paper and woodchip.
- Bucket, stirring stick, **paste brush or paste roller**.
- **Pastry table** (for paper and woodchip).
- **Wallpapering brush or pressure roller**, for non-woven also a plastic wallpapering spatula.
- **Cutter knife with many replacement blades** and a wide metal spatula as a cutting aid.
- **Seam roller** for the joints.
- Folding rule, spirit level or plumb bob, pencil.
- Sponge and water for paste residue.
- Director.

### How many roles?

A standard roll is 10.05 m long and 53 cm wide. With a room height of 2.50 m, one roll produces around three strips if no repeat pattern (pattern offset) has to be observed.

**Example calculation:** Room circumference 15 m, minus doors and windows about 2 m wide = 13 m. 13 m: 0.53 m ≈ 25 panels. 25 : 3 ≈ 9 rolls. For patterned wallpaper with a repeat, plan for one more roll. Wide non-woven wallpapers measuring 1.06 m halve the number of strips.

### The symbols on the label

On each roll of wallpaper there are small symbols that are standardized in the European standard EN 235. Those who can read them make more targeted purchases and experience fewer surprises on the wall.

- **Rapport:** An arrow with a zero means no gaps, the panels fit together without pattern matching. Two arrows at the same height indicate a straight approach, arrows offset from one another indicate an offset approach. Information such as 64/32 states the repeat height and offset in centimeters. Opposite arrows are called “sticking upside down”: Every second strip is attached rotated by 180 degrees so that slight differences in color at the edges are not noticeable.
- **Gluing:** A symbol with a brush shows whether the wall or the wallpaper strip is to be pasted.
- **Cleaning:** Wavy lines indicate whether the wallpaper is water-resistant, wash-resistant or even scrub-resistant. For the kitchen, hallway and children's room, it is worth at least being washable.
- **Fade resistance:** A sun symbol shows how much the colors can fade in sunny rooms.
- **Remove:** Further signs reveal whether the wallpaper can be completely removed dry, split or can only be removed wet. Splittable means: The top layer comes off, the backing paper remains on the wall and, if it sticks firmly, can serve as a base for the next wallpaper.

## Step 1: Prepare the surface

The surface must be **clean, dry, firm and level**.

- **Remove old wallpaper** (see below).
- **Fill and sand holes and cracks**. See [Filling holes in the wall](/anleitungen/loecher-in-der-wand-spachteln/).
- **Pre-paint highly absorbent walls** such as fresh gypsum plaster or plasterboard with primer or diluted paste. Otherwise the wall will absorb water from the paste too quickly and the wallpaper will not adhere well.
- **Sand smooth, non-absorbent surfaces,** such as oil paint.
- **For non-woven wallpaper**, apply a white base coat to dark or stained walls because some non-wovens show through easily.
- **Turn off fuse** and unscrew socket and switch covers.

### Three quick tests before the first paste

- **Wipe test:** Rub the wall with the flat of your hand. If white dust remains on the hand, the old paint is chalking. Then wash the wall and solidify it with primer.
- **Adhesive tape sample:** Press firmly on a piece of strong adhesive tape and pull it off quickly. If there are paint residues on it, the paint is not stable and must be scraped off. Otherwise, the drying wallpaper will pull it away from the wall later.
- **Wetting test:** Apply some water to the wall with a sponge or plant sprayer. If it beads up, the surface is hardly absorbent and should be sanded. If it absorbs immediately and stains the wall dark, it sucks hard and needs a primer.

The three rehearsals take a total of five minutes and often save a whole day of rework.

## Step 2: Align the first track

The first track determines whether all the others hang straight. **Room corners are rarely truly vertical.**

1. Start in a corner next to the window and work from there away from the light. This means that any overlaps on paper wallpaper are not noticeable as shadows.
2. Measure a strip width minus about 1 cm from the corner.
3. Draw a vertical line across the entire height of the wall using a spirit level, plumb line or cross-line laser. See [Measuring and Marking](/anleitungen/messen-und-anreissen-wasserwaage-laser/).
4. The first panel will be aligned with this line, not the corner. The small excess in the corner will be cut off later.

## Step 3a: Glue non-woven wallpaper

1. **Mix the paste** according to the package instructions and let it swell.
2. **Paste the wall:** Using a roller or brush, apply a strip evenly, slightly wider than a strip.
3. **Put the strip on dry from the roll**, leave about 5 cm overhang at the top, align with the plumb line.
4. **Press:** With a wallpapering brush or pressure roller from the middle outwards and from top to bottom. Squeeze out air bubbles.
5. **Cut off any excess:** Press a wide spatula into the edge between the wall and ceiling or baseboard and cut along it with a sharp cutter. **Break blade frequently:** A dull blade will tear the damp wallpaper.
6. **Place the next panel end to end**, without overlap, and press the seam with the seam roller.
7. **Immediately dab off any paste residue** with a damp sponge.

## Step 3b: Glue woodchip and paper wallpaper

1. **Cut lengths:** Room height plus about 10 cm.
2. **Paste onto the wallpapering table** evenly up to the edges.
3. **Place together:** Fold both ends towards the middle, paste on paste without creasing.
4. **Keep to the soaking time,** usually 5 to 10 minutes. Make sure all the strips are the same length, otherwise they will be different widths.
5. **Apply, align, press and cut off** as with fleece.

## Step 4: Corners

- **Inside corners:** Do not lead the membrane around the corner in one piece. Instead, pull the strip about 1cm around the corner, cut it off and start the next strip on a new plumb line on the other wall so that it covers the overlap in the corner.
- **Outside corners:** Run the membrane 2 to 3 cm around the corner. Place the next strip vertically and connect it neatly with a double seam cut, if necessary.

## Step 5: Sockets, Windows and Radiators

### Sockets and switches

Fuse off, cover off. Stick wallpaper over the can, cut a cross into the opening with the cutter, fold back the tabs and cut along the edge of the can. After drying, reinstall the cover.

### Windows and doors

Glue the strip over the frame, cut diagonally at the corner of the frame and cut off the excess along the frame. Reveals are wallpapered with our own cuts.

### Behind radiators

Push the strip as far as possible behind the radiator, cut into the brackets from below and press down with a long, narrow wallpaper brush or a radiator roller. Alternatively, just paint behind the radiator.

## Step 6: Drying

- **No drafts and no heating on high** during drying. Otherwise the wallpaper will dry too quickly and the seams will tear.
- **Only paint over the woodchip** when it is completely dry, usually after 24 hours. See [Paint Wall](/anleitungen/wand-streichen-anleitung/).

## Remove old wallpaper

| Wallpaper | Method |
|---|---|
| Non-woven wallpaper | Lift the corner and peel it off dry in full strips |
| Paper and woodchip | Perforate with a spiked roller, soak with warm water and detergent or wallpaper remover, scrape off with a spatula after 15 minutes |
| Multiple layers or stubborn | Steam wallpaper remover (available to borrow) |
| Vinyl | Peel off the top layer dry, soak the backing paper and peel it off |

**Caution:** When soaking, do not bring too much water to electrical outlets. Switch off fuse. Do not wet plasterboard walls, otherwise the cardboard will come loose.

## Common problems

| problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Seams are gaping | Drying too quickly, paper wallpaper soaked unevenly | Avoid drafts, adhere to soaking times, glue the seam with paste |
| Bubbles | Too little or uneven paste, not spread | Fill the blister with a syringe and paste or score it and press it on |
| Crooked paths | Not aligned per plumb | Always re-plumb the first path and corners |
| Frayed cut edges | Dull blade | Blade often breaks |
| Wallpaper is coming off | Absorbent or dusty surfaces | Prime, choose appropriate paste |

## Time required and costs

| step | Time for a room of about 15 m² |
|---|---|
| Remove old wallpaper | 3 to 6 hours |
| Fill and prime | 2 to 4 hours plus drying |
| Glue non-woven wallpaper | 4 to 6 hours |
| Glue woodchip | 5 to 7 hours |
| Paint the woodchip twice | 4 to 6 hours plus drying |

Material: wallpaper, paste, small tools. You can rent a steam wallpaper remover on a daily basis from a hardware store.

## Practical example: A children's room with non-woven wallpaper

A family covers a wall in the children's room with patterned non-woven wallpaper. The remaining walls remain painted white.

1. The old woodchip is soaked and removed with a spiked roller and warm water.
2. Dowel holes are filled and the wall is primed.
3. The first plumb line is projected with the cross line laser.
4. The panels are cut from the roll with a repeat pattern and numbered.
5. Paste the wall, lay out the strip, align it, press it on, cut it off.

The wall is finished in five hours and the seam is barely visible.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to paint over woodchip?

Yes, woodchip is intended to be painted over and can be painted over several times.

### When can I wallpaper on fresh plaster?

Only when the plaster is completely dry. As a rule of thumb, with good ventilation, there is about one day of drying time per millimeter of plaster thickness. If you wallpaper too early, you will lock in moisture and risk stains, bubbles and mold.

### Can I stick wallpaper over wallpaper?

This is not recommended. The old wallpaper can come loose due to the moisture and drag everything down with it.

### How long does a room last?

For a room of around 15 m² with non-woven wallpaper, beginners can expect one day plus preparation.

## Conclusion

Non-woven wallpaper is the easiest way for beginners to wallpaper: paste the wall, lay the strip dry, align it, press it on, cut it off. Prepare the surface thoroughly, align the first strip with a plumb line, treat corners with new plumb lines, cut with a sharp blade and avoid drafts when drying. Then the panels sit straight and the seams remain closed.
