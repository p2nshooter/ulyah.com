The door drags on the new carpet, only closes with a swing, jumps open again or squeaks with every movement: problems with room doors are commonplace and can almost always be fixed yourself. Common causes include sagging door hinges, a displaced strike plate, a new, higher floor covering or moisture that causes wooden doors to swell.

These instructions show you how to find the cause and make the door open again step by step: from adjusting the hinges to adjusting the strike plate and shortening the door leaf.

## Step 1: Diagnosis

Open and close the door slowly and observe:

| Symptom | Probable cause |
|---|---|
| Grinds at the bottom of the lock side | Door has sagged, hinges are worn out or misaligned |
| Grinds at the top of the lock side | Door is hanging crooked, top hinge is set too far or frame is warped |
| Grinds across the entire width of the floor | New flooring or carpet is higher |
| Doesn't fall into the lock | The striking plate does not fit the latch correctly |
| Rattles when closed | Too much play between the latch and the striking plate |
| Squeaks | Dry bands |
| Clamps alternately in summer or winter | Wood works through moisture |

**Check the gap size:** The gap between the door and the frame should be even all around, usually around 3 to 4 mm. Unequal columns show which direction the door is moved.

## Step 2: Adjust door hinges

Modern room doors have adjustable hinges. There are different systems:

### Drill-in hinges (classic)

The hinges consist of a part on the frame and one on the door leaf, which are inserted into each other. The height is adjusted using washers or an adjustment screw in the hinge part.

- **Raise the door:** Unhook the door (open and lift it upwards), place thin washer rings (door hinge rings) on the hinge pin of the frame, hang the door back in. So the whole door lifts.
- **Adjust laterally:** On some hinges, the hinge parts can be turned deeper or less deep into the frame or door leaf using an Allen key.

### 3D adjustable straps

Modern doors often have hinges with adjusting screws for three directions:

| direction | Effect |
|---|---|
| Height | Raise or lower door |
| Page | Move the door to the lock or hinge side |
| pressure | How tightly the door fits against the seal |

Remove the cover caps from the straps and adjust them in small steps using an Allen key. Check the door after every quarter turn.

### Typical correction

**Door is dragging on the bottom of the lock side:** The door has sagged. Remedy: Pull the door to the hinge side using the **upper hinge** (lateral adjustment) or lift the entire door with hinge rings.

## Step 3: Adjust strike plate

If the latch does not click into the striking plate:

1. **Mark:** Rub some lipstick, chalk or pencil graphite onto the trap and close the door. The marking on the striking plate shows where the latch hits.
2. **Slight deviation:** Use a file to widen the opening in the striking plate by one to two millimeters.
3. **Larger deviation:** Unscrew the striking plate, move the position up or down, pre-drill new holes, adjust the recess in the frame with the chisel. Fill old screw holes with wood glue and toothpicks. See [Use Wood Screws Correctly](/anleitungen/holzschrauben-vorbohren-und-senken/).
4. **Rattling door:** Many strike plates have an adjustable tab that you can bend slightly inward with a screwdriver or adjust with a screw to make the door fit tighter.

## Step 4: Lubricate squeaky belts

1. Unhook the door.
2. Clean tape pin.
3. **Lubricate with spray oil, silicone spray or a little petroleum jelly**. No cooking oil, it gums up.
4. Hang the door and move it several times. Wipe off excess.

## Step 5: Shorten the door

After laying a new floor or thick carpet, the door often drags across its entire width. Then the door leaf must be shortened.

### Check: Which door?

- **Solid wood doors** can be shortened without any problem.
- **Tubular or honeycomb doors** (most modern room doors) only have a frame of a few centimeters at the bottom. A few millimeters to around 1 cm are usually possible; anything more can remove the frame and make the door unstable. Check manufacturer information.

### Procedure

1. **Take measurements:** Floor clearance of around 5 to 8 mm above the highest point of the new floor (carpet) is common. In rooms with a ventilation system, a larger gap may be required.
2. **Unhook the door** and place it on two stands.
3. **Mark the cutting line** with a square and pencil.
4. **Apply tape to the cutting line** to prevent the surface from fraying.
5. **Sawing:** Best with a hand-held circular saw on a guide rail; With a jigsaw the cut is rarely completely straight. Use a fine saw blade and saw from the back when the saw teeth go up.
6. **Break edges** with sandpaper.
7. **Seal the cut edge** with varnish or edge protection to prevent moisture from penetrating.
8. **Reattach.**

## Wooden doors and moisture

Solid wooden doors can expand and jam in high humidity, especially in bathrooms or in summer. Before you plan:

- **Wait and see:** Often the pinching disappears in drier seasons.
- **ventilate and heat.**
- **Only if it is permanently clamped** carefully rework the area with a plane or sandpaper and seal the finished edge again.

## When the professional is needed

- The frame is warped or loose.
- The door and frame are badly damaged.
- Fire protection or apartment entrance doors: Changes here may only be made professionally and in accordance with the manufacturer's specifications.

## Hanging door handle and stiff lock

If the door handle hangs down or wobbles after being released, it is usually due to loose fastening screws on the rosette or long plate. Tighten it and check whether the square pin is seated correctly in the lock and the handle with its grub screw is firmly seated on the pin. If the handle still does not return to the horizontal position, the spring in the mortise lock is often tired; Then the only solution is a new lock, which is available cheaply in standard sizes. You can make a stiff lock with a bearded key work again with a little graphite powder or a special lock spray. Normal oil collects dust and gums up the mechanics over time.

## Seals and drafts

Many room doors have a rubber seal in the frame. If it is flattened or cracked, the door will rattle and let noise through. Most seals are only pressed into a groove and can be pulled out and replaced with a suitable profile. Brush strips or an automatic lowering seal, which presses down when closed, help prevent drafts on the floor.

Important: Not every door should be tight at the bottom. In apartments with an exhaust air system in the bathroom or with a gas boiler that depends on the room air, the gap under the door is often deliberately dimensioned so that air can flow in. Anyone who seals it can disrupt the ventilation and endanger the combustion air supply in gas appliances. In such cases, speak to the landlord, specialist company or chimney sweep beforehand.

## Place the door stop correctly

A door stop prevents the handle from punching holes in the wall or the door leaf from hitting furniture. Wall buffers are glued or doweled. Floor doorstops fit better if they are screwed on, but be careful with underfloor heating: you must not drill into the screed under any circumstances. Check beforehand whether and where heating pipes are located, as described in [Locate pipes before drilling](/anleitungen/leitungen-orten-vor-dem-bohren/), or use an adhesive stopper.

## Sliding doors and all-glass doors

Sliding doors that are difficult to move or jerk almost always have dirt in the track or worn rollers. Vacuum the rail and clean it with a damp cloth, check the rollers for abrasion and readjust the height setting on the carriage until the door runs evenly and does not touch anything at the bottom. The following applies to all-glass doors: Never drill into the glass or shorten it. The straps are adjusted using clamping plates, and only the glazier can work on glass.

## Practical example: The bedroom door drags after the new carpet

After laying a shag carpet, the entire width of the bedroom door drags. The door is a modern tubular chipboard door.

1. **Unhook the door** and check the manufacturer's information: According to the data sheet, the lower frame has a width of 25 mm; up to 10 mm can be shortened.
2. **Set floor air:** The carpet is 14 mm high. 6 mm of air is desired. 8 mm must be shortened.
3. **Marking and masking:** Line with square and pencil, painter's tape along the line on both sides.
4. **Sawing:** Hand-held circular saw with a fine blade on a guide rail, the visible side down because the blade teeth upwards.
5. **Seal the edge** with clear varnish to prevent moisture from penetrating.
6. **Hang and test:** The door runs freely and closes cleanly.

Duration: about an hour. Without the guide rail, the cut would hardly have been straight.

## Common mistakes

| Error | Episode |
|---|---|
| Bands suddenly adjusted significantly | Door hangs the other way |
| Tube chip door shortened too much | Lower frame gone, door unstable |
| Sawn without tape | Frayed surface |
| Cutting edge not sealed | Swelling when exposed to moisture |
| Striker plate offset without marking | New holes in the wrong place |

## Checklist

- Gap dimensions checked all around and grinding points marked.
- Bands adjusted in small increments.
- Check the striking plate with chalk or lipstick.
- Belts cleaned and lubricated.
- Door type and frame width clarified before shortening.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much air does a door need at the bottom?

Usually 5 to 8 mm above the floor covering, a little more for carpet.

### Can I shorten a honeycomb door?

A few millimeters usually yes. If more, the lower frame can be removed; then a new frame strip must be glued in.

### What helps with a door that opens by itself?

Often the door does not hang vertically. Adjust the hinges so that the door leaf is vertical.

### What do I use to lubricate a stuck room door lock?

With graphite powder or a lock spray that is expressly intended for locks. A few pushes into the slot of the latch and into the keyhole, then move the handle and key several times. Cooking oil and thick fat are unsuitable because they gum up and bind dust.

## Conclusion

Grinding and jamming doors can usually be fixed without special tools: Identify the cause by looking at the gap and grinding points, adjust the hinges or lift them with hinge rings, adjust the striking plate with markings, lubricate squeaky hinges. After a new floor, trim the door cleanly and seal the cut edge. Entrance and fire doors belong in specialist hands.
