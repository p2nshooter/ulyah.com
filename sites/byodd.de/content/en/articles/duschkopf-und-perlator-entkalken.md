If the shower jet sprays in all directions or only trickles thinly and the faucet sprays to the side, limescale is usually the cause. In many regions of Germany, the tap water is hard and limescale builds up in the fine openings of shower heads and aerators. This is annoying, but quickly fixed. If you have the opportunity, you can switch to economical models and thus save water and energy for hot water.

These instructions show you how to descale the shower head and aerator, which products are suitable for which surface, how to replace the parts and what you should pay attention to when buying.

## How hard is my water?

Your water supplier specifies the water hardness, usually on their website. In Germany it is often given in hardness ranges:

| Hardness range | Degree of German hardness (°dH) | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Soft | below 8.4 | Little lime |
| Means | 8.4 to 14 | Descale occasionally |
| Hard | over 14 | Descale regularly |

The harder the water, the more often the shower head and aerators need to be cleaned, approximately every one to three months for hard water.

## The aerator: small but important

The aerator (also known as aerator or aerator) is located at the end of the faucet. It mixes air with the water, ensures a soft, non-splashing jet and limits the flow rate in many models. A calcified aerator causes the jet to splash or become weak.

### Remove aerator

1. **Cover the sink** or close the stopper so that no small items disappear down the drain.
2. **Unscrew by hand:** Many aerators can be unscrewed counterclockwise.
3. **With tools:** If it is stuck, wrap a cloth around it and turn it carefully with pliers to protect the chrome surface.
4. **Internal aerators** (on modern faucets) require a special aerator key, which is often included with the faucet or can be purchased cheaply.
5. **Press out insert:** Carefully remove the sieve and inserts from the sleeve. Remember the order or take a photo.

## Descaling: means and methods

| Means | Effect | Suitable for | Be careful |
|---|---|---|---|
| Citric acid (powder, dissolved in warm water) | Very good, odorless | Aerators, shower heads, plastic, chrome (short) | Not hot: above around 40 °C, poorly soluble lime forms |
| Vinegar essence (diluted) | Good | Plastic parts, sieves | Smells strong, can attack rubber seals and some coatings |
| Commercially available descaler | Good | According to the manufacturer's information | Follow the instructions for use |
| Baking powder/baking soda | Low | Only light coverings | |

**The following applies to chrome surfaces:** Only allow acids to take effect for a short time and rinse thoroughly. Long exposure can make the surface dull. Abrasives and steel wool will scratch chrome.

### Descale the aerator

1. Place individual pieces in a container with warm citric acid solution (about a tablespoon to a cup of water).
2. Leave on for 30 minutes to a few hours, depending on the calcification.
3. Clean with an old toothbrush.
4. Rinse thoroughly.
5. Put it back in the correct order and screw it on hand-tight.

### Descale the shower head

**Method 1: Unscrew and insert**

1. Unscrew the shower head from the hose (usually by hand).
2. Place in a bowl with citric acid solution so that the nozzles are covered.
3. Allow to take effect, then clean the nozzles with a toothbrush.
4. Many shower heads have **rubber nozzles:** Simply rub them with your finger and the limescale will crumble out.
5. Rinse and screw back on. Check the seal in the hose connection.

**Method 2: Bag trick for fixed overhead showers**

If the overhead shower cannot be removed: Fill a freezer bag with descaling solution, place it over the shower head and secure it with a rubber band. Allow it to take effect, remove it, clean the nozzles and let the water run.

## Replace parts

Sometimes replacement is more worthwhile than cleaning:

- **Perlators** cost a few euros. When purchasing, pay attention to the thread size (internal or external thread, for example M22 or M24) and design. It's best to take the old aerator with you.
- **Shower heads** come in many variations. The connection is usually standardized (1/2 inch).
- **Check seals** during each assembly and replace if necessary.

## Save water and energy

A large part of the energy in the household goes into heating water. Economical aerators and shower heads reduce the flow without noticeably reducing comfort:

| Component | Usual flow | Economical variant |
|---|---|---|
| shower head | 12 to 15 l/min | 6 to 9 l/min |
| Aerator sink | 8 to 12 l/min | 4 to 6 l/min |
| Aerator kitchen | 10 to 15 l/min | 6 to 8 l/min |

For a four-person household that showers every day, an energy-saving shower head can save tens of thousands of liters of water per year, and thus also the energy to heat this water.

**Caution with instantaneous water heaters and old gas boilers:** Some devices need a minimum flow to start. With very economical shower heads, the water can remain cold. Check manufacturer information.

## Descale the thermostat fitting

If the temperature in the shower fluctuates, the thermostat handle is difficult to turn or only lukewarm water comes out, the thermostat cartridge is often calcified. This is how you do it:

1. **Turn off the water** either at the main shut-off valve or, if available, at the pre-shutoffs in the fitting's connections.
2. **Remove the handle:** There is usually a screw under a cover cap. Mark the position of the handle beforehand so that the temperature scale is correct again later.
3. **Unscrew the cartridge** with a suitable open-end wrench.
4. **Descaling:** Place the cartridge in a lukewarm citric acid solution for one to two hours and carefully brush off any limescale residue.
5. **Care for seals:** Coat the O-rings with some silicone-based fitting grease.
6. **Install,** Put the handle on, slowly turn on the water and check the temperature.

If the cartridge is difficult to move despite cleaning, there are replacement cartridges for most fittings. To do this, note the manufacturer and model of the faucet, which are often listed on the bottom or in the operating instructions. If you are unsure, an installer can help.

## Lime on glass, tiles and natural stone

Shower walls and tiles also quickly develop a gray haze when water is hard. Prevention is most effective: after each shower, dry the glass with a squeegee; this only takes half a minute. Existing limescale stains on glass and glazed tiles can be removed with diluted citric acid or a bathroom cleaner. Be careful with natural stone such as marble, limestone or travertine: These stones are themselves made of lime, and any acid attacks the surface and leaves dull spots. Only use neutral cleaners that are specifically suitable for natural stone.

## Shower heads and legionella

Legionella are bacteria that can multiply in standing warm water between around 25 and 45 °C. They become dangerous when you inhale fine water droplets like those produced when showering. After a long period of absence, such as after vacation, it makes sense to let the hot water in the shower and taps run for a few minutes before showering and not to stand directly over the shower head. Central hot water tanks should be set so that the water in the tank is hot enough; Experts usually recommend at least 60 °C.

## Prevention

- **After showering**, briefly rinse the shower head with cold water and wipe off the nozzles.
- **Descale regularly** before thick layers form.
- **Choose a shower head with rubber nozzles**: They are the easiest to clean.

## Practical example: A four-person household with hard water

A family in a region with hard water is annoyed by a shower head that only has some of the nozzles spraying evenly and a kitchen faucet that sprays. You do this:

1. **Unscrew the shower head** and place it in a warm citric acid solution for two hours. Then rub off the rubber nozzles with your fingers; the lime crumbles out.
2. **Unscrew the aerator in the kitchen** with the included key, soak the inserts in the solution and clean them with a toothbrush.
3. **Decision for economy models:** Because the shower head is already old, it is replaced by a model with 8 liters per minute. There is an aerator at the bathroom sink with 5 liters per minute.
4. **Testing the heater:** A short test shows that the instantaneous water heater starts reliably even with the economy shower head.

Make a note in your calendar to descale the shower head and aerator every two months. Since then, the jet has been consistent and water consumption when showering has noticeably decreased.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I clean the shower head in the dishwasher?

Not recommended. Heat and cleaners can damage plastic and coatings.

### Why does the faucet still spray after descaling?

Inserts may be incorrectly inserted or damaged. A new aerator costs little.

### Is lime harmful?

Not for your health, but it reduces the flow and efficiency of devices such as kettles and washing machines.

### How often should I descale?

Every one to three months for hard water, less often for soft water.

### Further questions from practice

**Does a water softener help?** A central softening system reduces limescale throughout the house, but is an investment with maintenance costs. Regular descaling is sufficient for the shower head and aerator.

**My shower hose is leaking.** The seal in the connection is often missing or hardened. replace gasket; If the hose itself is damaged, buy a new one.

**The thermostat cannot be turned above 38 degrees.** This is usually intentional: a safety lock protects against scalding. The temperature can be increased further by pressing the button on the handle. If the handle can hardly be moved, the cartridge is often calcified.

## Conclusion

Calcified shower heads and aerators can be cleaned quickly: unscrew, soak in warm citric acid solution, clean nozzles with a toothbrush or finger, rinse and reinstall. Treat chrome only briefly with acid; do not use abrasive cleaners. When replacing, it's worth choosing an economical model: it saves water and the energy for warm water without reducing comfort.
