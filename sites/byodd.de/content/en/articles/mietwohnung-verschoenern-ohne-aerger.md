A rental apartment should feel like a home. At the same time, it belongs to the landlord and there can be disputes when moving out if changes cannot be reversed or damage has occurred. The good news: A lot is allowed, and for almost every wish there is a solution that can be dismantled without leaving a trace.

These instructions provide an overview of what tenants are generally allowed to do, when they should ask the landlord, what dismantling ideas there are for walls, floors, kitchen, bathroom and lighting and how to move out without any hassle. It is not a substitute for legal advice; The decisive factors are the rental agreement and, in the event of a dispute, advice from a tenants' association or lawyer.

## Principles

In German tenancy law the following applies roughly:

- **Contractual use:** Everything that is part of normal living is allowed, such as hanging pictures, setting up furniture, attaching lamps, painting walls according to your own taste during the rental period.
- **Structural changes:** Interventions in the building structure, such as breaking through walls, removing tiles, gluing floors firmly, laying pipes, usually require the landlord's **consent.**
- **Obligation to dismantle:** When moving out, the tenant usually has to undo changes that go beyond the contractual use, unless otherwise agreed.
- **Cosmetic repairs:** Whether and when the tenant has to renovate depends on the rental agreement. Many clauses in older contracts are invalid. If in doubt, seek advice.

**Tip:** Get any consent in writing, including by email, and note whether dismantling is required when you move out.

## Which usually happens without consent

| Change | Note |
|---|---|
| Hang pictures, shelves, lamps with dowels | To the usual extent; Often close the dowel holes when moving out |
| Paint walls in color | If necessary, paint back in light, neutral tones when moving out |
| Carpets, loose runners, curtains | No problem |
| Replace lights on existing connections | Keep original lights |
| Furniture and built-in cupboards without a fixed connection | Anti-tip devices are useful and common |
| Replace shower head, aerator, toilet seat | Keep original parts |

## What you should ask for

| Change | Why |
|---|---|
| Paint or stick over tiles | Difficult to dismantle |
| Glue the floor covering firmly or remove the old one | Interference with the substance |
| Holes in tiles or facade | Damage to inventory |
| Cover walls with wallpaper that is difficult to remove | Dismantling is complex |
| Satellite dish, awnings, balcony cladding | Affects facade and common property |
| Change electrical installation | Only by specialists and with consent |
| Remove or move in walls | Structural intervention |

## Deconstructable ideas for walls

- **Color:** The easiest and cheapest change. See [Select wall color](/anleitungen/wandfarbe-auswaehlen/).
- **Removable wallpaper** (self-adhesive or “peel and stick”): on smooth, intact walls. Test on a small area first.
- **Non-woven wallpaper:** can be removed dry when moving out. See [Wallpapering](/anleitungen/tapezieren-raufaser-und-vliestapete/).
- **Picture strips** instead of lots of holes. See [Design a gallery wall](/anleitungen/bilderwand-gestalten/).
- **Adhesive strips and fine picture nails.** See [Hanging pictures without drilling] (/anleitungen/bilder-aufhaengen-ohne-bohren/).
- **Wall panels** with adhesive strips or removable fasteners.
- **Large fabrics or tapestries** on a rod.

## Degradable ideas for floors

- **Large rugs** are the biggest and easiest way to transform a room.
- **Floating click vinyl or laminate** over an existing floor, by arrangement. Remove again when moving out. See [Laying laminate flooring](/anleitungen/laminat-verlegen/) and [Vinyl flooring](/anleitungen/vinylboden-klicken-oder-kleben/).
- **Loosely layable vinyl planks** with non-slip backing (without glue).

## kitchen and bathroom

- **Tile stickers** for the kitchen back wall (by arrangement). See [Painting or sticking tiles](/anleitungen/fliesen-streichen-oder-bekleben/).
- **New handles** on kitchen cabinets; save old handles.
- **Furniture film** for cabinet fronts (choose a quality that can be removed without leaving any residue).
- **Mirror cabinet, shelves and towel holder** with adhesive systems instead of drilling holes in tiles or in the joints.
- **Shower curtain, bathroom textiles and plants** for a quick effect.
- **Renewing silicone joints** is maintenance and usually welcome. See [Renewing silicone joints](/anleitungen/silikonfugen-erneuern/).

## Light

- **Plug lights** and battery-powered lamps.
- **Smart bulbs** in existing sockets.
- **Own ceiling lights** to existing connections (keep originals).
- See [Planning light in the apartment](/anleitungen/licht-planen-in-der-wohnung/).

## window

## Balcony power plant in the rental apartment

Plug-in solar devices, often called balcony power plants, have been expressly favored in tenancy law since October 2024. Since then, tenants have generally been entitled to have the landlord agree to the installation. However, he is allowed to have a say in how and where the device is attached, for example so that the facade is not damaged and the modules are hanging in a storm-proof manner. Therefore, ask in advance in writing and describe the module size, holder and installation location. The devices must also be registered in the market master data register, which can now be easily done online. Inverters up to 800 volt amperes are intended for connection to a normal socket.

## The minor repair clause

Many rental agreements contain a clause that requires tenants to pay for small repairs themselves, such as taps, light switches, door handles or window handles. Such clauses are only effective under certain conditions: they may only affect parts with which the tenant comes into frequent contact, they require a maximum limit per repair, which courts usually consider to be permissible around 100 euros, and an additional upper limit per year. If one of these limits is missing, the clause is usually invalid. Even an effective clause only obliges you to pay, not to repair it yourself.

## Smoke detector

In all federal states, smoke detectors are required for apartments, at least in bedrooms, children's rooms and hallways that serve as escape routes. The landlord is usually responsible for installation. Who has to carry out the regular testing varies depending on the federal state. Even when renovating, do not permanently remove smoke detectors and never paint them as paint can close the openings.

## Document when moving in

A **handover protocol** with photos protects you when you move out:

- Condition of walls, floors, windows, doors, bathroom and kitchen.
- Existing damage, scratches, stains, dowel holes.
- Meter readings.
- Have it signed by the landlord.

## The excerpt

1. **Check contract:** What renovation obligations really apply? If you are unsure, seek advice.
2. **Dismantling:** Remove things you have installed yourself unless otherwise agreed.
3. **Close dowel holes** if required. See [Filling holes in the wall](/anleitungen/loecher-in-der-wand-spachteln/).
4. **Strong colors** paint over lightly if necessary.
5. **Reinstall original parts** (lights, shower head, handles).
6. **Clean thoroughly.**
7. **Handover with protocol** and photos.

## Deposit and apartment inspection

The rental deposit may amount to a maximum of three net rents and can be paid in three monthly installments. After moving out, the landlord may retain a portion for a reasonable period of time, for example for an outstanding utility bill. Go to the inspection with the move-in report and your photos, check room by room together and have any deviations recorded in writing. Don't sign anything that you think is wrong, but rather note your different point of view in the minutes.

## Takeovers from the previous tenant

Anyone who takes over a fitted kitchen, floor covering or built-in cupboards from the previous tenant often also assumes their dismantling obligation. Therefore, clarify with the landlord in writing before taking over whether the installations can remain in the apartment when you move out or must be removed. A short sentence in the handover protocol saves a lot of later arguments and sometimes high disposal costs.

## Practical example: An old building apartment in two weekends

A student moves into a two-room apartment in an old building with white woodchip walls, gray PVC flooring in the kitchen and brown tiles in the bathroom. The landlord allows changes if they can be reversed when moving out. Your plan:

| space | Measure | Dismantling when moving out |
|---|---|---|
| Living room | One wall painted in warm sage green, large carpet, picture rail | Paint the wall white, unscrew the strip, fill the holes |
| bedroom | Curtains on brackets, plug-in lights on the bed | Nothing |
| Kitchen | Loosely laid vinyl planks over the PVC, new handles | Pick up planks, install old handles |
| bathroom | Tile stickers only on the wall above the sink, new shower head | Remove the sticker with a hairdryer and install the old shower head |

Everything is coordinated with the landlord by email and documented with photos in the handover protocol when you move in. The original parts are labeled and stored in a box in the basement.

## Checklist for tenants

- Rental agreement checked for clauses regarding renovation and minor repairs.
- Handover protocol with photos when moving in.
- Consent for structural changes in writing.
- Original parts kept labeled.
- Degradable products selected.
- Dismantling planned when moving out.

## One more question

### Do I have to undo changes allowed by the landlord when I move out?

That depends on the agreement. Therefore, when giving your consent, state in writing whether dismantling is required or the change can remain.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I drill in the rented apartment?

To the usual extent, yes, for pictures, shelves, lamps and curtains. If possible, into the joints in tiles.

### Do I have to paint everything white when I move out?

Not necessarily white, but often in light, neutral colors if the apartment has been decorated with bold colors. The contract is decisive.

### Who pays if something breaks while doing DIY work?

Basically the culprit. Private liability insurance can cover damage to the rental property, depending on the contract.

### Can the landlord simply prohibit changes?

He does not need to tolerate structural changes. Tenants may be entitled to consent for certain measures, such as accessibility or charging electric cars.

### Can I replace the door lock?

You can usually replace the lock cylinder on the apartment door, for example if a key is lost. Keep the old cylinder, reinstall it when you move out and return all keys.

### Further questions from practice

**Can I design a balcony?** Plants, furniture, carpets and fairy lights usually yes. Privacy screens, awnings or drilling into the facade usually require approval because they change the appearance of the house.

**Can I connect a washing machine or dishwasher?** Setting up common household appliances is part of normal use. The connection must be professional and tight; If in doubt, hire an installer.

## Conclusion

More is allowed in a rented apartment than many people think: painting, hanging pictures, changing lamps and moving furniture are all part of normal living. Obtain written consent for structural changes. Use removable solutions such as removable wallpaper, carpets, adhesive systems, floating floors and plug-in lights, document the condition when you move in and plan for dismantling when you move out.
