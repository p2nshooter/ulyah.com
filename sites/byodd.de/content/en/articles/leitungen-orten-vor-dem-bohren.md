A drilled power cable or a punctured water pipe is one of the most unpleasant experiences when doing DIY. In the best case scenario, the fuse blows and you need an electrician. In the worst case scenario, you get an electric shock or water runs into the wall, ceiling and apartment below you. The good news: With knowledge of the installation zones, a tracking device and a little care, this risk can be greatly reduced.

This guide explains where pipes normally run in walls, how locating devices work and what their limits are, how to check a wall step by step and what to do if something does happen.

## Installation zones: Where cables should run

In Germany, the DIN 18015-3 standard specifies in which areas of a wall electrical cables should be laid. These so-called installation zones are horizontal and vertical strips:

| zone | Location | width |
|---|---|---|
| Upper horizontal zone | 15 to 45 cm below the ceiling | 30cm |
| Middle horizontal zone | 90 to 130 cm above the floor, especially in kitchens and on work surfaces | 40cm |
| Lower horizontal zone | 15 to 45 cm above the floor | 30cm |
| Vertical zones | 10 to 30 cm next to doors, windows and room corners | 20cm |

In addition, cables run vertically above and below sockets and switches because they connect them to the horizontal zones.

### The limits of the rule

The installation zones are an important guide, but not a guarantee:

- **Older buildings** were often installed before the standard was introduced. In old buildings, pipes sometimes run diagonally or across the wall.
- **Subsequent installations** by previous owners do not always follow the rules.
- **Water and heating pipes** do not have similarly fixed zones. They usually run near bathrooms, kitchens, radiators and riser shafts.
- **Underfloor heating and ceiling pipes** are a separate topic: Only drill into floors and ceilings if you know the location for sure.

## How tracking devices work

Locating devices for do-it-yourselfers usually combine several measuring principles:

| Measuring principle | Finds | Boundaries |
|---|---|---|
| AC voltage detection | Live power cables | No disconnected cables; False alarms in damp walls |
| Metal sensor (inductive) | Metal pipes, rebar, metal studs, nails | plastic pipes not; Depth limited |
| Capacitive measurement | Wooden beams and studs in drywall, some plastic pipes with water | Susceptible to failure with damp or dense building materials |
| Radar sensor (on professional devices) | Different materials in greater depth | Expensive |

A good do-it-yourself device costs between €30 and €100, while professional devices with radar cost significantly more.

### What tracking devices cannot do

- **Cables that are switched off or not loaded** are not detected by the voltage sensor. The device only finds them if they are metallic enough for the metal sensor.
- **Plastic water pipes** are invisible to many devices without water.
- **Deep pipes** in thick walls are often out of reach.
- **Damp walls** and walls with metal mesh or foil lead to false readings.

Therefore: The tracking device is an aid, not a free ticket.

## Step by step: Check a wall

1. **Calibrate the device:** Switch it on and calibrate according to the manufacturer's instructions at a point on the wall where nothing runs.
2. **Mark installation zones:** Use a pencil or painter's tape to mark the zones around doors, windows, corners, sockets and switches.
3. **Scan slowly:** Move the device flat and evenly over the wall, first horizontally, then vertically. Mark the locations with masking tape.
4. **Use different modes:** If the device has multiple modes, go through each one individually.
5. **Check plausibility:** Does a line shown run to a socket, a switch or a radiator? That speaks for a line.
6. **Choose drilling point:** if possible outside of the zones and at least a few centimeters away from each location.
7. **Limit drilling depth:** Only drill as deep as necessary. Cables are often a few centimeters beneath the plaster.

## Additional security measures

- **Turn off circuit:** If you need to drill close to an outlet or switch, turn off the associated fuse. Use a lamp to test whether the socket really has no power.
- **Check residual current circuit breaker (FI):** It protects when live parts are touched. Many old buildings do not yet have one; this is a reason for particular caution.
- **Use plans:** Some landlords, property managers or developers have installation plans. Ask.
- **Photos from the construction phase:** Anyone who is renovating or building should take photos of walls before plastering. This will help years later.
- **Being able to shut off water:** Know where your home's main faucet is.

## Emergency: Pipe drilled

### power line

1. **Do not touch the drill and immediately release the machine** if it is still running and sparks or a bang occurs.
2. **Turn off the fuse** or, if unclear, the main switch.
3. **Do not repair it yourself.** A damaged cable must be professionally repaired by a qualified electrician. A hole covered with plaster over a damaged cable is a fire hazard.
4. **In case of electric shock:** Call 112, even if the affected person feels well.

### water pipe

1. **Leave the drill stuck:** It partially closes the hole.
2. **Close the main tap** in the apartment or in the basement.
3. **Collect water** and unplug nearby electrical appliances.
4. **Notify neighbors below** if water may have leaked into the ceiling.
5. **Call the installer** and inform the property management or landlord. For rental apartments, liability insurance is also included.

### Gas pipe

Gas pipes rarely run unprotected in living spaces, but if you smell gas: don't turn on any switches, don't fire, open the windows, leave the apartment, call the gas supplier's emergency service from outside.

## Drywall: Studs and Conduits

In plasterboard walls, cables usually run through openings in the vertical metal or wooden studs. The stands are usually spaced at fixed intervals, often 62.5 centimeters, and are narrower if the walls are subject to greater loads. Once you've found a stand, such as a strong magnet that sticks to the screw heads or the locator, you can usually estimate the rest. Cables often run horizontally from stand to stand at the level of sockets and switches or vertically directly next to a stand. For pictures, shelves and wall cabinets in drywall, you need suitable cavity dowels, as described in [Select and place dowels correctly](/anleitungen/duebel-richtig-waehlen-und-setzen/).

## Know circuits and search for lines specifically

Label the fuses in your fuse box if you haven't already: Turn them off one at a time and note which rooms, outlets, and lights go dark. This way you know exactly which circuit you need to switch off before drilling. Professionals also use line finders with a transmitter and receiver: The transmitter is connected to a socket and feeds a signal into the line, which the receiver tracks through the plaster, even when the power is switched off. Such devices can be rented in some hardware stores and tool rental stores.

## Be careful with bathrooms, kitchens and pre-wall installations

Water pipes run vertically from below or above to fittings, cisterns and radiators. Therefore, keep enough distance from all connections at the sides and never drill directly above or below a fitting. Behind a wall-mounted toilet there is usually a pre-wall installation with a cistern, supply and waste water pipes and fastenings. Drilling into this wall is only carried out after careful inspection, ideally with the manufacturer's documents. Even in kitchens, there are often more pipes running behind the sink and dishwasher than you might expect.

## Mistakes that often happen

| Error | Better |
|---|---|
| Drill directly above an electrical outlet | Move to the side, avoid the zone |
| Just trust the voltage display | Additionally use metal and capacity mode |
| Do not calibrate the device | Calibrate according to manufacturer's instructions |
| Drill without depth stop | Limit depth |
| Fill drilled cable | Call an electrician |

## Frequently asked questions

### How deep are the pipes in the wall?

Usually only a few centimeters deep under plaster. That's why even a short hole is dangerous.

### Can a tracking device find plastic pipes?

Filled with water sometimes in capacitive mode, empty usually not. With underfloor heating, a thermal imaging camera helps while the heating is running.

### Can I drill into the ceiling?

Cables for lamps often run in concrete ceilings, and sometimes also for underfloor heating in the apartment above. Check with the locator and only drill as deep as necessary.

### Who is liable if I, as a tenant, drill into a pipe?

Usually the culprit. Private liability insurance can cover the damage. Report him to the landlord immediately.

## Checklist before drilling

- Installation zones marked.
- Tracking device calibrated, all modes used.
- Locations marked with masking tape.
- Drilling point chosen at a distance from all locations.
- Depth stop set.
- Fuse and main tap known.
- Put on safety glasses.

### Further questions from practice

**Why does my tracking device beep everywhere?** Often it is not calibrated correctly, or the wall contains moisture, metal mesh in the plaster or aluminum foil behind insulation. Recalibrate, start somewhere else and check the modes individually. If the display remains flat, the device is unsuitable for this wall; then installation zones, plans and switching off the circuit will help.

**Can I find lines with my smartphone?** Some apps use the phone's magnetic field sensor and react to metal at a shallow depth. This is a gimmick, but not a replacement for a tracking device.

**What about drilling near underfloor heating?** Never drill in heated screeds without knowing exactly where the pipes are. A thermal imaging camera shows the pipes when the heating is running. If in doubt, ask a specialist company.

**How ​​do I document pipes for later?** After renovations, take photos of the open walls with a ruler in the picture and store them in a folder for each room. This will help you and future residents.

**Do I need to be particularly careful with sockets in the outside wall?** Yes, especially with insulated facades or facing shells. There, cables can run in unusual paths and in the insulation. Only drill if you know the location and use appropriate fasteners for the wall construction.

## Conclusion

Cables usually run in the standardized installation zones, but not always. Mark the zones, check the wall with a calibrated locating device in all modes, keep your distance from discovery sites, limit the drilling depth and, if in doubt, switch off the circuit. If something does happen, the following applies: turn off the electricity or water, call experts, and don't fill anything up.
