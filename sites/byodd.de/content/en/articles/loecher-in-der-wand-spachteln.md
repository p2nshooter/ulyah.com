Dowel holes after moving out, a crack above the door, a dent from a chair that has been pushed against the wall for years, or a fist-sized hole in the plasterboard wall: holes and damage in walls are among the most common household repairs. With the right filler and a little patience, almost all damage can be repaired so that it is no longer visible after painting.

These instructions show which filler is suitable for which damage, how to repair small holes, cracks and larger breakouts in plaster and plasterboard and how to prepare the area for painting.

## Which filler for what?

| product | Properties | Suitable for |
|---|---|---|
| Finished filler (tube or bucket) | Ready to use, dries slowly, shrinks slightly | Small dowel holes, nail holes, fine cracks |
| Plaster putty (powder) | Mix with water, hardens quickly, hardly shrinks | Larger holes and breakouts in gypsum plaster and plasterboard |
| Filling putty or repair putty | Fills deep holes in one go | Deep holes, eruptions |
| Fine filler or surface filler | Very fine, smooth surface | Final layer, smoothing large areas |
| Acrylic (cartridge) | Elastic, paintable | Cracks that move, for example between the wall and ceiling, joints on door frames |
| Lime or cement plaster | For damp rooms and lime plasters | Bathrooms, old buildings with lime plaster, outdoor area |

**Important:** Do not fill acrylic over large areas. It is intended for joints and moving cracks. For rigid cracks and holes, use plaster or ready-made filler.

## tool

- Spatula in two widths: narrow (3 to 5 cm) and wide (10 to 15 cm).
- Japanese spatula set for fine work.
- Small bucket or mixing cup, for plaster a rubber cup.
- Sanding block with sandpaper P120 and P180 or sanding grid.
- Brush and water to moisten.
- Cutter knife.
- For plasterboard: leftover piece of plasterboard, jigsaw or plasterboard saw, fabric tape.
- Dust mask and safety glasses when sanding.

## Small holes: dowel and nail holes

1. **Remove the dowel:** Grab a screw and pull it out. If it gets stuck, turn it with pliers or carefully drill it out with a slightly larger drill. Alternatively, press the dowel a little deeper into the wall and fill it over.
2. **Clean hole:** Remove loose parts and dust, blow out.
3. **Wet the edge slightly:** With a wet brush. This prevents the surface from absorbing water from the filler too quickly.
4. **Fill the spatula:** Use the narrow spatula to press the mixture firmly into the hole so that no air remains trapped.
5. **Smooth off:** Pull the spatula flat over the area. With ready-made filler, overfill slightly because it shrinks slightly when it dries.
6. **Let dry:** Depending on the depth, a few hours.
7. **Re-filling if necessary:** If a depression is created, apply a second thin coat.
8. **Sanding:** Sand slightly flush with P180.

## Hairline cracks and cracks

### Fine cracks in the plaster

1. Use the cutter knife to widen the crack slightly into a V shape so that the filler can hold.
2. Remove dust and moisten edge.
3. Work in the finished filler or plaster filler and peel it off across the crack.
4. Sand after drying.

### Cracks that keep coming back

Cracks at transitions between walls and ceilings, on door frames or between different building materials are often caused by movements of the building. Helps here:

- **Acrylic** in the joint, removed with a wet finger or joint smoother.
- **Fabric or reinforcing tape** for cracks in the surface: Prepare the crack, embed the tape, fill it over.
- **Determine cause:** Wide or growing cracks, especially slanted cracks at window corners or cracks that you can see through, may indicate settlement or structural problems. Consult a specialist here.

## Larger holes in the plaster

1. **Remove loose parts:** Use the spatula to scrape off anything that is not firmly attached.
2. **Fill deep holes in several layers:** Mix plaster or filling putty and apply it in layers of no more than 1 to 2 cm, otherwise the mixture will crack. For very deep holes, first level them with plaster or filling putty.
3. **Wet** between coats.
4. **Generously smooth the last layer over the edge with the wide spatula** and allow the edges to become thin.
5. **Sand and check with grazing light:** A lamp held flat against the wall will show any unevenness.

### Mix plaster correctly

- **Sprinkle plaster of paris into the water,** not the other way around, until small islands form.
- **Wait briefly,** then stir without air bubbles.
- **Only mix small amounts:** Plaster putty has a processing time of around 30 to 60 minutes. Do not “refresh” the mixture that has started with water.
- **Clean tools:** Remains of hardened plaster accelerate setting.

## Holes in plasterboard

### Small holes up to about 5 cm

Fill with plaster putty. For holes larger than 1 cm, a self-adhesive repair grid made of metal or plastic, which is glued over the hole and filled over, helps.

### Big holes

1. **Cut out a rectangular hole:** Cut a clean rectangle around the damage using a plasterboard saw or utility knife.
2. **Back up:** Push two wooden slats into the hole and attach them to the remaining cardboard using drywall screws. They serve as a support.
3. **Cut fitting piece:** Cut a piece from a leftover piece of plasterboard of the same thickness (usually 12.5 mm) that fits exactly into the hole.
4. **Screwing in:** Place the piece in the opening and screw it onto the slats. Slightly countersink screw heads without tearing the box.
5. **Fill the joints:** Fill the joints with plaster filler, embed fabric or paper tape, allow to dry.
6. **Apply:** Spread over a wide area in two to three thin layers, allowing the edges to fade.
7. **Sand and prime.**

## Preparation for painting

Filler areas absorb paint differently than the rest of the wall. Without pretreatment, they appear as matt or shiny spots (“filler islands”) after painting. Remedy:

- **Pre-paint filler areas with deep primer** or diluted wall paint.
- **Paint entire wall twice.** See [Paint Wall](/anleitungen/wand-streichen-anleitung/).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Episode | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Filled too thickly in one go | Cracks, long drying, shrinkage | Work in shifts |
| Substrate not moistened | Spatula tears, sticks poorly | Pre-wet with a wet brush |
| Acrylic filled over a large area | Can't be sanded, shines through | Acrylic for joints only |
| Edges not drawn out | Visible edge under the paint | Use a wide spatula to spread out thinly |
| Painted without primer | Spotty result | Pre-paint filler areas |
| Plaster “revived” with water | Mass doesn't hold | Mix small amounts fresh |

## Chipped corners and edges

Outside corners of walls get a lot of wear and tear in everyday life: vacuum cleaners, suitcases, strollers. Fill small chips with plaster putty and smooth the edge with a wide spatula or a board as a stop. If a corner is severely damaged or is repeatedly hit, it is worth using an edge protection profile made of metal or plastic. It is embedded in plaster or ready-made filler, aligned and then filled over so that it is hardly visible after painting. For hallways with a lot of traffic, there are also visible corner protection strips that are just glued on.

## Damp spots and white efflorescence

If an area of ​​the wall is damp, bubbles form or white, crystalline efflorescence appears, filling will not help. The efflorescence is usually salts that are transported out of the masonry with moisture. No filler will stick to it permanently. First, clarify the cause, such as rising damp, a leak or condensation, and have larger damage treated by a specialist company with suitable renovation plaster. More about moisture in living spaces can be found in [Removing and preventing mold from the wall](/anleitungen/schimmel-an-der-wand-entfernen-und-vorbeugen/).

## Adjust the structure: woodchip and structural plaster

On smooth walls, a patch of filler disappears after painting. On the other hand, a smooth island is noticeable on woodchip or textured plaster. With woodchip, it helps to tear a piece of leftover wallpaper to fit, not cut it, and stick it to the sanded area; the torn edges are hardly noticeable. There are repair compounds for structural plaster whose surface can be adapted to the surroundings using a sponge, brush or spatula. Practice the technique on a piece of cardboard beforehand.

## Filling when moving out of the rental apartment

Many rental agreements require that dowel holes be professionally closed when moving out, at least if they go beyond the usual size. This can be done quickly with ready-made filler, sandpaper and some wall paint. On tiles that cannot be filled, grouting in a matching color or special repair waxes can help. More on this topic in [Beautify your rental apartment without any hassle](/anleitungen/mietwohnung-verschoenern-ohne-aerger/).

|---|---|---|
| Ten dowel holes | 20 to 30 minutes | a few hours |
| One meter crack | 30 minutes | a day |
| Hole in the plaster of 10 cm | 1 hour in several steps | a day |
| Hole in plasterboard of 20 cm | 1 to 2 hours | one to two days |

## Practical example: Extract after five years

When moving out of a rented apartment, around 40 dowel holes have to be closed, as well as two deep holes from a TV bracket. The small holes are filled with ready-made putty, the large ones with plaster putty in two layers. After sanding, the areas are primed with thinned wall paint and the walls are painted twice white. The handover of the apartment went smoothly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use toothpaste instead of spatula?

Maybe as a stopgap solution for tiny nail holes, but toothpaste shrinks, discolors and doesn't last long. Ready-made filler costs little and is the better choice.

### How long does putty have to dry before I paint?

Thin layers of finish filler take a few hours, deeper fillings take up to 24 hours. Plaster filler sets more quickly, but should also dry thoroughly.

### Why did my putty spot collapse?

Finished filler shrinks as it dries. For deep holes, work in several layers or use plaster or filling putty.

### Do I always have to use a new piece of plasterboard?

Only for larger holes. Small holes can be closed with a spatula and repair grid.

## Checklist

- Appropriate filler chosen for size and surface.
- Loose parts removed, hole cleaned and moistened.
- Filled in layers, edges thinly drawn.
- Thoroughly dried, sanded, checked with grazing light.
- Filler areas primed, then painted.

## Conclusion

Ready-made filler is sufficient for small holes, plaster or filling filler in several layers is sufficient for larger and deep damage, and acrylic for movable joints. Large holes in plasterboard are repaired with an inserted fitting. Moisten the surface, work in thin layers, spread the edges wide, sand cleanly and prime the areas before painting. Then the damage disappears under the paint.
