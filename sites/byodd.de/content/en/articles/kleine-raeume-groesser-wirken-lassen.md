A 12 square meter bedroom, a narrow hallway, a one-room apartment where living, working and sleeping come together: small rooms are the norm in many cities. You cannot increase their area, but you can increase their effect. With color, light, the right choice of furniture and clever storage space, small rooms appear more open, brighter and calmer.

This guide collects the most effective tricks and explains why they work.

## Basic principle: peace and light

A room appears large when the eye can wander through it undisturbed and when it is bright. Small rooms appear cramped when they are cluttered, dark and restless. Almost all tricks therefore follow three rules:

1. **More light** (natural and artificial).
2. **Less visual clutter** (colors, patterns, objects).
3. **More visible floor and more height.**

## color

### Bright colors

Light walls reflect light and allow room boundaries to recede. Off-white, light gray, sand or delicate pastel tones have a further effect than pure, cold white, which quickly looks gray and cool in northern rooms.

### wall and ceiling

- **Ceiling lighter than the walls** or in the same color: the room appears higher.
- **Pull the wall paint up to the ceiling** or paint the ceiling as well: The border becomes blurred and the room appears higher.
- **Colored ceiling** only in high rooms; in low rooms it is oppressive.

### Tone on tone

Walls, large furniture and curtains in similar colors make the room appear calmer and larger. Strong contrasts divide him.

### Accent wall?

A darker accent wall at the front of a long, narrow room visually brings out this wall and makes the room appear wider. More about this in [Select wall color](/anleitungen/wandfarbe-auswaehlen/).

## Light

- **Keep windows clear:** No heavy curtains in front of the window, no furniture in front of them.
- **Mount the curtain rod higher and wider:** Floor-length, light-colored curtains on a rod just below the ceiling that protrude significantly beyond the window make the window and room appear larger. See [Assemble the curtain rod](/anleitungen/vorhangstange-montieren/).
- **Multiple light sources** instead of a ceiling lamp: Light in the corners and on the walls expands the room. See [Planning light in the apartment](/anleitungen/licht-planen-in-der-wohnung/).
- **Indirect light** on the ceiling makes it appear higher.

## Mirror

A large mirror reflects light and space and can almost visually double a room.

- **Hang opposite or to the side of the window** to reflect natural light.
- **Large format mirrors** lean on the floor (secure against tipping over).
- **Mirror cabinet doors** in the bedroom or hallway.
- **Not directly opposite the bed** if that is perceived as restless.

## First plan, then buy

Many small rooms seem cramped because a single piece of furniture doesn't fit: the sofa protrudes into the walkway, the closet door hits the bed. A simple plan prevents this:

1. **Measure the floor plan:** Draw walls, windows, doors, radiators, sockets and light switches on graph paper at a scale of 1:20. A box of 5 mm then corresponds to 10 cm.
2. **Mark pivoting areas:** Where do room doors, window sashes and cupboard doors open? These areas must remain free, otherwise the furniture will later block everyday life.
3. **Cut out furniture:** Cut out each planned piece of furniture out of paper to scale and move it until walkways and pivoting areas fit.
4. **Test on the floor:** Tape the outline of a new piece of furniture to the floor with painter's tape and live with it for a day or two. Only then will it become apparent whether it feels tight.
5. **Check transport route:** Measure the stairwell, apartment door and hallway corners before ordering a large item.

## Furniture

### Furniture with legs

Sofas, armchairs, dressers and beds on slim legs leave the floor visible underneath. Visible flooring appears spacious.

### Few, matching furniture

- **It's better to have a larger sofa than three small armchairs.** Many small pieces of furniture seem restless.
- **Note scale:** Bulky furniture overwhelms small rooms.
- **Transparent furniture** made of glass or acrylic (coffee table, chairs) hardly takes up any visual weight.

### Multifunctional furniture

- **Bed with bed box** or drawers.
- **Folding table on the wall** or extendable dining table.
- **Stool with storage space.**
- **Sofa bed** in the study or guest room.
- **Secretary** instead of a large desk.

### Arrangement

- **Keep walking paths clear,** at least 70 to 80 cm.
- **Furniture along walls** and leave the center of the room free.
- **Bundle tall furniture along one wall** instead of spreading it around the room.

## Storage space in height

- **Floor-height cupboards:** A cupboard up to the ceiling appears quieter than a half-height cupboard with stuff on top and offers more space.
- **Wall shelves** above doors and windows. See [Build a wall shelf](/anleitungen/regal-bauen-und-sicher-an-der-wand-befestigen/).
- **Handleless fronts** in wall color: The cupboard almost disappears.
- **Under the bed** Boxes with wheels.
- **Door backs** for hooks and organizers.

## Order as a spatial trick

Nothing makes a small room feel cramped as quickly as open shelves full of things.

- **Closed storage space** for everyday stuff, open shelves only for selected decorations.
- **Keep surfaces clear:** Dressers, window sills, countertops.
- **Sort out regularly.**
- **Baskets and boxes** in a uniform color.

## Textiles and patterns

- **Large rug** instead of several small ones: it brings the room together. The front legs of the sofa and armchairs can rest on the carpet.
- **Quiet patterns:** Small, restless patterns make rooms appear narrower. It's better to go for monochrome or generous patterns in light tones.
- **Longitudinally striped rugs** in narrow rooms lengthwise increase depth; When placed horizontally, they visually widen.

## floor

- **Uniform floor covering** in adjacent rooms without thresholds makes the apartment appear larger.
- **Planks laid along the length of the room** emphasize the depth. See [Laying laminate flooring](/anleitungen/laminat-verlegen/).
- **Light floors** reflect more light.

## Special rooms: sloping ceiling, small bathroom, narrow kitchen

| space | What brings the most |
|---|---|
| Room with a sloping ceiling | Paint slopes and walls the same light color so that the crease line disappears; low furniture and drawers under the slope, walkways and high cupboards in the area with full headroom |
| Small bathroom | Glass shower screen instead of shower curtain, mirror cabinet instead of single mirror plus shelf, towels in a light color, cosmetics in closed boxes |
| Narrow kitchen | Upper cabinets up to the ceiling, light under the upper cabinets, rarely used devices in the cabinets instead of on the work surface |
| Work corner in the living room or bedroom | Desk in the color of the wall, documents in a closed cupboard compartment, cables bundled in a cable duct on the wall, screen on a monitor arm instead of on a wide stand |

If you have a sloping roof, it's worth taking a look at the knee floor, the low wall under the sloping ceiling: custom-made drawers or a continuous low shelf use the otherwise dead area and keep the remaining space free.

Dark, patterned tiles make small bathrooms particularly cramped. If renovation is not an option, they can be given a light makeover, see [Painting or sticking old tiles](/anleitungen/fliesen-streichen-oder-bekleben/).

## Example: A 12 m² bedroom

- Walls in light sand tone, ceiling white.
- Bed with bed box on legs, in the middle of the long wall.
- Floor-to-ceiling cupboard with handleless fronts in the color of the wall, a door with a mirror.
- Narrow bedside tables as wall consoles.
- Reading lamps as wall lights with plugs.
- Floor-length, light linen curtains on a rod just below the ceiling.

## Common mistakes

| Error | Better |
|---|---|
| Lots of small furniture | Less but suitable furniture |
| Dark, heavy curtains | Bright, floor-length curtains hung high |
| Just a ceiling lamp | Multiple light sources |
| Open shelves full of stuff | Closed storage space |
| Small carpet | Large rug that fills the room |

## Practical example: A 28 m² apartment with three zones

An apartment with 28 square meters should be a living room, bedroom and workplace at the same time. The resident implements these measures:

| zone | Solution |
|---|---|
| Sleep | Bed with bed box on the long wall, separated by an open, half-height shelf that serves as a room divider and bedside table |
| Housing | Compact sofa on legs, round glass coffee table, large light carpet |
| Work | Folding wall secretary next to the window that is closed after work |
| Storage space | Floor-to-ceiling built-in wardrobe with handle-free fronts in the color of the wall next to the entrance door |
| Light | Three plug-in lights, a strip on the shelf, warm white light |

The walls are painted a warm, light sandy tone, the floor-length curtains hang on a rod just below the ceiling, and a large mirror hangs opposite the window. Visitors regularly estimate the apartment to be significantly larger than it is.

## Where to start: Order by effort

Not every measure costs the same amount of time and money. This sequence quickly has a visible effect:

1. **Sort out and clear areas:** costs nothing and works immediately.
2. **Add light:** one or two plug-in lights for the dark corners.
3. **Swap curtains** and hang higher.
4. **Painting walls:** a weekend of work with a big impact, see [Painting walls](/anleitungen/wand-streichen-anleitung/).
5. **Replace furniture:** only at the end, when it is clear what is really missing.

In a rented apartment, the first three steps are unproblematic. What applies when painting and drilling and what counts when moving out is explained in [Beautify your rental apartment without any hassle](/anleitungen/mietwohnung-verschoenern-ohne-aerger/).

## Checklist

- Bright, calm tones, bright ceiling.
- Multiple light sources, windows free.
- Large mirror opposite or next to the window.
- Few pieces of furniture on legs.
- Storage space at height and behind closed fronts.
- Large carpet, calm textiles.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is white always the best color for small spaces?

Not always. In dark northern rooms, pure white often appears gray. Warm, light tones are better.

### Can dark colors work in small spaces?

Yes, used consciously: A dark, uniform color concept blurs edges and can make a small room appear cozy and less limited. But that requires good light.

### How big should a mirror be?

As big as possible. Small mirrors act as decoration, large ones as an extension of the room.

### Further questions from practice

**Do photo wallpapers with landscapes help?** A large-format, calm photo wallpaper with depth, such as a forest or a horizon, can visually open up a room. Small-scale motifs have the opposite effect.

**Aren't curtains too much in small rooms?** Bright, light curtains, hung high and wide, make windows appear larger. Heavy, dark fabrics directly on the window frame make the room cramped.

**How ​​do I design a narrow hallway?** Light walls, a large mirror on the long wall, wall lights instead of a ceiling light and a narrow wardrobe with closed cupboards. A lengthwise runner emphasizes the depth, while cross-striped carpets visually widen the space.

**How ​​much decoration can a small room tolerate?** Fewer, but larger pieces have a calmer effect than many small ones. Group small items on a tray or in a shelf rather than scattering them around the room, and give each wall at most one focal point.

**Is a loft bed worth it?** In rooms with a height of around 2.60 m or more, a loft bed can create space for a desk or sofa underneath.

## Conclusion

Small rooms appear larger when they are bright, quiet and open. Go for bright, calm colors, lots of light from multiple sources, large mirrors, few pieces of furniture on legs, storage space at height and closed cupboards. Bright curtains hung high and wide, a large carpet and continuous floors complete the concept.
