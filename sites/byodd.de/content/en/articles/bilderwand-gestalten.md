A picture wall, also known as a gallery wall, turns an empty wall into a personal eye-catcher: family photos, art prints, postcards, small mirrors and mementos tell a story together. At the same time, it is one of the projects with the most potential to go wrong: pictures hang too high, the spacing is uneven, the arrangement seems random, and in the end the wall is full of unnecessary holes.

This guide shows you how to plan a gallery wall that suits your space, how to test the arrangement without a single hole, and how to hang all the pictures straight and at the right distance.

## Step 1: Choose the location

Good places for a gallery wall:

- **Above the sofa:** The classic position. The picture wall should take up about two thirds of the width of the sofa.
- **Above a dresser or sideboard.**
- **In the hallway or stairwell:** Ideal for family photos.
- **Above the bed** (attach pictures securely, no heavy frames over the headboard).
- **At the dining table.**

## Step 2: Set the style and frame

A gallery wall looks harmonious when at least one element connects the images:

| Connecting element | Effect |
|---|---|
| Same frames (color and material) | Calm, orderly, even with different motifs |
| Same passe-partouts | Elegant, gallery-like |
| Uniform color world of the motifs | Harmonious, even with mixed frames |
| Same theme (travel, botany, black and white) | Tells a story |
| Mixed style | Lively, eclectic, needs a good eye |

**Tip:** If you are unsure, start with the same frame in two or three sizes and different motifs.

## Step 3: Choose arrangement

### Grid

Frames of the same size in rows and columns with exactly the same spacing. Very neat and modern, but requires precise hanging.

### Central axis

All pictures are aligned along an imaginary horizontal line: some hang above it, others below it. Seems organized even though the formats are different.

### Free arrangement (salon hanging)

Different formats are grouped into an overall form, often around a large main image. The outer silhouette results in a rough rectangle.

### Top edge or bottom edge

All pictures hang with the top edge or the bottom edge on a line. Good for hallways and long walls.

## Step 4: Test with paper templates

The most important trick to avoid holes in the wrong place:

1. **Place each frame on wrapping paper or newspaper** and trace the outline. Cut out.
2. **Mark the hanging point:** Turn the frame over, measure the distance from the top edge to the hanger and mark it on the template.
3. **Lay out on the floor first:** Arrange the stencils on the floor in front of the wall until you like the composition. Take a photo.
4. **Then stick it to the wall with painter's tape.** Leave it hanging for a few days and look at it from different angles.
5. **Adjust** until everything is right.

## Step 5: Spacing and Height

- **Distance between the frames:** 5 to 8 cm works harmoniously for small and medium-sized pictures, and up to 10 cm for large formats. It is important that the distances are **the same everywhere**.
- **Height:** The visual center of the entire picture wall should be approximately at eye level, around 145 to 155 cm above the floor.
- **Above furniture:** The bottom edge of the gallery wall approximately 20 to 30 cm above the sofa or chest of drawers.

## Step 6: Hanging

### tool

- Spirit level or cross line laser. See [Measuring and Marking](/anleitungen/messen-und-anreissen-wasserwaage-laser/).
- Folding rule, pencil.
- Picture nails, hooks, dowels or adhesive strips, depending on the wall and weight. See [Hanging Pictures Without Drilling](/anleitungen/bilder-aufhaengen-ohne-bohren/).

### Procedure

1. **Start with the largest or central image.** Drive the nail or drill the hole through the marked hanging point on the paper template. Then tear off the template.
2. **Hang the picture** and align it with a spirit level.
3. **Hang additional images stencil by stencil** in the same way.
4. **Measure the distances** before placing the next hole.

### Tips for straight images

- **Two hanging points** per picture instead of one prevent it from becoming crooked.
- **Small adhesive pads** on the bottom corners of the frames prevent slipping and protect the wall.
- **Laser** makes grids and center axes much easier.

## Picture bar: the flexible alternative

If you like to redecorate, mount one or two narrow picture rails and place the frames on them. Images can overlap, change and be rearranged without creating new holes. Ideal for renters.

## Example: Gallery wall above a 2 meter sofa

- **Width of the gallery wall:** approximately 130 cm.
- **Lower edge:** 25 cm above the back of the sofa.
- **Composition:** a large picture 50 × 70 cm slightly to the left of the center, around it four pictures 30 × 40 cm and three small 20 × 25 cm.
- **Distance:** 6 cm everywhere.
- **Frame:** all black, narrow, with white passe-partout.

## Common mistakes

| Error | Better |
|---|---|
| Images too high | Center point at about 150 cm |
| Unequal spacing | Cardboard spacers in desired width |
| Start drilling without a plan | Paper stencils |
| Too many different styles | Choose a connecting element |
| Gallery wall too small for the wall | About two thirds of the width of the furniture |

## Time requirements and materials

| step | time |
|---|---|
| Select and frame images | one or two evenings |
| Make paper templates | 30 to 60 minutes |
| Test arrangement | let it hang for a few days |
| Hang up | 1 to 3 hours |

**Material:** Wrapping paper, scissors, painter's tape, pencil, folding rule, spirit level or laser, suitable hooks, nails or dowels, small adhesive pads for the frame corners.

## Illuminate images correctly

Light turns a picture wall into an eye-catcher in the evening. Picture lights above individual pictures or swiveling spots on the ceiling that aim diagonally at the wall set accents. Look for warm white LED light with high color rendering, recognizable by an Ra or CRI value of 90 and more, so that colors appear natural. You can avoid reflections on glass by letting the light shine in steeply from above or by using anti-reflective glass. Direct sun, on the other hand, is an enemy of prints and photos: UV light bleaches colors over the years. Valuable originals are therefore better hung on walls without direct sunlight. More about lighting planning can be found in [Planning light in the apartment](/anleitungen/licht-planen-in-der-wohnung/).

## Print and frame motifs

The following applies to photos: the larger the print, the more resolution the file needs. Cell phone photos are usually sufficient for small and medium formats, but for large prints it's worth taking a look at the printing service's recommendation. Frames are available in standard formats such as 13 × 18, 20 × 30, 30 × 40, 40 × 50 and 50 × 70 centimeters; matching passe-partouts reduce the size of the section and give the motif space. For originals such as drawings, watercolors or old family photos, acid-free mounts and back panels are important because they prevent the paper from yellowing over the years.

## Children's pictures and changing motifs

Children are constantly bringing home new works of art. Instead of re-framing every time, you can use removable frames with a folding mechanism, clipboards on nails or a stretched rope with small wooden clips. This creates a gallery that can change every week and still looks neat.

## Prepare the wall beforehand

Don't plan the gallery wall until the wall itself is finished. Fill old holes, paint fresh if necessary and let the paint dry thoroughly before applying adhesive pads or adhesive strips. A colored wall behind the group of pictures, such as a muted green or a dark blue, makes light mounts and gold or black frames particularly shine. How to find the color can be found in [Select wall color](/anleitungen/wandfarbe-auswaehlen/).

## Practical example: Staircase with family photos

A family wants to hang 15 family photos on the staircase of their terraced house. Because the wall slopes upwards, they decide to **arrange it along a center line that rises parallel to the stairs.**

1. The cross line laser is used to project a line that runs at the same angle as the stairs, approximately 150 cm above the edges of the steps.
2. All frames are white with passe-partout, in three sizes.
3. The paper stencils are arranged so that the centers of the image alternately lie just above and just below the line.
4. Distance everywhere 7 cm.
5. Each picture gets two adhesive pads on the bottom corners to keep it in place as you walk past.

The result appears lively and organized at the same time and can be expanded to include additional images later.

## Checklist

- Location and size chosen to match the furniture or wall.
- A connecting element set.
- Paper templates with marked hanging points.
- Distances uniform.
- Center point at about 150 cm.
- Attachment suitable for wall and weight.

## Another question

### How do I mix portrait and landscape formats?

Large landscape formats often form the center, while portrait formats frame them on the sides. It is important that the outer edges of the overall composition form a calm shape.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many images do I need?

Five to seven pictures are enough for a small picture wall, twelve or more for a large wall.

### Can I install mirrors and objects?

Yes. Small mirrors, wooden letters, plates or textiles break up the wall. Make sure your weight is balanced.

### How do I deal with light switches and sockets?

Include it in the planning and leave enough distance so that nothing is obscured.

### Further questions from practice

**How do I prevent pictures from hanging crooked?** Two hanging points instead of one, small adhesive pads at the bottom of the frame, and hooks with locks for cable systems.

**Which frames are suitable for family photos?** Uniform frames with a passe-partout give even different photos a calm appearance. Black and white prints combine photos from different decades.

**How ​​do I change an existing picture wall?** Changes are possible at any time with a picture rail or gallery rail. For solid nails, plan new images to cover or utilize old holes.

**Does a picture wall also fit in small rooms?** Yes, if it remains reserved: a few, rather small formats in calm, identical frames and plenty of white space in the passe-partout. A single row on a picture rail often appears calmer in small rooms than a dense salon hanging.

## Conclusion

A harmonious picture wall is created through planning: choose a location and size that matches the furniture, determine a connecting element, choose an arrangement, test it with paper templates and only then hang it up. Equal spacing, the correct height and a spirit level or laser ensure a professional result, without unnecessary holes.
