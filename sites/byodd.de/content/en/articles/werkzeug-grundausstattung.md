Anyone who wants to hang up a shelf, mount a lamp or recreate a cupboard door for the first time is quickly faced with the question: What do I actually need? Hardware stores lure you with toolboxes full of shiny parts, many of which are never used, while the one tool you need is missing. Well-thought-out basic equipment saves money, nerves and trips to the hardware store.

This guide shows which tools belong in every household, what quality makes sense, how much the equipment roughly costs and how you can gradually expand them without spending a fortune.

## Principle: Better little and good

A cheap screwdriver whose tip wears out after three screws will damage screw heads and slip. Pliers whose jaws are distorted won't hold anything. Solid mid-range hand tools are worth it: they last for decades and make work safer. When it comes to power tools, the DIY class is often sufficient for occasional work if the brand and battery system are right.

Also, don't buy everything at once. Start with the core and add to it when a project requires it.

## The basic equipment: 15 parts for almost everything

### Measuring and marking

| tool | For what | What to pay attention to |
|---|---|---|
| Folding rule (2 m) | Measuring lengths, planning furniture | Easy to read scale, solid joints |
| Tape measure (5 m) | Measure rooms | Lock, belt clip |
| Spirit level (40 to 60 cm) | Straighten shelves, pictures and rails | Dragonflies easy to read, aluminum profile |
| Pencil and cutter knife | Mark, cut cardboard and wallpaper | Knife with snap-off blades and lock |

### Screwing, holding, gripping

| tool | For what | What to pay attention to |
|---|---|---|
| Screwdriver set | Slotted, cross (PH, PZ) and Torx in several sizes | Hardened tips, grippy handles |
| Bit set with holder | For cordless screwdrivers and hand holders | Torx T10 to T30, PZ1 to PZ3 |
| Combination pliers | Grabbing, bending, cutting off wires | Insulated handles |
| Water pump pliers | Siphon, nuts, pipe connections | Adjustable, with lock |
| Allen key set | Furniture, bicycles, fittings | Ball head on one side |
| Combination wrench (8 to 19 mm) | Bolts and nuts | Set in roll bag |

### Hitting and loosening

- **locksmith's hammer (300 g):** for nails, dowels and light blows.
- **Rubber hammer:** for click laminate, furniture kits and sensitive surfaces.

### Power tool

- **Cordless screwdriver (12 to 18 V):** the most important power tool in the household. For screwing, for small holes in wood and metal. More about this in [Buy a cordless screwdriver and use it correctly](/anleitungen/akkuschrauber-kaufen-und-benutzen/).
- **Impact drill or hammer drill:** for holes in concrete and masonry. For apartments with concrete walls, a small hammer drill is a better choice. See [correct drilling in concrete, brick and plasterboard](/anleitungen/richtig-bohren-in-beton-ziegel-gipskarton/).

### Security

- **Safety glasses, hearing protection, FFP2 masks, work gloves.**
- **Line finder** for power and water lines before drilling. See [Locating Pipes Before Drilling](/anleitungen/leitungen-orten-vor-dem-bohren/).

## Small parts that you always need

An assortment box with small parts saves a lot of trips:

- Universal dowels in 6 and 8 mm and matching chipboard screws.
- Wood screws in several lengths (3.5 × 30, 4 × 40, 5 × 60 mm).
- Nails and steel nails for pictures.
- Cable ties, insulating tape, fabric tape.
- Sandpaper in grit sizes 80, 120 and 180.
- Wood glue and assembly glue.
- Silicone and acrylic in white with cartridge press.

## Battery system: an important decision

Many manufacturers use standard batteries for all devices within one brand. If you decide on a system early on, you can later buy devices without a battery and charger cheaper. Pay attention to:

- **Voltage class:** 12V for light work, 18V (some marketed as 20V) for more power.
- **Availability of devices** in the same system: jigsaw, grinder, lamp, vacuum cleaner.
- **Replacement batteries** at reasonable prices.

## Borrow instead of buying

You can rent expensive special equipment that you only need once:

- **Hardware stores** rent hammer drills, tile cutters, carpet cleaners and grinders on a daily basis.
- **Neighborhood platforms and rental shops** often offer tools for free or for a small fee.
- **Friends and neighbors:** Anyone who buys a tool together shares the costs.

## Storage and care

- **Toolbox or wall bar:** Tools that are found are used and do not disappear.
- **Store in a dry place:** Moisture causes steel to rust. A basement with high humidity is not a good place.
- **Store batteries correctly:** at room temperature, about half charged for longer breaks.
- **Replace blades and bits** as soon as they become dull or worn out.

### Tool for specific living situations

- **Old building with wooden beam ceilings and floorboards:** A good set of wood drills, a chisel, wood putty and sandpaper are more important than a hammer drill because many walls are made of brick and plaster.
- **New construction with concrete walls:** A compact hammer drill with SDS-plus drill bits in 6, 8 and 10 mm is indispensable.
- **Drywall Apartment or Attic:** Cavity dowels, a locator that finds studs, and a drywall saw for small repairs.
- **House with garden:** Additional garden tools, a saw for branches and stainless steel screws for outdoor work.

This way, the equipment grows with your home instead of buying tools you never need.

## Common mistakes when buying tools

- **Huge toolbox** with many poor quality parts.
- **Cheap bits** destroy the screw heads.
- **Impact drill for concrete walls:** You can move much faster and more gently in reinforced concrete with a hammer drill.
- **No cable finder:** A drilled cable can be expensive and dangerous.
- **Forget safety equipment.**

## Example: The first three projects with the basic equipment

So that the list doesn't remain abstract, here are three typical projects for your first apartment and what you need from the basic equipment.

### Project 1: A wall shelf in the living room

You measure the desired height with the ruler, mark the drill holes with a pencil and align them with the spirit level. You check the wall with the cable finder, drill with the hammer drill or impact drill, insert universal dowels and screw the shelf supports with the cordless screwdriver. Time required: about an hour. Without basic equipment, you would have to borrow five tools.

### Project 2: Build a wardrobe from the furniture store

You will need Allen wrenches, Phillips screwdrivers or bits, a rubber mallet for the wooden dowels, and the spirit level to level the cabinet. A drill, dowels and screws are required to secure the wall from tipping over. The anti-tip device is not an optional extra for tall cupboards: it prevents the cupboard from falling over if a child pulls on an open drawer.

### Project 3: Clean the siphon under the sink

Use the water pump pliers to loosen the union nuts, a bucket catches the water, and gloves protect your hands. After cleaning, tighten the nuts hand-tight and with a quarter turn of the pliers. More about this in [Drain clogged: what to do?](/anleitungen/abfluss-verstopft-was-tun/).

## Quality features that you can check in the store

Even without specialist knowledge, good tools can be distinguished from bad ones:

- **Weight and Balance:** A hammer with a loose head or splintered handle is dangerous. Good hammers have a firmly connected head and a handle that fits comfortably in the hand.
- **Screwdriver Tips:** Look at the tip. It should be cleanly ground and hardened; Information such as chrome vanadium or S2 steel is a good sign.
- **Pliers:** The jaws must lie exactly on top of each other and there must be no play in the joint.
- **Spirit level:** Place it on a flat surface in the store, read the level, turn the scale 180 degrees and read again. The bubble must be the same position both times.
- **Battery devices:** Pay attention to the warranty, a charger with a charge level indicator and whether the manufacturer offers replacement parts.

## Tool for tenants

Anyone who lives in a rented apartment needs the same basic equipment, but should talk to the landlord beforehand about some work. Hanging pictures, assembling shelves and attaching lamps are part of normal home use. You need consent for larger interventions such as removing tiles, laying solid flooring or breakthroughs. A good set of tools also helps when moving out: closing dowel holes, removing lamps and dismantling furniture are quick. You can find ideas for changes without the hassle at [Beautify your rental apartment without the hassle](/anleitungen/mietwohnung-verschoenern-ohne-aerger/).

## First aid is part of the equipment

A small cut with the cutter knife or a splinter while sanding happens even to experienced people. That's why a small first aid box should be included next to the toolbox: plasters in various sizes, sterile compresses, a gauze bandage, tweezers for splinters and an eyewash bottle for dust in the eye. Replace used material immediately and keep an eye on the expiry date of the sterile parts. In the case of deep cuts, foreign bodies in the eye or an electric shock: do not tinker around yourself, but get medical help or call 112.

## Checklist before the first project

- Basic equipment complete and ready to hand in a suitcase or on the wall.
- Batteries charged, spare bits available.
- Safety glasses and hearing protection within reach.
- Line finder tested.
- First aid box ready and complete.
- Small parts range filled with dowels and screws.
- House rules with quiet times are known.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is one cordless screwdriver enough for everything?

For screws and drilling in wood, metal and soft masonry, yes. For concrete you need a hammer drill.

### Is a multi-tool worth it?

An oscillating multitool is practical for renovations, such as sawing off door frames or joints. It is not a priority for beginners.

### Which brand is the best?

This depends on the budget and the battery system. More important than the brand are solid workmanship, warranty and availability of spare parts.

### Should I buy used tools?

Hand tools in good condition are often a bargain when used. When using cordless devices, pay attention to the condition of the batteries.

## Conclusion

A good basic equipment consists of around 15 solid tools, a cordless screwdriver, a hammer drill or impact drill, safety equipment and an assortment of small parts. It's better to buy little and good, decide on a battery system early on, borrow special equipment and expand your equipment with the projects that you really want to tackle.
