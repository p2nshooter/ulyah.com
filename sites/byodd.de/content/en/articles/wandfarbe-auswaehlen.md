The color of the walls shapes the mood of a room more than almost any piece of furniture. The same living room can look cozy in a warm sandy tone, elegant in a cool blue-grey and cozy in a lush green. At the same time, the choice of color is one of the most common causes of renovation frustration: The color that looked perfect on the small color chart in the hardware store suddenly looks too bright, too dark or completely different than expected on the wall.

This guide explains how colors affect rooms, how light and direction change the color tone, how to test and combine colors correctly and how to recognize a good wall color.

## How colors work

| Color family | Typical effect | Good rooms |
|---|---|---|
| Warm white, cream, sand | Bright, friendly, quiet | Almost everywhere |
| Gray (warm or cool) | Elegant, reserved | Living room, hallway, study |
| Blue | Cool, calm, focused | Bedroom, bathroom, study |
| Green | Of course, balancing | Living room, bedroom, kitchen |
| Yellow, ochre | Warm, sunny, activating | Kitchen, hallway, dining room, north rooms |
| Terracotta, rose | Warm, safe | Living room, bedroom |
| Dark tones (petrol, dark green, anthracite) | Cozy, dramatic | Accent walls, large bright rooms, work niches |

### spatial effect

- **Light colors** make walls recede and the room appears larger.
- **Dark colors** bring walls closer, the room appears smaller but cozier.
- **Warm colors** (red, orange, yellow) appear close and vibrant.
- **Cold colors** (blue, green, cool gray) appear more distant and calmer.

More on small rooms in [Make Small Rooms Look Bigger](/anleitungen/kleine-raeume-groesser-wirken-lassen/).

## Light and direction

The same shade looks different in different rooms:

| Alignment | Light | Color effect | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| North | Cool, even light | Colors appear colder and grayer | Warm tones with yellow or red components |
| South | Warm, intense light | Colors appear warmer and brighter | Cool tones possible, intense colors have a stronger effect |
| East | Warm in the morning, cooler in the afternoon | Changing | Balanced tones |
| West | Cooler in the morning, warm in the evening | Very warm in the evening | Cool to neutral tones |

Artificial light also changes colors: warm white lamps (2,700 K) make colors appear warmer, neutral white lamps (4,000 K) make colors appear cooler. See [Planning light in the apartment](/anleitungen/licht-planen-in-der-wohnung/).

## Test color correctly

The color chart is only a rough guide. This is how you test correctly:

1. **Buy sample cans:** Many manufacturers offer small test containers.
2. **Create large samples:** At least 50 × 50 cm, better 1 × 1 m. Paint on cardboard or directly on the wall, twice so that the paint covers.
3. **Test on different walls:** The window wall is usually in the shade and appears darker than the wall opposite.
4. **View at different times of day:** Morning, midday, evening with artificial light.
5. **Compare with floor, furniture and curtains.**
6. **Allow a few days.**

**Rule of thumb:** Colors appear more intense and usually darker on a large area than on the small card. If in doubt, choose a tone lighter.

## Combine colors

### Tone on tone

Different brightnesses of the same color, about three shades of green: calm, elegant, easy to implement.

### Neutral plus accent

Neutral main color for walls and large furniture, an accent color for a wall, pillows, pictures or curtains. Best rule of thumb: about 60% main color, 30% secondary color, 10% accent color.

### Complementary contrasts

Opposite colors on the color wheel such as blue and orange or green and rose create tension. Better to use in steamed versions.

### Wall sections

- **Half-height walls:** Lower half of the wall is darker, the upper half is light. Looks modern and protects against wear and tear in the hallway.
- **Colored niches** or window reveals.
- **Arches and Shapes:** A colorful arch behind the bed or desk as an eye-catcher.

## How do you recognize good wall paint?

There are two important pieces of information on every bucket of emulsion paint according to the European standard DIN EN 13300:

| Information | Meaning | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Opacity (Class 1 to 4)** | How well the color covers, given a certain coverage | Class 1 or 2 |
| **Wet abrasion resistance (Class 1 to 5)** | How well the paint tolerates cleaning and abrasion | Class 1 or 2 for hallways, kitchens, children's rooms; Class 3 for living rooms and bedrooms |

Further information:

- **Yield** in m² per liter (usually 6 to 8).
- **Low emissions and solvent-free:** Please note the seal for low-pollutant products.
- **Level of gloss:** Matt forgives unevenness, semi-gloss is more wipeable.
- **Cheap paints** often cover poorly and require more coats, which negates the price advantage.

### Special colors

- **Lime paint** and **silicate paint:** mineral, permeable, mold-inhibiting due to high pH value. Good for old buildings and bathrooms.
- **Latex paint:** very washable, but less open to diffusion.
- **Chalk and clay colors:** velvety, natural surfaces.

## Rental apartment: What is allowed?

Tenants are generally allowed to decorate their apartment in color during the rental period. When moving out, however, you may be asked to paint over strong or dark colors again in neutral, light tones. You should keep this in mind when choosing a color. ## Common mistakes

| Error | Better |
|---|---|
| Decided based on a small color chart | Large sample on several walls |
| Cool gray in the north room | Warm gray or sand tone |
| Too many colors | 60-30-10 rule |
| Cheap paint with poor coverage | Coverage class 1 or 2 |
| Shiny paint on uneven wall | Matte color |

## Name colors clearly

“A light gray” means something different to everyone. So that you can buy a specific color later, write down the name from a color system. RAL colors with four-digit numbers are common, especially for paints, and the NCS system, which many wall paint manufacturers use. An NCS information like S 2010-Y30R can even be read: The first two digits indicate the proportion of black, the next two the chroma, the rest the color, here a yellow with 30 percent red. The higher the first number, the darker the color. Many manufacturers also have their own color fans with names and numbers. Take a photo of the bucket lid so that you have all the information to hand at all times.

## The brightness reference value

Some manufacturers specify the brightness reference value, HBW for short. It describes how much light a color reflects, from 0 for deep black to 100 for ideal white. In small or dark rooms, colors with a high light reference value provide more brightness, even if they are colored. Two shades of similar value appear calm next to each other; large differences create contrast. When planning, you can estimate whether a room will appear bright or cave-like.

## Where does the color end?

How you design the transition to the ceiling changes the effect of the room. If the wall color ends exactly at the ceiling, the room appears clear and classic. If it ends a few centimeters below and the ceiling is pulled down a bit with bright white, the room often appears lighter and slightly higher. In low rooms, however, it can work surprisingly well to paint the walls and ceiling in the same color: the edges disappear and the room appears closed and calm. Try out ideas like this first with a photo of the room that you color on the computer or on paper.

## Practical example: A north-facing room becomes warmer

A study with a north-facing window appeared dreary with a cool light gray, especially in winter. The resident tested three sample colors in large samples on the wall opposite the window and on the window wall: a warm greige, a light ocher and a muted sage green. For over a week, she looked at the patterns morning, afternoon and evening under the warm white desk lamp.

The result: The ocher appeared friendly in daylight, but too yellow in the evening. The sage green looked gray in the Northern Lights. The warm greige retained a soft, warm character in all situations. She painted the walls greige, the ceiling an off-white with a touch of the same color, and added accents using the 60-30-10 rule with an ocher pillow and a picture.

## Think about color and material together

Wall paint never works alone. It interacts with:

- **Floor:** A reddish cherry wood parquet reinforces warm tones, a gray vinyl reinforces cool ones.
- **Wooden furniture:** Oak and beech harmonize with green, sand and ocher; dark walnut with petrol or warm gray.
- **Textiles:** Curtains and carpets take up a lot of space and should be included in the color planning.
- **Metals:** Brass and gold go with warm tones, while chrome and stainless steel go with cool tones.

When testing, take fabric samples, floor samples or a piece of furniture.

## Checklist

- Effect and spatial function determined.
- Direction and light taken into account.
- Large sample samples on several walls.
- Checked at different times of the day.
- Coordinated with floors, furniture and textiles.
- Coverage and wet abrasion checked on the bucket.

## Frequently asked questions

### What color makes a room cozy?

Warm, muted tones such as sand, terracotta, ocher or warm green, combined with warm white light.

### Which color is suitable for the bedroom?

Calm, muted tones such as blue-grey, sage green or warm beige.

### How much paint do I need?

See the quantity calculation in [Paint Wall](/anleitungen/wand-streichen-anleitung/).

### Further questions from practice

**How do I find a suitable ceiling color?** An off-white with a touch of the wall color looks more harmonious than pure white. Many paint manufacturers offer ceiling white in several shades.

**Can I have the color remixed?** Yes, with the manufacturer's color number or a sample. For larger areas, have enough paint mixed at once, as subsequent mixing may vary slightly.

**Which color for children's rooms?** Calm, light basic tones with accents in textiles and furniture. Washable paint with wet abrasion class 1 or 2 is practical.

**Can I paint over dark paint with a light color later?** Yes, but two or three coats of well-covering paint are usually necessary. Pre-painting with a light-colored primer saves paint and time. Plan for this if you choose dark tones in a rental apartment.

## Conclusion

Choose the wall color based on effect, room size and direction, test it with large samples on several walls and at different times of the day and combine it with the 60-30-10 rule. When buying, pay attention to the hiding power and wet abrasion class, and if you are renting an apartment, think about moving out. So the color not only fits on the card, but also in the room.
