A single ceiling lamp in the middle of the room is the standard lighting in many homes, and it is usually the reason why a room feels uncomfortable, flat or too dark in the evening. Good light comes from multiple light sources that perform different tasks. With a little planning, almost any apartment can be lit in a much more comfortable and practical way, and much of this can be done without any electrical work.

These instructions explain the three light levels, show which light color and brightness fit in which room, give examples for living rooms, kitchens, bedrooms, bathrooms and hallways and present solutions for tenants.

## The three levels of light

| level | task | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic lighting** | Even brightness for orientation and everyday life | Ceiling lights, recessed spots, indirect ceiling lighting |
| **Zone light (work light)** | Bright, targeted light for activities | Reading lamp, under cabinet light in the kitchen, mirror light, desk lamp |
| **Accent light (mood light)** | Atmosphere, depth, highlighting | Table lamps, fairy lights, shelf lighting, wall lights, candles |

A well-lit room combines all three levels and allows them to be switched or dimmed separately. In the evening the basic lighting often remains off; Zone and accent lighting then create a warm, cozy atmosphere.

## Light color: warm or cold?

The light color is given in Kelvin (K):

| light color | Kelvin | Effect | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extra warm white | 2,200 to 2,700 K | Very cozy, candle-like | Living room, bedroom, accents |
| Warm white | 2,700 to 3,000 K | Homely | Living rooms, dining area |
| Neutral white | 3,300 to 4,000 K | Factual, activating | Kitchen (work surface), bathroom (mirror), study |
| Daylight white | over 5,000K | Cool, technical | Workshop, cellar |

**Tip:** Don't mix too many different light colors within one room. For living spaces, 2,700 K is the safe choice.

## Brightness: lumens instead of watts

With LED lamps, it is not the wattage that counts, but rather the luminous flux in **Lumen (lm)**:

| Old light bulb | Approximate lumens |
|---|---|
| 25W | 250 lm |
| 40W | 470 lm |
| 60W | 800 lm |
| 100W | 1,500 lm |

For basic lighting, you can roughly estimate 100 to 150 lumens per square meter in living rooms, more in kitchens and work areas.

**Color rendering:** The CRI or Ra value indicates how natural colors appear. For living rooms at least 80, for bathrooms, kitchens and wardrobes better 90 and more.

## Room by room

### Living room

- **Basic light:** dimmable ceiling light or indirect light.
- **Zone light:** Reading lamp on the armchair, light on the dining table (pendant light about 60 to 70 cm above the table top).
- **Accents:** Table lamps on sideboards, light on the shelf, a floor lamp that illuminates the wall or ceiling.
- **Rule of thumb:** Have at least three light sources distributed throughout the room, not just in one corner.

### Kitchen

- **Basic light:** bright ceiling light or spots.
- **Work Light:** Under cabinet lights above the countertop are the most important thing. Otherwise you will be in your own shadow.
- **Accent:** Pendant light above the dining table or kitchen island.

### bedroom

- **Basic light:** dimmed, warm.
- **Zone light:** Reading lamps on the bed, can be switched separately.
- **Wardrobe:** Interior lighting with motion detector or battery LED.
- **Evening:** warm, dimmed light helps you fall asleep.

### bathroom

- **Mirror light:** Light from the sides or all around the mirror illuminates the face without shadows. Light only from above casts shadows under the eyes.
- **Basic light:** Ceiling light suitable for damp rooms.
- **Note protection classes:** Lights in the bathroom need a suitable protection class against moisture (IP protection class), depending on the distance to the shower and bathtub.

### hallway

- **Even light** for orientation, preferably with a motion detector.
- **Mirror with light** on the wardrobe.

## Light without electrical work

Electrical installations may only be carried out by qualified electricians. This applies to new cables, sockets, switches and connecting permanently installed lights to the house installation, which goes beyond replacing a lamp on an existing connection. Nevertheless, a lot can be improved without electrical work:

- **Plug lights:** Floor, table and wall lamps with plugs. Wall lights with cable routing in cable channels or beautiful textile cables.
- **Rechargeable and battery-operated lights:** Wireless table lamps, LED strips on shelves or cupboards.
- **Smart bulbs:** Screw into existing sockets, dim via app or radio switch and change light color.
- **Radio switch:** Switch sockets via remote control, ideal for floor lamps.
- **Under-cabinet lights with plug** for the kitchen.
- **string lights** for mood.

You should only do how to attach lamps to existing connections if you know the rules: switch off the circuit, check that there is no voltage. If in doubt, hire a qualified electrician.

## Save energy with light

- **LED** instead of halogen or incandescent lamps: up to 80 to 90% less electricity.
- **Motion detector** in the hallway, storage room and basement.
- **Dimming:** Saves energy and creates atmosphere (only with dimmable LEDs and matching dimmers).
- **Timers** for decorative lighting.

## Common mistakes

| Error | Better |
|---|---|
| Just a ceiling lamp | Combine three levels |
| Cold white light in the living room | 2,700K |
| No work light in the kitchen | Under cabinet lights |
| Mirror light only from above | Side or surrounding light |
| Non-dimmable LEDs on the dimmer | flickering, humming; Choose dimmable LEDs |

## Light and sleep

Light controls our internal clock. Bright, bluish-cool light in the morning wakes you up; the same light late in the evening can make it difficult to fall asleep because it slows down the release of the sleep hormone melatonin. Therefore, use dimmed, warm light of 2,200 to 2,700 Kelvin in the evening, especially in the bedroom, and leave the ceiling light off. In the morning it can be bright and cooler, ideally with plenty of daylight. Smart lamps whose light color changes automatically over the course of the day make this convenient.

## Types of protection in the bathroom are understandable

The IP protection class consists of two numbers: The first describes the protection against foreign bodies and dust, the second describes the protection against water. IP44, for example, means protection against splash water from all directions. In the bathroom, protective areas apply around the shower and tub: Inside the tub and shower tray, only devices that can withstand permanent submersion are permitted; in the area directly above and in a radius of around 60 centimeters next to it, at least splash protection is required. Luminaires outside these areas do not require any special protection. If you would like to have new lights connected in the bathroom, it is best to discuss the positions with a qualified electrician.

## Socket, lumen, Kelvin: what you should pay attention to when buying

When replacing a light bulb, four pieces of information count. First the base: The large screw socket is called E27, the small one is E14, plus plug-in sockets such as GU10 for spots or G9 for small pin base lamps. Then the brightness in lumens, the light color in Kelvin and the color rendering. If you want to dim, buy expressly dimmable LEDs. When it comes to spots, it's also worth taking a look at the beam angle: narrow angles create accents, while wide angles illuminate surfaces.

## Avoid glare

Bright points of light in the field of vision are tiring. Naked LED filament lamps look beautiful, but they dazzle at eye level. Screens, diffusers and indirect light defuse this. On the television, a weak light source behind the device helps, which softens the contrast between the bright screen and the dark wall and relaxes the eyes.

## Practical example: A dark living room without an electrician

A 20 square meter living room in a rental apartment only has one ceiling outlet in the middle. In the evening the room appears flat and uncomfortable, the reading corner is too dark.

The residents implement the following plan:

1. **Basic light:** The old ceiling lamp is replaced with a dimmable lamp with a large fabric shade that distributes the light softly. The original light goes into the basement.
2. **Zone Light:** A floor lamp with an adjustable head next to the armchair; Above the dining table there is a pendant lamp with a plug, the cable of which is led to the socket via a ceiling hook.
3. **Accents:** A small table lamp on the sideboard, a battery-powered LED strip on the bookshelf and a floor lamp that illuminates a houseplant from below.
4. **Control:** The plug-in lamps hang on radio-controlled sockets, which are switched together with a wall switch next to the door.

All lamps have 2,700 K. Result: In the evening, the ceiling light usually stays off, the room appears larger and more comfortable, and the reading corner is bright enough.

## Checklist

- Three levels of light planned per room.
- Uniform light color (living rooms 2,700 K).
- Color rendering at least Ra 80, in the bathroom 90.
- Dimmable LEDs only with suitable dimmers.
- Switches and sockets for plug-in lamps are planned.
- Electrical work should only be carried out by specialists.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many lamps does a living room need?

Usually four to six light sources, distributed between basic, zone and accent lighting.

### Is indirect light bright enough?

As basic light in combination with zone light, yes. Activities require direct light.

### As a tenant, can I change lamps?

It is common practice to replace lights on existing connections with your own; When you move out, the original condition is restored. See [Beautify your rental apartment without the hassle](/anleitungen/mietwohnung-verschoenern-ohne-aerger/).

### Further questions from practice

**What does “flicker-free” mean?** Good LED lights have stable control without noticeable flickering. Cheap bulbs sometimes flicker, especially when dimming, which causes headaches for some people. Pay attention to information about low flicker.

**How ​​high should I hang a pendant light above the dining table?** Usually 60 to 70 cm above the table top so that it illuminates the table but does not glare and does not disturb eye contact.

**Can I use normal lamps in the bathroom?** Only outside the protected areas around the shower and bathtub. Luminaires nearby must have an appropriate protection class.

**Are motion detectors worth it?** In the hallway, basement and storage room, yes: the light is there when you need it and goes off by itself.

**How ​​do I dispose of old lamps?** LED lamps and energy-saving lamps do not belong in household waste, but rather at collection points in stores or at recycling centers. Energy-saving lamps contain small amounts of mercury and should therefore be returned undamaged.

## Conclusion

Good light comes from three levels: basic lighting, zone lighting and accent lighting. Choose warm white light around 2,700 K for living rooms, neutral white for work surfaces and mirrors, pay attention to lumens and color rendering and distribute several light sources throughout the room. With plug-in lights, battery-powered LEDs and smart bulbs, you can improve lighting even without electrical work.
