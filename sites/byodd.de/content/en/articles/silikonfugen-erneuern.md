Silicone joints are the seals of the bathroom. They connect tiles to the bathtub, shower tray and sink and prevent water from running behind the tiles. Over time, they become brittle, loosen at the edges or develop black mold spots that cannot be removed even with the strongest cleaner. Then at the latest it is time to renew the joint.

These instructions show you how to completely remove old silicone, prepare the surface, choose the right silicone and create a smooth, permanently tight joint.

## When does the silicone joint have to be replaced?

- **Black dots or spots** that cannot be cleaned away: Mold has grown into the silicone.
- **Detachment at the edges:** The joint no longer adheres to the tile or tub.
- **Tears or holes.**
- **Hardened, brittle silicone.**
- **Damp spots** on the wall behind the tub or stains on the ceiling below.

A leaky silicone joint can lead to water damage, which is much more expensive than replacing it.

### Which silicone?

- **Sanitary silicone (acetate curing):** smells like vinegar, adheres well to glass, tiles and enamel. Not for natural stone (discoloration) and some metals.
- **Neutral cross-linking silicone:** low odor, also for natural stone, acrylic tubs and many plastics. Use special natural stone silicone for natural stone.
- **Acrylic** is **not** suitable for wet areas. It can be painted over and is intended for joints on walls and door frames.

## Step 1: Remove old silicone

1. **Cut out:** Use the cutter knife or silicone knife to cut the joint along both sides without cutting into the tile or tub.
2. **Pulling out:** Grab the joint at one end and pull out in one piece.
3. **Remove residue:** Apply silicone remover, observe the exposure time, then scrape off the residue with the plastic scraper. Do not use metal blades on enamel and acrylic.
4. **Clean thoroughly:** Degrease everything with spirit. The smallest silicone residue prevents the new silicone from sticking.

## Step 2: Treat and dry mold

- **Treat mold in the side of the joint** with a mold remover or 70% alcohol. See [Removing mold from the wall](/anleitungen/schimmel-an-der-wand-entfernen-und-vorbeugen/).
- **Let dry:** The joint must be completely dry, preferably 24 hours. You can help with a hairdryer. Moisture under the silicone prevents adhesion and promotes new mold.

## Step 3: Prepare the tub

**Important for bathtubs:** Fill the tub with water for grouting. A full tub sinks a little due to its weight, just as it does later when bathing. If the joint on the empty tub is pulled, it will tear as soon as someone bathes. The water stays in until the silicone has hardened.

## Step 4: Masking (optional but helpful)

Apply painter's tape to both sides of the joint at the desired distance, approximately 3 to 5 mm from the corner. This will make the edge straight. Especially recommended for beginners.

## Step 5: Apply silicone

1. **Prepare the cartridge:** Cut off the tip, screw on the nozzle and cut it **diagonally**. The opening should be slightly smaller than the desired joint width.
2. **Insert into press.**
3. **Pull evenly:** Place the nozzle at an angle of approximately 45 degrees and move along the joint with even pressure and speed. It's better to have a little too much silicone than too little.
4. **Corners:** Briefly interrupt the strand in corners and start again.
5. **After setting down**, relieve the pressure on the press so that no silicone runs out.

## Step 6: Smoothing

1. **Spray smoothing agent** or wet the joint with dishwashing water. This means nothing sticks to the tool.
2. **Use the joint smoother** to remove the joint in one go. Excess silicone is taken away and wiped off.
3. **Alternatively with a wet finger,** but the result is usually less uniform.
4. **Take off the crepe immediately** while the silicone is still fresh, at an acute angle away from the joint.

## Step 7: Allow to harden

- Skin formation occurs after about 10 to 20 minutes. Don't touch it again after that.
- Complete curing takes approximately 24 hours or longer depending on joint thickness. Pay attention to package instructions.
- During this time, do not shower or bathe and ventilate well.
- Only then let the water out of the tub.

## Keep new joints looking good for a long time

- **After showering** dry tiles and joints with a squeegee.
- **ventilate well** so that moisture does not remain.
- **Clean mildly:** Aggressive cleaners attack silicone.
- **Check regularly.**

Depending on the use, silicone joints last for a few years. Joints are maintenance joints: they must be renewed regularly.

## Common mistakes

| Error | Episode |
|---|---|
| Old silicone not completely removed | New silicone does not stick |
| Subsoil moist | Detachment, mold under the joint |
| Tub empty | The joint cracked during the first bath |
| Acrylic instead of silicone in wet areas | Joint becomes leaky |
| Smoothed without smoothing agents | Silicone sticks to the tool, the joint tears open |
| Crepe removed too late | Edge moves along |

## Silicone is not the actual seal

A silicone joint is a maintenance joint. It's supposed to keep out splashes of water, but it's not designed to keep an entire bathroom watertight for years. In professionally built bathrooms there is a composite seal underneath the tiles that protects the masonry, especially in the shower area and floor-level showers. This is also the reason why experts recommend checking silicone joints regularly and replacing them in a timely manner: If water runs through a leaky joint for months, it can overwhelm the seal underneath or get into places where it is missing, such as in older bathrooms. If there are moisture spots on walls or ceilings, a new joint alone is often no longer enough; then a specialist company should check the cause.

## Joints in the kitchen

There are also silicone joints in the kitchen, for example between the worktop and the wall, around the sink or on the back wall. Sanitary or kitchen silicones with mold protection are suitable here; for worktops made of natural stone, a special natural stone silicone that leaves no edge discoloration. An alternative to the joint between the worktop and the wall is a plastic or aluminum end strip that is glued or clipped and is easier to clean. How to care for the worktop yourself can be found in [Care for and repair kitchen worktops](/anleitungen/kuechenarbeitsplatte-pflegen-und-reparieren/).

## How much silicone do I need?

A standard cartridge with 300 milliliters is enough for around 10 to 20 meters of joint, depending on the width of the joint. For a bathtub with three sides on the wall, one cartridge is usually sufficient. Buy a spare anyway, because an opened cartridge can only be stored for a limited time: closed with a nail or screw in the nozzle, it will last for a few weeks.

## Fresh silicone in the wrong place

If silicone goes wrong, don't smear it. Pick up fresh blobs with kitchen paper and wipe off the remains with a little spirit. Hardened silicone can only be removed from tiles and glass mechanically, for example with a glass scraper, and from sensitive surfaces with silicone remover.

## Practical example: The joint on the bathtub

The silicone joint between the bathtub and the tiles is black in several places and is coming loose in one corner. In the room below there is a small water stain on the ceiling.

1. Cut open the joint on both sides with the silicone knife and pull it out in long pieces.
2. Remains removed with silicone remover and scraped off with a plastic scraper.
3. Treated mold in the side of the joint with 70 percent alcohol.
4. Let it dry for a day with the bathroom door open and a fan.
5. Tub filled with water so it lowers.
6. Taped off, applied sanitary silicone, removed in one go with smoothing agent and joint smoother, crepe removed immediately.
7. Allow to harden for 24 hours, then drain the water.

The water stain on the ceiling dried over the following weeks and was then painted over.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I put new silicone over old?

No. Silicone does not stick to silicone. The old one has to come out completely.

### How wide should the joint be?

Usually 5 to 8 mm, depending on the width of the gap.

### Why does my new silicone turn black quickly?

Often the mold on the side was not removed or the joint was not dry. A lack of ventilation also promotes new infestations.

### What color should the silicone be?

Matching the tile joints or the sanitary object, usually white, gray or transparent. See [Renew tile grout](/anleitungen/fliesen-verfugen-und-fugen-erneuern/).

### Further questions from practice

**Transparent or white silicone?** Transparent silicone appears inconspicuous, but shows dirt and mold behind it. White or matching the color of the joints usually looks neater.

**Can I paint over silicone?** No, paint will not stick to it. Use acrylic for paintable joints in dry areas.

**Does silicone need a primer?** Not on glazed tiles, enamel and glass. A suitable primer significantly improves adhesion on absorbent surfaces such as natural stone, concrete or wood. The manufacturer of the silicone will name the appropriate one.

**Does ventilation help with curing?** Yes. Silicone hardens by reacting with moisture in the air, releasing substances that you don't want to breathe in. Ventilate well, but do not work in frost: below around five degrees, many silicones harden very slowly.

## Conclusion

A new silicone joint can be made in just a few hours and protects against expensive water damage. Remove all old silicone, treat mold, let the joint dry thoroughly, fill the tub with water before grouting, pull the silicone evenly, smooth with smoothing agent and let everything harden for 24 hours. With a squeegee and ventilation, the joint stays clean and tight for a long time.
