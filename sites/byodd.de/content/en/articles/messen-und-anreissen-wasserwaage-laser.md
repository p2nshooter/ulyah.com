“Measure twice, cut once” is the oldest craftsman rule and it still applies today. A shelf that is one centimeter crooked, a picture frame that is not aligned with the others, a laminate board that is two millimeters too short: almost all visible errors in DIY do not occur when drilling or sawing, but when measuring and marking.

These instructions show you how to work precisely with a folding rule, tape measure, square, spirit level and cross-line laser, how to clearly mark drill holes and cutting lines, how to check your spirit level and which measuring errors most often occur.

## The measuring tools at a glance

| tool | Strengths | Boundaries |
|---|---|---|
| Folding rule (folding rule) | Stiff, good for internal dimensions and heights, foldable | Joints can become imprecise |
| Tape measure | Long distances, curves, external dimensions | Sagging on long journeys |
| Try square | Rectangular lines on boards | Limited length |
| Spirit level | Align horizontally and vertically | Length limits accuracy over long distances |
| Cross line laser | Long lines across entire walls, several points at the same height | Difficult to see in bright light, price |
| Laser rangefinder | Measure room dimensions quickly and alone | Not for small dimensions on the workpiece |
| Chalk line | Long straight lines on the floor and wall | Chalk needs to be refilled |

## Measure correctly

### With the ruler

- **Fold out the links completely,** otherwise the dimensions will not be correct.
- **For internal measurements** (between two walls), place the ruler, take the measurement to the opposite side and read the last part using the pull-out brass slider at the end, if available.
- **Read vertically:** Look carefully at the scale from the front. Reading at an angle distorts the result by a millimeter or more.

### With the tape measure

- **The hook at the end is movable.** This is intentional: it compensates for its own thickness depending on whether you hook it (outside dimension) or butt it (inside dimension). A bent hook will distort all measurements.
- **Pull the tape tight** so that it doesn't sag.
- **For internal dimensions** press the case against the wall and add the printed case length if specified.

### For important dimensions

- **Measure twice,** preferably with a second tool.
- **Note down dimensions immediately,** with a sketch. Keeping a measurement in mind as you go to the saw is sure to lead to mistakes.
- **One tool per project:** Folding rule and tape measure may vary slightly. Use the same measuring tool for related measurements.

## Mark cleanly

- **Sharpened pencil** or a carpenter's pencil sharpened flat.
- **On dark or smooth surfaces** stick painter's tape on and draw on it; this can be removed without leaving a trace.
- **Mark drilling points with a cross**, not with a dot. The intersection of the lines is precise.
- **Draw cutting lines with a try square** and mark on which side of the line you will be sawing. The saw blade has a width, the so-called cutting loss.
- **For wood, mark with a knife:** For very precise cuts, a scribing knife scratches the fibers; the saw follows the notch more cleanly.

## The spirit level

### Reading dragonflies

A spirit level usually has a horizontal and a vertical bubble, sometimes a third for 45 degrees. The surface is horizontal or vertical if the air bubble is exactly between the two markings.

### Check spirit level

Spirit levels can become inaccurate due to falls. Here's how to check them:

1. Place the spirit level on an approximately flat surface and carefully note the position of the bubble.
2. Turn the spirit level 180 degrees (swap end to end) and place it in exactly the same place.
3. The bubble must be in the same position again. If it deviates, the scale is inaccurate.

For the vertical level, use the same principle on a door frame or wall.

### Longer routes

A 60 cm spirit level is enough for shelves and pictures. For longer distances, such as a row of wall cabinets, use a long spirit level or place the short level on a straight ruler.

## The cross line laser

A cross line laser projects a horizontal and a vertical line onto the wall. Most devices align themselves automatically using a pendulum.

### When is it worth it?

- **Picture walls and galleries:** all frames at the same height or a common center line. See [Design a gallery wall](/anleitungen/bilderwand-gestalten/).
- **Wall cabinets and wall shelves** over several meters.
- **Align tiles, panels and wallpaper strips** vertically.
- **Curtain rails** across the entire width of a window.

### This is how you use it

1. **Place on a tripod or stable surface** in the center of the room.
2. **Switch on and wait** until the pendulum has calmed down. If the line flashes, the device is too crooked.
3. **Adjust height:** Using a tripod or a base.
4. **Mark points with pencil** as long as the line is visible.
5. **In bright light** use laser glasses or a target board; Green lasers are more visible than red ones.

### Security

Never look into the laser beam or point it at other people's eye level.

## Check right angles: the 3-4-5 rule

You can check whether a corner is really right-angled using the Pythagorean theorem:

1. From the corner, measure and mark 30 cm on one side and 40 cm on the other.
2. Measure the distance between the two marks.
3. If it is exactly 50 cm, the corner is right-angled.

For larger areas the same works with 60, 80 and 100 cm. This is helpful when laying floors and aligning furniture. See [Laying laminate flooring](/anleitungen/laminat-verlegen/).

## Common measurement errors

| Error | Episode | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Keep measurements in mind | Swapped numbers | Write it down immediately |
| Read diagonally | One to two millimeters deviation | Read frontally |
| Spirit level not checked | Everything is systematically wrong | Envelope sample |
| Forget cutting loss | Board too short | Saw on the waste side of the line |
| Measured from the ground | If the floor is crooked, crooked shelves | Align with a spirit level or laser |
| Different measuring tools mixed | Small differences add up | One tool per project |

## Find the middle without calculating

An old carpenter's trick saves you from having to divide crooked measurements. For example, if a board is 17.3 centimeters wide, place the ruler diagonally across the board so that the zero is on one edge and the 20 on the other. At 10 you place a mark: it is exactly in the middle of the board width. If you repeat this further up or down on the board, connecting the two points creates the center line. This works with any conveniently divisible number that is larger than the width.

## Measure from a reference edge

If you mark several points in a row, you should take all measurements from the same reference edge instead of continuing to measure from point to point. With chain measurements, small inaccuracies add up: five distances with half a millimeter error each result in two and a half millimeters. Therefore, hold the tape measure in one place and read all positions one after the other. In a sketch you also write down the dimensions from the reference edge. This applies to drill holes in a rail as well as to the shelves of a cabinet.

## Use laser rangefinders correctly

By default, laser rangefinders usually measure from their rear edge. For internal dimensions, place the back of the device against the wall; check in the settings which reference point is active. A bright, flat target point provides the most reliable values, whereas glass and mirrors do not. Many devices calculate areas and room volumes directly, which helps when purchasing paint, flooring or wallpaper. Some even use Pythagoras to determine heights that cannot be directly targeted.

## Maintain measuring tools

Never let a tape measure snap back unchecked; The hook bends and all measurements become inaccurate. Keep the ruler and tape measure clean and dry, and do not place the level loosely in the toolbox where it will slide around. Cross line lasers can be tested with two opposite walls: The horizontal line is marked on both walls, the device is rotated and the markings are compared.

## Practical example: Three wall cabinets in a line

In a kitchen, three wall cabinets that are 60 cm wide should hang next to each other, with the bottom edge 55 cm above the worktop. The worktop itself is slightly crooked.

1. The desired height is measured at the highest point of the worktop.
2. Using the cross line laser, a horizontal line is projected across the entire wall and traced with a pencil.
3. The position of the hanging rail is measured upwards from this line.
4. The rail is screwed on and checked again with the long spirit level.

The cabinets hang in line and the small difference in height from the worktop is not noticeable.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my tape measure measure differently than the ruler?

Small deviations are normal. A loose hook on the tape measure or worn out joints on the ruler can cause major errors. Check both against each other.

### Is a cross line laser worth it for occasional DIY enthusiasts?

Not necessarily for individual images. It saves a lot of time for picture walls, kitchens and shelving walls. Simple devices no longer cost much these days.

### How do I adjust something if the ceiling and floor are crooked?

Align using a spirit level or laser, not the ceiling or floor. Visually, a compromise may be necessary if the crookedness is very noticeable.

### How do I mark on tiles?

With painter's tape and pencil. The crepe also protects when drilling.

## Checklist

- Measuring tools checked: hook of the tape measure, joints of the folding rule, spirit level.
- Measurements taken twice and noted.
- Drilling points marked as a cross on crepe for sensitive surfaces.
- Intersection side of the line marked.
- Align with a spirit level or laser, not to the floor or ceiling.

## Conclusion

Measuring accurately and marking accurately is the basis of every successful project. Use a ruler and tape measure correctly, note down every measurement, mark drilling points as a cross, check your spirit level with the envelope sample and use a cross line laser for long lines. With the 3-4-5 rule you can check right angles without special tools. So in the end everything hangs, stands and fits the way you planned.
