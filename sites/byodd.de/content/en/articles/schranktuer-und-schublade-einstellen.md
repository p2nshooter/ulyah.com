Kitchen cupboards, wardrobes and bathroom furniture have one thing in common: after a while, doors hang crooked, the joints between the fronts become uneven, a door no longer closes properly or a drawer gets stuck. This is rarely due to a defect, but rather because the furniture has settled, screws are loose or the fittings need to be adjusted. Modern hinges and pullouts are designed to be adjusted with a screwdriver.

This guide explains how cup hinges work, how to align cabinet doors step by step, repair broken hinges, and make drawers move and straighten their fronts.

## Understanding concealed hinges

Most modern furniture doors hang on **cup hinges:** A round cup is embedded in the door, an arm connects it to a **mounting plate** on the body. A concealed hinge usually has three setting options:

| adjusting screw | Location | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Wide adjustment** | On the front of the hinge arm, closer to the door | Slide the door to the left or right, gap width to the neighboring door |
| **Depth adjustment** | At the back of the arm or on the mounting plate | Door closer to the body or further away |
| **Height adjustment** | On the mounting plate (elongated holes or eccentrics) | Slide door up or down |

In some systems the screws are in slightly different places. The manufacturer's marking on the hinge helps you find the right instructions if in doubt.

## Aligning the cupboard door: step by step

### Preparation

- **Phillips screwdriver (PZ2)** by hand, not a cordless screwdriver, so that nothing is over-tightened.
- **Spirit level** or a straight board to check joints.
- **Check the body first:** Is the cabinet level? A crooked body leads to crooked doors. Align with leveling feet or wheel chocks. See [Assemble furniture without frustration](/anleitungen/moebel-aufbauen-tipps/).

### Order

1. **Check all mounting screws:** Tighten the screws that secure the pot and mounting plate. Often that is the solution.
2. **Adjust height:** Align the door so that it is flush with the neighboring doors at the top and bottom. For long holes, loosen the fastening screws of the mounting plates slightly, move the door, and tighten the screws. Adjust both hinges equally.
3. **Adjust side:** Use the side adjustment to make the gap between the neighboring door even (usually 2 to 3 mm).
   - **If the door hangs at the bottom of the handle side** (the joint at the top is wider than at the bottom), adjust the **upper** hinge so that the door moves to the top of the handle side, or the **lower** hinge so that the door moves to the bottom of the hinge side.
   - Always work in small increments (a quarter turn) and check after each change.
4. **Adjusting the depth:** If the door protrudes from the body at one corner, adjust the depth of the corresponding hinge until the door rests evenly everywhere.
5. **Check closing dampening:** Many hinges have integrated damping with a switch or regulator. If the door closes too slowly or slams, adjust.

## Repair a torn hinge

Chipboard only holds screws to a limited extent. If the screws on the mounting plate or pot loosen:

| Method | Procedure |
|---|---|
| Wood glue and toothpicks | Fill holes with wood glue and toothpicks or wood shavings, let dry, screw in screw again |
| Expansion dowels for furniture fittings | Special plastic dowels for chipboard that are inserted into the drilled hole |
| Move the mounting plate | Move slightly up or down if the slot offers room for maneuver |
| Repair plate for concealed hinges | There are repair kits for broken pot holes in the door |

See also [Use wood screws correctly](/anleitungen/holzschrauben-vorbohren-und-senken/).

## Drawers: stuck, crooked, jerky

### Wooden drawers without pull-outs

- **Clean treads** and rub with candle wax, soap or furniture lubricating wax.
- **Double up worn strips** with thin strips of hardwood or plastic sliding tape.
- **Swollen wood:** When the humidity is high, wooden drawers get stuck. Only reapply after it has been dry for a long time if it remains stuck.

### Drawers with roller or ball runners

1. **Removing the drawer:** Many full extension extensions have levers or clips on the side that you press when pulling them out.
2. **Check the running tracks:** Remove dirt, crumbs and hair. Lubricate ball extensions with a little silicone spray (not oil, which attracts dust).
3. **Tighten the rail screws** on the body.
4. **Replace bent rails**.

### Align the drawer front

Many modern drawer systems have adjustment options at the front:

- **Height and tilt:** Via adjusting screws on the front fastenings, often behind a cover cap inside the drawer.
- **Side:** Via eccentrics or slots in the front connection.
- **With simple drawers** the front is attached to the drawer box from the inside with screws. Loosen screws, align front, tighten. Tip: Stick double-sided adhesive tape on the inside of the front, press the front in the desired position, carefully open the drawer and screw the front tight from the inside.

## Joint pattern: the professional trick

In kitchens with many fronts, everything appears calmer if the joints are the same width. Use a piece of cardboard or plastic with the desired joint width (for example 3 mm) as a spacer between the fronts when adjusting.

## Common mistakes

| Error | Episode |
|---|---|
| Adjusted with the cordless screwdriver | Screws overtightened, thread damaged |
| Just one hinge adjusted | Door warps |
| Body not aligned | Doors cannot be permanently adjusted |
| Oil on ball focusers | Dust and dirt stick together the rails |
| Ripped holes ignored | Door keeps sagging |

## Recognize hinge types

If you want to replace a hinge, you need to know which one you have. What matters is how the door sits on the body. With **overlay** doors, the door completely covers the side wall; the hinge is straight. With **half-overlay** doors, two doors share a central wall, each covering half of it; the hinge is slightly cranked. With **internal** doors, the door sits in the body, flush with the front edge; the hinge is heavily cranked. There are also different opening angles, such as around 95, 110 or 155 degrees, and special hinges for corner cabinets or glass doors. Take the old hinge with you to the specialist retailer or write down the manufacturer's information, then the new one will surely fit.

## Remove clip hinges without tools

Many modern hinges are not screwed to the mounting plate, but rather clipped on. A small lever or button on the hinge arm releases the connection and the door can be removed without tools, for example to clean or paint the body. When reinserting it, hook the arm at the front and press it at the back until you hear it click into place. The settings are retained because they are saved in the hinge.

## Handleless fronts with pressure opening

In handle-free kitchens, doors and drawers open using a pressure mechanism: light pressure on the front triggers a spring-loaded plunger. If a door doesn't open or opens on its own, the distance between the front and the body is usually incorrect. Many of these openers can be adjusted in length, and the depth adjustment of the hinges also works here. Important: Hinges with built-in closing dampening work against the opener and usually have to be without damping when opening with pressure.

## Tall and heavy doors

The higher and heavier a door, the more hinges it needs. As a rough guide, on standard chipboard doors, two hinges support doors up to a height of almost one meter, above which a third hinge is added and, in the case of tall cabinet doors, a fourth hinge is often added. The manufacturer's information on door height and weight takes precedence. An additional hinge in the middle often prevents a tall door from sagging. If a long door becomes warped over time, there are straightening fittings for wardrobe doors that are mounted on the back and straighten the door again.

## Practical example: A kitchenette with crooked fronts

In a ten-year-old kitchen, the joints between the wall cupboard doors are uneven: one door hits the top of the neighboring door, another no longer closes completely, and the front of the drawer under the sink is crooked.

This is how the kitchen was cleaned in an hour:

1. **Check the body:** The spirit level shows that the base cabinet under the sink has sagged slightly due to moisture. The adjustable foot is re-turned.
2. **Tighten screws:** Screws on two mounting plates have become loose. One goes crazy. The hole is filled with wood glue and toothpicks, left to dry overnight and the screw is screwed in again the next day.
3. **Side adjustment:** The abutting door is adjusted half a turn to the hinge side on the top hinge until the joint at the top and bottom is the same width.
4. **Depth adjustment:** The non-closing door protrudes from the top. The depth screw on the top hinge is adjusted a quarter turn.
5. **Drawer front:** There are adjusting screws for height and inclination behind a cap inside the drawer. After one revolution the front is straight again.

A 3 mm plastic strip serves as a spacer for even joints.

## Checklist

- Body checked and aligned with a spirit level.
- Fastening screws tightened, torn holes repaired.
- Height, side and depth adjusted by hand in small increments.
- Both hinges of a door are taken into account.
- Extractors cleaned and lubricated with silicone spray.
- Joints checked with spacers.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my closet door keep hanging crooked?

Most of the time the fastening screws in the chipboard are torn out or the body is not standing straight.

### Can I replace old hinges with hinges with soft closing?

Often yes, if the pot diameter (usually 35 mm) and mounting plates fit. There are also retrofittable dampers.

### How do I adjust a glass door?

Glass door hinges have similar adjustment options but different fastenings. Pay attention to manufacturer's instructions.

### Further questions from practice

**The door slams loudly.** Concealed hinges without dampening can be retrofitted with attachable dampers or buffers. Small felt or rubber buffers on the body also provide dampening.

**A drawer opens by itself.** The body is tilted forward. Check with the spirit level and slightly raise the front leveling feet.

**The damping is no longer effective.** Built-in dampers wear off after many years or are set differently on several hinges on a door. Check that the same switch is activated on all hinges and replace a broken hinge with one of the same type.

## Conclusion

Crooked cupboard doors and stuck drawers can usually be fixed in just a few minutes: align the body, tighten the fastening screws and adjust the height, side and depth of the cup hinges by hand in small steps. Repair torn holes with glue and toothpicks or furniture dowels, clean pull-outs and lubricate them with silicone spray and align fronts with spacers to create even joints.
