The water in the sink is draining more and more slowly, it is ankle deep in the shower, and an unpleasant smell comes from the kitchen drain: clogged drains are one of the most common household annoyances. In most cases, the problem can be solved in a few minutes and without a drain cleaning service if you follow the correct sequence.

This guide shows you how to remove blockages step by step, from the simplest home remedy to the drain cleaning cable, why chemical drain cleaners are not a good idea and how to prevent new blockages.

## Typical causes

| Location | Most common cause |
|---|---|
| sink | Hair, soap residue, toothpaste |
| Shower and bathtub | Hair in connection with soap and lime |
| kitchen sink | Grease, leftover food, coffee grounds |
| toilet | Too much paper, wet wipes, hygiene products |

## First narrow it down: Where is the constipation?

A minute of observation often saves an hour of work. Run some water on each nearby drain one at a time and pay attention to how it drains:

| Observation | Likely location | Start here |
|---|---|---|
| Only one pool drains slowly, all others drain normally | Strainer, stopper or siphon of this basin | Step 1, then step 5 |
| Water drains well at first and then accumulates | Restriction in the siphon or just behind it | Clean the siphon, then the spiral |
| The sink and shower in the same bathroom are both sluggish | Common drain pipe in the wall | Spiral over the sink |
| When the dishwasher is drained, water rises in the sink | Kitchen siphon or line behind it | Sink siphon |
| Toilet, shower and sink affected at the same time | Downpipe or house connection | Landlord or specialist company |

The more drains are affected, the deeper the problem lies in the pipe network and the less effective the resources that are poured into an individual basin from above.

## Step 1: Clean strainers and stoppers

The blockage is often located directly under the drain strainer.

1. **Remove the drain stopper or strainer.** On sinks with a drain fitting, the stopper can usually be unscrewed or pulled out; sometimes the tie rod under the pelvis needs to be loosened.
2. **Remove hair and dirt,** preferably with gloves.
3. **Use a plastic hair catcher** (a thin strip with barbs, very inexpensive) into the drain and pull out balls of hair.

This is often the solution for showers and bathtubs.

### Remove plug with pull rod

Many sinks have a drain fitting with a pull button behind the faucet. The stopper then sits on a horizontal lever that reaches into the drain pipe from behind and cannot be easily pulled out.

1. **Look under the basin:** At the back of the vertical drain pipe there is a small union nut from which the horizontal lever comes.
2. **Loosen the nut by hand** and pull the lever out a few centimeters. Place a cloth underneath, it drips a little.
3. **Remove the plug upwards** and wipe off the hair and soap sludge that usually stick to the bottom part.
4. **When installing**, guide the lever back through the eyelet on the plug, otherwise the plug can no longer be operated. Tighten the nut hand tight and check for drips.

In bathtubs, the valve plug is located in the bottom of the bathtub and is operated using a rotary handle on the overflow. On many models it can be unscrewed or pulled upwards; hair often hangs on the valve body.

## Step 2: Hot water and dish soap

If there are grease deposits in the kitchen, it often helps to pour a pot of hot (not boiling) water with a little dishwashing liquid down the drain. **Caution with plastic pipes:** Boiling water can damage older plastic pipes and seals.

## Step 3: Pümpel (suction cup)

The plunger creates pressure and suction and loosens many blockages.

1. **Seal overflow:** Sinks and bathtubs have an overflow opening. Hold this tightly with a wet cloth, otherwise the pressure will escape.
2. **Leave some water in the basin** so that the rubber bell is covered.
3. **Put on the plunger** and press firmly and pull sharply several times.
4. **Check** whether the water is draining. Repeat if necessary.

## Step 4: Home Remedies Baking Soda and Vinegar

The mixture of baking soda (or baking powder) and vinegar bubbles and can loosen mild clogs:

1. Pour three to four tablespoons of baking soda down the drain.
2. Pour in half a cup of vinegar.
3. Close the drain and let it work for 15 to 30 minutes.
4. Rinse with hot water.

However, this hardly helps with stubborn hairballs or solid foreign bodies.

## Step 5: Clean the siphon

The siphon is the curved piece of pipe under the washbasin or sink. There is water in it that retains odors from the sewer system, and a lot of dirt also collects here.

1. Place **buckets and cloths** under the siphon.
2. **Loosing the union nuts:** Usually possible by hand, otherwise with water pump pliers and a cloth as protection.
3. **Remove the siphon** and let the water flow into the bucket.
4. **Cleaning:** Remove dirt with a bottle brush and warm water.
5. **Check Seals:** Replace porous or deformed seals.
6. **Reassemble:** Tighten the nuts hand-tight, then tighten them a quarter turn with the pliers. Do not overtighten, plastic threads are sensitive.
7. **Let the water run and check for leaks.**

In showers and bathtubs, the siphon is located under the tub and is often only accessible via an inspection opening or from above via the drain. Many shower drains have a removable odor trap.

## Step 6: Pipe cleaning spiral

If the blockage is deeper in the pipe, a pipe cleaning spiral (spiral with a crank, available from around €10 to €20) can help.

1. **Remove the siphon** (see above) and insert the spiral directly into the drain pipe in the wall.
2. **Slowly advance the spiral** while turning the crank.
3. **If there is resistance**, continue turning and moving back and forth slightly until the clog dislodges or gets caught in the spiral tip.
4. **Pull out the spiral,** Remove dirt, rinse with plenty of water.
5. **Reinstall the siphon.**

Electric pipe cleaning devices are available to rent from hardware stores.

## Why chemical pipe cleaners are problematic

Chemical pipe cleaners often contain highly corrosive substances such as sodium hydroxide.

- They can **burn skin and eyes**, especially if they splash.
- They **attack older pipes and seals.**
- If you don't clear the blockage, there will be a caustic mixture in the basin and the next step (removing the siphon) will be dangerous.
- They pollute the environment.

Mechanical methods are more effective and safer in almost all cases.

## Toilet clogged

1. **Don't rinse again and again,** otherwise the bowl will overflow.
2. Use **toilet plunger** (with collar) and work vigorously.
3. **Hot water with dish soap** can help with paper clogs.
4. **Toilet spiral** for deeper blockages.
5. If nothing works or multiple drains are affected at the same time, the problem is probably in the main line. Then inform the landlord or a pipe cleaning service.

## Dishwasher or washing machine does not drain

If water remains in the dishwasher or if the washing machine reports an error when pumping out, it is not always the drain in the house. Check in this order:

1. **Switch off the device and unplug it.** After a hot program, let the water cool down.
2. **Cleaning the sieve in the device:** The dishwasher has a removable sieve combination at the bottom of the washing compartment. The washing machine usually has a lint filter behind a flap at the bottom of the front. Beforehand, drain the remaining water into a shallow bowl using the small emergency drain hose and place towels underneath. Typical finds include coins, hair clips and buttons.
3. **Check the drain hose:** It must not be kinked or caught behind the device.
4. **Connection to the siphon:** Dishwashers often hang on the device connection of the kitchen siphon. If this siphon is blocked, the pumped water rises in the sink; then step 5 will help.

**Typical error after replacing a siphon:** Some siphons are delivered with a closed device connection. If the cap is not removed before connecting, the device will not be able to pump.

## When the professional should come

- Several drains in the apartment are blocked at the same time.
- Water pushes up from the drain when water flows elsewhere.
- The constipation keeps coming back.
- There is a constant smell of sewage.

In apartment buildings, this often affects the shared lines for which the landlord or property management is responsible.

## Common mistakes when clearing

| Error | Episode | Better |
|---|---|---|
| With the siphon removed, turn on the tap out of habit | Water runs into the base cabinet | Close corner valves or stick a note on the tap |
| Push the spiral forward with force without cranks | Spiral kinks, pipe bends suffer | Turn evenly, little pressure |
| Combine different cleaners | Violent reactions, dangerous fumes | Work mechanically |
| Plunge for a drain cleaner | Caustic splashes on the face | Rinse thoroughly first and wear safety glasses |
| Seals tilted or forgotten | Siphon drips | Take photos before dismantling |
| Spiral put away wet | Rust, difficult to move next time | Rinse, dry, lightly oil |

## Prevent

- **Use hair strainers** in the shower and bathtub.
- **Do not pour fat into the sink:** Allow the frying fat to cool and dispose of it in the trash.
- **Coffee grounds and leftover food** in the organic waste.
- **Never put wet wipes, hygiene products and cotton swabs** in the toilet.
- **Rinse regularly with hot water** and clean the sieves.
- **Clean the siphon once a year**.

## Practical example: The shower no longer drains

After a few minutes in a shower the water is ankle deep. Baking soda and vinegar didn't help.

1. **Cover removed:** The shower drain has an attached cover and a removable odor trap underneath.
2. **Odour trap pulled out:** A big ball of hair is hanging from it.
3. **Hair catcher introduced:** More hair pulled out of the tube.
4. **Rinsed with hot water,** Cleaned the trap and reinstalled it.
5. **Prevention:** A stainless steel hair strainer now lies on the drain and is emptied once a week.

The whole operation took ten minutes and cost nothing except a hair strainer for a few euros.

## Checklist

- Check the strainer and stopper first.
- Seal overflow when pumping.
- Open the siphon over a bucket.
- No caustic pipe cleaners.
- Report several clogged drains to the landlord.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does my drain smell even though it's not clogged?

The odor trap is often dried out (in rarely used drains) or the siphon is dirty. Add water and clean the siphon.

### Can I use baking powder instead of baking soda?

Yes, the effect is similar, but slightly weaker.

### How often should I clean the siphon?

Once a year for normal use, more often in kitchens with a lot of fat.

### How much does a drain cleaning service cost?

Depending on the region, time and effort, from around €100 to significantly more. Weekend emergency services are expensive; Ask for prices beforehand.

### I dropped a ring down the drain. What now?

Stop running water immediately. Heavy small parts usually remain in the siphon because there is water there. Place the bucket underneath, remove the siphon and search the contents in a bowl. When showering, it's worth taking a look at the removable odor trap.

### Further questions from practice

**Does the drain gurgle when you flush the toilet?** This may indicate a restriction in the common line or a clogged vent. If it persists, inform the landlord or an installer.

**Do enzyme cleaners help?** Biological cleaners with enzymes can slowly break down organic deposits and are gentler than caustic agents. In the case of acute constipation, mechanical methods work more quickly.

## Conclusion

Most blockages can be solved with simple means: clean the sieve, hair catcher, plunger with a sealed overflow, clean the siphon and, if necessary, a pipe cleaning cable. Avoid using caustic pipe cleaners, use hair strainers and no grease in the drain and get help if several drains are affected or the blockage keeps coming back.
