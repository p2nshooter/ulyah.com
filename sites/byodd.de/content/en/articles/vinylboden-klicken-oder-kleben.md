Vinyl floors, often also called design floors, have developed from a niche solution into one of the best-selling floor coverings in just a few years. They are warm underfoot, quiet when walking, easy to care for and, depending on the version, even suitable for the bathroom and kitchen. At the same time, the range is confusing: click vinyl, adhesive vinyl, rigid vinyl, vinyl with cork or HDF core, wear layers in tenths of a millimeter.

These instructions explain the differences, help with selection, show how to lay click and adhesive vinyl and give tips on care.

## What is vinyl flooring?

Vinyl floors consist of PVC core and several layers:

- **Wear layer:** transparent protective layer, crucial for durability.
- **Decorative layer:** printed film with a wood, stone or fantasy look.
- **Backing layer:** provides stability; Depending on the product, made of flexible vinyl, stiff mineral composite (Rigid) or wood fiber (HDF).
- **Counterforce or integrated insulation:** on the underside, often with impact sound insulation.

### Wear layer and usage class

| wear layer | Typical use |
|---|---|
| 0.2 to 0.3mm | Bedrooms, rooms with little use |
| 0.3 to 0.55mm | Living rooms, hallways |
| 0.55mm and more | Highly used areas, including commercial ones |

Also pay attention to the **usage class** according to the standard (e.g. 23 for heavily used living spaces, 33 for heavily used commercial areas) and the suitability for underfloor heating.

## The variants in comparison

| Variant | Construction | Laying | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adhesive vinyl | Thin, flexible planks (2 to 3 mm) | Fully glued | Very low, ideal for renovation, resilient, waterproof | The surface must be perfectly flat, glue is required |
| Flexible click vinyl | Vinyl core with click profile (4 to 6 mm) | Floating | No glue, waterproof | Soft, shows imperfections |
| Rigid vinyl (SPC) | Stiff mineral vinyl core | Floating | Bridges small unevenness, retains its shape during temperature changes, and is waterproof | Harder, a little louder |
| Vinyl with HDF carrier | Vinyl decor on fibreboard | Floating | Cheap, quiet | Not for damp rooms, swells when wet |

**Rule of thumb:** For bathrooms and kitchens, rigid or adhesive vinyl; all variants for living rooms and bedrooms; Rather rigid on uneven old building floors; If the installation height is low, for example in front of doors that should not be shortened, adhesive vinyl.

| criterion | Vinyl | Laminate |

| Walking noise | Quieter | Louder, “clacking” |

| Scratch resistance | Well, depending on the wear layer | Very good |

## Lay click vinyl

The procedure is similar to laminate, with some special features:

1. **Prepare the surface:** level, clean, dry and firm. Flexible click vinyl is less forgiving than rigid vinyl. Remove unevenness with leveling compound.
2. **Acclimate:** Store indoors for 24 to 48 hours.
3. **Impact sound insulation:** Only use if specified or permitted by the manufacturer. Insulation that is too soft will damage the click connection. Many products have integrated insulation.
4. **Vapor barrier** on mineral substrates if the manufacturer provides it.
5. **Expansion joint:** usually 5 to 8 mm to walls and fixtures.
6. **Cutting:** Vinyl can often be cut with a cutter knife: score the surface and break off the plank over the edge. Rigid vinyl as well, but with stronger cracks.
7. **Lay row by row,** joints staggered, remaining pieces at the beginning of the row.
8. **Attach skirting boards** to the wall.

### Click laying tool

For click vinyl you don't need much: a sharp cutter knife with spare blades, a try square, folding rule and pencil, spacer wedges for the expansion joint, a tapping block and a pull bar for the last row and a rubber mallet. A jigsaw or a fine Japanese saw can help to make recesses in door frames and pipes. Knee pads make a long day of laying much more pleasant. When laying, mix planks from several packages so that similar decors do not pile up next to each other and the floor appears more natural.

## Lay adhesive vinyl

Adhesive vinyl requires more preparation, but is particularly quiet and resilient.

1. **Smoothing the surface:** The surface must be smooth and even, as any unevenness will show through the thin covering. The floor is usually leveled and sanded over the entire surface with a filler.
2. **Prime** according to the adhesive manufacturer's instructions.
3. **Installation plan:** Mark the center line in the room and lay from there so that the edge strips are even.
4. **Apply glue:** Using the prescribed notched trowel and only as much surface as can be covered during the open time.
5. **Insert the planks** and press firmly, end to end.
6. **Rolling:** Rub the surface thoroughly with a heavy roller or pressure roller so that the adhesive covers the entire surface.
7. **Remove any adhesive residue** immediately with a damp cloth.
8. Wait for **setting time** before placing furniture.

Adhesive vinyl is more demanding for beginners. The filling work on the substrate is the most complex part; Many people have this step done by a specialist company and lay the covering themselves.

## Vinyl on tiles

Vinyl can often be installed directly over old tiles. Important:

- **With click vinyl:** Tiles firmly and evenly, joints no deeper than a few millimeters. Rigid vinyl bridges joints better.
- **With adhesive vinyl:** Joints must be filled, otherwise they will stand out.

## Underfloor heating

Most vinyl floors are suitable for hot water underfloor heating. Often the surface temperature must not exceed 27 °C. Observe the manufacturer's instructions and the suitability of insulation and adhesive.

## Care

- **Sweep or vacuum** with a soft brush.
- **Wipe with a damp cloth** using a pH-neutral cleaner. No abrasive cleaners, no solvent-based cleaners.
- **Felt gliders** under furniture, soft wheels under office chairs.
- **Avoid rubber:** Rubber feet and some mats can cause permanent discoloration.
- **Sun:** Strong direct sunlight may fade or stretch some vinyl floors. Rigid vinyl is more dimensionally stable.

## Common mistakes

| Error | Episode |
|---|---|
| Unsuitable impact sound insulation under click vinyl | Broken click connection |
| Uneven ground not balanced | Dents, visible joints |
| No expansion joint | Bulging when warm |
| HDF vinyl in the bathroom | swelling |
| Too much glue applied at once | Glue peeled off, floorboards don't stick |

## Calculate your needs correctly

Measure each room and add up the floor space. For the waste, you should add around 5 to 10 percent for straight installation in simple rooms, and more for many corners, niches or diagonal installation. If possible, purchase all packages from the same batch that is listed on the box, as the color tone may vary slightly between production runs. A leftover package in the basement is worth its weight in gold if a floorboard needs to be replaced later. With adhesive vinyl, glue, primer and filler are added; Their consumption per square meter is stated on the respective container.

## Doors, transitions and heating pipes

New ground is higher than the old. Before purchasing, check whether the doors still swing freely, or plan to shorten them, as described in [Door is stuck or dragging](/anleitungen/tuer-klemmt-oder-schleift/). At doorways, transition profiles separate the surfaces of two rooms; Many manufacturers also stipulate expansion joints above a certain room length because vinyl also expands when heated. For heating pipes, drill holes in the floorboard that are slightly larger than the pipe, cut out the piece behind it in a wedge shape and put it back in after laying. Pipe rosettes cover the joint cleanly.

## Check the old floor

Carpets must be removed before laying as they are too soft as a base. Solid tiles, parquet or floorboards can often be left in place if the manufacturer allows this. Be careful with very old floor coverings: Until the 1980s, so-called floor flex panels were installed, small hard vinyl panels that can contain asbestos, and some old black adhesives contain problematic tar substances. Do not break, grind or scrape such coverings. If in doubt, have a material sample examined and, if the suspicion is confirmed, hire experts or lay the new floor over it without damaging the old one.

## Practical example: Click vinyl in the bathroom on old tiles

A bathroom floor with old but solid tiles needs to be modernized without any chiseling work. A rigid vinyl with integrated insulation that is expressly approved for wet rooms is chosen.

1. **Check tiles:** All solid, no hollow spots. The joints are around 2 mm deep, which according to the manufacturer is not critical for rigid vinyl.
2. **Clean and acclimatize:** Floor cleaned thoroughly, packages stored in the bathroom for two days.
3. **Toilet:** For a clean solution, the toilet was dismantled by an installer and reassembled after installation. Alternatively, you can cut out the vinyl around the foot.
4. **Laying:** Expansion joint 5 mm, planks scored and broken with the cutter, carefully adjusted around the vanity unit.
5. **Sealing:** Edge joints to the tub and shower area closed with silicone as intended by the manufacturer.
6. **Plastic skirting boards** glued to the wall.

Effort: one day. Result: a quiet, wood-look floor that is warm to the feet.

## Checklist

- Variant suitable for the room (damp room, underfloor heating).
- Surface level and stable.
- Insulation only after manufacturer approval.
- Expansion joint maintained.
- Edge joints in the bathroom sealed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is vinyl harmful?

Modern branded floors are free of certain problematic plasticizers. Look for test seals for low emissions. Ventilate well after laying.

### How long does vinyl flooring last?

With a good wear layer and care, 15 to 20 years or longer.

### Can you sand vinyl?

No. Damaged floorboards are replaced with click vinyl, and with adhesive vinyl they are cut out and replaced.

### Is vinyl suitable for the bathroom?

Rigid and adhesive vinyl yes, if they are designated as waterproof. Seal the edge joints in the bathroom with silicone if the manufacturer specifies it.

### Further questions from practice

**Can I put vinyl under the fitted kitchen in the kitchen?** Floating floors should not be held in place by heavy fixtures. It is better to lay it up to the base panels and leave the kitchen on the old surface.

**How ​​do I repair a scratch?** Small scratches with manufacturer's repair paste. With click vinyl, damaged planks at the edge can be replaced.

**Do I need a vapor barrier under click vinyl?** This depends on the product. On mineral substrates such as cement screed, many manufacturers prescribe a vapor barrier, usually a PE film of 0.2 mm, the strips of which overlap and are glued to the joints. They have already integrated some insulation underlays. The deciding factor is the installation instructions for the floor.

## Conclusion

Vinyl floors are warm underfoot, quiet and easy to care for. Choose adhesive vinyl for low installation heights and maximum load capacity, rigid vinyl for uneven surfaces and damp rooms, flexible click vinyl for flat living spaces and vinyl with HDF backing only for dry rooms. Prepare the surface carefully, pay attention to the insulation, expansion joints and manufacturer's instructions, and you will enjoy the floor for many years to come.
