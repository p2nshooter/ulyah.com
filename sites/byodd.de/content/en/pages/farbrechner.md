Too little paint means a second trip to the hardware store and possibly a different shade from a new batch. Too much paint has been sitting in the basement for years. The calculator above determines the area and liters you really need.

## This is how the color calculator calculates

1. **Wall area:** Perimeter of the room times the room height, i.e. 2 × (length + width) × height.
2. **Deductions:** 2 m² per door, the specified area per window.
3. **Ceiling:** Length × width available upon request.
4. **Liter:** Area × number of coats ÷ coverage in m² per liter, plus 10% reserve.

| Example | Value |
|---|---|
| Room 4.5 × 3.6 m, height 2.5 m | Wall area 40.5 m² |
| a door, a window with 1.5 m² | 37.0 m² to be painted |
| two coats, coverage 7 m²/l | 10.6 l, with reserve about 12 l |

## Yield: the most important number

How far a liter goes depends on the color and the surface. The manufacturer states the yield on the bucket, often as a range. Roughly plastered, absorbent or very dark walls need more paint than smooth, already painted ones. New plaster and plasterboard are primed first. If in doubt, use the lower value of the range.

## Stroke healthily

Wall paints with the Blue Angel are low in emissions and do not require preservatives. Ventilate well when painting and in the days afterwards, especially in children's rooms and bedrooms.
