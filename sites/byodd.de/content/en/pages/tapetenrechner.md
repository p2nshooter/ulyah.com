When it comes to wallpaper, it's not the area that counts, but rather the number of strips that come out of a roll. A large repeat can cost an entire web per roll. The calculator above takes this into account.

## This is how the wallpaper calculator calculates

1. **Track length:** Room height plus 10 cm allowance at the top and bottom. For patterned wallpaper, the number is rounded up to whole repeats; If the approach is offset, half a repeat is added.
2. **Tracks:** Wall circumference divided by roll width. We only remove half of the doors and windows because there is still wallpaper hanging above and below them.
3. **Lanes per roll:** Roll length divided by the lane length, rounded down.
4. **Rolls:** Lanes divided by lanes per roll, rounded up.

| role | Dimension | At a room height of 2.5 m without rapport |
|---|---|---|
| Standard role | 0.53 × 10.05 m | 3 lanes per roll |
| double-wide non-woven wallpaper | 1.06 × 10.05 m | 3 strips per roll, twice as wide |
| woodchip | 0.53 × 17 m | 6 lanes per roll |
| woodchip | 0.53 × 25 m | 9 lanes per roll |

## Rapport and approach

The repeat is the height at which a pattern repeats itself. With a straight approach, the pattern is at the same height on all panels; with an offset approach, every other panel is shifted by half a repeat. Both are symbols on the label. Plain-colored wallpaper and woodchip have no repeat.

## Before purchasing

Make sure you have the same batch number on all rolls and buy a roll as a backup. If you are renting an apartment, it is worth taking a look at the rental agreement before working with bold patterns. The instructions [wallpapering with woodchip and non-woven wallpaper] (/anleitungen/tapezieren-raufaser-und-vliestapete/) show you how to do the wallpapering yourself.
