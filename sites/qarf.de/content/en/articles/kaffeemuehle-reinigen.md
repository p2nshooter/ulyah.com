The coffee grinder is the most neglected appliance in many coffee kitchens. The machine is descaled, the pot is washed, but the mill runs for years without anyone looking inside. Fine coffee dust and oils collect in the grinder, in the ejection opening and in the bean container, which become rancid over time. They give the freshly ground coffee a musty, bitter or rancid aftertaste and can even worsen the quality of the grind. Cleaning is not difficult and usually only takes a quarter of an hour. This guide shows you how to do it.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Coffee beans contain oils that build up in the grinder and become rancid. Dark, oily roasts contaminate the mill more quickly.
> - Wipe the bean container and chute **weekly**, clean the grinder thoroughly about **every one to three months**, more often for espresso and dark roasts.
> - Grinder **disconnect from power**, remove beans, remove grinder, clean with brush, brush and vacuum cleaner.
> - Only clean grinders wet if the manufacturer allows it and then allow them to dry thoroughly.
> - Special cleaning granules bind oils and dust, but do not replace occasional thorough cleaning.

## Why a clean mill tastes better

Coffee beans contain natural oils. During grinding, some of it is released and adheres to the grinding media, in the grinding channel and on the walls. In addition, there is fine coffee dust that gets stuck in corners and crevices. Both smell like fresh coffee. However, after a few weeks the oils oxidize and the powder becomes old. Each new portion takes some of these residues with it. The result is a coffee that tastes flatter, bitter or slightly rancid, even though the beans are fresh.

There is also a technical reason: sticky residues can clog the ejection and coat the grinding media so that they grind more unevenly. With some electric grinders, the motor runs harder and becomes louder.

## How often clean?

| task | How often | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| Wipe ejector and housing | daily to weekly | dry cloth, brush |
| Wash out the bean container | weekly to every two weeks | warm water, dish soap, dry well |
| Clean the grinder thoroughly | every 1 to 3 months | dismantle, brush, vacuum |
| Let the cleaning granules run through | According to the manufacturer's information, approximately monthly | Grind the granules, grind away the rest |

If you grind espresso every day or use dark, oily beans, you are more likely to clean it monthly. For light roasts and a few cups per week, an interval of two to three months is sufficient.

## What you need

- a stiff-bristled brush or a small cleaning brush
- a vacuum cleaner with a narrow nozzle
- a dry microfiber cloth and a damp cloth
- Toothpicks or a wooden skewer for narrow crevices
- warm water and mild detergent for removable plastic and glass parts
- the operating instructions for your grinder if you don't know how to remove the grinder

## Electric mill step by step

1. **Disconnect power:** Always unplug before reaching into the grinder.
2. **Remove the beans:** Remove the bean container and pour the beans into a can. Let the mill run empty beforehand to get any residue out of the grinder.
3. **Wash the bean container:** Rinse it with warm water and a little dishwashing liquid and allow it to dry completely. No dishwasher if the manufacturer doesn't allow it.
4. **Removing the grinder:** On many grinders, the upper grinder can be unscrewed or lifted off. Note the position or take a photo so that you can find the grind level later.
5. **Brushing:** Thoroughly brush the teeth of both grinders. Oily residue can be removed with a dry cloth or toothbrush.
6. **Suction:** Vacuum the grinding chamber and the ejection channel. This is often where the most old powder sits.
7. **Exposure sputum:** Carefully poke the sputum with a wooden skewer if there are any lumps stuck there.
8. **Assemble:** Put the grinder back in place, adjust to your usual grind setting, and grind away a small amount of beans before brewing again.

## Hand mill step by step

1. **Disassemble:** Unscrew the crank and adjusting nut and remove the axle with the inner cone. Pay attention to small parts like springs and washers and place them in the correct order.
2. **Brushing:** Brush the cone and grinding ring with a brush. Most hand mills get completely clean this way.
3. **Housing and collection container:** Wipe with a dry or slightly damp cloth. You can often rinse the collection container with water if it is made of glass or plastic.
4. **Assemble and calibrate:** Assemble everything in reverse order and find your zero point as described in [Set the grind correctly](/ratgeber/mahlgrad-richtig-einstellen/).

## Cleaning granules and tablets

For many mills there are cleaning granules made from pressed, food-safe materials, often grain-based. You grind a portion through the grinder like you would coffee. The granules absorb oils and dust and transport them out of the grinder. Then grind a few grams of coffee to remove any remaining granules.

This is practical for mills whose grinders are difficult to access and for intermediate cleaning. However, it is no substitute for thorough cleaning where you actually see the grinder. Only use products that are specifically designed for coffee grinders. **Rice**, which is sometimes recommended as a home remedy, is controversial: the hard grains can put a strain on the grinder or motor of some mills, and some manufacturers expressly advise against it.

## Clean impact knife mills

Simple electric mills with rotating blades, so-called impact blade mills, do not have a grinder to remove. Unplug, pour out the powder and wipe the grinding bowl with a dry cloth or brush. Powder tends to collect under the knife; work carefully with a brush there because the blades are sharp. If the shell is removable, it can be washed often, but the motor base can never be. Anyone who grinds spices in such a mill should clean it particularly thoroughly afterwards or use their own device for this, because cardamom, cinnamon or pepper taste good for a long time.

## Cone and disc grinders in comparison

With conical grinders, the inner cone or outer ring can often be unscrewed without tools. Disc grinders are often attached to the carrier with screws; It's worth taking a photo before loosening so that the washers and screws are in the same place again. The following applies to both: Do not hit the cutting edges against each other and keep the contact surfaces clean, otherwise the grinder will sit crooked after assembly and grind unevenly.

## What you should avoid

- **Grinder into the water:** Steel grinders can rust and residual moisture causes the next ground coffee to clump together. Clean only as directed by the manufacturer.
- **Dishwasher:** Not a good idea for most parts, especially wood, aluminum and coated parts.
- **Sharp tools on the cutting edges:** Knives or screwdrivers can damage the grinding media.
- **Disassembling with beans in the hopper:** With some grinders, beans would otherwise fall into the grinding chamber and block during assembly.
- **Forced opening:** If the grinder cannot be removed, check the instructions or ask the manufacturer instead of using force.

## Beans that pollute the grinder more

Very dark roasts that have an oily sheen leave significantly more residue behind. Flavored beans, such as those with vanilla or hazelnut aroma, stick particularly strongly and transfer their taste to everything that is ground afterwards. If you use such beans, clean the mill particularly thoroughly afterwards or use your own mill for it. How roasting levels differ is explained [Roasting levels explained](/ratgeber/roestgrade-erklaert/).

## After cleaning: Check the grinding level

After assembly, the grinder sometimes sits slightly differently. So check the processing time and taste the next time you make coffee and adjust if necessary. If the grinder has a note field or scale, enter your preferred settings. Tips for readjustment can be found in [Understanding Extraction](/ratgeber/extraktion-verstehen/).

## Less residue in everyday life

A lot of cleaning work can be avoided. Only put as many beans in the hopper as you will use in a few days to prevent them from aging openly and releasing their oil into the container. If your grinder is prone to static electricity and powder sticks to the walls, it often helps to wet the weighed beans with a single drop of water and shake them briefly before grinding. Some mills have bellows that you can use to push residue out of the grinding chamber. And after grinding, gently tap the sputum instead of hitting it with hard objects.

## Dry and store after cleaning

Parts that have come into contact with water must be completely dry before reinstalling them. It's best to leave them exposed to air for a few hours. Residual moisture in the grinding chamber causes the powder to clump together and, in the worst case, mold can form in poorly ventilated corners. If you don't use the grinder for a long time, empty the funnel, clean the grinder and store it dry.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does my grinder smell like old coffee even though I have cleaned it?

Then there are probably still residues in the ejection channel or under the lower grinding media. In some mills this can also be expanded. A vacuum cleaner with a narrow nozzle often reaches the last corners.

### Do I have to clean the grinder when I change bean types?

Not every time. It is enough to grind away a few grams of the new beans so that the remains of the old ones disappear.

### When do I have to replace the grinder?

If, despite cleaning, the grinder grinds significantly more unevenly, produces more dust and no longer produces coffee, the grinder may be dull. With normal household use this usually takes many years. More about grinder purchase and service life in [Coffee grinder: cone or disc grinder](/ratgeber/kaffeemuehle-kegel-oder-scheibenmahlwerk/).

### Can I oil the grinder?

No. Cooking oil goes rancid just like coffee oil. Some manufacturers recommend a special food-safe grease for bearings or threads, but the grinding media themselves remain dry and clean.

## Conclusion

A clean grinder is the easiest way to better coffee. Wipe it regularly, brush out the grinder every few weeks and let everything dry thoroughly: that's all you need to ensure that fresh beans taste fresh.
