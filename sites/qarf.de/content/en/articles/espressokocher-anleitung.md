The espresso maker is part of the basic kitchen equipment in Germany, Italy and many other countries. The octagonal aluminum jug with the black handle, often simply called “Bialetti” after the best-known manufacturer, costs little, requires no electricity and, if cared for properly, will last for decades. Nevertheless, the coffee often tastes bitter, burnt or metallic. This is almost never due to the pot, but rather to a few actions: ground too finely, pressed too tightly, cooked too hot, removed from the heat too late. With the right steps you can get a strong, sweet and full coffee that is close to an espresso.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - An espresso maker only produces around 1 to 2 bar of pressure. The result is strong coffee, but not real espresso with a dense crema.
> - Grind a little coarser than for espresso, like fine sand.
> - Fill with water until just below the safety valve, pour in the ground coffee loosely and **do not press down**.
> - Work with preheated water and medium heat, remove the pot from the heat as soon as the coffee comes out light and bubbling.
> - Clean only with warm water without detergent, check the seal and sieve regularly.

## This is how the espresso maker works

The pot consists of three parts: the **lower kettle** for the water with a safety valve on the side, the **funnel insert** for the ground coffee and the **upper container** in which the finished coffee rises. There is a metal sieve and a rubber seal between the funnel and the upper part.

When you heat the pot, steam forms in the lower kettle. The pressure increases and pushes the hot water up through the riser pipe and through the ground coffee. The finished coffee comes out through the tube in the middle of the upper container. The pressure is around one to two bar, significantly less than the around nine bar of a portafilter machine. Therefore, there is no dense crema and the coffee is slightly less concentrated than espresso, but much stronger than filter coffee. [Portafilter and espresso: the basics](/ratgeber/siebtraeger-espresso-grundlagen/) explains how real espresso is made.

## Aluminum, stainless steel or induction?

| material | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Aluminum | light, conducts heat well, classic taste, cheap | not dishwasher safe, does not work on induction |
| Stainless steel | robust, dishwasher-safe, often suitable for induction | heavier, heats up more unevenly |
| Aluminum with induction base | Compromise for induction cookers | more expensive, soil can loosen over time |

There are also metal adapter plates for induction cookers that lie on the cooking zone and give off the heat to the pot. They work, but take longer to heat up.

## What size?

Espresso makers are specified in “cups”, meaning small espresso cups of around 50 milliliters. A three-cup pot only produces around 150 milliliters of coffee. Important: An espresso maker works best when it is fully filled. Some manufacturers have reducing inserts for half quantities. Therefore, buy the size that you actually need most of the time.

## Grinding degree and type of coffee

For the espresso maker, grind a little **coarser than for espresso** and significantly finer than for filter coffee. A good starting point is a grind like fine sand or semolina. Coffee that is ground too finely clogs the sieve, the pressure rises sharply, and the coffee becomes bitter and over-extracted. Coffee that is ground too coarsely runs through too quickly and tastes thin and sour. How to set the grinding level on your grinder can be found in [Setting the grinding level correctly](/ratgeber/mahlgrad-richtig-einstellen/).

Ready-ground “espresso” from the supermarket is often too fine for the espresso maker. Many roasters offer their own grinding levels for the pot, often referred to as “mocha” or “for espresso makers”.

Medium roasts with chocolate, nut and caramel notes, such as those from Brazil or Central America, are well suited. Very dark roasts quickly become bitter and smoky in the pot. Very light roasts can appear sharp and sour. How roasting changes the taste is explained [roasting levels explained](/ratgeber/roestgrade-erklaert/).

## Step by step

1. **Preheat water:** Heat water in the kettle until just before boiling. With preheated water, the pot sits on the stove for a shorter time, the ground coffee is heated for less time and does not taste “cooked”.
2. **Fill the kettle:** Pour the hot water into the lower kettle until just **below the safety valve**. The valve must not be submerged in water.
3. **Fill the funnel:** Fill the ground coffee loosely up to the edge. Tap the side of the funnel lightly to distribute the flour evenly, and smooth out the excess with your finger or the back of a knife. **Do not press.**
4. **Clean Rim:** Wipe coffee crumbs from the rim of the funnel and kettle. Otherwise the jug will not seal properly.
5. **Screw together:** Insert the funnel and screw the top part tightly. Because the kettle is hot, use a kitchen towel for it.
6. **Heating:** Place the pot over medium heat with the lid open. With gas, the flame should not extend beyond the bottom of the pot, otherwise the handle will melt.
7. **Observe:** After a short time, dark, honey-like coffee slowly emerges from the riser. That's the best part.
8. **Remove in time:** As soon as the stream becomes brighter and begins to bubble and spit, remove the pot from the heat. The rest runs on with the residual heat. If you want, cool the soil briefly under cold water or with a wet cloth to stop the extraction.
9. **Stir and serve:** Stir the coffee in the top container once because the first and last drops taste very different, and serve immediately.

## Why you shouldn't press

With the portafilter machine, the ground coffee is pressed firmly with a tamper because the machine works with high, controlled pressure. Things are different with an espresso maker: pressed coffee grounds offer so much resistance to the water that the pressure in the kettle increases unnecessarily. The coffee becomes over-extracted and bitter, and in the worst case scenario the safety valve opens. Loosely filled, smooth ground coffee is the right choice.

## Typical mistakes

| problem | Probable cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| bitter, burnt | Heat too high, on the stove for too long, flour too fine or pressed | medium heat, remove earlier, grind more coarsely, do not press |
| sour, thin | Grinding level too coarse, water started too cold and then ran through too quickly | Grind finer, preheated water over medium heat |
| metallic taste | new pot or cleaned with detergent | Throw away the first fillings and clean only with water |
| Coffee comes out sideways | Gasket old, edge dirty or not screwed on tightly | Clean the edge, check and replace the seal |
| Valve hisses | too much water, ground too finely or pressed down | Water under the valve, coarser grind |
| Hardly any coffee comes | Sieve clogged or jug only half full | Clean the sieve, choose the jug to suit the quantity |

## Cleaning and care

Aluminum jugs **do not belong in the dishwasher**. The aggressive cleaners attack the metal, making it dull and gray and can discolor. Once cool, rinse all parts with warm water, without detergent, and leave them open and unscrewed to dry. Moisture in the closed pot leads to stains and a musty smell.

It's worth taking a closer look once a month:

- **Take out the strainer and seal** and clean under warm water with a soft brush. Coffee oils settle there and become rancid.
- **Check seal:** If it is hard, cracked or deformed, replace it. Replacement seals are available to match the size of the jug. Many users change the seal about once a year.
- **Safety valve:** It should move freely. Gently press the small pin inside to check that it is not stuck.
- **Limescale:** If the water is hard, a layer of limescale forms in the kettle. Then descale with diluted citric acid or vinegar, fill the kettle, let it stand for a while and rinse thoroughly. More about this in [Descaling the coffee machine and kettle](/ratgeber/entkalken-kaffeemaschine-wasserkocher/).

You should “brew” a new pot with coffee two to three times before your first coffee and pour the coffee away. This creates a thin layer that prevents the metallic taste.

## Security

An espresso maker is under pressure. The safety valve prevents it from bursting, but it has to work. Never fill water over the valve, do not press on the flour and never open the jug while it is hot and there is still pressure in the kettle. Do not place them on an empty, hot plate with no water in the kettle. And keep children away while the pot is on the stove, as the handle and metal get very hot.

## Variations from the pot

- **Caffè Latte from the espresso maker:** Coffee from the pot with hot, frothed milk. How to froth milk without a machine can be found in [Milk foam and cappuccino](/ratgeber/milchschaum-und-cappuccino/).
- **Café cubano:** Mix the first drops from the pot vigorously with sugar until a light cream forms, then pour the rest of the coffee over it.
- **Extended:** Infused with hot water, the coffee creates a drink similar to an Americano.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I fill the espresso maker halfway?

Only limited. The amount of ground coffee and water is tailored to the pot. Less coffee in the hopper results in a loose bed through which water flows too quickly. If you often need smaller quantities, use a smaller jug ​​or a suitable reducer.

### Why doesn't my coffee have crema?

Because the pressure in the espresso maker is too low. A little light brown foam is possible, especially with fresh beans, but a dense crema like the portafilter does not result. This is not a fault of the pot.

### How much caffeine does coffee from an espresso maker have?

More per milliliter than filter coffee because it is more concentrated. A small cup is often similar in size to an espresso. Exact values ​​depend on the bean and quantity, more on this in [Caffeine in Coffee and Tea](/ratgeber/koffein-in-kaffee-und-tee/).

### Is aluminum harmful to health?

When coffee is made from a well-maintained aluminum pot, only very small amounts of aluminum are released into the drink. Acidic foods and drinks as well as long standing times increase the transition. Therefore, do not leave the finished coffee in the pot for hours and do not store acidic liquids in it. If you want to be on the safe side, choose a stainless steel jug.
