Turkish coffee is one of the oldest ways to make coffee and also one of the simplest. All you need is a small pot with a long handle, very finely ground coffee, water and, if desired, sugar. The coffee is not filtered, but rather slowly heated with the water until a dense foam forms. It is served in small cups, often with a glass of water and something sweet. Turkish coffee culture is recognized by UNESCO as an intangible cultural heritage. And in Greece, the Balkans, Lebanon and many Arab countries, coffee is brewed in a similar way, with its own names and subtleties.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The coffee must be ground **dust-fine**, finer than for espresso.
> - Per cup about **7 to 8 grams of coffee** to **60 to 70 milliliters of cold water**.
> - Sugar is added **before cooking**, not after. So ask your guests beforehand.
> - Heat slowly over a low heat, **do not let it boil**, as soon as the foam rises, remove from the heat.
> - First distribute the foam among the cups, then pour the coffee. Before drinking, wait briefly until the grounds have settled.

## A look into history

Coffee reached the Ottoman Empire in the 16th century from Yemen via Egypt and the Arabian Peninsula. The first coffee houses opened in Istanbul around the middle of the century, and they quickly became places to meet, exchange news, play board games and listen to music. From there, the method of making coffee in small pots spread to the countries of the Balkans and the Middle East. The preparation is not just a recipe, but part of a guest culture that has grown over centuries.

## The equipment

- **Cezve:** The typical jug is called Cezve in Turkish, Ibrik, Džezva or Briki in other countries. It is wider at the bottom and narrower at the top so that the foam forms well. Traditionally it is made of copper with tin plating on the inside, but there are also models made of stainless steel or brass. Choose the size that matches the number of cups, because a jug that is too large will hardly produce any foam.
- **Grinder:** Turkish coffee requires a grinder that can grind really finely. Cylindrical hand mills made of brass are classic. Many electric grinders cannot achieve this grind level. But there is ready-ground coffee for Turkish mocha.
- **Small cups** of about 60 to 90 milliliters, a teaspoon and a glass of water for serving.

## Coffee and grind

Medium roast Arabica beans are usually used for Turkish coffee. Very dark roasts quickly taste bitter and burnt in this intensive preparation. The decisive factor is the degree of grinding: the powder should feel like flour or powdered sugar. This is the only way the coffee floats in the water, forms the typical foam and later settles as grounds at the bottom of the cup. Coarsely ground coffee floats in the mouth as crumbs and hardly produces any foam. More about setting grinders can be found in [Setting the grinding level correctly] (/ratgeber/mahlgrad-richtig-einstellen/).

## How much sugar?

Turkish has fixed terms for sweetness, and hosts ask about it before cooking:

| Designation | Meaning | sugar per cup |
|---|---|---|
| Sade | without sugar | no sugar |
| Az şekerli | little sweet | about half a teaspoon |
| Orta şekerli | medium sweet | about a teaspoon |
| Shekerli | sweet | about two teaspoons |

The sugar is heated together with coffee and water. It dissolves completely and also changes the texture of the foam. Stirring later would destroy the foam.

## Step by step

1. **Measure the water:** Pour one cup of **cold** water into the cezve per cup. So the quantity is exactly right.
2. **Add coffee and sugar:** Add a heaping teaspoon of coffee per cup, about 7 to 8 grams, and the desired sugar.
3. **Stir once:** Stir gently until the coffee and sugar combine with the water. Then don't stir anymore.
4. **Heat slowly:** Place the cezve on the lowest flame or low setting. The slower, the finer and denser the foam becomes. This can take three to five minutes.
5. **Observe the foam:** A dark ring initially forms at the edge, then a light, fine-pored foam layer rises.
6. **Remove in time:** Shortly before the coffee boils and the foam starts to rise over the edge, remove the cezve from the heat. The coffee must not boil until bubbling, otherwise the foam will collapse and the taste will be bitter.
7. **Spread the foam:** First, spoon some foam into each cup.
8. **Optional: Heat a second time:** Some people put the cezve on the flame again briefly until the foam rises again. This is a question of the family and the region.
9. **Pour:** Slowly pour the coffee around the edge of the cup so that the foam stays on top.
10. **Wait:** Let the coffee sit for a minute so the grounds settle to the bottom. Then you drink in small sips until you feel the sentence.

## On hot sand

A particularly nice variant is the coffee from the sand, in Turkish **kum kahvesi**. A bowl of fine sand is placed on a heat source. The cezve is pressed into the hot sand and buried deeper or pulled out depending on the desired heat. Because the sand releases heat evenly from all sides, the foam rises slowly and finely. At home this is hardly necessary; a very small flame or low heat setting serves the same purpose. Many cafés are happy to demonstrate the method, and it makes it clear what is important: gentle, even heat.

## Caring for the Cezve

Copper pots are usually coated with tin on the inside so that the coffee does not come into direct contact with the copper. This tin plating wears off over time. If the reddish copper shows through in larger areas, have the cezve re-tinned or replace it. Never heat it empty, because tin melts at just over 230 degrees. After use, rinse with warm water and a soft brush and dry well. Abrasives and the dishwasher damage the coating. Copper doesn't work on an induction stove; There you need a cezve with a magnetic base or an adapter plate.

## It is served with water and sweets

Turkish coffee goes with a glass of water. Many people drink it beforehand to cleanse the palate so that the coffee can be fully appreciated. This is often served with a piece of Lokum, i.e. Turkish honey, a praline or some pastry. The cup is not completely empty because the thick residue remains at the bottom. In some families, the cup is then turned over to playfully read “the future” from the sentence. This is a social custom, nothing more.

## Variants from other countries

| Country or region | name | Special feature |
|---|---|---|
| Greece | Ellinikos cafes | Same preparation in Briki, often with lots of foam |
| Bosnia | Bosanska kahva | Water is first boiled, then the coffee is stirred in and heated briefly; sugar is served separately |
| Lebanon, Syria, Jordan | Ahweh, Qahwa | often with ground cardamom |
| Arabian Peninsula | Qahwa | usually very light roasted, with cardamom and sometimes saffron, filtered and drunk from small, handleless cups |
| Ethiopia | Buna | own ceremony with roasting in front of the guests, cooked in the Jebena |

**With cardamom:** Add a pinch of freshly ground cardamom or a lightly crushed cardamom pod to the cezve. Cardamom has a fresh, spicy, almost lemony scent and goes very well with dark coffee.

## Typical errors and their solutions

| problem | Probable cause | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| no or little foam | Coffee too coarse, cezve too big, heated too quickly | Grind until fine as dust, suitable jug, small flame |
| Coffee boils over | not picked up on time | Never let Cezve out of your sight |
| bitter, burnt | cooked instead of just heated, very dark roast | Remove before boiling, medium roast |
| thin, watery | too little coffee | one heaped teaspoon per cup |
| sentence in the mouth | drinking too early or exercising too much | Wait a minute, drink slowly, not to the bottom |

## Is Turkish coffee stronger?

Turkish coffee tastes very intense because the coffee is not filtered and fine particles and oils remain in the cup. But the cups are small. The caffeine content of a cup is similar to that of an espresso, depending on the quantity and bean. More about this in [Caffeine in Coffee and Tea](/ratgeber/koffein-in-kaffee-und-tee/). The [French Press](/ratgeber/french-press-anleitung/) also shows how unfiltered coffee differs from filtered coffee.

## Unfiltered coffee and cholesterol

Coffee contains natural oils with the substances cafestol and kahweol. If consumed regularly and in large quantities, they can increase cholesterol levels. Paper filters largely retain these substances; with unfiltered coffee such as Turkish mocha, French press or boiled coffee, they get into the cup. For healthy people, an occasional cup is not a problem. If you have high cholesterol levels and drink a lot of coffee, you can consult a doctor to see whether filtered coffee is a better choice.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I make Turkish coffee without cezve?

In a very small pot if necessary. However, the foam then becomes flatter because the surface does not narrow as well. If you often drink Turkish coffee, you should buy a suitable pot.

### Can I drink Turkish coffee with milk?

Not traditionally. The coffee is drunk straight, sweetened or unsweetened. If you like it milder, you can reduce the amount of coffee slightly.

### How do I store ground Turkish coffee?

Finely ground coffee loses its aroma very quickly. Put it in an airtight, light-protected container and buy small quantities, see [Store coffee and tea properly](/ratgeber/kaffee-und-tee-lagern/).

## Conclusion

Turkish mocha requires little equipment but attention: fine powder, cold water, sugar from the start and a small flame until the foam rises. If you serve it with a glass of water and something sweet and allow the sentence to rest for a minute, you are serving a cup full of history and hospitality.
