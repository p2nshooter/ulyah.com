The filter coffee machine is still the most common way of making coffee in German households and offices. It is comfortable, makes many cups at once and requires little attention. Their reputation is still rather mediocre: the coffee often tastes thin, bitter or stale. A decent machine can make really good coffee if a few things are right: enough coffee, the right grind, fresh beans, a clean device and not keeping it warm for hours. This guide shows you how to get significantly more out of your existing machine.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Take about **60 grams of coffee per liter of water**, so around 6 grams per 100 milliliters. Many people dose significantly too little.
> - Medium grind, similar to coarse sand. A little coarser for large quantities, a little finer for small ones.
> - Fold the filter paper, insert it well and, if necessary, rinse it with hot water beforehand.
> - Do not leave the coffee on the warming plate for longer than about 20 to 30 minutes; it is better to pour it into a thermos flask.
> - Clean and descale the machine regularly. Old coffee oils and lime spoil the taste.

## How a filter coffee machine works

Cold water flows from the tank into a heating pipe under the warming plate. There it is heated, begins to boil and rises through a tube. From there it drips through a brewing head onto the ground coffee in the filter. The finished coffee runs into the pot. The brewing temperature of simple devices often fluctuates, and the water is not always evenly distributed over the flour. Better machines consistently reach around 92 to 96 degrees, distribute the water over a wide brew head, and brew the entire batch in four to six minutes. Some devices carry test seals from specialist organizations for filter coffee. But a lot can also be improved with a simple machine.

## The right amount of coffee

The most common mistake is not enough coffee. The spoon information on many packages is generous, and a “measuring spoon” is rarely accurate. A kitchen scale is the easiest tool for better filter coffee.

| Amount of water | Coffee at 55 g/l (milder) | Coffee at 60 g/l (standard) | Coffee at 65 g/l (stronger) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 500ml | 28g | 30g | 33g |
| 750ml | 41g | 45g | 49g |
| 1,000ml | 55g | 60g | 65g |
| 1,250ml | 69g | 75g | 81g |

Note: The water markings on the tank often count in small “cups” of around 120 to 125 milliliters. Measure how much water your machine actually holds for “8 cups” and then weigh the coffee accordingly.

If the right amount of coffee is too strong for you, it is better to dilute it with hot water afterwards rather than using less coffee. Less coffee with the same amount of water often leads to over-extraction: the water leaches out the few particles too much, and the coffee becomes thin and bitter at the same time.

## Grind and beans

A **medium grind**, roughly like coarse sand, is suitable for the filter machine. Ready-ground filter coffee from the supermarket is in this range. If you have your own mill, you can adjust it better:

- **Coffee runs through very quickly, tastes thin and sour:** Grind finer.
- **Coffee runs slowly, the filter overflows or the coffee tastes bitter and dry:** grind more coarsely.
- **Large quantities** require a slightly coarser grind because the coffee bed is higher and the water takes longer.

Freshly ground coffee made from whole beans tastes much better than pre-ground coffee. Why this is so can be found in [roasting date and freshness](/ratgeber/roestdatum-und-frische/). Medium, balanced roasts are well suited for the filter machine. Very dark espresso roasts quickly become bitter and smoky in the filter.

## The filter

Most machines use 1x4 or 1x2 conical filter bags. So that they sit well:

1. **Fold seams:** Fold the bottom seam and side seam in opposite directions. This means that the bag fits smoothly against the filter holder and does not tear.
2. **Pre-rinse:** If desired, pour some hot water through the empty bag to remove paper taste and discard. This is less important with high-quality filter bags, but it doesn't do any harm.
3. **Pour in flour and shake until smooth:** A flat surface ensures that the water flows evenly.

Unbleached, brown filters can taste a little more like paper than bleached, white ones. Permanent filters made of metal or plastic allow more oils and fine particles to pass through. The coffee becomes fuller but also cloudier, similar to the [French Press](/ratgeber/french-press-anleitung/).

## water

Coffee consists of around 98 percent water. Calcareous water makes the coffee dull and causes calcification in the machine. If the water is very hard, a water filter can help. Always use fresh, cold water and do not leave water in the tank for days. [Water hardness for coffee and tea](/ratgeber/wasserhaerte-fuer-kaffee-und-tee/) explains how water hardness changes the taste.

## Warming plate or thermos flask?

The warming plate keeps the coffee hot, but also continues to cook it slowly. After about half an hour it tastes noticeably more bitter and “cooked”. After an hour it is no longer edible for many people.

Better:

- Transfer the coffee into a **preheated thermos flask** immediately after brewing. It stays there for a few hours without boiling again.
- Buy machines with a built-in thermos flask if you often cook larger quantities.
- Only cook as much as you will drink in the next half hour.

## A better process in six steps

1. Pour in fresh, cold water and measure the amount.
2. Freshly grind coffee and weigh it, about 60 grams per liter.
3. Fold the filter bag, insert it and, if necessary, pre-rinse it.
4. Pour in ground coffee and level it by shaking gently.
5. Start machine. For devices with a drip stop, do not remove the jug in between, otherwise the filter will overflow.
6. Stir after going through, because the coffee that goes through first is stronger than the last, and then pour into a thermos or drink immediately.

## Brew small quantities

Many machines are built for a full can. If you only brew two cups, the coffee bed is flat, the water runs through quickly, and the heating system often doesn't reach full temperature before the tank is empty. The result tastes thin. Some devices have a **small quantity or aroma switch** that releases the water more slowly and at intervals. Without this function, it helps to grind the coffee a little finer for small quantities and not to choose the dosage too narrowly. If you only drink one or two cups regularly, you are often better off with a hand filter.

## Timer and preparation in the evening

A machine with a timer that has coffee ready in the morning is convenient. The catch: The ground coffee then lies open in the filter all night and loses its aroma. If you use the timer, add the flour just before you go to bed and cover the filter holder, or use a machine with a built-in grinder that only grinds when you start. The water can be left in the tank overnight, but should be filled with fresh water every day.

## How you can recognize a good machine

When you need a new machine, it's worth taking a look at the technology instead of just the design and number of cups. A good filter machine will quickly reach a brewing temperature of around 92 to 96 degrees and brew a full pot in four to six minutes. A wide brewing head with multiple outlets distributes the water evenly over the ground coffee. A thermos flask instead of a warming plate, a drip stop that closes reliably, and parts that are easy to clean make everyday life more pleasant. Test seals from specialist organizations for filter coffee confirm that a device maintains the temperature and brewing time.

## Cleaning and descaling

Coffee contains oils that build up in the filter holder, pot and brewing head and become rancid over time. Rinse the jug and filter holder daily with hot water and thoroughly with dishwashing liquid once a week. Remove coffee grounds immediately after brewing to prevent them from getting moldy.

Limescale settles in the heating pipe. The machine becomes slower, louder, the water no longer gets hot enough, and the coffee tastes flat. How often you should descale depends on the hardness of the water. With hard water it makes sense about every four to six weeks, with soft water less often. Instructions can be found in [Descaling the coffee machine and kettle](/ratgeber/entkalken-kaffeemaschine-wasserkocher/).

## Typical errors and their solutions

| problem | Probable cause | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| thin and watery | too little coffee, ground too coarsely | Weigh out more coffee and grind it finer |
| bitter and thin at the same time | too little coffee for the amount of water | Increase dosage, do not reduce it |
| bitter and stale | on the warming plate for too long | pour into a thermos flask |
| flat, papery | old coffee, paper taste | fresh beans, pre-rinse filter |
| Filter overflows | too finely ground, too much coffee, drip stop active | Grind more coarsely, do not remove the jug |
| Machine loud and slow | Limescale in the heating system | decalcify |

## Frequently asked questions

### Does filter coffee from the machine taste worse than from the hand filter?

Not necessarily. With the [hand filter](/ratgeber/handfilter-kaffee-anleitung/) you control the water temperature, pouring speed and distribution yourself and can brew particularly clear, aromatic cups. However, a good machine with the right dosage and fresh coffee comes close to this result and is much more convenient for larger quantities.

### Can I reheat coffee from the machine?

It is possible, but the taste suffers significantly. A thermos flask is better, or you can cook smaller quantities.

### How often should I clean the machine?

Rinse daily, clean thoroughly weekly and, depending on water hardness, descale every few weeks. Cleanliness makes a bigger difference than many people think.

## Conclusion

The filter coffee machine is better than its reputation. Weigh out enough coffee, grind it medium and fresh, insert the filter cleanly, pour the finished coffee into a thermos flask and keep the device clean: these steps turn the everyday device into a reliable source of really good coffee.
