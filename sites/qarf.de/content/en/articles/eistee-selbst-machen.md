Store-bought iced tea is often one thing above all: sweet. Many ready-made drinks contain a lot of sugar and little tea. Homemade iced tea, on the other hand, tastes like what it is: like fresh black, green or white tea, like fruits and herbs, as sweet or unsweetened as you like it. It's easy and cheap to prepare, and you can get very different results with two methods. A tea that has been brewed cold overnight becomes soft, sweet and hardly bitter. A hot brewed and quickly cooled tea remains strong and aromatic. This guide shows both ways, the right types of tea and some tried-and-tested combinations.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - **Pour cold:** about **8 to 10 grams of tea per liter** of cold water, let it steep in the refrigerator for **6 to 12 hours**. Result: soft, sweet, hardly bitter.
> - **Brew hot and quench:** Pour twice as much tea as usual with half the amount of water, then pour directly over **ice**. Result: strong and fresh.
> - Always brew herbal and fruit tea **hot** and then cool it down, not just make it cold.
> - Store finished iced tea in the refrigerator and drink within **one to two days**.
> - Sweeten with syrup instead of sugar, which dissolves poorly in cold tea.

## Method 1: pour cold

With cold infusion, the tea leaves steep in cold water for many hours. At low temperatures, the tart tannins and bitter substances dissolve only slowly, while the sweet and aromatic substances dissolve well. The tea becomes mild, round, often surprisingly sweet, and it hardly becomes cloudy. You may know the principle from coffee, see [Make your own cold brew](/ratgeber/cold-brew-anleitung/).

1. Place **8 to 10 grams** of loose tea in a clean glass carafe.
2. Fill with **one liter of cold water**.
3. Cover the carafe and place it in the **fridge**.
4. Depending on the type, let the tea steep for **6 to 12 hours**. Green and white tea need more like 6 to 8 hours, black tea and oolong 8 to 12.
5. **Strain** through a fine sieve and pour into a clean bottle.

## Method 2: brew hot and cool on ice

This method is faster and produces a stronger tea with more structure.

1. Brew the tea with **double the amount** of leaves, but only **half the amount** of water, at the usual temperature and steeping time. For one liter of iced tea, around 10 to 12 grams of tea with 500 milliliters of water.
2. Fill a carafe with about **500 grams of ice cubes**.
3. Pour the hot tea through a sieve **directly onto the ice**. It cools immediately and is diluted to the correct strength.
4. Stir and serve immediately or refrigerate.

Rapid cooling keeps the flavors fresh and reduces the cloudiness that often occurs with slowly cooling black tea. This **tea cloudiness**, also called “cream down”, is harmless. It occurs when tannins and caffeine combine during cooling and occurs primarily with strong teas and hard water.

## Quantities for glass, carafe and jug

So that you don't have to do the math every time, here are the two methods for usual amounts of loose tea. The values ​​are starting points that you adjust to taste.

| Finished quantity | Pour cold | Brew hot and over ice |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 liters | 4 to 5 g tea, 500 ml cold water | 5 to 6 g tea, 250 ml hot water, 250 g ice |
| 1 liter | 8 to 10 g of tea, 1 liter of cold water | 10 to 12 g tea, 500 ml hot water, 500 g ice |
| 2 liters | 16 to 20 g of tea, 2 liters of cold water | 20 to 24 g of tea, 1 liter of hot water, 1 kg of ice |

When brewing hot, the temperature of the respective variety applies: here, too, green tea remains well below the boiling point, otherwise it will be cold on ice, but no less bitter. The appropriate values ​​are in [Pour tea: brewing times and temperatures](/ratgeber/tee-ziehzeiten-und-temperaturen/).

## Japanese variants: Mizudashi and Koridashi

In Japan, cold-infused green tea is known as Mizudashi, meaning “extracted with water”. There are slim bottles with a sieve in the neck for this: put tea in, fill up with water and put it in the fridge. The principle corresponds to the cold infusion above and goes particularly well with Sencha.

Koridashi, the infusion with ice, is even slower. Place about five grams of high-quality green tea, for example Gyokuro or a good Sencha, in a small pot with a sieve, cover the leaves with ice cubes and let the ice slowly melt. Depending on the quantity and room temperature, this takes one to several hours. The cold melt water gently pulls the leaves out. Only a small amount comes out, but a strikingly sweet, dense tea with lots of umami that you enjoy in small sips rather than from a large glass.

## Which teas are suitable?

| Tea | Method | Tastes like iced tea |
|---|---|---|
| Ceylon, Assam, Darjeeling | both | classic, strong or fresh, good with lemon and peach |
| Sencha and Chinese green tea | cold | grassy, sweet, umami, little tartness |
| Gyokuro | cold | very sweet and full-bodied |
| White tea, Bai Mu Dan | cold | tender, honey, melon |
| Light oolong | cold | floral, creamy |
| Roasted Oolong, Hojicha | both | nutty, caramelly |
| Rooibos, honeybush | Brew hot, then cool | sweet, vanilla, without caffeine |
| Peppermint, hibiscus, fruits | Brew hot, then cool | refreshing, fruity |

## Herbal and fruit tea: first hot, then cold

Dried herbs and fruits can carry germs that can only be safely killed at high temperatures. The following applies to cold herbal or fruit tea: pour over **sparkling boiling water**, let it steep for at least five to ten minutes and only then cool down. [Herbal tea and fruit tea](/ratgeber/kraeutertee-und-fruechtetee/) explains why this is important. Some manufacturers offer mixtures that are expressly intended for cold infusion and treated accordingly. Then pay attention to the instructions on the packaging.

## Sweeten with syrup

Sugar dissolves poorly in cold tea and sinks to the bottom. A simple **sugar syrup** is better: Boil equal amounts of sugar and water until the sugar has dissolved, allow to cool and store in the refrigerator. For additional flavor, add lemon peel, ginger slices, mint or a vanilla bean. Honey can also be dissolved in a little warm water and then added. Start with a little sweetness and taste. Well-made iced tea often requires less than you think, especially when brewed cold.

## Recipe ideas

**Peach iced tea:** Mix one liter of cold-infused Ceylon tea with a ripe, sliced peach and a little syrup and let it steep for a few hours.

**Lemon-mint green tea:** Pour cold Sencha, serve with lemon slices and a few fresh mint leaves.

**Hibiscus ginger:** Pour hibiscus flowers and fresh ginger slices with boiling water, let steep for ten minutes, sweeten with syrup, cool and serve with orange slices.

**Earl Gray with Orange:** Brew Earl Gray hot and pour over ice, serve with orange slices and a sprig of rosemary.

**Hojicha milk:** Pour cold hojicha, mix in a ratio of one to one with cold milk or oat drink.

**Arnold Palmer:** Half iced tea, half lemonade, a classic from the USA.

## Combine lemon, milk and fruit correctly

Lemon visibly brightens black tea because the acid changes the color of the tea. This isn't a mistake, it's chemistry. Only put lemon slices in the carafe or directly into the glass shortly before serving, as the white inner skin of the peel becomes bitter when steeped for a long time. Milk and lemon, on the other hand, do not mix: the acid causes the milk to curdle. Strong Assam, roasted tea such as Hojicha or Rooibos, all without citrus notes, are suitable for iced tea with milk.

Soft fruits such as peaches, berries or mango release their aroma within a few hours. Afterwards they become soft and unsightly and the tea should be drunk soon. Solid ingredients such as ginger slices, citrus peel or a sprig of rosemary hold their shape longer, but release more spiciness or bitterness over time. Take them out once you like the taste.

## If the iced tea doesn't work

| problem | Probable cause | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| tart and bitter despite ice | too hot or steeped for too long when brewing hot | Adhere to the temperature and brewing time of the variety or pour cold |
| flat, little aroma | old tea, too little tea or very hard water | Take fresh leaf tea, increase the amount, use softer water |
| tastes like refrigerator | Carafe stored open | Always cover or refrigerate in a sealed bottle |

## Hygiene and durability

Iced tea is a good breeding ground for germs, especially if it contains fruit, herbs or sugar. Pay attention to these points:

- **Clean vessels:** Rinse carafes and bottles with hot water beforehand.
- **Store in a cool place:** Always store iced tea in the refrigerator, do not leave it out in the sun for hours.
- **Consume quickly:** Best within one to two days. With fruits and herbs, rather on the same or next day.
- **Sensory test:** If the tea smells sour, fermented or unusual, if it becomes slimy or if it forms streaks, pour it away.

## Iced tea for guests and summer parties

The cold infusion is particularly worthwhile for a garden party or a family celebration because you prepare it the evening before and don't have to do anything else. Set up several carafes with different teas, such as a black one with lemon, a green one with mint and a caffeine-free rooibos or hibiscus for children and anyone who doesn't want caffeine in the evening. Add the syrup separately so everyone can sweeten it themselves. Only add fruits and herbs to the carafe shortly before serving so that they look fresh and the tea doesn't taste like them too early. Label the carafes so guests know what caffeine contains. Place iced tea outdoors in the shade or in a cooler, and refill from the refrigerator more often rather than leaving large quantities in the heat for hours.

## The right water

Because iced tea consists almost entirely of water, you can taste every difference. Very hard water dulls the tea and promotes cloudiness. Filtered water or a still mineral water with few minerals produces a clearer, fresher iced tea. More about this in [Water hardness for coffee and tea](/ratgeber/wasserhaerte-fuer-kaffee-und-tee/).

## Tea ice cubes

Watery iced tea is created when ice cubes melt in the glass. Freeze some of the tea in ice cube trays. These tea ice cubes cool without watering down. To make pretty glasses, you can freeze mint leaves, lemon peel or small berries.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much caffeine does iced tea have?

Homemade iced tea made from real tea contains caffeine, depending on the amount and brewing time, about as much as hot tea. Cold brewed tea may contain slightly less. Caffeine-free alternatives include rooibos, herbs and fruits. More in [Caffeine in Coffee and Tea](/ratgeber/koffein-in-kaffee-und-tee/).

### Can I use tea bags for iced tea?

Yes. A tea bag usually contains around 1.5 to 2.5 grams of tea. For one liter of cold-infused iced tea, use four to six bags.

### Why is my iced tea getting cloudy?

When strong teas are slowly cooled, tannins and caffeine combine. Cooling down quickly on ice, cold infusion or softer water largely prevents this.
