Coffee is a fresh product, even if it looks like it will last for months in the package. After roasting, a clock starts ticking: the beans release gases, volatile flavors evaporate, and oxygen ages the oils. A coffee that smells of chocolate, cherry and caramel two weeks after roasting tastes flat, woody and like cardboard after three months. At the same time, freshly roasted coffee is not ideal either. This guide explains what is on the package, when coffee tastes best and how to keep your beans fresh for as long as possible.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Pay attention to the **roasting date**, not just the best-before date. Many specialty roasters print it on every package.
> - Freshly roasted coffee needs a few days of rest: around 3 to 7 days for filter, 7 to 14 days for espresso.
> - Coffee tastes best in the first four to six weeks after roasting.
> - Ground coffee loses its aroma in minutes to hours. If possible, grind just before brewing.
> - Buy small quantities to use in two to three weeks, or freeze portions in an airtight container.

## Roasting date or best before date?

Coffee from the supermarket usually only has a **best before date**, often twelve to twenty-four months after roasting. It guarantees that the coffee is harmless to health until then and retains its basic properties. It says little about the taste peak. A coffee that has a “shelf life” of eight months can already be four months old.

Many specialty roasters therefore indicate the **roasting date**. This shows you directly how fresh the beans are. If only the best-before date is stated, you can roughly calculate back with many manufacturers, but this is not reliable.

## Degassing: why completely fresh coffee is not ideal

When roasting, a lot of carbon dioxide is produced in the bean. It comes out slowly in the first few days after roasting. If you brew freshly roasted coffee, it bubbles heavily in the filter, the gas displaces the water, and the coffee extracts unevenly. It often tastes unpleasant, spicy or green. With espresso, too much gas leads to a thick but quickly disintegrating crema and an unbalanced taste.

The **flavor valve** on many coffee packs is a one-way valve: it lets carbon dioxide out but doesn't let oxygen in. Without a valve the bags would inflate. The valve does not protect the coffee from aging, but it slows down oxidation as long as the package is sealed.

## The ideal time window

| Preparation | Resting time after roasting | Best taste up to about |
|---|---|---|
| Hand filter, filter machine | 3 to 7 days | 4 to 6 weeks after roasting |
| French press, cold brew | 3 to 7 days | 6 weeks |
| Espresso | 7 to 14 days | 6 to 8 weeks |
| Espresso maker | 5 to 10 days | 6 weeks |

The values are guidelines. Light roasts often take a little longer to open, dark roasts age faster because the cell structure is more porous and there is more oil on the surface. After this window, the coffee will not go bad, but it will gradually lose its aroma and sweetness.

## Why ground coffee loses so quickly

When grinding, the surface area of the coffee multiplies. Volatile aromatic substances escape immediately and oxygen reaches all particles. After just a few minutes, freshly ground coffee smells less intense. After a few days in an opened package, ground coffee tastes noticeably flatter than freshly ground coffee from the same beans.

Having your own grinder is therefore the most effective investment in better coffee. [Buy a coffee grinder](/ratgeber/kaffeemuehle-kegel-oder-scheibenmahlwerk/) and [Hand grinder or electric grinder](/ratgeber/handmuehle-oder-elektrische-muehle/) explain which grinder suits you. If you buy ground coffee, get small packets and use them quickly.

## This is how you recognize old coffee

- **Smell:** Fresh coffee smells strong when you open the pack. Old coffee smells faintly, like cardboard, wood or slightly rancid.
- **When brewing:** Fresh coffee swells significantly in the filter when pre-brewing and forms bubbles. Old coffee stays flat.
- **Espresso:** A thin, light, quickly disappearing crema can indicate old coffee if the grind and machine are correct.
- **Taste:** flat, papery, bitter without sweetness, little fruit.
- **Appearance:** Very dark beans with an oily surface go rancid more quickly. With light beans, an oily shine after some time is a sign of aging.

## Shop wisely

1. **Whole beans** instead of ground coffee.
2. Check **roasting date** and buy freshly roasted goods.
3. **Small quantities:** One pack that you finish in two to three weeks. For a household with one to two cups a day, that's 250 to 500 grams.
4. **Local roasters** often roast weekly and sell directly.
5. **Do not stock up** buy several kilos unless you are freezing them.

## Freezing: yes, but right

Coffee can be frozen easily if you follow a few rules:

- Only freeze whole beans, as soon as possible after the resting period.
- Divide them into portions for about a week and pack them airtight, preferably vacuum sealed or in small, well-sealed containers.
- Take out only the portion you need and let it come to room temperature before opening. Otherwise moisture will condense on the cold beans.
- Do not refreeze thawed coffee.

Coffee that is constantly opened and resealed in the refrigerator, on the other hand, absorbs moisture and odors. How to store coffee in everyday life is described in detail in [Store coffee and tea correctly](/ratgeber/kaffee-und-tee-lagern/).

## A simple freshness test at home

If you are unsure how fresh your beans are, a little test can help. Place a handful of freshly ground coffee in a small freezer bag, squeeze out the air, seal it and leave it overnight. If it swells significantly, the beans are still releasing carbon dioxide and are fresh. If the bag stays flat, the coffee is older. A second clue is pre-brewing in a hand filter: fresh coffee arches into a dome that throws bubbles. Old coffee collapses immediately.

## Freshness in the café and at the roaster

Good cafés and roasters pay close attention to the freshness of their beans. You can simply ask when the coffee was roasted. Many roasters roast in small batches weekly and are happy to provide information about which coffee is currently in its prime. A coffee that needs to rest for a few days for espresso can taste wonderful in the hand filter. Also ask about the recommended preparation, as many roasting companies roast specifically for filters or espresso.

## What happens when we age

Roasted coffee contains hundreds of volatile aromatics, and it is the subtlest ones, such as fruity and floral notes, that disappear first. At the same time, the oils in the bean react with oxygen: they oxidize and taste rancid over time. Added to this is moisture from the air, which accelerates aging. Light and heat intensify all of these processes. That's why old coffee not only appears weaker, but also different: the sweetness disappears, woody and papery tones come to the fore.

## Freshness in tea

Tea also has a time window, but a longer one than coffee. Green tea tastes freshest in the first few months after harvest. Japanese spring teas like Shincha are specifically drunk young. Black tea is more stable and often retains its aroma for one to two years if stored well. An exception is Pu-Erh, which can be deliberately aged for years, as [Pu-Erh Tea](/ratgeber/pu-erh-tee/) explains. The following applies to all teas: store them airtight, dark, cool and away from strong-smelling foods, as tea leaves easily absorb odors.

## Freshly roasted by subscription

Many roasters offer subscriptions where the coffee is only roasted after the order is placed and then shipped every one to four weeks. This means you always have beans in the best time window without having to stock up. Just pay attention to the resting time: the beans may still be too fresh for espresso for a few days when they arrive.

## Use older beans wisely

Coffee that has passed its peak does not have to go in the trash. It is suitable for **cold brew** because the long, cold extraction is less sensitive to a lack of freshness. The age is hardly noticeable in **desserts** such as tiramisu or coffee cream, in pastries or as a seasoning for stews. And the coffee grounds can then be put in the compost.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is old coffee harmful?

No, as long as it has been stored in a dry place and does not get moldy. It just tastes less good. You can use older beans for cold brew or coffee in desserts.

### Why does coffee from vacation taste different at home?

Often it's the water, the freshness and the preparation. [Water hardness for coffee and tea](/ratgeber/wasserhaerte-fuer-kaffee-und-tee/) explains how much the water influences the taste.

### Do capsules stay fresh longer?

Coffee capsules and pads are usually packaged under protective gas and therefore remain stable for a long time. However, they can rarely compete with freshly roasted, freshly ground beans in terms of taste.

### How long does an opened pack last?

Whole beans in a well-sealed package with a flavor valve will stay in good shape for about two to four weeks after opening. After each removal, squeeze out the air, roll up the bag tightly, or place the beans in a dark, airtight container that is not much larger than the amount inside. Ground coffee, on the other hand, should be consumed within a few days.

### Does coffee belong in the fridge?

Rather not. It's humid in the fridge and every time you open it, condensation forms on the cold beans. Beans also absorb smells from cheese, sausage or onions. A cool, dark closet is a better choice for everyday life; For a longer period of time, only freezing in portions helps.

## Conclusion

Pay attention to the roasting date, buy whole beans in small quantities, grind them fresh and store them correctly: With these habits you will drink your coffee in its best time window. This includes a few days of rest after roasting, but not several months on the open shelf.
