In India, chai is simply the word for tea, and tea there almost always means: strong black tea, cooked in a pot with milk, sugar and often spices. It is prepared at train stations, in markets and in every kitchen; every family has its own recipe. What is sold in this country as a “Chai Latte” made from powder or syrup is usually a very sweet approximation. Homemade Masala Chai tastes spicier, fresher and can be tailored exactly to your taste. It takes about ten minutes to prepare and only requires a small pot. This guide shows you a basic recipe and how you can vary it.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Masala Chai consists of **strong black tea**, **milk**, **spices** and **sugar**, cooked in a pot.
> - Classic spices are **cardamom**, **ginger**, **cinnamon**, **cloves** and **black pepper**, sometimes star anise and fennel.
> - Basic recipe for two cups: 250 ml water, 250 ml milk, 2 tsp Assam or CTC tea, spices, sugar to taste.
> - First boil the spices in the water, then add tea, then milk and bring to the boil.
> - Fresh whole spices taste more intense than finished powder.

## The right tea

Chai needs a tea that stands out against milk and spices. **Assam** or a **CTC tea**, i.e. black tea that is machine-processed into small balls, is ideal. It quickly releases color and strength and becomes malty and round when cooked. Fine teas like Darjeeling go down in chai. More about the varieties can be found in [Black tea varieties](/ratgeber/schwarztee-sorten/). In a pinch, strong breakfast blends made from tea bags also work.

## The spices

| spice | Quantity for 2 cups | Effect in chai |
|---|---|---|
| Green cardamom | 3 to 4 capsules, pressed | fragrant, fresh, the most important chai spice |
| Fresh ginger | 2 to 3 thin slices or 1 tsp grated | sharp, warming |
| Cinnamon stick | 1 small piece | sweet, warm |
| cloves | 2 pieces | intensive, spicy, use sparingly |
| Black pepper | 3 to 4 grains, pressed | slight sharpness |
| Star anise | half a star, optional | licorice-like |
| Fennel seeds | a pinch, optional | sweet, fresh |

Lightly press the cardamom pods and peppercorns with the back of a knife or in a mortar to release the aromas. Whole spices produce a clear, aromatic chai. Ready-made chai spice powders are quicker, but can taste slightly dusty or bitter and make the tea cloudy.

## Basic recipe for two cups

1. **Boil the spices:** Put 250 milliliters of water with the pressed spices and ginger in a small pot. Simmer gently for about **3 to 5 minutes** until the water is fragrant and slightly colored.
2. **Add tea:** Add **two heaped teaspoons** of black tea and let it simmer for **1 to 2 minutes**.
3. **Add milk:** Pour in **250 milliliters of milk** and add sugar to taste, often one to two teaspoons per cup for an Indian chai.
4. **Boil:** Heat the chai until it foams and rises. Take the pot off the heat briefly so that it doesn't boil over and put it back on the stove. Repeat this two to three times. The chai becomes creamier and stronger.
5. **Strain:** Pour the chai through a fine sieve into cups or glasses.

In India, chai is often poured from one vessel to another from a great height. This cools it down, mixes everything thoroughly and creates some foam.

## Milk and plant drinks

Whole milk makes the chai round and creamy. It becomes lighter with low-fat milk. Oat and soy drinks work well, especially barista varieties that don't flocculate when cooked. Almond and rice drinks are thinner and slightly sweeter. With plant-based drinks, it is worth adding them at the end and only heating them briefly.

## Adjust spices according to the season

In many Indian families, chai changes with the weather. On cold days, add more ginger, cinnamon, black pepper and cloves to the pot because they are considered warming and hold their own against milk and sugar. In summer the chai is often lighter: more cardamom and fennel, less pepper and clove, sometimes a few fresh mint leaves. In the rainy season, lemongrass and tulsi, the Indian basil, are popular additions. When experimenting, change one spice at a time and note the amount you like. This will create your own house blend over time.

## Chai spice mix in stock

If you drink chai often, it's worth making your own spice mix. To do this, briefly roast cardamom, cinnamon, cloves, pepper and fennel in a dry pan until they smell fragrant, let them cool and grind them in a mortar or spice mill. You can add ground dried ginger. Pour the mixture into a dark, airtight jar. About half a teaspoon is enough for every two cups. Ground spices lose their flavor more quickly than whole spices, so only make small quantities. More about storing in [Store coffee and tea properly](/ratgeber/kaffee-und-tee-lagern/).

## Chai syrup for chai latte

For a quick café-style chai latte, you can make a syrup:

1. Simmer 300 milliliters of water with the spices from the basic recipe in double quantities for 10 minutes.
2. Add 3 teaspoons of black tea, let steep for 3 minutes and strain.
3. Stir in 200 grams of sugar or brown sugar and bring to the boil briefly until it has dissolved.
4. When cool, pour into a clean bottle and store in the refrigerator. Use within about two weeks.

To make a chai latte, put two to three tablespoons of syrup in a cup and top with hot, frothed milk. How to froth milk is described in [Milk foam and cappuccino](/ratgeber/milchschaum-und-cappuccino/). A shot of espresso turns it into a “dirty chai”.

## Chai without a stove: in the office or on the go

Without a pot, the chai will be a little less creamy, but still aromatic. For a large cup, put the pressed spices and two slices of ginger in a teapot or mug, pour about 150 milliliters of boiling water from the kettle over it and let everything steep, covered, for around five minutes. Then add the tea, a strong tea bag or a heaped teaspoon of loose tea in a sieve, for three to four minutes. In the meantime, warm 100 to 150 milliliters of milk in the microwave or froth it, pour it into the strained tea and sweeten to taste. If you do this often, fill the spice mixture from the stock in portions into paper tea filters, then the straining is done.

## Variants

- **Ginger Chai, Adrak Chai:** only with plenty of fresh ginger, very popular in northern India on cold days.
- **Cardamom Chai, Elaichi Chai:** only with cardamom, fragrant and elegant.
- **Without caffeine:** with **rooibos** instead of black tea. It tolerates cooking and tastes sweet, see [Rooibos and Honeybush](/ratgeber/rooibos-und-honeybush/).
- **Cold Chai:** Prepare the chai, let it cool and serve over ice. More ideas in [Make your own iced tea](/ratgeber/eistee-selbst-machen/).
- **Less sweet:** Reduce sugar gradually or sweeten with a little honey at the end. Do not cook honey, otherwise it will lose its aroma.

## Chai in everyday Indian life

In India, chai is more than just a drink. It is drunk for breakfast, in the afternoon with friends and colleagues, when visiting and on the go. On street corners and train stations, tea vendors called chaiwalas boil their chai in large pots over the flame and pour it into small glasses or simple clay cups called kulhad. The clay gives the hot tea a slightly earthy scent. Chai is usually served in small portions, several times a day. There is often something to nibble on, such as cookies, fried snacks or bread. The recipe varies from region to region and from house to house, and that's exactly what makes it so appealing: there is no one right chai.

## Regional Chai Styles

Masala chai is just one of many ways to drink tea in South Asia. Some styles you should know:

- **Cutting Chai:** In Mumbai, people like to order half a glass of a very strong, sweet chai. Small, strong and drunk quickly.
- **Irani Chai:** Associated with Iranian immigrant cafes in Hyderabad and Mumbai. The tea steeps long and slowly and is combined with boiled, creamy milk or condensed milk.
- **Sulaimani:** In Kerala, a black tea without milk, with lemon, a little sugar and spices such as cardamom or clove, often after a heavy meal.
- **Kashmiri Kahwa:** Green tea with saffron, cinnamon, cardamom and sliced ​​almonds, without milk, sweetened with sugar or honey.
- **Noon Chai:** Also from Kashmir, a salted milk tea that gets its pink color from long boiling with a pinch of baking soda.

Kahwa and Sulaimani show that Chai doesn't always need milk. If you want something lighter in the evening, you will find nice alternatives here.

## What goes well with chai

A spicy chai goes well with shortbread cookies, rusks, shortbread, banana bread or carrot cake. Savory little things like vegetable pakoras or roasted nuts are also a classic accompaniment. In winter, chai with gingerbread or speculoos is a nice combination because the spices are similar.

## Typical mistakes

| problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| watery, little tea | too little tea, boiled too briefly | more tea, simmer longer, use CTC |
| bitter, tart | Tea brewed too long, too much clove | Boil tea for a shorter time, use cloves sparingly |
| Spices can hardly be tasted | Spices not pressed, cooked too briefly | toast and simmer in water for 3 to 5 minutes |
| overcooked | not observed | Remove the pot from the heat briefly while foaming |
| Milk skin, burnt | too high heat | medium heat, stir |

## Frequently asked questions

### How much caffeine does chai have?

Chai contains caffeine due to the black tea, but due to the milk and the pot, it contains less than a large cup of filter coffee. More in [Caffeine in Coffee and Tea](/ratgeber/koffein-in-kaffee-und-tee/).

### Can I make chai with tea bags?

Yes. Take two to three strong black tea bags for two cups and cut them open if necessary so that the tea boils freely in the pot.

### Why does the milk in chai sometimes curdle?

Fresh ginger contains an enzyme that can curdle milk protein. If you first boil the ginger in water for a few minutes as in the basic recipe, the heat makes the enzyme ineffective and the milk remains smooth. It can also flocculate if it is no longer completely fresh, if lemon ends up in the pot or if a plant drink is not intended for cooking.

### Can I prepare chai?

It's best to just prepare the brew from water, spices and tea, strain it and keep it tightly closed in the refrigerator for one to two days. When serving, boil it with fresh milk. It's better to drink ready-made chai with milk on the same day and just warm it gently, because cooking it for a long time makes it tart.

### Why is chai boiled and not just poured?

Simmering causes spices and tea to dissolve more powerfully, milk and tea to combine, and the full, creamy taste that defines chai is created.
