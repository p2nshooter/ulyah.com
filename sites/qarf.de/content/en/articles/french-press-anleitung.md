The French press, also known as a stamp pot, coffee press or press stamp pot in Germany, is one of the easiest methods to make really good coffee. You don't need a filter, no pouring technology or electricity, just coarsely ground coffee, hot water and four minutes of patience. The result is a full-bodied, strong coffee with a lot of body because the coffee's oils are not retained by paper. In this guide you will learn the basic recipe, a refined method for less grounds in the cup and the most common mistakes.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Starting recipe: 60 to 70 g of coffee per liter of water, so for a 350 ml pot about 21 to 24 g.
> - Coarse grind, similar to coarse sea salt.
> - Water temperature 93 to 96 °C, steeping time 4 minutes.
> - Press the stamp slowly and just above the coffee grounds.
> - Pour the coffee immediately, otherwise it will continue to steep and become bitter.

## How the French Press works

The French press is an immersion method: ground coffee and water lie together in the pot all the time, unlike with a hand filter, where the water runs through the coffee bed. After the brewing time, press down a fine metal sieve that keeps the coffee grounds at the bottom. Because the metal sieve allows oils and very fine particles to pass through, French press coffee tastes fuller and heavier than filter coffee, but is also slightly cloudier.

A form of the pot with a stamp was patented in 1929 by the Italians Attilio Calimani and Giulio Moneta. Despite the name, the French Press is more of an Italian-French community story. In France it is often called “cafetière à piston”.

## What you need

- **French Press** made of glass, stainless steel or ceramic. Double-walled stainless steel jugs maintain the temperature better, glass jugs let you see the brewing process.
- **Mill** with grinder that can grind evenly and coarsely. Blade grinders produce a lot of dust that slips through the sieve.
- **Kettle** and ideally a thermometer.
- **Kitchen scale** and timer.
- **Spoon** for stirring, preferably made of wood or plastic to avoid scratching the glass.

## The basic recipe

| Jug size | Coffee | water |
|---|---|---|
| 350 ml (3 cups according to manufacturer) | 21-24g | 350g |
| 600ml | 36-42g | 600g |
| 1 liter (8 cups according to manufacturer) | 60-70g | 1000g |

The manufacturer's cup specifications often refer to small cups of around 120 milliliters. An “8-cup pot” is enough for three to four normal cups.

## Step by step

1. **Preheat the pot.** Rinse the pot with hot water and throw it away. Otherwise, a cold glass jug will cool down the brewing water significantly.
2. **Grind the coffee coarsely.** The flour should be about as coarse as coarse sea salt. Pour it into the pot.
3. **Pour water.** Pour all of the water at 93 to 96 °C quickly and evenly so that all the coffee grounds are wet. Start the timer.
4. **Wait.** Place the lid with the stamp up to retain the heat. A crust of coffee grounds forms on the surface.
5. **Break up the crust.** After about 4 minutes, stir the crust gently with a spoon. Most of the ground coffee sinks to the bottom.
6. **Press.** Place the lid on and press the stamp down slowly and evenly, about 15 to 20 seconds. If it is very difficult, the coffee is ground too finely.
7. **Transfer immediately.** Pour all the coffee into cups or a preheated thermos. In the French press it continues to pull even when the stamp is down.

## The refined method: less grounds, clearer cup

If you don't like the muddy sediment in the last cup, you can try a longer, calmer version made popular by baristas like James Hoffmann:

1. Grind coffee until medium coarse and pour in hot water at 95 to 100 °C.
2. Allow **4 minutes** to steep without stirring.
3. Break up the crust and skim off the foam and floating particles with two spoons.
4. **Wait another 5 to 8 minutes**. During this time, fine particles also sink to the bottom and the coffee cools down to drinking temperature.
5. Press the stamp down just enough so that it touches the surface. It now only serves as a sieve.
6. Pour in slowly, leaving the last bit in the pot.

The result is significantly clearer and sweeter. A longer steeping does not automatically make the coffee more bitter because the extraction slows down significantly after a few minutes and the coffee grounds collect at the bottom.

## Adjust the taste

- **Too thin and sour:** grind finer, use more coffee or let steep longer.
- **Too bitter and dry:** Grind more coarsely or lower the temperature slightly. Also check whether you leave the coffee in the pot after pressing.
- **Too much grounds:** Grind coarser and more evenly, press more gently, leave the last milliliters in the pot.
- **Too weak despite good extraction:** Increase ratio to 70 g per liter.

## Which coffee is right?

The French press enhances body and sweetness. Medium roasts with notes of chocolate and nuts, for example from Brazil, Guatemala or Sumatra, develop wonderfully here. Light, fruity coffees also work, but appear less clear than in a hand filter. Very dark roasts can quickly become bitter; then take water around 90 °C.

## The French press in comparison

Each preparation emphasizes different sides of the same coffee. Anyone who knows the differences chooses the method that suits the bean and the situation.

| Method | body | Clarity | Effort | Sentence in the cup |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| French press | full, oily | medium | low | a little, with careful technique little |
| [Hand filter](/ratgeber/handfilter-kaffee-anleitung/) | light to medium | high | medium, casting technique required | none |
| [Filter coffee machine](/ratgeber/filterkaffeemaschine-richtig-nutzen/) | light to medium | medium to high | very low | none |
| [Espresso maker](/ratgeber/espressokocher-anleitung/) | strong, concentrated | low | low | hardly |

The French press is the right choice if you want a round, strong coffee with little effort and like beans with chocolaty, nutty notes.

## For guests and on the go

A large jug with a capacity of one liter is practical for several people. Make sure that you immediately pour the finished coffee into a preheated thermos flask, otherwise if you use large quantities it will sit on the grounds for a long time. For travel and the office, there are small stainless steel cup presses in which you can brew and drink directly. Your disadvantage: The coffee remains in contact with the flour and continues to brew. Choose models where the sentence can be separated or drink quickly.

## French Press Care

Coffee oils get stuck in the sieve and become rancid over time. You can taste that.

- After each use, put the coffee grounds in the organic waste or compost, not in the drain, as they can clog pipes.
- Rinse the jug and stamp with warm water.
- Unscrew the sieve once a week. Most punches consist of a cross disc, screen mesh and spiral disc. Clean the parts with dish soap and a brush.
- Many jugs are dishwasher safe, but check the manufacturer's instructions, especially for wooden or plastic parts.
- A bent or frayed screen will allow more grounds to pass through. Replacement screens are available for most models.

## Security

Glass jugs can crack when exposed to thermal shock or impact. Do not pour boiling water into an ice-cold pot, place the pot on a hot stove, or force the plunger down. If it gets stuck, pull it up a little and press again, more slowly. When pressing, hold the handle firmly and the jug on a stable surface.

## French press for tea and cold brew

The stamp pot is a small universal device. You can brew loose tea in it; After the steeping time, press down the stamp and pour immediately. Then clean the pot thoroughly so that no coffee taste ends up in the tea. The French Press is also suitable for [Cold Brew](/ratgeber/cold-brew-anleitung/): Mix coarsely ground coffee with cold water, let it steep in the refrigerator for 12 to 18 hours and then press. To make milk foam, you can froth warm milk in the jug using quick stamping movements.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is French press coffee unhealthy?

French press coffee contains more coffee oils than filter coffee, including the diterpenes cafestol and kahweol. If consumed regularly and in large quantities, these can increase LDL cholesterol levels. Paper filters retain a large part of it. Anyone who has high cholesterol levels should preferably drink filter coffee and discuss the issue with their doctor.

### How long should the coffee steep?

Four minutes is the classic starting value. You can vary between three and five minutes, or with the refined method you can wait longer if the coffee grounds are undisturbed on the ground.

### Can I use pre-ground coffee?

Pre-ground coffee from the supermarket is usually ground for filter machines and therefore too fine for the French press. A lot of sentences are created and the stamp is difficult. If there is no other option, reduce the steeping time to three minutes and press very slowly.

### Why is my coffee lukewarm?

Glass jugs lose heat quickly. Preheat the pot and cups and pour immediately after pressing. A double-walled stainless steel jug maintains the temperature significantly longer.

### Can I keep the coffee warm in the pot?

Better not. The coffee continues to steep on the grounds and becomes bitter, and a glass pot does not belong on the stove or on a warmer with an open flame. After pressing, pour the coffee into a preheated thermos. It stays hot there for a while without drying out.

## Conclusion

The French press forgives a lot and rewards a few but important habits: grind coarsely and evenly, weigh out enough coffee, wait four minutes, press gently and pour immediately. If you also skim off the crust and wait a little longer, you will get an amazingly clear, sweet cup with the full body for which the stamp pot is loved.
