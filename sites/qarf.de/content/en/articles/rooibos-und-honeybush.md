Rooibos has found many friends in Germany: as a warm, sweet evening tea without caffeine, as a base for vanilla and orange mixtures or as a cold thirst quencher in summer. Its close relative honeybush is less well known, but tastes similarly pleasant, with an even more pronounced honey note. Both come from a small region in the southwest of South Africa and do not grow in significant quantities anywhere else. Botanically they have nothing to do with the tea plant, they are legumes. This guide explains where rooibos and honeybush come from, how they are processed and how best to prepare them.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Rooibos (**Aspalathus linearis**) and honeybush (**Cyclopia**) are shrubs from the Cape region of South Africa and are naturally **caffeine-free**.
> - **Red Rooibos** is fermented, i.e. oxidized, and tastes sweet, warm, like wood, honey and vanilla. **Green Rooibos** is not oxidized and tastes fresher, grassier.
> - **Honeybush** smells of honey and dried fruit and is slightly sweeter than rooibos.
> - Pour in **boiling water** and let it steep for **5 to 10 minutes**. Both hardly become bitter.
> - Both are great for iced tea, lattes and blends.

## Origin: the Cape and the Cederberg

Rooibos, meaning “red bush” in Afrikaans, grows in the **Cederberg** mountains and surrounding areas, about two to three hours’ drive north of Cape Town. The region has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers and wet winters and sandy, nutrient-poor soils. The shrub has needle-shaped leaves and forms long roots with which it reaches water deep in the ground. The people of the region have been using rooibos for a long time. It has been cultivated and exported on a larger scale since the 20th century.

Honeybush grows further south and east in the mountainous regions of the Eastern and Western Cape. There are several Cyclopia species. To this day, some of the honeybush is collected from the wild and some is cultivated. The yellow flowers smell strongly of honey, hence the name.

Rooibos is registered in the EU as a **protected designation of origin**. Only Rooibos from the specified region in South Africa can be called that.

## How rooibos is processed

In summer, the branches with leaves are cut, chopped, moistened and piled up in piles. Due to oxidation, the green parts of the plant turn reddish-brown and the typical sweet aroma is created. The rooibos is then dried in the sun, sieved and often steam sterilized to make it microbially safe.

**Green Rooibos** is dried immediately after cutting, without oxidation. It remains olive green and tastes lighter, grassier, with a fine malt note.

## A closer look at Honeybush

Honeybush is not a single shrub, but a collective name for several species of the genus **Cyclopia**. The best known include Cyclopia intermedia, which has long been collected primarily from the wild, as well as Cyclopia subternata and Cyclopia genistoides, which are increasingly being grown in fields. The processing is similar to that of rooibos: the cut branches are moistened and oxidized under heat until the green turns into a warm brown and the typical honey scent is created, then dried. Because wild stocks are limited and grow only slowly, cultivation is important so that honeybush remains available in the long term.

## Taste in comparison

| drink | taste | color | Caffeine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red Rooibos | sweet, woody, honey, vanilla, slightly nutty | reddish brown | no |
| Green Rooibos | fresh, grassy, slightly malty | golden yellow | no |
| Honeybush | Honey, dried apricot, flowers, sweeter than rooibos | amber colored | no |
| Black tea for comparison | malty, bitter | dark brown | yes |

Rooibos contains only a few tannins. It therefore hardly becomes bitter even after a long brewing time and can appear sweet without sugar.

## Preparation

| Quantity per 250 ml | Temperature | Brewing time |
|---|---|---|
| 2 to 3 g, about a heaped teaspoon | bubbling and boiling | 5 to 10 minutes |

Like herbal teas, rooibos and honeybush are among the infusions that should be prepared with **boiling water** so that the aroma and color can fully develop and possible germs are killed. More on this principle can be found in [Herbal tea and fruit tea](/ratgeber/kraeutertee-und-fruechtetee/). Rooibos is finely chopped and slips easily through coarse sieves. A fine tea strainer, a paper filter or a tea filter made of fine stainless steel helps, see [Tea accessories](/ratgeber/teezubehoer/).

A second infusion is possible and often tastes pleasant. In South Africa, rooibos is sometimes left in the pot for a long time or briefly boiled on the stove to get a particularly strong infusion.

## Rooibos variations

- **With milk and honey:** Common in South Africa. Rooibos tolerates milk well and becomes creamy and round.
- **Rooibos latte:** Make a very strong rooibos infusion, about 4 to 5 grams per 100 milliliters of water, top up with frothed milk. How to froth milk is described in [Milk foam and cappuccino](/ratgeber/milchschaum-und-cappuccino/).
- **Rooibos Chai:** Simmer rooibos with cinnamon, cardamom, ginger and cloves in milk, a caffeine-free version of the classic chai, see [Make your own chai](/ratgeber/chai-selbst-machen/).
- **Iced tea:** Pour hot rooibos, let cool, serve with orange or lemon slices and mint. More in [Make your own iced tea](/ratgeber/eistee-selbst-machen/).
- **Blends:** Vanilla, orange peel, cinnamon, caramel and cocoa shells work very well. Honeybush harmonizes with peach, apricot and lemongrass.

## Health: what can be said

Rooibos and honeybush contain polyphenols, including substances such as aspalathin, which are almost exclusively found in rooibos. A lot of research is being carried out into possible effects, but the results mainly come from laboratory and animal experiments. So far, no reliable statements about health benefits in humans can be derived from this. One thing is certain: Rooibos is a good caffeine-free alternative for the evening and for anyone who wants to avoid caffeine. How much caffeine is in other drinks is shown in [Caffeine in Coffee and Tea](/ratgeber/koffein-in-kaffee-und-tee/).

As with all herbal and plant infusions, variety makes sense rather than drinking large quantities of just one type over a long period of time. Pregnant and breastfeeding women, as well as people taking medication, should seek medical advice if they have any questions.

## Cultivation, climate and traditional knowledge

Because rooibos only grows in a small region, it is particularly sensitive to changes in the climate. Longer dry periods and heat take a toll on plants, and researchers in South Africa are investigating how cultivation and wild populations can be protected. This also includes the question of who owns the knowledge about the plant. Long before rooibos was traded, the San and Khoikhoi people used it. In 2019, the South African rooibos industry entered into an agreement with representatives of these communities to share in the proceeds. It is considered one of the first agreements of its kind for such a well-known food.

## What to look for when buying

- **Origin:** Real Rooibos comes from South Africa. The name is protected.
- **Cut:** Fine cut pulls faster and more powerfully. Longer needles and less dust indicate careful sorting.
- **Flavored or plain:** Many rooibos blends are flavored. Try pure rooibos to get to know the natural taste.
- **Organic and fair:** Some of the rooibos are grown organically or traded fairly. [Seal for coffee and tea](/ratgeber/siegel-fairtrade-bio/) explains what the seals mean.

## Rooibos in the kitchen

Rooibos is not just a drink. Because it is sweet, mild and hardly bitter, it is suitable for cooking and baking. A strong infusion replaces water when soaking dried fruits for muesli or fruit bread and gives them a subtle vanilla and honey note. Pears or apples can be poached in rooibos with a little cinnamon, lemon zest and honey until they are soft. To make a rooibos syrup, briefly boil a very strong infusion with an equal amount of sugar. It tastes good in iced tea, over yoghurt or in desserts. Rooibos is also used in marinades for poultry or in sauces in South Africa, where it is a natural ingredient in many households.

## Typical mistakes

| problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Tea tastes thin | too little rooibos, pulled too short, water not boiling | Take a heaped teaspoon and let it steep for five to ten minutes |
| Crumbs in the cup | coarse sieve | Use a fine sieve or paper filter |
| Taste artificial | strongly flavored mixture | Try pure rooibos |
| Aroma weak despite the right amount | old or damp stored goods | Store airtight, buy fresh pack |

## Storage

Like other dried plants, rooibos loses its aroma over time, but more slowly than many herbs. Stored airtight, dark, dry and away from spices, it tastes good for one to two years. More in [Store coffee and tea properly](/ratgeber/kaffee-und-tee-lagern/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is rooibos suitable for children?

Rooibos is caffeine-free and mild and is also offered to children in many families, diluted and without sugar. For infants and small children, talk to your pediatrician beforehand.

### Why does rooibos color so strongly?

The dye is created during oxidation and dissolves well in hot water. Rooibos stains on textiles can usually be removed like tea stains when they are fresh.

### Can I let rooibos steep for too long?

Barely. It becomes stronger and darker over time, but only slightly bitter. Many people simply leave the bag in the cup.

### Why is it called fermentation when the rooibos oxidizes?

In the tea trade, the word fermentation has become commonplace for oxidation, although it is primarily the plant's own substances that react with oxygen. The term is also used traditionally for rooibos. This refers to the step in which green needles become the red-brown, sweet-smelling rooibos.

### Can I chill rooibos?

Better not directly. As with herbal tea, it is recommended to pour boiling water over dried plant parts because they can carry germs. For a cold drink, brew the rooibos hot and twice as strong, let it cool down and then put it in the fridge.

## Conclusion

Rooibos and honeybush are more than a replacement for evening tea. When boiled and steeped generously, they taste sweet, round and without bitterness, either pure or with milk, as iced tea or in the kitchen. Anyone who tries pure red and green rooibos side by side will discover how much character there is in the inconspicuous needles.
