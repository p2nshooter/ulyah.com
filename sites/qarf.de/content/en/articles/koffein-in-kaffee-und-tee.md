Caffeine is the reason why many people reach for coffee or tea in the morning. At the same time, no other ingredient has as many half-truths circulating around it: espresso has the most caffeine, tea works differently than caffeine, and green tea is practically caffeine-free. In this guide you will find the actual amounts, the assessments of the European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) and the factors that determine how much caffeine ends up in your cup.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - According to EFSA, up to 400 mg of caffeine per day and up to 200 mg at once are safe for healthy adults.
> - There is an upper limit of 200 mg per day for pregnant and breastfeeding women.
> - A cup of filter coffee (200 ml) contains around 90 mg, an espresso around 80 mg, a cup of black tea around 50 mg.
> - Tea and caffeine are the same substance.
> - The half-life of caffeine in adults is usually around 3 to 5 hours, but can vary significantly.

## What caffeine is and how it works

Caffeine is a natural alkaloid found in coffee beans, tea leaves, cocoa beans, guarana, mate and kola nuts. The plants use this to protect themselves from predators. In the human body, caffeine works primarily by blocking the docking sites for adenosine in the brain. Adenosine accumulates throughout the day and signals fatigue. If the receptor is blocked, we feel more awake and more focused.

The effect usually sets in after about 15 to 45 minutes because caffeine is quickly and almost completely absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract. The liver then breaks it down gradually.

## Caffeine in typical drinks

In its 2015 assessment, the EFSA gave example amounts that consumer advice centers also use as a guide:

| drink | portion | Caffeine (approx.) |
|---|---|---|
| Filter coffee | 200ml | 90 mg |
| Espresso | 60ml | 80 mg |
| Black tea | 220ml | 50 mg |
| Cola | 355ml | 40 mg |
| Energy drink | 250ml | 80 mg |
| Dark chocolate | 50g | 25 mg |

The values are average values. Depending on the bean, leaf and preparation, a cup can contain significantly more or less. These rough ranges apply to other drinks:

| drink | portion | Caffeine (span) |
|---|---|---|
| Simple espresso | 25-30ml | about 50-80 mg |
| Cappuccino with simple espresso | 150-200ml | like the espresso |
| French press | 250ml | about 80-135 mg |
| Cold brew, ready to drink | 250ml | strongly dependent on the prescription, often 100 mg and more |
| Green tea | 200ml | about 20-50 mg |
| Matcha | 1g powder | about 20-35 mg |
| White tea | 200ml | about 15-50 mg |
| Decaffeinated coffee | 200ml | usually less than 5 mg |
| Rooibos, herbal tea | 200ml | 0 mg |

### The espresso fallacy

Espresso has by far the most caffeine per milliliter because it is so concentrated. However, a simple espresso often contains less caffeine per serving than a large mug of filter coffee. Around 12 to 15 grams of coffee are used for the filter cup, around 7 to 10 grams for a simple espresso. In addition, the contact time with the filter is longer.

## What determines the caffeine content

With coffee:

- **Type of coffee:** Robusta contains about twice as much caffeine as Arabica, around 2 to 2.7% instead of 1 to 1.5%. More about this in the guide [Arabica and Robusta](/ratgeber/arabica-und-robusta/).
- **Quantity:** More coffee powder means more caffeine. That is the most important factor.
- **Roasting level:** Caffeine is heat stable. Dark roasts contain almost the same amount of caffeine per bean as light roasts. Because dark beans are lighter, a spoonful of dark roast actually contains slightly less per volume.
- **Preparation:** Long contact times such as in the French press or cold brew dissolve the caffeine very completely.

With tea:

- **Leaf age:** Young shoots and buds contain more caffeine than old leaves. That's why silver needle teas and high-quality green teas are not automatically low in caffeine.
- **Shading:** Gyokuro and Matcha contain a particularly high amount of caffeine.
- **Stewing time and temperature:** Hot water and longer steeping time dissolve more caffeine.
- **Leaf Size:** Broken leaves in a tea bag release caffeine faster than whole leaves.

## Teain is caffeine

The word tea comes from the 19th century, when caffeine was discovered in tea and initially thought to be a separate substance. Chemically it is the same molecule. There are several reasons why tea often feels gentler: A cup of tea usually contains less caffeine than a cup of coffee, and tea contains the amino acid L-theanine, which studies have linked to calmer, more focused alertness. However, it has not been conclusively clarified how strong this effect is when drinking normal amounts.

## How much is too much?

In 2015, the EFSA came to the following estimates for the total intake from all sources:

| Group of people | Safe amount |
|---|---|
| Healthy adults, throughout the day | up to 400 mg per day |
| Healthy adults, as a single dose | up to 200 mg |
| Pregnant and breastfeeding women | up to 200 mg per day |
| Children and young people | about 3 mg per kg body weight per day |

400 mg roughly corresponds to four cups of filter coffee or five espressos throughout the day. For a single dose of 200 mg, the EFSA calculates around 3 mg per kilogram of body weight. Anyone who drinks caffeine or consumes alcohol before exercising should be more careful.

Possible signs of too much caffeine include nervousness, palpitations, tremors, stomach problems and difficulty sleeping. People with cardiac arrhythmias, high blood pressure, anxiety disorders or certain medications should discuss their caffeine intake with their doctor.

## A calculation example for your day

Numbers become more tangible when you calculate your own day. Let's say you drink a large 300 milliliter mug of filter coffee in the morning, a cappuccino with double espresso in the morning, a cup of black tea after lunch, and a cola in the afternoon.

| drink | Caffeine (estimated) |
|---|---|
| Mug of filter coffee, 300 ml | about 135 mg |
| Cappuccino with Doppio | about 120-160 mg |
| Black tea, 220 ml | about 50 mg |
| Cola, 330 ml | about 35-40 mg |
| **Total** | **about 340-385 mg** |

This is still below the 400 mg that the EFSA considers to be safe for healthy adults, but already quite close to it. If you add an energy drink or a piece of dark chocolate, the limit is quickly reached. The example also shows that it is not the individual cup that makes the difference, but the sum of all sources. If you want to reduce your quantity, it's best to start with the largest item, here the cup in the morning, and replace about half of the beans with decaffeinated ones.

## Children, teenagers and energy drinks

Children are more sensitive to caffeine because they weigh less. The EFSA guideline of around 3 milligrams per kilogram of body weight means around 90 milligrams per day for a child weighing 30 kilograms, i.e. roughly as much as in a cup of filter coffee or a large bottle of cola. In the EU, caffeinated drinks with more than 150 milligrams of caffeine per liter must bear a label stating that they are not recommended for children, pregnant or breastfeeding women. This particularly affects energy drinks, which are popular among young people and are often drunk in large doses.

## Less caffeine, same enjoyment

If you want to reduce caffeine, you don't have to give up coffee and tea. Smaller cups, pure Arabica instead of blends containing Robusta and a switch to half decaffeinated coffee in the afternoon reduce the amount noticeably. When it comes to tea, it helps to use less leaf or switch to rooibos and herbal tea in the evening. A common misconception is that a quick first brew of 30 seconds will “wash” the caffeine out of the tea. Measurements show that only a small part is dissolved. If you really want little caffeine, it's better to use caffeine-free alternatives.

## How long caffeine works

The half-life of caffeine is usually around 3 to 5 hours in healthy adults. After this time, half is still in the blood. But the range is large:

- **Smoking** significantly accelerates the breakdown.
- **Hormonal contraceptives** and **pregnancy**, especially in the last trimester, slow it down significantly.
- **Genetic differences** in liver enzymes explain why some people drink espresso in the evening and sleep well, while others lie awake after an afternoon coffee.

If you don't sleep well, it's worth a simple experiment: stop drinking coffee or black or green tea after around 2 p.m. and see if anything changes.

## habituation and renunciation

If you drink caffeine every day, you get used to some of the effects. If you stop taking it abruptly, you may experience headaches, tiredness and irritability for a few days. If you want to reduce, it is better to reduce the amount gradually, for example by gradually replacing some of the beans with decaffeinated coffee. More about this in the guide [Decaffeinated Coffee](/ratgeber/entkoffeinierter-kaffee/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does dark roast coffee have less caffeine?

Only insignificant per bean. However, because dark beans are lighter and larger, one scoop of dark roast contains slightly less caffeine per volume. If you dose according to weight, you will hardly notice a difference.

### Does coffee have a dehydrating effect?

In higher doses, caffeine has a slightly diuretic effect. The effect is small for people who drink coffee regularly. Coffee can therefore contribute to your daily fluid intake.

### Does decaffeinated coffee contain no caffeine at all?

Almost none. In Germany, according to the coffee regulations, decaffeinated roasted coffee may contain a maximum of 0.1% caffeine in the dry matter. One cup usually only delivers a few milligrams.

### Is mate a caffeine-free alternative?

No. Mate also contains caffeine, depending on how it is prepared, as much as a cup of tea or more. Rooibos, honeybush and most herbal and fruit teas are caffeine-free.

## Conclusion

How much caffeine is in your cup depends primarily on the amount of coffee or tea, rather than the roast or the name of the drink. If you add up your daily sources, pay attention to your own sleep and gradually reduce it if necessary, you can enjoy coffee and tea without any worries.
