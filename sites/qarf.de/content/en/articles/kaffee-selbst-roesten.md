Anyone who has ever drunk freshly roasted coffee understands why roasters are so proud of their craft. When roasted, a green, grassy-smelling bean turns into the brown, fragrant coffee bean we know in just a few minutes. Hundreds of aromatic substances are created, and every second, every degree changes the taste. Roasting at home is not rocket science. With some green coffee, a pan or a small roasting device and patience, you can get started with your own roasting. Perfect results like those from a professional roaster are rare in the beginning, but every attempt will teach you more about coffee than any book.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Green coffee is green, has a long shelf life and is available from specialist retailers, often in small quantities starting at one kilogram.
> - During roasting, the bean goes through the drying phase, browning and development. The “first crack”, an audible crack, indicates the beginning of a light roast.
> - At home, a pan, oven, converted hot air popcorn machine or small drum roaster are suitable.
> - After roasting, the beans need to cool quickly and then rest for one to three days before they reveal their full flavor.
> - Roasting produces smoke and silver skin. Ventilate well, never leave unattended and keep a fire extinguisher or fire blanket handy.

## Buy green coffee

You can get green coffee from specialized online retailers, and sometimes directly from small roasters. It is offered according to country of origin, region, variety and preparation. Well-washed coffees from Central and South America are suitable to start with because they roast evenly and are forgiving. Naturally prepared coffees from Ethiopia have an exciting aroma, but roast more unevenly. More about this in [Preparation: washed, natural, honey](/ratgeber/aufbereitung-gewaschen-natural-honey/).

Green coffee can last for many months when stored in a cool, dry and dark place, significantly longer than roasted coffee. However, only buy as much as you will roast in a year. When roasted, the bean loses around 15 to 20 percent of its weight, mostly water.

## What happens during roasting

| phase | What happens | How you recognize them |
|---|---|---|
| drying | Water evaporates from the bean | Color changes from green to yellow, smell of hay and grain |
| Tanning | Maillard reaction and caramelization begin | Beans turn light brown, smell like bread and caramel |
| First crack | Water vapor and gases break down the cell structure | audible cracking like popcorn, silver membranes come off |
| Development | Acidity is reduced, sweetness and body increase | Beans brown evenly and the surface becomes smoother |
| Second crack | Cell walls break again and oil leaks out | Quieter, faster crackling, beans have an oily shine |

You finish a light roast shortly after the first crack, a medium one about one to two minutes after, and a dark one shortly before or on the second crack. How the roasting levels differ in taste is explained [Roasting levels explained](/ratgeber/roestgrade-erklaert/). For the first roast, aim for a medium roast: it forgives small mistakes and tastes the same in filter and French press.

## Home methods

### pan

The easiest method: A heavy cast iron or stainless steel skillet, uncoated, over medium heat. Add 100 to 150 grams of green coffee and stir continuously so that nothing burns. The first crack occurs after about 8 to 12 minutes. The roasting is usually uneven because the beans are on the hot surface. But it's instructive to get you started because you see, hear and smell every phase.

### Oven

Spread the beans in a single layer on a perforated tray or in a sieve and roast at around 230 to 250 degrees. Turn them every few minutes. The oven roasts more evenly than the pan, but you have a harder time seeing what's happening and the silver skins spread throughout the oven.

### Hot air popcorn machine

Many home roasters start with a simple hot air popcorn machine. The hot air swirls the beans around, the roast becomes even and only takes five to eight minutes. However, the devices are not built for this purpose and can overheat, which will then void the warranty. Roast only small quantities, supervise the appliance at all times and use it outside or under a strong extractor hood.

### Small drum roasters

For serious home roasters, there are small electric drum roasters that handle a few hundred grams per batch. They can be controlled in temperature and airflow and deliver consistent, repeatable results. They are significantly more expensive, but are the best choice if you want to roast regularly.

## Step by step: the first roasting

1. **Prepare:** Open window or roast outside, hood on, metal sieve ready for cooling, stopwatch and notepad.
2. **Weigh:** 100 to 150 grams of green coffee.
3. **Roasting:** Preheat the device, pour in the beans, start the timer.
4. **Observe:** Note when the beans turn yellow, when they turn brown, and when the first crack begins.
5. **Finish:** For a medium roast, one to two minutes after the first crack begins.
6. **Cooling:** Immediately pour the beans into a metal sieve and toss, preferably in front of a fan, until they are lukewarm. Without rapid refrigeration, they will continue to roast in their own heat.
7. **Remove the skin:** Blow away the loosened silver skin while swinging, preferably outside.

## Assess the roast: weight, color and grind

After cooling, a few simple checks will tell you how well the roasting went.

- **Weight Loss:** Weigh the beans before and after roasting and calculate the loss as a percentage. The darker the roast, the more weight the bean loses. If two roasts of the same bean are close to each other, they were similarly developed. This is a comparison value that does not depend on the light in your kitchen.
- **Evenness:** Pour the beans onto a white plate. Individual, strikingly light beans often come from unripe cherries and hardly brown. Sort them out because they taste peanutty and flat. On the other hand, strong color differences throughout the batch indicate uneven heat.
- **Grinding pattern:** Grind a few beans and compare the color of the flour to the surface. If the flour is significantly lighter, the inside is less developed than the outside. Next time, start more gently and extend the development a little.
- **Surface:** Light and medium roasts are matte. Oily spots even on the first day show that you were close to or above the second crack.

## Rest period and storage

Freshly roasted beans continue to release carbon dioxide for days. If you grind and brew it straight away, you will get an unsettled coffee with lots of bubbles and an unbalanced taste. Therefore, let the beans rest: for filter coffee one to three days, for espresso often five to ten days. Store them in a container with an aroma valve or in a container that you do not close tightly for the first few days. The best way to store coffee afterwards can be found in [Store coffee and tea properly](/ratgeber/kaffee-und-tee-lagern/).

## Security

- Roasting produces smoke, especially dark roasts. Always roast in good ventilation or outside.
- Silver skins are highly flammable. Empty skin catchers after each pass and do not leave them in devices.
- Never leave the roaster unattended, even for a minute.
- Have a fire extinguisher or fire blanket ready. Coffee beans can burn if roasted very darkly.

## Typical mistakes

| problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Beans dark on the outside, light on the inside | too high temperature at the beginning | start more gently, dry longer |
| Grassy, sour, nutty-raw | Roasted too short, finished before or right at the first crack | Extend development after the first crack |
| Flat, papery | Roasted too long and too slowly | higher temperature in the browning phase |
| Smoky, burnt | too dark or uneven | finish earlier, stir more evenly |
| Very different colors | uneven heat | smaller quantities, constantly moving, better device |

## Keep a roasting log

If you roast regularly, you should write down each pass. Note the origin and quantity of the green coffee, the starting temperature or heating level, the times for yellowing, first crack and end of roasting, the weight before and after roasting and later the taste in the cup. After a few roasts you will see patterns: which development time after the first crack you like best with this coffee, whether the drying phase was too short or the roasting took too long. These notes are the basis for reliably repeating a good roast.

## Compare two roasts

The quickest way to learn is through direct comparison. Roast the same bean twice with the same quantity and heat setting and only change the end:

1. **Finish Roast A:** about a minute after the first crack begins.
2. **Roast B:** Finish about two minutes after the first crack begins.
3. **Let it rest:** both for the same amount of time, for example two days.
4. **Brew the same:** same dose, same grind, same method, preferably hand filter or French press.
5. **Try it blindly:** Have someone mark the cups or mix them up yourself before tasting.

The shorter roast usually appears lighter and more acidic, the longer roast appears sweeter and rounder, with more body and less fruit. Which one you like better is a matter of taste. What matters is that you taste the difference yourself and base your roasting protocol on it. [Tasting Coffee](/ratgeber/kaffee-verkosten/) shows you how to taste in a structured manner.

## Own mixtures: mix before or after roasting?

With several green coffees, it makes sense to create your own blend, such as a chocolaty Brazilian with a fruity Ethiopian. There are two ways to do this:

| Procedure | advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| mix before roasting | one pass, little effort | Beans of different sizes and densities roast at different rates |
| mix after roasting | Each variety is roasted to suit, proportions can be finely tuned | double the effort, more notes |

For starters, blending after roasting is more reliable. Try the proportions in small increments, such as two parts to one part, and note how the cup changes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is self-roasting worth it financially?

Green coffee is cheaper than specialty roasted coffee, but equipment, time and waste are involved in the beginning. The biggest gain is not the price, but the joy of the craft and the freshness.

### Can I roast espresso beans myself?

Yes, but espresso is less forgiving of roasting mistakes because it concentrates aromas strongly. Start with filter coffee and then move up to espresso as described in [Portafilter and Espresso](/ratgeber/siebtraeger-espresso-grundlagen/).

### I don't listen to first crack. What am I doing wrong?

The device often drowns out the cracking, especially popcorn machines or a running fan. Then pay attention to signs that you can see and smell: The beans become larger, the silver skins become more loose, the smell changes from bread to caramel and roasted aroma. If there is no crack at all, the heat was usually too low, perhaps because the amount was too large for the device. Then roast a smaller amount.

### How long does home-roasted coffee last?

Whole beans taste best about two to six weeks after roasting. After that they lose a lot of their aroma.
