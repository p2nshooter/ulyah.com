There is a saying among coffee lovers that is almost always true: Spend your money on the grinder first, then the machine. A good grinder makes more out of every preparation method; a bad grinder ruins even the most expensive espresso machine. The reason is simple: How evenly the coffee is ground determines how evenly the water leaches it. In this guide you will find out how the different grinders work, what advantages and disadvantages they have and what you should pay attention to when buying.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Blade grinders chop the beans unevenly. For good coffee, mills with grinders are much better.
> - Conical grinders work with a cone-shaped inner part in a ring, disc grinders with two flat disks.
> - Both types can be excellent. Precision, stability and sharpness are more important than shape.
> - For espresso you need a grinder with a very fine, continuous or very finely graduated setting.
> - Look for low dead space, easy cleaning, and available replacement parts.

## Why the mill is so important

When brewing, coffee is leached from the outside in. Fine particles release their substances quickly, coarse particles release their substances slowly. If your coffee grounds have very different particle sizes, both things happen at the same time: the fine particles are over-extracted and taste bitter, the coarse ones are under-extracted and taste sour. The result is a cup that appears bitter and sour at the same time and cannot be adjusted cleanly.

No grinder grinds perfectly evenly. Each produces a distribution of particle sizes, plus a proportion of very fine particles called fines. Good mills keep this distribution narrow and the proportion of fines controlled.

The second reason is freshness. Ground coffee has a much larger surface area than whole beans and loses volatile flavors within minutes. A dedicated grinder allows you to grind right before brewing.

## Impact knife mills: cheap, but uneven

Beater blade mills look like small blenders. A propeller knife rotates quickly and smashes the beans. How fine the flour becomes depends solely on how long you press the button.

**Advantages:** very cheap, small, can also be used for spices.

**Disadvantages:** The flour is very uneven, with dust and coarse chunks at the same time. The grinding level can hardly be reproduced. The long beating causes the flour to warm up a little.

If you only have a beater blade grinder, you can improve the results by gently shaking the grinder while grinding and grinding in short intervals. This works for French presses and filters in a pinch. It is unsuitable for espresso.

## Conical grinder

A conical grinder consists of a cone-shaped inner grinding body that rotates in a toothed outer ring. The beans are roughly broken at the top and then move down into the increasingly narrow gap, where they are finely ground. The distance between the cone and the ring determines the degree of grinding.

**Advantages:**

- Conical grinders work at low speeds, which is why they are often quieter and do not heat up the flour much.
- They are well suited for hand mills because the effort required is moderate.
- They often produce a slightly wider distribution of particle sizes, which can promote a round, full-bodied taste in espresso.

**Disadvantages:**

- Simple conical grinders can wobble if stored incorrectly, and the ground material will then become uneven.

## Disc grinder

With the disc grinder, two flat, toothed discs lie parallel to each other. One rotates, the other stands still. The beans are fed in the middle and are pushed outwards through the increasingly fine gap by centrifugal force.

**Advantages:**

- Precisely manufactured disc grinders often produce a very narrow particle distribution. This can lead to particularly clear, differentiated cups, especially with filter coffee and light espresso roasts.
- Large discs grind quickly and are suitable for large quantities, which is why you can find them in many catering mills.

**Disadvantages:**

- Disc grinders often rotate faster and require a powerful motor.
- Depending on the design, more coffee remains in the grinder and ejection.
- Good disc mills for the home are usually more expensive.

## Cone or disc: which is better?

The honest answer: Both can be excellent. A carefully crafted, sturdy mill with a conical burr is superior to a wobbly, cheap disc mill and vice versa. These points are particularly important for the decision:

| criterion | What you pay attention to |
|---|---|
| Size of the grinder | Larger grinding media grind faster and often more evenly |
| material | Hardened steel is common and durable, ceramic is hard but fragile |
| Storage | the axis should be stable and free of play |
| Setting | stepless or many fine levels for espresso, coarser levels are sufficient for filters |
| dead space | how much coffee remains in the device after grinding; less is better |
| Cleaning | The grinder should be able to be removed without tools or with simple tools |
| Spare parts | Grinding discs and cones wear out and should be replaceable |

## The right mill for your preparation

### Filters, French Press and AeroPress only

You don't need an extremely fine setting here. A good electric filter mill or a high-quality hand mill is sufficient. Make sure that the grinder grinds evenly in the medium and coarse range, as many inexpensive devices produce a lot of dust, especially with the coarse setting.

### Espresso

Espresso is the most demanding discipline. Even the smallest changes to the grinding level change the processing time by several seconds. You need a mill with very fine adjustment, ideally continuously variable, with a stable grinder and a grinding level that does not change even after days. Many espresso grinders are also designed for individual dosing: you weigh each portion and grind it completely.

### For everything

All-round grinders should be able to do espresso and filters. This is technically difficult because the adjustment path has to be very large. Make sure that you can quickly switch between both areas and that the setting has a marking or scale so that you can find your espresso point again.

## Hand mill or electric?

Good hand mills with conical grinders often provide better grind quality for the price than electric mills in the same price range because the money goes into the grinder and storage instead of the motor and housing. Grinding takes time and energy: depending on the grinder, you need roughly 30 to 60 seconds to make 15 grams of filter coffee, and significantly longer for espresso. ## Other features

- **Timer or Scale:** Some mills grind by time or by weight. Dosing by weight is more accurate because the grinding speed changes with the bean and grind size.
- **Anti-static:** Static electricity causes coffee grounds to stick to the container and fly around. Some mills have built-in countermeasures. A trick is to moisten the beans with a tiny drop of water before grinding, for example by stirring them with a moistened spoon handle.
- **Volume:** Slowly rotating mills are usually quieter, an advantage in the early morning.
- **Bean container:** You don't need a large container for single dosing. Beans that sit in the hopper for days age faster.

## Single dosage or bean container

Many home grinders today are designed for **single dosage**: you weigh the beans for exactly one preparation, put them in the small funnel and grind everything through. This keeps the remaining beans fresh in their can and makes switching between varieties easier. Such a grinder should retain as little coffee as possible inside so that the weighed amount ends up in the cup. Towards the end of the grinding process, the last beans tend to bounce around on the grinder instead of being drawn in; a weight on the beans or a pair of bellows helps against this. Grinders with large **bean containers** are more convenient if you make many cups a day of the same type. Then only fill in as much as you will use in a few days.

## Know the zero point

The zero point is the setting at which the grinding media just touch each other. Many scales refer to it, and anyone who knows it can compare settings with other users of the same grinder or quickly find them again after cleaning. Only determine it when the grinder is empty and turn carefully until you hear or feel a slight grinding noise. Do not let the grinder run in this position, otherwise the blades will become dull. How to find your grinding level from there is explained in [Setting the grinding level correctly](/ratgeber/mahlgrad-richtig-einstellen/).

## Buy used

High-quality mills are durable, which is why buying used can be worthwhile. Before buying, check whether the axle rotates without play, whether the motor runs smoothly and whether the cutting edges of the grinder are still sharp. Rounded, shiny polished edges indicate heavy wear. Ask about the age and approximately how much coffee was ground and find out whether there are replacement grinders for the model. Thorough cleaning before your first coffee is mandatory, because you can taste old oils straight away.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should a good coffee grinder cost?

Prices range from under 50 euros to well over 1000 euros. For filter coffee you can get very good results in the lower three-digit range or with a good hand grinder. You should budget more for espresso, especially with electric grinders.

### How long do grinding discs last?

This depends on the material, amount of beans and degree of roasting. In households, steel grinders last for many years and often several hundred kilograms of coffee. If, despite cleaning, the grinder becomes slower, produces more dust or the coffee no longer tastes clean, it may be necessary to replace it.

### Is a ceramic grinder better than steel?

Not fundamentally. Ceramic is very hard and does not rust, but can break if exposed to foreign objects such as small stones. Steel is more break-resistant and can often be manufactured more precisely. Steel grinders dominate high-quality mills.

### Do I have to clean the grinder?

Yes, regularly. Old coffee oils and powder residues become rancid and distort the taste. How to do this can be found in the guide [Cleaning the coffee grinder] (/ratgeber/kaffeemuehle-reinigen/).

## Conclusion

Whether cone or disc is less important than precision, stability and care. Buy the grinder to suit your preparation, pay attention to fine adjustability, little dead space and available spare parts, and if in doubt, it is better to spend a little more on the grinder than on the machine.
