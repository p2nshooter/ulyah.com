Limescale is the silent enemy of every coffee machine and every kettle. In many regions of Germany, tap water is hard, and every time it is heated, some of the dissolved minerals precipitate out as a white deposit. It accumulates on the heating element, in pipes, valves and nozzles. The consequences come gradually: the water heats up more slowly, the machine gets louder, the coffee tastes flat and lukewarm, the power requirement increases, and in the worst case, a pipe or valve becomes clogged. Regular descaling prevents this. This guide explains which products are suitable for which device and how to proceed safely.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - How often you need to descale depends on the **water hardness** and usage, often every four to six weeks for hard water.
> - **Citric acid** and **Amidosulfonic acid** are common descalers, best used for machines in products recommended by the manufacturer.
> - **Vinegar** is suitable for kettles and simple appliances, but can corrode seals, has a strong smell and is not recommended for many espresso machines.
> - Do not use citric acid hot, otherwise poorly soluble calcium citrate can form.
> - After descaling, rinse thoroughly with clean water until no smell or taste remains.

## Where the lime comes from

Tap water contains dissolved calcium and magnesium salts. Some of it is present as hydrogen carbonate. When heated, it breaks down and calcium carbonate, i.e. lime, is deposited. The higher the **carbonate hardness**, the more lime is formed. How hard your water is and how hardness changes the taste is explained in [Water Hardness for Coffee and Tea](/ratgeber/wasserhaerte-fuer-kaffee-und-tee/). You can find out the hardness of your tap water from your local water supplier, often on their website.

## How often do you need to descale?

| Water hardness | kettle | Filter machine | Portafilter, fully automatic |
|---|---|---|---|
| soft | every 2 to 3 months | every 3 months | according to the manufacturer's information, often rare |
| medium | every 4 to 6 weeks | every 6 to 8 weeks | according to manufacturer's instructions |
| hard | every 2 to 4 weeks | every 4 to 6 weeks | According to the manufacturer's instructions, water filter is strongly recommended |

These are guidelines for a household with daily use. Fully automatic machines usually report when descaling needs to be done if the water hardness is set correctly in the menu. Visible signs include a white coating in the kettle, longer brewing times, louder noises and less hot coffee.

## The descalers in comparison

| Means | Advantages | Disadvantages | Suitable for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Citric acid | low odor, readily available, effective | Do not use hot, otherwise residues may form | Kettles, filter machines, many devices according to the manufacturer's specifications |
| Amidosulfonic acid | very effective, can also be used warm | Only dose according to instructions, not for all materials | many machine descalers contain them |
| lactic acid | mild, gentle on materials | a little slower | some brand descalers for espresso machines |
| Vinegar essence, household vinegar | cheap, available everywhere | strong smell, can attack rubber seals and metals | Kettles, simple devices if the manufacturer allows it |
| Manufacturer descaler | tailored to the device, guarantee remains valid | more expensive | Fully automatic machines, portafilters |

Most important principle: **Read the instructions.** Many manufacturers prescribe a specific agent or concentration. If you use anything else, you risk damage and sometimes the warranty.

## Descale the kettle

1. Fill the kettle about halfway with **cold** water.
2. Add citric acid, as a powder about two tablespoons per liter or a descaler according to the instructions on the package.
3. Leave the solution to work for half an hour to an hour **without boiling**. A little longer if the surface is very thick.
4. Pour away the solution, rinse thoroughly, fill with fresh water, boil it and pour it away too. If using vinegar, repeat the rinsing process until no smell remains.

Why not hot? When citric acid reacts with lime in hot water, calcium citrate can form, a poorly soluble deposit that is harder to remove than the lime itself.

### Lime sieve, base and outside

Many kettles have a fine limescale filter in the spout. It catches lime flakes, but calcifies itself and then lets less water through. When descaling, take it out, place it separately in a small bowl with descaling solution and then rinse it thoroughly. The base with the power connection must remain dry: never immerse the kettle in the sink and make sure that nothing gets onto the contacts when pouring the solution. You can remove limescale stains on a stainless steel exterior with a damp cloth and a little citric acid solution and then wipe dry.

## Descale the filter coffee machine

1. Remove the filter and coffee grounds and rinse the pot.
2. Fill the tank with descaling solution according to the instructions; for citric acid, dissolve it in cold water.
3. Run about half of the solution through, turn off the machine, and let it sit for 15 to 30 minutes.
4. Let the rest run through.
5. Then rinse two to three times with clean water by running a full tank.

More information about caring for the filter machine can be found in [Use the filter coffee machine correctly](/ratgeber/filterkaffeemaschine-richtig-nutzen/).

## Portafilter machine and fully automatic

These machines have narrow pipes, solenoid valves and boilers that are sensitive. Be sure to stick to the manufacturer's program and resources. Fully automatic machines usually have their own descaling program that guides you step by step. For portafilter machines with a large boiler, some manufacturers recommend not decalcifying yourself, but rather using filtered water and taking the machine in for service if necessary. More about care can be found in [Portafilter and espresso: the basics](/ratgeber/siebtraeger-espresso-grundlagen/).

## Capsule and pad machines

Capsule and pad machines heat the water in a small instantaneous water heater with narrow pipes, which quickly become calcified if the water is hard. Many devices have their own descaling mode, which is described in the operating instructions. The process is usually similar:

1. Empty the capsule container and drip tray, do not insert any capsule or pad.
2. Remove an inserted water filter from the tank. The instructions usually explicitly require this.
3. Mix the descaler with water in the tank according to the instructions on the package and place a container under the spout that holds at least as much as the tank.
4. Start the descaling mode and let it run through completely.
5. Rinse the tank thoroughly, fill it with fresh water and flush through at least a full tank.

If your device does not have its own mode, you can run the solution through the brewing button in several portions, with a few minutes of exposure time in between, unless the manufacturer specifies otherwise.

## Descale the espresso maker

If the water is hard, a white coating forms in the boiler of an aluminum jug. Fill the kettle up to the valve with cold citric acid solution or diluted vinegar, let it stand for a few hours, pour it away and rinse thoroughly several times. Then brew coffee once or twice and pour it away. Aluminum reacts sensitively to long exposure times to acid, so do not leave it overnight. More in [Use the espresso maker correctly](/ratgeber/espressokocher-anleitung/).

## Tar edges, lime veils and cups

Hard water often creates a thin, iridescent film on black tea, and brown edges form on cups and teapots. The film consists primarily of lime, which combines with tannins and other ingredients in the tea. It is harmless to health, but it looks unsightly and dampens the aroma. Softer or filtered water largely prevents it, and a splash of lemon in tea also dissolves it. You can remove stubborn edges in cups and jugs with a warm solution of citric acid or a little baking soda and a soft brush. With unglazed clay pots such as Yixing pots, however, you avoid using acid and detergent because the clay absorbs smell and taste.

## If the lime doesn't want to budge

If the coating is very thick, it helps to repeat the procedure instead of increasing the concentration significantly. Several mild passes are more gentle on seals and metal than one very strong one. With kettles, you can carefully remove loose deposits after soaking with a soft brush, but never scratch with steel wool or sharp objects, otherwise you will damage the surface and new limescale will stick all the better. If a machine remains slow or loud after descaling, a valve or pipe may be blocked. Then customer service is the safe choice.

## Common mistakes when descaling

| Error | Episode | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Cancel the descaling program in the middle | Acid residue remains in pipes and boilers | Let the program run completely and restart after an interruption |
| Leave the water filter in the tank | The filter is put under unnecessary strain and the descaler has a weaker effect | Remove the filter beforehand and only reinsert it after rinsing |
| Add powder undissolved to the tank | Granules can clog valves and nozzles | Dissolve the powder completely in a container beforehand |
| Too small a container under the spout | Solution overflows and attacks sensitive surfaces | a vessel that holds the entire contents of the tank |
| Water hardness not reset after moving | the descaling indicator comes on too early or too late | Ask the water supplier for the new hardness and enter it in the menu |

## Safety when descaling

- Descalers are acids. Keep them away from eyes and skin and out of the reach of children.
- **Never** mix descalers with cleaners that contain chlorine. This can produce toxic chlorine gas.
- With vinegar, ventilate well, the smell is strong.
- Do not drink coffee while there may still be descaler in the system. Better rinse again.
- For devices with a guarantee: only use approved products.

## Prevention is easier than decalcification

- **Water filter:** Table or cartridge filters significantly reduce carbonate hardness. Change the cartridges regularly, as old filters can become contaminated.
- **Fresh Water:** Do not let water sit in the tank or stove for days.
- **Only heat as much as you need:** Less heating means less limescale, and it saves electricity.
- **Empty the device after use:** It helps, especially with kettles, to pour out any remaining water.
- **Setting the hardness:** For fully automatic machines, set the water hardness in the menu so that the descaling display is correct.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is limescale in coffee harmful to health?

No. Lime consists of calcium and magnesium compounds, which are also found in drinking water. However, it impairs the taste and function of the devices.

### Can I use baking powder or baking soda to descale?

Barely. Baking soda and baking powder are basic or only slightly acidic and do not dissolve limescale well. Acids such as citric acid are significantly more effective.

### Can the descaling solution go down the drain?

In normal household quantities, yes, rinsed with plenty of clear water, unless the package states otherwise. However, do not pour it over work surfaces or sinks made of natural stone such as marble, as acid attacks the stone and leaves dull stains.

### Do I also have to descale the thermos flask?

If the water is hard, a deposit can also form there, along with coffee or tea residue. A solution of citric acid or a special cleaner for vacuum jugs helps. More about tea accessories can be found in [Tea Accessories](/ratgeber/teezubehoer/).
