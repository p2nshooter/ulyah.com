Anyone who wants to grind freshly will sooner or later be faced with the question: crank or press a button? Hand mills have gotten much better in recent years. Good models grind as evenly as much more expensive electric mills, fit in any drawer and work without electricity. Electric grinders are quick and convenient, especially if several cups are needed in the morning or espresso is on the agenda. Which mill is right for you depends less on the technology than on your everyday life. This comparison will help you decide.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - **Hand mills** often offer better grinding quality for their price, are quiet, compact and travel-friendly. They cost time and energy.
> - **Electric grinders** are quick and convenient, good for many cups a day and for espresso with frequent readjustments. Good models are larger and more expensive.
> - A good hand grinder is sufficient for **one to two cups of filter coffee a day**.
> - For **espresso every day** and for households with several coffee drinkers, an electric grinder is usually the more pleasant choice.
> - What is crucial in both cases is a proper **cone or disc grinder**, not a hammer knife.

## What they both have in common

A coffee grinder essentially consists of two grinding elements that grind the beans between them. The distance between them determines the degree of grinding. Whether you turn the inner cone by hand or a motor does it doesn't change the principle. How cone and disc grinders work in detail can be found in [Coffee grinder: cone or disc grinder](/ratgeber/kaffeemuehle-kegel-oder-scheibenmahlwerk/). This is about the practical side.

| criterion | Hand mill | Electric mill |

| Time for 15g of filter coffee | Roughly 30 to 60 seconds of cranking | a few seconds |
| Time for 18g of espresso | significantly longer, requires more effort | a few seconds |
| Volume | quietly | medium to loud depending on the model |
| Space requirements | small, fits in the drawer | needs fixed space on the work surface |

## For whom a hand mill is worthwhile

- **One to two cups a day:** Cranking takes about as long as it takes the water to boil. Many even find it a pleasant morning ritual.
- **Filter, AeroPress, French Press:** For medium and coarse grinds, grinding is easy by hand.
- **Small kitchen:** A hand mill disappears into the cupboard after grinding.
- **On the go:** In the office, in the holiday home or while camping, you can grind without a power outlet. Combined with a hand filter or an AeroPress, you can have good coffee anywhere. How to brew using the hand filter is described in [Hand filter: Brew coffee by hand](/ratgeber/handfilter-kaffee-anleitung/).
- **Budget:** If you have limited money available, you will get better grinding quality with a good hand mill than with an electric mill for the same price.

When buying, look for a solid housing, double bearings on the axle so that the cone does not wobble, and an easy-to-read, reproducible adjustment with clicks. A long crank arm makes grinding easier.

## For whom an electric mill is worthwhile

- **Espresso every day:** Espresso requires very fine flour, which requires a lot of effort and time by hand. You'll also need to adjust the grinder more often as you approach a new bean. An electric espresso grinder with a fine setting is almost indispensable here. More on this in [Portafilters and espresso: the basics](/ratgeber/siebtraeger-espresso-grundlagen/).
- **Multiple coffee drinkers:** A pot for four people means 50 to 60 grams of coffee. Nobody likes to grind it by hand in the morning.
- **Limited hand strength:** If you have joint problems or little strength in your hands, an electric grinder is a much more comfortable choice.
- **Convenience:** Put the beans in, press the button, done. Many models dose according to time or weight.

When buying a real cone or disc grinder, pay attention to a small **dead space**, i.e. little coffee that remains in the grinder after grinding, and how easy it is for you to reach the grinder for cleaning.

## Hand mill in everyday life: crank easier and more evenly

A good hand mill is only fun if you use it skillfully. These steps save energy and make the result repeatable:

1. **Find the zero point:** With the mill empty, carefully turn the setting until the crank can no longer turn freely or the grinding media grind slightly. From this zero point you count the clicks outwards. This way you can write down settings and find them again after cleaning. Do not continue turning with force when the grinding media are on top of each other.
2. **Set as empty as possible:** With beans between the grinding media, fine-tuning is particularly difficult and less precise.
3. **Support well:** Hold the mill vertically and press it against your upper body or support it on your thigh. This is how the arm works, not the wrist.
4. **Crank evenly:** A steady pace is more comfortable than jerky bursts. Light roasts require more power because the beans are denser and harder than dark ones.
5. **Finally, tap off:** A light tap on the housing loosens adhering flour so that it ends up in the collecting container.

## Electric mill in everyday life: dosing and changing

Even an electric grinder only delivers consistent results if you know a few peculiarities:

- **By time or weight:** Grinders with timers dispense different amounts depending on the bean, the degree of roasting and the filling level of the container. Therefore, check the quantity with the scale every now and then, especially after changing beans. Models that dose directly according to weight do this for you.
- **Grind briefly after adjusting:** Some flour from the previous setting remains in the grinder and in the ejector. After a big jump, say from espresso to filter, grind a few grams and discard them. For mills with little dead space, a small amount is sufficient.
- **Fine adjustments according to instructions:** Some manufacturers recommend adjusting the grinding level finer while the motor is running so that no bean pieces get stuck. Recommendations vary, so follow the instructions for your model.
- **Cable and location:** Place the grinder on a solid, dry surface, not directly next to the sink or kettle. Before touching the grinder, pull out the plug.

## Single dose or bean container?

Many electric grinders have a large bean container at the top. It's convenient, but beans left in there for days lose flavor. If you change the type of coffee frequently or only drink a small amount of coffee, it is better to weigh each portion and grind it completely. This procedure is called single dosing. Hand mills work like this automatically. When it comes to electric mills, models with little dead space are particularly suitable for this. [How to store coffee and tea correctly](/ratgeber/kaffee-und-tee-lagern/) explains how else to store beans.

## Volume and time

An electric grinder can seem very loud in a quiet apartment early in the morning, especially fast-turning models. Slowly rotating mills with large grinders are usually more pleasant. A hand mill only makes a quiet, grinding noise. If you get up before everyone else, that can be a real argument.

## Typical bad purchases and how to avoid them

Many disappointments arise not from bad mills, but from mills that are not suitable for use.

| Bad purchase | Why he disappoints | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Mill with impact knife | smashes beans unevenly, lots of dust next to coarse pieces | Cone or disc grinder |
| Hand mill with wobbling axle | Grinding media run unsteadily and the flour becomes uneven | double bearing axle |
| Filter grinder for espresso | Setting steps too coarse, the cover cannot be finely adjusted | Grinder with fine or continuous espresso setting |
| large device for one cup a day | Beans become stale in the container and space is lost | Hand mill or grinder for individual dosing |
| Purchase by number of grinding levels | Many levels mean little if they are roughly distributed in the important area | check how fine the steps are in the area of your preparation |

## Care is equally important for both

Regardless of whether you use it manually or with electricity: coffee oils and fine dust get stuck in the grinder and become rancid over time. Hand mills are usually easy to disassemble and clean with a brush. In electric mills, the grinder is often more difficult to access, but cleaning is part of routine there too. How to do this is described in [Cleaning the coffee grinder](/ratgeber/kaffeemuehle-reinigen/). Do not rinse grinders under water unless the manufacturer specifically allows it, as steel can rust and residual moisture makes the flour lumpy.

## How long do grinders last?

Good mills last for many years with normal care. Steel grinding media wear out slowly after hundreds of kilograms of coffee. With hand mills, it's more likely that the axle, bearings and crank get play after a long time. In electric grinders, the motor and electronics are the parts that can fail at some point. Manufacturers who offer spare parts such as grinders, brushes and seals significantly extend the service life. Ask about it when purchasing.

## Five questions to decide

1. How many cups a day? Up to two: hand mill is a real option. More: more electric.
2. Espresso or filter? Espresso daily: electric. Filter: both possible.
3. Where is the milling done? At home, on the go or both?
4. How important are volume and space?
5. Which budget? Invest in the grinder rather than the machine. A good grinder improves every coffee, an expensive machine with a bad grinder does not.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do hand mills grind better than electric ones?

Not fundamentally. A very good electric mill grinds at least as evenly as a very good hand mill. In the lower and middle price range, you often get more grinding quality for your money with hand mills.

### Can I grind espresso with a hand grinder?

Yes, many good hand mills can produce a fine espresso grind. But it takes longer and requires energy. This is easy to do for the occasional espresso, but it is exhausting in the long run if you drink it several times a day.

### Is a hand mill worth using as a second mill?

Often yes. If you don't want to constantly switch between espresso and filter grinding levels on the electric espresso grinder, you can save yourself the trouble of changing, grinding and resetting with a hand grinder for filters. You also have a grinder for travel and in case the electric one fails.

### Does an electric grinder heat coffee?

With small quantities and good mills, the heating is minimal and hardly relevant to the taste. Small mills that rotate very quickly can get a little warm when working with large quantities.
