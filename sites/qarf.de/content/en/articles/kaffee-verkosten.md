If you drink a lot of coffee, at some point you will only taste “coffee”. There is an amazing variety in every cup: notes of berries, citrus, chocolate, nuts, caramel, flowers or spices, an acidity that can be fresh or soft, a body that ranges from tea-like to syrupy and an aftertaste that disappears briefly or lingers for a long time. Professionals at roasting companies and importers taste coffee according to a fixed process, the **Cupping**. You don't need any special training for this. With a few cups, a spoon, and a little attention, you'll learn to taste and put differences into words. Not only is this fun, it also helps you find coffees that you really enjoy.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - During **Cupping**, coarsely ground coffee is poured directly into the cup with hot water, after four minutes the crust is broken and tasted with a spoon.
> - The evaluations include **fragrance**, **aroma**, **acidity**, **sweetness**, **body**, **taste**, **finish** and **balance**.
> - You learn the most through **comparisons**: two or three coffees next to each other.
> - Coffee shows itself most clearly when **cooling**. Try several times as it gets warmer and cooler.
> - The **Aroma Wheel** helps label impressions from general to specific.

## Why cupping and not just drinking?

When cupping, all coffees are prepared in exactly the same way: same amount, same grind, same water, same time. Differences in the cup only depend on the coffee, not on how it is prepared. Roasteries use this to select green coffees, test roasts and develop blends. It's the easiest way for you to fairly compare several coffees.

## What you need

- two to four different coffees, ideally freshly roasted
- a mill and a scale
- One to two equal cups or glasses with around 200 milliliters per coffee
- a kettle, good water
- One soup spoon per person, preferably a round cupping spoon
- a glass of water to rinse the spoons, a cup to spit out if you try a lot of coffees
- Pen and paper or the notepad below

## Cupping step by step

1. **Grinding:** Grind approximately **12 grams** of coffee per cup **medium coarse**, slightly coarser than for the hand filter. Between different coffees, rinse the grinder with a few grams of the next coffee.
2. **Dry scent:** Smell each cup with the dry coffee grounds. Write down first impressions.
3. **Pour:** Pour about **200 milliliters** of water onto the flour shortly after boiling, around 93 to 96 degrees. Make sure everything is wetted. Start a clock.
4. **Wait:** Let the coffee stand for **four minutes**. A crust of coffee grounds forms on the surface.
5. **Break the crust:** Hold your nose close to the cup and gently push the crust back with the spoon three to four times. An intense scent arises, the **aroma** when wet.
6. **Skimming:** Using two spoons, remove the remaining foam and particles on the surface.
7. **Try:** When the coffee has cooled down a bit, take a spoon and **sip** it vigorously. Sipping distributes the coffee as a fine mist in the mouth and brings aromas to the nose. It sounds loud, but it's part of it.
8. **Repeat:** Taste each cup several times as it cools. Rinse the spoon between cups.

## What you pay attention to

| feature | Question | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Fragrance and aroma | What does the coffee smell like dry and wet? | Chocolate, flowers, berries, nuts, spices |
| Acid | How alive is he? What type of acid? | lemony, appley, berry, vinous, soft or pointed |
| Sweetie | How sweet does it seem without sugar? | Caramel, honey, brown sugar, ripe fruit |
| body | How does it feel in your mouth? | light like tea, round like milk, heavy like syrup |
| taste | What flavors do you taste? | as specific as possible |
| Exit | What remains after swallowing and for how long? | sweet and long, short, dry, bitter |
| Balance | Does everything fit together or does something stand out? | harmonious, acidic, flat |

Acidity is not a fault in coffee. A clean, fruity acidity makes it lively. An acid is only unpleasant if it appears sharp, vinegar-like or immature. [Understanding Extraction](/ratgeber/extraktion-verstehen/) explains why coffee can taste sour or bitter.

## The aroma wheel

The Specialty Coffee Association, together with World Coffee Research, has developed a **flavor wheel** for coffee, the “Coffee Taster's Flavor Wheel.” On the inside there are general categories such as fruity, floral, sweet, nutty-cocoa-like, spicy and roasted, on the outside the terms become more and more precise: from “fruity” to “berries” to “raspberry”. When tasting, you go from the inside out. First you ask yourself: Is it more fruity or more nutty? Then: Which fruit? If you're not sure, stay general. “Red fruit” is a good description, even if you can’t tell whether it’s a cherry or a currant.

## Train your sense of taste

- **Smell consciously:** Smell fruit, spices, nuts and chocolate in everyday life and remember the impressions. The more smells you know, the easier it is for you to recognize them in coffee.
- **Compare instead of trying individually:** Differences are much easier to notice next to each other.
- **Choose opposites:** Start with coffees that are significantly different, such as a washed coffee from Kenya and a natural from Brazil. How origin and processing shape the taste can be found in [coffee-growing countries and their taste](/ratgeber/kaffee-anbaulaender/) and [processing: washed, natural, honey](/ratgeber/aufbereitung-gewaschen-natural-honey/).
- **Taste with others:** Other people perceive different things. Talking about impressions sharpens your senses.
- **Start neutral:** Do not taste directly after brushing your teeth, after spicy food or with perfume.

## Ideas for tastings at home

| topic | What you are comparing | What you learn |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Ethiopia, Colombia, Sumatra | regional profiles |
| Processing | the same coffee washed and natural | Influence of processing |
| degree of roasting | same bean light, medium, dark | Effect of roasting, see [roasting levels explained](/ratgeber/roestgrade-erklaert/) |
| Freshness | freshly roasted and aged four weeks | how coffee ages |
| water | the same coffee with tap and filter water | Influence of water |
| Decaf | regular and decaffeinated | whether you can taste the difference, see [Decaffeinated Coffee](/ratgeber/entkoffeinierter-kaffee/) |

## A simple note sheet

For each coffee you write down:

- Name, origin, preparation, roasting date
- Fragrance dry, aroma wet
- Acidity: little, medium, high, and what type
- Sweetness: little, medium, a lot
- Body: light, medium, heavy
- Taste: three concepts
- Finish: short, medium, long, pleasant or not
- Overall impression and whether you would buy it again

After a few tastings, you'll see which profiles you like and can shop more specifically.

## A tasting with friends

Cupping is most fun when there are several people, and the impressions are more honest when no one knows which coffee is in which cup. This is how a small blind tasting works:

1. **One person prepares:** She only marks the bottom of the cups with letters and notes which coffee goes where.
2. **Three or four coffees** are enough for an evening. More tires the sense of taste.
3. **Water and neutral bread** are available to neutralize your mouth every now and then.
4. **Note first, talk later:** Each person writes down their impressions before they are discussed. This way no one influences anyone else.
5. **Finally, break it down** and compare who noticed what.

An attractive variant is the **triangle test**, which professionals also use for training: out of three cups, two contain the same coffee, one a different one. The task is to find the different cup. That sounds easy, but with similar coffees it's surprisingly difficult.

## Typical mistakes when tasting

- **Try too hot:** Very hot coffee masks many flavors. The most revealing impressions arise when it has cooled down a little.
- **Too many coffees at once:** After the fourth or fifth, concentration and perception decrease significantly.
- **Unequal conditions:** Different quantities, degrees of grinding or water temperatures distort the comparison.
- **Disturbing smells:** Scented candles, perfume or food smells in the room are distracting.
- **Only rate the first sip:** Many coffees change significantly as they cool, often for the better.
- **Do not rinse the spoon:** Remains of the previous coffee will distort the impression of the next cup. A glass of warm water next to the cups solves the problem.

## From cupping to your own cup

Once you've found a coffee that you like when cupping, next prepare it using your usual method. The cupping cup most closely resembles a [French Press](/ratgeber/french-press-anleitung/) coffee: oils and fine particles remain in the cup. The same coffee usually appears clearer and lighter in a hand filter. If the result is not correct, it is often due to the grinding degree, as [Set the grinding degree correctly] (/ratgeber/mahlgrad-richtig-einstellen/) shows.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I really have to sip?

It helps a lot. When you sip, air enters your mouth along with the coffee, aromas rise into the nasopharynx, and you perceive more. You can just try it normally at home.

### Can I also taste tea like this?

Yes. Tea tasters work according to a similar principle with fixed quantities and steeping times. You can find the basics for tea in [Tea: Brewing Times and Temperatures](/ratgeber/tee-ziehzeiten-und-temperaturen/).

### Why can't I taste the notes on the pack?

The information is information, not ingredients. Coffee doesn't taste like raspberry because there is raspberry in it, but because similar flavorings are present. It takes practice to recognize them, and every person perceives them slightly differently.

### How often should I taste to get better?

Regularity is more important than duration. A small comparative tasting with two coffees per week brings more than one long evening per month. After just a few weeks you will notice differences that you would not have noticed before.

## Conclusion

Cupping is not a ritual for professionals, but the simplest way to consciously experience coffee: the same preparation, attentive smelling and sipping, side-by-side comparisons and a few notes. Anyone who tastes this way will gradually discover which origins, preparations and roastings they really like.
