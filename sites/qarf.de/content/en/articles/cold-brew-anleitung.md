On hot summer days, cold brew is the best way to drink coffee: cool, smooth, with a natural sweetness and hardly any bitterness. Unlike iced coffee, cold brew is not brewed hot and then cooled, but rather the coffee steeps in cold water for many hours. Less acids and bitter substances are released, but a lot of chocolate, nutty and sweet things are released. Preparation is easy and requires hardly any equipment, just time. If you start in the evening, you will have a concentrate the next morning that will last for several days.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - For ready-to-drink cold brew, around 60 to 80 grams of coffee per liter of water, for a concentrate 100 to 150 grams per liter.
> - Grind coarsely, similar to the French press.
> - Let it steep for 12 to 24 hours, preferably in the refrigerator.
> - Filter through a sieve and a paper filter or cloth.
> - Stores refrigerated in a clean, sealed container for about a week. Dilute concentrate with water, ice or milk before drinking.

## What makes cold brew different

Hot water quickly dissolves aromatics, acids and bitter substances. Cold water dissolves more slowly and selectively. Many acids and some bitter compounds hardly dissolve at low temperatures, while sweet and chocolate notes are well extracted. That's why cold brew tastes smoother and rounder than hot brewed coffee, but also less lively and less fruity. If you love the bright, floral acidity of an Ethiopian coffee, you will miss some of it in cold brew.

The long steeping time compensates for the lower solubility. Cold brew is therefore not automatically weaker. Prepared as a concentrate, it often contains more caffeine per milliliter than normal filter coffee. How much caffeine is in which drink is explained in [Caffeine in Coffee and Tea](/ratgeber/koffein-in-kaffee-und-tee/).

## What you need

- a large glass or carafe, about a liter
- whole coffee beans and a grinder that can grind coarsely
- cold, good water
- a fine sieve and a paper filter, a cotton cloth or a nut milk bag
- optional: a stamp jug that makes filtering particularly easy, see [French Press](/ratgeber/french-press-anleitung/)

There are also special cold brew carafes with a built-in fine filter. They are practical, but not necessary.

## Which coffee is suitable?

Basically everyone. Coffees with chocolate, nut and caramel notes, such as from Brazil, Colombia or Guatemala, work particularly well in a medium roast. Fruity naturals from Ethiopia produce a cold brew with a berry aroma. Very dark roasts can quickly appear smoky and flat in cold brew. Cold brew is also a good way to use up beans that are already a few weeks old.

## Ratio and grind size

| Variant | coffee per liter of water | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Ready to drink | 60 to 80g | pure with ice |
| concentrate | 100 to 150g | Dilute 1 to 1 to 1 to 2 with water or milk |

Grind **coarsely**, similar to coarse sea salt. If the flour is too fine, filtering becomes laborious, the coffee becomes cloudy and can become bitter. How to find the grinding level on your grinder can be found in [Setting the grinding level correctly](/ratgeber/mahlgrad-richtig-einstellen/).

## Quantities and yield: what ends up in the bottle

Ground coffee absorbs itself and retains some of the water when filtered, roughly twice its own weight. This is significantly more important when it comes to concentrate. For example, if you mix 125 grams of coffee with one liter of water, around 250 milliliters remain in the grounds and you get around 750 milliliters of concentrate. Diluted in a ratio of 1 to 1, this yields approximately one and a half liters of ready-to-drink coffee.

| approach | Coffee | water | approximately after filtering |
|---|---|---|---|
| ready to drink, small | 35 to 40g | 500ml | a good 400 ml |
| ready to drink | 70 to 80g | 1 liter | around 850 ml |
| concentrate | 125g | 1 liter | around 750 ml, diluted about 1.5 l |
| Concentrate, big | 250g | 2 litres | around 1.5 l, diluted about 3 l |

If you need a certain amount, for example for guests, plan for this loss. Squeezing the grounds vigorously in the cloth gets a little more out of it, but makes the coffee cloudier.

## Step by step

1. **Grinding:** For example, weigh 80 grams of beans for one liter of ready-to-drink cold brew and grind them coarsely.
2. **Preparation:** Put the ground coffee in the container and slowly pour a liter of cold water over it.
3. **Stir:** Stir thoroughly once so that all of the flour is wetted and no dry lumps remain.
4. **Let it steep:** Cover the container and place it in the refrigerator for 12 to 24 hours. 16 hours is a good starting point.
5. **Filter:** First pour the coffee through a coarse sieve to remove most of the grounds, then through a paper filter or cloth. With the stamp pot, you slowly press down the stamp and then filter it finely again.
6. **Botting:** Pour the cold brew into a clean bottle and place it in the refrigerator.

## Adjust brewing time

If your cold brew tastes thin, watery, or a little green, extend the brewing time or use more coffee. If it tastes bitter, woody or dry, shorten the time or grind more coarsely. Longer than 24 hours usually doesn't help and makes the coffee rather woody.

The extraction is faster at room temperature; 10 to 14 hours is often enough. However, for hygiene reasons, we recommend making cold brew in the refrigerator, especially in summer.

## Durability and hygiene

Cold brew is not heated. To keep it safe, pay attention to cleanliness: Rinse the container, sieve and bottles with hot water before you start, use clean water and always store the finished coffee refrigerated. It lasts like this for about a week. If it smells sour, fermented or unusual, throw it away. Add milk when serving, not in stock.

## Serving and variations

- **Pure on ice:** A glass with ice cubes, cold brew on top, done.
- **With milk:** Concentrate 1 to 1 with cold milk or oat drink, a touch of vanilla.
- **Cold Brew Tonic:** A glass with ice, tonic water, a slice of lemon or orange and slowly pour cold brew over it. Refreshingly bitter-sweet.
- **Coffee Ice Cubes:** Freeze cold brew in ice cube trays. This way the iced coffee doesn't get watered down.
- **In dessert:** Cold brew concentrate is suitable for tiramisu, coffee cream or ice cream.

## Cold brew with spices and flavors

Because cold brew steeps for so long, it absorbs flavors from additives well. A cinnamon stick or a few crushed cardamom pods, a cut vanilla pod, a tablespoon of cocoa nibs or a strip of peel from an untreated orange per liter have proven useful. Simply add the additives to the container with the ground coffee and filter them out. Fresh fruit, on the other hand, should not be considered because it can ferment for many hours.

Cold coffee with roasted chicory is common in New Orleans. The root brings tart, malty notes and is traditionally combined with milk and sugar. For a first try, replace about a fifth of the ground coffee with ground chicory and work your way up to your taste from there.

## Japanese Iced Coffee as an alternative

If you want to enjoy the fruity acidity of a light coffee cold, try the Japanese method: You brew hot coffee directly over ice. For one cup you use around 20 grams of coffee, 180 grams of hot water for brewing and 120 grams of ice in the pot. The coffee is immediately cooled as it passes through and retains its aromas. You know the basic technique from [Hand filter: Brew coffee by hand](/ratgeber/handfilter-kaffee-anleitung/). Grind a little finer than for regular filter coffee because less hot water runs through the bed.

## Kyoto-Style: Cold Brew from the drip tower

In some cafes there are tall glass towers in which cold water runs drop by drop through a bed of coffee. This method is called Kyoto style or slow drip. Unlike classic cold brew, the ground coffee does not sit in the water, but is slowly dripped in over several hours. The result is usually clearer and lighter in body, often with finer flavors. There are smaller drip towers for use at home. You'll need some practice to get the dripping speed right, and more space than a jar in the fridge. The simple method is sufficient for everyday use, the drip tower is more for the curious.

## Cold brew, iced coffee and iced latte in comparison

In the café you will encounter several cold coffee drinks that are often confused. The main difference is whether and how hot the coffee is brewed.

| drink | Preparation | taste |
|---|---|---|
| Cold brew | coarsely ground, cold-drawn for many hours | soft, chocolaty, little acid |
| Japanese Iced Coffee | brewed hot directly on ice | clear, fruity, lively acidity |
| Iced latte | Espresso over ice and cold milk | creamy, strong, coffee-focused |
| German-style iced coffee | cooled coffee with vanilla ice cream and cream | sweet, dessert-like |

No variant is better than the other. Cold brew is suitable for preparation, Japanese iced coffee for fruity beans, the iced latte for everyone who is already making espresso.

## Typical errors and their solutions

| problem | Probable cause | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| thin, watery | too little coffee or brewed too briefly | more coffee, extend brewing time to 18 to 20 hours |
| bitter, woody | ground too finely or steeped for too long | Grind more coarsely, filter after 14 to 16 hours |
| muddy sediment | Fines from the mill | filter twice, use paper filter |
| bland, little aroma | very old or very dark beans | fresher beans in medium roast |
| sour smell after days | Storage too warm or container not clean | Rinse containers with hot water and always store in a cool place |

## Frequently asked questions

### Is cold brew easier on your stomach?

Many people find cold brew to be milder because it tastes less acidic. Whether it is better tolerated by the individual stomach depends on many factors. If you are sensitive, it is best to try it yourself, in small quantities.

### Can I heat up cold brew?

Yes, you can dilute a concentrate with hot water. It then tastes soft and chocolaty, but different than freshly brewed coffee.

### Why is my cold brew cloudy?

Fine coffee particles have gotten through the sieve. Filter through paper a second time and grind coarser next time.

### Can I prepare the coffee grounds a second time?

We advise against this. Most of the soluble substances are dissolved after the first batch; a second batch tastes thin and woody. The sentence is better kept in compost or organic waste.

### How much cold brew should I drink?

Keep an eye on the amount of caffeine, especially with concentrate. For healthy adults, up to 400 milligrams of caffeine per day is considered safe; pregnant women should consume a maximum of 200 milligrams.
