This table collects the starting values ​​that we work with in our guides. It is intended for printing and hanging. Important: These are **starting points**, not laws. If your coffee tastes sour and thin, grind finer or brew longer; If it tastes bitter and dry, grind it coarser or shorten the time. When it comes to tea, the following applies: water that is too hot and steeping time that is too long makes green tea in particular bitter.

> **How to read the table**
> - “g/l” means grams of coffee or tea per liter of water. 60 g/l corresponds to a ratio of approximately 1:16.7.
> - A kitchen scale with 0.1g increments is the best investment under 20 euros for coffee.
> - Temperatures apply to the water when pouring. How quickly boiling water cools depends heavily on the quantity and container. If you don't have a kettle with a temperature selector, a simple kitchen thermometer will help.

## Coffee by method

| Method | quantity | Grinding degree | water | time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hand filter (V60, Kalita, Melitta) | 55-65g/l, start 60g/l | medium fine | 92-96°C | 2½–4 minutes total |
| Filter machine | 55-65g/L | medium | 92–96 °C (device dependent) | 4-6 min brew cycle |
| French press | 60-70g/l | roughly | 93-96°C | Pull for 4 minutes |
| AeroPress | 12-18g per 200-250ml | fine to medium | 80-95°C | 1-2 mins |
| Espresso maker | Sieve loosely full, approx. 1:7 to 1:10 | medium fine, coarser than espresso | preheated, up to below the valve | 3-5 minutes on medium heat |
| Espresso (portafilter) | 16-20 g in a double sieve | fine | 90-96°C | 25-30 s, approx. 1:2 in the cup |
| Mocha (cezve) | approx. 7 g per 60-70 ml | powder fine | start cold | until just before cooking |
| Cold brew (concentrate) | 100-125g/L | roughly | cold | 12-18 hours in the fridge |
| Cold Brew (ready to drink) | 60-80g/L | roughly | cold | 12-18 hours in the fridge |

## Basic espresso recipe in detail

| Value | Single | Doppio |
|---|---|---|
| ground coffee | 7-10g | 16-20g |
| Drink in the cup | 20-30g | 32-45g |
| Lead time | 25–30 s | 25–30 s |
| Brewing pressure | about 9 bar | about 9 bar |

If the espresso runs faster than 20 seconds, grind it finer. If it takes well over 35 seconds, sometimes more coarse. Only change one value at a time.

## Tea by variety

| Type of tea | quantity | water | Brewing time | Infusions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Japanese green tea (Sencha) | 10-12g/L | 70-80°C | 1-2 mins | 2–3 |
| Gyokuro | approx. 5 g per 100-150 ml | 50-60°C | 1½–2½ mins. | 2–3 |
| Chinese green tea (Long Jing) | 8-10g/l | 75-85°C | 2-3 mins | 2–3 |
| Matcha (Usucha) | 1.5-2g per 60-80ml | 70-80°C | hit up | 1 |
| White tea (Bai Mu Dan) | 8-12g/l | 75-90°C | 3-5 mins | 2–3 |
| Oolong, light and rolled | 10-12g/L | 85-90°C | 2-4 mins | 3–5 |
| Oolong, dark roasted | 10-12g/L | 90-95°C | 2-4 mins | 3–5 |
| Darjeeling First Flush | 10-12g/L | 85-95°C | 2-3 mins | 1–2 |
| Black tea (Assam, Ceylon) | 12-15g/L | 95-100°C | 3-5 mins | 1 |
| Pu-erh | 10-15g/l | 95-100°C | 2-4 minutes, rinse briefly beforehand | several |
| Rooibos | 12-15g/L | 100°C | 5-8 mins | 1–2 |
| Herbal and fruit tea | 10-15g/l | 100 °C, bubbling | at least 5 minutes, covered | 1 |

With Gongfu Cha with a small pot or gaiwan, you use significantly more leaves, around 5 to 7 grams per 100 to 120 milliliters, and infuse very briefly: 10 to 30 seconds, a little longer with each infusion.

## Water at a glance

| Value | Recommendation for coffee | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Total hardness | SCA target value around 4 °dH (68 mg/l CaCO₃), around 3–8 °dH is good | in Germany it is often much harder |
| Carbonate hardness | SCA target value around 2 °dH (40 mg/l CaCO₃), around 2–5 °dH is good | buffers acid, causes limescale |
| pH value | around 7 | slightly acidic or basic is not critical |
| Odor, chlorine | none | Draw fresh water and let it run briefly |

1 °dH corresponds to 10 mg calcium oxide per liter or 0.179 mmol of hardness formers. In Germany, water with less than 1.5 mmol/l (around 8.4 °dH) is considered soft, up to 2.5 mmol/l (around 14 °dH) is considered medium and above that it is considered hard. You can find out the value for your place of residence from your water supplier.
