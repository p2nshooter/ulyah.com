Photographing a piece of jewelry seems simple until you try. Metal reflects everything around it, including the phone and the person shooting; diamonds appear gray or with black spots; Color gems change tone depending on the light; and the piece, so small, is never fully focused. However, a good photo is useful for many reasons: for an insurance inventory, to sell a piece, to share a mineral collection or simply to remember a family jewel.

In this guide you will see what equipment is needed, how to prepare the light and background, how to focus on such small objects, how to get a faithful color, what photos to take for insurance or for a sale, how to record a video and how to edit without cheating.

## First, honesty

Before talking about technique, it is worth setting a rule: **a photo of a piece of jewelry must show the piece as it is**. Editing lighting and framing is normal; changing the color of a gem, deleting inclusions or inventing sparkles is not. If the photo is used to sell, a misleading image can lead to a return, a claim, or worse. And if the photo is used for insurance, it needs to be faithful to serve as proof.

## The team: less than you think

- **A recent phone** is enough for most cases. If it has a macro mode or a close-up lens, the better.
- **A camera with a macro lens** gives more control and quality for very small pieces or collections.
- **A tripod** or a phone holder, to avoid movement.
- **A soft box** or a white fabric photography tent, which diffuses the light on all sides.
- **Two continuous lights** of neutral tone, similar to daylight.
- **Black and white cardboard**, which act as reflectors.
- **Removable adhesive putty**, to hold rings and earrings in the correct position.
- **Tweezers, microfiber cloth and cotton gloves**, to avoid leaving fingerprints.

## Prepare the piece

The cameras see everything: dust, fingerprints, cream residue, lint. Before photographing:

1. **Clean the jewelry** according to its material. The [jewelry maintenance and cleaning](/es/mantenimiento-y-limpieza-de-joyas/) guide explains how to do it without damaging it.
2. **Dry it well** and remove lint with a soft brush.
3. **Handle it with gloves or tongs** from that moment on.

## Light: the secret of everything

### Soft and diffuse light

Jewelry is best photographed in **soft light**, which surrounds the piece from several sides. Direct, harsh light, like a phone flash or midday sun, creates blown highlights and harsh shadows.

- **With natural light:** next to a window with indirect light, on a cloudy day or with a white curtain that diffuses the sun.
- **With artificial light:** two lights on either side of a softbox or behind a diffuser.

### Avoid the flash

The phone's flash flattens the piece, burns out reflections from the metal and alters the color of the gems. Turn it off.

### Black and white reflectors

- A **white cardboard** near the piece lightens the shadows and gives an even shine to the metal.
- A **black card** placed in the right place gives **contrast** to the diamonds and colorless gems, which otherwise look gray and flat. It's the trick many jewelry photographers use to make the diamonds "shine" in the photo without editing them.

### color temperature

Warm lights turn everything yellow, and cold lights turn everything blue. Use lights of the same type and adjust the **white balance** of your camera or phone so that the white background looks really white. A neutral **gray card** in the first photo helps correct the color later.

## The background

| Background | When to use it |
|---|---|
| White | catalogs, online sales, inventory; the most neutral |
| light gray | colored gems and light metals; reduces reflections |
| Black | diamonds, shiny gems and silver pieces for contrast |
| Natural textures (stone, wood, fabric) | ambient photos; not for inventories |

Avoid intensely colored backgrounds: they color the reflections of metal and gems.

## The focus

The jewels are small, and the closer you get, the less area is in focus. Some tips:

- **Don't use digital zoom** - get physically closer or use macro mode.
- **Touch the screen** on the gem or main part to set the focus and exposure.
- **Use the timer** or a remote shutter release so you don't move your phone when shooting.
- **Close the diaphragm** if you use a camera, to increase the area in focus.
- **Stack focuses** if you need the entire piece sharp: Some cameras and apps combine multiple photos focused on different points.

## What photos to take

### For inventory or insurance

For each piece:

1. a **general view** from the front;
2. a **side view** showing the setting and height of the stone;
3. a **rear view** or inside the ring;
4. a **close-up of the metal and maker's markings**;
5. a photo **with a ruler** next to it, for scale;
6. a photo of the **lab report** or appraisal next to the piece, if you have them.

Save photos with a clear name and a copy outside the home. The guide on [jewelry insurance and appraisal](/es/seguro-y-tasacion-de-joyas/) explains how to complete the documentation, and the guide on [storing and traveling with jewelry](/es/guardar-y-viajar-con-joyas/) explains how to protect it.

### to sell

In addition to the above:

- photos **in natural light**, which show the most faithful color;
- a photo **in hand or worn**, to give an idea of ​​the size;
- a **video** rotating the piece;
- close-ups of any **defects or wear**: showing them builds trust and prevents claims.

If you are going to buy, these same photos are the ones you should ask the seller for, as we explain in the guide on [buying jewelry online](/es/comprar-joyas-en-linea/).

### For a gem or mineral collection

- photograph each piece with its **label** and a **scale**;
- for minerals, shows the **crystal faces** and **matrix**;
- For cut gems, take one photo **across the table** and another **in profile**.

The guide on [collecting gems and minerals](/es/coleccionar-gemas-y-minerales/) explains how to catalog them.

## special gems

- **Color Change Gems:** Take at least two photos, one in daylight and one in warm light, and indicate which is which. The guide on [color changing gems](/es/gemas-que-cambian-de-color/) explains the phenomenon.
- **Stars and Cat Eyes:** Use **a single point light**; with various lights several stars or bands appear. We tell it in the guide on [optical phenomena in gems](/es/fenomenos-opticos-en-gemas/).
- **Opals and stones with color play:** a video captures the effect much better than a photo.
- **Pearls:** a gray background and very soft light show their orient without burning the shine.

## The video

A short video, 10 to 20 seconds, says more than ten photos:

- place the piece on a **turntable** or turn it slowly with a gloved hand;
- use **continuous light**, never flash;
- Set focus and exposure before you start;
- Record at least one video in **natural light**.

## The edition: what is allowed

| Adjustment | Is it honest? |
|---|---|
| Crop and straighten | yes |
| Correct white balance with neutral reference | yes |
| Slightly adjust exposure and contrast | yes, without exaggeration |
| Remove dust from the background | yes |
| Change the color or saturation of the gem | no |
| Erase inclusions, scratches or defects from the part | no |
| Add sparkles or glitter | no |
| Use filters that alter colors | no, in sales or inventory photos |

If a photo is heavily edited for aesthetic reasons, for example in a mood publication, it should be accompanied by other unedited photos.

## Frequent errors

- **The photographer's reflection** in the metal: surround the piece with white cardboard or use a light box.
- **Gray or "dirty" diamonds:** lack of contrast; Try black cardboard on the sides.
- **False Colors:** Mixed lights or confused auto white balance.
- **Shaky photos:** use a tripod and timer.
- **Only part in focus:** Get closer and crop later, or stack focuses.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a professional camera?

No. A recent phone, good diffused light, a tripod and some cardboard give excellent results for inventory, sales and social networks.

### Why does my gem look a different color in the photo?

Because the camera interprets light differently than the eye, and because many gems change tone depending on the light source. Adjust the white balance to a neutral reference and photograph in natural light for comparison.

### Do my photos serve as proof for insurance?

They help a lot, especially if they are accompanied by invoices, reports and appraisals. Check with your insurer what documentation is required.

## In summary

Photographing a jewel well is a matter of preparation rather than equipment: cleaning the piece, using soft, diffuse lighting without a flash, choosing a neutral background, using white cardboard for the metal and black for the contrast of the diamonds, adjusting the white balance and focusing with a tripod and timer. For insurance, it is advisable to photograph each piece from various angles, with its marks and a scale; to sell, add photos in natural light, the piece in place, a video and visible defects. And in editing, the rule is simple: correct the lighting and framing yes, never change the reality of the piece.
