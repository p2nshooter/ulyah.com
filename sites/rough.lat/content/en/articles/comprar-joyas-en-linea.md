Buying jewelry online is now an everyday thing. There are jewelry stores with online stores, workshops that sell through social networks, e-commerce platforms, digital auctions and sellers of raw gems that ship to any country. The advantages are obvious: more variety, comparable prices and access to pieces that do not exist in the neighborhood store. But there are also risks: photos that do not show reality, misleading descriptions, false certificates, shipments that do not arrive and impossible returns.

In this guide you will see how to evaluate a seller, what to look for in photos and descriptions, how to verify a certificate, what payment methods protect you, what to ask about shipping and returns, and what to do when the piece reaches your hands.

## First of all: the seller

An online gem is only as trustworthy as the person who sells it. Before looking at the price, look at the seller:

- **Clear identity:** business name, company name, registration or tax identification number and a verifiable physical address.
- **Real contact:** a phone or email that responds, not just a form.
- **Seniority and history:** how long you have been selling and if you have a coherent history.
- **Opinions outside your own website:** on independent platforms, forums or networks. Reviews from your own page may be curated.
- **Written conditions:** return policy, warranty, shipping and privacy, accessible before purchasing.
- **Concrete answers:** A serious seller answers with facts, not with evasion, when you ask about the metal, the gem or the treatments.

If any of these parts are missing, it is advisable to be much more careful or look for another seller.

## The words of the description

Online jewelry descriptions are full of expressions that sound good and say little. Read every word:

| Expression | What it can mean |
|---|---|
| "Golden", "gold tone", "gold color" | It is not gold; It is a metal with gold plating or color. |
| "Silver" | It is not silver; Is it a bath or a color? |
| "Dipped", "veneered", "laminated" | a layer of gold on another metal; its thickness varies greatly |
| "18k gold" without mark or weight | Ask for the exact grade, the weight in grams and the brand of the metal |
| "simulated diamond", "diamond type" | it's not diamond |
| "Laboratory created", "cultured" | synthetic gem: real in composition, but laboratory |
| "Natural" without further ado | Ask if it is natural untreated or natural treated |
| "Style", "inspired by", "type" | It is not the original brand or design |
| "AAA quality" | commercial label without a common standard |

To understand the laws of the metal, consult the guide on [gold, silver and platinum](/es/oro-plata-y-platino/). For laboratory gems, [synthetic and laboratory gems](/es/gemas-sinteticas-y-de-laboratorio/), and for treatments, [gem treatments](/es/tratamientos-de-gemas/).

### What a good description should include

- **metal, grade and weight** in grams;
- **measurements** of the piece and the stones;
- **species of the gem**, whether it is natural or laboratory-grown, and its **treatments**;
- **carat weight** of each stone or the total;
- **quality** according to a recognized system where applicable;
- **lab report number** if you have one;
- **ring size** and if it can be adjusted.

## The photos and videos

Online images can be deceiving without lying: good lighting, a dark background and a little editing transform any stone.

- **Ask for real photos** of the specific piece, not catalog images.
- **Ask for a video** in indirect natural light, rotating the piece. A video is much more difficult to retouch than a photo.
- **Look for a size reference**: a ruler, a coin or the piece in your hand.
- **Be wary of impossible colors** and images with extreme brightness at all points.
- **Do a reverse image search**: If it appears in many different stores, it is probably a generic photo.

The guide on [how to photograph jewelry and gems](/es/fotografiar-joyas-y-gemas/) explains what an honest photo is, and will also help you know what to ask for.

## Certificates and reports

In pieces with valuable gems, the laboratory report is essential, but it is also falsified.

1. **Check that the laboratory exists** and is recognized.
2. **Check the report number** on the laboratory's official website, not through a link sent to you by the seller.
3. **Compare the data**: weight, measurements, shape, color and, if applicable, the laser inscription on the stone.
4. **Be wary of generic "certificates of authenticity"** issued by the store itself: they are not a substitute for an independent report.

The guide on [gemological laboratory reports](/es/informes-de-laboratorio-gemologico/) explains how to read them.

## The price

- **Compare** the same part, or an equivalent one, at multiple sellers.
- **Be wary of prices well below the market**: in jewelry, an extreme bargain usually hides a synthetic gem, an undeclared treatment, a different metal or fraud.
- **Beware of urgency**: countdown counters, "last units" or discounts that expire in minutes make you buy without thinking.

## The payment

The payment method is your main protection if something goes wrong.

- **Prefer methods with buyer protection**, such as credit cards, which allow you to complain to the bank, or payment platforms with a dispute system.
- **Avoid wire transfers to strangers**, money transfers, gift cards and cryptocurrencies when you buy from a seller you don't know: they can almost never be recovered.
- **Do not pay outside the platform** if the seller asks you to "save commissions": you lose protection.
- **Save all receipts**: page screenshots, emails, chats, invoices.

## Shipping

- **Request insured shipping** for the actual value of the piece, with tracking number and delivery with signature.
- **Ask who assumes the risk** during transportation.
- **If the purchase is international, find out about customs**: taxes, tariffs and procedures can make the purchase very expensive, and are not always paid by the seller.
- **Please note deadlines**: some pieces are made to order and take weeks.

## Returns and warranty

Read the policy before paying, not after:

- **return period** and from when it counts;
- **who pays for return shipping**;
- if there are **restocking charges**;
- which pieces **cannot be returned**, such as those custom-made or engraved;
- if you can **change the size** and under what conditions;
- What does the **warranty** cover and for how long.

If you don't know your ring size, measure it before purchasing with the guide on [how to measure ring size](/es/medir-la-talla-de-anillo/).

### Your rights as a consumer

Consumer rights in distance purchases vary by country. In the region there are organizations that handle complaints, such as Profeco in Mexico, Sernac in Chile, the Superintendency of Industry and Commerce in Colombia, Indecopi in Peru, Procon in Brazil or the Consumer Defense offices in Argentina. Check their official pages if a store does not deliver as promised.

## Shopping through social networks and live broadcasts

Many small workshops sell honestly on social networks, but fake profiles are also abundant there.

- checks the **age of the profile** and the consistency of its publications;
- requests **billing information** and a secure payment method;
- be wary of profiles that only accept transfers or that pressure you to close the sale in minutes;
- In live sales, ask them to reserve the piece for you and send you photos and information before paying.

## Upon receiving the piece

1. **Record a video when opening the package** if the piece is valuable: it will help you if you have to claim.
2. **Compare the piece with the invoice and description**: metal, marks, measurements, stones, report.
3. **Check the report** on the lab page, if you haven't done so before.
4. **Have the piece inspected** by an independent jeweler or gemologist **within the return period**.
5. **Keep the original packaging** until you are sure you have it.
6. **Insure it** if it has value. The guide on [jewelry insurance and appraisal](/es/seguro-y-tasacion-de-joyas/) explains how to do this.

## Warning signs in summary

- seller without identity or verifiable address;
- generic photos and refusal to send real photos or videos;
- descriptions that are vague or have words like “gold tone” or “diamond type”;
- certificates that cannot be verified on the laboratory website;
- prices well below the market;
- pressure to pay quickly or off-platform;
- Non-existent or confusing return policy.

If you want to learn to recognize the most common imitations, consult the guide on [imitations, doublets and triplets](/es/imitaciones-y-dobletes/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to buy diamonds or expensive gems online?

It can be with a reputable seller, a verifiable report, secure payment, insured shipping, and a clear return policy. Without those conditions, the risk is high.

### What do I do if the part does not correspond to the description?

Contact the seller in writing within the return period, attach photos and the opening video, and, if they do not respond, initiate a claim on the payment platform or with the consumer protection agency.

### Is it worth buying rough gems online?

It may be worth it for collectors and carvers, but it is even more difficult to judge the material in photos. Buy from reputable sellers and start with small quantities. The guide to [buy raw gems without deception](/es/comprar-gemas-en-bruto-sin-enganos/) gives more advice.

## In summary

Buying jewelry online can be safe if done methodically. First, evaluate the seller: identity, contact, track record, independent opinions and written conditions. Then, read the description with a magnifying glass, be wary of words like "gold tone" or "diamond type", ask for real photos and videos and verify the report on the laboratory's website. Pay with methods that protect the buyer, require insured shipping with tracking, read the returns policy before paying and, upon receiving the piece, record the opening, compare it with the invoice and have it reviewed by a professional within the deadline.
