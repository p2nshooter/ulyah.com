Buying a rough gem is somewhat of an adventure. Faced with an uncut crystal, the buyer tries to guess what stone will come out of it: how much color it will have, how much light, how many inclusions it hides and how much weight it will lose on the lapidary's wheel. In Latin America there are markets where this scene is repeated every day, from the emerald merchants in the center of Bogotá to the stone fairs of Minas Gerais or the opal workshops of Querétaro.

But raw material is also fertile ground for deception. Water-polished glass, synthetic crystals, dyed stones, lots with the best on top and the worst hidden below, or stories of lucky finds that end up with a worthless stone. In this guide you will see where to buy raw material, what the most common scams are, what tools help you check it, how to look inside a stone, what cutting performance means and how to buy safely and legally.

## Why buy raw

Buying raw has advantages and risks:

- **Advantages:** prices closer to the origin, the possibility of ordering a custom size and the pleasure of following the complete process.
- **Risks:** It is much more difficult to judge the color and purity of an uncut stone, the final weight is uncertain, and imitations are easier to strain.

Therefore, for those who are starting out, it is usually more prudent to buy stones cut with a report and reserve the rough for when you have experience, a good trusted carver or an advisor.

## The most common deceptions

| deception | How it works | How to protect yourself |
|---|---|---|
| rolled glass | pieces of colored glass rounded by water or a drum, presented as gems | check for shine, bubbles and fracture marks; ask for a test |
| synthetic crystals | lab-created crystals with natural appearance | distrust perfect and cheap crystals; consult a laboratory |
| dyed stones | color added to pale or cracked material | look for concentrated color in cracks |
| Fillers and oils | cracks hidden with oils or resins | look with a magnifying glass and strong light |
| Prepared batch | the best up and the worst hidden | check stone by stone |
| Inflated weight | poorly calibrated scales or wet stones | weigh yourself, dry, with a reliable scale |
| History of the discovery | stories of inheritances or discoveries that justify a high price | Judge the stone, not the story |
| doubtful certificate | documents from non-existent or unrecognized laboratories | Verify the report directly with the laboratory |

## Basic tools for review

- **Tenx magnifying glass:** to see inclusions, cracks, bubbles and marks.
- **Intense white light flashlight:** to illuminate the interior of the stone.
- **Carat scale:** to check dry weight.
- **Container with water:** Wetting the stone reduces surface reflections and allows you to see the inside better.
- **Ultraviolet Lamp:** Some gemstones and some fillings react in characteristic ways, although interpretation requires experience.

These instruments do not replace a laboratory, but they help rule out the crudest deceptions.

## Look inside a rough stone

The surface of a raw glass is usually frosted, scratched or covered in rock debris, which prevents the interior from being seen. To judge it:

1. **Wet it:** Water or a light oil makes the surface more transparent.
2. **Illuminate it from behind and from the sides:** this is how cracks, inclusions and areas of color appear.
3. **Ask for permission to polish a small window:** This is a common practice for some valuable stones, which allows the interior to be seen without losing much material.
4. **Look at the color in different directions:** some gems change color depending on where you look at them.
5. **Look for areas without defects** large enough to give a cut stone the size you want.

## The yield: how much is left after carving

A cut stone retains only part of the weight of the rough, often much less than half. The performance depends on the shape of the crystal, where the inclusions and cracks are, the color and the size chosen. An elongated crystal, with a fissure in the center, can yield two small stones instead of one large one. We explain it in the guide on [from raw to gem: guidance and performance](/es/del-bruto-a-la-gema-orientacion-y-rendimiento/).

Before purchasing an expensive rough, if possible, consult a cutter: he can estimate what stone you will get and whether the price makes sense.

## Lots, packages and selection

In the rough trade it is common to sell lots or packages of several stones, at a total price or by weight.

- **Check the entire lot,** not just the top pieces.
- **Ask if you can choose** loose stones, even if it costs more per carat.
- **Separate stones by quality** and calculate how much useful material there really is.
- **Be wary of closed lots** or those that do not allow you to examine them calmly.

## Price per carat, per gram or per kilo

Quality rough gems are usually priced by the carat, which is equal to 0.2 grams. Less valuable or craft materials are sold by the gram or kilo. Make sure the price refers to the correct unit and that the weight is measured dry and with a reliable scale.

## Payments, invoices and conditions

- **Ask for invoice or receipt** with the description of the stone, the weight and the price.
- **Ask about return conditions** if a laboratory shows that the stone is not what was claimed.
- **Avoid paying in advance** to unknown sellers, especially online.
- **For important parts,** please agree that the purchase is subject to a laboratory result. We see it in the guide on [gemological laboratory reports](/es/informes-de-laboratorio-gemologico/).

## Legality and export

Gem mining and trading are regulated in many countries in the region. When purchasing raw:

- **Prefers formal sellers,** with invoice and known origin.
- **Learn about exporting:** Taking gems or minerals out of a country may require specific documentation, and regulations change.
- **Declare your purchases** at your country's customs when you return.
- **Do not buy fossils, archaeological pieces or material from protected areas,** whose trade is usually prohibited.

## Personal security

- **Do not carry large sums of cash** or brag about your purchases.
- **Do transactions in offices or known locations,** not on the street or in secluded places.
- **Do not travel alone to remote mining areas** without knowing the local situation or having trusted contacts.
- **Be wary of those who insist** on closing the deal in a hurry.

## Responsible purchasing

Behind every rough stone there are miners, often small producers with difficult working conditions. Paying fair prices, buying from transparent intermediaries and asking about origin are ways to contribute to a fairer chain. We explain it in the guide on [responsible gold and traceability](/es/oro-responsable-y-trazabilidad/), whose principles also apply to gems.

## Frequent errors

- **Buy for a story** and not for the stone.
- **Judge the rough dry** and in bad light.
- **Do not calculate size performance**.
- **Pay in advance** to unknown online sellers.
- **Trust certificates** without verifying them with the laboratory.
- **Ignore export regulations** and import regulations.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it cheaper to buy raw?

The price per carat is usually lower, but you have to add the cost of the size and weight loss, in addition to the risk of making a mistake. For those who do not have experience, a shapely cut stone may be safer.

### Can a laboratory analyze a rough stone?

Yes, in many cases, although some tests are easier with a polished surface. Check with the laboratory before sending it.

### How do I know if a glass is synthetic?

At first glance it is very difficult. Be wary of crystals that are too perfect and cheap, and consult a gemologist or a laboratory.

### Can I take gems out of the country without problems?

It depends on each country and the quantity and type of material. Find out about current regulations before buying and keep your invoices.

## In summary

Buying rough gems can be a great experience, but it requires knowledge and caution. Learn about the region's markets and the most common deceptions, such as rolled glass, synthetic crystals, dyes, fillers, prepared batches or dubious certificates. Check each stone wet, with good light and a magnifying glass, calculate the cutting performance and weigh it dry. Ask for an invoice, return conditions and, for important parts, a laboratory report. Comply with export regulations, take care of your safety and buy responsibly.
