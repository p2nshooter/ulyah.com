Rings get a lot of the attention in jewelry, but the pieces we use most every day tend to be others: earrings that we don't take off, a thin chain with a pendant, a bracelet that accompanies the watch. They are simpler jewelry in appearance and, however, the ones that are lost, broken or tangled the most. The cause is almost always the same: an inappropriate clasp, a chain that is too thin for the pendant or a poorly chosen length.

In this guide you will see the most common types of earrings, necklaces and bracelets, which closure is best for each use, how to choose the length according to the neckline and complexion, what differences there are between the types of chain, what it means for a piece to be solid, hollow or plated, and how to care for them so that they last.

## Earrings

### Types of earrings

| Type | How is it | For what use |
|---|---|---|
| Button or stud | a piece attached to the lobe, often with a gem | diary, work, first earrings |
| Hoops or hoops | a complete or almost complete circle | diary in small sizes; night in large sizes |
| Adjusted hoops | small hoops that hug the lobe | diary, several perforations |
| Pendants | a piece that falls under the lobe | events, to lengthen the face |
| Teardrop or candelabra | long and elaborate pendants | special occasions |
| Climbers | they go up the edge of the ear | modern style |
| ear cuff | attaches to cartilage without perforation | for those who don't want to get pierced |

### Closures, the key to not losing them

- **Pressure or butterfly:** the classic button. Comfortable, but may loosen over time. It is advisable to check it and tighten it gently from time to time.
- **Thread:** The post has a thread and the closure screws on. Very safe, ideal for children and for earrings with valuable gems.
- **Omega or French clip:** an articulated piece that hugs the lobe from behind. Widely used in large and heavy earrings.
- **Lever:** a hook with closure that snaps into place. Safe and practical in pendants.
- **Open hook:** Simple and light, but the easiest to lose. A silicone stopper can be added.
- **Hinge:** on rings, a piece that opens and snaps together. You have to make sure it fits with a firm click.

For earrings with valuable gemstones, the screwback or omega closure is the safest option.

### Weight and comfort

A heavy earring stretches the lobe over time. If you like large pendants, choose light materials, omega closures that distribute the weight and do not wear them all day. For recent piercings or sensitive skin, see the guide on [jewelry and metal allergies](/es/joyas-y-alergias-a-metales/).

## Necklaces and chains

### The usual lengths

Lengths vary by country and manufacturer, but these approximate references serve as a guide:

| Name | Approximate length | Where does it fall |
|---|---|---|
| Choker | 35–40cm | at the base of the neck |
| Princess | 43–48cm | on the clavicle; the most versatile |
| matinee | 50–60cm | on the chest |
| Opera | 70–90cm | under bust; can be worn double |
| Rope | more than 100cm | It is worn in several turns or knotted |

These names come from the tradition of pearl necklaces, which we explain in the guide on [natural and cultured pearls](/es/perlas-naturales-y-cultivadas/).

### How to choose the length

- **Depending on the neckline:** a V-neckline requires a pendant that follows the line; a high collar, a long chain over the garment; a bateau neckline, a choker or a princess length.
- **Depending on the complexion:** in short necks, medium lengths elongate the figure; On long necks, chokers look especially good.
- **To layer:** combine long chains about 5 cm apart so they don't get tangled.

More ideas in the guide on [how to wear jewelry judiciously](/es/como-llevar-joyas-con-criterio/).

### Chain types

| Link | How is | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Forced or roll | equal round or oval links | classic, sturdy, good for pendants |
| Barbada | twisted links resting flat | widely used in men's chains |
| Venetian | square links like little boxes | firm and elegant |
| Snake | articulated plate flexible tube | smooth and shiny; bends easily and is difficult to repair |
| Cord or rope | spiral braided links | a lot of shine |
| Figaro | alternate short and long links | classic mediterranean style |
| Spike | spike-shaped links | fine and resistant texture |
| Singapore | twisted links that flash | delicate and shiny |

### The chain and the pendant must go hand in hand

The most common mistake is hanging a heavy pendant from a very thin chain. Over time, friction wears down the links and the chain breaks. Some guidelines:

- choose a chain with a thickness proportional to the weight of the pendant;
- Check that the **ring of the pendant** allows the clasp to pass through and moves freely;
- Avoid snake chains for pendants with narrow rings, because they mark and bend.

### Necklace and bracelet clasps

- **Carabiner:** a spring-loaded hook. Very safe and easy to handle; the most recommended for most chains.
- **Reasa:** a spring-loaded ring. Light, but more difficult to open and somewhat less resistant.
- **Box:** a tab that fits into a small box, often with an additional latch. Typical of pearl necklaces and tennis bracelets.
- **Bar and ring:** decorative and easy to close, but can open if the bracelet is loose.
- **Magnetic:** comfortable for people with limited hand mobility, but not suitable for valuable pieces without additional insurance.

When a piece is valuable, additional insurance, such as a safety chain or a second closure, prevents many losses.

## Bracelets

### Types of bracelet

- **Rigid or slave:** one piece; some with hinge to open them.
- **Link:** thicker chains; the same variety of links as in the necklaces.
- **Tennis:** a line of identical gems set next to each other. It requires a good lock closure, because the stresses of daily use are many.
- **Charms:** a chain to which pendants are added.
- **Beaded:** with elastic thread, nylon or silk; The threads wear out and should be renewed.

### How to get the size right

Measure around your wrist just above the bone and add 1-2cm slack for a chain bracelet. The rigid ones also need to measure the width of the hand when passing them, unless they have a hinge. If it's a gift, a bracelet with several closure points or an adjustment link gives you room to go. The guide on [gifting jewelry](/es/regalar-joyas/) has more tricks to get it right.

## Solid, hollow, plated or laminated

Two chains that look the same can have very different values ​​and resistances.

- **Solid:** the metal is solid throughout its thickness. Heavier, more durable and easier to repair.
- **Hollow:** the links are empty inside. Light and economical, but it dents easily and is difficult to repair.
- **Dipped or plated:** a thin layer of gold or rhodium on another metal. The bathroom wears out with use, especially in areas of friction.
- **Laminated or plated:** a much thicker layer of gold, bonded to the base metal by pressure and heat. Lasts much longer than a bath.
- **Vermeil:** silver covered with a layer of gold.

The invoice must indicate the metal, the grade and whether the piece is solid, hollow or plated. The guide to [gold, silver and platinum](/es/oro-plata-y-platino/) explains the laws and trademarks.

## Care to make them last

- **Put your jewelry on last**, after perfume and creams.
- **Take off your chains to sleep**: knots and nighttime tensions break links.
- **Store them separately**, hanging or in individual bags, so they don't get tangled or scratched.
- **Detangle with patience**: Two thin pins and a flat surface help; never pull on the extremes.
- **Check the closures and claws** from time to time. A jeweler can check the settings on a tennis bracelet or gemstone earrings.
- **Renew the threads** of pearl and bead necklaces when they look stretched or dirty.

The [jewelry maintenance and cleaning](/es/mantenimiento-y-limpieza-de-joyas/) guide explains how to clean each metal and each gem.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which chain is the strongest for a pendant?

A forced, Venetian or herringbone chain, solid and with a thickness proportional to the pendant, with a lobster clasp closure.

### Why do my earrings turn black?

If they are silver, probably due to the natural oxidation of silver. If they are bathed, because the bath was worn out and the base metal is showing. A jeweler can clean the silver or re-plat the piece.

### Can a chain be shortened?

Yes, most solid chains can be shortened in a workshop. Hollow and snake ones are more difficult to modify.

More guides on metals, settings and design in the [Jewelry and design] section (/es/categoria/joyeria-y-diseno/).

## In summary

Earrings, necklaces and bracelets are the jewelry we wear the most and the ones that get lost or broken the most. For them to last, it is advisable to choose appropriate closures, such as the screw or omega on earrings with gems and the carabiner on chains; choose the length according to the neckline and complexion; adapt the thickness of the chain to the weight of the pendant; know if the piece is solid, hollow or plated; and take care of it with simple gestures, such as removing the chains when sleeping, storing them separately and checking the closures regularly.
