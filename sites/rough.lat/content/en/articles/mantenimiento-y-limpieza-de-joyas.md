A well-cared for piece of jewelry can last for several generations and reach the grandchildren almost as shiny as the day it was purchased. A neglected jewel, on the other hand, loses its shine in a few weeks, becomes scratched, tangled or, in the worst case, loses a stone that will never be found again. The difference is rarely in great care: it is almost always in small daily habits and a timely review.

The problem is that there is no universal cleaning method. What makes a diamond shine can ruin a pearl; ultrasound that thoroughly cleans a sapphire can crack an emerald; The product that restores shine to silver can erase the patina that the goldsmith applied on purpose. In this guide you will see what habits best protect your jewelry, how to clean it at home depending on the gem and metal, when to use or avoid ultrasound and steam, how to store it and take it on trips, what a jeweler checks each year and what signs indicate that a piece needs attention.

## Daily habits that protect the most

- **Last thing when dressing, first thing when undressing:** put on jewelry after creams, perfumes, hairsprays and makeup.
- **Take them off for demanding tasks:** sports, gym, gardening, cleaning, cooking with your hands or manual work.
- **Don't sleep in them:** chains get tangled, earrings bend and claws suffer.
- **Avoid the pool and the sea with jewelry on:** Chlorine and salt damage metals and stones.
- **Don't leave them on the edge of the sink:** This is the most common way to lose a ring down the drain.

## What harm chlorine, salt and cosmetics

- **Chlorine:** Over time it can weaken some gold alloys and damage porous stones and pearls.
- **Sea water:** Salt and sand scratch and corrode, especially silver and plated pieces.
- **Creams and sunscreens:** they leave a film that dulls the shine of the gems and accumulates in the settings.
- **Perfumes and hairsprays:** attack pearls, coral, amber and porous stones, such as turquoise or lapis lazuli.
- **Household cleaning products:** many contain substances that corrode metals and damage gems.

## Cleaning at home: the basic method

For most gold or platinum jewelry with resistant gemstones:

1. **Prepare a container with warm water** and a few drops of mild soap, without perfume or creams.
2. **Immerse the jewel for a few minutes,** unless the gem does not tolerate water, as you will see in the following table.
3. **Brush gently** with a soft-bristled toothbrush, insisting under the stone and between the claws.
4. **Rinse with clean water,** with the drain covered or over a container.
5. **Dry with a soft, lint-free cloth**.

## What to clean and how, depending on the gem

| Gem | Warm soapy water | Observations |
|---|---|---|
| Diamond, ruby and sapphire without fillings | yes | resistant; fat is your main enemy |
| Spinel, chrysoberyl, garnet, quartz | yes | with soft brush |
| Aquamarine, topaz, tourmaline | yes | avoid sudden temperature changes |
| Emerald | just a short rinse | no long soaks, ultrasounds or steam, because of the oils |
| Tanzanite | yes, carefully | avoid sudden temperature changes |
| Opal | damp cloth | do not immerse it for a long time or expose it to heat |
| Pearls | soft, barely damp cloth | never strong soap or soak |
| Turquoise, lapis lazuli, malachite, rhodochrosite | dry or slightly damp cloth | porous or chemical sensitive |
| Amber and coral | damp cloth | sensitive to solvents and acids |
| Treated or filled gems | consult the jeweler | some treatments do not tolerate hot water or chemicals |

If you don't know what gem it is or if it has been treated, limit yourself to a soft cloth until you check. We explain this in the guides on [hardness, toughness and stability](/es/dureza-tenacidad-y-estabilidad/) and [gem treatments](/es/tratamientos-de-gemas/).

## Ultrasounds and steam: when yes and when not

Ultrasonic cleaners generate vibrations in a liquid that release dirt, and steam cleaners project pressurized steam. They are very effective, but they are not for all jewelry.

| Jewel or gem | Ultrasounds | Steam |
|---|---|---|
| Diamonds without cracks or fillings | In general, yes | In general, yes |
| Rubies and sapphires without glass fillings | In general, yes | In general, yes |
| Gold and platinum without stones | yes | yes |
| Emeralds and gems with oils or resins | no | no |
| Glass Filled Rubies and Fissured Gems | no | no |
| Opals, pearls, amber, coral and organic gems | no | no |
| Turquoise, lapis lazuli and porous stones | no | no |
| Tanzanite and heat sensitive gems | with great caution | no |
| Jewelry with loose stones, pavé or antique settings | no | with caution |

Vibrations can also loosen small stones from a worn pavé or setting. When in doubt, leave this type of cleaning in the hands of the jeweler.

## Metals: each one with its own care

- **Yellow and Rose Gold:** Warm soapy water and a soft cloth. High-grade gold is softer and scratches more easily.
- **White gold:** the same method; Avoid abrasives, which wear down the rhodium plating, and ask for it to be renewed when the tone turns yellow.
- **Platinum:** very resistant; It develops a matte patina with use that can be polished in the workshop.
- **Silver:** darkens with air. Use specific cloths for silver and avoid cleaning baths on pieces with intentional patina or stones.
- **Dipped parts:** just a soft, dry cloth; Humidity, sweat and abrasives wear out the bathroom.

More information in the guide on [gold, silver and platinum](/es/oro-plata-y-platino/).

## Chains and pearls

- **Chains:** store them buckled or hanging so they don't get tangled. If a knot forms, patiently untie it using two thin pins on a flat surface.
- **Pearls:** Wipe them with a soft cloth after use, store them horizontally and re-string the necklaces from time to time. We explain it in the guide on [natural and cultured pearls](/es/perlas-naturales-y-cultivadas/).

## Store the jewelry

- **Separated:** each piece in its compartment or cloth bag, so that the hard gems do not scratch the soft ones or the metals.
- **Away from humidity and heat,** which darken the silver and damage some gems.
- **Away from direct light** gems that may discolor, such as kunzite or some amethysts.
- **With antioxidant strips** in the case of silver.
- **An inventory with photos** of your valuable pieces helps in case of loss or theft.

## On a trip

- **Take only the jewelry you are going to wear.**
- **Take them in your hand luggage,** never in your checked suitcase.
- **Use a travel case** with compartments.
- **Keep them in the safe** of the accommodation when you are not wearing them.
- **Check your insurance:** Many policies have special conditions for jewelry away from home. We see it in the guide on [jewelry insurance and appraisal](/es/seguro-y-tasacion-de-joyas/).

## The professional review

At least once a year, and more often if you wear a piece daily, it is a good idea to take important jewelry to a trusted jeweler.

| What do you review? | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Claws and settings | a worn or raised claw can loosen the stone |
| Pavé and small stones | detect gaps and loose stones in time |
| Chain clasps and rings | They are the weak point of necklaces and bracelets |
| Hoop thickness | Very worn rings become thin and can become deformed |
| rhodium plating | renewing it returns white to white gold |
| necklace thread | rethread before it breaks |
| Professional cleaning | removes dirt accumulated in inaccessible areas |

Always notify the jeweler if a stone is treated or heat sensitive before any repair, as we explain in the guide on [setting types](/es/tipos-de-engaste/).

## Warning signs

- **A slight jingling** when moving the jewel: a stone may be loose.
- **Clothing Snags:** One claw may be raised.
- **Stones dull** despite cleaning: there may be a film of grease or damage to the surface.
- **A closure that opens by itself.**
- **A deformed ring** or with a very thin ring.
- **Green marks on the skin:** These are usually due to the reaction of the copper in some alloys with sweat; They are not dangerous, but indicate low-grade or worn metal or plating.

## Frequent errors

- **Clean all jewelry the same way** with ultrasound or strong products.
- **Use toothpaste** or abrasives, which scratch soft metals and gems.
- **Bathing in the pool** with jewelry on.
- **Keep all the pieces together** in the same box.
- **Wait until you lose a stone** to check the settings.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I clean my jewelry?

Everyday pieces appreciate a gentle cleaning every few weeks at home and a professional cleaning and inspection at least once a year.

### Can I clean my jewelry with vinegar or baking soda?

Better not. Vinegar is acidic and damages pearls, coral and many porous gems, and abrasive powders scratch metals and stones. Warm water with mild soap is safest.

### Are ultrasonic cleaners safe?

For diamonds, rubies and sapphires without fillings and for metals without stones, generally yes. For emeralds, opals, pearls, porous or treated gems and loose stones, no.

### Why does my ring turn my finger green?

It is usually due to the copper in some alloys or worn baths reacting with sweat. It is not dangerous, but it is advisable to clean the piece and, if it happens again, check the metal.

## In summary

Jewelry lasts for generations with simple habits: put it on last and take it off first, avoid chlorine, salt and cosmetics, don't sleep or play sports with it on and store it separately. Clean them at home with warm water and mild soap if the gem will tolerate it, and with a soft cloth if it is delicate, porous, organic or treated. Reserve ultrasonics and steam for tough gems and metals without fillers. Travel only with what you need and in your hand luggage, have them checked at least once a year and pay attention to the warning signs before losing a stone.
