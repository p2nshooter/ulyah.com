The same gem can look radically different depending on how it is cut. A round diamond with dozens of facets sparkles like a small firework; a rectangular emerald with stepped facets shows its color like a window to a green garden; a dome-cut star sapphire reveals a six-armed star that no facet would show. Carving doesn't just shape the stone: it decides how much of its beauty is seen.

Knowing the types of cut helps to choose a gem better, to understand why certain stones are always cut in one way and to talk to a jeweler or a cutter. In this guide you will see the difference between shape and cut, the anatomy of a cut gem, the large families of faceted cuts, the most common shapes, some old cuts, the cabochon and other options, what size suits each gem and how to judge the quality of the work.

## Shape and size are not the same

- **The shape** is the contour of the stone seen from above: round, oval, square, rectangular, pear-shaped, heart-shaped.
- **The cut or faceting style** is the way in which the facets are distributed: in a fan, in steps or a combination of both.

Thus, a rectangular stone can have a stepped cut, such as the emerald cut, or a brilliant cut, such as the so-called radiant cut. When describing a gem, it is advisable to indicate both.

## Anatomy of a cut gem

| Part | What is it |
|---|---|
| table or board | the upper facet, the largest and flatter |
| Crown | the upper part, between the table and the snaffle |
| Fillet | the edge that separates the crown from the pavilion and that holds the setting |
| Pavilion | the lower part, which returns the light upwards |
| Cylinder head | the tip or small facet at the lower end of the pavilion |

The proportions between these parts, such as the size of the table or the depth of the pavilion, determine how much light the stone returns. We explain it in the guide on [brightness, windows and extinction](/es/brillo-ventanas-y-extincion/).

## Three large families of faceted carvings

| Family | What are the facets like? | Effect | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brilliant | triangles and rhombuses radiating from the center | lots of shine and sparkles | brilliant round,princess,oval,pear |
| Staggered | parallel rectangles in steps | shows color and purity elegantly | emerald cut, baguette, asscher |
| Mixed | combines shiny and stepped facets | balance between brightness, color and weight | many color gems in oval or cushion |

## The most common forms

| Shape | Features | Common Gems |
|---|---|---|
| Round | the most classic and the one that can give the most shine | diamonds, sapphires, garnets |
| Oval | elongated, favors the appearance of size | sapphires, rubies, aquamarines, diamonds |
| Cushion | square or rectangular with rounded corners | colored gems, diamonds |
| princess | square with brilliant cut | diamonds |
| Emerald | rectangular stepped with cut corners | emeralds, aquamarines, diamonds |
| Ascher | staggered square with cut corners | diamonds, colored gems |
| Pear or drop | round at one end and pointed at the other | diamonds, colored gems |
| Marchioness | elongated with two points | diamonds |
| heart | symbolic, requires great precision | diamonds, colored gems |
| trillion | triangular | side stones, colored gems |

Shapes with points, such as pear, marquise or heart, require settings that protect these fragile areas, as we explain in the guide on [setting types] (/es/tipos-de-engaste/).

## The round brilliant

The modern round brilliant, with 57 or 58 facets, is the most studied cut in the world. At the beginning of the twentieth century, calculations were published on the proportions that best used light in a diamond, and since then the cut has been perfected with increasingly precise measuring instruments. Its goal is to balance three effects: the white glow, the colorful flashes, called fire, and the twinkling when the stone moves. We see it in the guide on [diamonds: essential guide](/es/diamantes-guia-esencial/).

## ancient carvings

Before modern tools, carvers worked with other shapes that are back in fashion today:

- **Pink size:** Flat base and domed top covered with triangular facets; widely used in jewelry from past centuries.
- **Old Mine and Antique European Carving:** Hand-carved brilliants, with small table, high crown and open butt, giving wide flares and warm appearance.

These carvings have a loyal following today and appear on antique jewelry and vintage-inspired pieces. We discuss it in the guide about [inherited and second-hand jewelry](/es/joyas-heredadas-y-de-segunda-mano/).

## The cabochon

The cabochon is a facetless cut, with a domed and polished top. It is the oldest way of working gems and is still the best option in many cases:

- **Opaque or translucent gems** such as turquoise, lapis lazuli, jade or opal.
- **Optical phenomena:** the star of sapphires and star rubies or the luminous line of the cat's eye only appear in a well-oriented cabochon.
- **Stones with many inclusions,** that faceted would look cloudy but in cabochon they show off their color.

There are high and low cabochons, with a flat or domed base, and even double ones, with both sides curved. We see this in more detail in the guide on [Colombian emeralds](/es/esmeraldas-colombianas-de-la-mina-a-la-mesa/), where the cabochon is common for the most included material.

## Briolette, beads and fancy carvings

- **Briolette:** Fully faceted, tableless droplet, often pierced at the tip to hang from earrings or necklaces.
- **Beads:** spheres or perforated shapes for necklaces and bracelets.
- **Fantasy carvings:** free designs, with concave facets, geometric cuts or sculptural shapes, which we will see in the guide on [signature and modern carvings] (/es/tallas-de-autor-y-modernas/).
- **Carvings and engravings:** figures, cameos and engravings, in which the gem becomes a miniature sculpture.

## What size suits each gem

| Gem | Usual sizes | why |
|---|---|---|
| Diamond | round brilliant and shiny shapes | maximizes brilliance and fire |
| Emerald | emerald or cabochon cut | protects corners, shows color and retains weight |
| sapphire and ruby | mixed in oval or cushion; star cabochon | balance between color, brightness and performance |
| Aquamarine and topaz | staggered or mixed | they show off their transparency and color |
| Tourmaline | elongated stepped | They adapt to the elongated shape of the glass and its color changes depending on the direction |
| Opal | cabochon or free form | shows the play of colors |
| Turquoise, lapis lazuli, jade | cabochon, beads or carvings | are opaque or translucent |

## Calibrated or native

- **Calibrated size:** standard measurements, in exact millimeters, that fit standard frames. You usually sacrifice some weight to achieve those measurements.
- **Native or original size:** made to retain the maximum weight of the rough, often with irregular proportions or a very deep pavilion.

Recarving a native stone can greatly improve its beauty, but reduces its weight. It is a decision that should be made with an experienced cutter, as we explain in the guide on [from rough to gem: guidance and performance](/es/del-bruto-a-la-gema-orientacion-y-rendimiento/).

## How to judge the quality of a size

- **Symmetry:** the contour is regular and the facets coincide at their vertices.
- **Polished:** the facets shine without marks or matte areas.
- **Proportions:** The stone has no windows that can be seen through, nor excessive dark areas.
- **Filetín:** neither too thin, which splinters, nor too thick, which adds weight without beauty.
- **Front appearance:** the stone should look lively and the size its weight suggests.

## Frequent errors

- **Confusing shape with size** when describing or comparing stones.
- **Choose a size with prongs** without a setting to protect them.
- **Pay by weight** a stone with an excessively deep pavilion that looks small from the front.
- **Facet material that would look better in cabochon.**
- **Recarving an ancient stone** without assessing its historical interest.

## Frequently asked questions

### What size shines the most?

In diamonds, the well-proportioned round brilliant is the cut that returns the most brilliance and fire. In colored gems, the effect depends on the stone and its proportions.

### Why are emeralds almost always cut rectangular?

Because the emerald cut protects its corners, which are fragile, preserves the weight of the crystal and shows the color very well.

### Is a cabochon worth less than a faceted stone?

Not necessarily. Many gems look best in cabochon, and some, like star sapphires, only show their phenomenon that way.

### What is a mixed size?

A carving that combines brilliant and stepped facets, for example a brilliant crown with a stepped pavilion. It is very common in colored gems.

## In summary

The shape is the outline of a gem and the cut is the arrangement of its facets. Faceted cuts are grouped into brilliant, stepped and mixed, and are applied to shapes such as round, oval, cushion, princess, emerald, pear or marquise. The round brilliant is the most studied cut for the diamond, while the emerald cut protects and shows off the emeralds. The cabochon is ideal for opaque gems, with optical phenomena or very included. Choose the cut based on the gem, judge the symmetry, polish and proportions, and protect the prongs with the appropriate setting.
