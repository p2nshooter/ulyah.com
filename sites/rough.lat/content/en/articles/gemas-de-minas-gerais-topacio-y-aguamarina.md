The name says it all: Minas Gerais, "general mines." This state in southeastern Brazil experienced a gold rush in the 18th century that built baroque cities like Ouro Preto, and shortly after a diamond rush around Diamantina. But his wealth did not end there. Under its hills hide thousands of pegmatites, giant crystal rocks that have given the world aquamarines, topaz, tourmalines, morganites and other gems in quantities and sizes that few regions can match.

For those interested in gems, Minas Gerais is a place of reference. In this guide you will see what pegmatites are and why they produce such large crystals, which are the most important gems in the region, why aquamarine and imperial topaz deserve special attention, what blue topaz is and how to buy these stones judiciously.

## A territory made of mines

The history of Minas Gerais is marked by mining. Gold financed the golden churches of Ouro Preto, Mariana or Sabará; Diamantina diamonds made Brazil the world's leading producer for much of the eighteenth century. Later, the search for industrial minerals and gems led to the exploration of pegmatites in the northeast of the state, around cities such as Teófilo Otoni and Governador Valadares, which became centers of the stone trade.

## What is a pegmatite and why does it matter?

A pegmatite is a very coarse-grained igneous rock that forms with the last liquids of a granitic magma. These liquids concentrate water and unusual elements, such as beryllium, lithium, boron or fluorine, which do not fit into the common minerals of granite. By slowly crystallizing in cavities and fractures, they allow huge crystals to grow, sometimes several kilos, with the transparency necessary to be carved.

The best-formed crystals are found in the cavities of the pegmatites, sometimes called pockets. Finding a good bag can change a mine's fortunes; Spending months without finding any is the most common.

## The aquamarine

Aquamarine is the blue variety of beryl, the same mineral as emerald. Its color is due to iron, and ranges from very light blues to intense tones, often with a greenish tinge. It is a hard and resistant gem, suitable for daily use.

- **Heat treatment:** Many aquamarines are heated to eliminate greenish or yellowish tones and obtain a purer blue. It is a very widespread and stable practice.
- **Color and value:** Deep, saturated blue aquamarines are the most sought after. The name of a historic mine, Santa Maria, is sometimes used in the trade to refer to that deep blue, even though the stone does not come from there.
- **Large sizes:** When forming in pegmatites, aquamarine can appear in huge, clean crystals. One of the most famous examples is the Dom Pedro, an aquamarine from Minas Gerais carved as an obelisk that is today part of the collection of a large natural history museum in the United States.

## The imperial topaz of Ouro Preto

Imperial topaz is the most valued variety of topaz, with colors ranging from orange-gold to pink and reddish. His name is associated, according to different versions, with the imperial nobility of Russia or Brazil. The Ouro Preto region has been its main source in the world for centuries.

Unlike many gems from Minas Gerais, Ouro Preto imperial topaz does not come from pegmatites, but from veins in highly altered rocks, where it appears in elongated prismatic crystals. Mining is often done by removing large volumes of clay soil, and quality crystals are scarce.

- **Color:** intense reddish orange and pink tones are the most appreciated.
- **Treatments:** Some orange topazes are heated to obtain pink tones. A serious seller should report it.
- **Inclusions:** Elongated tubes and cavities parallel to the axis of the crystal are common.

## Blue topaz: a stable treatment

Most blue topaz seen in jewelry stores is not naturally blue. It is obtained from colorless topaz, very abundant in Brazil and other countries, which is irradiated and heated to produce different shades of blue, known in the trade by names such as light blue, Swiss or London depending on their intensity. It is a stable and accepted treatment, as long as it is declared. This is why blue topaz is much more affordable than an aquamarine of a similar color. We explain it in the guide on [gem treatments](/es/tratamientos-de-gemas/).

## Citrine is not topaz

For a long time, names such as Bahia topaz or Madeira topaz were used to refer to citrines, which are yellow or orange quartz, many of them heated amethysts. They are different minerals, with different properties. The correct name is citrine, and calling it topaz confuses the buyer.

## Garimpeiros and fairs

An important part of gem production in Brazil comes from small farms and independent prospectors, the garimpeiros. Their work, often hard and with uncertain results, supplies a chain of buyers, carvers and exporters. In cities like Teófilo Otoni, gemstone fairs are held that bring together merchants from all over the world. If you are interested in this circuit, read the guide on [buying raw gems without deception](/es/comprar-gemas-en-bruto-sin-enganos/) and the one on [lapidary workshops of Latin America](/es/talleres-lapidarios-de-america-latina/).

## Buy aquamarine and topaz

- **Distinguish aquamarine from blue topaz:** they are similar, but they are different minerals and with very different prices. A gemologist separates them easily with instruments.
- **Beware of Imitations:** Blue glass and synthetic spinel are sometimes used as imitation aquamarine.
- **Ask about the treatments:** heating in aquamarines, irradiation in blue topaz, heating in some pink topaz.
- **Ask for a lab report** for important pieces, especially imperial topaz. We see it in the guide on [gemological laboratory reports](/es/informes-de-laboratorio-gemologico/).
- **Be wary of trade names** that suggest an origin without evidence.

## Care: exfoliation of topaz

Topaz is hard, but it has a perfect cleavage: a plane where it can break cleanly if hit in the right direction. In rings for daily use, a setting that protects it and prevents knocks is advisable. Aquamarine is more resistant in this aspect, although it is not indestructible either. We explain it in the guide on [hardness, toughness and stability] (/es/dureza-tenacidad-y-estabilidad/).

## Visit the region

Ouro Preto, declared a world heritage site, preserves its baroque center almost intact and houses the mineralogy museum of its former School of Mines, with one of the most important mineral collections in Brazil. Many shops in the city sell gems and minerals; Compare before buying and ask for an invoice. Some mines host organized tours; Find out about safety and conditions before you go.

## Frequent errors

- **Confuse treated blue topaz** with aquamarine.
- **Call citrine topaz.**
- **Do not ask about heating** of pink topaz.
- **Ignore the exfoliation** of topaz in everyday wear rings.
- **Pay for a trade name** without laboratory support.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is heated aquamarine worth less?

Heating aquamarine is a widespread and stable practice, and is widely accepted by the market. Still, it must be declared, and an untreated stone of good color may be more appreciated by some buyers.

### Why is blue topaz so cheap?

Because it is obtained by treating colorless topaz, which is abundant. The treatment is stable, but the raw materials are not scarce.

### Where is imperial topaz found?

The Ouro Preto region, in Minas Gerais, is its main historical and current source. There are other deposits in the world, but much less important.

### What is morganite?

The pink variety of beryl, colored by manganese. It is a sister gem of the aquamarine and the emerald.

## In summary

Minas Gerais is one of the great gemological territories in the world thanks to its pegmatites, which produce large and transparent crystals of aquamarine, morganite, tourmaline or topaz. Aquamarine, a blue variety of beryl, is usually heated to purify its color; imperial topaz, golden or pink, comes mainly from veins near Ouro Preto; and the blue topaz in jewelry stores is almost always treated colorless topaz. Distinguish these stones from each other and from citrine, ask about treatments, request reports for important pieces and protect the topaz from impacts due to its exfoliation.
