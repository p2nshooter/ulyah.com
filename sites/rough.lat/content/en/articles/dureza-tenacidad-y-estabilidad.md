"Diamond is the hardest thing there is" is a phrase that almost everyone has heard. And it is true, but incomplete: a diamond can chip with a sharp blow against the edge of a table, while a piece of jade, much less hard, resists impacts that would break many other gems. An emerald can crack in a sudden change in temperature, an opal can crack if it dries too much, and a pearl can lose its shine due to perfume.

To choose a gem that lasts, you have to understand three different properties: hardness, tenacity, and stability. In this guide you will see what each one means, how the Mohs scale works, why hardness 7 marks a practical boundary, what exfoliation is, which gems are sensitive to light, heat or chemicals, and how to choose the right stone for each type of jewelry.

## Three different concepts

| Property | What does it measure? | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hardness | resistance to being scratched | diamond scratches all other gems |
| Tenacity | resistance to breaking, chipping, or cracking upon impact | jade is very tenacious although it is not very hard |
| Stability | resistance to light, heat, chemicals and humidity changes | opal and pearls are not very stable |

A good gemstone for daily wear needs a reasonable balance of all three.

## Hardness and the Mohs scale

At the beginning of the 19th century, mineralogist Friedrich Mohs proposed a simple scale: arrange ten minerals so that each one scratches the previous ones. It remains the most used reference in gemology.

| Hardness | Reference mineral |
|---|---|
| 1 | talc |
| 2 | plaster |
| 3 | calcite |
| 4 | fluorite |
| 5 | apatite |
| 6 | orthoclase |
| 7 | quartz |
| 8 | topaz |
| 9 | corundum |
| 10 | diamond |

It is a relative scale, not proportional: the difference in hardness between corundum and diamond is much greater than that between talc and gypsum.

## Why hardness 7 is a frontier

Dust in the environment contains quartz particles, with a hardness of 7. The softer gems are gradually scratched by the daily friction of this dust, on clothing, on surfaces or when cleaning them with a cloth. Therefore:

- **Gems with hardness 7 or higher** retain polish better in everyday jewelry.
- **Gems below 7,** such as opal, turquoise or lapis lazuli, end up showing rounded edges and a matte surface if worn daily without care.

## Tenacity: resisting blows

The toughness depends on the internal structure of the gem. Jade, made up of intertwined crystals or fibers, is extraordinarily tenacious: that is why it has been carved for millennia into fine and resistant objects. At the other extreme, gems with many inclusions or fissures, such as many emeralds, can break with moderate blows even though they are quite hard.

## Exfoliation and fracture

Some gems have internal planes of weakness, whereby they tend to break cleanly if hit in the right direction. It's exfoliation.

- **Diamond:** four-way exfoliation; That's why it can splinter despite its hardness.
- **Topaz:** perfect exfoliation in one direction, as we explain in the guide on [Minas Gerais gems](/es/gemas-de-minas-gerais-topacio-y-aguamarina/).
- **Tanzanite and Kunzite:** Marked exfoliation requiring protective settings.
- **Rhodochrosite:** perfect exfoliation in three directions and little hardness; We see it in the guide on [Argentine rhodochrosite](/es/rodocrosita-argentina/).

Other gems, without exfoliation, break with irregular or shell-shaped fractures, such as quartz or glass.

## stability

- **Light:** Some gemstones lighten with prolonged exposure to the sun, such as kunzite and sometimes amethyst.
- **Heat:** Opal, emerald, tanzanite, amber or treated stones can be damaged by heat or sudden changes in temperature, for example during a torch repair.
- **Chemicals:** Pearls, coral, rhodochrosite, malachite and other carbonate gems are attacked with acids; Porous stones, like turquoise, absorb oils and creams.
- **Moisture:** Opal can crack if it loses water quickly; Pearls suffer in very dry environments for a long time.

## Choose the gem according to the jewel

| Jewel type | Exhibition | Recommended gems |
|---|---|---|
| Everyday ring | very high: blows, friction, water, soap | diamond, ruby, sapphire, spinel, chrysoberyl, aquamarine |
| Occasional wear ring | average | almost all gems, with proper setting |
| Bracelet | high: hits against tables and doors | hard and tenacious gems, with protective settings |
| Pendant and necklace | low | also more delicate gems, such as opal or tanzanite |
| Earrings | very low | practically any gem |
| Brooch | low | practically any gem |

## Settings that protect

Setting can compensate for some of a stone's fragility:

- **Bezel:** a metal ring that surrounds the entire stone protects its edges and is ideal for soft or exfoliated gems.
- **Robust Claws:** Six claws hold better than four and protect the corners more.
- **Covered corners:** in sizes with tips, such as pear or marquise, it is advisable to protect the tips with metal.
- **Low stone:** a stone that protrudes less receives fewer hits.

We explain it in the guide on [types of setting](/es/tipos-de-engaste/).

## Habits that extend the life of a gem

- **Take off your rings** for sports, manual work, gardening or cleaning.
- **Put on your jewelry after** perfumes, creams and hairsprays.
- **Store each piece separately** so the hard gems don't scratch the soft ones.
- **Cleans according to the gem:** What works for a sapphire can damage a pearl or an emerald.
- **Notify the jeweler** of the gem and its treatments before any repair.

More tips in the guide on [jewelry maintenance and cleaning](/es/mantenimiento-y-limpieza-de-joyas/).

## Frequent errors

- **Confuse hardness with shock resistance.**
- **Choose soft gemstones** for an unprotected everyday wear ring.
- **Store all jewelry together** in the same compartment.
- **Expose sensitive gems** to sun, heat or chemicals.
- **Clean all gems the same,** with ultrasound or steam.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the strongest gemstone for an everyday ring?

Diamond, ruby ​​and sapphire are the most common options due to their hardness, and ruby ​​and sapphire also stand out for their tenacity. Spinel and chrysoberyl are also excellent.

### Why did my diamond break if it is so hard?

Because hardness measures resistance to scratches, not shocks. An impact in the direction of its exfoliation can chip it.

### Can I wear an opal in a ring?

Yes, with care: best in a bezel setting and for occasional use, taking it off for demanding tasks.

### Do pearls scratch?

Yes, they are soft. Store them separately from other jewelry and clean them with a soft cloth.

## In summary

Hardness measures resistance to scratches, toughness measures resistance to impacts, and stability measures resistance to light, heat, chemicals and humidity. The Mohs scale ranks the hardness of talc to diamond, and gems with a hardness of 7 or higher resist everyday dust better. Gems such as ruby, sapphire or diamond are ideal for everyday rings, while opal, turquoise, rhodochrosite or pearls look best in pendants, earrings or with protective settings. Choose the gem according to the jewel, protect it with the appropriate setting and adopt habits that extend its life.
