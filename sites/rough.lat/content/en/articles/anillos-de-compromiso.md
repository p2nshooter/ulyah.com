Few pieces of jewelry carry as much meaning as an engagement ring. It is a piece that is often chosen secretly, with nerves and with the intention that it last a lifetime, and that is why it is usually surrounded by doubts: what stone to choose, how much to spend, how to get the size right without ruining the surprise, what metal will resist better or how to combine it later with the wedding ring.

There is no single answer. Customs change from one country to another, tastes too, and today there are more options than ever, from the classic diamond solitaire to sapphires, emeralds, laboratory diamonds or custom-made designs. In this guide you will see the most common customs in the region, the ring styles, how to choose the stone, metal and setting, how to find out the size, how to think about the budget, what documents to request and how to care for the ring.

## Commitment and rings: customs of the region

In much of Latin America, the engagement ring is given in the marriage proposal, and later, at the wedding, the couple exchanges wedding rings or alliances, depending on what they are called in each country. Some customs vary:

- **The marriage proposal:** in many families, the commitment is also formalized in a meeting between the families, with different degrees of ceremony depending on the country and region.
- **The ring hand:** in some places the couple wears the rings on one hand during the engagement and switches them to the other on the wedding day. The custom of which hand and which finger each ring is worn on changes depending on the country and the family.
- **Rings for both:** More and more couples choose engagement rings for both people.

No rule is mandatory: the important thing is that the ring makes sense to the person who receives it.

## ring styles

| Style | How is it | for whom |
|---|---|---|
| lonely | a single stone protagonist | who is looking for a timeless classic |
| three stones | one central and two lateral | who wants more presence or special symbolism |
| Halo | central stone surrounded by small stones | who is looking for maximum brilliance and a larger looking center stone |
| Vintage or antique inspired | openwork details, engravings and fine settings | vintage design lovers |
| two stones | two gems together, sometimes of different types | who wants to represent the couple |
| Band with stones | stones set along the ring | who prefers something discreet and practical |
| color gem | sapphire, ruby, emerald or other as the protagonist | who is looking for a personal and different ring |

## Diamond, colored gem or alternative

| Stone | in favor | To take into account |
|---|---|---|
| natural diamond | classic, very hard, very bright | price; choose with lab report |
| laboratory diamond | same material, much cheaper | low resale value |
| Sapphire | very hard and tenacious; exists in many colors | ask for information about treatments |
| Ruby | hard, symbolic, intense color | high-quality ones are scarce; Be careful with glass fillings |
| Emerald | incomparable color | more fragile; needs protective crimping and care |
| Moissanite | very bright and hard, economical | It is another material, not diamond |
| Aquamarine or morganite | soft and bright colors | somewhat less hard; a protective crimp is advisable |

For a ring that will be worn every day for years, hard, tenacious gemstones, such as diamond, sapphire, and ruby, are the safest options. ## The metal

- **18K Yellow Gold:** Warm and traditional; It combines very well with somewhat warm tone diamonds and colored stones.
- **White gold:** modern; It usually has a rhodium plating that needs to be renewed.
- **Rose gold:** romantic and current.
- **Platinum:** natural white, dense, resistant and hypoallergenic.

Also think about the other jewelry that the person wears daily: a ring that matches their usual watch or earrings will be worn with more pleasure. More information in the guide on [gold, silver and platinum](/es/oro-plata-y-platino/).

## The setting

For an everyday ring, the setting should protect the stone without hiding it:

- **Six claws** give more security than four in a solitaire.
- **Bezel** is the most protective option, ideal for active people or delicate gemstones.
- **V-shaped claws** protect the tips of the pear, marquise or princess sizes.
- **A stone that is not too high** receives fewer hits and snags less.

We see it in the guide on [types of crimping](/es/tipos-de-engaste/).

## The size of the ring, without ruining the surprise

Getting the size right is one of the biggest concerns. Some ideas:

- **Borrow a ring** that the person wears on that same finger and take it to the jeweler.
- **Mark the inside diameter** of a ring on a piece of paper, if you can't take it with you.
- **Discreetly ask** family members or close friends.
- **When in doubt, choose a slightly larger size:** it is easier to reduce than to enlarge, and a large ring will not get stuck on the finger.
- **Choose an adjustable design** in size, without pavé around the entire band or tension setting, if you are unsure.

We explain it in detail in the guide on [how to measure ring size](/es/medir-la-talla-de-anillo/).

## Budget: neither rules nor myths

During the twentieth century, the idea spread that an engagement ring should cost a certain number of salaries. That myth was born from advertising campaigns, not from any tradition. What is reasonable is:

- **Set a comfortable budget** that does not compromise the couple's finances.
- **Decide what is a priority:** size, quality, type of stone or design.
- **Distribute intelligently:** in diamonds, the cut is what is most visible; a somewhat lesser color or purity is barely noticeable to the naked eye.
- **Remember that emotional value** does not depend on price.

## Buy with guarantees

Before paying, make sure you receive:

1. **Invoice** with complete description: metal and grade, weight, stones, treatments and measurements.
2. **Lab report** of the center stone, if it is a diamond or valuable gem. We see it in the guide on [gemological laboratory reports](/es/informes-de-laboratorio-gemologico/).
3. **Size change conditions** and written returns.
4. **Guarantee on setting** and work, if the store offers it.
5. **Maintenance policy:** cleaning, checking claws or renewing the rhodium.

## Think about the ring from the beginning

Many people discover after the wedding that their engagement ring and band do not fit well together. To avoid it:

- **Choose engagement rings with a compatible profile:** If the stone is too low or the halo protrudes, the band may become separated.
- **Consider rings with a curve or notch,** designed to fit the engagement ring.
- **Use the same metal** on both pieces so they wear similarly and the color matches.

## Custom design

A custom-made ring allows you to choose every detail, combine a special stone with a unique design or reuse gold and stones from family jewelry. It requires more time and good communication with the jeweler. We explain it in the guide about [custom jewelry](/es/joyas-a-medida/).

## Safe and careful

- **Insure the ring** from the moment of purchase; Many home policies do not cover undeclared valuable jewelry. We see it in the guide on [jewelry insurance and appraisal](/es/seguro-y-tasacion-de-joyas/).
- **Save photos, invoice and report** in a safe place and in digital copy.
- **Take it off** for sports, gym, gardening, cleaning or when applying creams.
- **Clean it** with warm water and mild soap, and have it checked at least once a year.

More tips in the guide on [jewelry maintenance and cleaning](/es/mantenimiento-y-limpieza-de-joyas/).

## Frequent errors

- **Be guided by budget myths** instead of by the couple's real finances.
- **Choose a delicate gemstone** in an exposed setting for everyday wear.
- **Do not ask for a report** on the central stone.
- **Forget compatibility** with the future ring.
- **Do not secure the ring** before traveling for the request.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does it have to be diamond?

No. Diamond is traditional, but sapphire, ruby, emerald or other gems are precious and meaningful options. The important thing is to choose a stone suitable for daily use and a setting that protects it.

### What do I do if I don't get the size right?

Most rings can be adjusted in a workshop, except for some designs such as full pave or tension. Ask about exchange conditions before purchasing.

### Are laboratory diamonds a good option?

They can be if they are chosen knowing what they are: real lab-created diamonds, much cheaper and with a low resale value.

### How much should I spend?

What you can spend comfortably. There is no rule, and the figures that circulate come from advertising campaigns.

## In summary

A good engagement ring combines meaning, beauty and strength. Respect the customs that matter to the couple, choose a style that suits the person and a hard, tenacious stone for daily wear, such as diamond, sapphire or ruby, or protect the most delicate gems well. Choose the metal and the setting thinking about daily life, find out the size discreetly, set a comfortable budget without paying attention to advertising myths and ask for a detailed invoice, stone report and written conditions. Think about the ring from the beginning, secure the ring and review it every year.
