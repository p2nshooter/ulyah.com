Most damage to jewelry occurs not while it is being worn, but rather when it is being stored. Necklaces that get tangled in the bottom of a drawer, pearls that lose shine in a plastic bag, rings that scratch against each other, silver that blackens in the bathroom. And when we travel, the risk changes: losses at the airport, forgetfulness in the hotel room, theft in crowded places. With a few simple habits, almost all of these problems can be avoided.

In this guide you will see how to organize jewelry at home according to material, how to protect it from humidity and theft, what to take and what to leave when traveling, how to transport it, what documents should be on hand and what to do if something is lost.

## Store jewelry at home

### The golden rule: every piece in its place

Gems and metals scratch each other. A diamond or sapphire will scratch almost anything else, and the chains tangle as soon as they come together. That is why the ideal is that **each piece has its own space**:

- jewelry boxes with separate **padded compartments**;
- **individual bags** of soft fabric for each piece;
- **hooks or bars** to hang necklaces and chains;
- **ring rolls** or slots so they don't touch.

### Depending on the material

| material | How to save it |
|---|---|
| Diamonds, sapphires, rubies | separated from other pieces; They scratch almost everything |
| Emeralds | separated, protected from shocks and sudden changes in temperature |
| Pearls | flat, in a cloth bag or a breathing case; never in closed plastic or hanging, so that the thread does not stretch |
| Opals | away from heat and very dry environments |
| Turquoise, coral, amber, malachite | separated, away from perfumes, creams and heat |
| Silver | in closed bags, with antioxidant strips or a specific cloth, away from humidity |
| Gold and platinum | separated so they don't get scratched |
| Watches | in its box or on a stand, away from magnets |

For pearls, the guide on [natural and cultured pearls](/es/perlas-naturales-y-cultivadas/) gives more details.

### Humidity, heat and light

- **The bathroom is a bad place to store jewelry.** Humidity accelerates the blackening of silver and can damage pearls, coral and porous stones.
- **Avoid direct sun** on the jewelry box: some gemstones, such as amethyst or rose quartz, can fade with very long exposures, and heat damages opals, pearls and resins.
- **A closed drawer in a bedroom**, away from windows and radiators, is usually a good place.

### Before saving them

- Clean the jewelry with a soft cloth after wearing it to remove sweat, creams and makeup.
- Let the pieces that have been wet dry well.
- Close the clasps on the chains before storing them: this way they will tangle much less.

The [jewelry maintenance and cleaning](/es/mantenimiento-y-limpieza-de-joyas/) guide explains how to clean each material.

## Safety at home

- **A safe fixed** to the wall or floor is the safest option for valuables. If it is not anchored, it can be carried whole.
- **Avoid obvious hiding places** such as the bedside drawer, freezer or shoe box - these are the first places to check.
- **Do not leave jewelry on display** in places visible from windows or doors.
- **Take an inventory**: photographs of each piece, invoices, gem reports and appraisals. Keep a copy away from home or in the cloud.
- **Secure important pieces.** The guide on [jewelry insurance and appraisal](/es/seguro-y-tasacion-de-joyas/) explains how to value and document them, and the [photographing jewelry and gems](/es/fotografiar-joyas-y-gemas/) guide explains how to take useful inventory photos.

## Before traveling

### Only carry what you are going to use

The best advice for traveling with jewelry is also the simplest: **carry little**. Choose versatile pieces that go with everything, and leave the ones that have great economic or sentimental value at home if you don't need them.

Some useful questions before packing your suitcase:

- Am I really going to use this piece during the trip?
- Could I replace it if I lose it?
- Is the place I go safe to carry it on sight?
- Am I going to the beach, the mountains or to play sports?

### Document what you carry

- make a **list with photos** of each piece that travels with you;
- bring a copy of the **invoices or appraisals** of the valuable pieces;
- Check if your **insurance** covers jewelry outside your home and abroad.

### Find out about customs

If you are traveling abroad with high-value items, check the customs regulations of the destination country and yours. Some countries allow you to **register valuables before leaving**, to prove when you return that they were already yours and not pay taxes on them. The rules change depending on the country, so it is advisable to inform yourself in advance on the official customs website.

## During the trip

### on the plane

- **Always carry jewelry in hand luggage**, never in a checked suitcase.
- Use a **travel case** with compartments, or individual bags inside a toiletry bag.
- At the security check, place the case inside your bag and watch the tray until you pick it up.
- If you are wearing jewelry, take it off before the check and keep it in your bag, not loose on the tray.

### at the hotel

- Use the **safe in the room** or, for important items, the one at the reception, asking for a receipt.
- Do not leave jewelry on the sink or table: this is where they are most forgotten when they leave.
- Before leaving the hotel, **check drawers, bathroom and safe**.

### on the street

- Avoid wearing high-value pieces in **crowded places**, public transportation or areas you don't know.
- Turn the ring stone towards your palm if you feel uncomfortable.
- Carry unused jewelry in an inside pocket or case, not loose in your bag.

### On the beach, the pool and the mountains

- **Take off your rings** before entering cold water: your fingers contract and the rings slide off.
- **Chlorine** and **seawater** damage some gems and metals, and sunscreen leaves films that are difficult to clean.
- **Sand** scratches metals and soft stones.
- During sports and hiking, the rings can get caught and cause injuries.

## A well-prepared travel case

| Element | What is it for? |
|---|---|
| Case with compartments or jewelry roll | that the pieces do not touch |
| Individual bags | for chains and pearls |
| Cut straws or tubes | to pass the chains and prevent them from tangling |
| Microfiber cloth | clean fingerprints and creams |
| Replacement Earring Plugs | butterfly closures are easy to lose |
| List and photos | know what you are carrying and demonstrate it if necessary |

For thin chains, an effective trick is to pass them through a straw and close the clasp on the outside: they arrive without a single knot. More ideas about closures in the guide about [earrings, necklaces and bracelets](/es/aretes-collares-y-pulseras/).

## If you lose a jewel or it is stolen

1. **Report it** to the local police as soon as possible and ask for a copy of the report.
2. **Notify the hotel, airline or place** where you think you lost it.
3. **Contact your insurer** with the complaint, photos and invoices.
4. **If the piece has a report number** or laser inscription, like some diamonds, save it: it can help identify it if it appears.

## Inherited jewelry of great sentimental value

Parts that cannot be replaced deserve special care: it may be better not to travel with them or to carry a replica. The guide on [inherited and second-hand jewelry](/es/joyas-heredadas-y-de-segunda-mano/) explains how to document and preserve it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to leave jewelry in the hotel safe?

It's safer than leaving them in the room, but it's not foolproof. For high-value pieces, the reception safe with receipt, or not taking them at all, are more prudent options.

### Can I put the jewelry in the checked suitcase if it is well hidden?

It is not recommended. Checked bags get lost, delayed, and pass through many hands. Jewelry should always go with you.

### Can pearls be kept in a safe?

Yes, inside a cloth bag or a case that breathes. What should be avoided is closed plastic and very dry or very humid environments for a long time.

## In summary

Storing jewelry well consists of giving each piece its own space, adapting the way it is stored to its material, keeping it away from humidity, heat and sun, and cleaning it before storing it. An anchored safe, inconspicuous hiding places, an inventory with photos and invoices, and good insurance serve to protect them at home. When traveling, the most effective thing is to carry a few pieces, always in your hand luggage and in a suitable case, use the hotel safe, check with customs if you are taking anything of value and take off your rings in the water and during sports.
