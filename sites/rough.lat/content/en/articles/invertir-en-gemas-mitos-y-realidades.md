"Diamonds are forever", "gems never lose value", "an emerald is a safe haven". Phrases like these have been circulating for decades and lead many people to think that buying precious stones is a sure way to protect or multiply their money. The reality is much more complex, and those who buy gems with an investment in mind often get an unpleasant surprise when they try to sell them.

This guide is not financial advice nor is it intended to tell you what to invest in. Its goal is to explain how the gem market really works so that any decision is made with information. You will see which myths are most common, why gems are not very liquid, what the difference is between the purchase and sale price, what costs are usually forgotten, how the diamond market has changed, what are the signs of fraud and what expectations to have when selling.

## Common myths

| Myth | Reality |
|---|---|
| Gems always go up in price | prices change with fashion, supply, economics and technology; some gems have lost value |
| A certificate guarantees the value | a report describes the stone; It does not set a price or guarantee that anyone will buy it |
| Can be sold at store price | When selling, you usually receive much less than what you paid at retail |
| Rare always sells quickly | Exceptional pieces can take a long time to find a buyer |
| Lab diamonds are an investment | Its price has dropped a lot and its resale value is low |
| A high appraisal shows a good purchase | Some insurance appraisals inflate the value and do not reflect what you would get when selling. |

## Why are gems not very liquid?

Liquidity is the ease of converting an asset into money without losing value. Gems are usually not very liquid for several reasons:

- **There is no single public price:** with some exceptions, there is no daily and transparent price like that of a currency or a metal.
- **Each stone is different:** the color, purity, cut, origin and treatments mean that two similar stones are worth very different things.
- **The valuation is partly subjective:** two experts can disagree about the value of the same stone.
- **The final buyer is limited:** many times you have to sell to a merchant, who needs margin to resell it.

## The difference between buying and selling

When you buy a piece of jewelry or a gem at retail, the price includes the value of the stone, the work, the design, the brand, and the margins of the entire commercial chain. When you sell it, the buyer, usually a merchant, will offer you a price that allows them to resell it at a profit. The difference between the two prices is usually considerable, and can take many years to be compensated, if at all.

## Forgotten costs

- **Lab reports** required to sell important parts.
- **Sure,** if you want to protect the piece.
- **Custody,** in a safe or depository.
- **Sales commissions,** in auctions or consignment sales.
- **Taxes,** according to the legislation of each country.

All of them reduce the final result of any purchase intended as an investment.

## Diamonds: a changing market

For much of the twentieth century, the price of diamonds was tightly controlled by a few market players, and advertising cemented the idea that they were a safe value. In recent years, the mass production of laboratory diamonds, identical in composition and much cheaper, has changed the market and put pressure on the prices of certain natural diamonds. No one can be sure how they will evolve. We explain it in the guide on [diamonds: essential guide](/es/diamantes-guia-esencial/).

## Colored gems: scarcity and subjectivity

Some exceptional colored gems, such as certain rubies, sapphires or emeralds of extraordinary quality and without treatment, have achieved very high prices at auction. But these cases are exceptional and do not represent what happens with the majority of stones. Besides:

- **Treatments weigh a lot:** A stone that was thought to be untreated can lose much of its value if a laboratory detects a treatment.
- **The origin influences:** but it is a laboratory opinion that can be discussed.
- **Fashions change:** Highly sought-after colors or gems may no longer be sought after.

We see it in the guides on the [quality of colored gems](/es/calidad-de-las-gemas-de-color/) and the [gemology laboratory reports](/es/informes-de-laboratorio-gemologico/).

## Gold: a jewel is not the same as an ingot

Gold has a public international price, making it more transparent than gems. But a gold jewel is not an ingot: its price includes the work and design, which are normally not recovered when selling it, because the buyer usually pays only for the weight of the metal according to its law. We explain it in the guide on [gold, silver and platinum](/es/oro-plata-y-platino/).

## Signs of fraud

There are scams that use gems as a hook. Be suspicious if you find any of these signs:

| Sign | Why worry |
|---|---|
| Unsolicited calls or messages offering gems as an investment | It is a common fraud technique |
| Promises of guaranteed profitability | no one can guarantee the future price of a gem |
| Pressure to decide quickly | prevents you from consulting third parties |
| Gems sealed in plastic with certificate | they prevent examining the stone; the certificates may be from unknown laboratories |
| Appraisals much higher than the purchase price | They are usually inflated to give a sensation of profit |
| Repurchase promises from the seller himself | They are worthless if the company disappears |
| Companies without verifiable address | make any claim difficult |

If you have any questions, consult an independent gemologist and financial advisor before paying.

## What auctions say, and what they don't say

News about gems selling for record figures at large auctions fuels the idea that any stone can be revalued. But these pieces are exceptional in size, quality, rarity and history, they arrive at auctions with reports from the most recognized laboratories and attract a very small number of buyers. Their prices say nothing about what a commercial stone purchased at a jewelry store will be worth, just as the price of a famous painting says nothing about that of a reproduction.

## If you still want to buy thinking about the value

- **Buy only what you understand** and, if possible, with the advice of an independent gemologist.
- **Demand reports from recognized laboratories** and verify them.
- **Prefers pieces of exceptional quality** over quantities of common stones.
- **Calculate all costs** and time horizon.
- **Do not invest money you may need,** nor a significant part of your savings.
- **Consult an independent financial advisor** who does not earn commission for selling you the gem.

## Selling a gem: realistic expectations

| Channel | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Merchant or jeweler | speed | price below resale value |
| Consignment sale | best potential price | takes time and charges commission |
| Auction | access to many buyers for important parts | commissions and uncertain price |
| Sale between individuals | possible best price | security and fraud risks |

Prepare the laboratory report, the original invoice and quality photos. We also explain it in the guide on [inherited and second-hand jewelry](/es/joyas-heredadas-y-de-segunda-mano/).

## Buying for pleasure: the value that is guaranteed

The most sensible way to buy gems is usually to buy them for what they are: beautiful objects, with history and meaning, that are enjoyable to wear. If they also retain or increase their value over time, the better; but if not, the pleasure of using them will have already justified the purchase. We see it in the guide on [how to wear jewelry wisely](/es/como-llevar-joyas-con-criterio/).

## Frequent errors

- **Believe that gems always go up.**
- **Buy at retail** hoping to sell at a profit.
- **Trust inflated appraisals** or certificates from unknown laboratories.
- **Respond to telephone offers** for gems as an investment.
- **Invest necessary money** in illiquid assets.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are gems a good investment?

For most people, they are not a simple investment: they are illiquid, their valuation is complex and the difference between buying and selling is large. If you are interested, educate yourself thoroughly and consult independent professionals.

### Does a diamond retain its value?

Not necessarily. The diamond market has changed a lot, and when selling you usually receive significantly less than the retail purchase price.

### What do I do if I am offered gems over the phone as an investment?

Be suspicious. It is a common fraud technique. Pay nothing and consult an independent gemologist and advisor.

### How much will they give me if I sell a piece of jewelry?

It depends on the piece, but it is usually much less than what was paid. For gold jewelry, you often pay only the value of the metal.

## In summary

Gems are not a simple investment: they do not have a single public price, each stone is different, the valuation is partly subjective and the difference between the purchase and sale price is large. Add to that the costs of reporting, insurance, escrow and commissions, and a diamond market that has changed with lab-grown diamonds. Be wary of guaranteed returns, sealed gems, inflated appraisals and telephone offers. If you buy with value in mind, do so with independent knowledge and advice; and, above all, buy what you are going to enjoy.
