An inherited jewelry box can contain a little of everything: a grandmother's engagement ring, an aunt's gold earrings, a baptismal medal, pieces of jewelry from other decades and, perhaps, some truly valuable jewelry that no one can identify. Behind each piece there are memories, but also practical questions: what exactly is it? How much is it worth? Should it be fixed, transformed or sold? How can it be distributed fairly?

At the same time, more and more people buy second-hand jewelry, due to price, a taste for vintage or sustainability. In this guide you will see how to take the first steps with an inheritance, how to identify metals and stones, what types of valuation exist, when to restore, redesign or sell, how to distribute jewelry among heirs and what to check before buying used jewelry.

## First steps with an inheritance

1. **Don't throw anything away** before checking it: a piece that looks like jewelry could be gold, and vice versa.
2. **Make an inventory** with photos of each piece, front and back.
3. **Separate the pieces** into individual bags or compartments so they don't get scratched.
4. **Gather any documentation** that exists: invoices, certificates, letters or family stories.
5. **Do not clean with harsh products** or try to fix anything on your own.
6. **Consult a trusted gemologist or jeweler** to identify important pieces.

## Identify what you have

- **Metal marks:** Look for numbers such as 750, 585, 925 or carat letters, and manufacturer's marks. We explain it in the guide on [gold, silver and platinum](/es/oro-plata-y-platino/).
- **Stones:** Glass, doublets and antique synthetic stones are common in antique jewelry. Only a gemologist can identify them with certainty, as we see in the guide on [imitations and doublets](/es/imitaciones-y-dobletes/).
- **Old cuts:** Old cut diamonds, rose cuts or other shapes can indicate the era of the piece.
- **Signatures:** Some jewelry bears the name of a jewelry house or designer, which can greatly increase its value.
- **Condition:** worn claws, loose stones, visible repairs or welds.

## Three different values

The same jewel can have very different values depending on what it is valued for:

| Value type | What does it represent? | What is it used for? |
|---|---|---|
| Replacement value | what it would cost to buy a similar piece new in a store | insurance |
| Market value | what a willing buyer would pay a willing seller, under normal conditions | inheritances, sales between individuals, distributions |
| Liquidation or meltdown value | what they would pay for the piece to melt it down or resell it quickly | quick sales to merchants |

That's why it doesn't make sense to compare an insurance appraisal with a gold buying store's offer: they measure different things. We explain it in the guide on [jewelry insurance and appraisal](/es/seguro-y-tasacion-de-joyas/).

## Preserve, restore or transform

| Option | When does it make sense |
|---|---|
| Use it as is | if it is in good condition and you like it |
| Restore it | if it has historical or sentimental value and needs repairs |
| Redesign it | if you like the materials but not the design |
| Save it | if you don't use it but want to keep it for the family |
| sell it | If it has no sentimental value for anyone and you prefer its economic value |

## Restore with respect

A good restoration restores safety and shine without erasing the history of the piece:

- **Reinforce claws and check settings** to avoid losing stones.
- **Restring pearl necklaces** and beads.
- **Clean with gentle methods** appropriate for each material.
- **Avoid excessive bathing or polishing** on antique pieces, which can erase details and reduce their value.
- **Keep the original parts** that are replaced, such as stones or closures.

More tips in the guide on [jewelry maintenance and cleaning](/es/mantenimiento-y-limpieza-de-joyas/).

## Redesign a family jewel

Transforming the gold and stones of an inherited piece of jewelry into a new piece is a beautiful way to wear a family keepsake every day. Before melting, assess whether the piece has value as an antique: some jewelry is worth more in its entirety than its materials separately. If you decide to redesign, ask that the gold be weighed and the stones described on a receipt, as explained in the guide on [custom jewelry](/es/joyas-a-medida/).

## Distribute jewelry among heirs

Jewelry can be a source of tension in a family. Some ideas for a fair distribution:

- **Make a complete inventory** with photos and appraisals from the same appraiser.
- **Talks about sentimental values:** sometimes a piece matters a lot to one person and little to others.
- **Use a common market valuation** to compensate for differences.
- **Consult a legal professional** about your country's inheritance regulations if there are doubts or disagreements.

## Sell inherited jewelry

- **Know what you are selling:** the grade of the metal, the weight and, if any, the important stones with their report.
- **Ask for several offers** in different businesses.
- **Be wary of those who do not weigh in front of you** or do not explain how they calculate the price.
- **For signed, antique, or pieces with significant stones,** consider auctions or specialty dealers, who may pay more than the value of the metal.
- **Sell in established locations,** with identification document and receipt, never to street buyers.

Keep in mind that in many cases only the value of the metal will be paid, and that small stones may contribute little. We see it in the guide on [investing in gems: myths and realities](/es/invertir-en-gemas-mitos-y-realidades/).

## History also has value

Before deciding what to do with an inherited jewel, it is worth reconstructing its history: who wore it, on what occasions, where it came from. Ask older relatives, look for old photos in which it appears, and write down what you know in the inventory. This information not only excites: in antique or signed pieces, a documented provenance can increase their interest for collectors and auction houses. And if you decide to keep it, the written history will travel with the jewel to the next generation.

## Buy second-hand jewelry

Used jewelry can offer unique pieces, designs from other eras and good prices. Before purchasing, check:

| What to check | why |
|---|---|
| metal marks | confirm the law |
| Claws and settings | a used part may need repairs |
| Hoop thickness | heavily worn rings become thinner |
| stones | They may have been replaced by imitations or may be chipped |
| Repairs | previous welding and size adjustments |
| Documentation | invoice, report and origin |
| Return Policy | in case a gemologist detects something unexpected |

Also calculate the cost of cleaning, sizing or repair.

## Pawn shops and auctions

In many countries in the region, pawn shops and auctions are an important source of second-hand jewelry. In Mexico, for example, the National Monte de Piedad, a collateral lending institution with more than two centuries of history, sells unrecovered jewelry. When purchasing from these channels:

- **Check parts in person** when possible.
- **Read the conditions of sale,** which often do not include returns.
- **Ask for a written description** of the metal and stones.
- **Consider the cost** of taking the piece to a gemologist.

## A sustainable option

Wearing, refurbishing and purchasing second-hand jewelry is one of the most sustainable ways to enjoy jewelry: it requires no new mining and extends the life of materials that already exist. We explain it in the guide on [responsible gold and traceability](/es/oro-responsable-y-trazabilidad/).

## Frequent errors

- **Sell the first offer** without identifying or comparing.
- **Cast old pieces** that were worth more whole.
- **Clean with aggressive products** delicate jewelry.
- **Confusing the insurance appraisal** with the sales value.
- **Buy used jewelry** without checking settings or asking for a written description.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know if an inherited piece of jewelry is gold?

Look for sterling marks, such as 750 or 585, and consult a jeweler, who can check the metal with specific tests.

### Why am I being offered so little for a jewel that was valued much more?

Because the appraisal probably indicated the replacement value for insurance, while the offer reflects the liquidation or metal value.

### Is it advisable to transform an old jewel?

Depends. If it has historical or artistic value or a recognized signature, it may be worth more. If not, redesigning it is a good way to put it to use.

### Is it safe to buy second-hand jewelry?

Yes, if you buy from established businesses, check the condition of the part, ask for a written description and, for important parts, a laboratory report.

## In summary

When faced with an inheritance of jewelry, do not throw away or clean anything, take an inventory with photos and consult a gemologist to identify metals, stones and signatures. Distinguishes the replacement value, the market value and the liquidation value. Decide piece by piece whether to use it, restore it respectfully, redesign it, save it or sell it, and distribute it fairly with common valuations. When selling, compare offers at established businesses; When buying second-hand, check for brands, settings, stones and repairs, and ask for a written description. Reusing jewelry is also a sustainable option.
