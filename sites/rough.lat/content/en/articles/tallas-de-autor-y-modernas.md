For centuries, gem cutting sought above all one thing: to return as much light as possible with increasingly precise shapes. In recent decades, however, a trend of cutters began to treat the gem as a small sculpture: curved facets, grooves carved into the reverse, free shapes that follow the contour of the crystal, combinations of several stones fitted together like a mosaic. They are called author's carvings or fantasy carvings.

These pieces are not governed by tables of ideal proportions, but by the vision of the person who carves them. They can be extraordinary or failed, and valuing them requires looking in a different way. In this guide you will see what a designer carving is, what techniques are used, from concave facets to sculptural carvings and intarsia, how technology has changed the craft, how to judge the quality of a designer carving and what to keep in mind when setting, caring for and paying for it.

## What is an author's size?

A signature cut is a gem cut with its own, non-standard design that reflects the style of a specific cutter. It can be:

- **A creative variation** on a classic size.
- **A new geometric design**, calculated facet by facet.
- **A sculptural carving,** in which the stone is sculpted rather than faceted.
- **A free form,** that respects the natural contour of the glass.

They are often unique pieces or very short series, signed or recognizable by their style.

## Concave and curved facets

In traditional carving, all facets are flat. In concave carving, some facets are cut with cylindrical or conical wheels that leave slightly inward curved surfaces. The effect is a more intense and fragmented sparkle, with flashes that seem to multiply when the stone is moved. It requires specific machinery and technique and very careful polishing within each curve.

## Sculptural carvings and engravings

Some carvers work the gem like a sculptor:

- **Carvings with optical grooves and cuts:** lines carved on the back of a transparent stone that, seen from the front, multiply and create illusions of depth. A reference of this style was the German carver Bernd Munsteiner, author of the great Minas Gerais aquamarine known as Dom Pedro, which we mentioned in the guide on the [Minas Gerais gems](/es/gemas-de-minas-gerais-topacio-y-aguamarina/).
- **Engravings on the reverse:** images carved from behind in a transparent stone, which seem to float inside.
- **Cameos and engravings:** figures in relief or in hollow, a very old tradition that is still alive.

## Old ideas, new ways

| chess board | the crown is covered with small square facets instead of a table | mosaic shine, widely used in quartz and colored stones |

| Briolette | fully faceted drop | movement and light in earrings and pendants |

## Modern geometric shapes

Contemporary jewelry has popularized shapes such as the hexagon, kite, shield, bullet or trapezoid, often in staggered cuts that give an architectural look. Combined in the same piece, they allow very original compositions. They require custom settings, because they do not usually fit standard frames.

## free forms

In a free-form carving, the carver follows the natural contour of the glass or boulder, rather than imposing a shape. It is common in opals, bicolor tourmalines, quartz with inclusions or agates, where the interior drawing is the most valuable. Done well, a free form retains more weight and enhances the unique character of each stone. We see it in the guide on [Mexican fire opal](/es/opalo-de-fuego-mexicano/).

## Intarsia and inlay

Intarsia consists of cutting pieces of different stones and fitting them together precisely to form a drawing, like a stone mosaic. It is used in jewelry, boxes and decorative objects, and combines materials such as lapis lazuli, malachite, opal, mother-of-pearl or turquoise. The simplest inlay fits sheets of stone into a hole in the metal. We discuss it in the guide about [ornamental stone carvings](/es/tallas-en-piedras-ornamentales/).

## Technology at the service of the carver

- **Facet design programs:** allow you to calculate how light will behave in a carving before cutting it, and share diagrams between carvers.
- **Precision faceting machines:** with digital heads that help repeat angles and positions exactly.
- **Laser cutting and scanning:** common in the diamond industry, where they allow planning and cutting with great precision.

Technology has made very complex designs possible, but the eye, patience and experience of the carver remain irreplaceable.

## How to judge an author's size

1. **Does it make sense for that stone?** A good signature cut enhances the color, transparency or pattern of the gem; a bad one only complicates it.
2. **Execution:** clean lines, matching facets, regular curves, no splinters.
3. **Polishing:** bright also inside grooves and curves, which are the most difficult to polish.
4. **Light behavior:** without windows or dead zones that are not part of the design. We explain it in the guide on [brightness, windows and extinction](/es/brillo-ventanas-y-extincion/).
5. **Durability:** Very fine stones, with deep grooves or sharp points can be brittle.
6. **Signature and documentation:** Many signature carvers deliver the piece with their name and a description.

## Setting and caring for designer pieces

- **Custom setting:** Non-standard shapes require mounts made for them, usually in bezel or with adapted claws. We see it in the guide on [types of crimping](/es/tipos-de-engaste/).
- **Careful cleaning:** grooves and curves accumulate dirt; A soft brush and warm soapy water are usually sufficient for tough gems.
- **Protection:** prevents bumps on the edges and tips.
- **Repairs:** an author's carving is difficult to recarve without losing its essence; consult, if possible, the original carver.

## Author sizes in Latin America

The region, with its abundance of colored gems, is ideal terrain for this type of work. Some carvers, especially in Brazil, apply signature carvings to aquamarines, tourmalines, quartz and agates, materials that allow for large and transparent pieces. In Mexico, opal lends itself to free shapes that follow its play of colors, and in southern Brazil and Uruguay agates are cut into sheets and shapes that highlight their bands. The emerald, due to its fragility, lends itself less to risky designs, although there are engraved pieces and original carvings in suitable material.

## Order an author's carving

1. **Choose the stone with the cutter:** Not all gems work for any design.
2. **Ask for sketches or simulations** of the result before starting.
3. **Remember the expected weight loss:** Many designs sacrifice more material than a classic size.
4. **Ask about delivery times,** which are usually longer than a standard size.
5. **Think from the beginning about the jewel** where the stone will go and its setting.

## Value and price

The price of a designer cut depends not only on the gem and its weight, but also on:

- **Working hours** are often many more than in a standard size.
- **The lost material,** because some designs sacrifice a lot of weight.
- **The reputation of the carver** and the uniqueness of the piece.
- **The quality of the starting gem**.

That is why two stones of similar weight can have very different prices. When purchasing, value the piece as a work of art, not just a carat number.

## Frequent errors

- **Judge a designer cut with the criteria** of proportions of a round brilliant.
- **Buying a non-standard shape** without anticipating the cost of a custom frame.
- **Use very thin or pointed pieces** in rings for intense daily use.
- **Recut an author's piece** to "correct" it.
- **Paying for a complicated cut** that does not improve the stone.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a designer size worth more than a classic size?

It may be worth more for the workmanship and uniqueness, but it depends on the quality of the gem and the workmanship. Not every original size is better.

### Are concave facets brighter?

They tend to give a more intense and fragmented scintillation. The final result depends on the stone and the design.

### Can they be set in any jewel?

They usually need a frame made to measure, especially if their shape is not standard.

### What is a free form size?

A carving that follows the natural contour of the stone rather than imposing a geometric shape.

## In summary

The signature cuts treat the gem as a work: concave facets that multiply the sparkles, sculptural cuts and engravings on the reverse, modern geometric shapes, free shapes that respect the crystal and intarsias that combine several stones. Technology helps, but the talent of the carver remains decisive. Assess them by asking if the cut enhances the stone, checking the execution, polishing, light behavior and durability, plan for a custom setting and care for them gently. Its price reflects the workmanship and uniqueness, not just the weight.
