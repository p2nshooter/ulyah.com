When talking about a piece of jewelry, the conversation usually revolves around the stone: its color, its shine, its size. But the metal is half the piece. It holds the gem, defines the color of the set, determines how much it will hold up to daily wear, and in many jewelry without stones, it's all there is. Choosing the wrong metal can mean a ring that is deformed, a chain that darkens the skin, an annoying allergy or paying a premium for a simple bath.

In Latin America, very different jewelry traditions coexist: 18-karat gold from many jewelry stores, silver from Taxco or Peruvian workshops, the widespread use of alpaca in crafts and a growing supply of steel, titanium and other metals. In this guide you will see what the carats of gold mean, why gold has different colors, what rhodium plating is, how the purity of silver is measured, what platinum and palladium provide, what the difference is between a plating and a laminate, how to read the marks and which metal is suitable for each use.

## Carat: one word, two meanings

In Spanish, the word "carat" is used for two different things, and it is advisable not to confuse them:

- **In gems,** the carat is a unit of weight: 0.2 grams.
- **In gold,** the carat expresses purity: how many parts of gold there are in twenty-four parts of metal.

An "18-karat gold ring with a one-carat diamond" uses the same word to measure things that have nothing to do with each other.

## The law of gold

Pure gold is very soft and easily deformed, so in jewelry it is almost always alloyed with other metals, such as silver, copper, palladium or zinc. The proportion of gold determines its law.

| Carat | Gold in alloy | Usual mark in thousandths | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| 24 | practically pure | 999 | intense yellow color, very soft; little used in everyday jewelry |
| 22 | about 91.6% | 916 | very yellow, still soft; traditional in some regions of the world |
| 18 | 75 % | 750 | good balance between color, resistance and value; widely used in fine jewelry |
| 14 | about 58.5% | 585 | more resistant and somewhat less intense in color |
| 10 | about 41.7% | 417 | harder and more affordable, with less gold |
| 9 | 37,5 % | 375 | low grade, common in some markets |

In many countries in the region, fine gold jewelry is usually 18 karat, although 14 and 10 karat pieces are also found, especially in some markets.

## The colors of gold

The color of gold depends on the metals with which it is alloyed:

| Color | Usual alloy | Observations |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow | gold with silver and copper | the classic; the more carat, the more intense |
| Pink or red | gold with more copper | warm tone, very fashionable; something harder |
| White | gold with palladium or nickel | usually plated with rhodium for a brighter white |
| Green | gold with more silver | greenish-yellow hue, rare |

The same carat can have different colors: an 18-carat ring can be yellow, pink or white.

## White gold and rhodium

White gold is not completely white: it usually has a slightly grayish or yellowish tone. To give it a brilliant white, most white gold jewelry is plated with rhodium, a platinum group metal.

- **Rhodium wears down** with use, especially in rings, and the original tone of the gold appears again.
- **Can be reapplied** in a jewelry workshop, from time to time depending on use.
- **Alloys with nickel** may cause allergies in some people; alloys with palladium are an alternative.

## The silver

Pure silver is also too soft for most jewelry, and is mostly alloyed with copper.

| Denomination | Silver in alloy | Use |
|---|---|---|
| fine silver | 99,9 % | special pieces, filigree threads |
| 950 silver | 95 % | common in some workshops, like many in Peru |
| 925 sterling silver | 92,5 % | the most common in jewelry around the world |
| Alpaca or German silver | does not contain silver | copper, nickel and zinc alloy, widely used in crafts |

Nickel silver deserves a special warning: its name confuses many buyers, but it is a much cheaper, silver-free alloy that may contain nickel. It is not a scam if it is sold as such, but it should not be paid as silver.

Silver darkens over time as it reacts with sulfur in the air, forming a dark patina. Some pieces have it intentionally to highlight the reliefs. We see it in the guide on [Latin American silverwork and filigree](/es/plateria-y-filigrana-latinoamericana/).

## Platinum and palladium

- **Platinum:** natural white metal, very dense and resistant, which does not need rhodium plating. It is usually used in a law of 950 thousandths. With use it develops a matte patina that many appreciate and that can be re-polished. It is hypoallergenic for most people.
- **Palladium:** from the same group as platinum, white and lighter. It is used in white gold alloys and sometimes as the main metal of rings.

Platinum is heavier than gold: a platinum ring weighs significantly more than an identical gold ring, and its workshop work requires its own techniques.

## Bathrooms, laminates and vermeil

| Term | What is it | Durability |
|---|---|---|
| Gold plating or plating | a very thin layer of gold deposited on another metal | It wears out with use and friction |
| Vermeil | gold plating on silver | depends on the thickness; over time the silver appears |
| Laminated or gold filled | a much thicker layer of gold, mechanically bonded to a base metal | quite durable, although it is not solid gold |
| solid gold | the entire piece is made of the indicated alloy | permanent |

A gold-plated ring may be beautiful and inexpensive, but it does not have the value or durability of solid gold, and should not be sold as such.

## How to read the marks

Many pieces of jewelry have small marks engraved on them that inform about the metal:

| Brand | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 750 or 18K | 18 karat gold |
| 585 or 14K | 14 karat gold |
| 417 or 10K | 10 karat gold |
| 925 | sterling silver |
| 950 | 950 silver, or platinum if accompanied by "PT" or "Plat" |
| GP, HGE, RGP | indications of plating or plating, not solid gold |
| GF | laminated or gold filled |

Marking regulations vary from country to country and, in many cases, the mark is placed by the manufacturer themselves. A mark is not a substitute for a test: a jeweler can check the grade of the metal with specific methods. In Mexico, for example, many silver pieces also have a code that identifies the workshop.

## Allergies

Nickel allergy is one of the most common contact allergies. It can appear with white gold alloyed with nickel, with nickel silver, with jewelry and with some steels. If you have sensitive skin:

- **Prefer platinum, palladium, titanium** or high grade gold.
- **Ask about the white gold alloy**.
- **Be wary of baths** on unknown metals, because when they wear out they leave the skin in contact with the base metal.

## Which metal to choose depending on the use

| Use | Recommended options |
|---|---|
| Engagement ring or alliance for daily use | 18 or 14 karat gold, platinum |
| Jewelry with valuable gems | 18-karat gold or platinum, for the security of the setting |
| fashion jewelry | sterling silver, vermeil, quality plating |
| Very sensitive skin | platinum, titanium, high-grade gold without nickel |
| Intense manual work | hard metals, such as platinum or 14-karat gold, or removing rings |

## What do you pay for a precious metal jewel?

The price of a gold or silver piece of jewelry does not depend only on the weight of the metal. Includes:

- **The value of the metal,** which changes with the international price.
- **Labor,** which can be very important in handmade or complex pieces.
- **The design and the brand.**
- **The margins of trade.**

Therefore, when selling used jewelry, it is usual to only pay the value of the metal, well below the purchase price. We explain it in the guide on [inherited and second-hand jewelry](/es/joyas-heredadas-y-de-segunda-mano/).

## Care according to the metal

- **Gold:** Clean with warm water and mild soap; Avoid chlorine, which can weaken some alloys over time.
- **White gold:** check the rhodium and renew it when it wears out.
- **Silver:** store it in closed bags or with antioxidant strips and clean it with specific cloths.
- **Platinum:** can be polished in the workshop to regain shine.
- **Baths:** avoid perfumes, creams, water and friction to extend its life.

More tips in the guide on [jewelry maintenance and cleaning](/es/mantenimiento-y-limpieza-de-joyas/).

## Frequent errors

- **Confuse the carats of gold** with the carats of gems.
- **Pay silver price for alpaca.**
- **Believe that white gold is white** without rhodium.
- **Buy dipped pieces** believing they are solid.
- **Ignore an allergy** to nickel when choosing white gold or costume jewelry.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is 18 or 14 karat gold better?

The 18-karat has more gold and a more intense color; the 14 is somewhat harder and cheaper. Both are good options; It depends on taste, budget and use.

### Is 950 silver better than 925?

It has more money and is somewhat softer. Both are good; The difference is in the proportion of silver and in the tradition of each workshop.

### Why does my white gold ring look yellowish?

The rhodium plating has probably worn off. A jeweler can reapply it.

### Does platinum scratch?

Yes, like all metals, but instead of losing material it moves and forms a patina that can be polished.

## In summary

In gold, carats indicate purity: 18 carats are 75% gold and are the most common in fine jewelry, although there are also 14 and 10 carats. Their color depends on the alloy, and white gold usually has a rhodium plating that must be renewed. Sterling silver is 925, 950 has more silver and nickel silver does not contain silver. Platinum is white, dense and hypoallergenic. Distinguish solid gold from plating, laminated and vermeil, learn to read the marks, take into account allergies and choose the metal according to the use that the jewel will have.
