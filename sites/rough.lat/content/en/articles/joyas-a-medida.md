A custom piece of jewelry is much more than a whim. It allows you to turn an idea, a memory or a special stone into a piece that does not exist in any shop window: the engagement ring that combines a grandmother's emerald with a current design, the pendant that includes a family symbol, the wedding ring that fits perfectly with another ring or the piece that reuses gold from jewelry that no one uses anymore.

Ordering a piece of jewelry, however, is a process with several stages, technical decisions and conversations that should be prepared. A good result depends on both the talent of the jeweler and the clarity of the order. In this guide you will see why to order a custom piece of jewellery, how to prepare the first appointment, what the process is like step by step, what options there are for stones and for reusing family gold, how to compose a budget, what deadlines to expect and how to choose the right workshop.

## Why order a custom piece of jewelry?

- **A unique piece,** designed for a specific person.
- **A special stone,** that deserves a design at its height.
- **Reuse materials with history,** such as gold or inherited stones.
- **Perfect fit:** to size, lifestyle or other jewelry worn on the side.
- **Symbolism:** initials, dates, engravings or shapes with meaning.

## Before the first date

Arriving with organized ideas saves time and misunderstandings:

1. **Define a budget,** even if it is approximate, and say it from the beginning: determine the metal, the stones and the complexity.
2. **Gather references:** photos of jewelry you like and also those you don't.
3. **Think about the use:** a piece for every day needs a different design than one for special occasions.
4. **Take the person into account:** their style, the size of their hands, the jewelry they already wear, and whether they work with their hands.
5. **Know the measurements:** the size of the ring or the length of the chain. We explain it in the guide on [how to measure ring size](/es/medir-la-talla-de-anillo/).
6. **Set a realistic deadline,** if the piece is for a specific occasion.

## The step by step process

| Stage | What happens | What do you decide |
|---|---|---|
| Consultation | we talk about the idea, the use and the budget | the starting point |
| Sketch | the jeweler draws one or more proposals | the design direction |
| Technical design | defined precisely, often in a 3D design program | measurements, proportions, details |
| Model | a test is made in resin or wax | whether the shape and size are correct |
| Manufacturing | casting or hand metal work | final approval before this step |
| Setting | the stones are set | review of the result |
| Finish | polishing, textures, engravings, bathrooms if any | final details |
| Delivery | the jewel with its documentation | review and compliance |

## Sketch, digital design and model

Many workshops today work with three-dimensional design programs, which allow the jewel to be seen from all angles before manufacturing it. From this design, a model is printed in resin, which can be tested on the finger or on the body to check the size and proportions. Other jewelers prefer to carve the model by hand in wax, a traditional technique that gives pieces with a lot of character.

This is the best time to ask for changes: correcting the model costs little; correcting the finished jewelry may be expensive or impossible.

## Casting or hand work

- **Lost Wax Casting:** The wax or resin model is cast in a sturdy mold, removed with heat, and molten metal is poured in its place. It is the most common method for pieces with complex shapes.
- **Manufacturing by hand:** The jeweler cuts, bends, welds and forges metal sheets and threads. It gives very resistant pieces and allows techniques such as filigree.

Many jewelry combines both techniques. We see it in the guide on [Latin American silverwork and filigree](/es/plateria-y-filigrana-latinoamericana/).

## The stones: own or chosen with the jeweler

- **If you bring your own stone,** the jeweler must check it first: it may have chips, cracks or treatments that affect the setting. Ask for a receipt describing the stone when you drop it off.
- **If the jeweler gets it,** he asks to see several options, with his reports when the stone justifies it, and chooses before closing the design, because the shape and measurements of the stone determine the setting.

More information in the guides on [color gem quality](/es/calidad-de-las-gemas-de-color/) and [gemological laboratory reports](/es/informes-de-laboratorio-gemologico/).

## Reuse gold and family stones

Transforming inherited jewelry into a new piece is one of the most beautiful reasons to order a piece of jewelry, but it has its peculiarities:

- **Ancient gold may have different grades** and remains of welding. The jeweler can melt it down, sometimes refine it or mix it with new gold to achieve the necessary grade and quality.
- **There is always a small loss** of metal in casting and working.
- **Ask that the gold be weighed** in front of you and recorded on a receipt.
- **Old stones** may need cleaning, repolishing, or even recutting if damaged.
- **Before casting,** assess whether the piece has historical or period value: some antique jewelry is worth more whole than cast.

We also explain it in the guide on [inherited and second-hand jewelry](/es/joyas-heredadas-y-de-segunda-mano/).

## The budget and the advance

A clear budget usually breaks down:

- **The metal:** type, grade and estimated weight.
- **The stones:** each one with its description.
- **The design:** is sometimes charged separately, especially if there are several proposals.
- **Labor:** casting, assembly, crimping, finishing and engraving.
- **The changes:** What happens if you modify the design after you approve it.

It is common to pay an advance when approving the design, especially if the workshop must purchase metal or stones. Ask that the budget, advance payment and conditions be in writing.

## Deadlines

A custom-made piece of jewelry can take from a few weeks to several months, depending on the complexity, the search for stones, and the workload of the workshop. Special dates, such as the end of the year or the wedding season, tend to saturate the workshops. If the piece is for a specific occasion, start early and agree on a delivery date with margin.

## How to choose the workshop

- **Look at previous works:** photos of real pieces, not just renders.
- **Ask who manufactures:** some jewelers make everything in their workshop and others order parts from specialists; Both options are valid if there is quality control.
- **Value communication:** a good jeweler listens, proposes and clearly explains what is possible and what is not.
- **Ask about the guarantee** of the work and subsequent maintenance.
- **Ask for transparency** about the origin of the metal and stones, if you care. The guide on [responsible gold and traceability](/es/oro-responsable-y-trazabilidad/) gives ideas.

## Inspiration yes, copies no

Bringing reference photos is useful, but exactly copying a brand's or another creator's protected design raises ethical and legal issues. A good jeweler will take your references as a starting point to create something of their own. In the end, the value of a custom piece of jewelry is precisely that it is unique.

## When picking up the jewel

1. **Check it calmly** and in good light: firm stones, clean finishes, correct engravings.
2. **Try it on** and check that the size or length is as agreed.
3. **Ask for the invoice** with the complete description of the metal and stones.
4. **Collect the reports** of the stones, if any, and the guarantee.
5. **Ask about care** and the recommended first check-up.

More tips in the guide on [jewelry maintenance and cleaning](/es/mantenimiento-y-limpieza-de-joyas/).

## Frequent errors

- **Do not say the budget** from the beginning.
- **Approve the design without seeing a full-scale model**.
- **Request changes when the part is already manufactured.**
- **Leave gold or stones without a receipt** in the workshop.
- **Order a piece at the last minute for a specific date.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a custom piece of jewelry much more expensive?

Not necessarily. It depends on the design, the metal and the stones. A simple custom piece can cost the same as a store-bought piece of similar quality, and reusing your own materials can reduce the cost.

### Can I use gold from several old jewelry?

In general, yes. The jeweler can melt it and, if necessary, refine it or mix it with new gold. There will always be a small loss of material.

### How long does it take?

From a few weeks to several months, depending on the complexity and availability of stones and the workshop.

### What happens if I don't like the result?

That is why it is so important to approve the design and model before manufacturing. Agree in writing what happens if the part does not conform to what was approved.

## In summary

Ordering a custom piece of jewelry allows you to create a unique piece, show off a special stone or give new life to familiar materials. Prepare the first appointment with a budget, references, measurements and a realistic date. Follow the process in stages, from sketch to technical design, model, fabrication, setting and finishing, and approve each step before the next. Check your own stones, weigh the gold you deliver, ask for a quote and conditions in writing, and choose a workshop that communicates well and supports its work. Get inspired by other pieces, but look for your own design.
