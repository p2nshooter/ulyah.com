For much of the 20th century, men's jewelry in many countries was reduced to three pieces: the wedding ring, the watch and, on formal occasions, cufflinks. However, history tells otherwise. Kings, warriors, priests and merchants of all cultures have worn rings, necklaces, bracelets and ornaments made of gold, silver and stones. In pre-Columbian cultures, high-ranking men wore gold pectorals, nose rings, and earflaps. Today, men's jewelry is back with a vengeance, and with it the doubts: what pieces to choose, how to combine them and how to avoid the set being excessive.

In this guide you will see the main pieces of men's jewelry, which metals and gems best withstand active use, how to choose proportions according to the hand and style, what to wear on each occasion and how to care for these jewelry.

## The main parts

### The signet ring

It is probably the male jewel with the most history. For centuries it literally served to seal documents: the engraving on its surface was printed on hot wax. Today it is worn as a family symbol, as a souvenir or simply for style.

- **Surface:** smooth, with initials, with a shield or with a flat stone.
- **Engraving:** in relief or indentation. An intaglio engraving, inverted, allows for sealing even today.
- **Common stones:** onyx, carnelian, lapis lazuli, heliotrope, hematite or ornamental stones from the region.
- **Finger:** the little finger is the traditional one in many cultures, but there is no fixed rule.

If you are attracted to the art of engraving stones for seals, the guide on [gem engraving, cameos and engravings](/es/grabado-de-gemas-camafeos-y-entalles/) tells its history and technique.

### The alliance and other rings

The wedding ring is the most common men's jewelry. For the man who uses his hands at work or in sports, it is advisable to choose comfortable profiles and resistant metals. We explain it in detail in the guide on [marriage rings](/es/alianzas-de-matrimonio/). In addition to the wedding ring, we are increasingly seeing plain designer rings, rings with a discreet gem, or textured bands.

### Chains and pendants

- **Thin chains** worn under the shirt or with a medal.
- **Medium or thick chains** of barbed, forced or Venetian links, visible.
- **Pendants:** religious medals, pendants with personal meaning, rough stones, ancient coins.

The most common length for men is between 50 and 60 cm, although it depends on the neck and style. In the guide on [earrings, necklaces and bracelets](/es/aretes-collares-y-pulseras/) you will find the most secure link types and closures.

### Bracelets

- **Link chains** in silver or gold.
- **Rigid bangles or bracelets**, smooth or engraved.
- **Beaded bracelets** made of stones such as tiger's eye, onyx, lava or wood.
- **Braided leather bracelets** with metal details.

A good rule to combine them with the watch: either on the opposite wrist, or on the same one if they are thin and do not rub the dial or the strap.

### Cufflinks, barrettes and pins

**Cufflinks** are the classic detail of the formal double cuff shirt. The **tie clips** keep the tie in place and the **lapel pins** add a personal touch to the jacket. They are the most suitable pieces for incorporating gems or striking designs in a formal context, because they are rarely seen and at specific times.

### Earrings

Men's earrings have a long history in many cultures and are common today. Discreet buttons, small hoops, and single-sided earrings are the most common styles.

## Metals for active use

| metal | Advantages | Limits |
|---|---|---|
| 14 or 18 karat gold | warm color, easily repaired and adjusted | gets scratched; the 18 is softer |
| White gold | silver appearance, resistant | the rhodium plating is renewed from time to time |
| Platinum | very dense and durable | high price, considerable weight |
| Sterling silver | economical, versatile, lends itself to textures | darkens and scratches easily |
| Steel, titanium, tungsten | resistant and economical | many cannot adjust the size |

The guide to [gold, silver and platinum](/es/oro-plata-y-platino/) explains the laws and trademarks.

## Gems that endure the day to day

If the jewel is going to receive knocks, it is advisable to choose hard and tenacious gems:

- **sapphire and ruby**, hardness 9;
- **diamond**, very hard although it can chip with a sharp blow to the edge;
- **spinel and chrysoberyl**, hardness 8 and 8.5;
- **onyx and other chalcedonies**, very tenacious and therefore traditional in signet rings;
- **maroons**, suitable for moderate use.

Soft or exfoliated gemstones, such as opal, turquoise, malachite, or tanzanite, look best in cufflinks, pendants, or rings for occasional wear. For comparison, see the guide on [hardness, toughness and stability](/es/dureza-tenacidad-y-estabilidad/) and [spinel, garnet and other alternative gemstones](/es/espinela-granate-y-gemas-alternativas/).

**Bezel**, flush, or rail settings protect stones better than tall prong settings, and tend to fit better with an understated masculine aesthetic. The guide on [setting types](/es/tipos-de-engaste/) compares them.

## Proportion and style

- **Large hands:** accept wide and voluminous rings; a very thin ring can be lost.
- **Small hands or short fingers:** thin, low-profile rings are best.
- **Wide wrists:** medium or thick link bracelets; on thin wrists, light chains.
- **Neck:** Thick chains call for a neck and build that balances them.

Some combination guidelines:

- **The same tone of metal** throughout the whole gives an orderly image. Mixing gold and silver works if done intentionally, for example in two clearly separate pieces.
- **One main piece** per outfit: a statement signet ring or a visible chain, not all at once.
- **Consistency with the watch:** its metal and style determine the rest.

The guide on [how to wear jewelry wisely](/es/como-llevar-joyas-con-criterio/) expands on these ideas.

## What to wear depending on the occasion

| Chance | Tip |
|---|---|
| office work | alliance, discreet ring, watch, thin chain under the shirt |
| Manual work or sport | few or no pieces; remove rings to avoid injuries |
| formal ceremony | cufflinks, tie bar, wedding ring, dress watch |
| Leisure | beaded or leather bracelets, visible chains, designer rings |

A safety note that is not often told: rings can get caught on machinery, fences or sports equipment and cause serious finger injuries. In manual work or contact sports, the most prudent thing is to remove them.

## Jewelry with meaning

Many men's jewels are chosen for what they represent rather than for their economic value, and it is advisable to think about them carefully:

- **Inherited pieces:** A grandfather's signet ring or a family medal can be adapted to the current size and taste without losing its history. Sometimes it is enough to adjust the size, change the chain or renew a worn engraving. The guide on [heirloom and second-hand jewelry](/es/joyas-heredadas-y-de-segunda-mano/) explains how to do this with respect for the piece.
- **Personal milestones:** Graduations, anniversaries, the birth of a child or the first job are common occasions to give or treat yourself to a piece of jewelry.
- **Religious or cultural symbols:** crosses, medals, pieces with motifs of native peoples. If you choose a design from a culture that is not yours, informing yourself about its meaning and buying it from its artisans is a form of respect.

Meaningful jewelry doesn't need to be big or expensive: it needs to be well made to last.

## Buy wisely

1. **Ask for the grade of the metal and whether the piece is solid or hollow**, especially in thick chains, which can seem heavy without being so.
2. **Check clasps** – A sturdy carabiner is best for chains and bracelets.
3. **Confirm the species of the gem** and if it has treatments.
4. **Ask if the ring can be sized** in the future.
5. **For signet rings with shield or initials**, review the sketch before engraving: errors are not erased.

## Care

- remove rings for gym, gardening or tool work;
- Clean parts with warm water and mild soap;
- silver darkens: a specific polishing cloth recovers it;
- Leather and bead bracelets should be kept away from water and excessive sweat;
- Store each piece separately.

More tips in the [jewelry maintenance and cleaning] guide (/es/mantenimiento-y-limpieza-de-joyas/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Which finger is the signet ring worn on?

Traditionally on the little finger, but today it is also worn on the ring finger or index finger. The important thing is that it is comfortable and does not get in the way.

### Can I mix gold and silver?

Yes, if it is done with intention. For example, a steel watch with a gold wedding band works well. What you should avoid is accumulating many pieces of different metals without a common thread.

### Which gemstone is best for an everyday men's ring?

Hard and tenacious gems, such as sapphire, spinel or onyx, in a protective setting.

More guides on metals, settings and design in the [Jewelry and design] section (/es/categoria/joyeria-y-diseno/).

## In summary

Men's jewelry has a long history and is making a strong comeback: signet rings, designer wedding bands and rings, chains and pendants, bracelets, cufflinks, barrettes and earrings. To choose well, you should think about the use: resistant metals that can be repaired, hard gems such as sapphire, spinel or onyx, and protective settings. The proportion with the hand, wrist and neck, a coherent metal tone and a single protagonist piece per set give a careful image. And in manual work or sports, it is safest to remove the rings.
