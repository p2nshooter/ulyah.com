A precious ring that falls off your finger when washing your hands, or that doesn't fit past your knuckle, is a small domestic tragedy. Getting the size right seems simple, but it is not: the fingers change size throughout the day and with the temperature, the knuckles can be wider than the base of the finger, a wide band is tighter than a thin one and, to complicate everything, each country uses its own numbering system.

The good news is that with a few precautions it can be measured very reliably. In this guide you will see why measuring in millimeters is the safest, how the different sizing systems are related, how to measure with a jewelry sizer or with a ring that already fits you, what factors change the size, what to keep in mind with wide bands and large knuckles, and when you can adjust an ill-fitting ring.

## Why is it so hard to get it right?

- **The fingers change:** they swell in the heat, in the afternoon, after exercising or eating salty food, and they shrink in the cold.
- **The knuckle rules:** the ring should fit over the knuckle, even if it then remains somewhat loose at the base of the finger.
- **Every hand is different:** the dominant hand usually has slightly thicker fingers.
- **The width of the band influences:** a wide band feels tighter than a thin one of the same size.
- **Sizing systems do not match** between countries or among some jewelers.

## Diameter and circumference: the measurement that does not deceive

Since numbering systems vary, it is safest to work with a physical measurement:

- **Inner diameter:** the distance from one side to the other of the inside of the ring, in millimeters.
- **Inner circumference:** The inner circumference of the ring, in millimeters.

With either, a jeweler can find the equivalent size in their system. In the region, American numbering, European numbering, which is based on the circumference in millimeters, and different local systems coexist, so it is advisable to always ask for the equivalence in millimeters.

## Approximate equivalences between systems

| inner diameter | Inner circumference | US size | European size |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15.7mm | 49.3mm | 5 | 49 |
| 16.5mm | 51.9mm | 6 | 52 |
| 17.3mm | 54.4mm | 7 | 54 |
| 18.1mm | 57.0mm | 8 | 57 |
| 18.9mm | 59.5mm | 9 | 59 to 60 |
| 19.8mm | 62.1mm | 10 | 62 |
| 20.6mm | 64.6mm | 11 | 65 |
| 21.4mm | 67.2mm | 12 | 67 |

They are approximate values. Between one US size and the next there are about eight tenths of a millimeter in diameter, and that is why there are half sizes. Always confirm the equivalence with the jeweler.

## How to measure with a jewelry sizer

The most reliable way is to try on measuring rings, the set of size rings that jewelry stores have.

1. **Measure at the end of the day,** with a comfortable temperature, when the finger is in an intermediate size.
2. **Try several sizes** until you find the one that passes the knuckle with slight resistance and does not rotate too much.
3. **Use a sizer that is the same width** as the ring you are purchasing, if possible.
4. **Repeat the measurement** another day to confirm.

## How to measure a ring that already fits you

If you have a ring that fits perfectly on that same finger:

- **Measure the inside diameter** with a ruler or, better yet, a caliper, from inside edge to inside edge, at the widest part.
- **Take it to the jewelry store,** where they will put it through a size measuring cone.
- **If it is for a surprise,** you can place the borrowed ring on a piece of paper and mark the inside outline with a fine pencil.

Please note that if the reference ring has a very different width than the new one, the size may vary slightly.

## The thread or strip of paper method: with caution

Wrapping a piece of thread or a strip of paper around your finger, marking the joining point, and measuring the length with a ruler gives the approximate circumference. It is useful as a first orientation, but it has limitations:

- **The thread stretches** and the paper wrinkles, which alters the measurement.
- **It's easy to overtighten** or too little.
- **Does not take into account the knuckle.**

If you use this method, measure several times, circle your finger at the base, and check that the measurement would fit over the knuckle. Printable meters have an added problem: if the printer changes the scale, the measurement will be wrong.

## Factors that change size

| factor | Effect | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Heat | fingers swell | do not measure after exercise or in high heat |
| cold | fingers are reduced | do not measure with cold hands |
| time of day | in the morning they are usually thinner | measure in the afternoon or at the end of the day |
| Pregnancy | fingers may temporarily swell | consider waiting or using a string |
| Weight changes | size may vary | Check size before final adjustments |
| Arthritis or wide knuckles | the ring must pass the knuckle | consult special solutions |

## Wide, thin and comfort bands

- **Wide bands,** larger than about six millimeters, usually require half a size larger than a thin band.
- **Thin bands** feel looser for the same size.
- **Comfort bands,** with a rounded interior, slide more easily over the knuckle and may feel slightly looser.

Therefore, if possible, measure with a ring measuring the width of the final ring.

## Large knuckles and other special situations

When the knuckle is much wider than the base of the finger, a ring that passes the knuckle can twist and become loose. Some common solutions:

- **Small metal balls** welded inside the ring, which reduce the space without changing the nominal size.
- **Hoops with hinge or closure,** that open to pass the knuckle.
- **A slightly square interior shape,** that rotates less.

A jeweler can recommend the most appropriate option for each case.

## Change a ring size

Most gold, silver or platinum rings can be made larger or smaller in a workshop by cutting the ring and adding or removing metal, within certain limits. But it is not always possible or advisable:

| Ring type | Ease of changing size |
|---|---|
| Smooth precious metal hoop | overall simple, within a few sizes |
| Solitaire with stone on top | normally possible |
| Ring with stones throughout the band | very difficult or impossible without redoing it |
| tension crimp | very complicated, because it alters the hold of the stone |
| Invisible crimping or full rail | complex and risky |
| Steel, titanium or tungsten carbide rings | often cannot be adjusted |

Additionally, if the ring has heat-sensitive or treated stones, the jeweler may have to disassemble them before using the torch. Inform him of the nature of the stones, as we explain in the guide on [types of setting](/es/tipos-de-engaste/).

## Temporary solutions

If a ring is a little too big for you and you don't want to adjust it yet, there are silicone or metal adjusters that fit inside the ring. They are useful temporarily, but can scratch metal or be uncomfortable in the long term.

## Frequent errors

- **Measure with cold hands** or after exercising.
- **Forget the knuckle** when choosing the size.
- **Use a printable gauge** without checking the print scale.
- **Do not take into account the width** of the band.
- **Buying a ring with stones throughout the band** without being sure of the size.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it better for the ring to fit a little tight or a little loose?

It should pass the knuckle with slight resistance and not turn too much once put on. If you hesitate between two sizes, the larger size is usually safer, because a tight ring can be uncomfortable in the heat.

### How many sizes can a ring be enlarged?

It depends on the design and the metal. Plain hoops usually allow adjustments of a few sizes; rings with stones in the band, very little or none.

### Does the size change from one finger to another?

Yes, and also from one hand to the other. Always measure the finger on which the ring will be worn.

### How do I find out the size for a surprise?

Borrow a ring that the person wears on that finger, mark it on a piece of paper, or take it to a jewelry store. We explain it in the guide about [engagement rings](/es/anillos-de-compromiso/).

## In summary

To get the size of a ring right, work with measurements in millimeters, diameter or inner circumference, and ask the jeweler for the equivalence in their system. Measure with measuring rings at the end of the day and in a comfortable temperature, make sure the ring passes the knuckle and take into account the width of the band. The thread method is for guidance only. Most precious metal hoops can be adjusted a few sizes, but rings with full-band stones, tension settings, and some modern metals are difficult or impossible to adjust, so you want to get it right from the start.
