Many people discover that their jewelry was not well protected just when they lose it. An engagement ring dropped on the beach, a home stolen, or a suitcase lost on a trip can become a double loss if there is no insurance, if the policy had unknown limits, or if there is no documentation to prove what you had and how much it was worth.

Protecting valuable jewelry is not complicated, but it requires a few steps: an appraisal by an independent professional, an appropriate policy, photos and documents kept in a safe place, and a periodic review. In this guide you will see what an appraisal is and how it differs from a laboratory report, what types of value exist, what a good appraisal should include, how to choose an appraiser, what insurance options there are, what they usually cover, what underinsurance is, how to document your jewelry and what to do if it is stolen or lost.

## What is an appraisal

An appraisal is a document in which a professional describes a piece of jewelry and assigns a value to it for a specific purpose, such as insuring it, distributing an inheritance, or selling it. It is not the same as a laboratory report:

- **The laboratory report** identifies the stone, indicates whether it is natural or synthetic and what treatments it has; He doesn't put a price on it. We explain it in the guide on [gemology laboratory reports](/es/informes-de-laboratorio-gemologico/).
- **The appraisal** describes the complete piece of jewelry, metal and stones included, and estimates its value based on purpose.

In important pieces, the appraiser usually relies on the laboratory report of the main stone.

## Value Types

| Value type | What is it for? |
|---|---|
| Replacement value | insure the jewel: what it would cost to replace it with a similar new one |
| Market value | inheritance distributions, sales between individuals, donations |
| Liquidation value | quick sales to dealers, often close to the value of the metal |

The same jewel can have very different values depending on the type of appraisal. We see it in more detail in the guide on [inherited and second-hand jewelry](/es/joyas-heredadas-y-de-segunda-mano/).

## What a good appraisal should include

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purpose | What is the appraisal done for and what type of value does it indicate? |
| Metal Description | type, grade, color and weight |
| Description of the stones | species, measurements, estimated or real weight, estimated quality and known treatments |
| Photographs | of the complete piece and important details |
| Brands and signatures | sterling seals, maker's or designer's marks |
| Status | wear, repairs or damage |
| Date and details of the appraiser | name, training, signature and contact information |
| Supporting documents | lab reports, invoices |

A vague appraisal, which only says "gold ring with stone" and a figure, will not be of much use to an insurer.

## How to choose an appraiser

- **Gemological training:** seeks professionals with recognized gemology studies and experience in jewelry.
- **Independence:** the ideal is that the appraiser has no interest in buying or selling the piece to you.
- **Form of charging:** A serious appraiser charges a fixed or time-based fee, not a percentage of the appraised value, which could encourage inflated valuations.
- **Transparency:** should explain how the figure was arrived at and what type of value it indicates.

## How often to update

Prices of gold, gems and labor change. It is a good idea to update appraisals of important jewelry every few years, or sooner if gold or gem prices change a lot, and whenever you make a major purchase, renovation, or receive an inheritance.

## Insurance options

| Option | How it works | To take into account |
|---|---|---|
| home insurance | some policies include jewelry within the contents | They usually have low limits per piece or in total and require safety measures |
| Extension of the home policy | specific jewels are declared with their value | better coverage, with an additional premium |
| Specific jewelry policy | insurance dedicated to valuable jewelry | broader coverage, also away from home and traveling |

Conditions vary greatly from one country and one insurer to another. Ask for the condition in writing and read it calmly, or consult an independent insurance broker.

## What they usually cover and what they don't

Before hiring, check:

- **Risks covered:** robbery, theft, loss, accidental damage or unexplained disappearance.
- **Place:** only at home or also outside, and if it includes trips abroad.
- **Limits per piece** and totals.
- **Deductible or deductible:** the part of the damage that you pay.
- **Form of compensation:** replacement for a similar piece or payment in money.
- **Exclusions:** wear, stones lost from claws in poor condition, jewelry left unattended or outside the safe when required.
- **Requirements:** appraisal, invoices, photos, safe or alarm for certain values.

## Underinsurance and overinsurance

- **Underinsurance:** If you insure a jewel for less than its real value, in the event of an accident you may receive reduced compensation, and in some policies a proportional reduction is also applied to partial damages.
- **Over insurance:** Insuring above the actual value does not usually make you charge more, because the insurance compensates the actual damage, but you do pay a higher premium.

The correct thing to do is to insure for the updated replacement value.

## Document your jewelry

1. **Photograph each piece of value** from various angles, with detail of marks and stones, on a neutral background.
2. **Save invoices,** appraisals and laboratory reports.
3. **Make a list** with the description of each jewel and its value.
4. **Keep digital copies** in a safe place away from home, such as a cloud service or a family member's home.
5. **Update inventory** when you buy, sell or transform a piece.

## Safety at home

Insurance compensates for a loss, but it is best to avoid it:

- **Store valuables in a safe** anchored to the wall or floor, and not in places anyone would check first, like the nightstand drawer.
- **Separate everyday jewelry** from important pieces that you rarely use.
- **Be discreet on social media:** Posting photos of valuable jewelry along with your location or travels can attract unwanted attention.
- **Check your policy requirements,** which sometimes requires a safe or alarm above a certain value.

## Appraisals for inheritances and distributions

When jewelry must be distributed among heirs or its value declared, the appraisal must indicate the market value, not the replacement value. Ask the appraiser to make it clear in the document and, if there are disagreements, for all parties to accept the same professional.

## If a theft or loss occurs

1. **Notify the police** and file a report in case of theft; the insurer will ask for it.
2. **Communicate the incident to the insurer** as soon as possible, within the policy term.
3. **Gather documentation:** photos, appraisals, invoices and reports.
4. **If it is a loss,** write down where and when it occurred and what steps you took to search for it.
5. **Keep a copy** of all communications.

## travel jewelry

- **Take only the jewelery you are going to wear** and, if it is valuable, check that your insurance covers it away from home and abroad.
- **Take them in your hand luggage,** never in your checked suitcase.
- **Use the property's safe**.
- **Have photos and documentation on hand** in digital format.

## Frequent errors

- **Assume home insurance covers all jewelry.**
- **Do not update appraisals** in years.
- **Insure for less than actual value** to pay less premium.
- **Do not have photos or invoices** of the parts.
- **Traveling with valuable jewelry** without checking coverage outside the home.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to have all my jewelry appraised?

No. It is advisable to appraise pieces of important economic or sentimental value. For costume jewelery and low value jewellery, photos and invoices are sufficient.

### Who can do an appraisal?

A professional with gemological training and experience in the jewelry market, preferably independent of the seller and the buyer.

### Why is my insurance appraisal so much higher than what I was offered when selling?

Because the insurance appraisal indicates the replacement value, what it would cost to buy a similar new piece, while when selling you receive a much lower market or liquidation value.

### Does insurance cover a stone that falls out of the setting?

It depends on the policy. Many exclude losses due to wear or lack of maintenance, so it is advisable to check the settings regularly, as we explain in the guide on [types of settings](/es/tipos-de-engaste/).

## In summary

To protect your valuable jewelry, have an appraisal done by an independent professional who describes the piece in detail and makes it clear what type of value it indicates; update it every few years. Check if your home insurance covers jewelry and within what limits, and consider an extension or a specific policy for important pieces. Insure yourself for the replacement value, document each piece of jewelry with photos, invoices, appraisals and reports stored away from home, and, if you suffer a theft or loss, report and notify the insurer as soon as possible.
