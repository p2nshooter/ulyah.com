Opening a geode is like looking into a miniature cathedral. On the outside it looks like a gray and rough stone; Inside, hundreds of violet crystals shine as if someone had arranged them by hand. Some of the most spectacular amethyst geodes in the world come from a territory shared by northern Uruguay and southern Brazil, where enormous lava flows poured out more than one hundred million years ago. Today, these geodes decorate homes, hotels and museums all over the planet, and some are so large that a person can fit inside.

Behind its beauty there is a fascinating geological history and a very particular mining profession. In this guide you will see what a geode is, how those in the region were formed, how those from Artigas differ from those from Rio Grande do Sul, what makes amethyst valuable as a gem, why so much citrine is actually heated amethyst, and how to buy, transport, and care for a geode.

## What is a geode

A geode is a cavity inside a rock, lined with crystals that grow towards the center. It is worth distinguishing some terms:

- **Geode:** hollow cavity with crystals inside.
- **Nodule:** completely filled cavity, without central gap; many agates are nodules.
- **Druse:** surface covered with small crystals, often a fragment of geode.
- **Chapel or cathedral:** name given to very large, elongated geodes, which are displayed standing.

## Lava, gas and water: how they were born

In the south of Brazil and the north of Uruguay, basalts emerge from one of the largest volcanic provinces on the planet, formed about one hundred and thirty million years ago, when South America and Africa were beginning to separate. The process that gave rise to geodes can be summarized in several stages:

| Stage | What happened |
|---|---|
| lava flows | Huge volumes of basalt covered the region layer upon layer |
| gas bubbles | The gases trapped in the lava formed cavities, some very large |
| First layer | solutions rich in silica deposited a crust of chalcedony or agate on the walls |
| Crystals | Over time, quartz crystals grew inward; where there was iron and natural radiation, quartz became amethyst |
| Other minerals | Crystals of calcite, gypsum or other minerals later appeared in some geodes. |

We explain it with more context in the guide on [how gems are formed](/es/como-se-forman-las-gemas/).

## Artigas, Uruguay

In the Artigas department of northern Uruguay, amethyst is extracted from basalt layers in rural areas known for their mines, such as the Catalán region. Uruguayan geodes are famous for the deep color of their crystals, often a very intense violet, although their crystals are usually smaller and tighter than those of many Brazilian geodes.

Mining is frequently done in galleries excavated in the basalt or in the open, following the level where the cavities appear. Removing a large geode without breaking it requires patience: you drill a small hole to see inside and, if it's worth it, carefully free the geode from the rock.

## Rio Grande do Sul, Brazil

Across the border, in the Brazilian state of Rio Grande do Sul, the municipality of Ametista do Sul bears the gem in its own name and is one of the world's great centers for amethyst geodes. Geodes of all sizes are extracted there, including large chapels that are displayed vertically. The region also produces enormous quantities of agate, especially around cities such as Salto do Jacuí.

## Uruguay and Brazil: general differences

| Appearance | Artigas Geodes | Geodes of Rio Grande do Sul |
|---|---|---|
| Color | often very intense violet | very variable, from light lilac to deep violet |
| Crystal size | often small and dense | often larger |
| Geode size | from small to large | from small to huge chapels |
| Production | smaller | very abundant |

They are trends, not rules: on both sides of the border there are geodes of very different qualities, and it is advisable to judge each piece for itself.

## Amethyst as a gem

Amethyst is the violet variety of quartz, with a hardness of 7 which makes it suitable for everyday jewelry. In addition to geodes for decoration, the cleanest and best colored material is carved for jewelry. When evaluating it, it is observed:

- **Color:** intense and saturated violets, sometimes with reddish or bluish flashes, are the most appreciated; The very pale lilacs are worth less, although they have their audience.
- **Color Distribution:** Many amethysts show lighter and darker areas of color; A good size hides them.
- **Purity:** Stones that are clean to the naked eye are preferred in jewelry.

It is the traditional stone of February, as we tell in the guide on [birthstones](/es/piedras-de-nacimiento/). To understand how a colored gem is judged, see the guide on [quality of colored gems](/es/calidad-de-las-gemas-de-color/).

## Heat: amethyst to citrine

When amethyst is heated, its color changes. Depending on the material and temperature, it can turn yellow, orange or reddish, and this is how a good part of the citrine sold around the world is obtained. Some Brazilian amethysts turn green when heated, and are sold under the name prasiolite or green quartz.

- **It is a common and stable treatment,** but it must be declared.
- **Natural citrine exists,** but it is less common and usually has softer tones.
- **"Citrine" geodes** with intense orange crystals at the tip and whitish base are usually heated amethyst.

More information in the guide on [gem treatments](/es/tratamientos-de-gemas/).

## Dyed agates and other products from the region

In the markets of the region and in stores around the world, agate sheets and objects in very bright colors, blue, pink, green or violet, abound. Most are dyed: the natural agate from the area usually has gray, white, brown and orange tones. Dyeing is a traditional and accepted practice on decorative objects, as long as the buyer is informed.

## Buy a geode

- **Look inside in good light:** shiny crystals, no broken tips or dull areas.
- **Look for repairs:** glued glass, resin filling or paint in damaged areas.
- **Be wary of colors that are too uniform** or exaggerated, which may indicate dye or paint.
- **Check the base:** Geodes for free-standing display should have a stable base, sometimes made of cement or metal.
- **Ask about the origin** and request an invoice.
- **Plan for transportation:** Large geodes weigh a lot and require professional packaging.
- **Learn about exporting:** Removing minerals from a country may require documentation; Check current regulations before purchasing large pieces.

The guide on [buy raw gems without deception](/es/comprar-gemas-en-bruto-sin-enganos/) expands on these tips.

## Caring for and displaying a geode

- **Avoid direct sunlight and heat:** Amethyst may lose color with prolonged exposure.
- **Remove dust with a soft brush** or low pressure air.
- **Wash it with water and without chemicals** if necessary, and let it dry well. If you have calcite crystals, avoid any acidic products, because the calcite dissolves.
- **Protects the tips** of the crystals from bumps and scratches.
- **Place it in a stable place,** away from traffic areas, especially if it is large and heavy.

## Visit the mines

In both the department of Artigas and Ametista do Sul there are mines and businesses that receive visitors and allow them to see how the geodes are extracted. Before going, ask about the conditions of the visit, wear closed shoes and always follow the safety instructions inside the galleries. If you're interested in starting a collection, the guide on [collecting gems and minerals](/es/coleccionar-gemas-y-minerales/) will give you ideas.

## Frequent errors

- **Leave a geode in the sun** next to a window.
- **Buy intense citrine** believing it is natural without asking.
- **Do not check for repairs** or stuck glass.
- **Clean with acidic products** geodes that have calcite.
- **Underestimate the weight** and transportation of a large piece.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is amethyst violet?

By small amounts of iron in the quartz combined with the natural radiation of the rocks, which create centers of color in its structure.

### Are geodes from Uruguay better than those from Brazil?

Not necessarily. Those from Artigas are famous for their intense color and those from Rio Grande do Sul for their variety and size. Each piece must be valued for itself.

### Is citrine heated amethyst?

Much of the citrine on the market is. Natural citrine exists, but is less common.

### Can a geode lose color?

Yes, amethyst can lighten with prolonged exposure to sun or heat. Place it away from direct light.

## In summary

The amethyst geodes from Artigas, in Uruguay, and Rio Grande do Sul, in Brazil, were born in gas bubbles from ancient basalt flows, which were later covered with agate and violet quartz crystals. The Uruguayan ones stand out for their intense color and the Brazilian ones for their variety and size. Amethyst is a hard and versatile gem; heated, it produces a good part of the citrine on the market. When purchasing, check for repairs, dyes and shipping, ask about export, and protect the geode from sun, shock, and acidic products.
