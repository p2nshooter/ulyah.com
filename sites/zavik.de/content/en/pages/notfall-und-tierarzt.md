Cats are masters of concealment. In nature, a cat that shows weakness is easy prey, and our sofa cats also carry this legacy. That's why signs of illness are often quiet and come late. This page will help you assess how urgent a vet visit is. It does not replace a phone call with practice: If you are unsure, call and describe what you observe.

## Immediately: emergency, don't waste any time

If you see these signs, you should immediately call a veterinary practice, a veterinary clinic or the veterinary emergency service and get on your way:

- **Difficulty breathing:** Breathing with an open mouth, visible abdominal breathing, wheezing or blue, gray or very pale mucous membranes
- **Disturbed urination:** The cat, especially a tomcat, repeatedly sits in the toilet, strains and passes little or no urine. A blocked urinary tract can become life-threatening within a day or two.
- **Suspicion of poisoning:** for example from lilies (even pollen or vase water can damage the kidneys), painkillers for humans such as paracetamol or ibuprofen, flea and tick products for dogs with permethrin, antifreeze, rat poison or cleaning products. If possible, take the packaging or part of the plant with you.
- **Accident or fall:** even if the cat appears to be walking normally afterwards. Internal injuries are often not visible from the outside.
- **Sudden paralysis of the hind legs,** often with loud screaming and cold paws
- **Seizures,** Loss of consciousness or severe staggering
- **Excessive bleeding,** deep wounds or bite wounds that swell significantly
- **Repeated vomiting** within a few hours, vomiting with blood, or a very bloated, painful stomach
- **A thread hanging from the mouth or anus:** Please do not pull on it. A swallowed thread can pull the intestines together like a ruffle and cause serious injury.
- **Overheating:** rapid panting, staggering, after being in a hot room or car

## Same day: don't wait until tomorrow

- your cat hasn't eaten for more than a day. In overweight cats, prolonged starvation can cause dangerous fatty liver disease.
- She hides in an unusual way, hardly reacts and is apathetic
- Diarrhea with blood, or diarrhea in a young kitten
- one eye is closed, cloudy, watering heavily or has been injured
- significant limping or a paw is not put under any strain
- Suspected fever: the cat is weak, the ears feel hot, it does not want to be touched

## In the next few days: Make an appointment

- your cat is drinking noticeably more than usual or the toilet is fuller than usual
- She is losing weight even though she is eating normally
- Bad breath, drooling or she only eats on one side
- dull, unkempt fur or bald spots
- She constantly scratches herself or shakes her head frequently
- she is suddenly unclean, even though she never was

## Prevention instead of emergencies

Many illnesses can be detected early if your cat is examined regularly. For young and adult cats, it is recommended to have at least one examination per year, often together with the vaccination appointment. From the age of around ten years, many veterinarians recommend two examinations a year, possibly including a blood test, urine test and blood pressure measurement. The guides in our topic [Health](/themen/gesundheit/) explain what is important.

## This way you are prepared

- Save the phone number of your veterinary practice and the nearest veterinary clinic with emergency service in your cell phone. Check which clinic is available at night and on weekends.
- Keep the **transport box** handy and get your cat used to it beforehand so that it can be done quickly in an emergency.
- Keep the **pet ID** or vaccination certificate in a fixed place.
- Write down your cat's approximate **weight** and the names of any medications he is taking.
- Call before you set off. This way the practice can prepare and tell you what you can do until you arrive.

## On the way

Put a towel in the carrier and half cover it with a cloth, this calms many cats. Do not give food, water, or medication without consultation before visiting the vet, and do not attempt to treat an injured cat yourself. Drive calmly and safely; an extra minute is better than an accident.
