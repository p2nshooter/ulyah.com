ZAVIK explains cat care, nutrition, health and behavior as thoroughly and understandably as possible. Please read this note before implementing a tip with your cat.

## No veterinary advice

Our guides are based on generally accepted knowledge about cats, such as specialist literature, recommendations from veterinary societies and information sheets from animal welfare organizations. They are not a diagnosis or treatment recommendation for a specific animal. Only those who examine your cat can judge what is missing and what will help.

## Cats hide pain

Cats often show illness and pain very late. If your cat becomes withdrawn, doesn't eat, walks differently, goes to the toilet frequently, or otherwise seems unusual to you, don't wait for it to "get better on its own." It's better to call the vet's office early than late.

## Go to the vet immediately

These signs are emergencies that require you to call a veterinary office, veterinary clinic, or emergency veterinary service immediately:

- Shortness of breath, breathing with your mouth open, blue or very pale mucous membranes
- a male cat who repeatedly pushes into the toilet and passes little or no urine
- Suspected poisoning, for example from lilies, medication, antifreeze or insect repellent for dogs
- Accident, fall from a height, heavy bleeding, seizures or loss of consciousness
- No food for more than a day, especially in overweight cats

Find out more on our page [Emergency & Vet](/notfall-und-tierarzt/).

## products and medicines

When we describe types of food, accessories or forms of treatment, we do so in general terms and without brand recommendations. Your cat should only receive medication and nutritional supplements after consulting a vet.
