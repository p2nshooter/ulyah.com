The suitcases are packed, the anticipation is great, and then the cat sits on the suitcase and looks at you reproachfully. For many cat owners, vacation planning involves a question that needs to be carefully considered: Who will look after the cat while we are away?

Unlike dogs, cats are strongly tied to their territory. For most people, a familiar environment is more important than the presence of their people. Therefore, in many cases it is best for the cat to stay at home and be cared for there. This guide compares the options and shows you how to prepare for them well.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Cats usually prefer to stay in their usual territory.
> - A cat should never be left alone for several days. At least one, preferably two visits a day are necessary.
> - Get to know the cat sitter before your vacation and do a test visit.
> - A good boarding kennel is an alternative if no one can come into the house.
> - To travel abroad you need an EU pet passport, a microchip and a valid rabies vaccination.

## Why cats prefer to stay at home

Cats are territorial animals. They know their home inside and out: every smell, every sound, every place to sleep. For them, a change of location means uncertainty and often stress, which can manifest itself in loss of appetite, hiding, uncleanliness or illness. Many cats can cope well with a few days without their people if everything else stays as it is.

There are exceptions: Some cats are very attached to their people, some are used to traveling, and for longer stays, such as if you move to another place for several months, it may make sense to take the cat with you.

## Option 1: Cat sitter at home

### Who is eligible?

- **Friends, family or neighbors** who like cats and are reliable
- **professional cat sitters** who offer home visits for a fee, often with insurance and experience with sick or shy cats
- **Neighborhood networks** in which cat owners help each other out

### How often does someone have to come?

A cat should not be left unchecked for a whole day. At least one visit a day is mandatory, two are better: morning and evening. Two visits are particularly important for cats that need medication, are very affectionate, or live in a multi-cat household where conflicts can occur.

A visit shouldn't just consist of filling a bowl and cleaning the toilet. Good cat sitters take time to play, pet and observe. This is how you notice when a cat doesn't eat, behaves differently or appears sick.

### A trial visit

Invite the cat sitter before your vacation, preferably several times. Show him where the food, litter, medication and the transport box are, how much is fed and what your cat's habits are. A test visit in which the cat sitter comes alone while you are still available is ideal.

### The cat sitter's checklist

Write everything important on a piece of paper or in a message:

- Type of food, quantity and feeding times
- where food, litter and replacement are located
- how often the toilet is cleaned
- Medication with dosage and timing
- Special features: Hide and seek, favorite game, what the cat doesn't like
- Name, address and telephone number of the veterinary practice and the nearest veterinary clinic with emergency service
- where the transport box and pet ID card are located
- your contact details while on holiday and an emergency contact person on site
- a written authorization that the cat sitter may arrange for veterinary treatment in an emergency and an agreement as to how the costs will be covered

Ask the cat sitter to send you a short message or photo every day. This calms you down and you quickly notice when something is wrong.

### Security in the house

- Close or secure all windows, especially tilted windows.
- Check faucets and appliances.
- Outdoor cats: Adjust the flap as agreed with the cat sitter. Many owners prefer to keep outdoor cats indoors while they are away so that the cat sitter can be sure that they are home.

## Option 2: Animal boarding

If no one can come to your home or the cat needs special care, a cat boarding facility is an option. Good boarding houses offer each cat their own, quiet area, separate from dogs, with climbing opportunities, hiding places and daily attention.

### How do you recognize a good guesthouse?

- **Visit possible:** Reputable guesthouses will show you the rooms beforehand.
- **Cleanliness and smell:** There should not be a strong smell of urine and the toilets should be clean.
- **Individual housing or small, known groups:** Cats from different households should not simply be placed together.
- **Quiet:** No dogs within sight or hearing.
- **Proof of vaccination required:** A good boarding house only accepts vaccinated cats, usually against cat flu and cat disease, often with protection against parasites.
- **Permit according to the Animal Welfare Act:** Anyone who cares for animals commercially needs an official permit in Germany. Ask about it.
- **Veterinary care:** What is the procedure in the event of illness?

Bring your own food, a familiar blanket, and perhaps a favorite toy. This gives the cat something familiar in a strange environment. For cats who are boarding for the first time, perhaps plan a short trial stay over a weekend.

## Option 3: Take the cat with you

### When it might make sense

For longer stays, for example if you spend several weeks in a holiday home, or for cats that are used to traveling and can cope well with changes of location, taking them with you can be an option. For most cats, a short vacation with a drive, a strange place and a return journey is more stressful than a few days at home with a cat sitter.

### Travel within Germany

Get your cat used to the transport box and car rides beforehand. Plan breaks, but only open the box when the car is closed. Take the toilet, litter, food, water, bowls, blanket and medication with you. Make sure you have a quiet, safe space at your vacation destination and definitely don't let the cat out there, even if it's an outdoor animal at home. She can't find her way back to unfamiliar surroundings.

### Traveling abroad

Uniform rules apply to traveling with cats within the EU:

- **EU pet passport,** issued by an authorized veterinarian
- **Microchip,** which is recorded in the ID card
- **Valid rabies vaccination:** The initial vaccination must generally have been given at least twenty-one days before entry and it must have been given after chipping

Some countries have additional requirements, such as tapeworm treatment before entry. Outside the EU, stricter rules often apply, including blood tests for rabies antibodies with long waiting times. Find out early at the veterinary practice and on the official website of the destination country. When traveling by air, the airline's conditions also apply.

## Before vacation

- **Veterinary check:** Is your cat healthy? Are the vaccinations up to date? Does she have enough medication to last?
- **Check chip registration:** Is your pet registry contact information up to date?
- **Stock up food and litter:** It's better to have a little more than too little.
- **Say goodbye calmly:** A big goodbye or stressful packing can unsettle cats. Keep the routine until the end.

## After returning

Some cats greet their people exuberantly, others initially give them the cold shoulder. Both are normal. Give your cat time to get used to you again, play with him and follow his usual routine. In the first few days, observe whether she eats, drinks and uses the toilet normally.

## Special cases: sick, old and shy cats

If your cat regularly needs medication or insulin, care should be provided by someone who has experience, such as a professional cat sitter with a background in veterinary medicine or a veterinary assistant who offers home visits. Practice entering it together beforehand and have the medication ready for each day labeled. A boarding house with veterinary care can also be a good solution in this case.

Old cats usually cope particularly poorly with a change of location and are best left at home with two visits a day. Very shy cats often don't show themselves to the cat sitter at all. Then indirect signs are important: Has the food been eaten, is there urine and feces in the toilet, are there traces of nocturnal activity? Ask the cat sitter to pay close attention and write to you if any of these signs are missing. If a shy cat remains invisible for more than a day and doesn't touch anything, you have to look for it.

## Technology as a supplement

Cameras with a cell phone connection, automatic feeders and drinking fountains can supplement the care. With a camera you can see whether the cat is eating and moving normally and you can consult the cat sitter. An automatic feeder distributes meals between visits. Technology can fail if electricity or the internet doesn't work, so it can never replace daily visits. If you use a camera, let the cat sitter know in advance.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I leave my cat alone for a weekend if I put out enough food?

This is not recommended. Feeders can break down, water can spill, and if the cat gets sick or injured, no one will notice. At least one daily visit should be ensured.

### Is an automatic feeder a solution?

An automatic feeder is a good addition, for example to distribute meals between visits, but is not a replacement for a human checking on the cat.

### My cat is unclean after vacation. Why?

Changes and stress can lead to uncleanliness and sometimes even a bladder infection. Give her time and rest. If the problem persists for more than a day or two or she urinates frequently and infrequently, have her examined.

### How long can I travel if someone comes every day?

Many cats do well with two visits a day for one to two weeks, some even longer. Cats who are very people-oriented suffer from loneliness more quickly. For longer trips, it is good if the cat sitter spends more time with the cat or even lives with you.
