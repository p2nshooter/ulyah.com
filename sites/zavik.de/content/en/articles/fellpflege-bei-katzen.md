Cats are famous for their cleanliness. They spend much of their waking hours grooming, and their rough tongue acts like a fine comb. Nevertheless, many cats need support with fur care: long-haired cats whose thick fur quickly becomes matted, cats in the process of shedding, older or overweight cats that can no longer reach certain parts of their body, and sick animals that groom themselves less.

Regular brushing reduces hairballs and hair on the sofa, prevents mats and is an opportunity to give your cat a thorough pat-down. For many cats, it also becomes a moment of closeness that they enjoy. This guide shows how to do it.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Short-haired cats usually only need occasional brushing, more often when they shed.
> - Long-haired cats should be combed daily, especially on the stomach, armpits, pants and behind the ears.
> - Get your cat used to grooming in short, comfortable sessions.
> - Never cut off mats directly on the skin with scissors. Stubborn felts belong in professional hands.
> - Bathing is almost never necessary for healthy cats.

## Why grooming is important

**Less hairballs:** Loose hair that you brush out won't end up in your cat's stomach.

**No matting:** Matted fur pulls on the skin, is painful, hinders movement and can hide underlying skin inflammation.

**Health Check:** When brushing you will notice fleas, ticks, lumps, wounds, dandruff or bald spots early.

**Bond:** Mutual grooming is a sign of belonging between cats who like each other. Many cats experience brushing in a similar way.

## The right tool

| tool | Suitable for | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Rubber curry comb or studded glove | Shorthair | Gently loosens loose hair and is readily accepted by many cats |
| soft brush | Short hair, finish for long hair | for shine and loose upper hair |
| Comb with wide and narrow teeth | Longhair | the most important helper against knots; broad side first |
| Slicker brush | Longhair, semi-longhair | removes undercoat; Use carefully, it can irritate the skin |
| Undercoat comb | dense undercoat during shedding | just gently, do not scrape the skin |
| Detangling comb | small knots | carefully, hold on to the skin |

Choose tools with rounded tines. You should use brushes that remove a lot of the undercoat sparingly so that the fur does not thin out or the skin becomes irritated.

## This is how often you should brush

- **Shorthair cats:** once a week is usually enough. In spring and autumn, when the cat changes its coat, several times a week.
- **Semi-long-haired cats:** two to three times a week, changing coats daily.
- **Long-haired cats such as Persians:** daily. Their fur is so thick and fine that even a few days without grooming can cause knots.
- **Older or overweight cats:** more often because they have difficulty reaching their back and rump.

## Get the cat used to being brushed

Many cats love being brushed, others are skeptical. With patience, most people get used to it:

1. **Choose the right moment:** When the cat is relaxed and tired, for example after playing or eating.
2. **Start with popular areas:** Cheeks, head and back. Belly, legs and tail come later.
3. **Keep it short:** Just a few strokes at first, then a reward. It's better to stop while the cat is still relaxed.
4. **Reward:** A treat or special petting after brushing makes it a pleasant ritual.
5. **Note signals:** Twitching tail tip, twisted ears and twitching skin are signs that enough is enough.
6. **Increase:** Expand the duration and areas of the body over days and weeks.

Kittens who are introduced to brushing at an early age usually accept it calmly throughout their lives.

## Brush properly

**Short hair:** Brush over the entire body with a rubber curry comb in the direction of growth. Loose hair quickly collects in the curry comb. A soft brush at the end ensures shine.

**Long hair:** Work in sections with the comb. Lift the fur slightly with one hand and comb the underlying layer from the skin outwards. The following are particularly at risk for knots:

- behind the ears
- under the armpits
- on the stomach
- on the inside of the hind legs and the “pants”
- on the neck under the collar if the cat is wearing one

Start with the wide side of the comb and switch to the narrow side when the fur is tangle-free.

## Loosen tangles

Small, loose knots can often be picked apart with your fingers. Hold the fur close to the skin so that it doesn't pull, and work your way from the outside in. A detangling comb can help.

**Do not cut with scissors** if the felt is close to the skin. Cat skin is very thin and stretchy and is easily lifted when cutting. Cuts are one of the most common accidents when grooming at home.

Large areas of matting or those close to the skin should be removed by an experienced veterinarian or cat groomer, usually with clippers. If cats are very anxious or have heavily matted fur, light sedation may be necessary so that the cat does not experience stress or pain.

## Bathing: almost never necessary

Healthy cats do not need to be bathed. Their coat cleans itself through grooming, and a bath removes protective skin oils. Most cats also find water very stressful.

Exceptions are:

- Heavy contamination with substances that the cat is not allowed to lick off, such as oil, paint or chemicals. In such cases, call the veterinary practice immediately.
- certain skin diseases for which the vet prescribes a medicated shampoo
- some older or sick cats who can no longer clean themselves after diarrhea

If a bath is necessary, use a shampoo specifically designed for cats and lukewarm water, keep the head and ears dry, rinse very thoroughly, and dry the cat with towels in a warm room. A hair dryer is too loud for many cats. It is often enough to just clean the dirty area with a damp cloth.

## What fur reveals about health

Healthy cat fur is shiny, supple and dense. Changes can indicate problems:

| Change | possible cause |
|---|---|
| dull, shaggy fur | Illness, pain, nutritional deficiencies, age |
| greasy fur on the back | The cat no longer grooms itself there, perhaps due to obesity, osteoarthritis or illness |
| Shed | dry skin, obesity, parasites, skin diseases |
| bald spots | excessive cleaning due to stress, allergies, fleas, skin fungus |
| black crumbs in the fur | Flea droppings |
| Increased hair loss outside of shedding periods | Stress, hormonal imbalances, illnesses |

If there are significant changes, especially if they are accompanied by itching, weight loss, or behavioral changes, have your cat examined. Incidentally, skin fungus can also be transmitted to humans.

## Complementary care

- **Eyes:** In some breeds with short noses, the eyes water a lot. Gently wipe the corners of your eyes with a soft, damp cloth, one for each eye.
- **Ears:** Healthy ears clean themselves. If necessary, only clean the visible part of the auricle with a soft cloth, never with cotton swabs in the ear canal.
- **Rear:** For long-haired cats, it can help to keep the fur around the anus short so that no feces get caught in it. The veterinary practice can show this.

## Breeds with special care requirements

Some breeds require customized care. **Persian cats** have such thick, fine fur that daily combing is mandatory, and because of their short noses, the corners of their eyes often need to be cleaned daily. **Maine Coon** and **Norwegian Forest Cats** have water-repellent semi-long hair that should be combed two to three times a week, more often when shedding. **Rex cats** with curly fur need a particularly soft brush because their fur is delicate. **Naked cats** like the Sphynx don't have fur, but their skin produces sebum that the fur would otherwise absorb. They therefore need to be washed or wiped regularly with a mild cat shampoo, they freeze quickly and need protection from sunburn in summer.

## Prevent hairballs

When cleaning, cats swallow loose hair, which is usually excreted in the feces. If a lot of hair gets into the stomach, for example during shedding or in long-haired cats, the cat regurgitates it as hairballs. Regular brushing is the most effective prevention. In addition, malt pastes or food with a higher proportion of fiber can help move the hair through the intestines. If your cat gags frequently, several times a week, or vomits even without hair, get it checked out. More on this can be found in [Vomiting and Hairballs](/katzenwissen/erbrechen-und-haarballen/).

## When professionals need to help

Some cats refuse to be brushed despite patient training, or their fur is so matted that you can no longer remove it at home. Then cat groomers with experience or the veterinary practice can help. When dealing with large areas of felt that are close to the skin, the cat is often lightly sedated so that the shearing can be done gently and without injury. Afterwards, it is important to start with regular care so that felt does not form again.

## Grooming and cat allergies

The most important allergen in cats comes primarily from saliva and skin glands and is spread on the fur when grooming. Regular brushing, preferably by someone without allergies and not in the bedroom, reduces the amount of hair and dander in the home. Frequently washing blankets, vacuuming with a fine particle filter, and an air purifier can also help. This does not make an allergy go away, but the symptoms become more bearable for some people. In any case, have an allergy checked by a doctor.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I have my long-haired cat clipped in summer?

Shearing only makes sense in exceptional cases, such as in the case of severe matting. The fur also protects cats from heat and sunburn. Regularly combing out the undercoat is usually the better solution in summer.

### My cat hates the brush. What to do?

Try a different tool, like a napped glove, that makes brushing feel like stroking. Keep the sessions very short and combine them with rewards.

### How often does a cat lose its fur?

There is a noticeable change of fur, especially in spring and autumn. Indoor cats that experience little temperature difference often lose hair moderately throughout the year.
