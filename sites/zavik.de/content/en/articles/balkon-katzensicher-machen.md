A balcony is a gift for an indoor cat: fresh air, sun, birdsong, smells from outside. At the same time, it is one of the biggest dangers in the life of a city cat. Veterinary clinics in major cities treat numerous cats that have fallen from balconies or windows every year, especially in the warm months. The injuries range from bruises to broken jaws and legs to serious lung injuries.

Many owners believe their cat is too cautious to fall. But even experienced, level-headed cats fall when a bird flies by, an insect distracts them or they slip on a narrow parapet. A cat net is the simplest and most reliable solution.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Cats also fall from low floors and even when they are otherwise very skilled.
> - A cat net should enclose the entire balcony without any gaps, including upwards if the cat can climb.
> - There are fastenings without drilling, with telescopic rods and clamps.
> - As a tenant, you should ask the landlord beforehand.
> - Secure windows are just as important: tilted windows are a deadly trap.

## Why cats fall

Cats have an excellent sense of balance and a reflex that allows them to turn when falling and land on their paws. Nevertheless, they often fall because they overestimate themselves or become careless in certain situations:

- **Hunting instinct:** A bird or insect flying by and the cat jumps without thinking about the height.
- **Slipping:** on a wet or narrow railing, on a slippery railing.
- **Shock:** a loud bang, a dog, another animal.
- **Sleep:** Cats like to doze on railings and parapets and can slip off in their sleep.
- **Inexperience:** Young cats often misjudge heights and distances.

The turning reflex does not protect against serious injuries. When falling from a height, the jaw, palate, chest and legs are particularly at risk. A cat that has fallen should always be examined by a veterinarian, even if it appears uninjured, because internal injuries are often not visible from the outside.

## The cat net

### Material and mesh size

Cat nets are usually made of plastic, often polyethylene or nylon, sometimes with wire reinforcement. Good nets are UV-resistant and tear-resistant. A mesh size of around three by three centimeters is common. Larger meshes may be too large for kittens and small cats, and some cats will try to put their heads through them.

Nets are available in transparent, black, green or other colors. Transparent nets are hardly noticeable from the outside. Black nets are almost invisible from the inside and disturb the view less than you think.

If your cat tends to chew on nets or is very strong, a wire-reinforced net may be useful.

### How high and how far?

The net should be placed so that there is no gap for a cat to squeeze through. Cats get through surprisingly small openings. Common vulnerabilities are:

- the gap between the net and the house wall
- the area under the railing if there are gaps there
- the top edge if the net does not reach the ceiling and the cat can climb up the net

The safest option is a net that completely encloses the balcony from the parapet to the ceiling or to the balcony above. If this is not possible, the top of the net should be bent inwards so that the cat cannot climb over it.

### Fastening without drilling

Many cat owners live in rented homes and are not allowed to drill. There are several solutions:

- **Telescopic or clamping rods** that are clamped between the balcony floor and ceiling. The net is attached to them.
- **Clamps and hooks** for the railing and parapet.
- **Tension wires or ropes** on which the net is hung.
- **Self-adhesive hooks** for light nets on smooth surfaces, but less resilient.

The poles should be secure and checked regularly, especially after storms. There are companies that install cat nets professionally, and animal welfare associations sometimes provide help.

### As a tenant

Whether a cat net requires the landlord's consent depends on the type of installation and the rental agreement. A network changes the appearance of the facade, and drilling into the building structure usually requires approval. It is safest to ask in writing beforehand and describe the planned solution. Many landlords agree to an inconspicuous network without drilling. When moving out, the net usually has to be removed without leaving a trace.

## Secure windows

What applies to the balcony also applies to windows:

**Tilted windows** are a particular danger. Cats try to climb through the gap, slide down and become trapped in the narrowing. The more they move, the deeper they slide. This can lead to serious injuries to the spine, kidneys and hind legs and even death. A side tilting window protection made of metal or plastic mesh reliably prevents this and is inexpensive.

**Open windows** can be secured with window grilles or insect protection frames with sturdy nets. Conventional fly screens are not stable enough for cats.

## Design the balcony

A secured balcony becomes a favorite place for many cats. Here's how to make it even more attractive:

- **Lounging areas in sun and shade:** Cats love the sun, but also need shade on hot days.
- **Elevated places:** a shelf, scratching post or lounger at railing height so the cat can look out.
- **Cat grass and non-toxic plants:** Check all balcony plants. Lilies, oleanders and many bulbous plants are poisonous to cats.
- **Water:** a bowl of fresh water, especially in summer.
- **A Hideout:** a box or cave in case something unexpected happens.

## Heat and cold

Cats tolerate heat well, but they can also overheat, especially on a south-facing balcony in midsummer. Provide shade and water and leave the balcony door open so that the cat can return to the cooler apartment. In winter, a secured balcony is no problem for short trips, as long as the cat can come in at any time.

## What you shouldn't do

- **Leave the cat unattended on an unsecured balcony,** not even for five minutes.
- **Rely on a leash:** A cat on a harness and leash on the balcony can become entangled and strangled if it jumps.
- **Rely on experience:** “She has never ventured onto the railing before” is no protection.

## If it does happen: first aid after a fall

Despite all caution, a cat can still fall. Then every minute counts:

1. **Stay calm and pick up the cat carefully.** An injured cat may bite or scratch out of pain and fear. Take a towel or blanket, place it gently over the top and, using the chest and pelvis, lift the cat without twisting the spine.
2. **Place in a transport box or cardboard box** on a soft surface and keep warm.
3. **Call the veterinary clinic or veterinary practice immediately** and announce that you are coming with a fallen cat.
4. **Don't give anything to eat or drink** if anesthesia is necessary, and no painkillers from the medicine cabinet.
5. **Even if cats appear to be uninjured, have them examined.** Typical consequences such as lung bruising, air in the chest or jaw fractures often only become apparent after a few hours through shortness of breath, nosebleeds or problems eating.

If your cat has disappeared after a fall, first search in the immediate area: injured cats usually hide in bushes, under cars or in cellar shafts and do not respond to calls.

## The first trips to the secured balcony

You should also accompany your first excursions on a well-secured balcony. Observe how your cat examines the net, whether it tries to climb it, and where it likes to sit. This way you can discover weak points before they become a problem. Some cats are initially unsettled by the noises of the street, birds or wind. Leave the balcony door open so that the cat can return to the apartment at any time and do not force it out. Kittens and young cats are particularly curious and will slip through gaps that would be too small for an adult cat.

## Check the net and fastenings regularly

A cat net is not a product that you put on once and then forget about. Sun, rain and frost make plastic brittle over time, and clamping rods can become loose due to temperature fluctuations. Therefore, check the net and fastenings regularly, about once a month and after every storm. Look for holes, frayed areas, loose ties, and splits at the edges. Many manufacturers specify after how many years a network should be replaced. A new net is needed when it feels hard or tears in one place.

## If there is no balcony

Even without a balcony, you can give your cat some fresh air. Windows can be secured with sturdy protective grilles or frames with cat nets so that they can remain open. There are also small window balconies that are placed in front of or in the window like a cage made of mesh and offer the cat a safe place to look out. If you have a garden, you can build an outdoor enclosure or secure part of the garden with a cat fence. ## Frequently asked questions

### At what height is a fall dangerous?

A cat can get injured even from the first floor. Interestingly, falls from medium heights are not always more harmless than from higher ones. Every fall should be checked by a veterinarian.

### My cat is nibbling on the net. What to do?

Check the net regularly for holes. A wire-reinforced net or a net made of harder material can help. Also offer cat grass so the cat has something to chew on.

### Can I use plexiglass instead of a net?

Yes, transparent panels are an alternative, as long as they have no gaps and are sufficiently high. They also block wind. Make sure that the balcony behind it does not heat up too much in summer.

### What do I do if my cat catches a wasp on the balcony?

Many cats hunt insects, and a bite in the mouth or paw is painful. The area usually only swells slightly and then subsides again. If the face swells significantly, the cat is breathing heavily or appears apathetic, go to the vet immediately, as swelling in the throat can hinder breathing.
