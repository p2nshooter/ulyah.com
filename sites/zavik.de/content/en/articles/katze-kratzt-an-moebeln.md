The new sofa is three weeks old and the first threads are already hanging out on the siderest. The wallpaper in the hallway has a frayed pattern at cat height, and the carpet in front of the bedroom door looks like someone used a rake on it. Many cat owners know these marks and wonder whether their cat is doing this on purpose.

The answer is: yes, intentionally, but not out of malice. Scratching is as important to cats as eating and sleeping. If you want to understand how to protect furniture, you must first understand why cats scratch.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Scratching is used to care for claws, stretch the muscles and mark territory.
> - A cat cannot stop scratching, but it can be redirected.
> - Good scratching options are stable, large enough and located where the cat wants to scratch.
> - Punishments, spraying water and scolding do not help and damage the relationship.
> - Removing claws is prohibited in Germany and is contrary to animal welfare.

## Why cats scratch

### Claw care

Cat's claws grow in layers. When scratching, the outer, worn-out coverings come off, revealing a new, sharp claw underneath. You can sometimes find the shed claw covers on the scratching post or on the carpet. They look like hollow, crescent-shaped claws and are nothing to worry about.

### Stretch and move

When scratching, the cat stretches its entire body: back, shoulders, front legs and paws. Many cats therefore particularly like to scratch immediately after waking up, similar to how people stretch after sleeping.

### Mark

There are scent glands between the pads of the paws. When scratching, the cat leaves behind a personal scent that other cats can detect. At the same time, the scratch marks are a visible sign. So scratching is also a message: I live here. That's why cats prefer to scratch in conspicuous places, such as entrances, passageways and near their favorite places, and on furniture that smells like their humans.

### express feelings

Many cats scratch when they are excited, such as when their humans come home or when there is food. Scratching can also relieve frustration or stress.

## What a cat likes in a scratching spot

If you want to understand why a cat prefers the sofa to the scratching post, you should look at the sofa from the cat's perspective: It is large, stable, does not wobble, has a surface that is easy to sink its claws into, is in the middle of the living room and smells like your favorite people. A good scratching place should have exactly these characteristics.

### Stability

A scratching post that wobbles or tips over when scratching is quickly avoided. Choose stable models with a heavy base or those that are braced between the floor and ceiling. Scratching boards for the wall should be firmly installed.

### size

The cat wants to be able to stretch completely while scratching. A vertical scratching post should therefore be high enough so that the cat can place its front paws far above itself when standing. Many commercially available scratching posts are too short.

### material

Many cats like sisal, a coarse natural fiber material that is easy to grip. Others prefer corrugated cardboard, carpet, wood or bark. Observe what your cat has been scratching so far: Is it a coarse fabric, a carpet, a wooden beam? Offer a similar material.

### direction

Some cats prefer to scratch vertically, others horizontally, and many cats prefer both. If your cat is working on the carpet, he will need a horizontal scratching mat or a flat scratching board made of corrugated cardboard. If she scratches the side of the sofa, she needs a vertical surface.

### Location

That is the most important point. A scratching post in the guest room, which hardly anyone enters, is rarely used. Place scratching options where the cat already scratches, where it likes to sleep and where it wants to mark its territory, i.e. in the living room, in the hallway, near the front door and next to its sleeping places.

## This is how you redirect the scratching

1. **Observe your favorite spot.** Where does your cat scratch, in which direction, on what material?
2. **Place an alternative directly in front of it.** If she scratches the corner of the sofa, place a vertical scratching board or sturdy scratching post directly in front of that corner.
3. **Make the old spot unattractive.** Temporarily cover the corner of the sofa with a smooth blanket, foil or double-sided tape. Many cats don't like the feeling of tape on their paws.
4. **Make the new spot attractive.** Rub some catnip or valerian on the scratching area if your cat responds, or play with it with a feather rod so the cat sinks its claws into it.
5. **Praise and reward.** When your cat uses the new space, calmly praise him or give him a treat.
6. **Be patient.** After a few weeks, you can carefully remove the cover on the sofa and, if necessary, slowly move the scratching area to a place that is more comfortable for you.

## Which doesn't help

**Punishments:** Swearing, clapping your hands, spraying water or even physical punishments do not help. The cat does not understand that scratching is wrong because for him it is a natural need. At most, she learns that she shouldn't scratch in your presence and then does it secretly. At the same time, she loses trust in you, and stress can actually increase the scratching.

**Carrying the cat to the scratching post and rubbing its paws on it:** Most cats don't like this and associate the scratching post with something unpleasant.

**Fragrance sprays with a strong smell:** Some owners try to protect furniture with citrus scents or vinegar. This can work, but it can also unsettle the cat overall. Essential oils are toxic to cats and should not be used.

## Trimming claws and capping claws

**Shortening claws:** For indoor cats that do not wear their claws sufficiently, especially older animals, it may make sense to trim the tips occasionally. This reduces the damage to furniture somewhat, but does not replace scratching. We explain how to proceed correctly in our own guide.

**Claw caps:** Small plastic caps are offered that are glued to the claws. They prevent scratching damage, but also natural scratching and claw care. Many cats feel uncomfortable with them. Animal protection organizations advise against long-term use.

## Claw removal: prohibited

In some countries, cats have their claws surgically removed. Not only the claw but also the last phalanx of the toe is amputated. The procedure is painful, can cause permanent pain, lameness and behavioral problems, and deprives the cat of an important tool. In Germany it is banned under the Animal Welfare Act, unless there is medical necessity for the animal itself.

## If the scratching suddenly increases

If your cat suddenly scratches a lot more than usual, especially at doors and windows, this could be a sign of stress: a new cat in the neighborhood, a move, a new family member or changes in everyday life. Then it's worth looking for the cause. More scratching opportunities at entrances and windows and more play can help.

## Protect furniture, wallpaper and carpets

While you redirect the scratching, simple protective measures will help limit damage. There are transparent scratch protection films for sofa corners and armrests that are attached with pins or adhesive dots. Cats find the smooth surface unattractive and the film can be removed later without leaving any residue. A blanket over the favorite spot or a slipcover also protects as long as the cat doesn't learn to scratch underneath. Scratching boards that are attached exactly to the affected area of ​​the wall work well on wallpaper, and horizontal scratching mats made of sisal or corrugated cardboard are best placed in front of carpet edges.

When you buy new furniture, it's worth taking a look at the upholstery material. Coarse, loosely woven fabrics with loops, such as bouclé or coarse linen, invite cats to scratch and quickly pull threads. Tightly woven, smooth fabrics such as microfiber are usually less attractive and more robust.

## Get kittens on the right path early

The easiest way is to encourage good habits from the start. Set up scratching facilities before a kitten arrives, ideally next to the sleeping area and in passageways. Play with the feather rod along the scratching post so that the kitten uses its claws while playing. If he scratches on the sofa, calmly pick him up and place him in front of the scratching board without scolding him, and reward him if he continues there. Kittens quickly learn which places feel good.

## Scratches in the rental apartment

In a rented apartment, scratching can also become a financial issue. Damage that goes beyond normal wear and tear, such as scratched door frames, wallpaper or window sills, can be billed for when you move out. Therefore, protect particularly vulnerable areas such as door frames and door leaves in good time with scratching boards or protective films, and offer an attractive alternative, especially on doors where your cat likes to scratch.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many scratching opportunities does a cat need?

At least two, or preferably several, distributed throughout the apartment: a large, stable scratching post and scratching boards or mats in their favorite places. Correspondingly more in multi-cat households.

### My cat just scratches at the door. Why?

Doors are boundaries of the territory. The cat may want to go in or out, or it may be marking the boundary. A scratching board directly on or next to the door is often the solution.

### Does a new scratching post help when the old one is worn out?

A scratched scratching post is often attractive to the cat because it smells like it. It is better to replace worn sisal parts individually rather than replacing the entire scratching post.

### Does my cat scratch out of boredom?

Sometimes yes. Indoor cats that don't move much often scratch specifically when their humans come home to get attention. More playing time and activity, as described in [Keeping indoor cats species-appropriate] (/katzenwissen/wohnungskatze-artgerecht-halten/), usually significantly reduces this scratching.

### Which upholstery materials are cat-friendly?

Tightly woven, smooth fabrics such as microfiber are the most robust. Leather is less interesting for cats to scratch, but every claw shows a trace. Coarse structures and looped fabrics are the most sensitive. You can test a sample from the retailer at home by placing it next to the scratching post and watching how your cat reacts.
