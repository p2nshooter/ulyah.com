Before a cat moves in, the apartment should be ready. Not because a cat is demanding, but because the first few days are exciting enough as it is. If you have to quickly buy a cat toilet at the hardware store on the day of collection, you are missing the opportunity to let your new roommate arrive in peace.

This checklist shows what is really necessary, what you should pay attention to when buying and what things you can save. There are countless products for cats in pet stores and on the Internet, and not all of them are useful.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The following are essential: cat toilet with litter, food and water bowls, food, scratching area, transport box, places to retreat and toys.
> - In the first few weeks, stick to the food and litter your cat already knows.
> - Secure windows and balcony before the cat moves in.
> - Rule of thumb for toilets: one more than cats in the household.

## Litter box and litter

The toilet is the most important item in the entire equipment. If a cat doesn't like its toilet, it will look for another place, and uncleanliness is one of the most common reasons cats are surrendered to shelters.

**Size:** Many commercial litter boxes are too small. The cat should be able to turn and scratch comfortably in it. As a guideline, the toilet should be at least one and a half times as long as the cat from the nose to the base of the tail. Large plastic storage boxes are a cheaper and often better alternative.

**With or without a lid:** Some cats like a hood because they feel protected in it. Others avoid them because smells collect underneath and they have no overview. If you don't know what your cat is used to, start with an open toilet or choose both options.

**Number:** The well-known rule of thumb is: one more toilet than cats in the household. So for one cat two, for two cats three. That sounds like a lot, but it prevents arguments and uncleanliness.

**Location:** quiet, easily accessible and not next to food and water. A place next to the noisy washing machine or in a room where the door is sometimes closed is not suitable.

**Litter:** Most cats prefer fine, clumping litter without fragrance. Perfumed litter smells fresh to human noses, but often unpleasant to sensitive cat noses. The most important thing in the beginning is to continue using the litter that your cat is already familiar with. If you want to switch later, do it slowly, gradually mixing in the new variety.

## Bowls for food and water

**Food bowls** should be flat and wide. Many cats don't like it when their whiskers touch the edge when they eat. Ceramic and stainless steel are easy to clean and do not absorb odors. Plastic bowls become scratched over time, and bacteria can build up in the scratches.

**Water bowls** do not belong right next to the food. In nature, cats avoid water near their prey because it might be contaminated, and many domestic cats drink more when the water is in a different location. It's best to distribute several water points throughout the apartment. A drinking fountain can encourage cats to drink, but is not a must. It must be cleaned regularly, otherwise deposits will form.

## Food for starters

Ask the animal shelter or the previous owner exactly what food the cat gets and buy a supply for the first one or two weeks. Moving is stressful enough; A sudden change in food can lead to diarrhea or loss of appetite. If you want to change the food later, do so slowly and mix the new food into the old over several days.

When choosing food, pay attention to the term “complete feed”. Only then will it contain all the nutrients a cat needs. Supplementary feed such as many snacks or some types of wet food are not suitable for daily nutrition alone.

## Scratching post and scratching boards

Scratching is a basic need for cats. They use it to care for their claws, stretch their muscles and leave scent marks from glands on their paws. A cat without a suitable place to scratch will look for the sofa, carpet or wallpaper.

A good scratching post is **stable**. If it wobbles when it jumps up, the cat will avoid it. For large or heavy cats, floor-to-ceiling models that are stretched between the floor and ceiling are a good choice. It is also important that the trunks are long enough so that the cat can stretch completely while scratching.

Some cats prefer to scratch horizontally. Flat scratching boards made of corrugated cardboard or sisal mats are ideal for them. Place scratching facilities where the cat likes to spend time, for example next to the sofa or near where she sleeps. Cats like to scratch right after they wake up.

## Transport box

You will need the transport box on the day of collection and later for every visit to the vet. Don't cut corners here. A good box is sturdy, well ventilated and can be **opened from the top** or has a removable top. Then you can have a scared cat checked out by the vet without having to drag him out of the box. Soft bags are light but offer little protection in an accident.

If you don't put the box in the basement after moving in, but leave it in the apartment as a comfortable place to sleep with a blanket, it will become a familiar place for your cat and not a harbinger of a visit to the vet.

## Sleeping places and hiding places

Cats sleep many hours a day and like to change places. They like elevated places from which they can see the room and sheltered caves to which they can retreat. A place on the windowsill with a view outside is very popular with many cats.

Especially in the first few days, a new cat needs a place to hide. This can be a box with a blanket, a cave in the scratching post or simply the space under the bed. Leave the cat there alone and don't bring it out.

## toys

For cats, playing is not a pastime, but rather a substitute for hunting. A spring fishing rod that you can use to mimic the movements of your prey is the most important toy of all. Small balls, play mice, rustling bags and intelligence toys from which the cat can fish for dry food are also suitable.

Be careful with toys with strings, ribbons or woolen threads: if a cat swallows them, it can be life-threatening. Put such toys away after playing.

## Grooming and health care

For short-haired cats, a soft brush or rubber curry comb is sufficient. Long-haired cats also need a wide-toothed comb and daily grooming. You only need claw trimmers if your cat's claws are not sufficiently worn, which is especially the case with older indoor cats.

## Security in the apartment

Before the cat moves in, you should walk through the apartment and look at it with a cat's eyes:

- **Windows:** Tilted windows are a known hazard. Cats try to climb through the gap, slip and become trapped. A tilt window protection made of metal mesh prevents this.
- **Balcony:** A cat net prevents falls. Even from low floors, cats can be seriously injured if they fall.
- **Plants:** Some houseplants are poisonous to cats, especially lilies. Check your plants and remove poisonous species.
- **Cables and Small Parts:** Young cats sometimes chew on cables. Household elastics, hair ties, threads and needles should be kept out of reach.
- **Washer and Dryer:** Cats like to sleep in warm, dark caves. Check it before every wash.

## What can wait

Many things that are offered in stores as essential can wait or are unnecessary: cat clothes, scented litter, automatic feeders with apps, cat milk, most nutritional supplements and treats in large quantities. Observe your cat in the first few weeks. Then you quickly notice what she likes and what she misses.

## How much does the initial equipment cost?

For a solid basic set of toilets, bowls, scratching post, transport box and toys, you should expect to spend a few hundred euros. There are also ongoing costs for food, litter and veterinary care. Also plan a reserve for unexpected veterinary costs or find out about health insurance or surgical insurance for cats.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I really need two toilets for one cat?

Not every cat insists on this, but many like to use one toilet for urine and one for feces. A second toilet is a simple insurance policy against uncleanliness.

### Can I take over used equipment from a friend?

Yes, as long as everything is thoroughly cleaned. When using other cats' toilets, the smells of the previous cat can be annoying; A new toilet is often the better choice here.

### When should I install a cat net?

Before moving in. New cats are often restless and explore everything, and a single jump can be fatal. If you live in rent, ask the landlord for permission beforehand.

### Does my cat need a collar?

An indoor cat doesn't need one. For outdoor animals, a microchip is a safer identification because collars can get caught on branches or fences. It is important to then have the chip registered free of charge with a pet registry such as TASSO or FINDEFIX, because only then can a found cat be assigned to its home. If you still want to use a collar, choose a safety collar that opens when pulled.
