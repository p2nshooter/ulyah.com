All cat owners know the typical noise: a rhythmic retching, often in the middle of the night or shortly after breakfast, and then something is lying on the carpet. A hairball, a puddle of undigested food, or some yellowish foam. Many people consider occasional vomiting in cats to be completely normal. It's not quite that simple.

For many cats, a hairball every now and then is actually nothing to worry about. However, if a cat vomits regularly, there is often a treatable cause behind it. This guide will help you differentiate between the situations.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - An occasional hairball is normal for many cats, especially long-haired cats.
> - If a cat vomits regularly, more than once or twice a month, it should be examined.
> - Brushing, fiber and cat grass can reduce hairballs.
> - Repeated vomiting on the same day, blood, apathy, or a bloated stomach are emergencies.

## Vomiting or vomiting?

It's worth taking a closer look because there are two different processes:

**Vomiting** is an active process. The cat becomes restless, smacks his lips, salivates, the stomach contracts rhythmically several times, and then stomach contents come out, often partially digested.

**Regurgitation**, also known as regurgitation, is passive. Shortly after eating, food comes out almost unchanged, often in an elongated sausage shape, without the stomach becoming very tense. This may mean that the food has not left the esophagus at all.

The distinction helps the veterinarian narrow down the cause. A cell phone photo or video can be valuable.

## Hairballs

### How hairballs form

Cats spend many hours a day grooming. Its rough tongue is covered with small, backward-facing hooks that pick up loose hairs, and the cat swallows them. Normally the hair travels through the intestines and is excreted in the feces. But if too many hairs collect in the stomach, they form into an elongated, felty ball that is vomited up.

### When hairballs are more common

- for **long-haired cats** such as Persians, Maine Coons or Norwegian Forest cats
- in the **coat change** in spring and autumn
- for cats that groom themselves **excessively**, for example because of itching, fleas, allergies or stress
- in cats whose **intestinal movement** is slowed down, which can also be due to illness

### What helps against hairballs

- **Regular Brushing:** Any hair that ends up in the brush doesn't end up in the stomach. Long-haired cats should be brushed daily, short-haired cats should be brushed several times a week when they change their coat.
- **Fiber:** There are foods with an increased fiber content, which supports the transport of hair through the intestines.
- **Malt paste:** It is said to make it easier to shed hair. Give them only occasionally and in small amounts because many pastes contain sugar and fat.
- **Cat Grass:** Many cats enjoy eating grass, and some vomit hair afterwards. Offer cat grass made from oats or wheat, not ornamental grasses.
- **Clarify causes of excessive cleaning:** Treat fleas, skin problems and stress.

## Other common causes of vomiting

### Eating too quickly

Some cats gobble down their food so quickly that it comes out undigested shortly afterwards, especially dry food that swells up in the stomach. This often happens in multi-cat households where cats compete for food. The following are helpful:

- smaller portions, more often per day
- Anti-sling bowls or fumble boards
- separate feeding areas for several cats
- Spread food on a large, flat plate

### Food intolerances

A sudden change in food, spoiled food or an intolerance to certain ingredients can trigger vomiting. If a food allergy is suspected, the veterinarian will recommend an exclusion diet that must be fed consistently for several weeks.

### Foreign body

Cats sometimes swallow things they can't digest: hair ties, toy parts, thread, ribbons, or tinsel. Threads that can get caught in the intestines and injure them are particularly dangerous. If a thread hangs out of the mouth or anus, do not pull on it, but go to the vet immediately.

### diseases

Regular vomiting can indicate a variety of medical conditions, such as:

- chronic intestinal inflammation
- Kidney disease
- Hyperthyroidism
- Liver or pancreatic diseases
- Diabetes
- Tumor diseases in the gastrointestinal tract
- Parasites
- Poisonings

Therefore, a cat that vomits regularly, even if it otherwise appears lively, should be examined thoroughly. Experts point out that chronic vomiting in cats is often underestimated because it is considered normal.

## What the vet examines

If your cat vomits regularly, the practice will take a step-by-step approach. At the beginning there is a detailed conversation. That's why it's so helpful if you've noted beforehand how often and when your cat vomits, what the vomit looks like, what it eats and whether anything has changed in its drinking behavior, its stool or its weight.

This is followed by a thorough physical examination, including a palpation of the abdomen, and often a blood test. It shows whether the kidneys, liver, pancreas and thyroid are working normally. A fecal examination will rule out parasites. Depending on the findings, an ultrasound examination of the abdomen may be useful, which may reveal, for example, thickened intestinal walls or enlarged lymph nodes. If a foreign body is suspected, an X-ray is taken.

If all of these tests are normal, veterinarians often recommend trying a hypoallergenic diet for several weeks. If the vomiting improves, a feed intolerance was probably the cause. If it persists, further examinations such as a stomach and colonoscopy with tissue samples may be necessary to distinguish chronic intestinal inflammation from other diseases.

These steps sound complicated, but they are worth it: many causes of chronic vomiting can be easily treated, and the cat feels much better afterwards.

## When you should go to the vet

| situation | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Vomiting a hairball once, cat otherwise cheerful | observe |
| occasional vomiting after eating too quickly | Adjust feeding, observe |
| Vomiting more than once or twice a month | Make an appointment |
| Vomiting along with weight loss, diarrhea, or changes in drinking | have it examined promptly |
| vomiting repeatedly within a few hours | to the practice on the same day |
| Vomiting with blood or coffee grounds-like contents | immediately |
| Gagging without results, bloated or painful stomach | immediately |
| Suspicion of swallowed foreign body or poison | immediately |
| Cat is apathetic, doesn't eat, doesn't drink | on the same day |
| Kitten, very old or chronically ill cat vomits repeatedly | on the same day |

## What to do after your cat vomits

- **Observe:** How does the cat act? Is she awake, is she eating again, is she drinking?
- **Note:** When, how often, what came out, how long after eating? A photo can help.
- **Small portions:** If the cat is alert, offer a small portion of its usual food after a few hours.
- **No long fasting:** Don't let your cat starve for more than a day to protect the stomach. This is dangerous in cats.
- **Not human medications:** Human anti-nausea medications may be toxic to cats.

## Kittens and seniors: act faster

Vomiting is more serious in kittens and very old cats than in healthy adults. Kittens have hardly any reserves and become dehydrated within a short time if they vomit or have diarrhea. Common causes are worms, giardia, infections or changing food too quickly after moving in. Older cats, on the other hand, often vomit because of chronic illnesses such as kidney problems, hyperthyroidism or intestinal inflammation, which are easily treatable if detected early. For both groups, you shouldn't wait and see, but if you experience repeated vomiting, call the practice on the same day.

## If diarrhea occurs

Vomiting and diarrhea often occur together. Soft stools after a change of food are usually harmless. If the diarrhea lasts longer than one or two days, is watery, bloody or slimy, or if the cat appears weak, it should be taken to the vet's office. For kittens, this applies from the first day. If possible, bring a fresh fecal sample with you so that parasites such as worms or giardia can be examined immediately. ## Light food: what really helps

In the past, cats were often recommended boiled chicken with rice after gastrointestinal problems. However, rice is not a particularly suitable food for cats, and a self-prepared bland diet is not balanced. Easily digestible diet foods for the stomach and intestines, which you can get from the veterinary practice, are more suitable. Offer them in several small portions throughout the day, slightly warmed. If you want to cook for yourself for a day, use cooked, unseasoned, skinless, boneless chicken and then slowly return to your usual diet. You should not feed this way for longer than one or two days without consulting your veterinarian.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it normal for my cat to vomit after eating grass?

Yes, many cats vomit after eating grass. Why cats eat grass is not completely understood. As long as it is non-toxic cat grass and the cat is otherwise healthy, it is harmless.

### My cat vomits yellow foam in the morning. What does that mean?

Yellow or white foam on an empty stomach may indicate that the stomach has been empty for too long. A small late meal in the evening or a meal early in the morning can help. If this occurs frequently, the cause should be clarified.

### Do home remedies like oil help against hairballs?

Oil in feed is not recommended. It can disrupt digestion and, if it enters the windpipe, cause pneumonia. Brushes, fiber and special pastes are better in moderation.

### How much water can my cat drink after vomiting?

Offer fresh water, but don't let her drink a large amount at once if she vomits again immediately afterwards. Small sips at intervals are better tolerated. If she vomits water repeatedly, call the office.

### Can stress trigger vomiting?

Yes, stress can also cause vomiting or diarrhea in some cats, for example after moving, when a new cat moves in or when a visitor comes. Before you assume stress is the cause, physical causes should be ruled out, especially if vomiting occurs frequently.
