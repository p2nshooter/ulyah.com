For an indoor cat, the scratching post is often the most important piece of furniture in the apartment. It is a scratching place and climbing wall, viewing point and retreat, playground and bedroom. Nevertheless, many households have scratching posts that are rarely used: too wobbly, too small, in the wrong place or with lying areas that cannot accommodate a full-grown cat.

A good scratching post doesn't have to be expensive, but it has to suit your cat. This guide shows what is important when buying, how to avoid bad purchases and how to make an existing scratching post more attractive.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Stability is the most important criterion. A wobbling scratching post is avoided.
> - The scratching posts should be long and thick enough for the cat to fully stretch.
> - Lying areas must fit the size of the cat; large cats need wide platforms and large dens.
> - The location determines the use: in the middle of life, with a view outside, not in the storage room.
> - Worn sisal trunks can be replaced individually in many models.

## What a scratching post should do

A scratching post fulfills several basic needs of a cat:

- **Scratching:** for claw care, stretching and marking territory.
- **Climbing and jumping:** Exercise and muscle training, especially important for indoor cats.
- **Overview:** Cats feel safe in elevated places and watch their territory from there.
- **Peace:** Caves and hollows offer sheltered sleeping places.
- **Game:** Hanging toys and different levels invite you to have fun.

A scratching post is not just protection for the sofa, but an essential part of the territory.

## Stability: the most important criterion

If a cat jumps onto a platform and the whole tree sways, it will no longer trust it. A single tipping over can be enough for a cat to avoid the scratching post forever. Therefore pay attention to:

- **a heavy, wide floor slab** that extends well beyond the upper levels
- **thick trunks** that don't give in when the cat pulls up on them
- **tight screw connections** that do not loosen after a few weeks
- **Wall mounting,** especially for tall models; Many manufacturers include a mounting set
- **Ceiling tensioner** for models that are tensioned between floor and ceiling; They are often particularly stable because they are supported at the top

A simple test in the store: shake the top vigorously. If the tree wobbles significantly, it will wobble even more under a jumping cat. When purchasing online, information about the weight of the base plate, the trunk diameter and reviews from owners of large cats will help.

## The right dimensions

### scratching posts

In order for a cat to be able to fully stretch while scratching, at least one trunk should have a long, uninterrupted scratching surface. For an adult cat this is often seventy centimeters or more. Many cheap scratching posts only have short pieces of trunk between the platforms, which are not enough for proper stretching.

The diameter also plays a role. Thin trunks of eight centimeters are fine for small cats. For large, heavy cats, trunks with a diameter of twelve to twenty centimeters are significantly more stable and offer more scratching surface.

### Lying areas and caves

This is where most bad purchases happen. Many scratching posts have lying troughs and caves in which a small cat can fit, but an adult Maine Coon or Norwegian Forest cat can only fit its head. For large cats, lying areas should be at least forty by forty centimeters in size, and caves should be spacious with a large entrance.

Recliners with raised edges are also important. Cats love to snuggle up in it and rest their heads on the edge.

### Height

Cats like high places. A scratching post whose top level is at eye level with people or higher is usually particularly popular. Ceiling-high models offer the best overview. For older cats or cats with limited mobility, low models with short distances between levels are better.

### Distances between levels

The levels should be distributed so that the cat can easily jump up and down again. Jumps that are too large are difficult for kittens, older cats and heavy animals and put strain on the joints when jumping off.

## Materials

### Sisal

Sisal is the classic material for scratching posts. It is made of natural fibers, is robust and easy to scratch. Thick, tightly wound sisal ropes last longer than thin ones. Make sure that the rope is well glued and does not unwind.

### Sisal carpet and fabric

Some trunks are covered with sisal fabric. Many cats like it because it offers a more extensive scratching structure.

### Plush

The lying surfaces are usually covered with plush. It is soft and comfortable, but absorbs hair and dirt. Removable, washable covers are practical.

### Natural wood

Scratching posts made from real tree trunks and branches are particularly beautiful and very popular with cats who like to scratch on bark. They are often heavier and more stable, but also more expensive. Look for treated, pest-free wood and good workmanship.

### Corrugated cardboard

Scratching boards made of corrugated cardboard are ideal for horizontal scratching. They are inexpensive, loved by many cats, and easy to replace.

## The right location

Even the best scratching post will remain unused if it is in the wrong place. Cats want to be where life happens. Good locations are:

- **in the living room,** where the family spends a lot of time
- **at the window,** with a view outside, especially if there are birds outside
- **near the front door or at passageways** where cats like to mark
- **next to favorite sleeping places** because cats scratch when they wake up

Storage rooms, cold corners, noisy places next to the washing machine or television and places directly next to the cat litter box are unsuitable.

In multi-cat households, it makes sense to have more than one scratching post so that one cat doesn't occupy the only vantage point and crowd out the other.

## If the scratching post is not accepted

Some cats need time to accept a new scratching post. These tips will help:

- **Smell transfer:** Rub a blanket that the cat likes to lie on over the lying surfaces or place it in a hollow.
- **Play:** Run a feather rod along the scratching post and up the levels. Many cats discover the tree while playing.
- **Hide treats:** Place small rewards on the platforms.
- **Catnip or Valerian:** Many cats react strongly to these scents.
- **Change location:** If the tree is still ignored after a few weeks, try a different location.
- **Check stability:** Maybe it wobbles more than you think.

## Care and exchange

A scratched scratching post is a good sign. The more it smells like the cat, the more attractive it is to the cat. Therefore, do not replace the entire tree as soon as the sisal frays. In many models, individual trunks or covers can be exchanged. Alternatively, you can wrap a worn trunk with new sisal rope.

Check all screws and connections regularly. Tighten any loose screws. Vacuum mattress surfaces and wash removable covers if they are dirty.

## Alternatives and additions

- **Climbing Walls:** Wall shelves, steps and hammocks that are mounted on the wall save space and provide climbing paths.
- **Scratching boards for the wall** in places where the cat likes to scratch.
- **Scratching barrels and scratching posts** as a compact solution for small apartments.
- **Build it yourself:** With sturdy materials, sisal rope and a little craftsmanship, you can build a scratching post exactly according to your cat's needs. Pay particular attention to stability and non-toxic materials.

## Hidden dangers on the scratching post

Pay attention not only to stability, but also to details that could injure a cat. Toys on long rubber bands or strings look tempting, but a cat can get tangled up in them or swallow parts. Shorten such bands or remove them when you are not there. Check for staples, nails, or sharp edges sticking out anywhere, especially under loose covers. Holes and crevices in which a paw can get caught are particularly dangerous for kittens. A strong chemical smell on a new scratching post indicates adhesives or treatments. Then let the tree dry out in a well-ventilated room for a few days before the cat uses it.

## The right scratching post for kittens

Kittens climb enthusiastically, but still misjudge heights and distances. A low scratching post with short spaces between levels, a ramp or steps and small platforms with edges is ideal for the first few months. It should still be stable, because kittens also jump with a lot of momentum. When your cat is fully grown, a larger tree can be added, and the small one remains in the bedroom or hallway as an additional scratching spot. ## Buy used or take over?

A used scratching post can be a cheap solution, but it has its drawbacks. It smells like the cats that used it before. Some cats don't mind it much, others avoid it or mark it with urine. In addition, flea larvae and eggs can get stuck in plush and cracks. Thoroughly vacuum a used tree, wash removable covers in hot water, and replace severely worn sisal trunks. Above all, check the stability, as loose connections are common in older models.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should a good scratching post cost?

Prices range from inexpensive models to high-quality natural wood trees. Stability, dimensions and workmanship are more important than the price. A very cheap scratching post that wobbles and needs to be replaced after a year often ends up being more expensive than a solid model.

### My cat sleeps on the scratching post but scratches somewhere else. What to do?

The tree probably doesn't offer the right scratching surface, perhaps the trunks are too short or too thin, or the cat prefers to scratch horizontally. Add suitable scratching options to the areas where she is currently scratching.

### Does an older cat still need a scratching post?

Yes, but one that suits her: lower, with short distances, wide lying areas and a ramp or steps. Older cats particularly appreciate elevated, warm places to lie down.

### How often does the sisal have to be replaced?

This depends on how intensively your cat scratches. For some cats a trunk lasts for years, for others it frays after a few months. As long as the cat enjoys using it, there is no reason to exchange it. Only when the fibers hang loose or the trunk underneath becomes visible is it time for new sisal.
