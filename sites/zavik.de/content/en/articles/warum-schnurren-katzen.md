There is hardly a sound that is as calming as a cat's purring. A deep, consistent hum that you not only hear but also feel when the cat is lying on your lap. For most people it is clear: a cat that purrs is happy.

That's often true, but not always. Cats also purr when they are in pain, scared, or sick, sometimes even dying. Purring is a versatile signal that science doesn't yet know everything about. This guide explains what we know about it today.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Cats purr by muscles in the larynx moving the vocal cords in a rapid rhythm when inhaling and exhaling.
> - Kittens that are just a few days old purr, presumably to signal to their mother that everything is okay.
> - Purring not only means satisfaction, but can also accompany stress, pain or illness.
> - Some cats use a special purr to beg for food.
> - It has not yet been proven whether purring promotes healing.

## How cats purr

For a long time it was unclear how purring actually occurs. The common explanation is that a rhythm generator in the brain controls muscles in the larynx. These muscles contract and relax very quickly, causing the glottis to open and close. The air flowing through vibrates when you inhale and exhale. This is why a cat can purr for minutes without audible interruption while breathing normally.

The frequency of purring is usually in the range of around 25 to 150 vibrations per second, i.e. in the low tone range. Every cat has its own purr: some purr so quietly that you can only feel it, others so loud that you can hear it throughout the entire apartment.

However, a study from 2023 has supplemented this explanation: Researchers were able to show on cat larynxes that the vocal cords can vibrate at purring frequencies when only air flows through them, and that there are special tissue pads in the vocal cords that promote such low tones. How exactly nerves, muscles and vocal cords interact has not yet been fully clarified.

## Purring from the start

Kittens begin to purr in the first few days of life, often while drinking. Since they are still blind and deaf at this time, purring is one of the first forms of communication. It probably signals to the mother: I'm here, I'm fine, I'm drinking. The mother, in turn, purrs while nursing her kittens, and the kittens feel the vibration, even if they don't hear it yet.

This early purr could explain why adult cats purr in situations where they feel safe: on the lap of a familiar person, when milk is released on a soft blanket, when being petted.

## When cats purr

### Contentment and relaxation

This is the most well-known situation. A cat lying on the sofa, eyes half closed, paws relaxed and purring feels comfortable. Purring is often accompanied by the release of milk, slow blinking and giving of the head.

### Contact us

Many cats purr when they approach a human or another cat they like. Purring can express a friendly intention: I come in peace.

### Pain, fear and illness

Cats also purr in situations that are anything but pleasant: on the veterinary table, after an accident, in pain, during birth and sometimes when dying. They probably use this to calm themselves, similar to how people hum or rock themselves back and forth in difficult moments. It may also be a signal to others: I'm helpless, please don't hurt me.

Therefore, purring alone is not a sign of health. If a cat is purring and at the same time withdrawn, not eating, sitting hunched over, or showing other signs of illness, you should have it examined by a veterinarian.

### The begging purr

Many cat owners know it: early in the morning, the cat sits next to the bed and purrs in a particularly insistent way that is difficult to resist. In 2009, British researchers investigated what was behind it. They found that some cats use a different purr when begging for food than in relaxed situations. There is a high, mournful sound that is reminiscent of the cry of an infant. Humans find this purring more urgent and unpleasant, and it apparently causes them to get up and fill the bowl.

This begging purr is probably learned: cats that are successful with it use it more often. It shows how cleverly cats adapt their sounds to living together with people.

## Can purring heal?

We often read that purring helps broken bones heal faster or helps people combat stress and high blood pressure. The background is that vibrations in certain low frequencies are being studied in medicine to promote bone growth, and that cats' purring partly falls into this frequency range.

However, it has not been proven whether purring actually has a healing effect on the cat itself. It is an interesting hypothesis, not a confirmed finding. Of course, purring is not a substitute for treatment when cats are injured.

For many people, relaxing in the company of a purring cat is an everyday experience. Living with pets can improve well-being, even if individual health effects are difficult to prove.

## Do all cats purr?

Almost all domestic cats purr, although some purr so quietly that you hardly notice it. If your cat never seems to purr, place your hand gently on its neck or chest while it is relaxed. You can often feel the vibration even if you don't hear anything.

Purring is also common among the wild relatives of the domestic cat. Cheetah, puma, lynx, ocelot and many other smaller wild cats purr. The large “roaring cats” such as lions, tigers, leopards and jaguars, on the other hand, can roar, but cannot purr continuously in the same way. Your larynx is built differently. Some of them make a similar humming noise when exhaling, but it is not continuous.

## Other sounds that sound like purrs

- **Coo or trill:** a short, cooing sound made with the mouth closed, often as a greeting. This is how mother cats call their kittens.
- **Mumbling:** Some cats make a contented grumble when they eat.
- **Growling:** A deep, threatening sound that is sometimes confused with purring. But it comes from a tense position, often with ears flattened, and is a warning.

## Purring as a sign of trust

Many cats purr especially in the presence of people and animals they trust. A new cat will often only purr for the first time after a few days or weeks when it feels safe. For many owners, this moment is a small milestone. Even between cats who like each other, purring can often be heard while grooming each other and cuddling. Purring also seems to have a social function: it signals closeness and harmlessness and helps to strengthen relationships.

## Purring at the vet: a bit of a challenge

What sounds reassuring to people sometimes makes the vet's job difficult. When listening to the heart and lungs, the purring masks the subtle sounds that are important. Many veterinarians therefore know little tricks to briefly interrupt the purring, for example by turning on a tap, gently touching the cat's larynx or distracting it slightly. So don't be surprised if the water suddenly rushes in the practice while your cat is being examined.

## Purring in the last hours of life

Some owners experience that their cat continues to purr even in its final hours, for example when it is seriously ill or is put to sleep. This is not a sign that she is doing well, but probably a form of self-soothing in a stressful situation. For people, this purring can still be comforting, as a familiar sound when saying goodbye. ## Getting to know your cat's purring

Every cat purrs a little differently: louder or quieter, higher or lower, evenly or with short pauses. If you memorize the usual sound, you will notice if something changes, for example if the purring sounds rattling, the breathing seems labored or your cat purrs noticeably a lot in unusual situations. Such changes are a good reason to take a closer look and, if in doubt, ask the vet. A short audio recording with your cell phone can help you with this.

## Frequently asked questions

### My cat purrs at the vet. Is she relaxed?

Probably not. Many cats purr to calm themselves in stressful situations. Pay attention to the other signals such as flattened ears, large pupils and a crouched posture.

### Why does my cat purr and then bite?

Purring and increasing irritation from stroking may occur at the same time. The cat enjoys it at first, but eventually becomes overstimulated. Pay attention to the warning signs like the flapping tail and stop in time.

### My cat suddenly stopped purring. Is that worrying?

A temporary lack of purring is usually harmless. At the same time, if your cat eats less, becomes withdrawn, or sounds hoarse, have him examined.

### Why is my cat purring so loudly?

How loudly a cat purrs varies greatly from animal to animal and usually has no particular meaning. Some cats purr so quietly that you can only feel it, others can be heard throughout the entire apartment. What matters is whether the purring changes, not how loud it is. However, a sudden rattling or whistling sound should be checked by a vet.
