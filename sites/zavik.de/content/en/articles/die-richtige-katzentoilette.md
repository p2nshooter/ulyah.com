Hardly any other item in the cat household is given as little thought as the litter box. You buy one from a pet store, fill it with litter, put it in the bathroom and hope it works. Most of the time it works. But if not, things quickly become unpleasant, and uncleanliness is one of the most common reasons why cats lose their homes.

The toilet is an important place for a cat. She's vulnerable there, and she has very specific ideas about what a good place looks like. This guide shows what is important.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The toilet should be at least one and a half times as long as the cat.
> - Rule of thumb: one more toilet than cats in the household, in different places.
> - Most cats prefer fine, clumping, unscented litter.
> - Remove lumps daily, clean the entire toilet regularly.
> - Changes in the toilet are often the first sign of illness.

## size

Many commercially available cat litter boxes are too small. A cat wants to turn around in the toilet, choose a spot, scratch, and do its business without any part of it hanging over the edge. As a rule of thumb: the toilet should be at least one and a half times as long as the cat from the nose to the base of the tail. For a large cat this can easily be sixty centimeters or more.

A cheap and proven alternative are large plastic storage boxes. For older cats, you can cut out a low entry on one side and smooth the edge.

## shapes

### Open toilet

A simple bowl with a rim. Most cats do well with it. You have an overview, can escape quickly, and smells don't build up. Disadvantage: Litter is scraped out more easily.

### Toilet with hood

The hood holds back odors and litter, which many people appreciate. But it can be unpleasant for the cat: smells accumulate under the hood, it is dark, and one unsteady cat can be intercepted by another at the entrance. Some cats like hoods, others avoid them. If you are unsure, offer both options and see which is preferred. If in doubt, you should remove a flap at the entrance.

### Toilet with entry from above

The cat jumps into the toilet from above. This keeps out litter and dogs, but is unsuitable for kittens, older or overweight cats, and cats with joint problems.

### Self-cleaning toilets

Electric toilets that automatically remove clumps sound convenient. However, some cats are frightened by the noises and movements and avoid the toilet afterwards. You also lose sight of the amount and consistency of urine and feces on a daily basis, an important indicator of health. If you use such a toilet, get the cat used to it slowly and also offer a normal toilet.

## How many toilets?

The rule of thumb is: number of cats plus one. So one cat needs two toilets, two cats need three. Many cats like to use one toilet for urine and one for feces. In multi-cat households, having multiple toilets in different locations prevents one cat from stopping the other from going to the toilet.

By the way, two toilets next to each other often only count as one for cats. Distribute them throughout the apartment, in multi-story houses on every floor.

## Location

A good location is:

- **quiet:** not next to washing machine, dryer or heating system, not in a hallway
- **Easily accessible:** Accessible at any time, without closed doors
- **clear:** with a view of the entrance and an escape route, not in a narrow niche where the cat can be cornered
- **separate from food and water:** Cats don't like to eat next to their toilet

If you need to move a toilet, do it gradually, by a few feet per day, or place a second toilet in the new location before removing the old one.

## Cat litter comparison

| Type of litter | Properties | To be noted |
|---|---|---|
| clumping mineral litter (bentonite) | Binds urine into solid lumps, fine grain, preferred by many cats | heavy, dusty depending on the type, belongs in the trash |
| Silicate litter | very absorbent, binds odor well, light | does not clump, urine remains in the granules, some cats don't like the grain |
| clumping plant litter (wood, corn, grain, tofu) | Lightweight, often compostable or disposed of in small quantities with organic waste, depending on the municipality | Clumps sometimes become softer, can become moldy when moist, follow manufacturer's instructions for disposal |
| non-clumping wood pellets | cheap, absorbent | coarse grain, many cats don't like the feeling on their paws |

Most cats prefer a fine, sand-like, clumping litter without fragrance. Fill the toilet with a few inches of litter so the cat can scratch.

Perfumed litter, scented beads and toilet deodorants mask odors for human noses, but are often unpleasant for sensitive cat noses. Cleanliness is the better way to have a low-odor toilet.

If you want to change the litter, gradually mix the new type into the old one over one to two weeks.

## Cleaning

- **Daily:** Remove lumps and feces at least once, preferably twice a day. Refill used litter.
- **Regularly:** Depending on the type of litter, replace all the litter every one to four weeks and wash out the toilet with hot water and a mild, odorless dishwashing liquid. Allow to dry well.
- **No harsh cleaners:** Chlorine, ammonia and strongly scented cleaning agents are unsuitable. Ammonia smells similar to urine to cats.
- **Hygiene:** Wash your hands thoroughly after cleaning. Pregnant women should avoid cleaning the toilet or wearing gloves if possible.

Plastic toilets become scratched over time and odors become trapped in the scratches. Replace the toilet about once a year.

## The toilet as a health mirror

A daily look into the toilet reveals a lot about your cat's health. Pay attention to:

- **Size and number of urine clumps:** More or larger clumps may indicate kidney disease, diabetes, or hyperthyroidism.
- **Many small lumps:** typical of a bladder infection.
- **Blood in the urine:** reddish discoloration, often associated with cystitis or urinary stones.
- **Feces:** Consistency, color, quantity, worms or blood.
- **No urine clumps despite frequent trips to the toilet:** Emergency, especially with tomcats.

## Get your kitten used to the toilet

Kittens usually learn to use the toilet from their mother, and many are already house trained when they go to their new home at twelve weeks. Offer small kittens a low-sided toilet, place them in it after eating and sleeping, and praise them when they use it. Several toilets in the apartment make it easier to get used to it.

## Toilets for older and sick cats

As cats get older, going to the toilet becomes more difficult for many cats. Osteoarthritis makes climbing over a high edge painful, failing eyesight makes it difficult to walk in the dark, and kidney disease leads to more urine, so the cat sometimes doesn't make it in time. Therefore, offer older cats a large toilet with a low entrance, such as a shallow tub or box with a piece cut out on the side. Place an extra toilet near your favorite sleeping spot to keep the walk short, and provide a small light at night. If something goes wrong, it's not misbehavior, but rather an indication that the cat needs support. More about this can be found in [Accompanying the Old Cat](/katzenwissen/die-alte-katze-begleiten/).

## Where should the toilet go in small apartments?

In a small apartment, the perfect location is often difficult to find. The bathroom with a permanently open door, a quiet corner in the hallway where there is not constant traffic, or a place in the bedroom are well suited. Toilet cabinets in which the cat litter box disappears into a piece of furniture look tidy. However, they have the same disadvantages as a hood: smells accumulate and the cat has no overview. If you use such a cabinet, make sure it has a large interior space, good ventilation and particularly thorough cleaning.

## Litter, environment and disposal

Mineral clumping litter is obtained from bentonite, a clay that is mined in opencast mines. It is heavy and creates a lot of waste. Clumping plant litter made from wood, corn or other renewable raw materials is lighter and is also readily accepted by many cats. When it comes to disposal, the following applies in most communities: Used cat litter belongs in the residual waste, even if it is advertised as compostable, because cat feces can contain pathogens. Find out from your municipality what rules apply locally. If you want to change the type of sprinkle, gradually mix the new one into the old one.

## The toilet on the way

For longer car trips, moving or vacationing with a cat, there are foldable travel toilets made of coated fabric or plastic. Fill it with the usual litter so that the cat recognizes it and offer it during breaks in the closed car or immediately after arriving at the new place.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I put the litter box in the bathroom?

Yes, if the door is always left open and the toilet is not right next to the noisy washing machine.

### Can I flush cat litter down the toilet?

No, even if some litter is advertised as “flushable”. Cat feces can contain pathogens, and litter can clog pipes. Dispose of them in the residual waste or, if the municipality and the manufacturer allow it, in the organic waste.

### My cat no longer paws at the store. Is that worrying?

Some cats scratch little or not at all. If a cat suddenly changes this and only enters the toilet for a short time or balances on the edge, something about the toilet may be bothering him or he is in pain. Check litter and cleanliness and have it checked if there are any further abnormalities.

### Does an activated carbon filter in the hood help?

A filter in a hooded toilet traps some of the odors, making them less noticeable to people. This doesn't change much for the cat, because it sits in the middle of the odor cloud. The most important thing to combat odors is daily cleaning.
