Hardly any other question is discussed as passionately among cat owners as this one: wet food or dry food? Some people swear by cans and bowls, others swear by a bowl of croquettes that stays full all day. The truth, as so often, is not an either/or. But there are good reasons why many veterinarians recommend giving wet food a permanent place in your diet.

To understand the question, it's worth taking a look at how cats originally lived and ate.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Cats are pure carnivores and cover a large part of their water needs through food.
> - Wet food contains a lot of water, dry food contains very little. This is important for the kidneys and urinary tract.
> - Dry food is very high in energy. Anyone who offers it freely accessible risks becoming overweight.
> - What is crucial is a good complete feed in the right amount, no matter in what form.
> - A combination can make sense if the total calories are right.

## How cats naturally eat

The domestic cat is descended from the African wildcat, an animal from dry regions. It hunts small prey, especially mice, and eats many small meals throughout the day and night. A mouse is about two-thirds to three-quarters water. This is why cats naturally drink little: they are used to getting most of their fluids from their prey. Your feeling of thirst is comparatively weak.

Cats are also real carnivores. They need a lot of animal protein and certain nutrients that are almost only found in animal tissue, such as the amino acid taurine, the fatty acid arachidonic acid and vitamin A in its finished form. A good cat food must contain these substances, whether wet or dry.

## Wet food: lots of water, fewer calories

Wet food consists of around three quarters to four fifths water and is therefore very close to natural prey. A cat that eats mostly wet food will automatically consume a large portion of its fluids, even if it rarely drinks from the bowl.

**Advantages:**

- **More fluids:** This can help dilute the urine. For cats with a tendency to urinary stones, bladder infections or kidney diseases, veterinarians often recommend a high proportion of wet food.
- **Less energy per bite:** Due to the high water content, wet food contains fewer calories per gram. Cats feel full faster without taking in too much energy.
- **High acceptance:** Many cats particularly enjoy eating wet food, which is important for picky or sick animals.

**Disadvantages:**

- **Shelf Life:** Opened cans must be stored in the refrigerator and should be used within one to two days. Wet food becomes unappetizing after a few hours in the bowl, and more quickly in the summer.
- **Costs and waste:** Wet food is usually more expensive per daily ration than dry food and creates more packaging waste.
- **Organization:** Multiple fresh portions a day require more planning, especially for working people.

## Dry food: practical but energy dense

Dry food only contains around eight to ten percent water. Every gram contains many times the energy of wet food.

**Advantages:**

- **Practical:** It doesn't spoil so quickly in the bowl, is easy to dose and can be stored for a long time.
- **Activity:** Dry food is well suited for food balls and fiddling boards with which the cat has to work for its meal.
- **Cost-effective:** Usually cheaper per daily ration.

**Disadvantages:**

- **Little water:** Cats that only eat dry food drink more, but often do not completely make up for the difference. Your urine is more concentrated.
- **High energy density:** A handful too much a day is hardly noticeable, but over months it adds up to significant excess weight. If the bowl is constantly full, many cats, especially neutered indoor cats, eat more than they need.
- **More carbohydrates:** Making croquettes usually requires a certain amount of starchy ingredients. Cats can utilize carbohydrates, but they are designed for a diet rich in protein.

A common myth says that dry food cleans your teeth. Normal dry food has hardly any cleansing effect because cats usually swallow the kibble whole or only bite briefly. There are special dental care foods with a special structure that can have a certain effect, but they do not replace a dental check-up at the vet.

## The comparison at a glance

| | Wet food | Dry food |
|---|---|---|
| Water content | about 75 to 80 percent | about 8 to 10 percent |
| Energy per 100 grams | low | very high, many times over |
| Durability in the bowl | a few hours | longer |
| urinary tract and kidneys | favorable due to lots of liquid | less favorable if the cat drinks little |
| Risk of obesity | lower | higher with free access |
| Employment | restricted | good for food toys |
| Cost per day | higher | lower |

## It's the mixture that does it

For many cats, a combination is a good solution: wet food as main meals, plus a measured, small amount of dry food distributed throughout the day in food toys. This way the cat gets plenty of fluids but still has something to do when no one is at home.

It is important to keep an eye on the total amount. The feeding recommendations on the packages apply to feeding this product alone. If you combine, you have to reduce both quantities accordingly. An example: If according to the package your cat needs either four bowls of wet food or sixty grams of dry food a day, with a mixture it could be three bowls and fifteen grams of dry food. The exact amount depends on your cat, its age, activity level and whether it is neutered.

## An example of a daily schedule

This is what a day of mixed feeding might look like for an adult, neutered indoor cat. The exact amounts depend on the information on your food and your cat's weight:

- **Morning:** a portion of wet food, slightly warmed.
- **During the day:** a small, pre-measured amount of dry food, distributed in a food ball or food board, so the cat can work on its meal while no one is home.
- **Evening:** a second portion of wet food after a game round.
- **Late evening:** a small portion of wet food or part of the daily ration as a snack before going to bed, which helps many cats get through the night more calmly.
- **All day:** fresh water in several places in the apartment, not directly next to the food.

Weigh the total amount in the morning and distribute it throughout the day. Whatever is left in the food ball in the evening will not be refilled. Weigh your cat every two to four weeks to see if the amount is right.

## What really counts: quality and quantity

No matter whether wet or dry: The food should be labeled as **complete feed**. Only then will it contain all the nutrients a cat needs. However, many snacks, pastes and some wet food specialties are **supplementary feed** and are not suitable as daily main food.

A high price is not automatically a sign of quality, and cheap food is not automatically bad. We explain how to read labels and compare types of food in our own guide.

And finally, quantity is crucial. Most nutritional problems in domestic cats arise not from the wrong type of food, but from too much food. Weigh your cat regularly and adjust portions.

## When should the vet have a say?

For some illnesses, the choice of food is part of the treatment, for example urinary stones, chronic kidney disease, diabetes or food allergies. The vet then often prescribes a special diet food, which can be available in wet or dry form. In such cases, don't change anything on your own initiative, but rather talk to the practice.

## Store food properly

Even the best food loses quality if it is stored incorrectly. It is best to store dry food in its original packaging, which you close tightly or place in a tightly fitting container. This means that the batch and shelf life information is retained and the fats in the feed are less likely to go rancid. Store it in a cool, dry and dark place and use an opened bag within a few weeks. Large storage packs are only beneficial if they are used up in good time, as food mites can multiply in old, open bags.

Cover opened wet food cans and put them in the refrigerator. Do not offer the food cold in the fridge, but let it come to room temperature or warm it slightly with a little warm water. Cold food hardly smells, is less well accepted and is vomited by some cats shortly after eating.

## Cook it yourself or feed it raw?

Some owners prepare the food themselves, cooked or raw. This can work, but it's more challenging than it sounds. Cats need, among other things, taurine, certain vitamins and a precise ratio of calcium to phosphorus. If any of it is missing for a long time, there is a risk of heart problems, vision problems or bone damage. Raw meat can also contain pathogens such as salmonella, which also pose a risk to people in the household. If you want to feed yourself, have a veterinarian with additional qualifications in animal nutrition calculate a ration and stick to the plan exactly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a cat live healthily on dry food alone?

Many cats grow old on high-quality dry food. What is then important are sufficient water points, precise quantity control and regular examinations. For cats with urinary tract or kidney problems, a higher proportion of wet food is usually better.

### Should I soak dry food?

This can increase the water content. However, soaked dry food spoils quickly and should not be left in the bowl for more than an hour.

### Can the dry food bowl be filled all day?

This can work for slim, active cats that regulate themselves well. In most indoor cats it leads to obesity. It is better to weigh the daily amount and distribute it over the day.

### How many meals a day are ideal?

Cats naturally eat many small meals throughout the day and night. For adult cats, at least two, preferably three to four portions make sense. An automatic feeder with a timer helps if you are not at home during the day.

### My cat only eats dry food. Can I change that?

Often yes, but with patience. Many cats imprint themselves on one type of food at a young age. At the beginning, only mix in small amounts of wet food or offer it slightly warmed as an extra, and increase the proportion over weeks.
