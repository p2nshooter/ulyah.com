Cat claws are amazing tools. They are retractable to prevent wear and tear when running, sharp enough to climb and hold onto prey, and grow in layers that peel off when scratched. For most cats, the length of their claws is limited by scratching, climbing and running.

However, there are cats whose claws become too long: indoor cats with little opportunity to scratch, older cats who scratch less and whose claws become thicker, and cats with extra toes. Claws that are too long can get caught, click when walking on smooth floors, break off or even grow into the ball of the foot. This guide shows you how to tell if nail care is necessary and how to do it safely.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Many cats do not need claw care. Only cut if the claws are really too long.
> - Older cats and their thumb claws, which are not worn out when scratching, are particularly at risk.
> - Only cut off the transparent tip, never the pink “life” with blood vessels and nerves.
> - Use sharp nail clippers for cats, not nail clippers for humans.
> - If you are unsure, have it shown to you at the vet's office.

## Do cats need claw care?

For young, healthy outdoor cats and indoor cats with good scratching opportunities, claw trimming is usually unnecessary. The claws are groomed by scratching, and the outer covering sheds periodically.

Care may be necessary for:

- **Older cats:** With age, the claws become thicker and more brittle, the cat scratches less, and the claws can no longer retract properly.
- **Indoor cats with little exercise**
- **the thumb claws:** The claw on the inside of the front paw, the so-called thumb or dewclaw, hardly touches anything when running and scratching and is therefore hardly worn out. In some cats it grows in an arch in the pads.
- **Cats with extra toes,** so-called polydactyls, whose extra claws are often in an awkward position.
- **Cats with osteoarthritis or other illnesses** that scratch less.

## How to recognize claws that are too long

- The claws clack on tiles or parquet.
- The cat gets its claws caught on carpets, blankets or clothing.
- The claws visibly protrude above the fur, even when the cat is relaxed.
- One claw is strongly curved and approaches the ball of the foot.
- The cat noticeably licks one paw or limps.

If a claw has already grown into the pad, the area is often inflamed and painful. Then you shouldn't cut it yourself, but go to the vet's office.

## The structure of the claw

Before you cut, you should know how a cat's claw is constructed. When you gently squeeze the paw, the claw extends. If the claws are light-colored, you will see a pink area extending from the toe. This is “life”, a tissue with blood vessels and nerves. If it is injured, it bleeds and hurts.

Only the transparent, hard tip in front of the pink area is cut. Maintain a safe distance of around two millimeters from life. With dark claws, life is difficult to see. Then only cut off very small pieces of the tip, preferably too little rather than too much.

## The right tool

- **Claw scissors for cats:** There are small scissors with curved blades or pliers with a cutout for the claw. It is important that they are sharp. Blunt scissors crush the claw, which is uncomfortable and can cause it to splinter.
- **Not nail scissors for humans:** They are not made for the round shape of cat claws and crush easily.
- **Hemostatic powder or cotton wool:** In case you happen to die.
- **Good light** and **treat** as a reward.

## This is how you get your cat used to claw care

Most cats don't like having their paws touched. That's why it's worth practicing claw care in small steps, long before it's necessary.

1. **Touch paws:** While stroking, briefly touch your cat's paws again and again when he is relaxed. Reward her.
2. **Hold the paw:** Hold the paw loosely in your hand for a few seconds. Reward.
3. **Extend claws:** Gently press the ball of the ball so that one claw extends and release again. Reward.
4. **Show scissors:** Let your cat sniff the claw scissors. Hold it near the paw without cutting. Reward.
5. **Cut a claw:** Cut the tip of a single claw and reward immediately. It doesn't have to be more than that at the beginning.

Many cats have their claws trimmed using this method after a few weeks. It's okay to trim just two or three nails one day and the rest the next day.

## Step by step: cutting claws

1. **Choose a quiet moment:** It's best to do this when the cat is sleepy, for example on your lap.
2. **Hold the cat:** Place him on your lap with his back to you, or let him lie next to you on a stable surface. Some cats are calmer when a second person gently holds and pets them.
3. **Take the paw and extend the claw:** Gently press the ball of the foot and the top of the toe with your thumb and forefinger.
4. **Cutting:** Place the scissors across the claw, in front of the pink area, and cut off the tip with a quick movement.
5. **Don't forget the thumb claw:** It sits a little higher on the inside of the front paw.
6. **Hind paws:** The claws on the hind paws usually wear better and need to be cut less often.
7. **Reward:** There is a treat after every claw or paw.

Stop if the cat resists. A short, pleasant experience is more important than completely trimmed nails.

## If it bleeds

If you accidentally hit life, the claw will bleed. Stay calm. Press some styptic powder, cornstarch, or clean cotton onto the tip of the claw until the bleeding stops, usually after a few minutes. Finish grooming for the day and give your cat an extra good reward. If the bleeding doesn't stop, call the vet's office.

## How often?

That is very different. For cats that need claw care, checking every two to six weeks is often enough. Older cats need more frequent attention, especially around their thumb claws. Check the claws during regular grooming.

## What you shouldn't do

- **Have claws removed:** Surgical removal of claws, in which the last phalanx of the toes is amputated, is prohibited in Germany under the Animal Welfare Act and is painful and harmful for cats.
- **Cut claws very short to prevent scratching:** This takes away the cat's ability to climb and hold on, and it does not solve the scratching problem. Scratching is a need that requires good scratching opportunities.
- **Cut the claws of outdoor animals:** They need their claws to climb, escape and defend themselves.

## Help in practice

If your cat refuses to be touched, you are unsure, or the claws are very thick and ingrown, have the claws trimmed at the vet's office. Many practices do this as part of the check-up and show you how to do it.

## Scratching is the best way to care for claws

The cat takes care of its own claws most naturally by scratching. The old, outer horny layers of the claws loosen, the new ones underneath appear, and the tips remain at a good length. The more attractive scratching options your cat has, the less often you have to resort to scissors yourself. Offer vertical scratching posts where she can fully stretch and horizontal scratching boards made of corrugated cardboard or sisal, as many cats prefer to scratch while lying down or on the floor. ## Ingrown claws: what the practice does

In very old or sick cats, claws sometimes grow unnoticed in an arc into the pad. The cat is limping, licking its paw, and the area is red, oozing, or smelly. In the veterinary practice, the claw is carefully shortened and removed from the pad, the wound is cleaned and, if necessary, treated with painkillers or an antibiotic. The bunion usually heals quickly afterwards. So that it doesn't happen again, you should check this cat's claws regularly in the future, for example every time you brush him and especially keep an eye on the thumb claws.

## Checking claws as part of the routine

Claw care is easiest when it becomes a regular part of your care routine. During your weekly grooming, take a quick look at the paws: Are the claws too long, is there something caught between the pads, are there redness or injuries? A cat that knows how to touch its paws from an early age will also be easier to treat when it gets older or injured. ## Frequently asked questions

### My cat has lost loose, hollow claws. Is that normal?

Yes. These are the old outer coverings that come off when scratched. A new claw has already grown underneath.

### Can I shorten the claws with a file?

Some holders file the tips lightly after cutting to smooth any sharp edges. However, most cats don't like filing more than they like a quick cut.

### My older cat has very thick claws. Is that worrying?

Thick, brittle claws are common in older cats. If they can no longer be retracted or change significantly, talk to your vet, as sometimes claw or thyroid diseases are behind this.

### Do I have to cut a kitten's claws?

Mostly not. The claws of young cats are very sharp, but they wear out well when they scratch and climb a lot. But kitten time is ideal for practicing touching their paws. If the needle-sharp claws get in the way while playing, you can carefully shorten just the very tip.
