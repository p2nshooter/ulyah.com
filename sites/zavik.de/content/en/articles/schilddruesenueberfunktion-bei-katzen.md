The cat is fourteen years old, eats more than ever before, constantly begs for food and is still getting thinner. She is restless, calls at night, drinks more than before, and her fur looks shaggy. Many owners are initially happy about their old cat's good appetite and don't think of anything bad. But it is precisely this combination that is a classic picture of hyperthyroidism, the most common hormonal disorder in older cats.

The disease is easily treatable, and most cats live with a good quality of life for many years after diagnosis. However, if left untreated, it puts a strain on the heart, circulatory system and kidneys and literally causes the cat to starve, even though it eats. This guide explains how you can recognize the disease, how the vet diagnoses it and what treatment options are available.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Hyperthyroidism occurs primarily in cats aged around eight to ten years.
> - Typical symptoms include weight loss despite a large appetite, restlessness, increased drinking, vomiting and shaggy fur.
> - The cause is usually a benign growth of the thyroid gland that produces too much hormone.
> - Treatment is with tablets or gel, a low-iodine diet, radioiodine therapy or, more rarely, surgery.
> - After starting treatment, thyroid and kidney values ​​must be checked regularly.

## What the thyroid does

The thyroid gland sits as two small lobes on the left and right of the trachea in the neck. It produces hormones that control the metabolism of the whole body: how quickly energy is used, how fast the heart beats, how warm the body is. If it is overactive, it produces too much of it. The metabolism is then constantly running at full speed. The cat burns more energy than it can absorb, its heart beats faster, and many organs are permanently overloaded.

In cats, the cause is almost always a benign enlargement or growth of thyroid tissue, usually on both sides. Malignant thyroid tumors are rare in cats. Why the disease has become so common is not fully understood. Among other things, environmental influences and components of feed or packaging are discussed, but a clear trigger has not been proven.

## The typical signs

The disease begins gradually, and the first signs are easily mistaken for normal signs of aging.

- **Weight loss despite good or increased appetite:** the most important warning sign
- **Hungry:** The cat is constantly begging, eats quickly and a lot, sometimes even unusual things
- **Increased drinking and urination:** Larger clumps of urine in the toilet
- **Restlessness and restlessness:** The cat appears driven, sleeps less, calls loudly at night
- **Vomiting and diarrhea:** often shortly after eating, plus large amounts of feces
- **Shaggy, unkempt coat:** sometimes with hair loss or matted areas
- **Rapid heartbeat** and sometimes audibly rapid breathing
- **Irritability:** Some cats become more impatient or sensitive to touch

In a smaller proportion of cats, the disease progresses differently: they become tired, eat less and lose weight. This picture should also make you think of the thyroid in an older cat. Further warning signs that indicate illnesses in old age are described in [Recognizing signs of illness] (/katzenwissen/krankheitsanzeichen-erkennen/).

## How the diagnosis is made

During the examination, the veterinarian feels the neck. An enlarged thyroid can often be felt as a small, mobile nodule beneath the larynx. She listens to the heart and lungs, measures blood pressure and weighs the cat. The blood test is crucial: an increased level of the thyroid hormone T4 confirms the diagnosis in most cats. If the value is in the borderline range even though the cat shows typical signs, repeating it after a few weeks or further tests will help.

At the same time, kidney values, liver values ​​and a blood count are determined, because many cats with hyperthyroidism have other age-related diseases. If heart problems are suspected, an ultrasound examination of the heart may be useful.

## Why treatment is important

Untreated hyperthyroidism progresses. The heart has to work faster and harder over the long term, and this can cause the heart muscle to thicken. Blood pressure rises, which damages the eyes, kidneys and brain. High blood pressure can cause retinal detachments and sudden blindness in cats. At the same time, the cat breaks down muscles and becomes increasingly thin. The good news: Many of these changes improve when the thyroid returns to normal functioning.

## The treatment options

Which treatment is best depends on the cat's health, its character, other illnesses and the owners' options.

| Treatment | How it works | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medication (tablets, solution or gel) | inhibit hormone production | Available everywhere, easy to control | Lifelong daily administration, regular checks, possible side effects |
| low iodine diet | Without iodine, the thyroid can hardly produce hormones | no tablets | only effective if the cat only eats this food |
| Radioiodine therapy | radioactive iodine destroys the overactive tissue | usually permanent healing with one treatment | only in a few specialized facilities, inpatient stay |
| Operation | Removal of the altered thyroid | permanent solution possible | Risk of anesthesia, danger to the parathyroid glands, rarer today |

### Medication

The most common treatment is medication that inhibits the production of thyroid hormones. They are usually given once or twice a day, as a tablet, as a liquid or as a gel that is applied to the inside of the ear and works through the skin. The effects begin after a few days to weeks. Treatment must be continued for life because it does not cure the disease but controls it.

Possible side effects include loss of appetite, vomiting, tiredness, itching of the face and rarely changes in blood counts or liver values. Report such observations to the practice immediately. [Enter medication](/katzenwissen/medikamente-eingeben/) explains how best to enter tablets. Wear gloves when applying the gel so that you do not absorb the active ingredient through your skin, and wash your hands afterwards.

### Low iodine diet

The thyroid needs iodine to make its hormones. A special diet food with very little iodine can therefore significantly reduce hormone production. But this only works if the cat really doesn't eat anything else: no treats, no other types of food, no prey and no food from its roommates. This is difficult to implement for outdoor cats and multi-cat households. For indoor cats who like the food, it can be a good alternative to tablets.

### Radioiodine therapy

During radioiodine therapy, the cat receives an injection of radioactive iodine. It accumulates exclusively in the overactive thyroid tissue and destroys it, while the healthy tissue is largely spared. For most cats, the disease is permanently cured and tablets are no longer necessary. Due to radiation protection regulations, the cat must remain in the specialized facility for some time after treatment until the radiation has subsided. In Germany only a few clinics offer this therapy. Beforehand, tablets are usually used to test how the kidneys react to normal thyroid levels.

### Operation

During the operation, the changed thyroid tissue is removed. The method can cure the disease, but carries risks: anesthesia is more stressful for older cats with heart changes, and the tiny parathyroid glands that control calcium levels can be injured. Since effective medications and radioiodine therapy have been available, surgery has become less common.

## Thyroid and kidneys

Many older cats have hyperthyroidism and early kidney disease at the same time. Hyperfunction increases blood flow to the kidneys and can therefore mask kidney weakness. If thyroid levels drop during treatment, it sometimes only becomes apparent how severely the kidneys are actually impaired. That's why, after starting therapy, the veterinarian carefully checks not only the thyroid but also the kidney values ​​and coordinates the treatment of both diseases. More information can be found in [Renal Insufficiency in Cats](/katzenwissen/niereninsuffizienz-bei-katzen/).

## Controls and everyday life

After starting drug treatment, the blood is usually checked again after two to three weeks and the dose is adjusted. If the cat is well adjusted, checks every few months are sufficient. Weight, blood pressure, thyroid and kidney values ​​are checked.

You can contribute a lot at home:

- Weigh your cat regularly and record the weight.
- Reliably give medications at the same time and record missed doses.
- Observe appetite, drinking quantity, vomiting, behavior and coat condition.
- During the period of weight gain, offer a high-quality food so that the cat can rebuild lost muscles. [Nutrition of older cats](/katzenwissen/ernaehrung-aelterer-katzen/) describes how senior women are well fed.

Many owners report that after a few weeks of treatment their cat is calmer, more balanced, has a nicer coat and is gaining weight.

## Detect early rather than prevent

There is currently no reliable way to prevent hyperthyroidism. This makes it all the more important to recognize them early. Many veterinarians regularly check the thyroid levels in the blood of cats aged around eight to ten years as part of preventative care, even if there are no symptoms yet. Together with monthly weighing at home, the onset of illness is often noticed before the heart and circulatory system are damaged. A weight loss of a few hundred grams in an older cat with a good appetite is already a good reason to ask.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can hyperthyroidism go away on its own?

No. The disease progresses without treatment. It can be controlled with medication or diet and, in many cases, cured with radioiodine therapy or surgery.

### Is the disease painful?

The enlarged thyroid itself usually does not cause pain. However, the consequences, such as restlessness, nausea, muscle breakdown and cardiac strain, significantly impair well-being. That's why most cats feel noticeably better after starting treatment.

### Can I skip the tablets for once?

Better not. Just a few days without medication causes hormone levels to rise again. If it is difficult to enter, talk to the practice about a different dosage form, such as a liquid or gel for the inside of the ear.

### How long does a cat with hyperthyroidism live?

With treatment, many cats have a good prognosis and live for several years. Other illnesses such as kidney failure or heart changes are crucial. The earlier the disease is detected, the better.
