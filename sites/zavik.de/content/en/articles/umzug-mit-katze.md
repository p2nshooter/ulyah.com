Moving is stressful for people, but it is often a bit of a catastrophe for cats. Within a few days, its entire territory, every smell, every berth and every path, is packed into boxes, strangers carry furniture out, and in the end the cat ends up in an environment that smells of nothing familiar. It's no wonder that many cats react to a move by hiding, losing their appetite, becoming unclean or calling loudly.

The good news: With a little planning, the stress can be significantly reduced. Most cats adapt to their new home within a few days to weeks if they are given time, rest and familiar things. This guide shows what you can do in the weeks before the move, on the day of the move itself and in the period afterwards.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Start weeks in advance: train the carrier, introduce boxes slowly, maintain routines.
> - On moving day, the cat stays in a quiet, closed room with a toilet, water and hiding place, in both the old and new home.
> - In the new home, the cat first explores a single room and then gradually explores the entire apartment.
> - Familiar smells help: unwashed blankets, the scratching post, the usual litter.
> - Outdoor animals remain indoors for at least four to six weeks after moving.

## Why moving is so stressful for cats

Cats are strongly tied to their territory. It gives them security because they know every corner and have left their own scent marks everywhere. A move takes away this security in one fell swoop. Add to that noise, hustle and bustle, strangers and a car journey, all within a short space of time. How strongly a cat reacts depends on its character, age and experiences. Young, curious cats often explore their new apartment on the first evening. Older or anxious cats sometimes need weeks before they can move around in a relaxed manner again.

## Weeks before: the preparation

### Practice transport box and driving

If your cat only knows the transport box from the vet, start training early. Place the box open in the apartment, put a blanket and treats in it and practice closing the door, carrying it and taking short car rides. [Transport box training](/katzenwissen/transportbox-training/) describes how to do this step by step. A cat that goes into its box in a relaxed manner will save you a chase between half-packed boxes on moving day.

### Insert boxes slowly

Set up the first moving boxes a few weeks in advance instead of packing everything in the last few days. Many cats find empty boxes exciting and use them as a hiding place or vantage point. If the apartment changes gradually, the change is less abrupt. If possible, pack the cat's things, such as a scratching post, sleeping places and toys, last.

### Maintain routines

Maintain feeding times, play sessions and rituals as much as possible, even if you are stressed about moving. Especially when everything else changes, fixed processes provide stability. A few minutes of playing with the feather rod in the evening will help the cat relieve tension.

### Vet, chip and address

Moving is a good reason for a health check. Have your cat checked to make sure its vaccinations are up to date, especially if your cat is going to be allowed to go outside in its new home, and if your cat is very anxious, ask about ways to calm them down on moving day. Update the address with the pet registry where your cat's microchip is registered so that it can find its way back to you if the worst happens. If you move to another city, look for a new veterinary practice in good time and note the nearest veterinary clinic with emergency service.

## Cat-proof your new home

Before the cat moves in, you should look at the new apartment through his eyes. Are windows and balconies secured? Are there tilted windows, gaps behind kitchen units or open shafts that she could crawl into? Are there still cables, screws or remnants of renovation work lying around? Fresh paint, solvents and adhesives should be well ventilated. How to secure your balcony and windows can be found in [Make your balcony cat-proof](/katzenwissen/balkon-katzensicher-machen/). Also check whether plants from the previous tenants or in the garden are poisonous to cats.

## Moving day

### A safe room in the old apartment

On moving day itself, the greatest danger is that the cat escapes through an open door in the rush or gets locked in a box or piece of furniture. Therefore, set up a quiet room beforehand that will be cleared out last, such as the bathroom. There is a toilet, water, a hiding place and the transport box. Close the door and hang a clear sign saying something like “Cat in the room, please keep the door closed.” Inform all helpers and the moving company. Some owners also take their cat to a friend's house or a cat boarding facility on moving day, which can be a good solution for very anxious animals.

### The ride

Don't put the cat in the box until everything else has been loaded and you're driving off. The box should be secured in the footwell behind the passenger seat or strapped into the back seat, never in the trunk of the moving truck between furniture. Partially cover the box with a cloth, speak calmly and drive evenly. For long journeys lasting several hours, you can offer a small travel toilet and water in the closed car. Never open the box with the car door open.

### A cat room in the new home

The principle is repeated in the new home: Before the cat is allowed out of the box, you set up a quiet room in which the toilet, water, food, scratching area, hiding place and familiar blankets are already available. The best place is a room where little will happen in the next few days, such as the bedroom. Put the box in, open the door and let the cat decide when to come out. Don't pull them out. While furniture is still being assembled in the other rooms, the door to this room remains closed.

## The first days in the new area

### Explore step by step

If your cat is moving around, eating and using the toilet in its first room in a relaxed manner, you can gradually open other rooms. Many cats prefer to explore their new apartment in the evening or at night when it is quiet. Let her go at her own pace and see where she likes to be. These places are later suitable for scratching posts and places to lie down.

### Use familiar smells

Smells are the most important anchor for cats. Don't wash their blankets and pillows for the first few weeks, put up the old scratching post instead of a new one and use the usual litter. You can also gently rub your cat's cheeks with a soft cloth and touch door frames and furniture edges at cat height. This is how you distribute your own scent in the new environment. Diffusers with synthetic cat pheromones also help some cats.

### Position toilets correctly

Place the litter boxes in their final locations from the start and show them to your cat as he explores the rooms. If the toilet was in the bathroom in the old apartment, the bathroom is also a good place in the new apartment. Be prepared for something to go wrong in the first few days. Clean such areas thoroughly with an enzyme cleaner and stay calm. If the uncleanliness lasts longer, the guide [cat pees in the apartment] (/katzenwissen/katze-pinkelt-in-die-wohnung/) can help.

### Watch eating and drinking

Many cats eat less than usual on moving day and the day after. This is normal. Offer her usual food in small, fresh portions, perhaps slightly warmed. But if your cat doesn't eat anything for more than a day, call the vet's office. Cats that don't eat for a long time can develop dangerous fatty liver disease. This is especially true for overweight cats.

## Several cats and other animals

If several cats move together, tensions can arise between them after the move because they are unsettled and smell different than usual. Give them enough resources in different places, i.e. several toilets, feeding areas and places to retreat, and let them explore the new territory at their own pace. If serious arguments occur, separate them for a few days and gently bring them back together as described in [Reunite a second cat](/katzenwissen/zweitkatze-vergesellschaften/).

## Release after the move

Outdoor animals are particularly at risk after moving. They don't know the area, don't know where their home is, and sometimes even try to find their way back to their old territory if it's not far away. The following applies: An outdoor cat stays indoors for at least four to six weeks after moving, or longer if cats are insecure. Only when the cat is completely relaxed in the new apartment and knows its home as a safe place does the first excursion begin, briefly, in daylight and shortly before a meal. How the first excursions are successful is described in [Begin your release safely](/katzenwissen/freigang-sicher-beginnen/).

If the old home is only a few streets away, your cat may run back there. Inform the new residents and the neighbors of the old apartment, give them your phone number and ask them not to feed the cat. Sometimes it helps to significantly extend the acclimatization period indoors.

## Warning signs after moving

A certain amount of restlessness in the first few days is normal. But you should take the following signs seriously:

| characters | What to do |
|---|---|
| Cat doesn't eat anything for more than a day | Call the vet's office |
| Cat continues to hide permanently after several weeks | Check places to retreat, create peace, get advice |
| frequent trips to the toilet with little urine, blood in the urine | Veterinary practice, for tomcats without urination immediately |
| persistent uncleanliness | Check toilets, check health |
| excessive grooming leading to bald spots | Reduce stress, check with a vet |

Stress can cause bladder infections in cats, which are manifested by frequent trips to the toilet with small amounts of urine. This is an emergency in cats who are straining but cannot urinate.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take for a cat to settle in after moving?

That is very different. Some cats feel at home after a few days, others need several weeks. Older and anxious cats usually take longer. As long as your cat eats, uses the toilet and gradually becomes braver, he is on the right track.

### Should I take the cat with me while the new apartment is being renovated?

Better not. Noise, dust, paint smells and open doors are stressful and dangerous for cats. Only move with your cat once the major work has been completed and the apartment has been aired out.

### Can I give my cat a sedative?

Only after consulting the vet. There are medications that can help very anxious cats before a trip, but they must be appropriate for the cat and its health. Means for people are taboo.

### What if my cat calls at night in the new apartment?

Nighttime calling is common in the first few nights because the cat feels insecure. A game and a small snack before bed will help, as will a place to sleep near you. If it lasts for weeks, you can find more tips in [Nighttime Rage](/katzenwissen/naechtliches-toben/).
