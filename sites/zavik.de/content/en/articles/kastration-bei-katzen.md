A single unneutered female cat can have dozens of kittens over the course of her life, and her offspring can have even more. There are estimated to be hundreds of thousands, and according to some estimates over a million, homeless cats living in Germany, many of them sick, malnourished and plagued by parasites. Almost all of them originally come from domestic cats that were not neutered. Castration is therefore one of the most important measures in animal protection.

But it also has advantages for the individual cat and its people. This guide explains why, when and how to castrate and what changes afterwards.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - During castration, the gonads are removed: the testicles in male cats and the ovaries in female cats.
> - The usual time is around the fifth to sixth month of life, i.e. before sexual maturity.
> - Many cities and municipalities require the castration of outdoor dogs.
> - Neutered cats mark less often, stray less and have a lower risk of some diseases.
> - After castration, the energy requirement decreases. Adjust the amount of food.

## Castration or sterilization?

The terms are often confused. During **castration** the gonads are removed, i.e. the testicles in male cats or the ovaries, sometimes together with the uterus, in female cats. This also means that the production of sex hormones stops. During **sterilization** only the sperm or fallopian tubes are severed. The cat can then no longer reproduce, but remains hormonally active and continues to be in heat or marked. Cats are therefore almost always castrated.

## Why castrate?

### Against unwanted cat suffering

The most important reason is to avoid uncontrolled reproduction. Animal shelters are overflowing with kittens every spring and summer, and farms, allotments and industrial areas are home to colonies of feral cats suffering from diseases such as cat flu, parasites and injuries.

### Compulsory castration for outdoor animals

Many cities and municipalities in Germany have therefore issued so-called cat protection regulations. They oblige owners to have outdoor cats neutered, marked and registered. You can find out whether such a regulation applies where you live from the public order office or the local animal protection association. But even where there is no obligation, castration of outdoor animals is strongly recommended.

### For the she-cat

An unneutered female cat will go into heat regularly as soon as she is sexually mature. She calls loudly and persistently, even at night, rolls around, rubs herself on furniture, and tries to get outside. A heat usually lasts several days and can repeat itself at short intervals if the she-cat is not mated. This is stressful for cats and humans.

Early castration, ideally before the first or second heat, significantly reduces the risk of mammary tumors. These tumors are usually malignant in cats. In addition, neutered kittens cannot develop uterine infections if the uterus has been removed and ovarian diseases.

Hormonal agents to suppress heat, such as injections or tablets, are not a good long-term solution. Long-term use can cause serious side effects, including mammary tumors, diabetes and uterine inflammation.

### For the hangover

An unneutered tomcat marks his territory with strong-smelling urine, even in the apartment. He roams widely, often for days, to find she-cats in heat and fights with other tomcats. This results in bite wounds that can develop into painful abscesses, and viral diseases such as FIV, the so-called cat AIDS, are primarily transmitted through bites. Unneutered male cats are also more likely to be injured in traffic.

## The right time

Cats often become sexually mature at four to six months, some females even earlier. It is therefore common for kittens to be castrated at around five to six months of age, before they go into heat for the first time.

In animal shelters and some veterinarians, cats are also neutered earlier, sometimes from around twelve weeks of age. This early castration is common internationally and studies have shown no serious disadvantages. Young kittens often recover particularly quickly from surgery. You can discuss with the practice which time is best for your cat.

Outdoor animals should definitely be neutered before they are allowed out for the first time.

## How does the operation work?

Castration is a routine procedure under general anesthesia. The cat should be fasting on the day of the operation. The practice will tell you how long before she can stop eating. She can usually drink water until shortly beforehand.

**With a male cat** the testicles are removed through two small incisions on the scrotum. The procedure only takes a few minutes and often no stitches are necessary.

**For the female cat** the procedure is more complex because the abdominal cavity has to be opened. The ovaries and sometimes the uterus are removed through an incision on the abdomen or flank. The wound is stitched, often with stitches that dissolve on their own.

Both cats are usually picked up on the same day as soon as they wake up from anesthesia.

## After the operation

For the first few hours after anesthesia, cats are often dazed and unsteady on their feet. This is how you help her:

- **Warm and Calm:** Give her a quiet, warm spot on the floor, away from elevated surfaces where she might fall.
- **Eating and Drinking:** Offer a small portion of food and water in the evening. Some cats don't have their appetite again until the next day.
- **Check the wound:** Check daily whether the wound is dry and clean. Slight redness is normal, but severe swelling, oozing or pus is not.
- **Prevent Licking:** If the cat licks the wound or picks at the stitches, the wound may become infected or open. A neck collar or a tight-fitting bodysuit helps.
- **Pain medication:** Administer the pain medication provided exactly as directed. Never give painkillers to people.
- **Leave outdoor animals inside:** until the wound has healed, usually around ten to fourteen days.

Call the practice if your cat is still very weak, not eating at all, is vomiting, the wound is bleeding, or has significant changes the day after surgery.

## What changes after castration

### behavior

Most male cats stop marking with urine after neutering, especially if they are neutered early. In older male cats, it can take a few weeks for the hormones to wear off, and sometimes marking persists as a habit. Neutered cats stray less, fight less and are often more even-tempered. But her character, her playfulness and her bond with you don't change.

### Weight

After castration, energy requirements decrease, often by a fifth to a third, while appetite tends to increase. If you continue to feed as before, you will soon have an overweight cat. After neutering, reduce the amount of food you eat, weigh your cat regularly and ensure plenty of exercise and play. There are also special foods for neutered cats with lower energy density.

## Costs

The costs are based on the fee schedule for veterinarians and depend on the effort involved. Because of the larger procedure, neutering a female cat is significantly more expensive than neutering a tomcat. In addition, there is anesthesia, medication and, if necessary, follow-up checks. Ask your practice for a cost estimate. Some animal protection associations and communities support castration campaigns, especially for cats from farm populations or from people with little money.

## Neutering of adult and older cats

It is never too late for castration. Adult male and female cats that come from animal welfare, breeding or a farm can also be neutered. In older cats, the vet often recommends a blood test before anesthesia to check kidneys, liver and other values ​​and to tailor the anesthesia accordingly. In older male cats who have been marking for a long time, the behavior may persist after castration because it has become a habit. Then the measures described in [Cat pees in the apartment](/katzenwissen/katze-pinkelt-in-die-wohnung/) also help. If a cat is currently in heat, many practices postpone the procedure for a few days because the tissues then have more blood supply. The operation is also possible then.

## Preparation for the day of surgery

Get your cat used to the transport box weeks in advance so that the trip to the practice isn't the first shock of the day. [Transport box training](/katzenwissen/transportbox-training/) explains how this works. Follow the practice's instructions exactly as to when your cat is last allowed to eat and put away leftover food the evening before. Water can usually remain standing until shortly before the appointment. Place a soft, familiar blanket in the crate and prepare a quiet, warm place at home for the return.

If there are several cats in the household, hissing may occur upon return because the cat that was operated on smells of practice and disinfectant. Then let them rest in their own room for a few hours, rub them with a cloth that smells like home, and then calmly reunite the cats.

## Castration campaigns and street cats

Many animal welfare associations take care of homeless cats that live in backyards, on farms or in allotments. They capture the animals, have them castrated, tagged and given medical care and then release them back to their usual place where they continue to be fed. In this way, the misery of uncontrolled reproduction is curbed step by step. If you notice unneutered, homeless cats in your area, contact your local animal welfare association. Some clubs and communities also offer discounted castrations for owners with low incomes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Shouldn't a she-cat even have had kittens?

No. This is a common myth. There is no health or emotional reason why a female cat should have a litter before being neutered. On the contrary: Castration before the first heat is the best way to protect against mammary tumors.

### Will my cat become lazy after neutering?

Neutered male cats are often calmer, but not lazy. If you continue to play with them and keep them busy, you have an active cat. Sluggishness is more related to being overweight.

### Does an indoor cat have to be neutered?

Indoor cats also benefit from castration. Unneutered male cats mark in the apartment, and unneutered female cats suffer from repeated heat events. In addition, cats sometimes escape, and a single encounter is enough to produce a litter.
