It's three o'clock in the morning. Something thunders through the hallway that can't possibly weigh just four kilograms. Then a jump onto the bed, a jump down, the clatter of an object being knocked over in the living room. At five o'clock the cat sits on your chest, taps its paw on your nose or meows next to your ear until you get up and fill the bowl.

Nocturnal activity is one of the most common complaints from cat owners. The good news: In most cases, the problem can be significantly improved by understanding why cats are active at night and adjusting their daily routine a little.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Cats are primarily active at dawn and dusk and are not awake at night like owls.
> - A lot of sleep during the day and little activity lead to excess energy at night.
> - An intensive game session followed by a meal before going to bed is the most effective remedy.
> - Don't respond to nighttime awakenings with food or attention, otherwise the cat will learn that it's worth it.
> - If older cats suddenly call a lot at night, have them examined.

## Why cats are active at night

Cats are not purely nocturnal animals, but are primarily active at dusk. Their prey, mice and other small rodents, are out and about at dusk, and the cat's rhythm has adapted to this. Their eyes are specialized for dim light, and their hunting instinct is strongest in the early morning and evening.

In addition, indoor cats often sleep for many hours during the day, especially when their humans are out of the house. When everyone sits on the sofa tired in the evening and goes to bed early, the cat still has a lot of energy that has to go somewhere. The result is the well-known nightly running through the apartment, often seemingly for no reason.

Kittens and young cats are particularly active. Many become calmer around the age of two.

## The principle: hunt, eat, clean, sleep

In nature, a cat's behavior often follows a cycle: it hunts, captures something, eats, grooms itself and sleeps. You can use this cycle to prepare your cat for the night.

**This is what a good evening ritual looks like:**

1. **About an hour before bed:** an intensive session of play with a feather fishing rod or other toy that mimics prey. Let the cat sneak, rush, jump and catch. Ten to fifteen minutes is often enough for her to stop panting or breathing heavily. Don't stop abruptly, but let the game slow down, like a prey that is exhausted, and let the cat make the catch in the end.
2. **Immediately afterwards:** a meal. Take part of the daily ration, not additional food.
3. **Afterwards** the cat will usually groom itself and look for a place to sleep.

Many cat owners report that the nights calm down significantly after just a few days of this ritual.

## More activity during the day

A cat that sleeps alone in a low-stimulus apartment for twelve hours during the day can hardly be tired in the evening. More stimulation during the day helps:

- **Food toys:** Distribute some of the dry food in food balls, fiddle boards or small hiding places. This means the cat has to work for its meals even when no one is at home.
- **Window Seats:** A spot with a view outside, perhaps of a birdhouse, is like watching TV for many cats.
- **Changing toys:** Put toys away after a few days and bring them out later. What's new is more interesting.
- **Cartons and paper bags** without handles offer variety for climbing and hiding.
- **A second cat:** For young, very active single cats, a suitable playmate can be a great help, even against nighttime romps.
- **Play in the morning:** If possible, have a quick game before you leave the house.

## Early waking: What the cat learns

If your cat wakes you up at five o'clock and you get up to feed him, he learns: It's worth waking him up. With each success she becomes more stubborn. Likewise, when you pet her, talk to her, or even just carry her into the living room, she learns that her behavior brings attention.

The solution sounds simple and yet is difficult: don't react. Stay there, close your eyes, don't talk to her. This doesn't mean you should scold them, because scolding is also attention.

Expect things to get worse at first. When a previously successful behavior suddenly no longer works, cats initially try it more intensively: meow louder, tap more, throw objects from the bedside table. Experts call this a deletion rebellion. If you persevere, the behavior will usually decrease significantly afterwards.

**Also helpful are:**

- **An automatic feeder with a timer:** It opens at the desired time, around six o'clock, and feeds the cat without you having to get up. The cat then associates the food with the machine instead of with you.
- **Get up first, then feed:** Don't feed immediately after getting up, go to the bathroom first, make yourself some coffee. This means there is no direct connection between your waking up and the food.
- **Put away items:** Anything that can be thrown off the bedside table belongs in a drawer in the evening.

## The bedroom door: open or closed?

Whether your cat can sleep in the bedroom at night is your decision. It is important that you remain consistent. If the door is sometimes open and sometimes closed, the cat will scratch and call in front of it again and again because it's worth it every now and then.

If you want to close the door at night, make the rest of the home attractive for the night: a comfortable, warm place to sleep, water, a toilet, and perhaps a food toy. If the cat initially scratches at the door, a carpet runner or an upside-down scratching board in front of the door can reduce the noise. The same applies here: Don't open the door when she calls.

## When nighttime restlessness is a warning sign

If a cat that was previously quiet suddenly becomes very active at night, calls loudly or wanders around, there may be an illness behind it, especially in older animals:

- **Hyperthyroidism:** Restlessness, cravings, weight loss, increased drinking.
- **High blood pressure:** can cause agitation, disorientation and vision problems, often associated with kidney disease.
- **Pain,** for example due to osteoarthritis, which increases at night when lying down.
- **Decreased vision and hearing:** Cats with poor vision or hearing call more often at night because they feel unsafe.
- **Age-related mental changes,** similar to dementia, with disorientation and nocturnal calling.

In such cases, have your cat examined. Many of these causes can be treated. For older cats with diminishing senses, a small night light and a place to sleep near their humans often help.

## Unneutered cats

Unneutered female cats often cry loudly for hours during heat, even at night. Unneutered male cats are restless when female cats in heat are nearby. Castration usually solves this problem.

## Games that really make you tired

Not every game is stressful for a cat. A ball lying alone in the corner or a mouse that doesn't move become boring after a few seconds. What makes you tired is the game that imitates a real hunt: lurking, sneaking up, running after, jumping and finally catching. The best way to do this is with a spring rod that you move like a fleeing animal, sometimes quickly, sometimes hesitantly, disappearing behind a piece of furniture and reappearing again. Vary the height, let the prey escape onto the scratching post, and give the cat short breaks to lurk. A kicker, i.e. an elongated cushion that you can hug and kick, is ideal for finishing off because the cat can then properly grab the prey. If you play with a laser dot, let it land on a toy at the end for the cat to catch.

## Everyone in the household pulls together

Ignoring nighttime wake-ups only works if everyone cooperates. If a person does get up and feeds, the cat learns that persistence is worth it and the waking becomes even more persistent. Therefore, discuss in the household who will feed when, what the evening ritual is and that no one reacts at night. A shared plan on the fridge helps to include guests and children.

## Kittens at night

Young cats have a lot of energy and don't yet know a fixed rhythm. But they also need a lot of sleep, often more than adult cats. Several rounds of games throughout the day and a long round in the evening help to direct your energy in the right direction. A second kitten is the best play partner for many young cats, even at night, and people sleep more peacefully. ## Frequently asked questions

### My cat runs around the apartment like crazy every night. Is that normal?

Short, wild periods of running, often called “zoomies,” are very common in young cats and are usually harmless. They dissipate excess energy. More play in the evening helps.

### Can I give my cat something to calm him down?

Do not give sedatives without a veterinary examination. Many remedies for humans are dangerous for cats. Changes in your daily routine usually help more than any other remedy.

### How long does it take until the nights get quieter?

With a consistent evening ritual and ignoring the wake-up call, many people notice an improvement after just one to two weeks. If your habits are very strong, it may take longer.

### Can I leave my outdoor dog outside at night?

This is not recommended. In the dark, the risk from cars, turf wars and wild animals is higher. Bring your outdoor animal in in the evening with a fixed feeding time and offer him a play session inside so that he spends the night peacefully.
