Uncleanliness is one of the most common reasons why cats are surrendered to animal shelters. Hardly anything puts as much strain on living together as a cat that urinates on the bed, sofa or carpet. Many owners are desperate, some are angry, and quite a few believe that their cat wants revenge because it was left alone or because a new partner has moved in.

But cats don't know revenge. When a cat avoids the toilet, it has a reason, and that reason is important to it. The job of their humans is to find him. This guide shows you how to do it.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Get your cat checked by a vet first. Bladder infections, urinary stones, pain and other illnesses are common causes.
> - Distinguish between urinating on horizontal surfaces and marking on vertical surfaces.
> - Check the toilet: number, size, location, litter and cleanliness.
> - Thoroughly remove odors with an enzyme cleaner, not ammonia or vinegar.
> - Never punish. It makes the problem worse.

## Step 1: rule out medical causes

Before you think about reasons for the behavior, your cat should be examined. Many cases of uncleanliness have physical causes:

- **Cystitis:** In cats, the cause is often not a bacterial infection, but rather a so-called idiopathic cystitis, an inflammation of the bladder that is closely related to stress. The cat has pain when urinating, goes to the toilet frequently, and only passes small amounts, sometimes with blood. Many cats then associate the toilet with pain and look for other places, often soft surfaces such as a bed or carpet.
- **Urinary stones and crystals:** They irritate the bladder and can block the urethra in male cats. Pushing without urine is an emergency.
- **Kidney disease, diabetes, hyperthyroidism:** They lead to larger amounts of urine. Sometimes the cat doesn't make it to the toilet in time, or the toilet fills up quickly.
- **Osteoarthritis:** Older cats with joint pain may have difficulty climbing the high edge of the toilet or the stairs to the toilet.
- **Constipation or diarrhea:** If you have pain when defecating, avoid using the toilet to defecate.
- **Cognitive changes** in very old cats.

If possible, take a fresh urine sample with you or ask the practice how you can obtain it. There are special, non-absorbent granules for the cat litter box that can be used to collect urine.

## Step 2: Urinate or Mark?

For further search, it is important to know whether your cat is urinating or marking.

**Urinating:** The cat squats down and releases a large amount of urine on a horizontal surface, such as the carpet, bed, pile of clothes, or in the bathtub. This usually indicates a problem with the toilet, pain or stress.

**Mark:** The cat stands with its tail raised, often trembling, in front of a vertical surface and squirts a small amount of urine against it, for example on walls, doors, curtains or furniture. This is a message to other cats and is related to the territory. Unneutered male cats almost always mark, but neutered cats of both sexes can also mark when they are unsettled.

## Step 3: check the litter box

Many cases of uncleanliness can be solved by looking at the toilet from the cat's perspective.

**Number:** The rule of thumb is: one more toilet than cats in the household. So two cats need three toilets, in different places, not next to each other.

**Size:** Many cat litter boxes are too small. The cat should be able to turn and scratch in it without protruding over the edge. A large plastic storage box is often better than a standard toilet.

**Hood:** Some cats avoid closed toilets because they accumulate odors and have no escape route. Offer an open version as a test.

**Litter:** Most cats prefer fine, clumping, unscented litter at a height of a few centimeters. A sudden change in litter can cause uncleanliness. Fragrance additives and scented litter bother many cats.

**Cleanliness:** Cats have a much more sensitive nose than humans. Remove clumps at least once a day, preferably twice a day, and clean the entire toilet regularly with hot water and mild dish soap. Strong cleaning agents with smells are unsuitable.

**Location:** quiet, easily accessible, not next to food and water, not next to loud equipment. In multi-cat households, no cat should be able to intercept another in front of the toilet. A toilet in a dead end, such as at the end of a narrow hallway, can become a trap for an insecure cat.

**Liners and Mats:** Some cats don't like liners or liners in the toilet because their claws get caught in them.

## Step 4: Recognize stress

Cats are sensitive to changes. Common triggers for uncleanliness and marking are:

- a new cat in the household or neighborhood, especially if strange cats appear at the window
- Conflicts between cats in the household, which often take place quietly
- Moving, remodeling, new furniture
- a new person in the household, a baby, visitors
- Changes in the daily routine, such as a new job for the caregiver
- Boredom and lack of activity in indoor cats

If you find a trigger, try to reduce it. If there are strange cats at the window, a privacy film in the lower area can help. In conflicts between cats, more resources help, i.e. more toilets, feeding places, sleeping places and elevated levels in different places.

## Step 5: Thoroughly remove odors

Cats like to return to places that smell like urine. Even if you no longer smell anything, your cat can still detect the smell. This is how you clean properly:

1. **Absorb urine:** Soak up as much as possible with absorbent paper, do not rub.
2. **Use enzyme cleaners:** Special cleaners with enzymes break down the components of urine that are responsible for the smell. Follow the instructions, the cleaner often has to work for a long time.
3. **No cleaners containing ammonia:** Ammonia smells similar to urine to cats and may encourage them to urinate in that spot again.
4. **Avoid vinegar and fragrances:** They mask the smell for humans, but not for cats.
5. **Wash textiles:** Wash blankets, pillowcases and clothing with enzyme detergent or an additive.

A UV flashlight can help find old urine stains that you missed.

## Step 6: redesign the affected areas

After cleaning, you can make the areas less attractive to the cat:

- Temporarily place an additional toilet in the exact spot the cat chose. If it is accepted, you can slowly move it later.
- Place a food bowl or toy in the spot. Cats don't like to urinate where they eat.
- Cover affected areas with foil or close the door to a room if possible.
- Don't leave piles of laundry on the floor if the cat likes to use it.

## What you should never do

Punishment makes everything worse. If you yell at your cat, push his nose into urine, or lock him up, he won't learn to use the toilet. She learns to be afraid of you and looks for even more hidden places. Stress itself is also a common cause of bladder problems, so punishment increases circulation.

## If it doesn't get better

Uncleanliness often has several causes at the same time. If nothing improves despite veterinary treatment and changes to the toilet, it is worth seeking advice from a veterinarian with additional qualifications in behavioral medicine. Sometimes calming food additives, pheromone atomizers or, in severe cases, medications that are only used after a veterinary examination can help.

## Emergency: if there is no urine

Especially in male cats, the urethra can become blocked by crystals, stones or inflammatory material. The cat then repeatedly goes to the toilet, strains, but does not release any or only a few drops of urine. She often licks her genital area, screams when pushing, vomits or quickly becomes exhausted. This is a life-threatening emergency because within a short period of time, toxins can build up in the blood and damage the heart and kidneys. Go to the vet's office or animal clinic immediately, even at night and on weekends. Don't wait to see if it gets better on its own.

## A diary helps to find the cause

Write down each incident for a while: date, time, location, whether it was a large puddle or a splash on the wall, and what was different that day, such as a visitor, a fight between cats, a strange cat at the window, a new litter, or a long absence. Also record when you cleaned the toilets. After two to three weeks, you will often recognize a pattern that will help you and the vet find the cause.

## Pheromones and drugs

There are commercially available atomizers for the socket that emit an artificial scent that is based on the facial pheromones of cats. Some cats become calmer and mark less as a result, while others have no effect. An attempt can make sense, but it does not replace the search for the trigger. If the stress is severe and persistent, the vet may also prescribe medication to ease the anxiety. They usually only take effect after a few weeks and are combined with changes in the environment.

## Frequently asked questions

### My cat always pees in our bed. Why there?

The bed is soft, absorbent and smells strongly of their humans. For a cat with bladder pain, it feels more comfortable than the toilet. For unsettled cats, it can also be a way of combining their own smell with that of their caregiver. Get her examined first.

### Does castration help against marking?

In uncastrated male cats, marking usually stops after castration. Cats that have already been neutered are often caused by stress or territorial insecurity.

### How long does it take for my cat to be clean again?

Once the cause is found and resolved, it often only takes a few days. If there is chronic stress or a recurring bladder infection, it can take weeks and relapses.

### Can urine stains on the mattress be removed?

With patience, often yes. Immediately soak up the urine, soak the area thoroughly with an enzyme cleaner, and leave it on for as long as the manufacturer directs. If the urine has penetrated deeply, several passes are usually necessary. A waterproof mattress protector will prevent it from happening again while you investigate the cause.
