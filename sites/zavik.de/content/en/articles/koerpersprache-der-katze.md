Cats are often seen as enigmatic, aloof or moody. They communicate constantly and very precisely, just differently than humans and differently than dogs. Anyone who learns their language suddenly understands why the cat strikes after being stroked, why it hides from visitors or why it comes into the kitchen in the morning with its tail raised straight up.

Cats primarily speak with their bodies. Sounds play a role, especially in contact with people, but the most subtle messages are found in the position of the tail and ears, in the eyes and in the tension of the whole body. This guide explains the most important signals.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Always read the whole picture: the tail, ears, eyes and posture together create the message.
> - A raised tail is a friendly greeting.
> - A thrashing, whipping tail means excitement or anger, not joy.
> - A cat that shows you its stomach trusts you, but usually doesn't want to be touched there.
> - Blinking slowly is a sign of trust that you can reciprocate.

## The tail: the mood barometer

The tail is a cat's most prominent means of expression and is the easiest for humans to read.

**Stretched steeply upwards, the tip perhaps slightly curved:** This is a friendly greeting. Cats exhibit this attitude when approaching someone they like, whether human or cat. This is how kittens greet their mother. If your cat greets you at the door with its tail raised, it is happy.

**Trembling up:** A vibrating, raised tail shows great joy and excitement, such as when the cat greets you after a long absence. In uncastrated animals it can also be associated with urine marking.

**Loosely horizontal or slightly lowered:** Relaxation, neutral mood.

**Hold low or pinched between legs:** Insecurity, fear or submission.

**Bulked like a bottle brush:** The cat is frightened or feels threatened. Standing up your hair makes it look bigger.

**Flapping, whipping:** A tail that beats vigorously from side to side is not a sign of joy in cats as it is in dogs, but rather of excitement, frustration or anger. If your cat flaps its tail like this when you pet it, you should stop.

**Only the tip of the tail twitches:** Attention, concentration, often the beginnings of irritability. When hunting, the tip twitches just before the cat jumps.

**Wrapped Around You:** When a cat wraps its tail around your leg, it's a friendly, bonding gesture.

## The ears: small radar antennas

Cat ears are very mobile. Each ear can rotate independently to locate sounds. Your position reveals a lot about the mood.

- **Upright, facing forward:** Attention, curiosity, relaxation.
- **Turned sideways, slightly flattened** (sometimes called “airplane ears”): Uncertainty, anger, or discomfort.
- **Flat to the head:** Fear or defensiveness. The ears are protected in case a fight breaks out.
- **Turned backwards but upright:** The cat is focused on a noise behind her or is easily irritated.
- **Constantly twitching, turning in different directions:** The cat is tense and listening to its surroundings.

## The eyes: pupils and glances

**Pupils:** The size of the pupils depends on the light, but also on the mood. Widely opened, round pupils in normal light can mean excitement, playfulness, fear or defensiveness. Very narrow, slit-shaped pupils in normal light can indicate tension or anger. The pupils must always be read in conjunction with the other signals.

**Direct, Staring:** Among cats, a long, direct stare is a threat. That's why many cats approach the people they ignore and avoid those who stare expectantly at them.

**Slow Blinking:** When a cat looks at you and slowly closes and reopens its eyes, it is a sign of trust and relaxation. She's showing you that she doesn't see you as a threat. A study by British researchers has shown that cats return this look from people and are more likely to approach people who slowly blink at them. Try it: Look at your cat with a relaxed face, slowly close his eyes, keep them closed for a moment and open them again.

**Half-closed eyes:** Relaxation and well-being, for example when dozing in the sun. In conjunction with a tense, crouched posture, they can also indicate pain.

## The whiskers

The whiskers are sensitive organs of touch, but also means of expression.

- **Loosely protruding to the side:** Relaxation.
- **Facing forward, fanned out:** Interest, curiosity, hunting mood. When sniffing a new object or just before grabbing it.
- **Flat to cheeks:** Fear or uncertainty.

## The posture

**Lying relaxed, stretched, perhaps on its side:** The cat feels safe.

**On your back, stomach facing up:** This is a huge vote of confidence because the stomach is the most vulnerable place. In most cases, however, it is not an invitation to rub your stomach. Many cats react to being touched on the stomach with claws and teeth because they reflexively defend themselves in this position. Some cats like it, but that is the exception.

**Bread loaf position:** The cat lies with its paws folded under it. With your eyes and ears relaxed, this is a comfortable resting position. If the body is tense, the head is bowed and the eyes are half closed for a long time, it can be a sign of discomfort or pain.

**Crouched, body close to the ground, paws under body:** Fear, uncertainty. The cat makes itself small and is ready to escape.

**Cat hump with ruffled fur, placed to the side:** The cat feels threatened and is trying to appear larger. She is torn between attack and flight. Give her space.

**Playful cat humping:** Young cats sometimes jump around sideways with a hunchback, which is an invitation to play.

## Greetings and friendly gestures

**Heading:** When your cat presses her head against you or rubs her cheek against you, she leaves behind scents from glands on her head. This marks you as part of her group. A big compliment.

**Stroke around the legs:** Also a scent marking and a greeting, often with the tail raised.

**Nose nudge:** Cats who like each other touch their noses in greeting. Some cats also do this to their humans when you hold out a finger.

**Milk kick:** When your cat alternates between kicking its front paws on your stomach or a soft blanket, it is reminiscent of kittens kicking their mother's teat, which stimulates the flow of milk. Adult cats show this when they feel comfortable and secure.

**Mutual Grooming:** Cats who like each other will groom each other, especially on the head and neck. When your cat licks you, it accepts you into this community.

## Warning signs you should take seriously

Many conflicts between humans and cats arise because warnings are overlooked. A cat that scratches or bites has almost always been warned in advance:

1. The ears turn to the side.
2. The tip of the tail twitches, then the whole tail thumps.
3. The skin on the back twitches or rolls.
4. The cat turns its head to the hand, looks at it.
5. She freezes, growls or hisses.

If you notice these signs, stop touching the cat immediately and give it space.

## How cats talk to each other

If several cats live together, it is worth observing their signals between each other. Cats who like each other greet each other with raised tails and nose contact, lie close to each other, groom each other, and rub their heads together. Tensions, on the other hand, often show up quietly: one cat blocks the hallway to the toilet, stares at the other for a long time or pushes it away from its favorite place without saying a word. Such conflicts should be taken seriously, even if there is never any hissing.

You can distinguish between game fights and real fights based on a few characteristics. In the game the roles change, sometimes one hunts, sometimes the other, there are breaks, hardly any sounds and no flying hair. In a real argument, there is hissing, growling or screaming, fur stands on end, ears lie flat, and in the end a cat runs away and hides. If such conflicts occur more often, more resources in separate locations will help, as described in [Sociate a second cat](/katzenwissen/zweitkatze-vergesellschaften/).

## Recognize pain in the face

Cats are great at hiding pain. Researchers have therefore developed a scale that can be used to measure pain based on facial expressions. She looks for five characteristics: the ears are turned outward and spread apart, the eyes are squinted, the muzzle appears tense and rather oval, the whiskers are straight and directed forward, and the head is held below the shoulder line. If you notice several of these signs in your cat, especially along with withdrawal, changes in eating behavior, or crouching posture, have him examined by a veterinarian. ## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my cat showing me her bottom?

What seems rude to humans is a friendly gesture to cats. Cats sniff each other's rear ends, and a cat that turns its raised tail and bottom toward you is, in a sense, inviting you to greet them.

### Why is my cat staring into space?

Cats hear and see things that we don't notice, such as an insect on the ceiling or a noise in the wall. However, sudden, persistent staring in conjunction with other changes, especially in older cats, should be checked by a veterinarian.

### Can I train my cat's body language?

No, but you can learn to read them better. Observe your cat in different situations and pay attention to how it looks at rest, when playing, when visiting and at the vet. Each cat has its own nuances.

### Why does my cat move the tip of its tail when I talk to it?

A slight twitch or tap of the tip of the tail while the cat is lying relaxed is often a kind of response: it has heard you and is aware of you, but may not want to get up. If the knocking gets stronger and the whole cock is beating, it's better to leave her alone.
