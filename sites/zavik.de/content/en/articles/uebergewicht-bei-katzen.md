A round cat on the sofa looks cozy, and many people even find a few extra pounds on their cat cute. Veterinarians see it differently: Obesity is one of the most common health problems in domestic cats, and it shortens their lives. It is estimated that in many industrialized countries a large proportion of domestic cats are overweight, and indoor cats and neutered animals are particularly common.

The good news: Obesity can be prevented and treated. The bad news: Getting a cat to lose weight requires patience and consistency, because losing weight too quickly is dangerous for cats.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The body condition score shows whether a cat is too fat better than the scales alone.
> - Being overweight promotes diabetes, osteoarthritis and skin problems and shortens life expectancy.
> - Neutered cats need significantly less energy than before.
> - A cat should lose weight slowly, about one percent of its body weight per week. Never let yourself go hungry.
> - Weighing out food, counting treats and ensuring exercise are the most important levers.

## Is my cat too fat?

The weight alone says little. A large, strong cat can be slim at six kilograms, while a petite cat may be too heavy at five kilograms. Veterinarians therefore use a scale called the **Body Condition Score**, which assesses body structure. On the commonly used nine-point scale, four to five points are considered ideal.

You can roughly assess your cat's condition at home:

| Assessment | Ribs | Waist from above | Belly from the side |
|---|---|---|---|
| too thin | clearly visible | strongly retracted | strongly raised |
| ideal | easily palpable under a thin layer of fat | recognizable | slightly raised, small abdominal flap possible |
| overweight | can only be felt with pressure | barely noticeable | round, hanging belly |
| very overweight | not palpable | no waist, back wide | clearly hanging, fat pads on flanks |

**How to feel the ribs:** Place both hands flat on the sides of the ribcage and stroke them with light pressure. You should be able to feel the ribs, much like you can feel the knuckles on the back of your hand. If you only feel it when you press hard, the fat layer is too thick.

A small, loose abdominal flap between the hind legs is normal in many cats, even slim ones. It is not a sure sign of overweight.

## Why being overweight is dangerous

Fat tissue is not just a cushion. It is metabolically active and influences hormones and inflammatory processes in the body. Overweight cats are at increased risk of:

- **Diabetes mellitus:** Obesity is one of the most important risk factors for diabetes in cats.
- **Arthrosis:** Overloaded joints wear out more quickly. Cats often only show joint pain by jumping less and lying down more.
- **Skin problems:** Fat cats can no longer reach their backs and bottoms when grooming. The fur becomes matted, dandruff and inflammation occurs.
- **Urinary tract problems:** Overweight, inactive cats are more commonly affected.
- **Hepatic steatosis:** If an overweight cat suddenly stops eating, perhaps due to stress or illness, it can develop life-threatening fatty liver disease.
- **Anesthesia risk:** Operations and anesthesia are riskier in severely overweight animals.

## How cats get too fat

The cause is almost always the same: the cat absorbs more energy than it uses. Typical reasons are:

- **Castration:** After castration, energy requirements decrease significantly, often by a fifth to a third, while appetite tends to increase. If you don't adjust the amount of food you eat, you'll soon have a fat cat.
- **Apartment keeping and boredom:** Little exercise, little hunting, eating as an activity.
- **Dry food freely available:** A constantly full bowl of energy-rich kibble.
- **Inaccurate measurement:** A cup “approximately full” is not an indication of quantity. A few grams of dry food too much a day adds up.
- **Treats and human food:** A piece of cheese or sausage is a significant amount of calories for a cat relative to its size.
- **Multiple food sources:** Outdoor animals that eat with neighbors or several family members that feed independently of each other.

## Lose weight: slowly and controlled

### First to the vet

Before you reduce the amount of food, have your cat examined. The practice can rule out illnesses, determine target weight and calculate the correct amount of energy. For severely overweight cats, she often recommends a special diet food that provides sufficient protein, vitamins and minerals with less energy and is more satiating.

### The right pace

Cats should lose weight slowly, as a rule of thumb about one percent of their body weight per week, up to a maximum of two. For a cat weighing six kilograms, that's sixty to one hundred grams per week. That doesn't sound like much, but over six months it adds up to one to two kilograms, which is a lot for a cat. Losing weight too quickly or fasting can put your liver at risk.

### Weigh out food

A digital kitchen scale is the most important tool. Every morning, weigh out the daily ration, both wet and dry, and divide it into several small meals. Whatever is left in the evening will not be replenished.

### Count treats

Treats are not forbidden, but they are part of the daily ration. As a rule of thumb, they should make up no more than about ten percent of your daily energy. Even better: take some kibble from the daily ration as a reward.

### More wet food

Wet food contains significantly fewer calories than dry food for the same volume and makes you fuller thanks to the water content. A higher proportion of wet food makes it easier for many cats to lose weight.

### Make eating an activity

Food balls, fidget boards and hidden kibble slow down eating and keep the cat busy. She has to work for her meal, just as she would in nature when hunting.

### movement

Play with your cat several times a day, preferably with a spring fishing rod. Even two or three game sessions of five to ten minutes each make a difference. Put food and water in different places, preferably elevated, so that the cat has to move. For overweight cats with joint problems, increase activity carefully.

## Measure progress

Weigh your cat every one to two weeks, preferably at the same time of day. A baby scale is more accurate than the method of placing yourself on the bathroom scale with and without a cat. Write down the values. If the weight does not decrease over three weeks, talk to your vet about further adjusting the amount.

When the target weight is reached, the amount of food is increased slightly so that the cat maintains the weight. Many cats regain weight after dieting as old habits return.

## Multi-cat household: If only one needs to lose weight

This is one of the biggest challenges. Possible solutions:

- separate feeding in different rooms at fixed times
- Automatic feeders that only open for a specific cat via microchip
- Increased feeding areas for the slim, sporty cat that cannot reach the heavier one

## Roughly estimate the energy requirement

How much energy a cat needs can be estimated using a formula that veterinarians also use. First, the rest requirement is calculated: 70 times body weight in kilograms to the power of 0.75. For a cat with an ideal weight of four kilograms, this amounts to around 200 kilocalories per day. A neutered, less active indoor cat often only needs about this value or just above. In order to lose weight, the veterinarian usually sets the daily amount slightly below the requirement for the target weight and adjusts it based on regular weighings. Using the energy content of your food, which you can estimate as described in [read food labels](/katzenwissen/futteretiketten-lesen/), you can calculate the daily amount in grams.

## Losing weight can reverse diabetes

For overweight cats with diabetes, weight loss is an important part of treatment. Together with insulin and a high-protein, low-carbohydrate food, some cats achieve what is known as remission: They no longer need insulin after a few weeks or months, as long as their weight and diet are correct. The earlier the treatment begins, the more likely this will be successful. However, never change the food or amount of insulin on your own for a cat with diabetes, as a sudden drop in blood sugar can be life-threatening.

## Everyone pulls together

A diet often fails not because of the cat, but because of the people. If a family member secretly gives out treats or the children refill the bowl when the cat begs, it won't eat. Discuss the plan together, determine who will feed, and record each meal. Outdoor dogs are sometimes pampered by several neighbors. Speak kindly to the neighbors and explain that your cat needs to lose weight.

## When it stalls

Almost every diet has phases in which the weight stagnates for weeks. That's no reason to give up. Check whether additional food sources have sneaked in, whether the scale measures correctly and whether the cat is still moving enough. The vet then adjusts the amount slightly. A cat who is slowly but steadily losing weight is on the right track.

## Frequently asked questions

### My cat is constantly begging. Is she hungry?

Begging is often learned: the cat has learned that meowing brings food. Solid meals, food toys and attention in the form of play instead of food help to reduce begging.

### Can I just halve the amount of food?

Please not without consultation. Too much reduction can endanger the liver and lead to nutrient deficiencies. The vet calculates the safe amount.

### Does light food make sense?

Light products usually contain less energy, but not always significantly. A targeted diet with veterinary supervision is usually more effective for losing weight. Light food is more suitable for stabilizing a maintained weight.

### How long does it take to reach your goal weight?

At a rate of about one percent per week, a cat weighing six kilograms who needs to lose one kilogram needs around four to five months. If you are more overweight, it can take a year or longer. Patience is worth it, because a slow approach is safest for the cat's health.

### Can my cat still have treats while on the diet?

Yes, in small quantities and included in the daily ration. Individual croquettes of the diet food that you use as a reward while playing or training are particularly suitable.
