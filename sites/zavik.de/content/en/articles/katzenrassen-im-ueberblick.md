Most cats in Germany are domestic cats with no pedigree, and many of them are the best companions you could ask for. There are also dozens of recognized cat breeds that differ significantly in size, fur, temperament and sometimes also in their health risks. If you are interested in a purebred cat, you should not only pay attention to its appearance, but above all to whether its character and needs fit your own life.

This guide introduces some of the most popular breeds, explains what you need to pay attention to when caring for them, and shows which health tests are a given for reputable breeders. He also explains why some forms of breeding are considered cruel breeding and why animal shelters are a good place to go, even if you are looking for a specific breed.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Choose a cat based on temperament and needs, not looks.
> - Many breeds have typical hereditary diseases. Reputable breeders test their breeding animals and show the results.
> - Extreme characteristics such as very short noses, folded ears or missing whiskers can harm the cat and are sometimes considered to be torture breeding.
> - Kittens are not handed over before the twelfth week of life, vaccinated, chipped and with a purchase contract.
> - Pedigree cats are also waiting for a home in animal shelters and specialized agencies.

## Domestic cat or purebred cat?

Domestic cats without a pedigree are usually very genetically diverse and often robust. Her personality is less predictable than some breeds, but with an adult cat from the shelter you already know her personality before she moves in. Pedigree cats, on the other hand, offer a certain degree of predictability in terms of size, coat and temperament. However, there is no guarantee that they will have a specific character, because every cat is an individual. [Kitten or adult cat](/katzenwissen/kitten-oder-erwachsene-katze/) describes how you decide between young and older cats.

## Popular breeds in portrait

### British Shorthair

The British Shorthair is compact and powerfully built, with a round head, small ears and a thick, plush-like coat that comes in many colors, most famously blue-gray. She is considered calm, balanced and rather reserved, likes company, but often doesn't like being carried around all the time. Many animals tend to be overweight, so quantity control and play are important. In breeding, attention should be paid to hereditary heart diseases and kidney cysts.

### Maine Coons

The Maine Coon is one of the largest domestic cat breeds. Male cats often become significantly heavier than average domestic cats and grow slowly, sometimes until their third or fourth year of life. Their medium-long coat with a ruff and bushy tail is robust and needs regular combing. Maine Coons are considered friendly, playful and sociable. They need lots of space, large, sturdy scratching posts and large toilets. Well-known health issues include a hereditary form of heart muscle thickening, hip dysplasia and a hereditary muscle disease, for which there are some genetic tests.

### Norwegian forest cat

The Norwegian Forest Cat comes from Scandinavia and is adapted to harsh climates with its water-repellent, semi-long fur. She is strong, loves climbing and active and needs plenty of climbing opportunities. When she sheds her coat, she loses a lot of undercoat, which requires frequent combing. There is a genetic test for a rare hereditary metabolic disease in the breed.

### Persian

Persian cats have very long, thick fur and are considered calm and affectionate. Their fur needs daily care, otherwise it will become matted within a few days. Breathing problems, tearing, eye inflammation and misaligned teeth are common in animals with very short, flat noses. Reputable breeders pay attention to a moderate head shape and have their animals tested for the hereditary kidney cyst disease that is common in Persians.

### Siamese and Oriental Shorthair

Siamese cats are slim, elegant, with large ears and blue eyes. Their typical coloring with dark markings on the face, ears, legs and tail is caused by a temperature-dependent pigment enzyme: more color is produced on the cooler parts of the body. Siamese cats and the closely related Oriental Shorthairs are considered to be very talkative, intelligent and very attached to their people. They suffer from loneliness and are usually happier as a couple or with lots of company.

### Ragdoll

Ragdolls are large, semi-long-haired cats with blue eyes and Siamese-like markings. They are considered particularly gentle and people-oriented. The name means ragdoll and refers to the fact that some animals hang relaxed when you pick them up, but this does not apply to every ragdoll. There is a genetic test for hereditary heart muscle disease in the breed.

### Bengal

Bengal cats were created by crossing domestic cats with the Asian leopard cat. Their fur with spots or marbling is reminiscent of a small wild cat. Bengals are very active, curious, intelligent and playful, many even like water. They need plenty of activity, climbing opportunities and hunting games. In a small apartment with people who have little time, they quickly get bored.

### Holy Burma

The Sacred Birman has a semi-long, silky coat, blue eyes, dark markings and white paws that look like gloves. She is considered calm, friendly and adaptable. Their coat becomes less matted than that of Persians, but still requires regular grooming.

## Torment breeding: when traits make you sick

Some traits that are popular with humans are harmful to the cats themselves. In Germany, the Animal Welfare Act prohibits breeding in which the offspring suffer because of inherited traits. Experts and animal protection organizations include, among others:

- **Folded ears:** In the Scottish Fold, the genetic mutation that causes the ears to fold forward leads to changes in the cartilage and bones of all animals, which can cause painful joint diseases.
- **Extremely short noses:** Very flat faces have compromised respiratory tracts, tear ducts and teeth.
- **Lack of whiskers:** Hairless cats without whiskers lack an important sensory organ.
- **White cats with blue eyes bred specifically for this trait:** Congenital deafness is common in them.

If you are looking for a pedigree cat, do not support breeds with such characteristics, even if the animals look cute.

## How you recognize good breeding

Reputable breeders have nothing to hide and take a lot of time to answer your questions. Pay attention to these points:

- You can visit the kittens together with the mother cat in the apartment where they are growing up.
- The kittens are handed over at the earliest at twelve weeks, are dewormed, vaccinated at least twice and chipped.
- The parent animals are tested for the diseases typical of the breed and the results are shown to you.
- There is a written purchase contract and a pedigree from a breeding association.
- The breeder also asks you: where you live, how much time you have, whether there are already animals in the household.
- She is also there to answer questions after the purchase and will take a cat back in an emergency.

Caution is advised when it comes to offers in which pedigree cats are sold very cheaply, without papers, immediately and without a visit, often via classified ads. Behind this are often breeders who make money from sick animals.

## Pedigree cats from animal welfare

Pedigree cats also end up in animal shelters, for example because their humans get sick, move or are overwhelmed. There are also specialized placements for certain breeds. Check with animal shelters in your area. An adult pedigree cat from animal welfare has the advantage that its character is known. [Adopt a cat from an animal shelter](/katzenwissen/katze-aus-dem-tierheim-adoptieren/) describes how an adoption works.

## Make the right choice

Before you decide on a breed, it's worth taking an honest look at your everyday life. How much time do you have for play and activity every day? Are you ready to comb a long coat every day? How big is your apartment and is there a secured balcony? Are there children, a dog or other cats in the household? And can you also cover higher veterinary costs if an illness typical of the breed occurs? It's also worth getting to know adult cats of the breed, for example from breeders or at cat shows. A cute kitten reveals little about how big, how active and how talkative it will be as an adult cat. If you answer these questions beforehand, you will find a cat that you can live with for many years.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are purebred cats healthier or sicker than domestic cats?

This cannot be said in general terms. Domestic cats tend to be more genetically diverse, which lowers the risk of some hereditary diseases. When it comes to pedigree cats, a lot depends on breeding: Well-managed breeding with health tests produces healthy animals, while breeding without control often produces sick animals.

### Are there cats for allergy sufferers?

There is no such thing as a truly allergen-free cat. The main allergen comes from saliva and skin glands and is produced by all cats, although in different amounts. Some allergy sufferers tolerate individual cats better than others. Before making a decision, spend a lot of time with the exact cat you want to move in and get medical advice.

### Which breed suits a small apartment?

Quieter breeds such as British Shorthairs, Sacred Birmans or Persians usually do well in an apartment if they are given enough activity. Very active breeds like Bengal or Siamese need more time, play and climbing opportunities. More important than the breed, however, is how you design the apartment, as described in [Keeping your indoor cat species-appropriate](/katzenwissen/wohnungskatze-artgerecht-halten/).

### Do pedigree cats need different food?

As a rule not. Breed-specific food is usually primarily marketing. What is crucial is a high-quality complete feed in the right amount, adapted to age, size and activity. Very large breeds like the Maine Coon grow slower and are often given kitten food for longer.
