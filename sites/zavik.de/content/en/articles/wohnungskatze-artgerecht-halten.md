In cities, many cats live exclusively indoors. Some because the street is too dangerous, others because they shouldn't go outside for health reasons, still others because they've never known any other way. Housing is not automatically worse than freedom. An indoor cat is protected from accidents, territorial fights, infectious diseases and poisoning and, on average, lives longer.

But an apartment is a small, often low-stimulus territory. A cat that would hunt, climb, explore and observe outside for hours needs a substitute for these experiences in the home. If it is missing, she becomes bored, becomes overweight, restless, unclean or develops behavior problems. This guide shows you how to make your apartment cat-friendly.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Think in three dimensions: Cats use height. Shelves, scratching posts and window seats increase the area.
> - Provide hiding places, views outside and different places to sleep.
> - Daily hunting games are mandatory, not optional.
> - Make your cat work for its food.
> - Many indoor cats are happier in pairs than alone.

## The territory at altitude

Cats don't just live on the ground. They like to climb, jump and lie on higher ground where they can see their territory and feel safe. For a cat, an apartment with shelves, cupboards and scratching posts is much larger than the same area without them.

How to create vertical space:

- **Multi-level cat tree,** preferably up to the ceiling, in a location with a view
- **Wall shelves and climbing walls** that lead as a path through the room, with gaps that the cat can easily jump over
- **Cleared closet surfaces** with a blanket
- **Window sills,** possibly widened by a window lounger

Elevated spaces are particularly important in multi-cat households, where they prevent conflicts, and in households with dogs or children, where the cat needs a place to retreat.

## Hiding places and retreats

In addition to vantage points, cats need caves in which they can retreat: cardboard boxes, cat caves, a place under the bed, an open closet drawer with a blanket. At least one retreat per cat should be in a quiet place where it will not be disturbed.

## View outside

For indoor cats, the window is a gateway to the world. Birds, people, leaves in the wind, rain: something is always happening outside. A cozy place by the window, perhaps with a view of a birdhouse, is one of the most important activities.

A secured balcony or a cat net in front of a window expands the territory with fresh air, smells and sun. A cat enclosure in the garden or a window balcony are also options. We explain how to make your balcony cat-proof in our own guide.

## Hunting game: the most important thing of all

Cats are hunters. An outdoor female makes many hunting attempts during the day, most of which are unsuccessful. Sneaking, lurking, rushing and grabbing is deeply anchored in them. Indoor cats need a replacement, and the best one is to play hunting games with their humans.

**How ​​to play correctly:**

- **With a spring rod or game rod:** The prey moves, you keep your distance, and your hands stay out of claw reach.
- **Like real prey:** Let the rod dart away, hide, peek out from behind a corner, fidget on the ground. Move it away from the cat, not toward it. Prey never runs towards a cat.
- **Enable success:** The cat should also be allowed to catch the prey. A game without success is frustrating.
- **Short, intensive rounds:** Two to three rounds of five to fifteen minutes each day are ideal. Many cats become exhausted after a few minutes of intense hunting.
- **End with a Catch:** At the end, let the cat catch the prey and then give it a treat or meal, just like after a successful hunt.

**Laser pointers** are popular but controversial. The cat can never catch the point of light, which can be frustrating. If you use one, have the game end on a tangible toy that the cat can grab. Never shine a light in your eyes.

Toys that are always lying around quickly become boring. Change it regularly and only take out the fishing rod when playing together.

## Develop food

In nature, a cat has to work for every meal. The food is in a bowl in the apartment. With a few simple measures, eating becomes an activity again:

- **Food balls:** Balls with openings from which dry food falls out when rolled.
- **Fumbling Boards:** Boards with troughs, tubes and obstacles from which the cat has to fish for food.
- **Foraging:** Distribute small portions around the apartment, on shelves, in boxes, under blankets.
- **Several small meals:** Instead of two large portions, have many small ones throughout the day, for example with an automatic feeder.

Feeding toys slow down eating, prevent obesity and give the cat something to do when no one is home.

## Sensory stimuli

Cats experience the world largely through their noses. A few ideas:

- **Catnip, Valerian or Matatabi:** Many cats respond enthusiastically to these plants. Not all cats respond to this.
- **New smells:** A pine cone, a piece of wood from the forest, a box from the grocery store. What smells outside is interesting.
- **Cat grass** for nibbling.
- **Rustle materials:** Paper bags without handles, rustle bags, tissue paper.

## Society

Cats are not the loners they were long thought to be. Many cats enjoy living with other cats, especially if they grew up together. For indoor cats whose people work during the day, a suitable partner can be a great asset.

But there are also cats that prefer to live alone. If your cat doesn't like other cats, a second cat is not a solution. Then you are even more important as a playing partner.

## Toilet, food and water

These basic needs are particularly important in the home:

- **Enough toilets:** one more than cats, in quiet, easily accessible places.
- **Food and water separated:** Water points not directly next to the bowl and not next to the toilet.
- **Several water points:** Indoor cats often drink little.

## Security in the apartment

The apartment also harbors dangers: tilted windows, open balconies, poisonous plants, cables, cords, medication, hot stoves and washing machines. Go through the apartment regularly with a cat's eye.

## A day in the life of a happy indoor cat

How can all of this be implemented in everyday life when you work? A possible daily routine:

- **Before work:** five minutes of playing with the spring fishing rod, then breakfast. Food toys with part of the daily ration are distributed around the apartment.
- **During the day:** The cat sleeps, watches the birds from the window seat, looks for food and climbs on the scratching post. A cardboard box or paper bag that appears new in the morning provides variety.
- **After work:** Greetings, petting, then a longer hunting session of ten to fifteen minutes.
- **Evening:** Meal, relaxing on the sofa together, grooming.
- **Before going to bed:** a quick last game and a small snack to keep you calm at night.

Just thirty to forty minutes of conscious attention a day makes a big difference for an indoor cat.

## Signs of boredom

An indoor cat that is missing something often shows this through:

- excessive sleeping, obesity, sluggishness
- Nocturnal romping, waking, constant meowing
- Destroying objects, scratching furniture
- excessive grooming leading to bald spots
- Attacks on hands and feet
- uncleanliness

If you notice such signs, first rule out health causes and then specifically expand your employment opportunities.

## Clicker training: brain teaser for cats

Cats are more capable of learning than many people think. Tricks and useful everyday exercises can be taught using a clicker, a small device that makes a clicking sound. First, associate the clicking with a reward: click, immediately give a treat, and repeat this a few times. Then you always click at the exact moment the cat does something you want, such as sitting down, touching a finger with its nose, or walking on a mat. Keep the sessions short, two to five minutes is enough, and stop while the cat is still happy. Clicker training challenges the mind, strengthens the bond and also helps with practical things, such as voluntarily going into the transport box, as described in [transport box training](/katzenwissen/transportbox-training/).

## Outside with dishes?

Some indoor cats get used to a harness and enjoy short trips to a quiet garden or park. This can only be achieved with a lot of patience: first the cat wears the harness around the apartment for a short time, then with a leash, and only when it is relaxed with it does it go outside. Use an escape-proof harness that fits snugly enough and follow the cat instead of pulling it. Never tie them anywhere. Not every cat likes these trips. If your cat seems anxious outside, a secured balcony or window seat is a better choice.

## Bring variety into everyday life

An area that always looks the same becomes boring. Swap out toys weekly so that only a few things are accessible at a time. Place an empty box in a new location, build a small obstacle course out of boxes and blankets, or hide part of the daily ration in different places. A birdhouse within sight of the window becomes a favorite for many cats. This keeps the apartment exciting without you having to constantly buy new things.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a cat that has known freedom become an indoor cat?

This is more difficult but possible, especially for older cats or cats with limited health. A secured balcony or enclosure helps with the transition. Young cats that are used to being outdoors often suffer more from the change.

### Is indoor housing cruelty to animals?

No, if the cat's needs are met. Many indoor cats live long and contented lives. What is crucial is a rich area and sufficient activity.

### How big does an apartment have to be for a cat?

The design is more important than the square meters. A small apartment with lots of levels, hiding places and a view is better for a cat than a large, empty one.

### Does an indoor cat need vaccinations?

Yes. Indoor cats should also be vaccinated against cat disease and cat flu, as the pathogens can enter the apartment through shoes, clothing or visitors. Which vaccinations are useful can be found in [Vaccinations for cats](/katzenwissen/impfungen-bei-katzen/).
