Many cat owners worry because they hardly ever see their cat at the water bowl. This worry is often unfounded because cats get a large portion of their fluids through their food. Nevertheless, many domestic cats actually drink too little, especially if they are fed mostly dry food. And this can have long-term consequences for the kidneys and urinary tract.

This guide explains how much fluid a cat needs, how you can recognize a deficiency and what simple ways you can get your cat to drink. You will also find out why drinking a lot of water is a reason for a visit to the vet.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Cats have a weak feeling of thirst and naturally meet their water needs primarily through their prey.
> - Cats that eat a lot of wet food drink less, without anything missing.
> - Several watering holes away from the food, wide bowls and fresh water encourage drinking.
> - Suddenly drinking significantly more can be a sign of kidney disease, diabetes or hyperthyroidism.

## How much fluid does a cat need?

As a rough guide, a healthy cat needs around 40 to 60 milliliters of fluid per kilogram of body weight per day. So a cat weighing four kilograms needs about 160 to 240 milliliters, about the same as a small cup. But she doesn't have to drink this amount from the bowl. A large part of it comes from the feed.

An example: A bowl of wet food containing one hundred grams contains approximately eighty milliliters of water. A cat that eats three such bowls a day is already consuming a large part of its needs and therefore only drinks a little. This is completely normal.

Things are different with dry food. Sixty grams of dry food contains only a few milliliters of water. A cat that only eats dry food would have to drink almost all of its needs, which many cats don't do.

## Why cats drink little

The ancestors of our domestic cats lived in dry areas and learned to get by with little water. Your kidneys can concentrate urine to a great extent, and your feeling of thirst doesn't start until late. What was an advantage in the desert is more of a disadvantage for an indoor cat on dry food.

There are also preferences that have to do with the bowl:

- **Water next to food:** In nature, cats avoid water near their prey because it might be contaminated.
- **Narrow, deep bowls:** Many cats don't like their whiskers touching the edge.
- **Stale water:** Cats have a very subtle smell and prefer fresh water.
- **Unfavorable location:** next to the noisy washing machine, in a corner where the cat can't escape, or next to the toilet.

## How do you recognize a lack of fluids?

Mild dehydration is difficult to recognize from the outside. These signs may indicate:

- **little urine:** The lumps in the litter box are small and rare.
- **Skin Fold Test:** If you gently lift and release the skin on the back of your neck, it should slide back immediately. If it stops for a moment, it can indicate a lack of fluids. However, this test is less meaningful for older and very slim cats.
- **dry, sticky gums**
- **sunken eyes** and dullness with more severe deficiency
- **hard, dry stool** or constipation

A cat that appears significantly dehydrated, is apathetic or is not eating needs to be seen by the vet the same day.

## Why it matters in the long run

If you drink little, you produce concentrated urine. Crystals and urinary stones can form more easily in concentrated urine and the bladder is less likely to be flushed. Lower urinary tract diseases are one of the most common reasons for cats to visit the vet. It is particularly dangerous for tomcats: their urethra is narrow, and if it becomes blocked by crystals or inflammatory material, the cat can no longer pass urine. This is a life-threatening emergency.

Sufficient fluids are also important for older cats whose kidneys lose performance over the years.

## 12 tricks to make your cat drink

1. **Feed more wet food.** This is the most effective way. Just one or two wet food meals a day makes a big difference.
2. **Mix water into the wet food.** One or two tablespoons of lukewarm water make the sauce even more liquid. Most cats don't notice it.
3. **Separate water and food.** Place water bowls at least a few meters away from the feeding area.
4. **Provide multiple watering points.** One in each room where the cat likes to be. So she passes the water as she walks past.
5. **Use wide, shallow bowls.** Ceramic, glass or stainless steel are ideal. Plastic can have a taste of its own.
6. **Fill the bowl to the brim.** Many cats prefer to drink when they don't have to lean deep into it.
7. **Give fresh water daily.** Rinse the bowls daily. A biofilm at the edge is a sign that it's time.
8. **Try a drinking fountain.** Many cats are attracted to running water. However, the fountain must be cleaned regularly and the filter changed.
9. **Use the faucet.** Some cats prefer to drink from the running faucet. If your cat is one of them, let him have this joy when you are around.
10. **Choose interesting places.** A bowl on the windowsill or scratching post is more attractive to some cats than a bowl on the floor.
11. **Offer a hydration treat.** There are liquid snacks for cats. Check whether it is a supplementary feed and count the calories into the daily ration.
12. **Unseasoned broth is an exception.** A little home-made, unsalted chicken broth without onions and garlic can entice a cat who is lazy to drink. Ready-made broths for humans almost always contain salt, onions or garlic and are unsuitable.

**What not to do:** Cow's milk is not a water substitute. Many adult cats cannot tolerate milk sugar and get diarrhea. Even special cat milk is more of a calorie-filled treat than a drink.

## If the cat suddenly drinks a lot

The opposite is at least as important as drinking too little. If your cat suddenly stands at the bowl a lot, you have to refill the bowl more often and the urine clumps in the toilet become larger, this is a reason to make an appointment with the vet as soon as possible. Possible causes are:

- **chronic kidney disease,** which is very common in older cats
- **Diabetes mellitus,** especially in overweight cats
- **Hyperthyroidism,** often associated with weight loss despite a good appetite
- **other illnesses** or side effects of medications

All of these diseases can be diagnosed with blood and urine tests and, in many cases, can be treated well, especially if they are detected early.

A note: If you have recently switched from wet food to dry food, it is normal for your cat to drink more. Cats also drink more on hot days.

## This is how you keep track

It is difficult to accurately measure how much a cat drinks, especially if there are several animals in the household. Simple help is:

- Fill the bowl with a measured amount in the morning and check in the evening how much is missing (note evaporation)
- pay attention to the size and number of urine clumps in the toilet
- Make a quick note of any noticeable changes so that you can describe them to the vet

## Fluid in sick and old cats

When vomiting, diarrhea or fever occurs, a cat quickly loses a lot of fluid, and kittens and old cats dry out particularly quickly. If a sick cat hardly eats or drinks for more than a day, you should not wait and call the vet's office. There the cat can receive infusions if necessary.

Cats with chronic kidney disease lose a lot of water through their kidneys and often need ongoing support. In addition to wet food with additional water, the vet can recommend infusions under the skin, which many owners give themselves at home following instructions. This sounds difficult at first, but it is well tolerated by many cats and can significantly improve their well-being. More information can be found in [Renal Insufficiency in Cats](/katzenwissen/niereninsuffizienz-bei-katzen/).

## Drink on hot days

In summer, cats need more fluids. Place additional water bowls in cool, shady areas and change the water more often because it gets stale more quickly when it is warm. A few ice cubes in the bowl make the water more interesting for some cats. Wet food spoils more quickly in hot weather and should not be left in the bowl for long after eating.

Cats rarely pant. If a cat is panting with its mouth open on a hot day, salivating heavily, staggering or appearing apathetic, there is a risk of heat stroke. Take her to a cool place, wet the fur with lukewarm, not ice-cold, water, offer water and go to the vet's office immediately.

## Frequently asked questions

### My cat almost never drinks, but only eats wet food. Is that worrying?

Mostly not. Wet food covers a large part of the needs. As long as she regularly passes a normal amount of urine, is alert and eats well, everything is fine.

### Is tap water suitable for cats?

In Germany yes. Tap water is strictly controlled. Very hard water is not harmful to cats. Boiled or filtered water is not necessary, but may be more popular with some cats.

### Can my cat drink from puddles and flower pots?

Many cats love stale rainwater. Usually it doesn't do any harm. Be careful with water containing fertilizer, saucers from plants that have been treated with pesticides, and vase water from lilies, as this is toxic to cats.

### How often do I have to clean the drinking fountain?

Change the water every one to two days and disassemble and clean the fountain thoroughly about once a week with hot water and a brush, including the pump. Replace the filter as often as the manufacturer specifies. A slimy coating or a musty smell shows that cleaning is overdue.
