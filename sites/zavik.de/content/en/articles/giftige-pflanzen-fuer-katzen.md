Many cats like to nibble on plants. Some do it out of boredom, some out of curiosity, some because they're looking for grass to regurgitate hair and can't find any. In an apartment with houseplants, on a planted balcony or in the garden, this habit can be dangerous because many popular plants contain substances that are toxic to cats.

The good news: Most plant poisonings are mild, with salivation or vomiting. However, some plants are so dangerous that they have no place in a cat household. This guide introduces the most important ones and shows which plants you can safely install.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Lilies are extremely dangerous for cats. Even pollen or vase water can trigger kidney failure.
> - Oleander, lily of the valley, yew, rhododendron, azalea and kalanchoe are also very poisonous.
> - Many houseplants such as Dieffenbachia, Monstera or Ivy irritate the mouth and stomach.
> - If poisoning is suspected, call the vet's office immediately and take the plant or a photo with you.
> - Cat grass and non-toxic plants offer a good alternative to nibbling.

## Lilies: the greatest danger

Real lilies (genus Lilium) and daylilies (Hemerocallis) are among the most dangerous plants for cats. All parts are poisonous: flowers, leaves, stems, pollen and even the water in the vase. A cat that licks pollen from its fur after brushing past a bouquet can become seriously ill.

The poison damages the kidneys. Vomiting, drooling and loss of appetite occur within a few hours, after which the cat sometimes seems to feel better. However, in the following one to three days, acute kidney failure can develop, which is fatal if left untreated. Early treatment, ideally within a few hours of admission, greatly improves the outlook.

Because lilies are so common in bouquets, if you have a cat you should not have lilies in your home and ask guests not to bring lilies. They are also risky on the balcony and in the garden. If you suspect that a cat has come into contact with a lily, it is an emergency, even if it still appears healthy.

## Other very poisonous plants

These plants can cause severe poisoning, including cardiac arrhythmia, convulsions or death:

| plant | Where it occurs | Possible consequences |
|---|---|---|
| Oleander | Potted plants on balconies and terraces | Cardiac toxins: vomiting, cardiac arrhythmias, collapse |
| Lily of the valley | Garden, bouquets of flowers in spring | Heart poisons, the vase water is also poisonous |
| Yew | Hedges in gardens and parks | Needles and seeds very poisonous; Heart disorders |
| Rhododendrons, azaleas | Garden, indoor azaleas | Vomiting, salivation, heart problems |
| Kalanchoe (Flaming Kat) | popular houseplant | Heart poisons, vomiting, weakness |
| Christmas rose (Helleborus) | Garden, Christmas decoration | Vomiting, diarrhea, heart problems |
| Foxglove | Garden | Heart poisons |
| Autumn crocus | meadows, gardens | severe gastrointestinal and organ damage |
| Flower bulbs of tulips, daffodils, hyacinths, amaryllis | Spring decoration, garden, room | Vomiting, diarrhea, heart problems in large quantities |

The bulbs of spring flowers in particular are problematic because they are often left loose on the table or in pots with loose soil that cats like to dig into.

## Houseplants that irritate the mouth and stomach

Many popular houseplants contain calcium oxalate crystals or other irritants. If a cat bites into it, tiny needle-shaped crystals drill into the mucous membrane. Typical symptoms include violent salivation, shaking of the head, paws on the mouth, swelling and vomiting. Such poisonings are usually not life-threatening, but painful. If there is severe swelling in the throat, breathing may be difficult.

These include, for example:

- Dieffenbachia
- Monstera (window leaf)
- Philodendron
- Ivy plant
- Single leaf (Spathiphyllum)
- Room calla
- Alocasia

Other houseplants are particularly irritating to the stomach and intestines, including:

- Aloe vera
- Birch fig and other Ficus species whose milky sap irritates the skin and mucous membranes
- Yucca palm
- Dragon tree (Dracaena)
- Ivy
- Cyclamen, especially the bulb
- Poinsettia, whose milky juice irritates the mouth and stomach; It is less dangerous than often assumed, but still does not belong within cat's reach

## How do you recognize poisoning?

The signs depend on the plant. Common are:

- Salivating, smacking, paws on the mouth
- Vomiting, diarrhea, loss of appetite
- Weariness, withdrawal
- Trembling, staggering, convulsions
- noticeably slow or fast heartbeat, breathing problems
- Increased drinking and urination or lack of urination in the event of kidney damage

You often first notice nibbled leaves or plant remains in the vomit.

## What to do if poisoning is suspected?

1. **Remove the cat from the plant** and remove plant debris from its mouth if it is safe to do so.
2. **Call the vet's office or animal hospital immediately.** Name the plant, the suspected amount and the time.
3. **Take a plant with you** or take a photo where the leaves and flowers are clearly visible. If you don't know the name, the label from the pot will help.
4. **Do not induce vomiting** and do not give home remedies such as milk or oil.
5. **Don't wait,** especially with lilies, oleander, lily of the valley, yew and other highly poisonous plants, even if the cat is still alert.

## Safe plants for cat households

There are many beautiful plants that are considered safe for cats. Even with non-poisonous plants, a cat that eats large quantities may vomit, but serious poisoning is not to be expected. A selection:

| plant | Note |
|---|---|
| Cat grass (oats, wheat, barley) | ideal for nibbling, sow regularly |
| Catnip | many cats love the scent |
| spider plant | robust, popular with cats who like to nibble |
| Golden fruit palm (Areca) | large, decorative indoor palm |
| Mountain palm (Chamaedorea) | tolerates little light |
| Calathea (Calathea) | strikingly patterned leaves |
| Pilea (UFO plant) | easy to care for |
| African violets | flowering houseplant |
| Echeverias and many other succulents without latex | determine exactly, as not all succulents are harmless |
| Basil, thyme, rosemary, lemon balm | Herbs for windowsills and balconies |
| Marigold, nasturtium, sunflower | Balcony and garden |

Before purchasing a new plant, look up its botanical name and see if it is suitable for cats. Common names are often misleading: a “lily” in the name does not always mean a real lily, and vice versa.

## Keep plants and cats together

Even with non-poisonous plants, it's worth keeping a few things in mind:

- **Offer cat grass:** Cats that have their own nibble plant are often more likely to leave other plants alone.
- **Cover soil:** If your cat digs into flower pots or uses them as a toilet, cover the soil with large pebbles or a screen.
- **Store fertilizer and pesticides safely:** Many fertilizers, slug pellets and insecticides are poisonous. Only use it where the cat can't reach and don't leave it open.
- **Note watering water and coasters:** Some cats like to drink from coasters. If fertilizer is added to the water, it can be harmful.
- **Check cut flowers:** In addition to lilies, lilies of the valley, tulips, daffodils and amaryllis are also common in bouquets. Keep bouquets out of reach or avoid poisonous species.

## Outdoors and the garden

Outdoor animals also come into contact with poisonous plants in neighboring gardens without being able to prevent it. However, most cats avoid bitter and unpleasant-tasting plants. Situations in which pollen gets into the fur, for example from lilies, or in which bored cats nibble out of curiosity are particularly dangerous. In your own garden, you can avoid particularly dangerous species such as lilies, oleander and yew and plant cat grass or catnip.

## Poisonous plants throughout the year

Which plants come into your home depends largely on the season. In **spring** there are tulips, daffodils, hyacinths and lilies of the valley in vases and pots, often with bulbs. In **summer**, potted plants like oleanders come onto the balcony, and lilies are often found in bouquets. In **autumn**, chrysanthemums and arrangements with fruits and branches bring new dangers, and the autumn crocus blooms in meadows. In **winter** poinsettias, Christmas roses, amaryllis and mistletoe decorate the apartment. Every season, check what's new coming into the house and put anything new out of reach until you're sure it's safe.

## Identify plants safely

Many plants are sold under fancy names, and popular names refer to different species in different regions. The decisive factor is the botanical name, which should be on the label or label. Save these signs or take photos of them. Plant identification apps can help, but they are not always correct. If in doubt, ask the nursery and treat a plant that you cannot identify with certainty as if it were poisonous. In an emergency, the vet will know what to do more quickly if she knows the exact name.

## Plant the balcony and garden in a cat-friendly manner

There are many plants for balconies and gardens that delight cats instead of endangering them. Catnip and valerian attract many cats, and cat gamander also smells irresistible to cats. Robust grasses, herbs such as thyme, rosemary and lemon balm as well as nasturtiums and marigolds are harmless and offer variety. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is the poinsettia really that dangerous?

The poinsettia's milky sap irritates the mouth, stomach and intestines and can cause salivation and vomiting. Severe poisoning is rare. Still, it doesn't belong within the reach of a cat.

### My cat only eats the plants when I'm not there. What to do?

Place poisonous plants in rooms where the cat cannot enter or give them away. With non-toxic plants, cat grass helps increase activity and play.

### Are artificial flowers an alternative?

Yes, as long as they do not consist of small parts that can be swallowed. Some cats also nibble on artificial plants, and plastic pieces in their stomachs are also dangerous.

### Are fragrance oils and home fragrances dangerous for cats?

Many essential oils, such as tea tree oil, can harm cats because their livers have difficulty breaking down certain substances. In cat households, avoid using scented oils on the cat's skin and using scented lamps in rooms that the cat cannot leave.

### Can my cat eat grass in the garden?

Yes, most cats eat grass occasionally, and that's normal. Lawns that have been treated with fertilizer or pesticides and grasses with sharp leaf edges that can injure the mouth and throat are problematic. Cyprus grass and similar ornamental grasses with hard, cutting leaves therefore do not belong near cats.
