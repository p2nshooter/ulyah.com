Most houseplant care mistakes have two causes: the wrong place and too much water. The profile above summarizes nine popular plants, what they need and whether they are an issue for children and pets. The table below shows everything at once.

## All plants at a glance

| plant | Light | Temperature | Pouring | Children and pets |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Monstera | bright, without blazing midday sun | 18-25°C, not below 15°C | when the top 3-5 cm is dry | slightly toxic (calcium oxalate) |
| Ivy plant | light to partial shade | 18-25°C, not below 15°C | when the top 2-3 cm is dry | toxic to pets and small children |
| Peace Lily | light to partial shade | 18-25°C | evenly slightly moist | slightly toxic (calcium oxalate) |
| Lucky Feather | light to shady | 18-26°C | only when the earth is almost dry | toxic (calcium oxalate) |
| Bow hemp | bright to partial shade, also sun | 18-27°C, not below 12°C | only when the earth is completely dry | slightly toxic to pets (saponins) |
| Calathea | bright to partially shaded, no sun | 18-25°C, not below 16°C | evenly slightly moist | is considered non-toxic |
| Fiddle fig | very bright, some morning or evening sun | 18-26°C | when the top 3-5 cm is dry | Milky sap is irritating and slightly toxic to pets |
| Orchid (Phalaenopsis) | bright, without midday sun | 18-26°C, cooler at night | dive when the roots are silvery | is considered non-toxic |
| Cacti and succulents | sunny | warm, cool calm in winter | Soak, then dry completely | mostly non-toxic; some euphorbia, kalanchoe, aloe poisonous |

## Toxic usually means: irritating

Many popular houseplants contain calcium oxalate crystals. Anyone who chews leaves feels a burning sensation in their mouth and throat, and animals salivate or vomit. Severe poisoning is rare, but these plants should be kept out of the reach of small children, cats and dogs. Real lilies, which do not belong on this list, are particularly dangerous for cats: even pollen or flower water can damage their kidneys. The ASPCA emphasizes that its lists are not exhaustive – a plant missing is not automatically harmless.
