Fertilizer bottles say a dosage like “5 ml to 1 liter of water”. However, watering cans hold 1.5, 2 or 5 liters, and a measuring cup is rarely at hand. The calculator above converts the information to your pot.

## This is how it is calculated

Milliliters per liter according to the package × liter in the jug × starch. Half strength means half of the package instructions.

| pack | pot | full strength | half strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 ml to 1 l | 2 litres | 10 ml (2 tsp) | 5 ml (1 tsp) |
| 4 ml to 1 l | 1.5L | 6ml | 3ml |
| 10 ml to 5 l | 5 litres | 10ml | 5ml |

## When is half strength?

For young plants and freshly rooted cuttings, for sensitive plants such as orchids and calathea and in the low-light season when the plants grow more slowly. After repotting, a plant does not need any fertilizer for 6 to 8 weeks because fresh soil is pre-fertilized.

## Three rules

1. Fertilize only on moist soil, never on dry roots.
2. It's better to use weak fertilizers on a regular basis than strong ones on rare occasions: too much fertilizer leaves salts in the soil, roots burn and leaf tips turn brown.
3. White crusts on the soil indicate salt deposits: remove the top layer and rinse thoroughly.
