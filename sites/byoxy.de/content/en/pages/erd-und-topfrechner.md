The volume is rarely written on pots, but it is always on earth bags. The calculator above brings both things together: It calculates the volume of round, conical and square vessels and tells you how many bags you need.

## This is how it is calculated

- **Round pot:** A slightly conical pot is a truncated cone: volume = π × height × (R² + R × r + r²) ÷ 3, with the radius at the top (R) and bottom (r). Without a diameter at the bottom, the calculator calculates with a cylinder.
- **Box or square bucket:** Length × width × height.
- **Fill:** about 90% of the volume because the soil ends 1 to 2 cm below the edge.

| Vessel (inner dimension) | Volume | Earth to fill |
|---|---|---|
| Pot 14 cm Ø, 13 cm high | about 2 l | almost 2 l |
| Pot 30 cm Ø at the top, 24 cm at the bottom, 28 cm high | about 16 l | about 14 l |
| Balcony box 80 × 18 × 16 cm | about 23 l | about 21 l |

## Repotting

The new pot should only be 2 to 4 cm larger in diameter than the old one. In pots that are too large, the soil stays wet for a long time and the roots rot more easily. A vent hole is mandatory; a thin layer of expanded clay prevents waterlogging.

## Which earth?

The Federal Environment Agency recommends peat-free soil because peat bogs, which store a lot of carbon, are drained for peat. The list of ingredients reveals whether a soil is peat-free; Terms like “peat reduced” or “peat farm” often still mean a lot of peat. Peat-free soil dries out more quickly, so water more often in smaller quantities.
