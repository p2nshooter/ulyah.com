Basil is the most popular culinary herb for pasta, tomatoes, pizza and pesto. Many people buy it as a potted plant in the supermarket, and many experience the same thing: after a few days, the leaves droop, the stem turns black, and the plant is dead. This is not because of a lack of talent, but because of how these plants are produced. With just a few simple steps, basil can be kept for months and harvested again and again.

This guide explains why supermarket basil is so sensitive, how to save it, which location is ideal, how to water and harvest correctly and how to propagate and overwinter basil.

## Why supermarket basil is dying

In a typical supermarket pot, there are **20 to 40 individual plants** close together in a little soil. They were quickly grown in the greenhouse under ideal conditions. In the kitchen they then encounter less light, drier air and fluctuating temperatures. The many plants compete for water, light and nutrients, the air stands between the stems and the soil either stays too wet or dries out quickly. The result: stem rot, fungal infestation and wilted leaves.

## Rescue: divide basil

The most important thing to do immediately after purchase:

1. **Unpotting:** Carefully pull the root ball out of the pot.
2. **Divide:** Pull the bale apart into three to four parts with your hands. The roots will tear a little, that's okay.
3. **Planting:** Place each part in its own pot with a diameter of 12 to 15 cm with fresh herb soil. Several parts can also be placed in a balcony box at a distance.
4. **Water** and place in light but not direct sun for a few days until the plants have recovered.

After dividing, the plants have more space, more soil and more air. They grow stronger and are less vulnerable. More about the technology in [Dividing plants](/ratgeber/pflanzen-teilen/).

## Location

Basil comes from warm regions and loves **warmth and light.**

| factor | Optimal |
|---|---|
| Light | Very bright, several hours of sun, slightly shaded at midday in midsummer |
| Temperature | 20 to 25°C, not below 12°C |
| Wind | Protected |
| Inside | South or west window |
| Outside | From mid-May on the balcony or terrace, sheltered, sunny spot |

Basil does not tolerate cold well. Below 10°C the leaves develop black spots. So bring it in at night in spring and autumn.

## Pouring

Basil likes **even moisture, but not wetness.** It is particularly sensitive to wet stems and leaves.

- **Water from the bottom:** Place the pot in a saucer with water and let it soak for 15 to 20 minutes, then pour off any excess water. This keeps the stems and leaves dry.
- **Water in the morning** so that the plant gets through the day well.
- **Finger test:** Water when the top inch or two is dry. In summer on the balcony often every day.
- **Do not water over the leaves.**

Drooping leaves in the afternoon can mean lack of water, but also heat. If the plant recovers in the evening, it was heat.

## Soil and fertilizing

- **Soil:** Loose, nutrient-rich herb or vegetable soil. In contrast to Mediterranean herbs such as thyme, basil is a **heavy eater** and needs a lot of nutrients.
- **Fertilizing:** Start about four weeks after repotting, then every one to two weeks with an organic liquid herb fertilizer at half the dosage.

## Harvest correctly

The type of harvest determines whether basil becomes bushy or bare and long.

**Wrong:** Pluck individual large leaves. The plant continues to grow taller and the stems become bare.

**Correct:** Cut or snap off entire **shoot tips above a pair of leaves**.

1. Look for the second or third pair of leaves from the top of a shoot.
2. Cut right above it. Small buds sit in the leaf axils below the cut site.
3. Two new shoots grow from these buds.

The plant branches out with every harvest, becomes bushier and produces more and more leaves. Never harvest more than a third of the plant at once.

## Remove flowers

As soon as basil begins to bloom, it puts its power into flowers and seeds; the leaves become smaller and taste more bitter. Therefore, pinch off flower heads at the shoot tips early. If you want to do something good for insects, let one or two plants bloom. Basil flowers are edible and popular with bees. See [Bee-Friendly Balcony](/ratgeber/bienenfreundlicher-balkon/).

## Propagation

### cuttings

Basil roots very easily in water:

1. Cut off shoot tips 8 to 10 cm long.
2. Remove lower leaves.
3. Place in a glass of water in a bright place.
4. After one to two weeks, roots will appear.
5. Plant in pots with herb soil.

See [Cuttings in a Water Jar](/ratgeber/stecklinge-im-wasserglas/).

### sowing

Basil is a **light germinator.** The seeds are scattered on moist soil from April and only pressed down, not covered. At 20 to 25 °C they germinate after about one to two weeks. See [Sowing on the windowsill](/ratgeber/aussaat-auf-der-fensterbank/).

## Varieties

| variety | Taste and characteristics |
|---|---|
| Genovese basil | The classic, large leaves, ideal for pesto |
| Thai basil | Anise-like, purple stems, for Asian cuisine |
| Lemon basil | Fresh and lemony, for fish and salads |
| Red basil (Purple Ruffles, Dark Opal) | Decorative, mildly spicy |
| Greek bush basil | Small leaves, compact growth, robust |
| African bush basil | Perennial, very robust, blooms a lot, popular with bees |
| Cinnamon basil | Cinnamon-like aroma |

If you want to keep basil permanently, you should try perennial varieties such as bush basil.

## Basil in winter

Most basil varieties are annuals and die down in the fall. With a little effort you can still get them through the winter:

- **Brightest place by the south window**, ideally with a plant lamp.
- **Temperature above 15 °C**, not directly on the cold window.
- **Water sparingly**, from below.
- **Harvest little.**

It is easier to root cuttings in late summer and keep them as young plants on the window.

## Typical problems

| problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Black, soft stems at the base | Too wet, too dense, fungus | Divide, water from below, remove affected plants |
| Black spots on leaves | Cold below 10°C | Make it warmer |
| Yellow lower leaves | Nutrient deficiency or waterlogging | Fertilize, check watering |
| Long, bare stems | Too little light, wrong harvest | Place in a brighter spot and harvest the shoot tips |
| Gray coating on the underside of the leaves | Downy mildew | Remove affected leaves and keep them drier |
| Aphids | Weak plants | Rinse off. See [Fighting Aphids Naturally](/ratgeber/blattlaeuse-natuerlich-bekaempfen/) |

## Practical example: Pesto from your own balcony

A supermarket basil was purchased in May and immediately divided into three parts. The parts went into a balcony box with herb soil on a west-facing balcony. Watering took place in the morning, from below over the box's water reservoir. Only pairs of leaves were harvested, flowers were pinched off, and organic fertilizer was given every two weeks. In August the three plants were each 40 centimeters high and bushy. There was enough for several jars of pesto, and cuttings were grown for the windowsill in September.

## Basil in midsummer

Basil loves warmth, but on a south-facing balcony in the blazing midday sun it can sometimes be too much for it in midsummer. Small pots then dry out within a few hours and the leaves develop light, papery spots. Therefore, plant basil in pots with a capacity of at least two to three liters, water thoroughly in the morning on hot days and, during hot periods, place the plants so that they get some shade from larger neighbors at midday.

Basil, on the other hand, does not tolerate cold well. Growth stops at temperatures as low as ten degrees, and black spots appear on the leaves after cold nights. Therefore, only put the pots outside after the Ice Saints and bring them in in good time in late summer when the nights get cool.

## Basil care calendar

- **March and April:** Sow on the warm windowsill. Basil is a light germinator; the seeds are only pressed on and not covered with soil.
- **May:** After the Ice Saints in mid-May, hardy plants are allowed on the balcony or terrace.
- **June to August:** Harvest regularly, break out the flower heads, water evenly and fertilize lightly about every two weeks.
- **September:** Take cuttings to get plants for the winter and use the last big harvests to make pesto.
- **October to February:** Continue to cultivate a few plants in the brightest window, water sparingly and harvest carefully.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I plant basil with other herbs?

Best with plants with similar needs such as parsley or chives. Mediterranean herbs like thyme and rosemary require lean, dry soil and don't mix well together.

### How do I store basil?

Freshly harvested shoots will keep in a glass of water at room temperature for a few days. The leaves turn black in the refrigerator. Pesto, freezing in oil or drying are suitable for preserving, although dried basil loses much of its aroma.

### Why does my basil taste bitter?

Mostly because it blooms. Remove flowers early.

### Can I freeze basil?

Yes, but whole leaves turn black and mushy when frozen. It is better to chop the leaves finely and freeze them in ice cube trays with a little olive oil or water. Pesto without cheese can also be frozen in portions; Stir in the cheese after it has defrosted.

### Why is my basil getting brown or black spots?

Black spots after cool nights are usually cold damage. If, on the other hand, the leaves turn yellow from below and a grayish-purple fuzz appears on the underside, it is downy mildew, a fungal disease that occurs in damp, cool weather. Remove infected shoots immediately, water only on the soil and place the plants in a more airy place. Bright, dry spots after a hot day, on the other hand, are sunburn.

## Conclusion

Basil is not a disposable herb. Divide supermarket pots immediately after purchase, keep the plants warm and bright, water from below and in the morning, fertilize regularly and always harvest entire shoot tips above a pair of leaves. With this care, basil provides fresh leaves all summer long. More about growing herbs: [Herb garden on the windowsill](/ratgeber/kraeutergarten-auf-der-fensterbank/).
