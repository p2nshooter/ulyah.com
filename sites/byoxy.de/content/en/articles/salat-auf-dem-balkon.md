Lettuce is one of the easiest vegetables to have on the balcony. It grows quickly, requires little space, copes with partial shade and can be harvested repeatedly from spring to autumn. Lettuces in particular provide fresh leaves for weeks without having to harvest the entire plant. Even a single balcony box can regularly supply a household with crisp greenery.

This guide explains which salads are suitable, how to sow or plant them, which containers and soil are suitable, how to water and harvest and how to avoid typical problems such as shoots or snails.

## Suitable salads

| Salad type | Features | Harvest | Suitability |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pick lettuce (cut lettuce) | Does not form a head, outer leaves are harvested continuously | Over many weeks | Ideal for balconies and beginners |
| Lettuce | Forms a solid head | Once, whole head | Good, needs a little more space |
| Romaine lettuce (romaine lettuce) | Elongated, crisp, heat tolerant | Once or sheet by sheet | Good |
| Oak leaf lettuce | Lobed leaves, green or red, mild | Sheet by sheet or as a head | Very good |
| Lollo Rosso and Lollo Bionda | Curly leaves, decorative | Sheet by sheet | Very good |
| Arugula | Spicy, quick | After 4 to 6 weeks, cut several times | Very good, shoots quickly in hot weather |
| Asian salads (Mizuna, mustard cabbage, pak choi) | Spicy to spicy, quick | Cut multiple times | Very good, also for autumn |
| Lamb's lettuce | Small rosettes, hardy | Autumn and winter | Good for the cold season |
| Spinach | Classic, likes it cool | Sheet by sheet | spring and autumn |

For the balcony, **pickle lettuce, oak leaf, lollipop and rocket** are the best choice because they deliver for a long time and over and over again.

## Location

Lettuce likes **bright, but not too hot places.**

- **Spring and Fall:** Sunny balconies are ideal.
- **Summer:** Partial shade or east and west balconies are better. In full sun and heat above 25°C, lettuce begins to sprout, form a flower stalk and become bitter.
- **North balcony:** Lettuce and lamb's lettuce also grow there if there is enough bright daylight. See [Plants for the Shade Balcony](/ratgeber/schattenbalkon-pflanzen/).

## vessels and earth

- **Boxes:** Balcony boxes at least 15 cm deep are sufficient because lettuce has shallow roots.
- **Extractor holes** are important.
- **Soil:** Loose, humus-rich vegetable or herb soil. Lettuce is a low to medium eater; too much nitrogen promotes nitrate in the leaves.
- **Raised beds and plant tables:** Back-friendly and snail-proof.

## Sowing or young plants?

### Young plants

From April onwards, young lettuce plants will be available in garden centers and at weekly markets. They are the fastest way to harvest. When planting, make sure that the root neck is not set too deep. Lettuce should “flutter”, i.e. sit so high that the ball pokes slightly out of the ground. Lettuces planted too deeply rot easily.

### sowing

Lettuce is a **light germinator.** The seeds are just scattered on the soil and pressed lightly.

| period | sowing | Note |
|---|---|---|
| February to March | Prefer on the windowsill | Outdoors from April, lettuce tolerates light frost |
| April to June | Straight into the box | Germination temperature 15 to 18 °C |
| July | Heat tolerant varieties | Lettuce germinates poorly above 20 °C, keep it cool |
| August to September | Autumn salads, Asian salads, lamb's lettuce | Ideal for late summer |

Separate the seedlings about 20 to 25 cm apart. Lettuces can be a little denser. More about the technology in [Sowing on the windowsill](/ratgeber/aussaat-auf-der-fensterbank/).

### Sow in batches

Instead of sowing everything at once, reseed a small amount every **two to three weeks**. So there is always salad in different sizes and never too much at once.

## Pouring

- **Evenly Moist:** Lettuce is over 90 percent water and reacts quickly to dryness with wilting, bitter taste and bolting.
- **Water in the morning**, directly onto the soil. Wet leaves overnight encourage rot and attract slugs.
- **In midsummer** often daily.
- **Mulch** made from grass clippings or straw keeps the soil moist.

## Fertilize

With fresh, pre-fertilized soil, lettuce usually doesn't need any additional fertilizer. For lettuces that have been harvested several times, a weak organic liquid fertilizer can help after four to six weeks. It is better to fertilize sparingly.

## Harvest

### Pick lettuce

Always harvest the **outer leaves** and leave the heart. The plant produces new leaves from the inside. A lettuce can be harvested over six to eight weeks.

### Lettuce

Harvest when the head is firm and before it begins to shoot. Cut off just above the soil with a knife.

### Rocket and Asian salads

Cut about two to three centimeters above the soil. They sprout again once or twice.

### Best harvest time

In the morning when the leaves are crisp. Nitrate levels are lower in the late afternoon after sunny days, if that matters.

## Prevent shooting

Shoot means the lettuce forms a flower stalk. The leaves become bitter and small. Causes include heat, long days and drought.

- **Choose heat-tolerant varieties** for summer. Many variety descriptions mention “bolt resistant”.
- **Partial shade** in midsummer.
- **Water evenly.**
- **Harvest in time.**

## Typical problems

| problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Snails, eaten leaves | Damp, ground-level boxes | Place the boxes elevated, copper tape, collect them in the evening |
| Aphids, often on the underside | Warm phases | Rinse off, see [Fight aphids naturally](/ratgeber/blattlaeuse-natuerlich-bekaempfen/) |
| Rotting lower leaves | Planted too deep, too wet | Set higher, water in the morning |
| Bitter taste | Heat, dryness, shooting | Partial shade, water evenly |
| Brown leaf edges | Calcium deficiency due to drought | Water evenly |
| Gray coating | Gray mold or downy mildew | Remove affected leaves and plant in a more airy manner |

## Combinations in the box

Salad goes well with radishes, chives, nasturtiums and marigolds. Radishes are ready to harvest quickly and use the space between the lettuces before they get big. Nasturtiums are edible and attract aphids away from salads.

| Box idea | Content |
|---|---|
| Colorful picking box | Lollo Rosso, Lollo Bionda, oak leaf green and red |
| seasoning box | Rocket, mizuna, red mustard cabbage |
| Salad and herbs | Lettuce, chives, parsley |
| Autumn box | Lamb's lettuce, Asian salads, winter purslane |

## Practical example: Salad for a family

Two 100 cm boxes with lettuces in four colors were planted on an east-facing balcony. A row of rocket and Asian lettuce was sown every three weeks. Watering took place in the morning and the boxes were placed on a plant shelf at waist height so that snails had little chance. From May to October, the outer leaves were harvested twice a week. In July, two lettuces became bitter and were replaced by new young plants. The family barely had to buy lettuce all summer.

## Nitrate and hygiene

Lettuce can store different amounts of nitrate depending on the season, light and fertilization. With little light and a lot of nitrogen, the content increases. This can be easily controlled on the balcony: fertilize sparingly, use sunny places in spring and autumn, and do not eat the outer leaf stalks, where the most nitrate accumulates.

Before eating, balcony lettuce is washed thoroughly, especially if it grows on a busy street or if birds visit the balcony. Toss the leaves briefly in cold water, let them drain and use them fresh. Harvested leaves will last for two to three days in a moist container in the vegetable compartment.

## Add wild herbs and edible flowers

A salad box becomes more varied if you add a few unusual plants. Sorrel brings a fresh, sour note, the small meadow button, also known as pimpinelle, tastes slightly like cucumber, and purslane has fleshy, juicy leaves. Nasturtiums provide hot, spicy leaves and edible flowers, and borage flowers and daisies also make beautiful, edible decorations.

It is best to harvest flowers in the morning when they are freshly opened and only use flowers from plants that you have grown yourself without pesticides. Flowers from the garden center are usually not suitable for consumption.

## Lettuce in plant bags, hanging baskets and raised beds

Lettuce has shallow roots and does not need deep containers. Just fifteen to twenty centimeters of soil depth is enough, which is why plant bags, shallow raised balcony beds and hanging baskets in which lettuce grows decoratively over the edge are also suitable. Vertical planting systems on the wall make particularly good use of space, but they dry out quickly and need to be checked daily on warm days.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I regrow lettuce from the supermarket?

Romaine lettuce stalks sprout some new leaves in water. But that is hardly enough for a real harvest. Sowing your own seeds is more productive.

### How much lettuce do I need for a household?

Six to eight lettuces with regular reseeding are usually enough for two people.

### Does lettuce grow on the balcony in winter?

Lamb's lettuce and winter purslane are hardy and provide fresh leaves in mild winters.

### Why is my salad bitter?

Heat, long days and drought cause many salads to sprout and become bitter. Older leaves also taste more bitter than young ones. Therefore, harvest young leaves regularly, water evenly and sow heat-resistant varieties in mid-summer or place the boxes in partial shade.

### Can I get seeds from my lettuce myself?

For seed-resistant varieties, yes: let a few plants shoot and flower and harvest the seeds when the inflorescences become fluffy. In the case of F1 hybrids, which are marked as such on the seed bag, inconsistent plants usually develop from their own seeds.

### How do I protect lettuce from slugs?

Snails usually get onto the balcony with purchased plants or soil. Check new plants, collect snails in the evening and put copper tape around boxes and containers. If you do use slug pellets, choose preparations based on iron III phosphate, which are also approved for organic farming.

Beer traps, on the other hand, do not belong on the balcony because they attract additional snails.

## Conclusion

Lettuce is the ideal balcony vegetable: choose lettuce, oak leaf, lollipop and rocket, resow every two to three weeks, keep the soil evenly moist and always harvest the outer leaves. In midsummer, partial shade protects against shooting. From spring to autumn there is fresh salad straight from the balcony.
