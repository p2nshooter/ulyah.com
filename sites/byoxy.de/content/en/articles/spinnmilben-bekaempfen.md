Spider mites are one of the most persistent pests on houseplants. They are so small that you can hardly see them with the naked eye, multiply explosively in warm, dry heating air and leave behind mottled, matt leaves and fine webs. However, if you act early and remain consistent, you can get them under control.

This guide explains how you can recognize spider mites, why they appear so often in winter, which control methods work and how you can prevent a new infestation.

## Recognize spider mites

The most common species is the common spider mite (Tetranychus urticae). It belongs to the arachnids, not to the insects.

| feature | Description |
|---|---|
| size | About 0.5 millimeters, visible as dots with a magnifying glass |
| color | Yellowish-green to reddish, often orange-red in winter |
| Whereabouts | Especially on the undersides of leaves and in leaf axils |
| webs | Fine, silky threads between leaves and stems, in severe infestations |
| damage pattern | Fine, light speckles on the upper side of the leaves, later silvery-yellow or brownish leaves |

### The handkerchief test

Hold a white tissue or sheet of paper under a suspicious leaf and tap the leaf firmly. If tiny dots then move on the paper, they are most likely spider mites. When you wipe your finger across the paper, they leave reddish or greenish streaks behind.

### Particularly endangered plants

- Calatheas and Maranten
- Alocasia and Colocasia
- Fiddle fig and birch fig
- Ivy and ivy plant
- Palm trees, especially areca and kentia palms
- Schefflera
- Citrus plants, roses and angel trumpets
- Monstera and Philodendron

## Why do spider mites appear especially in winter?

Spider mites love **warm, dry air.** They reproduce particularly quickly when the humidity is below 40 percent and temperatures above 20 °C: A female lays up to 100 eggs, and a new generation cycle only lasts about one to two weeks in warm weather. These are exactly the conditions in apartments during the heated winter months. Plants near the heater are most at risk. ## Immediate measures

1. **Isolate:** Immediately separate the infected plant from other plants. Spider mites migrate to neighboring plants via leaves, window sills and clothing.
2. **Check neighboring plants:** Check the undersides of all plants in the area.
3. **Remove severely affected leaves** and dispose of them in a closed bag with household waste.

## Control methods

### Shower off

Rinse the plant vigorously in the shower or bathtub with lukewarm water, especially the undersides of the leaves. Put the pot in a plastic bag beforehand so that the soil is not washed out. Showering removes many mites and webs at once, but never removes all of the eggs. It's a good start, but needs to be repeated and combined with other methods.

### Foil method (moisture retention)

After showering, the still wet plant is covered with a transparent plastic bag or film and placed in a bright place without direct sun for two to three days. Spider mites feel very uncomfortable in the moist air under the film. The method is suitable for smaller, robust plants. Then remove the foil and ventilate the plant well. There is a risk of mold on sensitive, hairy leaves.

### Potash soap or soft soap solution

Spray a solution of potassium soap or soft soap without additives, about 15 to 20 ml per liter of water, onto all parts of the plant, especially the undersides of the leaves. The soap clogs and destroys the mites' breathing openings. The treatment is repeated every three to five days for at least two to three weeks. Test a leaf first to see whether the plant can tolerate the solution.

### Rapeseed oil or paraffin oil preparations

Commercial oil-based pesticides form a thin film that suffocates mites and eggs. They often work on eggs too, which is a benefit. Be sure to follow the instructions for use, do not use in direct sunlight and test on a leaf first.

### Neem oil preparations

Neem preparations work by inhibiting feeding and development. They are available in Germany as approved plant protection products and should only be used according to the package leaflet. Even mixed neem oil solutions are not permitted.

### predatory mites

Biological control with predatory mites (Phytoseiulus persimilis or Amblyseius californicus) is particularly effective on many plants or in winter gardens. The predatory mites are delivered in litter boxes or bags and distributed to the affected plants. They eat spider mites and their eggs.

- **Conditions:** Predatory mites need humidity above 60 percent and temperatures above 18 to 20 °C.
- **Do not use chemical agents** at the same time, they also harm the beneficial insects.
- **Patience:** The effect occurs after two to three weeks.

### Systemic means

Plant protection sticks with systemic active ingredients are inserted into the soil and the plant absorbs the active ingredient. Not all sticks are effective against spider mites because mites are not insects. Pay close attention to its effectiveness against spider mites and follow the application instructions. Consider carefully in households with children and pets.

## Treatment plan

| day | Measure |
|---|---|
| 1 | Isolate the plant, remove severely affected leaves and shower |
| 1 to 3 | Foil method or first treatment with potassium soap or oil preparation |
| 5 | Second treatment, check undersides of leaves |
| 10 | Third treatment |
| 15 | Fourth treatment, tissue test |
| 21 to 28 | Another tissue test. Only after several negative tests did I return to the other plants |

The reason for repeated treatment: the eggs survive most treatments. If new mites hatch, they must be captured before they lay eggs again.

## Prevention

- **Increase humidity:** Above 50 percent inhibits reproduction.
- **Distance from heating:** Do not place plants directly above radiators.
- **Check regularly:** Look at the undersides of the leaves once a week, especially for endangered species.
- **Wipe or shower leaves:** Dust-free leaves are healthier and mites are removed early.
- **New plants in quarantine:** Keep separated and controlled for two to three weeks.
- **Strong plants:** Properly watered and fertilized plants are less susceptible.

## Practical example: The Alocasia on the radiator

An Alocasia stood on a windowsill above the radiator in winter. The leaves became dull and finely speckled, and fine webs appeared on the petioles. The tissue test confirmed spider mites. The plant was isolated, showered and placed under foil for two days, then treated with potassium soap solution every five days. After three weeks the test was negative. The Alocasia was given a new place one meter away from the radiator next to a humidifier and has remained infestation-free ever since. Of the neighboring plants, there was only a slight infestation on one ivy plant, which disappeared with showering and potassium soap.

## Spider mites or something else?

| damage pattern | Spider mites | Thrips | deficiency or sunburn |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top of leaf | Fine light speckles | Shiny silvery spots with black dots of feces | Even lightening or dry spots |
| Underside of leaf | Tiny dots, fine webs | Slender, elongated animals | No animals |
| Tissue test | Moving points | Elongated, nimble animals | Nothing |
| Preferred time | Winter, dry heating air | All year round, often summer | Independent |

Thrips are often confused with spider mites because they also cause light spots. However, they can be caught with blue plates and also react to showering and soap or oil preparations. If you are unsure, look at the underside of the leaf with a magnifying glass with tenfold magnification: spider mites are round and have eight legs, while thrips are narrow and elongated.

## Spider mites on balconies and terraces

Spider mites are not just an apartment problem. In hot, dry summers they also attack many plants outside, especially beans, cucumbers, zucchini, roses and potted plants in sheltered, rain-protected places. Finely mottled, later silvery-gray leaves and webs in the shoot tips are typical.

Outside, regularly rinsing the undersides of the leaves helps, preferably in the morning, so that the leaves dry by the evening. Rain often provides relief, which is why plants under eaves are particularly at risk. Predatory mites can also be used on terraces and balconies in summer if the temperatures are consistently high enough; Providers usually specify a minimum temperature for use.

## New plants under quarantine

Many spider mite problems start with a new plant. Therefore, place new additions separately from your other plants for two to three weeks and check them several times with a magnifying glass during this time, especially on the undersides of the leaves and in the leaf axils. A thorough rinse immediately after purchase will remove some possible pests.

You should also carefully check plants that have spent the summer on the balcony before putting them away in the fall. This way you don't bring pests into the house, where they would multiply quickly in the dry heated air.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are spider mites dangerous to people?

No. They only live on plants and do not bite people or animals.

### Can I put the plant outside?

In summer, a downpour or a place with higher humidity can help. In winter, the cold would be more harmful to tropical plants than the mites.

### Where do spider mites even come from?

They are introduced with new plants, cut flowers, through clothing or through open windows.

### Do spider mites survive the winter outside?

Yes. Female spider mites overwinter in protected places, such as in bark cracks, in plant residues or on trellises, and often turn orange-red. They begin laying eggs in spring. Therefore, remove old, infected plant remains from the balcony in autumn.

### How long does the fight take?

Usually several weeks because many agents do not reach the eggs and new mites constantly hatch when it is warm. Therefore, repeat the treatment every five to seven days for at least three to four weeks and then continue to check regularly.

### Do predatory mites also help in the home in winter?

Yes, some species of predatory mites are specifically used indoors and work all year round in warm homes. However, very dry heating air causes problems for them. Therefore, place affected plants slightly moister and follow the provider's instructions on temperature and humidity.

## Conclusion

Spider mites are stubborn but defeatable. Recognize them early by the speckle pattern and with the handkerchief test, isolate infected plants, shower them and treat them several times every few days with soap or oil preparations or use predatory mites. In the long term, higher humidity is the best protection. For mealybugs, however, [mealybugs and mealybugs](/ratgeber/wollaeuse-und-schmierlaeuse/) helps.
