The suitcases are packed, but the plants stay at home. For many plant lovers, the holiday is associated with worrying about whether monsteras, basil and balcony boxes will survive the absence. The good news: With a little preparation, most plants can survive for a week without help, and with simple watering aids for two to three weeks.

This guide shows you how to prepare your plants, which watering methods are available, which ones are suitable for how long and what you should pay attention to when you return.

## Which plants are at risk?

Not all plants are equally sensitive:

| group | Examples | Without help |
|---|---|---|
| Very robust | Cacti, succulents, arched hemp, lucky feather | 3 to 4 weeks, often longer |
| Robust | Monstera, ivy, philodendron, rubber tree | About 1 to 2 weeks after thorough watering |
| Sensitive | Ferns, calathea, peace lily, orchids in moss | A few days to a week |
| Very sensitive | Balcony boxes in summer, herbs, vegetables, young plants, cuttings | One to three days |

Balcony plants in full sun use a lot of water every day in midsummer. For them, vacation provision is the most difficult.

## Preparation: one week in advance

1. **Health check:** Check plants for pests and treat affected plants beforehand. A spider mite infestation can spread significantly in two weeks.
2. **Stop fertilizing:** Fertilized plants grow faster and use more water.
3. **Remove faded and wilted plants.**
4. **Test the irrigation system:** Try out new systems such as clay cones or wicks a few days in advance and see whether they provide too much or too little water.
5. **Do not repot plants**, the roots need time to recover.

## Preparation: on the day of departure

1. **Water thoroughly:** Water all plants except succulents thoroughly, dipping when the pads are dry. Drain excess water from houseplants.
2. **Cooler and shady locations:** Move plants a little further away from the window so that they are bright but out of direct sun. Less light and heat means less evaporation.
3. **Group:** Place plants close together. They create a wetter microclimate.
4. **Heating and shutters:** In winter, keep the heating low, but not completely off. In summer, half close the shutters on south-facing windows.
5. **Balcony plants in the shade:** If possible, move pots and boxes to a shady spot or close to the wall of the house.

## Irrigation methods at a glance

### Wick irrigation

A cotton or synthetic wick connects the pot to a water vessel. The water is drawn into the earth by capillary force.

1. Wet the wick thoroughly beforehand.
2. Stick one end several centimeters deep into the soil, as close to the roots as possible.
3. Place the other end in a container of water that is slightly higher or at the same level as the pot.

Suitable for medium-sized pots, one to three weeks, depending on the size of the container. Wool threads work in a pinch, synthetic cords are more reliable.

### clay cones

A porous clay cone is inserted into the ground and connected to a water container via a hose. When the earth dries out, it draws water through the clay. The method is reliable and can also be used for balcony boxes if several cones are used. It is important to test the correct container height: if the container is too high, overwatering can occur.

### Water storage balls and upside down bottles

Glass balls or upside-down bottles filled with water are inserted into the soil and gradually release the water. They are suitable for a few days, but their release is unpredictable: in loose soil they sometimes empty within hours.

### Bathtub or sink method

1. Place an old towel or capillary mat in the bathtub or sink.
2. Let in a few centimeters of water so that the cloth is soaked.
3. Place pots with a drain hole on the cloth. The earth draws water from below.

Important: The pots should not stand directly in water. The method only works if the bathroom has a window, otherwise there is no light. For robust species it is suitable for one to two weeks.

### Drip irrigation with timer

For balconies with many boxes and buckets, drip irrigation with a pump and timer connected to a tap or water tank is the most reliable solution. It requires some effort to set up and should be tested several days before departure. More about this in [Balcony irrigation](/ratgeber/balkon-bewaesserung/).

### Self-watering pots and boxes

Pots and balcony boxes with water storage in the bottom provide plants with water for a week or longer. Anyone who travels frequently should consider making a permanent change. Fill the storage tank completely before you leave.

### Plastic bag (mini greenhouse)

For small, moisture-loving plants and cuttings: Water the plant thoroughly, drain and place a clear plastic bag over it, supported by sticks to keep it from touching the leaves. Place in a bright place, but out of direct sun. The water evaporates, condenses and drips back. If there is too much heat, there is a risk of mold.

## Which method for which duration?

| Duration | Houseplants | Balcony plants in summer |
|---|---|---|
| weekend | Water thoroughly, place in shade | Water thoroughly, move to the shade |
| 1 week | Pour thoroughly, group, for sensitive wicks or bathtubs | Water storage box, clay cones or help |
| 2 weeks | Wick, clay cone, bathtub | Drip irrigation or neighborhood help |
| 3 weeks and more | Clay cones with large container, neighborhood help | Drip irrigation with large tank or help |

## Neighborhood help

No technology is as reliable as a person who stops by. To make it work well:

- **Group plants** so that the helper doesn't have to go through the whole apartment.
- **Colored markings:** e.g. E.g. a green dot for “water every time”, yellow for “only when the soil is dry”, red for “do not water”.
- **Short written instructions** with photos.
- **Provide a watering can** and explain whether water should be drained from the planters.
- A thank you, for example a plant or herbs from the balcony.

## After returning

- **Don't flood everything immediately:** Check the soil first. Some plants are still wet.
- **Dip dry bales** until no more air bubbles appear.
- **Floppy plants** often recover within a few hours after watering.
- **Remove dried leaves.**
- **Check for pests**, especially spider mites.
- **Put back in the usual place**.

If something went wrong despite everything, [Yellow leaves: causes](/ratgeber/gelbe-blaetter-ursachen/) and [Watering houseplants properly](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-richtig-giessen/) will help with the diagnosis.

## Practical example: Two weeks of summer vacation

Before a vacation in August, the plan was to get ten houseplants and three balcony boxes with geraniums and herbs. The houseplants were watered thoroughly and placed on wet towels in the tub in the bathroom with a window. The cacti just stayed at the window. A small drip irrigation system with a timer on the tap was installed on the balcony and tested for a week. A neighbor stopped by once a week. After returning, all the plants were healthy, only the basil suffered a little and was cut back.

## Arrange plants and adjust the location

Even before any technical solution, you can significantly reduce the water consumption of your plants. Move plants away from sunny, south-facing windows and place them closely together in a bright room out of direct sun. The air in the group is more humid and the plants evaporate less. A cooler room also helps because plants need less water at lower temperatures.

Small pots dry out quickest. Place them in a large tub or box on a layer of expanded clay that you fill with a little water without the pots standing in it. Half-close blinds or curtains on sunny windows, but allow enough light into the room so that the plants don't end up in the dark for weeks.

## Instruct the plant sitter well

When neighbors or friends water, clear instructions prevent most problems. Mark the pots with colored stickers, for example green for watering a lot once a week, yellow for watering a little once a week, and red for not watering once a week. A watering can with a mark for the right amount and a piece of paper with photos of the plants also help.

Expressly point out that cacti, succulents, arched hemp and lucky feathers do better with too little water than with too much. Many well-intentioned plant sitters tend to water too much. Also leave your phone number so they can send you a photo if there are any problems.

## Test run before departure

Try each watering method a few days before the vacation. This way you can see whether a wick is drawing enough water, whether a clay cone is draining too quickly and whether the drip irrigation is tight. A test run is particularly important with automatic systems, because a leaky hose can cause a lot of damage in two weeks.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I put plants in water before vacation?

Just for diving. Plants that sit in water for days develop root rot. The exception is hydroponic plants with the right water level.

### Do hydrogel crystals help?

Water-storing granules can increase the soil's water-holding capacity. However, they should be mixed in when planting and not scattered on the surface shortly before departure.

### What about plants during winter holidays?

In winter, most plants use significantly less water. Watering thoroughly and not turning off the heating completely is often enough for one to two weeks.

### Should I fertilize or repot before vacation?

Rather not. Newly repotted plants need attention, and fertilizer stimulates growth and therefore water requirements. Therefore, repot a few weeks before or after your return and do not fertilize in the week before you leave.

### How long do cacti last without water?

Healthy cacti and succulents usually survive two to three weeks without water in summer, and even significantly longer during winter dormancy. Water them as usual before you leave and don't put them behind glass in the blazing sun.

## Conclusion

With the right preparation, a holiday for plants is no problem. Water thoroughly, keep them cooler and shadier, group them together, and choose a watering method that suits the duration and plants. Test new systems beforehand and organize help from the neighborhood for longer absences or balcony plants in the summer.
