Some plants do the work of propagation almost completely for you. They form small, finished young plants on long runners, at the base of the mother plant or even on their flower stalks. These offshoots and children often already have their own roots or form them very quickly. This is the safest way for beginners to get new plants and an inexhaustible source for anyone who likes to give plants as gifts.

This guide explains the different types of offshoots, which plants they form, when is the right time to cut them off and how the young plants grow well.

## Terms: offshoot, kindle, runner, sinker

In everyday life the terms are often used interchangeably. They can be roughly distinguished as follows:

| term | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Kindel | Young plant directly at the base of the mother plant or on the trunk | Aloe, bromeliads, echeverias, cacti, pilea |
| foothills | Long shoots, at the end of which young plants grow | Spider plant, strawberry, Jew's beard |
| Brood plants on the leaf | Tiny plants on the edge of the leaves | Brood leaf (Kalanchoe daigremontiana) |
| Keiki | Young plant on the flower spike of orchids | Phalaenopsis, Dendrobium |
| lowering | A shoot is placed in soil on the mother plant and roots there | Money plant, ivy, hoya, philodendron |
| Brood bulbs and daughter tubers | New bulbs or tubers on the mother bulb | Amaryllis, lucky clover, lucky feather |

What they all have in common is that the young plants are genetically identical to the mother plant. A variegated variety remains variegated and a certain flower color remains.

## The right time

The most important rule: **Don't cut off the children too early.** The bigger the young plant and the more roots it has, the more securely it will grow.

| plant | Disconnect if... |
|---|---|
| spider plant | The young plants show small roots, often already on the mother plant |
| Aloe vera | The child is about a quarter to a third the size of the mother plant and has its own roots |
| Bromeliad (e.g. Guzmania) | The child is about a third to half the size of the mother plant |
| Echeveria | The daughter rosette shows its own small rosette image and roots |
| Pilea (UFO plant) | The offshoot is a few centimeters high and has several leaves |
| Orchid keiki | Three to four leaves and several roots three to five centimeters long |
| Cactus Kindel | They can be easily removed, usually with roots |

The best season is spring to early summer when plants are growing and days are bright.

## Spider plant: the classic

The spider plant (Chlorophytum comosum) forms numerous young plants on long, overhanging runners.

### Method 1: Cut it off and put it in the water

1. Cut off a young plant with visible roots at the foothills.
2. Place in a small glass of water so that only the base is in the water.
3. After one to two weeks, the roots will be long enough to plant the plant in soil.

### Method 2: Directly into soil

Place the young plant directly in a small pot with moist soil and keep it evenly moist for the first two weeks.

### Method 3: Remove moss from the foothills

Place a small pot with soil next to the mother plant, place the young plant on the runner and secure it with a hairpin. When it has grown, cut the runner. This method is particularly safe because the young plant is cared for by the mother until it has its own roots.

## Aloe vera and other rosette succulents

Aloe vera forms numerous buds at the base that fill the pot over time.

1. Unpot the plant and carefully remove the soil.
2. Feel the kindle with its own roots and cut it off at the connection point with a clean knife.
3. Allow the interfaces to dry for one to two days.
4. Place in small pots with dry succulent soil.
5. Water lightly for the first time after about a week.

Echeverias, haworthias and many cacti are propagated in the same way. More about caring for this group in [Caring for Cacti and Succulents](/ratgeber/kakteen-und-sukkulenten-pflege/).

## Bromeliads

Bromeliads like Guzmania, Vriesea or Aechmea only bloom once. After flowering, the mother rosette slowly dies back, but first forms one or more children at the base.

1. Continue to care for the mother plant after flowering until the children are about a third to half their size.
2. Use a sharp knife to cut off the seeds as close to the mother plant as possible, possibly with a small piece of tissue.
3. Place in a loose substrate made from orchid soil and some potting soil.
4. Place bright, warm and with high humidity. For many bromeliads, a little water is added to the funnel.

Alternatively, you can carefully cut off the mother plant after it dies and let the children continue to grow in the pot.

## Orchid keiki

Sometimes a small plant grows at the nodes of a Phalaenopsis panicle instead of a flower. Leave the keiki on the mother plant until it has three to four leaves and several roots measuring three to five centimeters. Then it is cut off with a small piece of panicle and placed in a small, transparent pot with fine orchid substrate. More in [Care for orchids](/ratgeber/orchideen-phalaenopsis-pflege/).

## Pilea (UFO plant)

The Pilea peperomioides produces numerous offshoots directly from the soil, often a few centimeters from the trunk, and sometimes on the trunk itself.

1. Cut off cuttings that grow from the soil with a knife below the surface of the soil, if possible with some roots.
2. Place in a small pot with loose soil or first root in water.
3. Carefully cut off offshoots from the trunk and root them in water.

Pilea cuttings are a popular exchange item among plant lovers, which is why the plant is also called the “friendship plant”.

## Lowering for climbing plants

Lowerers work on many climbing and hanging plants:

1. Place a small pot of soil next to the mother plant.
2. Place a long shoot on top so that one or two nodes touch the soil.
3. Fix the shoot at the nodes with a hairpin or a bent wire.
4. Keep the soil in the small pot moist.
5. After three to six weeks, when the shoot has rooted in the new pot, cut the connection to the mother plant.

This method is very safe because the young plant is cared for until it is separated from the mother plant. It is suitable for ivy, philodendron, hoya and ivy. Alternatively: [Cuttings in a water glass](/ratgeber/stecklinge-im-wasserglas/).

## Care of young plants

| factor | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| pot | Small, 6 to 10 cm, with a trigger hole |
| Substrate | Loose and suitable for the species, mineral for succulents |
| Light | Bright, without direct midday sun |
| Temperature | 20 to 25°C |
| water | Evenly slightly moist, reserved for succulents |
| fertilizer | Only after four to six weeks, at half the dosage |
| Humidity | Increased for tropical species, possibly under a hood |

[Potting soil and substrate](/ratgeber/blumenerde-und-substrat/) helps when choosing the substrate.

## Typical mistakes

1. **Separated too early:** Children without their own roots dry out or rot.
2. **Water succulents immediately:** Fresh cuts will rot. Let it dry first.
3. **Too big pot:** Lots of wet soil around small roots.
4. **Bright sun:** Young plants burn quickly.
5. **Too cold:** Many young plants hardly grow below 18 °C.

## Practical example: The Aloe family

After three years, an aloe vera had filled its pot with eight children. She was potted out in April. Five larger children with well-developed roots were separated, dried for two days and placed in clay pots with cactus soil and pumice. Three very small children without roots remained with the mother plant, which was placed in a slightly larger pot. After a week, watering was carried out carefully for the first time. All five young plants grew and formed new leaves in the summer.

## Give away and exchange offshoots

Offshoots are the classic currency among plant lovers. In order for the young plant to be well received by the new owner, it should already have its own roots and have been in its own pot for at least two to three weeks. To transport it, place the pot in a bag or box to protect the soil and leaves and avoid long journeys through the cold in winter. A small piece of paper with the name of the plant and the most important care instructions, such as “bright, but without midday sun” or “only water when the soil is dry at the top,” will help the recipient more than any long instructions. At plant exchanges, neighborhood groups, and swap days in community gardens, you can also find people who will offer you varieties that are rare in stores in return.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will it harm the mother plant if I leave Kindel?

No. The plant will only become denser and fill the pot faster. At some point things will get tight and it's time to divide or repot. See [Dividing Plants](/ratgeber/pflanzen-teilen/).

### Can I save Kindel without roots?

With succulents and cacti, often yes: let them dry and place them on dry substrate; they will form roots over time. It's more difficult with bromeliads and orchids.

### Why doesn't my spider plant form runners?

Young plants or plants that are too dark rarely form runners. Older plants in somewhat narrow pots in bright places are particularly productive.

### How long does it take for a young plant to form its own children?

That depends on the species. Spider plants often form their own runners in the first or second year if they are bright and the pot is well rooted. Aloe and many other succulents usually take several years until the first children appear at the base. Bromeliads usually only form after the mother plant has flowered.

### Can I also remove offshoots in winter?

This is possible in a warm apartment. Because of the lower light, roots form more slowly in winter, and young plants without their own roots rot more easily. If you have the choice, wait until spring to separate or leave the offshoot on the mother plant until then.

## Conclusion

Offshoots and seedlings are the easiest way to propagate houseplants. Wait until the young plants are large enough and have their own roots, separate them with clean tools, let the interfaces of succulents dry and place them in small pots with suitable substrate. With light, warmth and patience they grow into strong new plants.
