Sun-ripened tomatoes from your own balcony taste incomparably better than those from the supermarket. And tomatoes are one of the vegetables that are best grown in a container. With the right variety, a large container, a sunny, rain-protected place and regular care, a rich harvest is possible even in a small space.

This guide explains which varieties are suitable for the balcony, how to plant and support tomatoes, how rooting, watering and fertilizing work and how to prevent the most common problems.

## The right location

Tomatoes need **lots of sun and warmth.**

- **Ideal:** South, southwest or west balcony with at least six to eight hours of direct sun.
- **Eastern balcony:** Possible, but the harvest will be smaller. Prefer small-fruited varieties.
- **North balcony:** Not suitable.
- **Rain protection:** A covered balcony is ideal. Wet leaves are the main cause of the dreaded late blight.
- **Windbreak:** Strong winds bend plants and dry them out.

## Varieties for the balcony

| Variety type | Features | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Stick tomatoes | Growing on a main shoot to a height of 1.5 to 2 m must be pinched out | Lots of cocktail, salad and beef tomatoes |
| Bush tomatoes | Compact, 40 to 80 cm, branched, not cultivated | For small balconies and boxes |
| Ampel tomatoes | Hanging, for hanging baskets and balcony boxes | Small cherry tomatoes |
| Cocktail and cherry tomatoes | Small, very productive, ripens early, less prone to bursting | Ideal for beginners |
| beefsteak tomatoes | Large fruits, long ripening time | Only for very sunny, warm balconies |
| Brown rot tolerant varieties | Less susceptible to late blight and brown rot | Good for balconies without rain protection |

For beginners, **cocktail and bush tomatoes** are the best choice. They bear early, are productive and forgiving of mistakes.

## bucket and soil

- **Bucket size:** At least **20 to 30 liters** per plant for stake tomatoes, 10 to 15 liters for bush tomatoes. Larger containers mean more water and nutrient reserves and less stress.
- **Drain holes** and a drainage layer made of expanded clay.
- **Water storage buckets** make supplying supplies much easier in midsummer.
- **Soil:** High-quality tomato or vegetable soil, nutrient-rich and structurally stable.
- **No space?** Tomato bags or plant bags with soil also work.

## Cultivation or young plants

Tomatoes can be grown on the windowsill from mid-March. See [Sowing on the windowsill](/ratgeber/aussaat-auf-der-fensterbank/). Alternatively, strong young plants are available in garden centers from April. Good young plants are compact, dark green, have a strong stem and no spots.

## Plants

Tomatoes only come outside permanently **after the Ice Saints, in mid-May**, if the nights remain above 10 °C. Harden off for a week beforehand.

1. Fill the bucket with drainage and soil.
2. **Plant Deep:** Tomatoes form additional roots on the stem. Therefore plant the plant deeper than in the growing pot, just below the lowest leaves. Remove the lower leaves first.
3. **Place the support directly:** Insert a tomato stick or spiral when planting to avoid damaging the roots later.
4. **Pour on.**

## Supports

Stake tomatoes need a support 1.5 to 2 meters high:

- **Spiral rods** made of metal: The shoot is simply twisted into the spiral.
- **Wooden or bamboo sticks:** The shoot is tied loosely every 20 to 30 cm with soft ribbon.
- **Strings from above:** On covered balconies, a string can be attached to the ceiling and wrapped around the shoot.

When tying, always leave room for the stem to grow in thickness.

## Maxed out

In stick tomatoes, new side shoots, the so-called **stingy shoots, are constantly growing in the leaf axils, between the main shoot and the leaf stalk. If they are not removed, a dense bush will result with many small, late-ripening fruits and poor ventilation.

1. Check the leaf axils once a week.
2. Pluck young shoots from the sides with your fingers while they are still small.
3. Cut off larger shoots with clean scissors.
4. Don't be confused: Inflorescences do not grow from the leaf axils, but between the leaves on the stem.

**Bush tomatoes** and hanging basket tomatoes are not or only slightly exploited.

Spent shoots can be rooted in water and produce new plants.

## Pouring

Tomatoes need a lot of water and, above all, **evenly.**

- **Water in the morning**, directly at the base, **never over the leaves.**
- **Pervasive:** Daily in mid-summer, perhaps twice for large plants in small pots.
- **Evenness:** Significant fluctuations between dry and wet result in split fruit and blossom end rot.
- **Mulch:** A layer of straw, grass clippings or coconut fiber on top of the soil reduces evaporation.

More tools in [Balcony Irrigation](/ratgeber/balkon-bewaesserung/).

## Fertilize

Tomatoes are **heavy feeders.** Start about two to three weeks after planting and then fertilize weekly with tomato fertilizer. Tomato fertilizer contains more potassium, which is important for fruit formation and flavor. Organic fertilizers such as horn shavings incorporated during planting ensure a basic supply.

## Flowers and pollination

Tomatoes are self-pollinators. On windless balconies, it helps to gently shake the plants or inflorescences every day during flowering so that the pollen falls. Bumblebees are excellent pollinators.

## Typical problems

| problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Brown spots on leaves and fruits, rapid death | Late blight caused by wet leaves | Rain protection, do not water over leaves, remove lower leaves, tolerant varieties |
| Black, sunken spot on the base of the fruit | Blossom end rot caused by calcium deficiency due to uneven watering | Water evenly, mulch |
| Burst fruits | Strong fluctuations in water supply | Water more evenly |
| Yellow lower leaves | Nutrient deficiency or natural aging | Fertilize, remove lower leaves |
| Flowers fall off | Heat over 30 °C, cold, lack of water | Shade in heat waves, water evenly |
| Green shoulders on the fruits | Strong sun, heat | Do not remove leaves too much |
| Aphids, whitefly | Pests | See [Fighting Aphids Naturally](/ratgeber/blattlaeuse-natuerlich-bekaempfen/) |

## Harvest

Tomatoes are ripe when they have the typical color of the variety and give slightly when pressed lightly. It's best to cut off the stem. Green tomatoes can be harvested in the fall before the first frost and ripen indoors, for example in a box with an apple. Green tomatoes contain solanine and should not be eaten raw.

From the end of August, the top of the stalk tomatoes is cut off so that the plant puts its energy into the existing fruits instead of forming new flowers that no longer ripen.

## Practical example: three buckets, twenty kilos

In mid-May, three tomato plants were planted on a covered south-west balcony: a cocktail tomato, a salad tomato and a brown rot-tolerant variety, each in a 30 liter water storage pot with tomato soil. The plants were planted deep, guided on spiral rods and trained weekly. The water reservoir was watered in the morning and fertilized weekly. The peaks were cut at the end of August. From July to October, residents harvested around twenty kilos of tomatoes, without brown rot.

| month | Tasks |

| April | Prick out, move on bright and cool, buy young plants |
| May | Harden off, plant deeply after the ice saints, provide support |
| June | Pinch out, water regularly, start fertilizing |
| July to August | Harvest begins, thin out and fertilize weekly, remove lower leaves |
| End of August | Cut shoot tips |
| September to October | Last harvest, allow green fruits to ripen, dispose of plants |

## Prevent late blight and brown blight

The most feared disease of tomatoes is late blight. It is caused by a pathogen that spreads quickly in wet weather and appears as brown, rapidly enlarging spots on leaves, stems and later on the fruit. On the balcony you have an advantage over the garden bed: under an eaves or a small tomato canopy, the leaves stay dry when it rains, and this significantly reduces the risk.

Always water directly onto the soil and not over the leaves, remove the lowest leaves once the plant is tall enough to avoid soil splashing on them, and leave room for air between plants. Cut off affected leaves immediately and dispose of them in the trash. If you have problems every year, choose varieties that are described as resistant to late blight and brown rot.

## Obtain your own seeds

You can harvest your own seeds from seed-resistant varieties. Choose a beautiful, fully ripe fruit from a healthy plant, scrape the seeds and jelly into a glass with a little water and let it sit for two to three days until a light layer forms. This fermentation dissolves the germination-inhibiting coating. Then rinse the seeds clean in a sieve and dry them on a plate or coffee filter. Stored dry, cool and dark, tomato seeds remain viable for several years. This is not worthwhile with F1 hybrids because the offspring are very different.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I plant tomatoes in window boxes?

Only bush tomatoes or hanging basket tomatoes. Stake tomatoes need significantly more soil.

### Do I need to remove tomato leaves?

The lower leaves that touch the ground or turn yellow should be removed. Leave the rest as it protects the fruit from sunburn.

### Can I overwinter tomatoes?

Tomatoes are actually perennial, but are usually cultivated as annuals. Overwintering is complex. It's easier to put on new clothes in the spring.

### Why do my tomatoes stay green and hard at the base of the stem?

Green or yellow, hard spots around the base of the stem, so-called green collars, often occur when the fruit is exposed to extreme heat and strong sunlight. Potassium deficiency can also play a role. Leave enough foliage to provide shade and use a tomato fertilizer with sufficient potassium.

### Are tomato leaves poisonous?

The green parts of the plant contain bitter substances that are not suitable for consumption, and unripe green fruits also contain more of them than ripe ones. The ripe fruits, on the other hand, are harmless. Discourage small children and pets from nibbling on leaves and shoots.

## Conclusion

Tomatoes on the balcony need sun, a large container, nutrient-rich soil, a stable support and a place where the leaves stay dry. Plant them deep, plant tomatoes weekly, water evenly at the base in the morning and fertilize weekly. Then you harvest aromatic fruits from July to autumn.
