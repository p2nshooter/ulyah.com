Mint is one of the most rewarding herbs of all. It grows quickly, tolerates partial shade, comes back every year and provides leaves for tea, lemonade, salads, desserts and cocktails. At the same time, it is notorious for its urge to grow: in the bed, it quickly overgrows everything in the neighborhood. In the pot, however, it can be kept well under control and is ideal for balconies and windowsills.

This guide introduces popular mint varieties, explains location, pot and care, shows how to harvest and prune correctly, and gives tips on propagation, overwintering and preservation.

## Popular varieties

There are dozens of types and varieties of mint with very different flavors.

| variety | aroma | Usage |
|---|---|---|
| Peppermint (Mentha × piperita) | Strong, mentholated, cooling | Tea, sweets |
| Moroccan mint (Mentha spicata “Morocco”) | Mild, sweet, little menthol | Oriental-style peppermint tea, lemonade |
| Spearmint (Spearmint, Mentha spicata) | Fresh, mild | Mojito, salads, sauces |
| Strawberry mint | Fruity, mild | Desserts, punch |
| Chocolate mint | Strong with a chocolate note | Desserts, tea |
| Apple mint (Mentha suaveolens) | Mild, fruity, hairy leaves | Tea, salads |
| Lemon mint | Fresh and lemony | tea, fish |
| Banana mint | Fruity, unusual | Desserts, smoothies |
| Polei mint | Very intense | Only sparingly, poisonous in large quantities, not during pregnancy |

If you keep several varieties, you should plant them in separate pots because mints cross with each other and the flavors can mix when sown. They can only be propagated true to variety through cuttings and division.

## Location

| factor | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Light | Sunny to partially shaded; The leaves remain more tender in partial shade |
| Temperature | Robust, hardy |
| Wind | Tolerates wind well, but then dries out more quickly |
| Inside | Bright window, east or west |

Mint is one of the few herbs that thrives on shady balconies. See [Plants for the Shade Balcony](/ratgeber/schattenbalkon-pflanzen/). In the blazing midday sun, however, it needs a lot of water.

## Pot instead of bed

Mint forms underground runners (rhizomes) with which it spreads in all directions. Therefore:

- **Always in its own pot**, not with other herbs. She would displace her neighbors.
- **Pot size:** At least 20 to 25 cm in diameter, preferably larger. Mint takes root quickly.
- **Wide instead of deep containers** because the runners grow flat.
- **In the garden bed** only with a root barrier or in a buried bottomless bucket.

## Earth

Mint likes humus-rich, nutrient-rich soil that stores water well. Herb or vegetable soil, possibly with a little compost, is well suited. Unlike thyme or rosemary, mint does not need poor, sandy soil.

## Pouring

- **Evenly moist:** Mint tolerates drought poorly. The earth should never dry out completely.
- **Often daily in summer**, especially in small pots and in the sun.
- **No waterlogging:** Exhaust hole and drainage are still important.
- **Water in the morning**, if possible not over the leaves to avoid fungal diseases.

## Fertilize

In the spring, incorporate some compost or organic slow-release fertilizer and top-dress with organic liquid fertilizer every three to four weeks from May to August. Too much nitrogen makes the leaves soft and less flavorful.

## Harvest correctly

- **Harvesting shoot tips:** Cut off entire shoots or shoot tips above a pair of leaves. New shoots grow from the leaf axils and the mint becomes bushier.
- **Harvest regularly**, even if you don't need everything. Unharvested mint becomes long and woody.
- **Best Time:** Late morning on a dry day. The aroma is strongest just before flowering.
- **Larger quantities:** For tea or drying, the entire plant can be cut to about a hand's width above the ground. It sprouts again within a few weeks.

## Flowering and pruning

In summer, mint produces spikes of purple, white or pink flowers. They are very popular with bees, hoverflies and butterflies. See [Bee-Friendly Balcony](/ratgeber/bienenfreundlicher-balkon/). After flowering, the leaves often become a little tarter. A compromise: let some of the shoots bloom and harvest some regularly for use in the kitchen.

**Pruning:** After flowering or in late summer, cut the plant back vigorously to a hand's width. It sprouts fresh and produces new, tender leaves until autumn. In spring, cut off last year's dead stems close to the ground.

## Repotting and dividing

Mint roots quickly through its pot. It then ages in the middle of the pot and only grows at the edge. Every one to two years in spring:

1. Unpot the plant.
2. Divide the root ball into several pieces with a knife or spade.
3. Place a strong piece of fresh soil back in the pot.
4. Give the remaining pieces away or plant them in other pots.

See [Dividing Plants](/ratgeber/pflanzen-teilen/).

## Propagation

- **Cuttings:** Cut off shoot tips 10 cm long, remove the lower leaves and place in a glass of water. After a week, roots will form. See [Cuttings in a Water Jar](/ratgeber/stecklinge-im-wasserglas/).
- **Runners:** Separate a piece of runners with roots and shoots and pot them up.
- **Division:** As described above.
- **Sowing:** Possible, but the offspring of many varieties are not true to the variety.

## Overwinter

Most mints are **hardy.** They grow above ground in autumn and sprout again in spring.

- **In the pot outside:** Place the pot on a protected wall of the house, wrap it with fleece or jute and place it on a wooden plate. Water lightly on frost-free days to prevent the ball from drying out.
- **Harvest indoors in winter:** A section can be placed in a pot in the fall and placed in a bright, cool window. It provides some fresh leaves in winter.

## Typical problems

| problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Orange-brown pustules on undersides of leaves | Mint rust (mushroom) | Cut off affected shoots close to the ground, dispose of them, and keep the plant dry |
| White coating on leaves | Powdery mildew | Remove affected parts and place in a more airy place |
| Leaves small and woody stems | Aging, no harvest | Divide, cut back |
| Holes in leaves | Mint leaf beetle, caterpillars | Collect |
| Aphids | Soft shoots | Rinse off, see [Fight aphids naturally](/ratgeber/blattlaeuse-natuerlich-bekaempfen/) |
| Wilted leaves in the midday sun | Lack of water, heat | Water more, place in partial shade |

## Preserving mint

- **Drying:** Harvest whole shoots shortly before flowering, bundle them into small bouquets and hang upside down in an airy, shady place. After drying, peel off the leaves and store in airtight jars.
- **Freezing:** Finely chop the leaves and freeze with water in ice cube trays, ideal for drinks.
- **Syrup:** Bring mint to the boil with sugar and water, let it steep, strain and bottle.

## Practical example: The mint for peppermint tea

A Moroccan mint was planted in a 30 cm pot on an east-facing balcony. It grew so vigorously that a large bunch could be harvested every week for fresh tea. In July she showed rust pustules. The plant was completely cut back to ten centimeters, the cuttings were disposed of in the household waste and in the future only watered at the base. Within three weeks she sprouted again healthy. The following spring the bale was divided, one piece remained in the pot and two went to neighbors.

## Recognize and treat mint rust

Mint rust is a fungal disease that first appears as small, orange-brown pustules on the undersides of leaves. Later, the leaves become discolored, mottled and fall off, and the shoots may twist. The spores survive in the soil and on old plant parts, which is why the disease often comes back every year.

Cut off infected shoots just above the ground and dispose of them in household waste, not in the compost. The mint then usually sprouts again healthy. An airy location, watering without wet leaves and regular harvesting help preventatively. If the rust still occurs every year, plant a healthy piece of mint in fresh soil and a clean pot.

## Mint in the kitchen

Mint is more than a tea herb. Fresh leaves go well with yogurt dips, tabbouleh and couscous, with peas, beans and lamb, with fruit salad, melon and chocolate and of course in lemonades and iced teas. To keep the flavor fresh, add the leaves just before serving and pick them instead of chopping them finely, as bruised leaves quickly darken.

The most aromatic shoots are those that are harvested shortly before flowering. To make tea, you can pour hot water over entire branches. Peppermint essential oil, on the other hand, is highly concentrated and should not be used near the face or nose of infants and small children.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can mint be put in a box with other herbs?

Only with a root barrier or in its own inner pot. Otherwise it will crowd out the other herbs.

### Is mint poisonous to cats?

Mint is usually not a problem for cats in small quantities, but essential oils in large quantities can cause problems. See [Plant Safety](/pflanzensicherheit/).

### Why does my mint taste like nothing?

There is often a lack of sun or the plant receives too much fertilizer. The variety also makes a big difference.

### Can I grow mint in a glass of water?

Yes, mint shoots usually root in a glass of water within one to two weeks. For a long-lasting, strong plant, place the rooted shoots in a pot with soil, because the mint will become weak in the long term in water alone.

### Why does my mint grow worse after a few years?

After two to three years in the same pot, the middle becomes woody, the soil is exhausted and the roots fill every nook and cranny. Then divide the plant in spring, place strong edge pieces in fresh soil and discard the woody center.

### Can I harvest mint in winter?

Mint grows outdoors in winter and sprouts again in spring. A pot on a bright, cool windowsill, on the other hand, produces small harvests even in winter.

## Conclusion

Mint is robust, fast-growing and versatile. Plant it in its own large pot with humus-rich soil, keep it evenly moist, regularly harvest entire shoot tips, cut it back heavily after flowering and divide it every one to two years. This means you have fresh leaves for tea and cooking every year. More herbs: [Herb garden on the windowsill](/ratgeber/kraeutergarten-auf-der-fensterbank/).
