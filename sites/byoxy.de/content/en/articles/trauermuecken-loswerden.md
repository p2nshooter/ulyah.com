Small black mosquitoes that emerge from the potting soil when watering and buzz around the plants: Fungus gnats are the most common pest on houseplants. The adults are harmless but annoying. Their larvae, which live in the soil and can eat fine roots and seedlings in severe infestations, are problematic. The good news: With the right approach, you can get rid of them in just a few weeks.

This guide explains where fungus gnats come from, what their life cycle looks like, which control methods really work and how you can prevent a new infestation.

## Recognize fungus gnats

| feature | fungus gnat |
|---|---|
| size | 2 to 4 millimeters |
| color | Black to dark gray, dark wings |
| behavior | Flies poorly, walks a lot on the ground, rises when watering |
| larvae | Whitish-glassy with a black head, up to 7 mm long, in the upper layers of the soil |
| Favorite place | Moist, humus-rich soil |

Fungus gnats are sometimes confused with fruit flies. However, fruit flies are brownish with red eyes and tend to sit on the fruit basket rather than on the potting soil.

## Where do fungus gnats come from?

- **Introduced with soil:** Open soil bags, especially if stored outdoors or in a warm basement, often contain eggs or larvae.
- **With new plants:** Nursery plants sometimes bring larvae with them.
- **Through open windows:** In summer they fly in from outside.

But what matters is why they stay: **permanently moist soil.** Fungus gnats lay their eggs in moist, organic substrate. There the larvae find fungi and decaying plant remains as food. They avoid dry earth surfaces.

## The life cycle

| stage | Duration at room temperature |
|---|---|
| Eggs in the moist soil surface | About 4 to 6 days |
| larvae in the soil | About 10 to 14 days |
| Dolls in the ground | About 4 to 6 days |
| Adult mosquitoes | Live about a week and lay up to 200 eggs |

So a cycle lasts around three to four weeks. That's why it's not enough to just catch the adults. The control must last **at least four weeks** and capture both mosquitoes and larvae.

## Damage

The adult mosquitoes do not harm plants. The larvae primarily eat dead plant material and fungi. However, if the infestation is very severe, they eat fine roots. Those particularly at risk are:

- Seedlings and sprouts
- Fresh cuttings
- Young plants with few roots
- Plants that are already weakened

Large, healthy houseplants can usually cope with a mild infestation without any visible damage.

## Combat: the four-pillar plan

A combination of several measures is most effective.

### 1. Allow the soil to dry out

The most important measure. Allow the top two to three inches of soil to dry completely between waterings. Eggs and young larvae dry up and the females find no place to lay their eggs.

- **Water from below:** Place the pot in a bowl of water and let it soak from below. The surface remains drier.
- **Check planters:** No standing water.
- **Water less** if the plant can tolerate it. More about this in [Water houseplants properly](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-richtig-giessen/).

### 2. Yellow boards

Yellow sticky boards attract adult mosquitoes. They are placed in the pots, the bottom just above the soil. This way the females are caught before they lay eggs.

- One small board per pot or group of plants.
- Renew every two to three weeks or when full.
- Yellow panels also show whether the infestation is decreasing.

Yellow panels alone do not solve the problem because the larvae continue to live in the soil. In combination with drying they are very effective.

### 3. Nematodes

Nematodes of the type Steinernema feltiae are microscopic roundworms that specifically attack fungus gnat larvae. They are completely harmless to people, pets and plants and are sold in specialist stores or online as powder.

1. Dissolve the powder in room temperature water according to the instructions on the package.
2. Pour the solution into the watering can and water all affected pots with it.
3. The soil must remain **slightly moist** for the following week or two to allow the nematodes to move.
4. The earth's temperature should be above 12°C.

Nematodes are the most effective biological method, especially in severe infestations. After two to three weeks the larvae are significantly reduced. A second application after three to four weeks may make sense. Note: Do not allow the soil to dry out significantly during nematode treatment. Therefore, combine drying and nematodes one after the other, not at the same time.

### 4. Sand or granulate layer

A one to two centimeter thick layer of quartz sand, fine gravel, pumice or lava granules on the surface of the earth makes the surface dry and unattractive for laying eggs. It is a good prevention and supports the fight. Disadvantage: The finger test becomes more difficult, a wooden stick helps.

## Other methods

| Method | Effect | Rating |
|---|---|---|
| Repot in fresh soil | Removes many larvae at once | If there is a severe infestation, it makes sense to dispose of old soil with household waste |
| Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis (BTI) | Biological agent against mosquito larvae, added to irrigation water | Effective, commercially available |
| Predatory mites (Hypoaspis) | Eat larvae and pupae in the soil | Effective, especially on many plants |
| Carnivorous plants (oilwort) | Catch mosquitoes with sticky leaves | Nice extra, not a sole solution |
| Stick matches into the ground | Sulfur is said to kill larvae | Effect unreliable |
| Cinnamon or baking powder on earth | Is intended to inhibit fungi and larvae | Effect not proven |
| Garlic in the ground | Should be a deterrent | Not very effective, smells |
| coffee grounds | Frequently recommended | More likely to encourage fungi and mosquitoes |

## A four-week plan

| week | Measures |
|---|---|
| 1 | Put in yellow boards, let the soil dry out, water from below, and possibly repot heavily infested pots |
| 2 | Apply nematodes, keep the soil slightly moist, check yellow plates |
| 3 | Continue to observe, renew yellow plates, prepare a second dose of nematodes if necessary |
| 4 | Apply a layer of sand, maintain the watering rhythm with drying, possibly a second nematode application |

After about four to six weeks, there should hardly be any mosquitoes landing on the yellow boards.

## Prevention

- **Store soil correctly:** Close bags tightly and store in a cool, dry place. Use up opened soil as soon as possible.
- **Check new plants:** Check soil for larvae, stick yellow board for a week.
- **Do not overwater:** Allow the soil to dry out occasionally.
- **Remove organic residues:** Remove fallen leaves from the soil.
- **Mineral substrates:** For susceptible plants, use mixtures with pumice or lava. See [Potting soil and substrate](/ratgeber/blumenerde-und-substrat/).
- **Hydroculture:** Plants in expanded clay or mineral substrate are hardly affected.

## Practical example: mosquitoes after repotting

In the spring, five houseplants were repotted in fresh soil from a bag that had been in the garage over the winter. Two weeks later, dozens of fungus gnats were buzzing through the apartment. The owner put yellow plates in all the pots, let the soil dry out more and a week later watered all the pots with nematodes. After another three weeks, a layer of pumice was added to the earth. After six weeks, no new mosquitoes were stuck to the boards. She disposed of the rest of the dirt from the garage.

## Fungus gnats in herb pots and growing trays

Fungus gnats particularly like to reproduce where it is moist and rich in humus: in seed trays, in freshly purchased herb pots and in pots with cuttings. The larvae are more dangerous for seedlings and young cuttings than for adult plants because they eat the fine, young roots. For sowing, use fresh, nutrient-poor potting soil, water from below and cover the surface with a thin layer of sand.

To combat the larvae, there are also preparations containing the bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis, which are applied with the irrigation water. They have a targeted effect against mosquito and fungus gnat larvae and are harmless to people and pets. As with nematodes, patience is necessary because adult mosquitoes live for a few more days.

## Mineral substrates as a way out

Fungus gnats need organic material to lay their eggs and feed their larvae. They hardly find any food in purely mineral substrates such as expanded clay, pumice or lava. Plants grown in hydroculture or semi-hydroculture are therefore practically free of fungus gnats, and anyone who keeps having problems can switch particularly susceptible plants to such substrates. Also keep opened bags of potting soil tightly closed, as open bags are ideal breeding grounds.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are fungus gnats dangerous to people or pets?

No. They do not sting or transmit diseases. They're just annoying.

### Why do I have more fungus gnats in winter?

In winter, plants use less water, the soil stays moist longer, and this provides ideal conditions. Watering less helps.

### Can fungus gnats also attack cacti?

Rare because their soil is usually dry. However, if there is too much water, they can also appear there.

### Do carnivorous plants help against fungus gnats?

Butterworts and sundews actually catch many adult fungus gnats on their sticky leaves. However, they do not help against the larvae in the soil. They are therefore a nice addition, but do not replace letting the soil dry out and the other measures.

### How long does it take for the fungus gnats to disappear?

A development cycle from egg to mosquito takes a few weeks at room temperature. Therefore, plan at least four weeks of consistent measures, even if there are fewer mosquitoes flying after the first few days. The infestation is only over when no new mosquitoes stick to the yellow boards for several weeks.

### Can I hang yellow boards in the kitchen?

Yes, yellow signs are harmless in the home. Outdoors, however, they also catch useful insects and should only be used specifically and briefly. Replace the boards when they are full or dusty.

### Do matches in the ground help?

The home remedy of sticking matches upside down in the ground is widespread, but its effectiveness has not been proven. Allowing to dry, yellow plates and nematodes are more reliable.

## Conclusion

Fungus gnats are a sign of permanently moist soil. Allow the soil surface to dry, catch the adult mosquitoes with yellow panels, control the larvae with nematodes or BTI and protect the soil with a layer of sand. With patience for at least four weeks and an adjusted watering behavior, you can get rid of the mosquitoes permanently. If there is white deposits on the soil, [mold on potting soil](/ratgeber/schimmel-auf-blumenerde/) also helps.
