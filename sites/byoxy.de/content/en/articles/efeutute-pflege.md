The ivy plant, botanically Epipremnum aureum, is one of the most frugal and grateful house plants. Their heart-shaped, often yellow or white patterned leaves hang in long tendrils from shelves and cupboards or climb up a moss stick. It forgives mistakes, grows quickly and is very easy to propagate. Ideal for beginners, but also for anyone who loves green curtains in their home.

This guide explains location, watering, soil and fertilizer, how to avoid bare tendrils, how to propagate the ivy and what problems can arise.

## Profile

| feature | Ivy plant |
|---|---|
| Origin | Tropical forests of Southeast Asia and the Pacific |
| growth | Climbing, hanging or climbing |
| Light | Bright to partially shaded, no direct sun |
| Temperature | 18 to 25°C, not below 15°C |
| Pouring | When the top 2 to 3 cm of soil is dry |
| Difficulty | Very light |
| Toxicity | Toxic to pets and small children (irritation) |

## Location

- **Bright without direct midday sun:** Here the tendrils grow densely and the pattern on the leaves remains strong.
- **Partial shade:** The ivy also grows in shady corners, the leaves then become greener and the spaces between the leaves become larger.
- **Dark Locations:** The plant survives but forms long, bare tendrils with small leaves.

Strongly patterned varieties with a lot of white or yellow need more light than purely green varieties because the light leaf parts hardly carry out any photosynthesis.

## Pouring

1. **Finger test:** Water when the top 2 to 3 cm of soil is dry.
2. **Pour thoroughly,** Pour off excess after 15 minutes.
3. **Hanging leaves as a hint:** If the ivy leaves its leaves hanging limply, it needs water. After watering, they will straighten up again within a few hours. Often it shouldn't get that far, but it's useful as a signal.
4. **Avoid waterlogging:** Permanently wet soil leads to yellow leaves and root rot.

See [Watering houseplants properly](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-richtig-giessen/).

## Soil, pot and fertilizer

- **Soil:** Normal houseplant soil, loosened with a little perlite or pine bark.
- **Pot:** With drain hole; Traffic light pots with water storage are practical, but check regularly that there is no standing water.
- **Fertilizer:** From March to September, green plant fertilizer every two to four weeks according to the package instructions. Do not fertilize in winter.

## Hanging or climbing?

### As an ampel plant

The tendrils hang freely and can be several meters long. They can also be guided along hooks over shelves or windows.

### On the moss stick

In nature, the ivy climbs up trees and its leaves become larger and larger. When pulled on a moss stick, it also forms significantly larger leaves indoors. Attach the tendrils loosely to the stick and guide the aerial roots towards the stick.

## Bushy growth: cutting is worth it

After a while, many ivy plants look bare at the top, while long tendrils hang at the bottom. Remedy:

1. **Cut back tendrils:** Cut off long shoots directly above a node (the thickening where the leaf and aerial root are located). New tendrils sprout from the knot.
2. **Put the cuttings back into the pot:** Root the cut pieces and then plant them in the same pot. This will make the plant fuller at the top.
3. **Lay tendrils over the soil:** Place long shoots in a circle over the potting soil and secure them with small hooks. Roots form at the nodes.

## Multiply

The ivy is one of the easiest plants to propagate:

1. Cut off the shoot with at least one, preferably two to three nodes.
2. Remove the lower leaves so that no leaves stand in water.
3. Place in a glass of water, bright but without sun.
4. Roots will appear after two to four weeks. Plant in soil when the roots are 3 to 5 cm long.

More about this in [Cuttings in a water glass](/ratgeber/stecklinge-im-wasserglas/).

## Typical problems

| Symptom | Possible cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow leaves, especially at the bottom | Too much water or natural aging | Check watering rhythm |
| Brown, dry leaf edges | Air too dry, lack of water | Water more regularly, increase humidity |
| Leaves lose pattern | Too little light | Make it brighter |
| Long bare tendrils | Too little light, no pruning | Lighten up, cut |
| Black, mushy stems | Rotting due to waterlogging | Save healthy parts as cuttings |

## Practical example: From a bare shelf dweller to a dense plant

An ivy plant on a high shelf had three tendrils over two meters long, but hardly any leaves at the top of the pot. The owner cut all the vines back to around 40 cm, placed ten cuttings with two nodes each in the water and planted them back in the pot after four weeks. After a summer in a brighter spot, the plant was dense, bushy and had new, even tendrils.

## The ivy during the course of the year

| time | What to do |
|---|---|
| March to May | Repot if roots fill the pot; shorten long tendrils; start fertilizing |
| June to August | Water regularly, fertilize every two to four weeks, protect from blazing midday sun, and cut cuttings |
| September to October | Allow the fertilizer to expire, extend the watering intervals, and move the plant closer to the window again |
| November to February | Choose the brightest spot, water sparingly, keep a distance from the heater, and wipe the leaves occasionally |

In winter the ivy hardly grows in most apartments. This is normal and not a sign of a care error. If you water them as often during this time as in summer, you risk wet soil and yellow leaves. A good rule of thumb is: the cooler and darker the location, the longer the soil can dry out between two waterings.

## The right pot size

Ivy plants are usually sold in small plastic pots measuring 12 or 14 centimeters. This is enough for the first one to two years. When repotting, choose a pot that is only two to three centimeters larger. A pot that is much too large contains a lot of soil that stays wet for a long time because there are no roots in it, and this promotes rot. Lightweight plastic or fiber pots with a drain hole that are placed in a decorative hanging basket are suitable for hanging baskets. This means you can remove the inner pot for watering, let it run through the sink and only put it back in when the water stops dripping.

## Ivy plant in hydroponics and semi-hydro

Because the ivy roots so easily in water, it is well suited for hydroponics with expanded clay or for so-called semi-hydro with mineral granules such as Pon or Seramis. The change is important: A plant that has been standing in soil for years should not simply be rinsed out and placed in expanded clay. Their roots die in the water and rot. It is better to root cuttings in water and keep them hydroponically from the start. The water level remains in the lower third of the container and fertilization is carried out with special hydro-fertilizer in a low concentration.

## Care for colorful varieties properly

Variegated varieties of ivy with white, cream or light green leaf markings need more light than the purely green form. Their light leaf parts contain little or no green leaf and contribute little to the plant's nutrition. In dark places they grow more slowly and new leaves often become greener because the plant produces more green leaves to compensate for the lack of light.

Therefore, place colorful varieties in a brighter area, but without the blazing midday sun, and cut shoots that only produce green leaves back to a leaf with good markings. White leaf parts turn brown easily if there is too much sun, and if they are too wet they get dark spots. Therefore, water colorful varieties a little more carefully than the robust green ivy.

## Small leaves, large leaves: juvenile and mature forms

In its homeland, the ivy climbs up tree trunks and forms large, sometimes slitted leaves. The potted plants on our shelves mostly show the juvenile form with small, heart-shaped leaves. Hanging shoots are even more likely to form smaller leaves because the plant does not invest in size as it grows down.

If the ivy is allowed to climb up a moss stick or a rough board and push its aerial roots into moist material, the leaves will become significantly larger over time. To do this, keep the moss slightly moist and tie new shoots loosely. The leaves in the apartment are rarely as big as in the rainforest, but the difference to the hanging plant is clearly visible.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the ivy plant poisonous?

Yes, it contains calcium oxalate and irritates the mouth and mucous membranes. Place out of reach of cats, dogs and small children. See [Plant Safety](/pflanzensicherheit/).

### Can ivy live in water?

Rooted cuttings can grow in water for a long time if the water is changed regularly and a little liquid fertilizer is added every now and then.

### Why does my ivy have small leaves?

Hanging vines produce smaller leaves than climbing ones. A moss stick and more light encourage larger leaves.

### Why is my ivy plant getting brown leaf tips?

Common causes are very dry heating air, irregular watering with long dry phases or too much fertilizer, the salts of which accumulate in the soil. Water more evenly, fertilize more sparingly and rinse the soil thoroughly every few months with plenty of water that drains well.

You can trim dried tips along the shape of the leaf with clean scissors without cutting into the green tissue; new leaves then grow intact again.

## Conclusion

The ivy is an ideal beginner plant: place it in a bright place without direct sun, water it when the top layer of soil is dry, fertilize during the growing season, cut back regularly and replant the cuttings. This means it stays dense and lively, whether as a hanging green curtain or as a climbing plant on a moss stick.
