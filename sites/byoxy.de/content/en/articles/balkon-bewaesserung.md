Plants have little soil available on the balcony, and sun, wind and heat dry out the boxes quickly. On hot summer days, a sunny balcony box can be bone dry within a few hours. At the same time, watering too frequently and a lack of drainage holes lead to waterlogging and root rot. Proper watering is therefore one of the most important factors for a healthy, blooming balcony.

This guide explains how much water balcony plants need, how to water correctly, which tools reduce the effort and how to set up automatic watering.

## What the water requirement depends on

| factor | High demand | Low demand |
|---|---|---|
| Alignment | South, West | North, East |
| Wind | High floors, open location | Protected location |
| Vessel size | Small pots and narrow boxes | Large buckets and deep boxes |
| material | Terracotta, dark vessels | Plastic, light-colored containers, water storage |
| Plants | Petunias, tomatoes, cucumbers, hydrangeas, bamboo | Succulents, lavender, rosemary, thyme, sedum |
| season | July, August | Spring, autumn |

A south-facing balcony with small terracotta pots and petunias often needs water twice a day in midsummer. A north-facing balcony with ferns in large plastic boxes, on the other hand, only every few days.

## Water correctly

### When?

- **Early in the morning** is the best time. The plants start the day well cared for, and leaves that get wet dry quickly.
- **Evening** is the second best time. Disadvantage: Wet leaves overnight promote fungal diseases, and damp soil attracts slugs.
- **Midday in the heat** is unfavorable. A lot of water evaporates immediately. Emergency watering of wilted plants is still better than nothing.

### How?

- **Directly on the ground**, not on leaves and flowers. Geraniums, petunias and tomatoes react to wet leaves with disease.
- **Pervasive:** Water until water comes out of the drainage holes. Frequent sipping only keeps the surface moist.
- **Finger test:** Before watering, check whether the top two to three centimeters are dry.
- **Coasters:** For pots, pour off any standing water after half an hour, except for heavy drinkers like hydrangeas on hot days.

### Which water?

Rainwater is ideal because it is soft and temperate. Tap water is fine for most balcony plants. Lime-sensitive plants such as hydrangeas, azaleas and blueberries prefer soft water. Ice-cold water straight from the tap can stress sensitive plants. If you leave a watering can overnight, you will have tempered water in the morning.

## Water storage boxes and buckets

Planters with an integrated water reservoir in the bottom are one of the best investments for balconies.

- **Function:** An intermediate floor separates the earth and the water reservoir. Wicks or pillars of earth pull the water upwards. A water level indicator shows the filling level.
- **Advantages:** Watering is only necessary every few days, plants are evenly supplied, no overflow.
- **Notes:** After planting, water from above for the first two to three weeks until the roots reach the reservoir. In case of heavy rain, there should be an overflow so that the soil does not become waterlogged.

Existing boxes can also be retrofitted with inserts.

## Clay cones and dropper bottles

### clay cones

Clay cones are placed in the ground and connected to a water container via a thin hose. If the soil becomes dry, it draws water through the porous clay. The system is largely self-regulating. Depending on the length, two to three cones are required per balcony box. Place the container slightly lower than the cones, otherwise too much water will flow.

### Bottle drip attachments

Attachments for PET bottles release water over a period of several hours to days. They are suitable for small pots and short periods of time, but are less reliable in dosage.

## Automatic drip irrigation

For larger balconies or for frequent absences, drip irrigation is the most convenient solution.

### Components

| part | Function |
|---|---|
| water source | Faucet on the balcony or a water tank |
| Watering computer or timer | Controls timing and duration |
| pump | Necessary for tanks without water pressure, often with a solar module |
| Pressure reducer and filter | Protects the droppers |
| distribution pipe | Main line along the boxes |
| Dripper or drip hose | Release the water directly to the plants |

### Setup step by step

1. **Plan:** Count the number of plants and containers and sketch out the routing.
2. **Choose a dripper:** Adjustable drippers allow you to allocate the right amount to each pot. Large buckets get several drippers.
3. **Install:** Lay the distribution pipe, place the dripper, attach the end plug.
4. **Test:** Test several days before an absence. Check whether all vessels receive enough water and not too much water.
5. **Settings:** In midsummer, typically 5 to 15 minutes in the morning, depending on the dripper performance, possibly a second short round in the evening.

### Solar powered systems

For balconies without a water or electricity connection, there are systems with a solar pump that pump water from a tank. Practical: When there is a lot of sun, more is pumped, although the demand is higher.

## Use rainwater

Rainwater can be collected on the balcony to a limited extent, for example with a small rain barrel under a downspout if the rental agreement and building allow it, or with bowls and buckets when it rains. Covered containers prevent mosquitoes from breeding in them.

## Save water

- **Large containers:** More soil stores more water.
- **Mulch:** A layer of bark mulch, coconut fiber, expanded clay or gravel on top of the soil reduces evaporation.
- **Water-retaining additives:** Clay minerals such as bentonite or coconut fibers in the soil increase the water-holding capacity.
- **Light-colored vessels:** Dark pots heat up significantly in the sun.
- **Choice of plants:** On hot south-facing balconies, prefer drought-tolerant plants such as lavender, thyme, sedum and purslane.
- **Wind protection:** A green privacy screen reduces drying out. See [Balcony privacy screen with plants](/ratgeber/balkon-sichtschutz-mit-pflanzen/).

## Signs of too much or too little water

| Signs | Too little water | Too much water |
|---|---|---|
| leaves | Limp, wilted, especially at midday | Yellow, soft, wilted despite wet soil |
| flowers | Fall off, dry up | Rotting, gray mold |
| Earth | Dust dry, peeling away from the edge | Constantly wet, smells musty |
| More | Spider mites in heat | Fungus gnats, snails, root rot |

Slight wilting in the midday heat can also occur in moist soil because the plant evaporates more than the roots supply. If she recovers in the evening, everything will be fine.

## Practical example: The west-facing balcony in the attic apartment

On a west-facing balcony under the roof in July, two full watering cans were distributed daily across six boxes and four buckets, but the petunias still wilted every afternoon. The solution consisted of three steps: the small boxes were replaced with water storage boxes, the soil was covered with a layer of expanded clay, and simple drip irrigation with a water computer on the tap supplied the tubs every morning at six o'clock. Water consumption decreased and plants bloomed all summer, even during a two-week vacation.

## Water by plant group

Not every balcony plant needs the same amount of water, and grouping your plants accordingly saves time and water. Pelargoniums tolerate short dry periods surprisingly well and react more sensitively to constant wetness than to a dry day. Petunias, surfinias, fuchsias and begonias, on the other hand, are thirsty and quickly lose their leaves in dry conditions; In midsummer they often need water twice a day on sunny balconies.

Above all, tomatoes and cucumbers want to be evenly moist. If drought and sudden watering alternate, tomato fruits burst more easily and blossom end rot occurs more frequently, in which the bottom of the fruit becomes brown and leathery because the plant does not transport enough calcium into the fruit if the supply is irregular. Mediterranean herbs such as rosemary, thyme and sage, on the other hand, are undemanding and tolerate it if the soil dries well between two waterings. Therefore, do not plant them in a box with thirsty bloomers.

## Water in spring, fall and winter

In the cooler months, much less water evaporates, and the most common damage is caused by too much moisture. Before each watering in spring and autumn, check with your finger whether the soil is dry a few centimeters deep. However, evergreen potted plants that overwinter outside occasionally die of thirst in winter: on frost-free, sunny days, their leaves continue to evaporate water while the ball is dry. Therefore, water them moderately on mild days.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much water does a balcony box need per day?

An 80cm box on a south-facing balcony can use two to four liters a day in midsummer. On shady balconies it is significantly less.

### Do I have to water when it rains?

Often yes. Balconies under a roof or balconies above hardly receive any rain. The finger test decides.

### Can I water plants with dishwater?

Water containing detergent or salt damages plants. However, cooled, unsalted vegetable cooking water is fine.

### What to do if the earth no longer absorbs water?

If a pot ball is completely dry, the irrigation water often runs past the side of the ball and out again at the bottom. Then place smaller pots in a bucket of water for ten to twenty minutes until no more air bubbles appear. You water large buckets in several small portions a few minutes apart so that the soil can slowly soak up again.

### Are coasters useful?

Coasters protect the balcony floor and the neighbors below from water runoff. However, empty it about half an hour after watering, because standing water in the saucer leads to waterlogging and root rot for most plants. Only on very hot summer days should a small residue be left on thirsty plants such as tomatoes or petunias, which the plant will absorb in a few hours.

### Water in the morning or evening?

It's best in the morning, because then the plants go into the heat of the day with full water reserves and the leaves dry quickly. Evening watering is fine if you water directly onto the soil and keep the leaves dry, as wet leaves overnight promotes fungal diseases.

## Conclusion

Correct watering determines success on the balcony. Water thoroughly and directly onto the soil in the morning, check with a finger sample beforehand, choose large containers with water storage and reduce evaporation with mulch.
