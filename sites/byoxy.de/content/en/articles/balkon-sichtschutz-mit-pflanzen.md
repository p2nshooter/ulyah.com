A balcony should be a place of retreat. But often the neighbors are only a few meters away, the street is visible or there is an office building opposite. Instead of plastic mats or fabric panels, a green privacy screen made from plants can provide privacy. It looks natural, filters wind and dust, cools in summer and provides a habitat for insects and birds.

This guide introduces the best plants for a green privacy screen, explains trellises and containers, provides information on statics and tenancy law and shows how to maintain the privacy screen.

## What should the privacy screen do?

Before you choose plants, clarify a few questions:

| Question | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Only in summer or all year round? | Annuals or deciduous plants vs. evergreens |
| How high? | Parapet height, seat height or up to the ceiling |
| Which location? | Sunny, partially shaded or shady |
| How much space? | Narrow boxes or large buckets |
| How fast? | Fast-growing annual plants or slow-growing trees |
| How much care? | Watering, cutting, wintering |

## Annual climbing plants: fast and flowering

Annual climbing plants grow two to four meters tall within a summer and form a dense, often flowering curtain. They die off in the fall. They are ideal for quickly creating privacy screens or bridging the time until perennial plants have grown.

| plant | Height | Location | Special feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Morning glory | 2 to 4 m | Sunny | Large, funnel-shaped flowers, seeds poisonous |
| Black-eyed Susan | 1.5 to 2 m | Sunny to partially shaded | Orange, yellow or white flowers with a dark center |
| Bell vine | 3 to 4 m | Sunny, protected | Large, purple bell flowers |
| Runner bean | 2 to 4 m | Sunny | Red flowers, edible beans (only cooked) |
| Nasturtium (climbing) | 1.5 to 3 m | Sunny | Edible flowers and leaves |
| sweet pea | 1.5 to 2 m | Sunny, cool | Fragrant flowers |
| Ornamental pumpkin and cucumber | 2 to 3 m | Sunny | Fruits as decoration or for eating |

Annuals are grown on the windowsill from April or bought as young plants and planted after the ice saints. See [Sowing on the windowsill](/ratgeber/aussaat-auf-der-fensterbank/).

## Perennial climbing plants

Perennial climbing plants take one to three years to become dense, but then remain dense for many years.

| plant | foliage | Location | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ivy | Evergreen | Partial to shady | Year-round privacy protection, late flowering for insects, poisonous |
| Clematis | Summer green | Sunny, roots shaded | Magnificent flowers, trellis needed |
| Honeysuckle | Partly evergreen | Sunny to partially shaded | Fragrant flowers |
| Climbing rose | Summer green | Sunny | Requires a large container and care |
| Wild wine | Summer green | Sunny to shady | Red autumn color, grows strongly |
| Climbing hydrangea | Summer green | Partial to shady | Slowly, later very densely |
| Evergreen honeysuckle (Lonicera henryi) | Evergreen | Partial shade | Robust, all year round |
| Wisteria | Summer green | Sunny | Very vigorous, only for large balconies and strong trellises |

## grasses and bamboo

Tall grasses and bamboo quickly form a dense, moving green wall.

- **Umbrella bamboo (Fargesia):** Clump-forming, i.e. without underground runners, evergreen, frost hardy, 1.5 to 3 m high. Very suitable for buckets. Needs a lot of water.
- **Miscanthus:** Up to 2 m high, straw-colored in winter, cut back in spring. Provides privacy protection from July until pruning.
- **Pampas Grass:** Tall, with decorative feathers, needs lots of sun and space.
- **Sweet millet (Panicum):** 1 to 1.5 m, beautiful autumn colors.

Be careful with bamboo: only choose **clust-forming species** such as Fargesia. Runner-forming species such as Phyllostachys can be kept in containers, but they sometimes burst containers.

Be careful with poisonous plants such as yew, thuja and cherry laurel when children or pets play on the balcony. See [Plant Safety](/pflanzensicherheit/).

## Trellises

Climbing plants need different climbing aids depending on their climbing technique:

| Climbing type | Examples | climbing aid |
|---|---|---|
| Schlinger (winding around supports) | Morning glory, runner bean, honeysuckle, wisteria | Vertical ropes, rods, wires |
| Rankers (hold on with tendrils) | Clematis, sweet pea, cucumber, pumpkin | Grids, nets, thin bars |
| Spreader clips (support yourself) | Climbing roses | Tie trellis, shoots |
| Self-climber (adhesive roots or disks) | Ivy, Virginia creeper, climbing hydrangea | They don't need any help, but they can damage facades |

Trellis can be integrated into plant boxes, attached to railings or free-standing in pots. Tension wires and ropes between the floor and ceiling are an elegant solution, but should be installed without drilling or after consultation with the landlord.

## Planters

- **Size:** Climbing plants and woody plants need a lot of root space. At least 30 to 40 liters, for bamboo and woody plants 50 liters and more.
- **Plant boxes with integrated trellis** are a space-saving, stable solution.
- **Water storage:** Large plants evaporate a lot. Vessels with a water reservoir reduce the amount of watering required. See [Balcony Irrigation](/ratgeber/balkon-bewaesserung/).
- **Lightweight materials** such as fiberglass, plastic or polyrattan reduce weight.
- **Winter protection:** Perennial plants in pots need protection for their roots in winter. See [Overwintering potted plants](/ratgeber/kuebelpflanzen-ueberwintern/).

## Statics and tenancy law

- **Weight:** Large pots of wet soil can easily weigh 100 kg or more. If in doubt, have the load-bearing capacity checked for older balconies or many large containers. Place heavy containers against the wall or in the corners where the load capacity is greatest.
- **Windproof:** Tall plants and trellises offer plenty of surface area for wind to attack. Secure vessels and secure trellises stably.
- **Tenancy law:** Plants on the balcony are generally allowed. Drilling into facades or parapets and permanent constructions usually require the consent of the landlord. Plants must not grow into neighbors or cause damage to the facade.
- **Facade protection:** Self-climbers such as ivy and wild vine can damage plaster and joints. It is better to pull on bars on balconies.

## Care

- **Watering:** A tight privacy screen causes a lot of water to evaporate, especially in the sun. Often daily in summer.
- **Fertilize:** Weekly to bi-weekly during the growing season.
- **Tying and guiding:** Direct young shoots regularly to the trellis.
- **Pruning:** Perennial plants and woody plants in spring or after flowering, depending on the species.
- **Control:** Watch out for aphids and spider mites.

## Practical example: The privacy screen to the neighboring balcony

Two balconies were right next to each other, separated only by a low railing. The resident placed two large plant boxes with integrated trellises to the side. In the first year it grew morning glories and runner beans, which formed a dense curtain of flowers until July. At the same time, two evergreen honeysuckles were planted in the boxes. In the second year these were already two meters high, and in the third they formed a year-round privacy screen. The annual plants were only planted as supplements.

## Plan the privacy protection in stages

A green privacy screen is rarely created in a single season. A three-stage plan has proven successful. In the first summer, fast-growing annuals such as runner beans, morning glories or black-eyed Susans close the gaps. At the same time, you plant perennial climbers such as clematis, honeysuckle or wild vine, which mainly form roots in the first year and only become really dense from the second or third year onwards. In the winter, evergreen plants such as clump-growing bamboo of the Fargesia genus, which does not sprout, or ivy on a sturdy trellis ensure that the balcony is not visible from November to April.

This means you have protection from the first year onwards, and the annuals gradually become redundant as permanent planting takes over the area. Plan the pots large enough for the perennial plants right from the start, as later moving a densely planted trellis is laborious.

## Think about insects and birds

A privacy screen made of plants is also a small living space. Runner beans, nasturtiums and clematis provide flowers for bumblebees and bees, and the fragrant honeysuckle attracts moths in the evening. Virginia creeper produces berries in the fall that birds love to eat. Before you do any heavy cutting in spring or summer, it's worth taking a look at the dense greenery, because birds occasionally nest in climbing plants on balconies. Heavy pruning is therefore best done in late winter.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which privacy screen grows fastest?

Annual climbing plants such as morning glories and runner beans reach two to four meters in one summer.

### What stays green in winter?

Ivy, evergreen honeysuckle, umbrella bamboo, yew and thuja.

### Does a privacy screen for the north balcony need other plants?

Yes, ivy, climbing hydrangea and Virginia creeper do well in shade. More in [Plants for the Shade Balcony](/ratgeber/schattenbalkon-pflanzen/).

### Are runner beans poisonous?

Raw beans, i.e. seeds and pods, contain the protein phasin and must not be eaten raw. They are a good vegetable when cooked. If small children are playing on the balcony, harvest the pods in good time and explain to older children that the beans are only edible when cooked.

### How high can a green privacy screen be on the balcony?

There is no general maximum limit; the rental agreement, house rules, statics and wind are decisive. Plants and trellises should not grow over the parapet into the neighbors' area, and tall pots must be secured against tipping over, because a dense, two-meter-high plant wall offers a lot of surface for the wind to attack. As a tenant, you should ask the landlord beforehand about permanently installed trellises or structures on the facade.

### Which climbing plants can tolerate a lot of wind?

Ivy, Virginia creeper and honeysuckle are robust and hold up well even on high, windy balconies. Clematis shoots, on the other hand, break easily in a storm and need a more protected place. Bamboo tolerates wind, but curls its leaves in dry and frosty conditions and needs water on frost-free days in winter.

## Conclusion

A green privacy screen is more beautiful and lively than any mat. Combine annual climbing plants for a quick effect with perennial plants, grasses or potted trees for the future, choose suitable trellises and sufficiently large containers, and pay attention to statics and tenancy law. With regular watering, fertilization and management, a green retreat is created.
