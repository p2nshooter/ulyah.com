The lucky feather, botanically Zamioculcas zamiifolia, is one of the most robust houseplants with its shiny, dark green pinnate leaves. It thrives on little light, survives weeks of drought and grows slowly but steadily. In offices, hallways and apartments where other plants give up, it is often the last one that is still green. Nevertheless, many lucky feathers die, and almost always from too much care in the form of water.

This guide explains how the lucky feather is built, why it needs so little water, which location and soil are suitable, how to repot and propagate it and what is behind yellow leaves or soft stems.

## Profile

| feature | Lucky Feather |
|---|---|
| Origin | Dry areas of East Africa |
| growth | Upright, pinnate petioles from underground tubers (rhizomes) |
| Light | Bright to shady, no blazing midday sun |
| Temperature | 18 to 26°C, not below 15°C |
| Pouring | Only when the earth is almost completely dry |
| Difficulty | Very light |
| Toxicity | Toxic to humans and pets (calcium oxalate) |

## How the lucky feather is built

What looks like a stem with many leaves is actually a single, large, feathery leaf. Each “branch” grows directly from a thick, potato-like tuber underground. These tubers and the fleshy petioles store large amounts of water. This is why the plant can survive long periods of drought in its homeland, and why it reacts so sensitively if its soil remains permanently wet: the storage organs rot before you can see anything above.

## Location

- **Bright without direct midday sun:** This is where the lucky feather grows fastest and forms the most new shoots.
- **Partial shade and shade:** It grows much slower there, but remains healthy. Many specimens have been left for years in hallways with little daylight.
- **Direct Sun:** Short morning or evening sun is not a problem. Bright midday sun behind glass can cause yellow or brown spots on the leaves.

The less light the plant gets, the less water it uses. If you place a lucky feather in a dark corner, you have to extend the watering intervals accordingly. How to estimate the amount of light in a place is explained in [Light for Houseplants](/ratgeber/licht-fuer-zimmerpflanzen/).

## Pouring: the most important point

1. **Allow soil to dry out almost completely.** Check deep in the pot with a wooden stick or your finger. If the earth is also dry underneath, it's time.
2. **Water thoroughly** until water runs out of the drain hole.
3. **Remove excess water after 15 minutes.** Nothing should be left in the planter.
4. **If in doubt, wait.** A lucky feather that is too dry will recover quickly after watering, a rotting one will only rarely recover.

| season | Rough orientation |
|---|---|
| Spring and summer, bright location | Every 2 to 3 weeks |
| Spring and summer, shady location | Every 3 to 5 weeks |
| Autumn and winter | Every 4 to 8 weeks |

A helpful signal: slightly wrinkled petioles or leaves that lose their shine indicate real thirst. It doesn't have to come to that point, but it's safe. More about watering technology in [Water houseplants correctly](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-richtig-giessen/).

## Soil and pot

- **Soil:** Permeable and rather poor. A mixture of two parts houseplant soil and one part pumice, lava granules or coarse sand is well suited. Pure cactus soil also works.
- **Pot:** Must have a drain hole. Clay or terracotta pots are ideal because they allow moisture to evaporate.
- **Size:** The lucky feather can be close. A pot that is too large holds too much water.

The tubers can literally deform plastic pots as they grow. This is a clear sign that a larger pot is due. Further information on substrates can be found in [Potting soil and substrate](/ratgeber/blumenerde-und-substrat/).

## Fertilize

The lucky feather has low nutrient requirements. From April to August it is sufficient to add half the dosage of green plant fertilizer to the irrigation water every four weeks. There is no fertilization in autumn and winter. Too much fertilizer will result in brown leaf tips rather than faster growth.

## Repotting

Repotting is necessary about every two to three years in spring, or when the pot becomes bulging from the tubers.

1. Do not water the plant for a few days beforehand so that the root ball loosens more easily.
2. Carefully remove the pot or, if necessary, cut open plastic pots.
3. Loosely remove the old soil and look at the tubers: They should be firm and light. Soft, brown or foul-smelling areas are removed with a clean knife.
4. Place in a pot that is two to four centimeters larger and fill with fresh, well-drained soil. Leave the tubers just below the surface.
5. Do not water for a week to allow minor injuries to heal.

Detailed instructions are available in [Repotting step by step](/ratgeber/umtopfen-anleitung/).

## Multiply

The lucky feather can be propagated in three ways, all of which require patience:

- **Division:** When repotting, separate the rootstock so that each part has at least one tuber and one shoot. This is the fastest method. See [Dividing Plants](/ratgeber/pflanzen-teilen/).
- **Whole petioles:** Cut off a stem at the base, allow the cut to dry and place in water or moist, sandy soil. First a small tuber forms, and after months a new shoot forms.
- **Individual leaflets:** Peel off the leaves at the base and stick one third into moist substrate. A tiny tuber grows at the base. It often takes six months or more until the first shoots appear.

## Typical problems

| Symptom | Possible cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow leaves, soil moist | Too much water | Stop watering, check tubers |
| Soft, falling stems | Rot at the base | Unpot, remove rotten parts, reposition dry |
| Yellow leaves, soil bone dry | Long-term lack of water or natural aging | Water thoroughly and remove individual old leaves |
| Long, weak, crooked shoots | Too little light | Place in a lighter place and rotate the pot regularly |
| Brown spots on leaves | Sunburn or cold damage | Remove from the midday sun and protect from drafts |
| No new shoots | Winter rest, too dark or pot too big | Patience, more light in spring |

Yellow leaves have many causes. A systematic overview is provided by [Yellow Leaves: Causes](/ratgeber/gelbe-blaetter-ursachen/). If the tuber is already soft, [Detect and save root rot](/ratgeber/wurzelfaeule-erkennen-und-retten/) will help.

### Save rot in five steps

1. Unpot the plant and remove the soil completely.
2. Cut back soft, brown tuber parts and roots down to the light, firm tissue.
3. Use healthy but broken stems as cuttings.
4. Allow the tubers to air dry for two to three days.
5. Place in dry, very well-drained soil and water carefully after a week at the earliest.

The black variety “Raven” is particularly popular. Your new leaves initially appear fresh green and turn dark within a few weeks. Those who don't know this sometimes think the bright shoots are a mistake.

## Practical example: The lucky feather at the reception

In a doctor's office, a large lucky feather stood at the reception, near a north-facing window. It was cast every Friday by changing employees. In the fall, several stems toppled over one after the other, the base was glassy and soft. When unpotting it, it became apparent that the pot had no outlet and that there was about two centimeters of water at the bottom. Two of the five tubers were rotten. After cutting, drying and planting in a clay pot with lava granules, the plant was given a fixed watering date: once a month, but only after a stick test. The following spring it sprouted three new, strong stems.

## What the lucky feather can endure in dark places

The lucky feather is considered a plant for dark corners, and in fact it survives there longer than most other houseplants. But surviving is not the same as thriving: in low light it grows very slowly, new shoots become thin and long, and the soil dries out much more slowly. In dark locations, water even less often and rather a week too late than too early.

The lucky feather feels most comfortable in a bright place without direct midday sun. Blazing summer sun behind glass can burn the shiny leaves, but they tolerate morning or evening sun well. If it's been dark for a long time, slowly get used to a brighter place so that the leaves don't get sunburned.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the lucky feather poisonous?

Yes. All parts contain calcium oxalate and irritate skin, mouth and mucous membranes. Wear gloves when repotting and keep the plant out of the reach of children and pets. More on this in [Plant Safety](/pflanzensicherheit/).

### How fast does a lucky feather grow?

Slow. In a bright location it forms a few new stems in summer, in shady areas sometimes only one or two per year.

### Can I wipe the lucky feather?

Yes, the shiny leaves collect dust. Wiping occasionally with a damp cloth is sufficient. Leaf shine sprays are not necessary.

### Does the lucky feather bloom?

Older plants occasionally form an inconspicuous, flask-like inflorescence close to the ground. It is decoratively unobtrusive and can remain or be removed.

### What are the thick tubers on the roots?

These are rhizomes in which the lucky feather stores water and nutrients. They are the reason why the plant survives long periods of drought and should not be removed when repotting. If a plastic pot bulges or a clay pot cracks, this is a clear sign that the rhizomes need more space.

Then repot the plant in a slightly larger, stable container in spring.

## Conclusion

Above all, the lucky feather needs restraint: a bright to shady place without the blazing midday sun, well-drained soil in a pot with a drainage hole and water only when the soil is almost completely dry. If you don't water according to the calendar, but rather according to the feeling in the pot, you will have one of the most grateful houseplants there is.
