Cacti and succulents are considered the plants for people who don't have a green thumb. In fact, they forgive forgotten watering appointments like no other group of plants. Nevertheless, many of them end up in apartments, usually for two reasons: too little light and too much water at the wrong time. If you understand the natural living conditions of these plants, you can keep them for decades and even make them bloom regularly.

This guide explains what cacti and other succulents have in common, how to properly position, water and fertilize them, why hibernation is so important and what problems often arise.

## What are succulents?

Succulents are plants that store water in thickened leaves, stems or roots. Cacti are a family of succulents, recognizable by the so-called areoles, small cushions from which thorns, hairs, shoots and flowers grow. The most popular non-cactus succulents include echeverias, aloes, haworthias, crassula (money tree), sedum, kalanchoe, and living stones (lithops).

| group | Examples | Special feature |
|---|---|---|
| Desert cacti | Mammillaria, Echinocactus, Opuntia | Lots of sun, strict winter rest |
| Leaf cacti | Christmas cactus (Schlumbergera), Easter cactus | Partial shade, more water, from forests |
| Rosette succulents | Echeveria, Sempervivum, Aeonium | Lots of light, otherwise they will rot |
| Shade tolerant succulents | Haworthia, Gasteria | Bright, but without blazing sun |
| Stem succulents | Money tree, elephant foot | Robust, like summer freshness |

This guide focuses on desert cacti and classic succulents. Leaf cacti like the Christmas cactus come from moist forests and need significantly more water and less sun.

## Location: as bright as possible

- **Ideal:** a south or west window with several hours of direct sun.
- **East window:** sufficient for many species, scarce for sun-hungry cacti.
- **North window:** only suitable for haworthias and gasterias.
- **Summer outdoors:** Many cacti and succulents enjoy a sunny, rain-protected spot on a balcony or patio. Get used to the sun slowly, otherwise you risk getting burned.

Too little light is clearly evident in succulents: rosettes stretch, the leaves are far apart, cacti grow thin and pointed towards the window. This “horning” cannot be reversed; it can only be overgrown by new, compact growth in a brighter place. More in [Light for Houseplants](/ratgeber/licht-fuer-zimmerpflanzen/).

## Watering: soak and allow to dry

The tried and tested method is called “soak and dry”, i.e. soak and let it dry out completely:

1. **Check:** The soil must be completely dry, even at depth. A wooden stick or the weight of the pot will help.
2. **Soak:** Water thoroughly until water runs out the bottom, or place the pot in water briefly.
3. **Drain:** Completely remove excess water.
4. **Wait:** Only water again when the soil is completely dry again.

Small sips every few days are worse than infrequent, thorough watering. They keep the surface moist while the roots below remain dry and encourage rot at the root collar.

| season | Rough orientation |
|---|---|
| Spring and summer | Every 1 to 3 weeks, depending on pot size and sun |
| Autumn | Increase watering intervals gradually |
| Winter (rest time) | Desert cacti hardly or not at all; Plant succulents sparingly every 4 to 6 weeks |

More about general watering techniques in [Water houseplants correctly](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-richtig-giessen/).

## Soil and pot

- **Soil:** Cactus and succulent soil, improved with 30 to 50 percent mineral content such as pumice, lava granules or quartz sand. Pure potting soil holds too much water.
- **Pot:** Always with a drain hole. Clay and terracotta pots are ideal because they release moisture to the outside.
- **Size:** Rather narrow. The pot should be only slightly larger than the plant or root ball.
- **Decorative jars without a drain** are unsuitable for succulents in the long term, even though they are often sold that way in stores.

Further information in [Potting soil and substrate](/ratgeber/blumenerde-und-substrat/).

## Fertilize

From April to August every four weeks with a special cactus fertilizer that contains little nitrogen in the specified dosage. From September onwards there is no more fertilization so that the plants can mature and go into the dormant period. Too much nitrogen makes cacti soft, vulnerable and lazy to bloom.

## Winter dormancy: secret of flowering

Many desert cacti only bloom when they have had a real rest period in the winter. In their homeland, winters are dry and cool at night.

- **Period:** approximately November to February.
- **Temperature:** ideally 5 to 12 °C, depending on the type. A cool, bright staircase, an unheated guest room or a frost-free winter garden are suitable.
- **Water:** Desert cacti are hardly watered at all during this time. They will shrink slightly, which is normal.
- **Light:** They need to be bright even when they are cool.

Real hibernation is difficult in a warm living room. The rule there is: as bright as possible, water sparingly and do not give any fertilizer. Many succulents such as echeverias and money trees cope better with warm winters than flowering desert cacti.

In spring, watering is slowly increased again as soon as new thorns or shoots become visible.

## Repotting

Cacti and succulents are repotted every two to four years in spring when the pot is rooted.

1. Let the soil dry beforehand.
2. Grab cacti with grill tongs, kitchen gloves, newspaper or a folded cloth.
3. Carefully remove old soil and cut off dead roots.
4. Place in dry, fresh soil and do not water for a week to allow injured roots to heal.

## Typical problems

| Symptom | Possible cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Soft, glassy, dark spots at the base | Rotting due to wetness | Cut off the healthy part, let it dry and re-root |
| Long, thin, pale shoots | Too little light | Make it brighter |
| Brown or white, dry spots on the sunny side | Sunburn | Get used to the sun slowly |
| Shriveled leaves, dry soil | Lack of water | Water thoroughly |
| Shriveled leaves, wet soil | Roots rot and no longer absorb water | Unpot, remove rotten roots |
| White, woolly spots | Mealybugs or root lice | See [Mealybugs and Mealybugs](/ratgeber/wollaeuse-und-schmierlaeuse/) |
| Lower leaves of an echeveria dry out | Natural leaf change | Plucking, no problem |

Rot is the most common death sentence for succulents, but the healthy top part can often be saved. More in [Detect and Save Root Rot](/ratgeber/wurzelfaeule-erkennen-und-retten/).

Many succulents are very easy to propagate, for example via leaf cuttings, child plants or head cuttings. You can find detailed instructions in [Propagate succulents using leaf cuttings](/ratgeber/sukkulenten-vermehren-blattstecklinge/) and [Offshoots and children](/ratgeber/ableger-und-kindel/).

## Practical example: The window sill collection

On a north-facing window in the office were a dozen small succulents in decorative jars with gravel. After half a year the echeverias were long and pale, several cacti were brown and soft at the base. The surviving plants were placed in clay pots with mineral cactus soil and placed in a southwest window in the stairwell. Watering was only done when the pots were light. The decayed echeverias were dead and re-rooted. After one summer the plants were compact, colorful and healthy, and the following spring two Mammillaria bloomed in the stairwell after a cool overwinter.

## Cacti on the balcony in summer

Many cacti and succulents benefit from a summer outdoors. The stronger light and the temperature differences between day and night promote compact growth and, in many species, the formation of flowers the following year. But get used to the plants slowly: the first one to two weeks should be in a bright place but protected from the midday sun, otherwise yellowish or brown sunburn spots will appear that will not disappear.

A place under an eaves protects against constant rain, which keeps the soil and plants wet for days. Bring most of the cacti back in before the nights consistently drop below about ten degrees. A bright, cool place in the house is ideal for winter rest.

## Handle and repot cacti safely

You don't have to touch a cactus with your bare hands to repot it. A folded strip of newspaper or cardboard placed around the body, kitchen tongs with rubber tips or thick leather gloves provide reliable protection. The tiny barbed bristles of the Opuntia, the glochids, are particularly treacherous. They easily penetrate the skin and break off. They are often easier to remove with a strip of adhesive tape that is pressed onto the skin and pulled off than with tweezers.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are cacti poisonous?

Most cacti are not poisonous, but their thorns can hurt. Some succulents, such as certain types of euphorbia, kalanchoe, and aloe, can be toxic to pets. More in [Plant Safety](/pflanzensicherheit/).

### Do cacti need spray water?

No. Desert cacti and most succulents do not need increased humidity. Spraying is more likely to encourage rot and fungal spots.

### What about colored or glued cacti?

Colored cacti and cacti with strawflowers glued on them should be cleaned after purchase if possible. The paint doesn't grow back and glue damages the fabric.

### Why is my cactus turning brown and hard at the bottom?

In many columnar cacti, the base corks with age. It becomes brown, firm and bark-like, which is completely natural. On the other hand, a brown spot that is soft, wet or sunken is a cause for concern: This indicates rot and you should check the soil and roots immediately.

### How often do I have to repot cacti?

Young cacti every one to two years, older ones about every three to four years or when the pot has roots. After repotting, do not water for about a week to allow small injuries to the roots to heal before they come into contact with wet soil.

### Do cacti need special fertilizer?

Cactus fertilizer contains less nitrogen than normal green plant fertilizer and thus promotes compact growth. Fertilize lightly about every two to four weeks during the growing season and not at all during winter dormancy.

## Conclusion

Cacti and succulents need lots of light, very well-drained soil in a pot with a drainage hole, and water using the soak-and-dry method. In winter the following applies: cool, bright and dry. Anyone who follows these rules will be rewarded with compact, colorful plants and, for many cacti, spectacular flowers.
