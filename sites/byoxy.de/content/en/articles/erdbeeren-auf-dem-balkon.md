Fresh, sun-warmed strawberries straight from the balcony are one of the most beautiful experiences for hobby gardeners, and for children they are often the introduction to gardening in general. Strawberries are surprisingly well suited to pots, containers and hanging baskets: they have shallow roots, remain compact and, with the right choice of variety, last from early summer to autumn.

In order for the harvest to be plentiful, it depends on a few things: the right variety, the right planting time, good soil, even watering and help with pollination when there are few insects on the balcony. This guide explains everything step by step.

## Which strawberries are suitable for the balcony?

### Single-bearing varieties

Classic garden strawberries bear abundant and large fruits once a year, usually in June. They are well suited for the balcony if you want a concentrated harvest for cakes, jam or a big strawberry evening. Afterwards they mainly form leaves and runners.

### Multi-bearing varieties

Multiple or everbearing strawberries bloom and fruit in several spurts from early summer to autumn. Each individual harvest is smaller, but there is always fresh fruit to snack on for months. They are often the best choice for balconies because they use the space for a long time.

### monthly strawberries

Monthly strawberries come from the small-fruited wild strawberry. Their fruits are small but particularly aromatic, and they bear from early summer until frost. Many varieties hardly form any runners and are therefore ideal for boxes and pots. They also tolerate partial shade better than large-fruited strawberries.

### Trailing and climbing strawberries

These varieties form long runners that bear flowers and fruit themselves. They hang decoratively in hanging baskets and can be tied up on a trellis. They need regular water and fertilizer because many fruits ripen on the long shoots at the same time.

## The right location

Strawberries need a lot of sun. A spot with at least six hours of direct sun per day ensures sweet, aromatic fruit. They also work well on east and west balconies, but on north balconies only monthly strawberries with a small harvest. A place protected from the wind is an advantage because wind dries out the plants and can damage the flowers.

## Containers: pot, box, hanging basket or tower

- **Balcony boxes:** Plant large-fruited strawberries about 20 to 25 centimeters apart, monthly strawberries a little closer. Three to four plants fit into an 80 centimeter box.
- **Pots:** A pot with at least 15 to 20 centimeters in diameter is sufficient for one plant.
- **Traffic lights:** ideal for hanging strawberries. The fruits hang freely, remain clean and are protected from slugs.
- **Strawberry pots and towers:** Containers with side plant pockets use the height space. But they dry out quickly and need water every day in summer.

All vessels need drainage holes and a drainage layer, because strawberries cannot tolerate waterlogging.

## The right earth

Strawberries love humus-rich, loose and slightly acidic soil. Special strawberry soil or high-quality potting soil that you mix with some ripe compost is well suited. They don't like lime-rich soil or heavy, dense soil. Use fresh soil for each new planting, as pathogens may have accumulated in old soil.

## Planting time

- **Single-bearing varieties:** The best planting time is mid-summer, around July to the end of August. By autumn, the plants form strong roots and produce flower buds for the next year. Those who plant in spring usually only get a small harvest in the first year.
- **Multi-bearing varieties and monthly strawberries:** They are usually bought and planted as potted plants in spring, as soon as there is no longer any threat of severe frost, and bear in the same year.

## Plant correctly

The most important thing when planting is the right height. The heart of the strawberry, i.e. the place from which the leaves grow, must be exactly at the level of the earth's surface. If it sits too deep, it rots easily; If it sits too high, the roots will dry out.

1. Water the plant in the pot thoroughly.
2. Fill the box or pot with drainage and soil.
3. Dig a planting hole that is slightly larger than the root ball.
4. Place the plant so that the heart remains free and press the soil all around.
5. Pour in vigorously.

## Pouring

Strawberries need consistent moisture, especially during flowering and fruiting. Dryness leads to small, hard fruits, waterlogging leads to root rot. It is best to water in the morning and directly onto the soil, not over leaves and fruits, because wet fruits rot more quickly.

A thin layer of mulch made from straw or wood shavings keeps the soil moist and the fruit clean and dry. The soil in boxes and pots dries out quickly on hot days. Then check them daily. You can find helpful methods for hot weeks in the guide [Balcony Watering](/ratgeber/balkon-bewaesserung/).

## Fertilize

Strawberries are not heavy eaters, but enjoy regular nutrients. An organic berry fertilizer is ideal. You fertilize single-bearing varieties in the spring when they sprout and again after the harvest when they produce flower buds for the next year. Multi-bearing varieties and monthly strawberries need a weak dose approximately every two to four weeks during the long harvest season. Too much nitrogen, on the other hand, promotes soft leaves and gray mold instead of fruit.

## Pollination on the balcony

Strawberry flowers are pollinated by insects. There are often too few bees and bumblebees on high-altitude or windy balconies. Poorly pollinated flowers produce small, stunted or one-sided fruits.

You can help: Carefully stroke the center of each open flower with a soft brush, moving from flower to flower. It's best to do this several times a week on sunny days during lunchtime. In the long term, you will attract more insects with flowering herbs and flowers. You can find ideas for this in the guide [Bee-Friendly Balcony](/ratgeber/bienenfreundlicher-balkon/).

## Harvest

Strawberries are harvested when they are red all over and smell pleasant. White tips or shoulders show that they are not yet fully ripe. It's best to pick in the morning when the fruits are cool and take the green calyx with you, then they will last a little longer. Strawberries do not ripen after picking and taste best fresh.

## Foothills: remove or use

Large-fruited strawberries form long runners with small young plants in summer. They cost the mother plant strength. If you want a lot of fruit, cut off the runners regularly. If you want new plants, leave the strongest ones and place a small pot of soil under each young plant. After a few weeks it will have its own roots and you can cut the connection to the mother plant. The guide [Offich and Kindel](/ratgeber/ableger-und-kindel/) explains more about this method.

## Overwinter

Strawberries are hardy, but the roots freeze faster in pots than in garden soil. This is how they get through the winter well:

- Place boxes and pots close to the wall of the house, ideally under an eaves.
- Place the containers on an insulating surface and wrap them with jute, coconut mats or bubble wrap.
- If there is a frost, cover the plants with some fleece or brushwood.
- Water moderately on frost-free days, because the roots must not dry out completely even in winter.

In spring, remove old, brown leaves and add some fresh soil or compost. After two to three years, the yield decreases significantly. Then replace the plants with young runners or new plants.

## Typical problems

- **Gray mold:** Gray, furry coatings on the fruit, especially in wet weather. Remove affected fruits immediately, plant in air, do not water over the plants and keep the fruits dry with straw.
- **Powdery mildew:** white, mealy coating on the leaves that curl upwards. Remove affected leaves and provide air movement.
- **Snails:** They prefer to eat ripe fruits. Hanging lights and tall containers provide the best protection.
- **Birds:** Blackbirds love strawberries. A fine net over the plants keeps them away.
- **Small, stunted fruits:** usually poor pollination or drought during fruit formation.
- **Low fruit:** too shady, too much nitrogen, old plants or single-bearing varieties planted in spring.

## Practical example: Strawberries for two children

A family with two children wanted to snack on strawberries all summer long on their southwest balcony. In May she planted two balcony boxes with multiple-bearing strawberries and hung a hanging basket with hanging strawberries. There was also a pot with monthly strawberries in a partially shaded corner.

In the first few weeks many fruits remained small and crooked. The children then took on the task of “playing bee” with a brush twice a week. Together with a layer of straw mulch and regular watering in the morning, the fruits became significantly larger. From June to October there was a bowl of fresh strawberries almost every week, and the monthly strawberries provided aromatic small fruits until the first frost.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I combine strawberries with other plants in balcony boxes?

Yes, with plants that have similar requirements. Chives, thyme on the edge or marigolds go well. On the other hand, strong-growing neighbors that deprive the strawberries of light are unfavorable.

### Why do my strawberries produce lots of leaves but hardly any fruit?

They often receive too much nitrogen or too little sun. Even single-bearing varieties that were planted in spring produce little in the first year. Old plants also decline.

### Can I grow strawberries from seeds?

This is quite possible with monthly strawberries. However, the fine seeds germinate slowly and it takes time until the first harvest. You can propagate large-fruited garden strawberries more easily via runners.

### Are strawberries suitable for children on the balcony?

Yes, strawberries are ideal for children: non-toxic, quickly visible and delicious. Nevertheless, wash the fruit briefly before eating, especially if birds or insects are nearby.

## Conclusion

Strawberries thrive on sunny balconies in boxes, pots and hanging baskets. Choose multi-bearing varieties or monthly strawberries for a long harvest, plant single-bearing varieties in mid-summer, do not plant the heart too deep, water the soil evenly and fertilize moderately. If there are a few insects, use the brush to help with pollination, protect the boxes from frost in winter and renew the plants every two to three years. Then there are fresh strawberries right on your doorstep from early summer to autumn.
