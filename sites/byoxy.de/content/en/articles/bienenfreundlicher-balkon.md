Wild bees, bumblebees, hoverflies and butterflies are finding less and less food in cities. Paved areas, short-mown lawns and double ornamental flowers offer them hardly any pollen and nectar. A balcony can make a real difference here: just a few boxes and pots with the right plants become a gas station for insects. And for the people on the balcony, the humming and fluttering is a beautiful natural experience in the middle of the city.

This guide explains what is important when it comes to bee-friendly plants, which plants are suitable for which location, what a flowering calendar looks like from spring to autumn and how you can do even more with watering holes and nesting aids.

## Why wild bees?

There are over 550 species of wild bees living in Germany, including bumblebees, which are also bees. Many species are endangered. Unlike honey bees, which are looked after by beekeepers, wild bees rely on natural food sources and nesting places. Some species are specialized for very specific plant families. Wild bees generally do not sting or barely sting; most species are very peaceful.

## What is important about plants?

### Unfilled flowers

Double flowers, such as those found in many cultivated forms of roses, dahlias, petunias or geraniums, have additional petals instead of stamens. They provide little or no pollen and nectar and are of no value to insects. **Unfilled flowers** with a visible flower center, on the other hand, are easily accessible.

### Variety of flower shapes

Different insects have proboscises of different lengths. Cupped flowers like cosmos are accessible to many insects, labial flowers like sage and lavender prefer bumblebees and bees, tubular flowers like phlox prefer butterflies.

### Native plants

Many specialized wild bees rely on native wild plants. Native species such as bluebells, viper's head, meadow sage and wild mallow are particularly valuable. But many Mediterranean herbs and some ornamental plants are also popular.

### Long flowering period

Insects need food from spring to fall. A well-planned balcony offers continuous blooms from March to October.

## Water point

Insects also need water, especially on hot days. A shallow bowl of water in which stones, marbles or moss are placed provides them with safe landing spots. Change the water every few days to avoid mosquito larvae. Place the bowl in a partially shaded place.

## Nesting aids

### Insect hotel

Many wild bees nest in cavities. A good insect hotel consists of:

- **Hardwood blocks** with holes from 3 to 9 mm in diameter, drilled cleanly into the longitudinal wood, not into the end wood, so that no cracks occur.
- **Hollow plant stems** such as bamboo or reeds, cleanly cut, closed at the back.
- **No pine cones, wood shavings or straw**, these are hardly used by wild bees.

Hang in a sunny, rain-protected place, facing south or southeast, firmly mounted so that it does not sway.

### Sandbox for ground nesters

About three quarters of all wild bee species nest in the ground. A box or pot of unwashed, clayey sand in a sunny spot can be a nesting place for them.

### Pithaceous stems

Dead stems of blackberries, mullein or thistles, attached vertically to the railing, are used by some species for nesting.

## What you should avoid

- **Insecticides and chemical pesticides:** They also kill beneficial insects. Use natural methods when dealing with pests. See [Fighting Aphids Naturally](/ratgeber/blattlaeuse-natuerlich-bekaempfen/).
- **Double flowers** and pure foliage plants as the only planting.
- **Tidying up:** Leave spent stems and seed heads partially standing in the fall. They serve as wintering quarters and food for birds.
- **Peat-containing soil:** Peat-free soil protects moors. See [Potting soil and substrate](/ratgeber/blumenerde-und-substrat/).

## Practical example: From a geranium balcony to an insect oasis

A west-facing balcony was planted with double geraniums and petunias for years. Insects were hardly visible. In the spring, two of the four boxes were redesigned: one with lavender, thyme and catnip, one with cosmos, zinnias and fan flowers. A sedum was added in a large pot for the fall, as well as a small insect hotel and a water bowl. Bumblebees were already buzzing on the lavender in June, and butterflies visited the sedum in September. Die beiden Geranienkästen blieben, bekamen im Folgejahr aber ungefüllte Sorten.

## Buy seeds and plants correctly

When buying plants and seeds, it's worth taking a closer look:

1. **Choose unfilled varieties:** Pay attention to terms such as “unfilled,” “single-flowering,” or “insect-friendly” in variety descriptions.
2. **Regional wild plants:** Nurseries that specialize in native wild perennials offer plants that are a perfect match for the local insects.
3. **Saatgutmischungen prüfen:** Mischungen mit der Bezeichnung „Bienenweide“ enthalten manchmal vor allem nicht heimische Arten. Mischungen mit regionalem Wildpflanzensaatgut sind ökologisch wertvoller.
4. **Pesticide-free origin:** Some young plants from the mass trade have been treated with insecticides that can still be detected in the pollen for weeks. Bio-Gärtnereien oder eigene Aussaat sind die sichere Wahl.
5. **Buy peat-free soil** at the same time.

Viele einjährige Bienenpflanzen wie Kornblume, Ringelblume, Borretsch und Kapuzinerkresse lassen sich ab April direkt in den Kasten säen. It's cheap and fun. Wer im Herbst die Samenstände stehen lässt, kann eigenes Saatgut ernten und im nächsten Jahr wieder aussäen.

## Watch wild bees on the balcony

Wer einmal genau hinsieht, entdeckt auf einem bienenfreundlichen Balkon erstaunlich viele Arten. The first mason bees often fly in early spring, such as the red mason bee, which likes to use nesting tubes and close its brood cells with clay. Hummelköniginnen suchen dann nach den ersten Blüten und nach einem Nistplatz. In summer, small hole bees are on the move on daisies such as daisies and tansy, and leafcutter bees reveal themselves by circular cutouts in rose petals, which they use to line their nests. Die Garten-Wollbiene sammelt die Haare von Woll-Ziest für ihre Nester.

Ein kleines Bestimmungsbuch oder eine Bestimmungs-App macht aus solchen Beobachtungen ein schönes Hobby, auch für Kinder. Photos with the date and plant show you after a season which plants are best visited on your balcony and help you plan for the next year.

## Der bienenfreundliche Balkon im Herbst und Winter

Don't empty the balcony completely in the fall. Insects overwinter in hollow and pithy stems, such as those of mullein, blackberries or thistles, and seed heads of sunflowers or wild teasels are food for birds. Schneide abgestorbene Stauden deshalb erst im Frühjahr zurück.

Nisthilfen bleiben auch im Winter draußen. The larvae and pupae inside need the cold so that they can hatch at the right time in spring. Ein Platz unter einem Dachvorsprung schützt vor Schlagregen. If the nesting aid is moved to a warm apartment, the animals will hatch too early and will not find any flowers outside.

## Frequently asked questions

### Locke ich damit Wespen an?

Wasps are less interested in flowers than in sweet foods and drinks. A bee-friendly balcony primarily attracts bees, bumblebees, hoverflies and butterflies.

### Sind Geranien schlecht für Bienen?

Viele gefüllte Zuchtformen bieten kaum Nahrung. Unfilled geraniums and wild forms are slightly better, but herbs and native plants are significantly more valuable.

### Wie viele Pflanzen brauche ich?

Even a single box with well-chosen plants helps. The more variety and the longer the flowering period, the better.

### Brauchen Bienen im Winter Futter vom Balkon?

No. Wild bees usually overwinter in the nest as larvae, pupae or finished insects, while bumblebee queens rest in the ground. However, the first flowers in February and March, such as crocuses, snowdrops or winter aconites, are important because the first bumblebee queens and mason bees then need food immediately.

### Sind Insektenhotels aus dem Baumarkt sinnvoll?

Viele billige Modelle sind eher Dekoration. Pine cones, wood shavings and straw are hardly used by wild bees, and holes drilled into the end wood tear open when they dry. Good nesting aids have clean, smooth tubes made of cardboard, reed or bamboo or holes three to nine millimeters in diameter in seasoned hardwood, drilled across the grain and without frayed edges that could injure the bees' wings.

## Conclusion

A bee-friendly balcony does not need a large area. Choose unfilled flowers, native plants and flowering herbs, plan a flowering calendar from March to October, offer water and nesting opportunities and avoid poison. This makes your balcony a valuable habitat for wild bees and other insects. More ideas in [Plant balcony box](/ratgeber/balkonkasten-bepflanzen/).
