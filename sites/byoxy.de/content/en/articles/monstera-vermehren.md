The Monstera is not only one of the most popular houseplants, it is also surprisingly easy to propagate. If you understand their anatomy, you can grow several new plants from a large Monstera and shape the mother plant at the same time. There is a single crucial detail that many people overlook: the knot. Without nodes, each cutting remains a leaf in the vase that never becomes a new plant.

This guide explains how a Monstera is structured, where you cut it, what rooting methods there are and how you can get the young plants through well.

## The anatomy of Monstera

| part | Description | Importance for reproduction |
|---|---|---|
| trunk (shoot axis) | Thick, green main stem from which all leaves grow | This is where the nodes are located |
| knot | Thickened area on the trunk where a leaf stalk attaches, often with a ring-shaped scar | Roots and new shoots grow from the node |
| aerial root | Brown, thick root strand growing from the node | Accelerates rooting enormously |
| petiole | Stem between stem and leaf | Does not form new shoots |
| sheet | Large, slotted leaf | Provides energy to the cutting |
| eye (bud) | Sleeping bud at the node, often barely visible | The new shoot grows from this |

**Important:** A leaf with a petiole but without a stem and node will sometimes form roots but never a new shoot. Such “zombie leaves” last for months in the vase, but do not grow any further.

## The right time

Spring and summer are ideal. The plant grows actively, it is light and warm, and the cuttings root faster. In winter it is possible, but it takes longer and the risk of rot is higher.

## Suitable cuttings

| cutting | Description | Success |
|---|---|---|
| Head cutting | Shoot tip with youngest leaf, one or two nodes and aerial root | Very tall, continues to grow immediately |
| Stem cutting with leaf | Trunk piece with a node, aerial root and leaf | High |
| Stem cutting without leaf | Just a piece of trunk with a knot and an eye | Good, but slower because energy has to come from the trunk |
| Leaf with stem without nodes | No stem tissue | No new shoot |

## Cutting step by step

1. **Prepare tools:** Sharp knife or clean secateurs, disinfected with alcohol. Wear gloves, the plant sap can irritate the skin.
2. **Find a node:** Look along the trunk for the place where a leaf stalk attaches and an aerial root grows.
3. **Make the cut:** Cut about two centimeters below the node, and for the stem cuttings also a little above it, so that a stem piece is created with the node in the middle. For head cuttings, only cut below.
4. **Aerial roots:** Do not cut off. They are the future main roots. Very long aerial roots can be shortened to around 10 to 15 centimeters.
5. **Reduce leaves:** For large cuttings, leave only one, maximum two leaves to reduce evaporation.
6. **Dry the cut surface:** Allow to air dry for a few hours to cork slightly.

The mother plant sprouts again from the next node below the cut point. This way you can shorten a Monstera that has become too tall at the same time.

## Rooting methods

### In the water

The most clear method:

1. Place the cutting in a glass or vase with room temperature water so that the node and aerial root are in the water and the leaf is above it.
2. Place it in a bright, warm place without direct sun.
3. Change water every three to five days.
4. After two to six weeks, new, white roots grow from the aerial root and the node.
5. When the roots are three to five centimeters long, place them in soil.

More details in [Cuttings in a water glass](/ratgeber/stecklinge-im-wasserglas/).

### In sphagnum moss

Moist, expressed sphagnum moss keeps moisture and air in the ideal ratio.

1. Pour moss into a transparent container.
2. Place the cutting so that the knot is in the moss.
3. Keep the moss moist but not wet.
4. If necessary, cover with a hood and ventilate daily.

The roots that grow in moss later adapt to soil more easily than pure water roots.

### In perlite or mineral substrate

A container is filled with coarse perlite or pon, with some water in the lower third. The cutting is inserted so that the node is in the moist area. The capillary action keeps it moist, while at the same time a lot of air reaches the roots. This method is considered particularly rot-proof.

### Directly into soil

Strong cuttings with large aerial roots can be planted directly in loose soil with plenty of perlite and bark. Keep the soil evenly slightly moist. Disadvantage: You can't see the progress.

| Method | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| water | Simple, visible | Water roots, change necessary |
| Sphagnum | Moist and airy, good roots | Moss must be procured |
| Perlite | Very rot proof | Some effort |
| Earth | No moving necessary | Progress invisible, higher risk of rot |

## Mossing: rooting on the mother plant

Moss removal is the safest method because the cutting is nourished by the mother plant until it roots.

1. Select a node with an aerial root on the trunk.
2. Place moist sphagnum moss firmly around the node and aerial root.
3. Wrap with cling film or a cut clear plastic cup and secure loosely at the top and bottom with binding wire or tape.
4. Moisten the moss every few days, perhaps with a syringe through a small opening.
5. After three to six weeks, many white roots will be visible through the film.
6. Cut the trunk below the moss package and pot the new cutting including the moss.

## Planting the young plants

- **Pot:** Not too big, 12 to 17 cm depending on the cutting, with drainage hole.
- **Soil:** Loose mix of houseplant soil, pine bark and perlite. See [Potting soil and substrate](/ratgeber/blumenerde-und-substrat/).
- **Support:** A small moss or coconut stick provides support.
- **Several cuttings** in one pot will produce a bushy plant more quickly.

## Care after rooting

| period | Care |
|---|---|
| First 2 to 3 weeks in soil | Evenly slightly moist, bright without sun, warm, possibly higher humidity |
| After that | Normal Monstera care: water when the top few inches are dry |
| From 6 to 8 weeks | Fertilize lightly |

The first new leaf of a cutting is often smaller and less slit than the mother leaf. With each new leaf the plant grows larger. More about care in [Care for Monstera](/ratgeber/monstera-pflege/).

## Typical problems

| problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Cutting rots in water | No water change, too cold, cut surface not dried | Cut off the rotten part, dry it, fresh water |
| Roots, but no new shoots | No knot or eye | Cut new cuttings with nodes |
| Leaf turns yellow | Cuttings use reserves, often normally | Wait and see |
| Cutting wilts after potting | Switching from water to earth | Keep evenly moist, increase air humidity |

## Practical example: Monstera that is too tall

A monstera had arrived on the ceiling after five years and was hanging crookedly over its moss stick. In May, the top shoot with two nodes, two aerial roots and two leaves was cut off as a top cutting, with a second stem cutting underneath with a node. Both came in perlite with water in a large glass jar. After four weeks they had strong roots, and after six weeks they were placed together in a pot with a moss stick. The mother plant sprouted new shoots from the top remaining node. One plant that was too big had turned into two well-proportioned ones.

## Give away, exchange and send cuttings

Monstera cuttings are popular barter items. To transport it, wrap the interface in slightly damp kitchen paper, put it in a small bag and protect the sheet from bending with a cover made of newspaper or cardboard. An unrooted cutting can easily survive a few days and the recipient roots it as usual. It is best to send already rooted cuttings with a little moist moss around the roots so that they do not dry out.

Within the European Union, private individuals are allowed to exchange plants with each other. Plants and cuttings from countries outside the EU, on the other hand, almost always need a phytosanitary certificate to ensure that pests and diseases are not introduced. Anyone who orders from online retailers abroad should check this beforehand, otherwise the shipment may be destroyed at customs.

## Cuttings of colorful varieties

With variegated Monstera varieties, the knot determines the result. The pattern of new leaves depends on how much light tissue the shoot contains at the interface. A cutting from a well-marked section is very likely to produce colorful leaves again, while a purely green section will usually only produce green leaves. Purely white shoots and leaves have no leaf green and cannot survive on their own.

Some varieties, such as the very evenly speckled 'Thai Constellation', are propagated in the laboratory and show quite stable markings. In other variegated forms, the variegation is inconsistent and can vary greatly from leaf to leaf. Colorful cuttings require a lot of patience and a bright place without direct sun to root.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I grow a Monstera from a single leaf?

No, just a piece of trunk with knots. A leaf alone forms at most roots.

### Does a cutting have to have an aerial root?

Not necessarily, but with aerial roots it's much quicker.

### When is the cutting ready for soil?

When the new roots are three to five centimeters long.

### How long does it take until the first new leaf?

After rooting, it usually takes a few weeks until a new leaf develops. Things happen faster in spring and summer than in winter. As long as the roots grow and the cutting remains firm and green, everything is fine.

### Do I have to change the water in the glass?

Yes, about once a week or as soon as it gets cloudy. Use room temperature water and rinse the glass briefly. An opaque or colored glass prevents a lot of algae from forming.

## Conclusion

The Monstera can be easily propagated if you always take a node with you. Cut head or trunk cuttings with nodes and aerial roots, root them in water, moss or perlite or moss them directly from the plant and plant them in loose soil if the roots are long enough. With a little patience, one Monstera can become a whole family.
