A yellow leaf on a houseplant quickly causes unrest. Sometimes it is harmless because the plant rejects an old leaf. Sometimes it is a warning sign of root rot, nutrient deficiencies or pests. The key is to look closely: which leaves are affected, what does the discoloration look like, and how does the soil feel?

This guide will help you systematically find the cause of yellow leaves and choose the right solution.

## First observe, then act

Before you make any changes, answer these questions:

1. **Which leaves are yellow?** Only the oldest, lowest ones? The young, new ones? Or leaves all over the plant?
2. **How ​​many?** A single sheet or many at once?
3. **What does the yellow look like?** Even yellow, yellow with green veins, yellow with brown spots, speckled with spots?
4. **How ​​does the earth feel?** Wet, damp, dry or bone dry?
5. **What has changed?** New location, repotting, season, vacation, new fertilizer?
6. **Are there other signs?** Soft stems, musty smell, pests, webs, mosquitoes?

## Diagnostic table

| Pattern | Earth | Probable cause |
|---|---|---|
| One lower leaf slowly turns yellow, the rest are healthy | Normal | Natural aging |
| Several leaves yellow, soft, possibly brown spots | Wet for a long time, musty | Too much water, root rot |
| Lower leaves yellow and dry, limp plant | Dry as dust | Lack of water |
| Older leaves uniformly light yellow, new leaves small | Normal | Nitrogen deficiency |
| Young leaves turn yellow, veins remain green | Normal, often hard water | Iron deficiency, limescale |
| Older leaves yellow between the veins | Normal | Magnesium deficiency |

| Suddenly lots of yellow leaves after moving | Normal | Change of location, lack of light |
| Yellow or light spots on the sunny side | Normal | Sunburn |
| Yellow leaves in winter, plant near window | Normal | Cold, drafts |
| Leaf edges yellow and brown, crust on the ground | Normal | Over-fertilization, salts |

## The most common causes in detail

### 1. Too much water

The most common cause of all. If the soil remains permanently wet, the roots will suffocate and begin to rot. The plant can no longer absorb water and displays yellow, soft leaves, often starting on the lower leaves. Later, stems become soft and brown.

**Detect:** Soil moist or wet, even days after watering, musty smell, fungus gnats, water in planter.

**Solution:** Stop watering, empty the planter, let the soil dry. If rot is suspected, check the roots. Detailed information in [Detect and save root rot](/ratgeber/wurzelfaeule-erkennen-und-retten/) and [Water houseplants correctly](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-richtig-giessen/).

### 2. Too little water

Less common, but also common. The plant draws water from the older leaves, which turn yellow and then dry brown. The plant appears limp.

**Recognize:** Soil is bone dry, detaches from the edge of the pot, pot is very light.

**Solution:** Water thoroughly, dip when the bales are completely dry. Then water more evenly.

### 3. Lack of light

Plants in locations that are too dark drop leaves that can no longer provide them with sufficient supplies. The lower and inner leaves usually turn yellow and fall off, and the plant becomes bare and long.

**Recognize:** Long, thin shoots, small new leaves, plant leans towards light, often located far from window or in winter.

**Solution:** Make it brighter or add a plant lamp. See [Houseplant Light](/ratgeber/licht-fuer-zimmerpflanzen/).

### 4. Nutrient deficiency

After a long time in the same soil without fertilizer, nutrients are missing. Depending on the nutrient, different patterns emerge:

- **Nitrogen:** Older leaves turn a uniform pale yellow and the plant hardly grows.
- **Iron:** Young leaves yellow with green veins, often caused by hard water that blocks iron.
- **Magnesium:** Older leaves turn yellow between the veins, veins remain green.

**Solution:** Fertilize regularly during the growing season, use softer water if iron is deficient and possibly use an iron fertilizer. See [Fertilizing Houseplants](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-duengen/).

### 5. Pests

Sucking pests deprive the plant of sap. The leaves become mottled yellow or completely yellow.

- **Spider mites:** Fine, light speckles, webs, especially in dry heating air. See [Fighting spider mites](/ratgeber/spinnmilben-bekaempfen/).
- **Mealybugs and mealybugs:** White cotton in leaf axils. See [Mealybugs and Mealybugs](/ratgeber/wollaeuse-und-schmierlaeuse/).
- **Thrips:** Silvery shiny spots with black dots.
- **Scale insects:** Brown bumps on stems and leaf veins.

### 6. Cold and drafts

Tropical plants react to temperatures below 12 to 15 °C or to cold drafts with yellow leaves and leaf drop. In winter, window sills, tilted windows and places near front doors are critical.

**Solution:** Make it warmer, move plants away from the window when airing, place a polystyrene plate under pots on cold window sills.

### 7. Change of location and repotting stress

After purchasing, moving or repotting, many plants shed some leaves. This is a normal adjustment. They usually recover after a few weeks if you leave them alone.

### 8. Natural aging

Every plant renews its leaves. Older, lower leaves eventually turn yellow and fall off, while new ones grow on top. If this only occasionally affects a leaf and the plant otherwise grows healthily, everything is fine.

## Should I cut off yellow leaves?

A completely yellow leaf will not turn green again. You can remove it as soon as it peels off easily or has completely yellowed. As long as it is still partially green, the plant draws nutrients from it. In the event of rot, fungal infestation or pests, remove affected leaves immediately to prevent it from spreading.

## Practical example: The yellow peace lily

A peace lily grew five yellow leaves in two weeks. The owner suspected she was thirsty and watered more frequently. The diagnosis based on the questions in this guide revealed: Yellow leaves spread over the plant, soil permanently wet, there was three centimeters of water in the planter, slight smell. When unpotted, about a third of the roots were brown and mushy. After removing the rotten roots and repotting in fresh soil with perlite, plus the rule of “water only when the top two centimeters are dry,” the plant produced new leaves and, after two months, a new flower.

## Checklist to check off

1. Is the soil wet, moist or dry? Finger sample at a depth of 3 to 5 cm.
2. Is there water in the planter or saucer?
3. Does the pot have a drain hole?
4. How far is the plant from the window and has the location changed?
5. Is it on a cold window in winter, in a draft or directly above the radiator?
6. When was the last time you fertilized and when was it repotted?
7. Are there animals, webs or cotton wool on the undersides of leaves or in the leaf axils?
8. Are the yellow leaves old and below or young and above?

If you answer these eight questions one after the other, you will usually find the cause without having to guess.

## Water quality as a hidden cause

Sometimes the light, watering rhythm and fertilizer are right, and the leaves still turn yellow. Then it's worth taking a look at the irrigation water. Very hard, calcareous water raises the pH of the soil over time. Lime-sensitive plants such as azaleas, gardenias or calatheas are then less able to absorb certain nutrients such as iron, and their young leaves turn yellow while the leaf veins remain green.

Fertilizer salts also accumulate if you fertilize regularly and only water enough to prevent water from running out of the pot. White crusts on the soil or on the edge of the pot and brown, dry leaf tips are typical indications. Then rinse the soil thoroughly with plenty of water or repot it in fresh soil and water lime-sensitive plants with rainwater or filtered water.

## When professional advice helps

If several plants turn yellow without you being able to find the cause, or if noticeable spots spread quickly, a second look from outside can help. Good nurseries often provide advice based on photos, and the plant protection services of the federal states provide information on pests and diseases. To do this, photograph the entire plant, a typical leaf from both sides and, if possible, the roots.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a yellow leaf turn green again?

If there is a slight nutrient deficiency, slightly pale leaves can become a little greener again. Completely yellow leaves do not recover.

### Are yellow leaves normal in fall?

For some plants, yes, because they adapt to less light. If many leaves are yellowing at the same time, you should check light, temperature and watering.

### How quickly should I act?

If the stems are soft, there is a musty smell, or there are a lot of yellow leaves at the same time, immediately. With a single lower leaf you can observe in peace.

### Can a planter without a drainage hole be to blame?

Yes, very often. Excess irrigation water collects unnoticed in the planter and the roots remain wet for days. Therefore, always place plants in an inner pot with drainage holes and pour out any water that collects in the planter half an hour after watering.

### Do coffee grounds or eggshells help against yellow leaves?

Only very limited. Although coffee grounds contain some nitrogen, they easily mold in the pot if they are left on the ground. Eggshells decompose so slowly that they do not correct an acute deficiency. If there is actually a nutrient deficiency, a suitable houseplant fertilizer in the recommended dosage will help more reliably.

Compost or organic fertilizer improves the soil in the long term, but also works slowly.

## Conclusion

Yellow leaves are a symptom, not a disease. Observe which leaves are affected and how the soil feels, and review the most common causes: watering, light, nutrients, pests, temperature and stress. In most cases the problem is with the water. If you find the cause and fix it, you can see from the next new leaves that the plant is recovering.
