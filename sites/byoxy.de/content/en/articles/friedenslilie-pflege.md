The peace lily, also called single-leaf or spathiphyllum, is one of the most popular houseplants ever. It has shiny, dark green leaves, tolerates places that are a little away from the window, and always adorns itself with elegant white flowers. There is also a feature that beginners especially appreciate: the peace lily shows very clearly when it needs water by letting its leaves hang and stands up again within a few hours after watering.

Nevertheless, there are typical problems: brown leaf tips, yellow leaves, flowers that have turned green or a plant that no longer wants to bloom after the first year. This guide explains how the peace lily is built, what location it needs, how to water, fertilize, repot and divide it, and what you can do to make it bloom again.

## Leaf or flower? How the peace lily is built

What we perceive as the white flower of the peace lily is actually a bract, the so-called spathe. It encloses a bulb-shaped inflorescence on which tiny real flowers sit close together. This structure is typical of the aroid family, which also includes monstera, anthurium and indoor calla. The name monoleaf refers to this one striking bract.

The spathe is initially greenish, then becomes bright white and turns green again after a few weeks. This is not a sign of illness, but rather the natural aging of the inflorescence. The leaves grow from a short, underground rootstock from which new shoots emerge over time. That's why a peace lily becomes bushier over the years and is easy to divide.

## The right location

In its homeland, the peace lily grows in the shade of large trees on the forest floor, where it is moist and warm, but the sun only comes in a filtered form. This determines the ideal space in the apartment.

- **Ideal:** bright but without direct midday sun, about one to two meters from an east or west window or directly on a north window.
- **Direct sun:** results in light, yellowish or brown spots on the leaves. It usually tolerates morning sun well, but does not tolerate blazing southern sun behind glass.
- **Too dark:** The peace lily survives in darker corners, but grows slowly there and hardly blooms anymore. If you want flowers, it needs brightness.
- **Temperature:** Normal room temperatures are ideal. She doesn't like cold drafts, cold window sills in winter and places directly above the heater.

It's easy to underestimate how bright a place really is. Our guide [light for houseplants] (/ratgeber/licht-fuer-zimmerpflanzen/) explains more about this.

## Watering: The plant shows you when

The peace lily wants to be evenly, slightly moist. The soil should dry on the surface between two waterings, but not dry out completely.

1. **Check:** Water when the top two inches of soil is dry. In summer this often happens once or twice a week, but less often in winter.
2. **Water Thoroughly:** Water until water runs out of the drainage hole so that the entire root ball is moist.
3. **Remove excess:** Tip out water from planter or saucer after about half an hour. Constantly wet feet lead to root rot.
4. **Water quality:** Room temperature water with as little lime as possible is ideal. Rainwater or filtered water prevents brown leaf tips.

If the peace lily leaves all its leaves hanging limply, it is usually thirsty. Then water thoroughly or submerge the pot in a bucket of water for a few minutes. After a few hours the leaves will stand again. But don't make this a habit: If the plant often dries out to the point of wilting, it will develop brown leaf edges and bloom more poorly.

Important: Drooping leaves can also be a sign of too much water. If the soil is wet and the leaves still hang, the roots may be damaged. Then no further watering will help, just a look at the roots. More about this in [Detect and save root rot](/ratgeber/wurzelfaeule-erkennen-und-retten/).

## Humidity

As a plant of the tropical forest floor, the peace lily appreciates rather high humidity. It copes well in normally heated apartments, but reacts in very dry heated air with brown leaf tips and becomes more susceptible to spider mites.

Simple measures help: do not place them directly above a radiator, group them with other plants, and use a humidifier during the heating season if necessary. Bathrooms and kitchens with windows are often good locations. You can find more options in the guide [Humidity for Houseplants](/ratgeber/luftfeuchtigkeit-fuer-zimmerpflanzen/).

## Soil and pot

A good, humus-rich potting soil is sufficient for the peace lily. To keep it loose and allow water to drain away easily, mix in about a fifth of perlite, pumice or coarse bark. The pot definitely needs a drainage hole.

The peace lily feels comfortable in a pot that is not too large. In a huge container with a lot of wet soil, the risk of waterlogging increases, and the plant puts its strength into roots instead of flowers. When repotting, only choose a pot that is about two to four centimeters larger than the old one.

## Fertilize

From spring to the end of summer, fertilize the peace lily about every two weeks with a liquid green plant or flowering plant fertilizer at half the dosage. In autumn and winter, when it grows more slowly, one dose per month or none at all is sufficient.

Too much fertilizer is a common mistake. The salts accumulate in the soil, the tips of the leaves turn brown, and white crusts form on the soil. Then rinse the soil thoroughly with plenty of water and do not fertilize for a few weeks. The guide [fertilizing houseplants] (/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-duengen/) explains the basics of dosage.

## Repotting and dividing

Approximately every one to two years in spring it is time for fresh soil, at the latest when roots grow out of the drainage hole or the soil dries out very quickly after watering. You can also use this opportunity to divide large plants.

1. Water the plant the day before so the ball is moist and supple.
2. Carefully remove it from the pot and shake off any loose soil.
3. Look for natural separation points between the shoots. Pull the pieces apart with your hands or separate them with a clean, sharp knife.
4. Each section should have several leaves and a strong root package.
5. Place the parts in suitable pots with fresh soil, water them and put them in a little shade for a few weeks.

After dividing, it may take a while for the new plants to bloom again. You can find step-by-step instructions with pictures for this method in the guide [Dividing houseplants](/ratgeber/pflanzen-teilen/).

## Flowering: This is how the peace lily blooms again

Many peace lilies are purchased in full bloom and then only produce leaves for months. There is a simple reason for this: In nurseries, the flowering of young plants is often triggered with a plant's own growth substance so that they look attractive when sold. In the apartment, the plant must first become strong enough to produce new flowers.

These points promote flowering:

- **More light:** The most common reason for no flowers is a location that is too dark. Place the plant in a brighter place, but without direct sun.
- **Patience:** Young plants often take a year to bloom on their own.
- **Not too big a pot:** A slightly narrow pot promotes flower formation.
- **Regular fertilization:** During the growing season, the plant needs nutrients to produce flowers.
- **Rest phase:** Slightly cooler temperatures around 16 to 18 °C and more economical watering for a few weeks in winter can stimulate flowering in spring.

Cut off faded, green inflorescences as far down the stem as possible. This way the plant saves energy and looks neater.

## Typical problems

- **Hanging leaves:** mostly thirsty. If the soil is wet, however, there are root problems due to waterlogging.
- **Brown leaf tips:** dry air, hard water, too much fertilizer or irregular watering with long dry periods.
- **Yellow leaves:** Individual old leaves may turn yellow. If many of them turn yellow, it is usually due to too much water, rarely too much sun or a lack of nutrients. See [Yellow Leaves on Houseplants](/ratgeber/gelbe-blaetter-ursachen/).
- **Brown or black spots:** Cold, drafts or waterlogging; sunburn in light, dry patches.
- **Green flowers:** normal spathe aging. Cut them off when they are no longer beautiful.
- **No flowers:** too dark, too young, too big a pot or too few nutrients.

## Pests

Healthy peace lilies are quite robust. However, [spider mites](/ratgeber/spinnmilben-bekaempfen/) can settle in dry heating air, recognizable by finely speckled leaves and webs. [Mealybugs](/ratgeber/wollaeuse-und-schmierlaeuse/) in the leaf axils and [sick gnats](/ratgeber/trauermuecken-loswerden/) in soil that is too moist also occur. Wipe the leaves regularly, check the undersides and occasionally give the plant a lukewarm shower. This removes dust and keeps many pests away.

## Does the peace lily purify the air?

The peace lily appears in almost every list of “air purifying plants.” The basis is a well-known study by the American space agency from the late 1980s, in which plants absorbed certain pollutants from the air in small, closed chambers. Later evaluations have shown that these results can hardly be transferred to normal living spaces: in order to noticeably improve the air, a lot of plants would be necessary, and regular ventilation has a much greater effect.

The peace lily is not an air purifier, but simply a beautiful, easy-care plant. The fact that plants increase well-being and make a room appear more lively is still a good reason to keep them.

## Varieties

There are numerous varieties on the market that differ primarily in size and leaf pattern:

- **Compact varieties** remain small at 30 to 40 centimeters and fit on window sills and shelves.
- **Large-leaved varieties** like 'Sensation' grow to more than a meter high and are suitable as solitary plants for bright corners of rooms.
- **Varieted varieties** such as 'Domino' or 'Picasso' have white striped or speckled leaves. They need a little more light than green varieties and grow slower.

## Peace lily, children and pets

Like many arum plants, the peace lily contains needle-shaped crystals of calcium oxalate. Anyone who chews leaves or flowers will experience a burning sensation in the mouth and throat and may experience salivation, swelling and vomiting. Serious poisoning is rare because the unpleasant taste is usually a deterrent.

However, keep the plant out of the reach of small children, cats and dogs. If a child or animal has chewed on the plant, rinse their mouth with water and contact the poison control center, pediatrician's office or veterinary practice if you have any symptoms.

## Practical example: The peace lily in the hallway

A reader placed her newly purchased, blooming peace lily in a windowless hallway because she had read “Shadow Plant” there. The flowers withered, new ones failed to appear, and the leaves became pale and soft after a few months. At the same time, the soil remained moist for weeks and small mosquitoes flew around the pot.

The solution was to move to a north window in the living room. The plant was given fresh, loose soil in a slightly larger pot, only watered when the surface had dried out, and fertilized regularly from spring onwards. After a few months it produced strong new leaves and flowers again the following spring. “Shade-tolerant” does not mean “lightless”.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often does a peace lily bloom?

Well-cared for, mature plants usually bloom in spring and summer, some several times a year. Each inflorescence lasts several weeks.

### Can I grow peace lily hydroponically?

Yes, the peace lily is one of the plants that can be grown well in hydroponics. The change is best done with young plants in spring. The guide [Hydroculture: Houseplants without Soil] (/ratgeber/hydrokultur-umstellen/) explains how this works.

### Why does my peace lily have brown flower edges?

The perianths are sensitive to drought, sun and water left on them when sprayed. Water onto the soil rather than over the flowers, and keep the plant out of the sun.

### Do I have to wipe the leaves?

Yes, occasionally. Dust on the large leaves robs the plant of light. Wipe the leaves with a soft, damp cloth, without leaf shine spray.

## Conclusion

The peace lily is a grateful houseplant for bright to semi-shady places without direct sun. Keep the soil evenly slightly moist, avoid waterlogging, water with low-lime water if possible and fertilize regularly in summer at half the dosage. With enough light, a pot that isn't too big and a little patience, it will bloom again and again. And if it drops all of its leaves, it's usually not a cause for concern, but simply a clear request for water.
