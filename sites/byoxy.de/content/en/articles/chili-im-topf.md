Chili plants are ideal potted plants for balconies and windowsills. They are decorative with their white flowers and colorful fruits in red, yellow, orange, purple or brown, and they produce a harvest that ranges from mildly fruity to extremely spicy. At the same time, chilies are perennial and can live for many years if cared for properly. What they need above all is: lots of warmth, lots of light and early cultivation.

This guide explains varieties and levels of spiciness, cultivation, location, pot, care, pollination and harvesting and shows how to overwinter chilis.

## Varieties and degrees of spiciness

The spiciness of chili is measured in **Scoville Units (SHU)**. It is caused by the substance capsaicin, which is located primarily in the light-colored septum (placenta) inside the fruit.

| Art | Well-known varieties | sharpness (coarse) | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capsicum annuum | Sweet peppers, jalapeño, cayenne, ornamental peppers | 0 to 50,000 SHU | Easiest, quick, great variety |
| Capsicum chinense | Habanero, Scotch Bonnet, Carolina Reaper | 100,000 to over 1,000,000 SHU | Very aromatic, very spicy, long maturation time, lots of warmth |
| Capsicum baccatum | Aji Amarillo, Bishop's Crown | 30,000 to 50,000 SHU | Fruity, large plants |
| Capsicum frutescens | Tabasco | 30,000 to 50,000 SHU | Small, upright fruits |
| Capsicum pubescens | Rocoto | 30,000 to 100,000 SHU | Hairy leaves, black seeds, tolerates cool temperatures |

**Capsicum annuum** varieties such as Jalapeño, Cayenne or compact ornamental varieties are suitable for beginners and small balconies. They grow faster and produce earlier than the very hot Chinense varieties.

## Cultivation: start early

Chilies need a long growing season. Particularly hot varieties require five to six months from seed to ripe fruit.

| Art | sowing |
|---|---|
| Capsicum annuum | End of February to mid-March |
| Capsicum chinense, baccatum | January to February, with plant lamp |
| sweet peppers | End of February to mid-March |

### Germination

- **Temperature:** 25 to 28°C. Below 20 °C, many chilies germinate very slowly or not at all. A heating mat is helpful.
- **Sowing depth:** About half a centimeter.
- **Moisture:** Evenly moist, never wet.
- **Duration:** Annuum varieties germinate in one to two weeks, Chinense varieties can take three to four weeks.

### Light after germination

As soon as the seedlings appear, they need **a lot of light** and slightly cooler temperatures around 20 °C. In February and March, a plant lamp is almost indispensable, otherwise the seedlings will rot. More about this in [Sowing on the windowsill](/ratgeber/aussaat-auf-der-fensterbank/) and [Light for houseplants](/ratgeber/licht-fuer-zimmerpflanzen/).

### Pricking out and repotting

After the first pair of real leaves, prick out into pots of 8 to 10 cm, then move to larger pots after a few weeks. Chilies can be planted a little deeper.

## Location

- **From mid-May** on the balcony after hardening.
- **Sunny and warm:** A south or west facing balcony with as many hours of sunshine as possible. The more sun, the more fruit and the more spiciness.
- **Protected:** Protect from strong winds and continuous rain.
- **Indoors:** Also possible on a very bright south-facing window, but often less productive there. Prefer compact varieties.

## pot and soil

| variety | Pot size |
|---|---|
| Compact ornamental varieties | 3 to 5 liters |
| Annuum varieties | 7 to 12 liters |
| Chinense, Baccatum, large plants | 12 to 20 liters |

- **Drain hole** and drainage layer.
- **Soil:** High-quality vegetable or tomato soil with some perlite for better aeration.
- **Dark pots** warm the roots, which chilies like, but dry out quicker.

## Pouring

- **Even, but not wet.** Chilies like to be allowed to dry slightly between waterings.
- **Water in the morning**, directly to the base.
- **In midsummer** often daily, or twice for large plants in small pots.
- Light drought stress shortly before maturity can increase spiciness but should not cause wilting.

See also [Balcony Irrigation](/ratgeber/balkon-bewaesserung/).

## Fertilize

Chilies are medium nutritious. Starting about four weeks after the last repotting, fertilize with tomato or vegetable fertilizer every one to two weeks. Tomato fertilizer with more potassium promotes flowers and fruit. Too much nitrogen leads to lots of foliage and little fruit.

## Supporting and breaking out

- **Support:** A stick helps when the plant bears a lot of fruit. Tie loosely.
- **Royal Flower:** The first flower on the first branch is sometimes broken off to encourage the plant to branch more. This is optional.
- **Principling** like with tomatoes is not necessary.

## Flowering and pollination

Chilies are self-pollinating. On the balcony, wind and insects ensure pollination. In the apartment or on windless balconies, it helps to gently shake the plant or dab it from flower to flower with a soft brush.

Flowers often drop when temperatures are above 32°C, below 15°C, or the plant is stressed. This is normal during heat waves. New flowers follow.

## Harvest

- **Detecting ripeness:** The fruits have their final color, the skin is shiny and can be easily separated from the stem. Many chilies can also be harvested green; they are then less aromatic.
- **Cut off instead of tearing off:** Harvest with scissors including the stem base so as not to injure the branches.
- **Harvest regularly:** This stimulates the formation of new fruits.
- **Be careful with hot varieties:** Wear gloves when processing and do not touch your face.

## Preserve chili

- **Drying:** Thread the fruit onto a thread and hang it in an airy, warm and shady place, or dry it in a dehydrator at 50 °C.
- **Freezing:** Freeze whole or sliced.
- **Pickling:** In vinegar or oil. When using oil, pay attention to hygiene due to the risk of botulism and store it in a cool place or dry it beforehand.
- **Make your own chili sauce or chili flakes**.

## Overwinter

Chilies are perennial. Overwintered plants bear earlier and more in the second year.

1. **Bring indoors before the first frost**, usually at the end of September or October. The plant suffers below 10 °C.
2. **Pruning:** Cut all shoots back to about a third, leaving a small framework of main stem and branches.
3. **Control Pests:** Especially aphids and spider mites. See [Fighting spider mites](/ratgeber/spinnmilben-bekaempfen/).
4. **Location:** Bright and cool at 10 to 15 °C, or warm and very bright with a plant lamp.
5. **Water sparingly** and do not fertilize.
6. **Repot in fresh soil in spring**, place warmer and brighter, slowly increase watering and fertilizing.

See also [Overwintering potted plants](/ratgeber/kuebelpflanzen-ueberwintern/).

## Typical problems

| problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Seedlings long and thin | Too little light, too warm after germination | Plant lamp, put in a cooler place |
| Flowers fall off | Heat, cold, drought, lack of pollination | Pour evenly, shake, be patient |
| No fruit until August | Sowing too late, too little sun | Sow earlier, sunnier spot |
| Yellow leaves | Nutrient deficiency or waterlogging | Fertilize, check watering |
| Aphids | Soft shoots | Rinse off, see [Fight aphids naturally](/ratgeber/blattlaeuse-natuerlich-bekaempfen/) |
| Fruit rot at the top | Calcium deficiency due to uneven watering | Water more evenly |

## Practical example: The jalapeño in its third year

A jalapeño was grown on the windowsill under an LED lamp at the beginning of March and was placed in a 10 liter pot on a south-facing balcony in mid-May. From July onwards it bore around 40 fruits. In October it was cut back to a third and overwintered in a cool, bright guest room at 12°C. The following spring it sprouted vigorously, bloomed in May and produced over 80 fruits. In the third year it was a small tree with a woody trunk and once again bore abundantly.

## Protection during harvesting and processing

The substance that makes chilies hot is called capsaicin. It is mainly located in the light septa and at the seed base inside the fruit and stubbornly sticks to the fingers. Wear disposable gloves when cutting sharp varieties and do not touch your eyes, nose or contact lenses afterwards. If the skin still burns, water is of little help because capsaicin is fat-soluble. A little cooking oil, which you then wash off with soap, or dairy products work better.

When drying in the oven, roasting in a pan and grinding very hot varieties, irritating fumes and fine dust arise which can trigger violent coughing. Ventilate well, work at a distance and keep children and pets away. Children should not pick hot chilies on the plant anyway, as a single bitten fruit can be very painful for them.

## Several varieties next to each other

Although chilies tend to pollinate themselves, insects can carry pollen from one variety to the next. The fruits of the current year do not change as a result, because their pulp comes solely from the mother plant. However, the seeds in these fruits can produce hybrids whose plants look different the next year and are unexpectedly spicy or mild. If you want to obtain pure seeds, put a fine bag made of organza or fleece over a few flowers before they bloom, shake the plant slightly from time to time and mark the fruits that develop from it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I sow chili from purchased fruit?

Yes, the seeds often germinate. With hybrids, however, the offspring are not true to the variety.

### Are chili plants poisonous?

The leaves and unripe fruits contain solanine and should not be eaten. The ripe fruits are edible. The spiciness is unpleasant for pets. See [Plant Safety](/pflanzensicherheit/).

### How do I make my chilies hotter?

Lots of sun, warmth and light drought stress before ripening increase the capsaicin content. However, it is the variety that determines the spiciness the most.

### Do green chilies ripen after picking?

Green chilies are edible, but taste different from ripe fruits. If they have already started to change color, they will often continue to ripen a little at room temperature. On the other hand, fruits that are picked completely green usually do not change color completely. To get the full flavor, let the pods ripen on the plant.

## Conclusion

Chilies need early cultivation with plenty of warmth and light, a large pot, a sunny, sheltered place, even watering and regular fertilization. Then they bear plenty of fruit until autumn. If you cut your plants back and overwinter, you will harvest even more in the second year. Matching: [Tomatoes on the balcony](/ratgeber/tomaten-auf-dem-balkon/).
