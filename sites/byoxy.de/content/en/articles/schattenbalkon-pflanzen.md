Many balconies are on the north side, in a narrow courtyard or under a deep roof overhang. There is hardly any direct sun here. It is often concluded that nothing beautiful can grow there. That's not true. There are numerous plants that not only tolerate shade, but love it. And a shaded balcony even has advantages: it hardly heats up in summer, the plants need less water, and foliage plants shine in lush green.

This guide will help you assess the lighting conditions, introduce the best plants for shade and partial shade and give tips on design and care.

## How shady is your balcony?

| Category | Meaning | Typical situation |
|---|---|---|
| Partial shade | 2 to 4 hours of direct sun, morning or evening | East or west balcony with obstacles |
| Light shadows | Hardly any direct sun, but lots of bright, scattered light | North balcony with open sky |
| Deep shadow | Hardly any direct and little scattered light | Courtyard, under trees, under large balconies |

Watch your balcony on a sunny day in early summer. How many hours of direct sun falls on the planting sites? Are there differences between front and back, top and bottom? The parapet is often lighter than the floor.

Fuchsias and busy lizards are the classics for the shaded balcony. They bloom for months without the sun having to shine.

## Leaf ornamental plants

Leaves play the main role in the shade. Exciting compositions are created with different leaf shapes, colors and textures.

| plant | Effect | Light |
|---|---|---|
| Hostas | Large, often variegated or blue-green leaves | Partial shade to shade |
| Purple bells (Heuchera) | Leaves in red, purple, amber, silver | Partial shade to shade |
| Ferns (e.g. worm fern, lady fern, Japanese rainbow fern) | Fine, delicate fronds | Shadow |
| Ivy | Hanging or climbing, evergreen | Partial shade to deep shade |
| Coleus | Colorful leaves, annual | Partial shade |
| Grasses such as Japanese forest grass (Hakonechloa) or sedge (Carex) | Movement, structure | Partial shade to shade |
| Caucasus forget-me-not (Brunnera) | Silvery leaves, blue spring flowers | Partial shade to shade |
| Golden strawberry, Günsel | Ground cover, hanging | Partial shade |

## Herbs for shade

Many herbs need sun, but some grow well in partial shade.

| herb | Light | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Parsley | Partial shade | Likes consistent moisture |
| Chives | Partial shade | Robust, perennial |
| Mint | Partial shade to light shade | Overgrown, put in its own pot |
| Woodruff | Shadow | For punch and syrup |
| Wild garlic | Partial shade to shade | In spring, can be confused with poisonous plants |
| Chervil | Partial shade | Likes cool conditions |
| Lemon balm | Partial shade | Robust, fragrant |
| sorrel | Partial shade | Sour leaves |

Mediterranean herbs such as rosemary, thyme, lavender and basil, on the other hand, will hardly thrive in the shade. More about mint in [Planting and harvesting mint](/ratgeber/minze-pflanzen-und-ernten/).

## Vegetables and fruits in partial shade

- **Lettuce and lettuce** actually grows better in partial shade than in full sun because it shoots less quickly there. See [Salad on the Balcony](/ratgeber/salat-auf-dem-balkon/).
- **Radish, spinach and chard** thrive in partial shade.
- **Wild strawberries** and monthly strawberries also produce in partial shade.
- **Currants** in pots tolerate partial shade.

Tomatoes, peppers, chili and cucumbers, on the other hand, need a lot of sun.

## Design ideas

### Light versus dark

Bright, white and variegated plants bring light into dark corners. White busy lizards, white-edged hostas and silvery leaves appear almost glowing in the shade.

### Green on green

A balcony made only of foliage plants with different shades of green and textures looks calm and elegant, similar to a forest garden.

### Pop of color

Individual strong colors such as pink fuchsias, red begonias or purple heuchera set accents.

### Multiple levels

Hanging baskets, boxes on the parapet and buckets on the floor create depth. It is often brighter at the top; plants that require a little more light can be there.

### Example plantings

| box | Plants |
|---|---|
| Classic flowering | Hanging fuchsia, busy lizard, lobelia |
| Forest garden | Fern, hosta, golden strawberry |
| Colorful | Purple bells, tuberous begonias, coleus |
| Edible | Parsley, chives, lettuce, wild strawberries |

## Care in the shade

- **Water less:** The soil dries out much more slowly in the shade. Only water when the top centimeters are dry. Avoid waterlogging.
- **Fertilize:** Flowering plants such as fuchsias and begonias weekly to bi-weekly with flowering plant fertilizer, foliage plants more sparingly.
- **Air circulation:** Fungal diseases and snails occur more easily in damp, shady corners. Do not plant plants too close and remove wilted leaves.
- **Snails:** Check near the ground on balconies, especially with hostas.

## Overwinter

Many shade plants are perennial. Hostas, ferns, purple bells, ivy and grasses can overwinter outside with container protection. Fuchsias and tuberous begonias are overwintered frost-free. See [Overwintering potted plants](/ratgeber/kuebelpflanzen-ueberwintern/).

## Practical example: The inner courtyard balcony

A small balcony on the second floor of an old building faced the inner courtyard and only received one hour of evening sun in midsummer. Geraniums cared for there for years. In the spring it was redesigned: white busy lilies and hanging fuchsias were placed in the boxes on the parapet, and on the floor there were three tubs with hostas, worm ferns and purple bells, as well as a hanging basket with ivy and golden strawberries. A box with parsley, chives and mint served as a kitchen garden. The balcony bloomed from May to October and only had to be watered every two to three days.

## A care calendar for the shade balcony

| month | Tasks |
|---|---|
| March | Cut back ferns and hostas, check bulbs of spring bloomers, plant horned violets |
| April | Slowly encourage overwintered fuchsias and tuberous begonias and renew the soil in pots |
| May | After the ice saints, plant busy lizards, begonias and fuchsias and start snail control |
| June to August | Water evenly, fertilize flowering plants weekly, remove dead flowers |
| September | Autumn planting with purple bells, heather and grasses, fuchsias should be put away before the first frost |
| October to February | Pack perennial containers and water lightly on frost-free days |

A shaded balcony is more forgiving of maintenance errors than a sunny balcony because the soil does not dry out as quickly. That's precisely why it's worth not watering according to a fixed schedule in summer, but rather doing a finger test every time. Many shade plants such as hostas and ferns react to permanently wet soil by rotting their roots, and snails feel particularly comfortable in damp boxes. If you place boxes on feet or attach them to the parapet instead of placing them directly on the ground, you will have significantly fewer problems with snails.

## Wetness, moss and algae in the shade

On a shady balcony, the earth, floor and furniture dry more slowly. Therefore, water more sparingly than on a sunny balcony and check the soil with your finger beforehand. Pots with a drainage hole, a drainage layer and small feet under the pots prevent waterlogging, and the balcony floor underneath also stays drier.

The best way to remove green deposits of algae and moss on the floor and railings is mechanically with a brush and water. Aggressive cleaners get into the water through the drain and harm the plants. Sweep away fallen leaves regularly, as wet leaves are a popular hiding place for snails.

## White glows in the shadows

Bright flowers and leaves look particularly beautiful in the shade, especially at dusk. White busy lizards, white begonias, hostas with light leaf edges and silvery dead nettles seem to literally glow in the evening. Solar lights, on the other hand, often do not charge enough on a shady balcony. If you want light in the evening, it is better to choose an outdoor light with a mains connection.

## Food for insects in partial shade

A shady balcony can also help insects. Lungwort blooms in early spring and is an important food source for the first bumblebees. Caucasian forget-me-nots, bluebells and woodruff thrive in partial shade and are also visited by insects. Choose unfilled flowers if possible, because bees hardly get any nectar and pollen from double varieties.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do geraniums bloom in shade?

Barely. Geraniums need a lot of sun. In the shade they produce long shoots and few flowers.

### Can hydrangeas go on the shaded balcony?

Yes, hydrangeas like partial shade and light shade. They need large containers and lots of water.

### Which plants stay green in winter?

Ivy, some ferns such as deer's tongue fern, purple bells (partly), sedge and golden strawberry.

### Can I grow tomatoes on a north-facing balcony?

Usually with little success. Tomatoes need many hours of direct sun to produce abundant fruit. Although they grow on a north-facing balcony, they hardly bear any fruit. Salads, chard, parsley, chives and mint work better there.

### How much do I have to fertilize on the shade balcony?

Less than on a sunny balcony. Plants in the shade grow slower and therefore need fewer nutrients. For most, half the recommended amount is enough, and too much nitrogen will only make them soft and vulnerable.

### Which climbing plants are suitable for the shade?

Ivy, climbing hydrangea and Virginia creeper do well with little sun. The climbing hydrangea blooms in early summer with large white umbels and climbs with adhesive roots, but needs a few years in the pot before it really gets going.

### What to do if the plants are barely blooming?

Place slow-flowering plants in the brightest part of the balcony, do not fertilize with too much nitrogen and permanently replace sun-hungry species with real shade-flowering plants such as busy lizards, fuchsias or begonias.

Begonias with large, double flowers also show their full splendor in partial shade.

## Conclusion

Even a shady balcony can bloom and green. Choose plants that love shade, such as fuchsias, begonias, busy lilies, ferns, hostas and purple bells, rely on bright colors and interesting leaf structures, water more sparingly than on sunny balconies and use herbs such as parsley and mint.
