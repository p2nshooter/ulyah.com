Root rot is one of the most common causes of death for houseplants and is also one of the most insidious. It begins invisibly underground. When yellow leaves, limp shoots and soft stems appear above ground, a large part of the roots are often already affected. Nevertheless, many plants can be saved if you act quickly and decisively.

This guide explains how root rot occurs, how you can recognize it, how to save an infected plant and how to prevent it from happening again.

## How does root rot occur?

Roots need oxygen. If the soil remains permanently wet, the air pores fill with water. The roots suffocate and die. Dead root tissue is colonized by fungi and bacteria, which spread in the wet, oxygen-poor soil and also attack healthy roots. Typical pathogens are fungi and fungus-like organisms such as Pythium, Phytophthora and Fusarium.

Main causes:

- **Watering too frequently** without allowing the soil to dry out
- **Pots without a drain hole** or planters with water in them
- **Too big pots** with lots of soil without roots
- **Dense, used soil** that hardly contains any air
- **Cold locations** where the soil stays wet for a long time
- **Low light**, so the plant hardly uses any water

## Signs

### Above ground

- Yellow leaves, often starting on the lower leaves
- Limp, drooping leaves despite wet soil
- Brown, soft spots on leaves or on leaf edges
- Soft, dark, glassy stems at the base
- growth arrest
- On succulents: glassy, translucent leaves that fall off

### In the earth

- Soil smells musty, putrid or musty
- Soil stays wet for days
- fungus gnats
- Mold on the surface

### At the roots

| Healthy roots | Rotten roots |
|---|---|
| Firm and elastic | Soft, squishy, slimy |
| White, cream, light brown or green/silvery for orchids | Dark brown to black |
| Odorless or earthy | Foul, unpleasant smell |
| Skin is tight | The outer skin peels off when gently pulled, leaving a thin thread |

Attention: Some plants naturally have brown roots. Strength is crucial. Firm brown roots are healthy, soft brown roots are rotten.

## Rescue step by step

### 1. Unpotting

Carefully remove the plant from the pot. If you suspect root rot, don't hesitate, waiting will make the situation worse.

### 2. Remove soil

Completely remove the wet soil from the root ball. Loosen with your fingers and rinse carefully under lukewarm running water until the roots are clearly visible.

### 3. Cut off rotten roots

Using clean, sharp scissors or a knife, trim any soft, brown, mushy roots back to the firm, healthy tissue. If in doubt, remove a little more. Clean the blade with alcohol every now and then to avoid introducing pathogens.

### 4. Adjust above ground parts

If a large portion of the roots have been removed, the remaining root system cannot support the leaf mass. Then remove some leaves or shorten shoots in approximately the same proportion as roots have been lost. Also cut soft, rotting stems back to the healthy tissue.

### 5. Allow to dry

Allow the roots to air dry for a few hours so that the cuts heal. For succulents, cacti and arched hemp, one to three days.

### 6. Fresh soil and clean pot

Place in fresh, loose and well-drained soil that contains slightly more mineral components such as perlite or pumice than usual. The pot should fit the remaining root ball, i.e. be smaller than before, and definitely have a drainage hole. Clean old pots with hot water. Dispose of the old soil in household waste. See [Potting soil and substrate](/ratgeber/blumenerde-und-substrat/).

### 7. Water carefully

After planting, only water lightly, for succulents only after a week. Then only water again when the top layer of soil has dried significantly.

### 8. recovery

Place the plant in a bright place without direct sun, do not fertilize and be patient. New roots form within two to six weeks. New leaves are the sign that the rescue has been successful.

## Plan B: cuttings

If the roots are almost completely rotten or the trunk is already rotting from below, the mother plant can often no longer be saved. Then it is worth rooting healthy shoots as cuttings:

- **Ivy, Monstera, Philodendron:** Root healthy shoots with nodes in water. See [Cuttings in a Water Jar](/ratgeber/stecklinge-im-wasserglas/).
- **Succulents:** Remove healthy leaves or heads, allow to dry and place on dry substrate. See [Propagate Succulents Using Leaf Cuttings](/ratgeber/sukkulenten-vermehren-blattstecklinge/).
- **Cacti:** Cut off the healthy upper part, let it dry for several days to weeks and then place it on dry substrate.
- **Bow hemp and lucky feather:** Save healthy leaves or rhizome parts.

## When can a plant no longer be saved?

- All the roots are rotten and the trunk is soft all the way up.
- There are no longer any healthy shoots for cuttings.
- The point of vegetation (the heart) is rotten, for example in orchids or palm trees.

Then it is better to dispose of the plant including the soil and clean the pot thoroughly.

## Prevention

| Measure | Why |
|---|---|
| Only water when the soil has dried out, depending on the type | Roots get air |
| Pots with drain hole | Excess water can drain away |
| Check planters after watering | No standing water |
| Appropriate pot size | Less wet soil without roots |
| Loose, structurally stable soil | More air pores |
| Water less often in winter | Less consumption in low light |
| Repot regularly | Replace used, compacted soil |

More about this in [Water houseplants properly](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-richtig-giessen/) and [Repotting step by step](/ratgeber/umtopfen-anleitung/).

## Practical example: The Monstera in a ceramic pot

A monstera was placed in a decorative ceramic pot without a drain hole and was watered once a week. In winter the leaves turned yellow, two stems collapsed and the earth smelled rotten. When unpotting it, it became clear that about half of the roots were brown and mushy, and there was water at the bottom of the pot. The rotten roots were cut off, as were three leaves to relieve the strain on the plant. Two healthy shoots with aerial roots were placed in the water as cuttings to be on the safe side. The mother plant was placed in a smaller plastic pot with a drainage hole and a mixture of soil, bark and perlite and stood in the ceramic pot as a planter. After five weeks a new leaf appeared and the cuttings were potted as a second plant in the spring.

## Which plants are particularly at risk

| Plant group | Why endangered | Special note |
|---|---|---|
| Succulents and cacti | Store water, need dry phases | Rot often begins invisibly at the root collar |
| Bow hemp, lucky feather | Rhizomes and tubers rot quickly | Never water according to a fixed plan |
| Orchids | Roots need air | Do not leave water standing in the planter |
| Large plants in giant pots | Lots of wet soil with no roots | Adjust pot size to root ball |
| Plant in dark locations | Low water consumption | Significantly extend watering intervals |

With these plants it is worth watering them particularly carefully in winter and checking the weight of the pot regularly. If you are unsure, it is better to water too little than too much. A slightly thirsty plant recovers within hours, while a rotting plant often doesn't recover at all. A wooden stick that is permanently stuck in the pot and pulled out before each watering is also helpful.

## The first weeks after the rescue

After repotting, the most delicate phase begins. Do not water at all or only water very little in the first week so that the cuts heal and the remaining roots get air. Then you only water when the top two to three centimeters of soil are dry and also check the moisture using the weight of the pot. A small pot that fits the reduced amount of roots will dry faster and reduce the risk of relapse.

Place the plant in a bright spot, but out of direct sun, and do not fertilize for at least six to eight weeks. Fertilizer does not heal roots, but puts additional strain on them. New, bright root tips on the wall of the pot or a fresh leaf are the best signs that the rescue has been successful.

## Root rot in hydroponics and semi-hydro

Hydroponic plants can also develop root rot, usually because the water level is permanently too high. The water level indicator should drop to the minimum between waterings so that the roots can get some air in between. Brown, slimy roots and an unpleasant smell from the container are warning signs.

Then rinse the roots and expanded clay thoroughly, cut off any rotten roots, clean the container and then fill in less water. The same applies to semi-hydro systems with a water reservoir: the reservoir is allowed to drain occasionally before you refill it.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does hydrogen peroxide help against root rot?

Diluted hydrogen peroxide is sometimes recommended for rinsing roots. More important than any remedy are removing rotten roots, fresh soil and proper watering.

### Can I reuse the old soil?

No. It contains pathogens and should be disposed of with household waste.

### How long does recovery take?

Depending on the plant and the extent of the damage, two to three months until it grows vigorously again.

### Does cinnamon or charcoal help at the interfaces?

Some plant lovers dust fresh cuts with cinnamon or charcoal powder because both have a slightly drying effect and an anti-fungal effect. This can help, but clean cuts, allowing them to dry out and fresh, well-drained soil are crucial.

### Can root rot spread to other plants?

The pathogens can be spread via water, soil and tools. Therefore, do not use common saucers for sick and healthy plants, clean pots and tools thoroughly and dispose of the old soil in the household waste.

Also rinse watering cans and planters with hot water before using them again for healthy plants.

In this way you prevent the pathogens from spreading further unnoticed.

## Conclusion

Root rot occurs due to permanently wet soil and a lack of oxygen. If you notice yellow, limp leaves in wet soil, pot up immediately, remove rotten roots, place the plant in fresh, loose soil in a suitable pot with a drainage hole and then water with restraint. Secure additional healthy shoots as cuttings. If you eliminate the causes, you prevent it from happening a second time.
