The butterfly orchid, botanically Phalaenopsis, is by far the most common orchid on German windowsills. It blooms for months, is available in many colors and yet is considered difficult by many. The reason is usually that it is treated like a normal potted plant. In nature, Phalaenopsis do not grow in soil, but rather sit on tree bark. Anyone who understands this understands almost all of their needs.

This guide explains how Phalaenopsis live, how to water them correctly, which location and substrate are suitable, how to repot and how to trigger a new panicle after flowering.

## Profile

| feature | Phalaenopsis |
|---|---|
| Origin | Tropical forests from Southeast Asia to northern Australia |
| growth | Epiphytic (on trees), few broad leaves, thick aerial roots |
| Light | Bright, without direct midday sun |
| Temperature | 18 to 26 °C, a few degrees cooler at night |
| Water | When the roots are silvery-gray, usually every 7 to 14 days |
| Difficulty | Light to medium |
| Toxicity | Considered non-toxic to humans and pets |

## This is how phalaenopsis live in nature

In their homeland, Phalaenopsis cling to tree trunks and branches with their thick roots. Rain runs over the roots, which soak up water, and then quickly dries off again. The roots are covered with a sponge-like covering (velamen) that absorbs water and releases it into the air. So you need water and air alternately. In dense, wet soil they suffocate and rot.

This results in three basic rules: a coarse, airy substrate, a pot through which water drains quickly, and never standing water at the roots.

## Location

- **Ideal:** an east or west window, or a south window with a curtain or at some distance.
- **North window:** possible, but the plant often blooms less often there.
- **Too much sun:** Direct midday sun behind glass causes yellow or white, later brown spots on the leaves.
- **Too dark:** The leaves become dark green, soft and long, new flower spikes do not appear.

A good tip: Healthy Phalaenopsis leaves are firm, medium green and shiny. Very dark leaves indicate too little light, yellowish-pale leaves indicate too much.

## Diving instead of watering

The most proven method for phalaenopsis in the bark substrate is diving:

1. **Know when:** The roots in the clear pot are silvery gray instead of green, the substrate feels dry, and the pot is light.
2. **Dipping:** Place the culture pot in a container with room temperature water for 10 to 15 minutes. The water should reach just below the edge of the pot, not into the leaf axils.
3. **Drain:** Remove the pot and drain thoroughly before returning it to the planter.
4. **Check the planter:** After half an hour, check whether water has collected and pour it out.

| situation | Rough orientation |
|---|---|
| Summer, bright window | Approximately every 7 days |
| Winter, heating air | Every 7 to 10 days |
| Winter, cool room | Every 10 to 14 days |

Important: **Do not get water into the leaf axils and into the heart of the plant.** If water remains there, the growing point can rot. If it does happen, soak up the water with a paper towel. More general information in [Water houseplants properly](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-richtig-giessen/).

### Water quality

Phalaenopsis are sensitive to hard, lime-rich water. Lime is deposited on roots and substrate. Rainwater or boiled, cooled tap water is ideal. In regions with very hard water, you can mix tap water with distilled water.

## Humidity

Phalaenopsis cope surprisingly well with normal room air. In heated apartments in winter, it still helps to increase the humidity slightly: for example with a bowl of water and expanded clay on which the pot is placed without standing in the water. Spraying the flowers directly leads to stains and should be avoided. More options in [Humidity for Houseplants](/ratgeber/luftfeuchtigkeit-fuer-zimmerpflanzen/).

## Substrate and pot

- **Substrate:** Special orchid soil made from coarse pine bark, possibly with some coconut chips or sphagnum. Normal potting soil is unsuitable for Phalaenopsis.
- **Culture pot:** Clear plastic pots with lots of holes are ideal. They allow light to reach the roots, which do some photosynthesis themselves, and you can see immediately whether they are dry or moist.
- **Planter:** A glass or ceramic planter is fine if the culture pot in it is not submerged in water.

## Fertilize

During the growth of new leaves and roots, add special orchid fertilizer to the immersion water every two to four weeks, in the concentration stated on the package or slightly weaker. Diving without fertilizer should be done once a month to rinse out any salt residue. During the dormant period without new leaves, fertilization is carried out less frequently or not at all.

## Repotting

Phalaenopsis should be repotted about every two years, ideally after flowering. Bark substrate decomposes over time, becomes dense and holds too much water.

1. Carefully remove the plant from the pot; Make established roots supple beforehand by dipping them.
2. Completely remove old substrate.
3. Cut off dead roots: They are hollow, brown and mushy or papery dry. Healthy roots are firm, green or silvery.
4. Place in a slightly larger culture pot and let fresh orchid substrate trickle loosely between the roots. Tap gently instead of pressing.
5. Do not dive for the first few days so that the incision sites can heal.

Aerial roots growing out of the pot are normal and healthy. They are not cut off or forced into the pot by force. General information in [Repotting step by step](/ratgeber/umtopfen-anleitung/).

## After flowering: cut the panicle or leave it standing?

When the last flower has fallen off, there are two options:

- **Panicle is still green:** Cut off from below above the second or third node (the small thickenings on the stem). A side panicle with new flowers can grow from a node below.
- **Panicle turns yellow or brown:** It dies and is cut off at the base. The plant then puts its strength into new leaves and roots and later blooms with a new panicle.

### Trigger a new bloom

Phalaenopsis often form new flower panicles after a **cooler phase.** If the plant is in a bright place for four to six weeks in autumn where the night temperature drops to around 16 °C, it will often produce a new panicle. A cool bedroom window or a place close to the window pane is often enough. It is important to have enough light.

## Typical problems

| Symptom | Possible cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Leaves limp and wrinkled | Roots rotten (too wet) or dried out | Check roots, remove rotten ones, repot |
| Bottom leaf turns yellow | Natural aging when only one is affected | Let fall away |
| Several leaves yellow, roots brown | Waterlogging, decomposed substrate | Repot in fresh bark |
| Buds fall off before opening | Drafts, change of location, temperature fluctuations | Keep calm and evenly warm |
| No new blooms | Too dark or too warm in autumn | Make it brighter, offer a cool phase |
| White cotton wool in leaf axils | Mealybugs | See [Mealybugs and Mealybugs](/ratgeber/wollaeuse-und-schmierlaeuse/) |
| Sticky drops on the panicle | Mostly harmless nectar, rarely pests | Check for scale insects and mealybugs |

## Kindel: Babies on the panicle

Sometimes a small plant, a so-called childel, grows on a node of the panicle instead of a flower. Leave it on the mother plant until it has formed three to four leaves and a few roots three to five centimeters long. Then it can be cut off with a piece of panicle and placed in a small pot with fine orchid substrate. More about this in [Offshoot and Kindel](/ratgeber/ableger-und-kindel/).

## Practical example: The gift orchid

A white phalaenopsis came to the house as a gift in a decorative ceramic pot. After flowering, it was watered weekly with a shot glass of water. After a few months the leaves were soft and wrinkled. When unpotting it, it became clear that there was water in the pot under a layer of moss and most of the roots were rotten. After removing the dead roots, transplanting them into fresh bark in a transparent pot and switching to diving, the plant grew new roots and two new leaves in the summer. The following winter, after a cool phase, a panicle appeared again on the bedroom window.

## More orchids for beginners

If the Phalaenopsis is thriving, it's worth taking a look at other orchids that can thrive in the home. Lady's slipper orchids of the genus Paphiopedilum like it a little shady than Phalaenopsis, have no storage organs and therefore want to be kept evenly, slightly moist. Cambria and Oncidium hybrids bloom in many colors and like bright places without midday sun. Dendrobium nobile, on the other hand, needs a cool, bright resting period in winter to produce flowers, and cymbidiums prefer to spend the summer outside and the flowering period in a cool, bright place.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are orchids poisonous to cats?

Phalaenopsis are considered non-toxic. Nevertheless, fertilizer or pesticide residues can cause harm, and cats should not nibble on the leaves. More in [Plant Safety](/pflanzensicherheit/).

### Should I water orchids with ice cubes?

This is not recommended. The tropical roots do not like cold and the substrate is only moistened unevenly. Diving is more reliable.

### How long does a phalaenopsis bloom?

A panicle often blooms for two to four months. Many plants bloom once or twice a year.

### What do silvery and green roots reveal?

The aerial roots of the Phalaenopsis are surrounded by a sponge-like layer that soaks up water. It looks silvery gray when dry and green when wet. In the transparent pot, silvery roots show you that it is time to dive. Brown, mushy roots, on the other hand, have rotted and thin, papery, dry roots have died.

### Why do the leaves become limp and leathery?

Most of the time the plant cannot absorb enough water, either because it has not been dipped infrequently or because many of the roots have rotted or dried out. Check the roots, cut off dead parts and place the orchid in fresh bark substrate. Higher humidity helps until new roots grow.

## Conclusion

Phalaenopsis are not difficult plants, but tree-dwellers with special roots. Give them a coarse bark substrate in a transparent pot, dip them when the roots are silvery, keep the leaf axils dry, place them brightly without midday sun and give them a few cooler nights in autumn. Then they bloom reliably year after year.
