Calatheas, also known as basket marants, are among the most beautiful foliage plants for the home. Their leaves are striped, spotted or patterned, often with purple undersides. Many species fold their leaves upwards in the evening and open them again in the morning, which is why they are also called “prayer plants”. At the same time, calatheas have a reputation for being sensitive: brown leaf edges and curled leaves are common complaints.

Almost all of these problems can be attributed to three factors: water quality, humidity and uniformity. This guide shows you how to get them under control.

| growth | Bushy, rhizome-shaped, patterned leaves |
| Light | Bright to partially shaded, no direct sun |

| Difficulty | Means |

## Location

Calatheas grow naturally in the undergrowth of the rainforest, in filtered light under large trees.

- **Ideal:** bright, but without direct sun. One to two meters from an east or west window, or on a north window.
- **Direct sun:** bleaches the patterns and burns the leaves.
- **Too dark:** The pattern becomes weaker and the plant hardly grows.
- **Even heat:** No drafts, no cold window sills in winter, not directly above the heater.

Bathrooms with windows are often ideal Calathea locations because the humidity is higher there. More about lighting conditions in [Light for Houseplants](/ratgeber/licht-fuer-zimmerpflanzen/).

## Water: evenly and with soft water

1. **Evenly slightly moist:** The soil should never dry out completely, but should not be wet either. Water when the top inch or two is dry.
2. **Low lime water:** Calatheas are sensitive to lime, chlorine and fluoride in tap water. Brown leaf edges are often the result. Rainwater, filtered or stale, decalcified water is ideal.
3. **Room temperature:** Cold water stresses the roots.
4. **Remove excess:** No water should remain in the saucer after watering.

| season | Rough orientation |
|---|---|
| Spring and summer | Approximately every 4 to 7 days |
| Autumn and winter | Approximately every 7 to 12 days |

Curled leaves during the day are a sign of thirst or dry air. After watering, they usually open again within hours. More about watering technology in [Water houseplants correctly](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-richtig-giessen/).

### Prepare water

- Collect **rainwater** in a clean can or bin.
- **Leave tap water** overnight to allow chlorine to escape. If the water is hard, also use a water filter.
- **Distilled water** can be mixed with tap water to reduce limescale.

## Increase humidity

Calatheas love humidity above 50 percent. In heated apartments it often drops to 30 percent or less in winter. Effective measures:

- **Humidifier** near the plant, the most effective method.
- **Group plants:** Multiple plants together create a wetter microclimate.
- **Bowl with water and expanded clay:** Place the pot on top without letting it stand in water.
- **Bathroom or kitchen** as a location if there is enough light there.

Frequent spraying only increases the humidity for minutes and can leave white spots if the water is hard. Detailed information in [Humidity for Houseplants](/ratgeber/luftfeuchtigkeit-fuer-zimmerpflanzen/).

## Soil, pot and fertilizer

- **Soil:** Loose, humus-rich, slightly acidic and water-retaining, but permeable. Houseplant soil with some perlite and coconut fiber or bark works well.
- **Pot:** With drain hole. Plastic pots retain moisture more evenly than clay.
- **Fertilizer:** From April to September, green plant fertilizer at half the dosage every three to four weeks. Calatheas are sensitive to salt; too much fertilizer causes brown tips.

## Repotting and dividing

It is repotted every one or two years in spring into a slightly larger pot. Large plants can be divided: carefully pull the root ball apart or separate it with a knife so that each part has several leaves and rhizomes. The cuts then need particularly uniform moisture and high humidity for a few weeks. More in [Dividing plants](/ratgeber/pflanzen-teilen/) and [Repotting step by step](/ratgeber/umtopfen-anleitung/).

Calatheas are practically impossible to propagate from cuttings. Division is the reliable method.

## Typical problems

| Symptom | Possible cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Brown, dry leaf edges | Dry air, hard water, fertilizer salts | Increase humidity, use soft water, fertilize less |
| Leaves curl up during the day | Thirst or dry air | Water, increase humidity |
| Yellow leaves, wet soil | Too much water | Reduce watering, check roots |
| Faded pattern | Too much direct sun | Place in more shade |
| Hanging, soft stems | Cold or root rot | Make it warmer, check roots |
| Fine webs, bright speckles | Spider mites | See [Fighting spider mites](/ratgeber/spinnmilben-bekaempfen/) |

Spider mites love dry, warm heating air. Calatheas with too low humidity are therefore particularly vulnerable. It's worth checking the undersides of the leaves regularly.

### Brown edges: what to do?

Leaf edges that are already brown will not turn green again. You can cut them off along the shape of the leaf with clean scissors, leaving a narrow brown edge so that no new wounds are created in the green tissue. What is more important is to correct the cause so that new leaves remain healthy.

## Practical example: Brown edges in winter

A Calathea orbifolia stood in the living room next to the sofa, a good meter away from the radiator. In the summer it grew splendidly, in the winter every new leaf had brown edges. A hygrometer showed 28 percent humidity. The owner put a small humidifier in the corner, grouped three more plants around the calathea and switched to filtered water. The humidity rose to about 50 percent, and the following leaves unfurled without brown edges.

## Calathea throughout the year

| time | Care |
|---|---|
| March to May | Repot or divide, start fertilizing, wipe off leaves |
| June to August | Water evenly, fertilize lightly every three to four weeks, and protect from direct sun |
| September to October | Stop fertilizing, slightly extend watering intervals, prepare humidifier |
| November to February | Distance from the heater, keep the humidity above 50 percent, check for spider mites |

Calathea is most challenged in winter. The heating air dries out the leaves, at the same time the light is weak and the plant grows slowly. Many owners water too much during this time because the leaves curl up. However, curled leaves in moist soil indicate dry air rather than thirst. A hygrometer next to the plant prevents wrong decisions.

## The first few weeks after purchase

Calatheas usually come from greenhouses with high humidity and even heat. The transition to the drier air of an apartment is stressful for them, and a few brown leaf edges in the first few weeks are normal. Do not repot the plant immediately after purchase, but give it two to three weeks to get used to the new location.

During this time, place a new calathea a little away from your other plants and check the undersides of the leaves for spider mites, which particularly like to attack calatheas and often come with them from the market. Fine webs and light speckles on the leaves are typical signs. Right from the start, choose a bright place without direct sun and without heated air, because every further move means new stress for the plant.

## Calathea and Goeppertia: a new name

Many plants that are sold commercially as Calathea now have the botanical name Goeppertia. After genetic studies, a large proportion of the earlier Calathea species were moved to this genus, including popular houseplants such as the peacock plant. This doesn't matter for maintenance, but the new name can be helpful when looking for information.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are calatheas poisonous?

They are considered non-toxic to people, cats and dogs, making them suitable for households with animals. More in [Plant Safety](/pflanzensicherheit/).

### Why do the leaves move?

Calatheas follow a day-night rhythm. In the evening they raise the leaves, in the morning they lower them. This behavior is called nyctinasty and is a sign of a healthy plant.

### Can I put Calatheas in the terrarium?

Small varieties do very well in large, open planters or display cases with high humidity as long as there is sufficient light.

### Why are my Calathea leaves curling?

Curled leaves are usually a sign of thirst or too dry air, less often of too much light. Check the soil first: If it's dry, water thoroughly. If the leaves curl up despite moist soil, the roots may be damaged by waterlogging and you should check the root ball.

### How often do I have to repot a calathea?

Approximately every one to two years in spring when the roots fill the pot. Just choose a pot size larger and a loose, humus-rich soil. You can divide large plants immediately when repotting.

### Can I treat the leaves with leaf shine?

No. Leaf shine sprays close the stomata and damage the sensitive leaves. Instead, wipe off dust with a soft, damp cloth, preferably with low-lime water to avoid white spots.

A lukewarm shower in the shower every few months also removes dust and also helps against spider mites.

## Conclusion

Calatheas are delicate, but not difficult if you take their origins in the rainforest undergrowth seriously. Place it brightly without direct sun, water evenly with soft, room temperature water, ensure humidity is above 50 percent and fertilize sparingly. Then they show their patterns in all their glory and move their leaves day after day in the rhythm of the light.
