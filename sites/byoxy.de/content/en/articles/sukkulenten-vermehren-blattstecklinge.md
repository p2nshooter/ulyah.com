Succulents are among the easiest plants to propagate. For many species, a single leaf is enough to create a completely new plant. After a few weeks, tiny roots and a mini rosette form at the base of a fallen leaf. Anyone who has ever seen a new plant grow from an echeveria leaf is usually thrilled. With a few basic rules, this almost always works.

This guide explains which succulents can be propagated using leaves, how to remove and treat leaves correctly, how to save dead plants by deadheading them and how to care for the young plants.

## Which succulents are suitable?

| plant | Leaf cutting | Head or shoot cuttings | Kindel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Echeverias | Very good | Very good | Yes, in many species |
| Graptopetalum, Graptoveria | Very good | Very good | Yes |
| Sedum (stonecrop, e.g. Sedum morganianum) | Very good | Very good | Partial |
| Money tree (Crassula ovata) | Good | Very good | Rare |
| Pachyphytum | Good | Good | Yes |
| Kalanchoe | Good | Very good | In brood leaves on the edge of the leaf |
| Haworthia, Gasteria | Medium, slow | Rare | Very good |
| Aloe | Not suitable | Rare | Very good |
| Aeonium | Not suitable | Very good | Partial |
| Cacti | Not suitable (except leaf cacti and Opuntia limbs) | Very good | In many species |
| Bow hemp | Sheet sections possible | No | About rhizomes |

Some succulents with narrow, thin leaves or no leaf bases cannot be propagated via leaves. For aloe, haworthia and many cacti, Kindel are the more reliable method. See [Offshoot and Kindel](/ratgeber/ableger-und-kindel/).

## Remove leaves correctly

The most important point: **The leaf must be removed completely with the base of the leaf.** This is the only place where the cells from which new roots and shoots grow are located. A torn leaf with an injured base usually only forms roots, but no rosette.

1. Choose a healthy, strong leaf from the lower or middle part of the plant.
2. Take the leaf between your thumb and forefinger and carefully **turn it side to side** until it comes away cleanly from the stem.
3. The base should be undamaged and slightly concave; you can often see a small, clean attachment point.

When repotting or pulling off old lower leaves, leaves that are suitable often fall off anyway.

## Allow to dry

Leave the leaves to lie in a bright, airy place without direct sun for a few days, depending on their thickness **two to seven days**, until the break area closes with a dry layer (callus). This prevents rot.

## Place on the substrate

1. Fill a shallow bowl with **dry, mineral succulent soil**, such as cactus soil with pumice or sand.
2. The dried leaves **lay flat on the surface** or lightly insert the base into the substrate. Many prefer lying down as the base remains airy.
3. Place in a bright place, but without direct midday sun, at 18 to 25 °C.
4. **Do not water** unless there are roots. The paper supplies itself from its own reserves.

## Patience: the process

| Time after lying down | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 to 3 weeks | Fine, pink or white roots at the base of the leaves |
| 3 to 6 weeks | A tiny rosette appears at the base |
| 2 to 4 months | The rosette grows, the mother leaf shrinks |
| After the mother leaf dries up | The young plant is independent and can be potted |

As soon as roots appear, you can cover them lightly with substrate and **spray very sparingly** or give a little water from below if the substrate is completely dry. Do not remove the mother leaf until it dries up on its own. It nourishes the young plant.

Not every leaf becomes a plant. A success rate of 50 to 80 percent is normal. Therefore, always lay out several sheets.

## Heads of rotten rosettes

Echeverias and other rosette succulents stretch when there is a lack of light. They become long, the leaves are widely spaced, and the color fades. This cannot be reversed, but the plant can be rejuvenated by deadheading:

1. Using a clean, sharp knife or dental floss, cut off the rosette about two to three centimeters below the compact head.
2. Remove the lower leaves from the stem, leaving one to two centimeters of stem free. The removed leaves can be used as leaf cuttings.
3. Let the head dry for a few days.
4. Place on dry substrate. It forms roots after two to four weeks.
5. The remaining stem in the pot often sprouts several new rosettes.

Afterwards, be sure to **make it lighter** so that the yellowing does not repeat itself. See [Houseplant Light](/ratgeber/licht-fuer-zimmerpflanzen/).

## Shoot cuttings

Shoot cuttings work very well for shrub-like succulents such as money trees, sedums, aeoniums or kalanchoes:

1. Cut off a shoot 5 to 10 cm long.
2. Remove lower leaves.
3. Allow to dry for a few days.
4. Place in dry substrate and water lightly for the first time after a week.

Money tree cuttings also root in water, but are more easily lost to rot.

## Cactus cuttings

Many cacti can be propagated using separated shoots, limbs or heads:

- **Columnar cacti:** Cut off a piece, let it dry for several days to two weeks until the cut surface is well sealed, and then place it upright on dry substrate, possibly with supports.
- **Opuntia:** Break off individual links, let them dry and place them upright.
- **Christmas and Easter cactus:** Twist off two to three limbs, dry briefly and place in slightly moist soil.

## Care of young plants

| factor | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Light | Bright, slowly get used to the sun |
| water | Use sparingly and allow the substrate to dry between doses |
| Substrate | Mineral, permeable |
| pot | Small, with a drainage hole, likes to put several young plants in a bowl |
| fertilizer | Only after a few months, very weakly with cactus fertilizer |

More about caring for adult plants in [Caring for Cacti and Succulents](/ratgeber/kakteen-und-sukkulenten-pflege/).

## Typical problems

| problem | Cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Leaf becomes mushy and translucent | Rotting due to moisture or lack of drying | Remove, dry longer next time |
| Leaf shrivels without roots | Base injured or too dry and hot | Twist off a new leaf cleanly and place it in a shaded area |
| Roots but no rosette | Base damaged, patience required | Wait and maybe spray lightly |
| Young plants long and pale | Too little light | Make it brighter |
| Mold on the substrate | Too humid, bad air | Moist less, ventilate |

## Practical example: The echeveria bowl

After moving, an echeveria was severely rotten and its stem was 15 centimeters long. The owner beheaded the rosette, twisted the 20 lower leaves off the stem and let everything dry for a week. The head was placed upright in a small clay pot and the leaves were laid flat in a bowl with mineral substrate. After three weeks the head had roots and 14 leaves had pink roots. After six weeks, small rosettes appeared. The stem in the old pot sprouted three new heads. One rotten plant had grown into almost twenty within one summer.

## Roots or rosette first?

With leaf cuttings, fine, pink roots sometimes appear first, sometimes a tiny rosette appears first. Both are normal. As soon as roots appear, carefully cover them with some substrate or press them lightly into the surface so that they do not dry out. The mother leaf remains on the young plant until it dries completely by itself, because it supplies the young plant with water and nutrients.

## Why some leaves don't grow

Not every leaf becomes a new plant. Leaves that are torn when removed or whose base is missing usually do not form roots because that is precisely where the cells from which roots and shoots arise are located. Therefore, carefully twist leaves sideways instead of picking them. Not all succulents are suitable for leaf cuttings: Aeoniums, for example, are best propagated from head or shoot cuttings, and variegated varieties often produce weak or purely green plants from leaves.

## Succulents from seeds

If you want a lot of plants at once, you can also sow succulents. You scatter the seeds, which are often fine as dust, on the surface of a fine, mineral substrate and do not cover them with soil. A cover made of glass or foil keeps the moisture in, warmth of twenty to twenty-five degrees promotes germination, and the place should be bright but without direct sun. However, it takes months before the seedlings become attractive plants.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to spray leaf cuttings?

Only when there are roots, and then very sparingly. Not at all beforehand to avoid rot.

### Can I put succulent leaves in water?

This sometimes works, but carries a high risk of rot. Dry substrate is more reliable.

### When is the best time?

Spring to late summer. In winter it is slower, but possible in a bright place.

### When can I remove the dried mother leaf?

Only when it has completely shriveled up and can be removed without resistance. Until then, it will still provide the young plant with reserves.

### Why are my leaf cuttings shrivelling without forming roots?

The leaves often lie in the blazing sun or in a very warm, dry place for too long and lose their reserves before roots form. Place it brightly, but out of direct sun. Even leaves with damaged bases often shrivel without growing. Also, expect that not every hand will be successful, so it's better to lay out a few more.

### How do I water the young plants correctly?

As soon as roots are visible, lightly moisten the substrate when it is dry, preferably with a small spoon or a squirt bottle directly onto the soil and not into the rosettes. As the roots grow, water gradually as you would with adult succulents: thoroughly, then allow to dry thoroughly.

## Conclusion

Succulents can be easily propagated using leaves, heads, shoots and stems. Twist the leaves cleanly with the base, let all interfaces dry, place them on dry, mineral substrate, only water after rooting and give the young plants plenty of light. You can save decayed rosettes by cutting them off. This is how a single plant becomes a whole collection.
