Aphids are probably the most well-known plant pests. In spring they sit in dense colonies on the tender shoot tips of roses, herbs, balcony flowers and vegetables, and houseplants are not spared either. The good news: Aphids are comparatively easy to combat, and without any chemical agents. This is particularly important when it comes to herbs and vegetables that are eaten.

This guide explains how you can recognize aphids, why they suddenly appear in large numbers, which natural methods work and how beneficial insects and wise plant choices prevent an infestation.

## Recognize aphids

| feature | Description |
|---|---|
| size | 1 to 4 millimeters |
| color | Green, black, grey, yellowish, reddish or whitish powdered, depending on the species |
| shape | Pear-shaped body, two small tubes on the abdomen |
| Whereabouts | Shoot tips, buds, young leaves, undersides of leaves |
| Side effects | Sticky honeydew, black sooty mold, ants, white molt residue |

### Damage

- Curled, curled or stunted young leaves and shoots
- Deformed buds that do not open properly
- Sticky leaves due to honeydew, on which black sooty mold grows
- Weakened plants that grow and flower less
- Transmission of viral diseases, especially in vegetables

## Why there are so many

Aphids reproduce asexually in spring and summer: females give birth to live young without fertilization, which themselves have offspring after about a week. One louse can turn into thousands in just a few weeks. If it becomes too crowded, winged animals emerge that fly to new plants.

Plants with soft, fast-growing tissue, such as that caused by too much nitrogen fertilizer, are particularly susceptible.

## Natural control methods

### 1. Stripping and cutting off

For light infestations, wipe off the lice with your fingers (wearing gloves) or wipe with a damp cloth. Cut off and dispose of severely affected shoot tips. With herbs like basil or parsley, that's often enough.

### 2. Powerful water jet

Spray robust plants on balconies and terraces with a hard jet of water. The lice are washed away and often cannot find their way back. Rinse houseplants in the shower. Repeat several times at intervals of two to three days. Be careful with delicate flowers.

### 3. Soft soap solution

The classic natural method: Dissolve 15 to 20 ml of pure soft soap or potassium soap without fragrances in one liter of warm water. Allow to cool and spray the affected parts of the plant thoroughly, especially the undersides of the leaves. The soap destroys the protective layer and blocks the lice's breathing openings.

- Repeat every three to four days until no more lice are visible.
- Test on a leaf first.
- Do not spray in direct sunlight, preferably in the evening.
- For herbs, wash thoroughly before harvesting.

Commercially available products based on potassium soap are also available and approved as plant protection products.

### 4. Nettle extract

A cold water extract from nettles is traditionally used against aphids. To do this, let 100 g of fresh nettles steep in a liter of water for 12 to 24 hours, strain and spray undiluted on the lice. The effect is rather weak and is particularly suitable for light infestations. Not to be confused with fermented nettle manure, which serves as fertilizer and has a strong smell.

### 5. Oil preparations

Commercial rapeseed oil-based preparations suffocate lice. They are approved for many ornamental and useful plants. Pay attention to package information and waiting times until harvest.

## Beneficial insects: the natural enemies

Beneficial insects are the best allies on balconies and terraces. A single ladybug eats several thousand aphids over the course of its life.

| Beneficial | Effect |
|---|---|
| Ladybugs and their larvae | Eat large quantities of aphids |
| Lacewing larvae (“aphid lions”) | Very voracious, commercially available |
| Hoverfly larvae | If lice eat, the adult flies pollinate flowers |
| Parasitic wasps | Lay eggs in aphids, recognizable by brown, bloated “mummies” |
| Earworms | Eat aphids at night |
| Birds such as titmice and sparrows | Feed their young insects |

### Promote beneficial insects

- **No chemical insecticides:** They also kill beneficial insects.
- **Flowering plants:** Hoverflies and parasitic wasps need nectar and pollen. See [Bee-Friendly Balcony](/ratgeber/bienenfreundlicher-balkon/).
- **Shelter:** A small insect hotel or an upside-down clay pot filled with wood shavings for earwigs.
- **Patience:** Beneficial insects often arrive a few days after the lice. Anyone who sprays immediately will drive them away.

### Buy beneficial insects

Lacewing larvae, ladybird larvae and parasitic wasps can be ordered for winter gardens, greenhouses and balconies. In living spaces they are particularly useful with many plants.

## Ants and aphids

Ants “milk” aphids, eat their honeydew, and defend them against ladybugs and other enemies. Many ants on a plant are therefore an indication of lice. A ring of glue around the pot or trunk or a moat under the pot (saucer in a larger bowl of water) keeps ants away.

## Prevention

- **Fertilize moderately:** Too much nitrogen promotes soft, vulnerable tissue. See [Fertilizing Houseplants](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-duengen/).
- **Correct location:** Stressed plants in incorrect locations are more vulnerable.
- **Mixed culture:** Herbs such as lavender, thyme, sage and nasturtium can distract lice from neighboring plants. Nasturtiums actually attract lice and serve as a catch plant.
- **Regular inspection:** Look at shoot tips and buds twice a week in spring.
- **Airy position:** Do not plant too closely.

## Aphids on herbs and vegetables

You should be careful with basil, parsley, lettuce or peppers on the balcony if you eat them later. Preference will be given to:

1. Strip off and cut off affected shoots.
2. Rinse off with water.
3. Soft soap solution, followed by thorough washing before consumption.
4. Promote beneficial insects.

More about growing in [Caring for basil](/ratgeber/basilikum-pflegen/) and [Lettuce on the balcony](/ratgeber/salat-auf-dem-balkon/).

## Practical example: The rose pot

In May, on a roof terrace, all the buds of a container rose were covered with green aphids, and ants were running through them. The owner sprayed the lice with a hard jet of water twice, three days apart, placed a ring of glue around the bucket and planted marigolds and nasturtiums next to it. A week later she discovered the first ladybird larvae and hoverflies. After three weeks the lice had largely disappeared and the rose was blooming without the need for a single spray.

| 6 | Second treatment with soft soap solution if necessary |
| 10 | control; Observe beneficial insects and do not disturb them |
| 14 | Final check; Check fertilizer and location |

It is important not to spray immediately if there are already many beneficial insects on the plant. Ladybug larvae, which look like small, black and orange crocodiles, are often mistaken for pests. However, they are the most effective aphid hunters around. Hoverfly larvae, small, maggot-like, translucent animals between the lice, should also be spared.

## The most common aphid species

Not all aphids are the same. There are hundreds of species in Central Europe alone, and you will come across some of them particularly often on balconies and windowsills. The green peach aphid is an omnivore and attacks houseplants, herbs and vegetables alike. The black bean louse forms dense, dark colonies on beans, nasturtiums and many flowers. You can recognize the mealy cabbage aphid by its grey-white, waxy coating on cabbage and kohlrabi, and on roses there are often large, green or reddish rose aphids on the buds.

A special group are root lice. They live invisibly on the roots, for example of lettuce or house plants, and are usually only noticed when repotting due to the whitish, waxy coating on the roots. The affected plant takes care of, even though the light, water and fertilizer are correct. The only thing that helps here is to rinse the roots thoroughly and place the plant in fresh soil.

## Nasturtium as an attractant plant

Nasturtiums almost magically attract black aphids. Some gardeners use this specifically: a pot of nasturtiums a little away from the sensitive plants attracts the lice, and the colonies that form there in turn attract ladybirds, lacewings and hoverflies, which then attack lice on other plants.

This method is not without risk, as a heavily infested decoy plant can also become a source of new lice. Therefore, check them regularly and cut off heavily infested shoots before winged lice emerge and move on.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do coffee grounds or garlic help against aphids?

Their effect has not been reliably proven. Rinsing, soft soap and beneficial insects are significantly more effective.

### Are aphids dangerous on houseplants?

They weaken the plant, but are easy to control. Mealybugs and spider mites are more dangerous. See [mealybugs and mealybugs](/ratgeber/wollaeuse-und-schmierlaeuse/) and [fighting spider mites](/ratgeber/spinnmilben-bekaempfen/).

### Why do lice come back every year?

Aphids overwinter as eggs on trees or fly to them in spring. An annual infestation is normal, what matters is how quickly the beneficial insects follow.

### Can I move ladybugs from the garden to the balcony?

Adult ladybugs usually fly away quickly. The larvae stay better and also eat more lice. You can buy larvae of the domestic two-spotted ladybird from specialist retailers. Pay attention to native species, as the Asian lady beetle is an introduced species that displaces native lady beetles.

### Why do some aphids have wings?

When a colony becomes too dense or the host plant deteriorates, winged lice emerge and colonize new plants. For some species, flight is also part of the annual cycle because they alternate between a winter and a summer host plant. Winged lice are therefore a sign to act quickly before they move on.

### Can I just use dishwashing liquid instead of soft soap?

Better not. Many dishwashing detergents contain fragrances, dyes and strong surfactants that can damage the leaves. Use pure potassium soap-based soft soap without additives or a potassium soap-based plant protection product from a specialist retailer and test the solution on a single leaf first.

If the infestation is severe, repeat the treatment after a few days, as the solution only works on the lice that it directly wets.

## Conclusion

Aphids can be fought naturally: wipe them off, rinse them, treat them with a soft soap solution and, above all, encourage beneficial insects such as ladybirds, lacewings and hoverflies. Moderate fertilization, regular inspection and diverse planting prevent a small infestation from becoming a nuisance.
