The Monstera, also known as window leaf, is one of the most popular houseplants ever. Its large, deeply slotted leaves immediately bring a jungle feel to the home, and it is considered hardy and forgiving. Nevertheless, it has clear preferences: If you keep it too dark, water it too much or never repot it, you will get a plant with small, unslit leaves, long shoots and yellow spots.

This guide explains step by step what a Monstera needs: location and light, watering, soil and fertilizer, climbing aid, repotting, pruning and solving the most common problems.

## Profile

| feature | Monstera deliciosa |
|---|---|
| Origin | Tropical rainforests of Central America |
| growth | Climbing plant with aerial roots |
| Light | Bright, without the blazing midday sun |
| Temperature | 18 to 25°C, not below 15°C |
| Pouring | When the top 3 to 5 cm of soil is dry |
| Difficulty | Easy |
| Toxicity | Mildly toxic to humans and pets (mouth irritation) |

## The right location

In its homeland, the Monstera climbs up trees, from the shady forest floor into the brighter light. In the apartment she therefore needs **a lot of bright but indirect light.**

- **Ideal:** one to two meters from an east or west window, or on a south window behind a light curtain.
- **Too dark:** The new leaves remain small and unslit, the stems become long and thin, the plant leans towards the window.
- **Too much sun:** Direct midday sun behind glass causes brown, dry burn marks.
- **Temperature:** Normal room temperature is perfect. Avoid cold drafts and heating air directly on the radiator.

You can find out more about how to estimate the amount of light in your location in [Light for Houseplants](/ratgeber/licht-fuer-zimmerpflanzen/).

## Pouring

The Monstera likes consistent moisture, but not wet feet. The most common cause of death is too much water.

1. **Finger test:** Only water when the top 3 to 5 cm of soil is dry.
2. **Watering thoroughly:** Add enough water until it runs out of the bottom of the pot.
3. **Remove excess:** After 15 minutes, drain the water from the saucer or planter.
4. **Rare in winter:** With less light and lower temperature, the plant uses less water.

As a rough guide: about once a week in summer, every two to three weeks in winter, depending on pot size and location. Fixed watering days are less reliable than finger testing. See [Watering houseplants properly](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-richtig-giessen/).

## Soil and pot

- **Soil:** A loose, well-drained mix. Houseplant soil with a third of coarse components such as pine bark, perlite or pumice has proven to work well. This way the roots get enough air.
- **Pot:** Always with a drain hole. A planter without a hole is fine if it doesn't retain water.

More about substrates in [potting soil and substrate](/ratgeber/blumenerde-und-substrat/).

## Fertilize

From spring to late summer, i.e. during the growing season, fertilize every two to four weeks with a liquid fertilizer for green plants in the specified dosage. Not or only very sparingly in autumn and winter. Newly repotted plants do not need fertilizer for the first two to three months.

## Moss stick and climbing aid

As a climbing plant, the Monstera grows more vigorously and produces larger leaves if it can hold on.

- **Moss stick or coconut stick:** Insert into the pot and loosely tie the shoots to it.
- **Aerial Roots:** The long, brown roots that grow from the trunk can be directed into the soil or around the moss stick. Cutting is possible, but not necessary.
- **Keep moss stick moist:** A slightly moistened moss stick invites the aerial roots to grow in.

## Why doesn't my Monstera have holes?

The typical slits and holes only form on **more mature plants** and with **sufficient light.** Young plants have heart-shaped, entire leaves. If an older plant continues to produce unslit leaves, it is usually too dark. A moss stick and regular fertilizer during the growing season also encourage larger, slitted leaves.

## Repotting

Every year or two in spring when the roots fill the pot or grow out the bottom.

1. New pot: two to four centimeters larger in diameter.
2. Carefully remove the plant from the old pot and loosely shake off the old soil.
3. Cut off rotten, brown and mushy roots.
4. Place in fresh soil, no deeper than before.
5. Pour on and place in a bright place without sun.

You can find the detailed instructions in [Repotting step by step](/ratgeber/umtopfen-anleitung/).

## Pruning and propagation

If the Monstera has become too large, you can cut back long shoots above a node. The cut pieces with at least one node and one aerial root become new plants. How to do this is written in [Propagate Monstera](/ratgeber/monstera-vermehren/).

**Note:** The plant sap can irritate skin. Wear gloves when cutting.

## Typical problems

| Symptom | Possible cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Yellow leaves, soil wet for a long time | Too much water, risk of root rot | Water less, check roots |
| Brown, crispy leaf edges | Air that is too dry or watering irregularly | Water more evenly, increase humidity |
| Brown spots in the middle of the leaf | Sunburn | Away from direct midday sun |
| Small, unslit new leaves | Too little light | Make it brighter |
| Water drops on leaf edges | Guttation in moist soil | Safe, water less |
| Fine webs, bright dots | Spider mites | See [Fighting spider mites](/ratgeber/spinnmilben-bekaempfen/) |

The guide [yellow leaves: causes] (/ratgeber/gelbe-blaetter-ursachen/) also helps with yellow leaves.

## Care throughout the year

| time | Care |
|---|---|
| Spring | Repot, start fertilizing, replace the moss rod |
| Summer | Water and fertilize regularly and protect from direct sun |
| Autumn | Stop fertilizing, reduce watering |
| Winter | Brightest place, water sparingly, distance from heating |

## Practical example: The Monstera in the hallway

A Monstera stood in a hallway with a small north-facing window for two years. It only formed small leaves without slits and the stems became longer and longer. After moving into the living room, one and a half meters in front of a large west window, with a new moss stick and fertilizer from April, it unfolded three large leaves with the first slits in the summer. The old, small leaves remained, but the plant soon appeared strong and healthy again.

## Monstera varieties in stores

| variety | feature | Care instructions |
|---|---|---|
| Monstera deliciosa | Large, slotted leaves | The classic needs space |
| Monstera adansonii | Smaller leaves with holes instead of slits | Vines or hangs, likes slightly higher humidity |
| Monstera deliciosa “Thai Constellation” | Speckled cream color | Needs more light, grows slower |
| Monstera deliciosa “Albo” | White variegated | Expensive, delicate, white leaf parts turn brown easily |

Variegated Monsteras are significantly more demanding than the green form. White leaf surfaces do not contain chlorophyll, so the plant grows slower and needs a brighter location. At the same time, the white parts burn faster in the sun. A bright place with filtered light is ideal.

## Aerial roots: cut or leave?

The brown, strand-like aerial roots that grow from the stems of the Monstera are completely normal. In nature, they hold the plant to tree trunks and absorb water and nutrients. In your home you can guide them into the ground, tie them to a moss stick or simply leave them hanging.

If individual aerial roots are causing problems, you can cut them off with a clean, sharp knife without damaging the plant. But don't remove all of them, because they help the Monstera climb and support large shoots. Aerial roots that you direct into a moist moss stick often ensure particularly strong growth and larger leaves.

## Yellow leaves on the Monstera

It is normal for an old lower leaf to turn yellow and die every now and then, as long as new leaves appear on top. However, if several leaves turn yellow at the same time, too much water is usually the cause. Check the soil: If it is still wet deep down, let it dry thoroughly and check the roots if it smells rotten.

Yellow leaves with brown, soft spots also indicate root problems. On the other hand, if the leaves become pale and light green overall, there is a lack of light or nutrients. A brighter place and regular fertilization during the growing season will help.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Monstera poisonous?

It contains calcium oxalate crystals, which irritate the mouth and throat when chewed. Keep out of reach of children and pets. See [Plant Safety](/pflanzensicherheit/).

### How big does a Monstera get?

In the apartment it can grow to be several meters high and wide. It can be kept in good shape by pruning.

### Should I wipe the leaves?

Yes, dust on the large leaves reduces light absorption. Wipe with a damp cloth every few weeks.

### Can the Monstera go outside in summer?

In a protected, partially shaded place, yes, from stable night temperatures above 15 °C. Get used to the light slowly and protect yourself from wind.

### Why is my Monstera dripping?

Water droplets on the edges or tips of leaves are caused by guttation. If the soil is very moist and the air can hardly absorb water, the plant actively squeezes excess water out of the leaves. This is not dangerous, but it shows that the earth has plenty of water. If the Monstera drips regularly, water more sparingly.

### Why is my Monstera getting brown edges?

Common causes include dry heating air, irregular watering with long dry periods, too much fertilizer or cold drafts. First check the watering rhythm and location.

### Can I shorten a Monstera that has grown too large?

Yes. Use a clean knife to cut off the main stem above a node. The cut head piece with at least one node and, if possible, an aerial root becomes a new plant, and the mother plant sprouts again from one of the remaining nodes.

Place the headpiece in water or moist moss to root. Our guide to propagating Monstera explains exactly how this works.

## Conclusion

The Monstera is easy to care for if it is placed in a bright place without direct sun, is only watered when the top centimeters of soil are dry, is given loose soil and a pot with a drainage hole, and is fertilized during the growing season. A moss stick, regular repotting and patience will reward you with large, slitted leaves.
