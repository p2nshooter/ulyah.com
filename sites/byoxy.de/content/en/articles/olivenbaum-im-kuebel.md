An olive tree brings a piece of the Mediterranean to the balcony and terrace. With its silvery-green leaves, gnarled trunk and calmness, it appears elegant, and many people associate it with vacation and sun. Olive trees are long-lived and slow-growing, and a well-cared-for tree can thrive in a container for many years.

However, in our climate, it is not possible without knowledge. The olive tree needs a lot of sun, well-drained soil, the right amount of water and, above all, a well-planned winter. This guide explains step by step how to care for, cut and overwinter your olive tree in a pot, and what is behind typical problems such as leaf fall.

## The right location

Olive trees are sun worshipers. In summer they belong in the sunniest place that the balcony or terrace offers, ideally facing south or southwest and protected from the wind in front of a house wall that radiates additional heat. The more sun the tree gets, the more compact it grows and the greater the chance of flowers.

Although the olive tree grows in partially shaded places, it becomes light and sparse, loses leaves more easily and hardly blooms. It is therefore not suitable for a north-facing balcony. He also doesn't particularly like constant rain because wet soil in the pot quickly leads to waterlogging; a spot under an eaves is ideal.

If you buy an olive tree in spring, you slowly get it used to full sun. Plants that come from the greenhouse or winter quarters will otherwise get sunburn: the leaves become light, spotty and fall off.

## bucket and soil

The bucket should have a large drainage hole and be only slightly larger than the root ball. A pot that is too large with a lot of soil will stay wet for a long time and increases the risk of root rot. Terracotta is breathable and stable, plastic is lighter and retains moisture longer. There are small feet under the bucket so that excess water can drain away.

Olive trees need permeable, rather poor soil. A structurally stable container plant soil that you mix with about a third of mineral material such as expanded clay, lava, pumice or coarse sand has proven to be effective. A drainage layer made of expanded clay or clay shards at the bottom prevents the drain hole from becoming blocked. You can find out more about choosing soil in the guide [potting soil and substrate] (/ratgeber/blumenerde-und-substrat/).

## Pouring

Olive trees are naturally adapted to drought, but in the pot they need regular water because the root space is limited. The rule is: water thoroughly, then allow the top layer of soil to dry.

- **Summer:** Check the soil with your finger. If it is dry a few centimeters deep, water until water runs out the bottom. On hot days this may be necessary every day for small tubs.
- **No coaster full of water:** Dump standing water after half an hour.
- **Spring and autumn:** Water significantly less because less water evaporates.
- **Winter:** only enough so that the bale does not dry out completely. In cool winter quarters, a small amount of water every two to four weeks is often enough.

Both drought stress and waterlogging lead to leaf fall. When it is dry, the leaves first curl up and become dull; when they are waterlogged, they turn yellow and fall off, even though the soil is wet. You can find general tips in the guide [Balcony irrigation](/ratgeber/balkon-bewaesserung/).

## Fertilize

From March to August, fertilize the olive tree about every two to four weeks with a fertilizer for Mediterranean potted plants or a liquid complete fertilizer in the recommended dosage. From the end of August, stop fertilizing so that the young shoots mature and become more resilient before winter. Too much nitrogen makes the shoots soft and long and reduces flower production.

It is only repotted every few years in spring when the roots fill the pot. For large, heavy trees it is often enough to replace the top layer of soil.

## Cutting

Olive trees tolerate pruning well, and regular pruning keeps them compact. The best time is early spring, when severe frosts are no longer expected and the tree begins to sprout.

- **Thinning:** Remove branches that are growing inward, crossing, or rubbing against each other to allow light and air into the crown.
- **Shape:** Shorten shoots that are too long to create a compact, harmonious crown.
- **Remove water shoots:** Cut off strong, vertical shoots on the trunk or from the root area directly at the base.
- **Remove dead wood:** You can cut out dead branches at any time.

Olive trees bloom on shoots that grew the previous year. If you shorten all the shoots every year, you will prevent flowering. A moderate cut, in which some of the previous year's shoots remain, combines shape and flowering.

## Flowers and fruits

In late spring or early summer, olive trees produce small, creamy white, fragrant flowers in clusters. In order for them to bloom at all, olive trees need a cool phase in winter. Trees that have overwintered in a warm living room often do not bloom.

Whether olives develop afterwards depends on the variety, pollination and the summer. Many varieties also bear fruit on their own, usually more with a second tree nearby. In our climate, the fruits in the bucket often do not fully ripen. Important to know: Freshly picked olives are inedibly bitter even when ripe. They must first be debittered in water and brine for weeks before they can be eaten. For most balcony gardeners, the olive tree is primarily an ornamental tree.

## Overwintering: the most important question

Winter decides whether an olive tree will thrive in a pot. Planted, older trees can tolerate short-term light frosts in mild regions. The situation is different in the pot: the roots are unprotected there, and a frozen ball can seriously damage the tree. Basically you have two options.

### Inside the house: bright and cool

The safest solution is a bright, cool room with temperatures of around five to ten degrees, for example an unheated stairwell with a window, a cool winter garden or a bright guest room. Only clear the tree when severe night frosts are forecast. Slight cold in autumn hardens it and promotes later flowering.

In winter quarters, water sparingly, do not fertilize and check regularly for pests. A warm, dark living room, on the other hand, is unsuitable: the combination of heat and little light leads to leaf fall, pest infestation and no flowers. The guide [Overwintering potted plants] (/ratgeber/kuebelpflanzen-ueberwintern/) describes more options.

### Outside with protection

In mild wine-growing regions and in cities with a protected microclimate, some gardeners overwinter their olive trees outside. This can only be achieved with good protection and is always associated with a residual risk:

- Place the bucket close to a protective wall of the house, if possible under an eaves.
- Place the bucket on an insulating base made of wood or Styrofoam and wrap it thickly with jute, coconut mats or bubble wrap.
- In severe frosts, cover the crown with a breathable winter fleece, but not with plastic film, under which moisture accumulates.
- Water moderately on frost-free days, because the evergreen leaves evaporate water even in winter.

If there are long periods of severe frost, the tree should still be temporarily moved to a protected, cool room.

### Clear out in spring

Clear out the olive tree in spring when severe night frosts are no longer expected. In the first week or two, slowly get him used to the sun in a partially shaded spot, then he can go to his summer spot.

## Typical problems

- **Leaf fall in winter:** usually too warm and too dark, rarely dry or waterlogged. Place cooler and brighter, check watering.
- **Yellow leaves:** often waterlogged, rarely nutrient deficiency. Check the vent hole and coaster.
- **Rolled, dull leaves:** Lack of water. Water thoroughly.
- **Round, dark spots with a yellowish area:** This so-called peacock eye disease is a fungal disease that occurs in damp weather. Remove affected leaves, place the tree in an airy place and avoid watering over the foliage.
- **Pests:** Scale insects, mealy bugs and spider mites often appear in winter quarters. Check the undersides of leaves and shoots regularly. See [Combat Mealybugs and Mealybugs](/ratgeber/wollaeuse-und-schmierlaeuse/) and [Combat Spider Mites](/ratgeber/spinnmilben-bekaempfen/).

## What you should pay attention to when buying

Olive trees range from small, young plants to very old specimens with thick, gnarled trunks. Ancient trees are often dug up from plantations in the Mediterranean, with much of the roots lost. They then need time to recover and are sensitive to care errors. Younger trees that have been grown in pots usually adapt better.

Look for healthy, dense foliage without spots, clean shoots without pests and a firm root ball. In the European Union, olive trees are among the plants that can only be traded with a plant passport due to dangerous plant diseases. Reputable dealers can prove this to you.

## Practical example: The olive tree in the living room

A reader brought her olive tree into the warm living room in the first winter so that it would be “nice and cozy”. By January it had lost most of its leaves, and the shoots had sticky spots and small brown shields: scale insects.

In the second winter, the tree instead stood in the bright, unheated stairwell at around eight degrees. It was only watered moderately every three weeks and checked regularly. It retained almost all of its leaves, and small, fragrant flowers appeared for the first time the following May. The difference lay solely in the temperature and light of the winter quarters.

## Frequently asked questions

### How old can an olive tree grow in a pot?

Olive trees are among the longest-lived trees of all. Even in a pot, a well-cared-for tree can live for many decades if it regularly receives fresh soil, enough light and suitable winter quarters.

### Can I keep my olive tree indoors all year round?

This is difficult in the long run. In the apartment he misses the sun in the summer and the cool rest period in the winter. A summer outside and a bright, cool winter spot are much better.

### Why isn't my olive tree blooming?

Usually the cool winter phase is missing, or it is too shady in summer. Pruning the previous year's shoots too severely also prevents flowering. Young trees also need a few years before they bloom reliably.

### Are olive trees poisonous?

No, olive trees are considered non-toxic to people and pets. However, raw olives are inedible because of their bitter substances.

## Conclusion

An olive tree in a pot needs three things above all: lots of sun in summer, permeable soil without waterlogging and a bright, cool winter. Water thoroughly and let the soil dry out in between, fertilize until August, prune moderately in spring and check the tree for pests in winter quarters. Anyone who respects the cool rest period will be rewarded with a healthy, densely leafy tree and, with a bit of luck, fragrant flowers.
