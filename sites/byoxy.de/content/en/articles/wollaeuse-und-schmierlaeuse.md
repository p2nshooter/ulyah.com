White, cottony flakes in the leaf axils, sticky leaves and a plant that is careless despite good care: these are typical signs of mealybugs, also known as mealybugs. These scale insect relatives are among the most persistent pests on houseplants because they hide well and are protected by their wax coating. With perseverance and the right methods, they can still be removed permanently.

This guide shows you how to recognize mealybugs, which plants are particularly at risk, which control methods work and how you can prevent a new infestation.

## Recognize mealybugs

| feature | Description |
|---|---|
| size | 2 to 5 millimeters |
| Appearance | Oval, pink to gray bodies covered with white, waxy powder |
| wax threads | White, cottony excreta containing eggs and young animals |
| Whereabouts | Leaf axils, undersides of leaves, shoot tips, under leaf sheaths, on roots |
| Honeydew | Sticky excrement on leaves and furniture |
| sooty mold | Black fungal coating on the honeydew |

The term mealybug is often used synonymously. Strictly speaking, both belong to the Pseudococcidae family. The fight is identical.

### What they do

Mealybugs bite the plant and suck plant sap. They release saliva, which disrupts growth. Typical damage:

- Yellow, stunted or falling leaves
- Stunting growth, especially at shoot tips
- Sticky honeydew and black sooty mold, which hinders photosynthesis
- If the infestation is severe, the plant dies

### Endangered plants

- Cacti and succulents, especially echeveria, aloe and money tree
- Orchids, especially in the leaf axils and on flower spikes
- Rubber tree and birch fig
- Monstera, Philodendron and Money Plant
- citrus plants
- Hoya, croton and palm trees

## Why mealybugs are so stubborn

1. **Wax layer:** The white wax powder protects the animals from many pesticides and water.
2. **Hidden:** They sit in leaf axils, under leaf sheaths, in cracks and on roots where sprays cannot reach.
3. **Eggs in the wax nest:** A female lays several hundred eggs in the protective cotton wool.
4. **Rapid propagation:** At room temperature, one generation takes about four to six weeks, generations overlap.
5. **Wandering:** Young lice crawl on neighboring plants.

## Immediate measures

1. **Isolate:** Immediately separate affected plants from others.
2. **Check all plants:** Especially the leaf axils and undersides of neighboring plants.
3. **Cut off severely infested parts** and dispose of them in a sealed bag.

## Control methods

### Dab with alcohol

The most effective mechanical method: Dip a cotton swab in spirit or isopropyl alcohol (70 percent) and dab every visible louse along with the cotton. The alcohol dissolves the wax layer and kills the animals. Then wipe off the residue with a damp cloth.

- Particularly suitable for smaller plants, orchids and succulents.
- Test on a leaf first, sensitive leaves can get spots.
- Repeat every three to four days because new lice always appear from hiding places and eggs.

### Wash up and shower

Rinse robust plants vigorously with lukewarm water to remove lice and cotton wool. Make sure that succulents and orchids are dried well so that there is no water in the rosettes and leaf axils.

### Spirit soap solution

A self-mixed solution of one liter of water, 15 ml of soft soap or potassium soap and 15 ml of spirit is sprayed onto the affected parts of the plant, especially in the leaf axils and on the undersides of the leaves. The soap sticks the breathing openings together, the alcohol dissolves the wax. Repeat every three to five days for at least three to four weeks. Test on a leaf first. Do not use on plants with a waxy layer such as echeverias, otherwise they will lose their natural powder.

### Oil preparations

Commercial paraffin oil or rapeseed oil preparations suffocate lice and eggs. Spray according to package instructions, not in direct sunlight, and repeat several times.

### Systemic means

Plant protection sticks with systemic active ingredients are absorbed through the roots and also reach hidden lice, which absorb the active ingredient when sucking. They are an option for stubborn infestations. Follow the instructions for use and consider using in households with children and pets.

### Beneficial insects

Beneficial insects can be used in winter gardens or with many plants:

| Beneficial | Effect | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| Australian ladybird (Cryptolaemus montrouzieri) | Larvae and beetles eat mealybugs | Above 20°C, lots of light |
| Lacewing larvae (Chrysoperla carnea) | Eat various pests | Above 12°C |
| Parasitic wasps (Leptomastix) | Parasitize certain species of mealybugs | Above 22°C |

## Root lice

A particularly insidious variant are root lice, which live on the roots. Signs: The plant takes care of itself without any visible pests above ground, and when it is potted out, white, waxy spots are found on the roots and the edge of the pot. Cacti and succulents are particularly affected.

This is how you do it:

1. Unpot the plant and remove all soil.
2. Rinse roots thoroughly under lukewarm water.
3. Cut off severely affected roots.
4. If necessary, dip the roots in a soap solution for a few minutes and then rinse.
5. Clean the pot with hot water or use a new one.
6. Place in fresh soil. Dispose of the old soil in household waste.

More about the procedure in [Repotting step by step](/ratgeber/umtopfen-anleitung/).

## Treatment plan

| week | Measures |
|---|---|
| 1 | Isolate, remove heavily infested parts, shower, dab all lice with alcohol |
| 1 to 4 | Dab or spray every 3 to 5 days and target hiding places |
| 2 | Check roots and repot if infested |
| 5 to 6 | Weekly check; If there is a new infestation, restart treatment |
| 8 | If lice are no longer visible for several weeks, put the plant back |

## Prevention

- **New plants in quarantine:** Keep them separate for two to four weeks and check thoroughly, including the roots.
- **Regular inspection:** View leaf axils and undersides weekly.
- **Strong plants:** Plants with too much nitrogen and soft tissue are more vulnerable. See [Fertilizing Houseplants](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-duengen/).
- **Use clean tools and pots**.
- **Observe ants:** Ants protect lice because they eat honeydew.

## Practical example: The orchid windowsill

On a windowsill with six phalaenopsis, white cotton flakes appeared on a flower spike. Upon closer inspection, mealybugs were also found in the leaf axils and on the underside of the leaves of three other plants. All orchids were separated, the lice were removed with a cotton swab dipped in alcohol and the plants were then checked every four days. New lice were swabbed immediately. One plant also had lice on the aerial roots in the pot; she was repotted. After six weeks without any new finds, the orchids were allowed back on the windowsill.

## Mealybugs, scale insects or mold?

| feature | Mealybugs | Scale insects | White mold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Appearance | White, cottony flakes, oval powdered animals | Brown or gray, solid bumps | Fluffy, even coating |
| movement | Young animals crawl | Adult animals are stuck | None |
| Location | Leaf axils, undersides of leaves, roots | Stems, leaf veins, undersides of leaves | Earth's surface, dead tissue |
| Honeydew | Yes | Yes | No |
| Treatment | Alcohol, soap, oil | Scrape, oil preparations | Remove, keep dry |

Mealybugs can be easily seen with a magnifying glass: oval, segmented animals with fine wax threads on the edge sit under the cotton wool. Scale insects, on the other hand, look like small, immobile scales that can be lifted off with a fingernail. Mold has no structure and can be smudged away. See also [Mold on potting soil](/ratgeber/schimmel-auf-blumenerde/).

## Mealybugs on cacti and succulents

Mealybugs hide particularly cleverly in cacti and succulents: between the thorns, in the areoles, deep in the leaf axils of echeverias and at the base of rosettes. A cotton swab often can't reach there. A fine brush that you dip in alcohol or a spirit soap solution is more suitable. When repotting, also check the roots, as root lice are common on cacti.

Be careful with succulents with bluish leaves, such as many echeverias. The fine wax layer protects the leaves from the sun and can be permanently damaged by wiping or using oily preparations. For these plants, only treat the affected areas specifically and test each treatment on a single leaf first.

## Ants as helpers of lice

Mealybugs and mealybugs, like aphids, secrete sweet honeydew, and ants value this food. They therefore protect the lice from predators and sometimes even carry them to new plants. On the balcony and terrace, it's worth interrupting ant trails to potted plants, for example with a glue ring on the trunk if you're fighting lice. Ants are less likely to play a role in the home.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are mealybugs dangerous to humans?

No, they only harm plants.

### Why do the lice keep coming back?

Eggs usually survive in hiding places or lice survive on the roots. The treatment must be repeated consistently over several weeks.

### Can I save a severely infested plant?

Often yes. If the plant is very badly infested, it may make more sense to root healthy shoot tips as cuttings and dispose of the mother plant. See [Cuttings in a Water Jar](/ratgeber/stecklinge-im-wasserglas/).

### Can I put infected plants on the balcony?

In summer, natural enemies outside and rain occasionally help to contain the infestation. At the same time, the lice can spread to other potted plants. What is more important than the location is that the affected plant is separated from the others and treated consistently.

### How do I know that the treatment has worked?

If no new white cotton balls appear over several weeks and the leaves no longer become sticky. Check leaf axils, shoot tips and root necks every week for six to eight weeks, as individual overlooked females can trigger a new infestation.

### Do yellow plates help against mealybugs?

Barely. The female mealybugs that feed on plants have no wings and stay in place. Only the short-lived males fly. Yellow signs therefore only indicate that mealybugs are nearby and do not replace treatment of the plant.

A quick look with a magnifying glass at the leaf axils and root neck will tell you more than any trap.

## Conclusion

Mealybugs hide well and are protected by their wax layer. Isolate infected plants, swab visible lice with alcohol, treat repeatedly over several weeks with soap or oil preparations, check the roots and keep new plants in quarantine. Consistency is the key to success.
