A white, fluffy coating on the potting soil is a frightening moment for many people. Is it dangerous? Does the plant have to go? In most cases the answer is: no. Superficial mold on potting soil is common and usually harmless to the plant, but it is a clear sign that something is wrong in the pot. And not all white deposits are mold: they are often lime or fertilizer salts.

This guide explains how to distinguish mold from limescale, why mold occurs, how to remove it and how to prevent it permanently.

## Mold or lime?

| feature | Mold | Lime or fertilizer salts |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Downy, cotton-like, thread-like, sometimes greenish, yellow or gray | Hard, crusty, grainy, white to yellowish |
| Feel | Soft, crumbles into dust or wipes off | Hard, scratchy, can be scraped off |
| Location | On the surface of the earth, often in damp areas | Soil surface and edge of pot, especially in clay pots |
| smell | Often musty, musty | Odorless |
| reaction to water | Don't disappear | Partially dissolves, comes back after drying |
| Cause | Moisture, organic components, little air movement | Hard water, fertilizer, evaporation |

A simple test: Remove a little of the coating with a wooden stick and rub it between your fingers. If it falls apart and becomes soft and dusty, it is mold. If it crunches like sand, it's salts.

### Mushroom network in new soil

Sometimes a white fungal network appears in freshly purchased soil, especially in peat-free soil with wood fibers or bark humus. These are often natural soil fungi that decompose organic material. They are generally harmless and disappear when the soil dries out a little.

## Why does mold appear on earth?

Mold spores are everywhere in the air. They germinate when they find good conditions:

- **Permanently moist soil surface** due to too frequent watering
- **Low light** and therefore low water consumption by the plant
- **Poor air circulation**, such as in corners, behind curtains or in groups of closely packed plants
- **Organic material** on the surface: coffee grounds, fallen leaves, pieces of bark, compost
- **Cool temperatures** in winter
- **Soil of poor quality** or soil already contaminated with spores
- **Pots that are too big** and a lot of soil stays wet for a long time

## Is mold dangerous?

### For the plant

Superficial mold usually does not directly harm the plant. However, it indicates conditions that also promote root rot and fungus gnats. If the mold runs deep into the soil, it smells musty, and the plant has yellow leaves, you should check the roots. See [Detect and Save Root Rot](/ratgeber/wurzelfaeule-erkennen-und-retten/).

### For people

Mold spores can cause problems in sensitive people, allergy sufferers, asthmatics or people with weakened immune systems. A small deposit on a flower pot is usually not a problem for healthy people, but should not be ignored. It is better to act quickly in bedrooms and for sensitive people.

## Remove mold

### Mild infestation

1. **It's best to work outside or at an open window** so that no spores are spread around the apartment. Maybe wear a mask.
2. **Remove the top layer:** Carefully remove the top one to two centimeters of soil with a spoon and dispose of it in a closed bag with household waste.
3. **Fill with fresh soil** or better yet, apply a layer of mineral material such as quartz sand, pumice or lava granules. It dries quickly and does not provide a breeding ground for mold.
4. **Change watering behavior:** Allow the soil to dry out more before the next watering.

### Heavy infestation

If the mold reaches deep into the soil, the soil smells musty or the mold keeps coming back:

1. Unpot the plant.
2. Remove as much old soil as possible.
3. Check roots and cut off rotten parts.
4. Clean the pot with hot water.
5. Repot in fresh, loose soil. See [Repotting Step by Step](/ratgeber/umtopfen-anleitung/).

### home remedies

Cinnamon, baking powder or chamomile tea are often recommended against mold on potting soil. Cinnamon has a mild anti-fungal effect and can be sprinkled on the surface. However, it does not replace removal and changes to the conditions. Baking soda and vinegar can alter the pH of the soil and should not be used.

## Remove lime and salt deposits

If the topping is hard and crusty:

1. Scrape off the crust with a spoon and replace the top layer of soil.
2. Rinse the soil thoroughly: Slowly pour three to four times the amount of water into the pot in the shower or sink.
3. Wipe the limescale edges of the pot with vinegar water without touching the soil.
4. In the future, use softer water, such as rainwater or filtered water, and fertilize more sparingly. See [Fertilizing Houseplants](/ratgeber/zimmerpflanzen-duengen/).

## Prevention

| Measure | Effect |
|---|---|
| Allow the soil surface to dry between waterings | Removes moisture from mold |
| Pour from below | Surface remains drier |
| Mineral cover (sand, pumice, lava) | Dries quickly, no breeding ground |
| Use good, fresh soil and store it properly | Fewer spores and decomposition processes |
| No coffee grounds and no leftover food on the ground | Less food for mushrooms |
| Remove fallen leaves | Less organic material |
| Ventilate regularly and do not place plants too close together | Better air circulation |
| Matching pot size with drain hole | Less permanently wet soil |
| Bright location | Higher water consumption by the plant |

A layer of sand also helps against fungus gnats, which often occur together with mold. See [Get Rid of Fungus Gnats](/ratgeber/trauermuecken-loswerden/).

## Mold on seeds and cuttings

When growing under covers, the humidity is high and mold is a common problem. Remedy:

- **Air daily:** Remove the cover for a few minutes.
- **Use germ-free potting soil**.
- **Don't keep it too wet.**
- **Remove affected seedlings immediately.**

## Practical example: The ficus in the corner of the room

A ficus stood in the corner of the room behind a curtain, about two meters from the window, and was watered weekly. In winter a white, fluffy coating formed on the earth and there was a slightly musty smell. The top layer of earth was removed outside and replaced with lava granules. The ficus moved to the window and was only watered after a finger test, about every two to three weeks in winter. The mold did not return, and the fungus gnats that had previously appeared occasionally also disappeared.

## Step by step: Renovate the surface

1. **Prepare materials:** Spoon, garbage bag, fresh soil or mineral covering material, possibly gloves and mask.
2. **Go outside** or work at an open window.
3. **Take off the top layer:** Put one to two centimeters, more if the infestation is deeper, directly into the bag and close it.
4. **Wipe the edge of the pot:** With a damp cloth, or with limescale edges with diluted vinegar.
5. **Recover:** With quartz sand, pumice or lava, about an inch thick.
6. **Check location:** Is the plant bright enough? Is it too close to other plants or against a wall?
7. **Adjust watering plan:** From now on, only water using a finger test or wooden stick.

If you follow these steps, in most cases you will no longer see any new mold. If it still comes back after two to three weeks, the problem is deeper in the pot and repotting is the better solution.

## Mold in hydroponics, planters and terrariums

White deposits on expanded clay in hydroponics are usually not mold, but lime and salt deposits from the water and fertilizer. They feel hard and crusty and wash off. Greenish deposits on the inside of transparent vessels are algae that grow in the light. An opaque planter can help here.

In closed terrariums and bottle gardens, mold develops if there is too much water in the glass and the air is stagnant. Then open the lid for a few hours or days to allow excess moisture to escape and remove any affected plant parts. In bioactive terrariums, springtails, tiny soil animals that eat mold and dead material, help.

## Mold on the wall behind plants

Not only the soil, but also the wall behind large plants can become moldy. Cold external walls, in front of which there are dense groups of plants and the air can hardly circulate, are particularly at risk. Many plants together release a considerable amount of water into the indoor air, which condenses in the coolest place.

Therefore, leave a few centimeters of space between the pots and the outside wall, ventilate regularly and check the humidity with a hygrometer. If it is consistently well above sixty percent in winter, it is worth keeping fewer plants in a small space or ventilating them more often.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to throw away a plant with mold on the soil?

No. In most cases it is enough to remove the top layer and adjust the care.

### Can mold transfer to the plant?

Superficial soil mold usually stays on the ground. Gray mold on leaves or stems is a different problem and occurs primarily in high humidity and poor air circulation. Remove affected parts.

### Is yellow mold more dangerous?

Yellow, slimy deposits are often slime molds that are harmless to the plant. They also indicate conditions that are too wet and are removed like mold.

### Does cinnamon help against mold on earth?

Cinnamon is often recommended as a home remedy because it has a mild anti-fungal effect. It can push back a superficial plaque in the short term, but does not eliminate the cause. Economical watering, good ventilation and loose, permeable soil remain crucial.

### Can I compost moldy soil?

Yes, the common white mold on potting soil is made up of harmless fungi that break down organic material and continue their work on the compost. However, you should not reuse the soil for houseplants.

Add them to the compost in the garden or into the organic waste bin.

## Conclusion

White deposits on potting soil are either mold or limescale. You can tell the difference between the two with a simple touch and wipe test. Mold indicates conditions that are too humid: Remove the top layer, cover the soil with minerals, water less often, and provide light and air. You can remove limescale and salt crusts by scraping, rinsing and using softer water. Further: [Potting soil and substrate](/ratgeber/blumenerde-und-substrat/).
