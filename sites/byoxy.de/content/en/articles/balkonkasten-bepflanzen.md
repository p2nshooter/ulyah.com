A lushly flowering balcony box transforms even a small balcony into a green retreat. At the same time, the box is a demanding habitat: little soil, a lot of sun or a lot of shade, wind, heat and sometimes dryness for days. If you assess the location correctly, select the right plants and follow a few basic rules when planting, you will be able to enjoy your boxes until autumn.

This guide shows you how to determine the location, which boxes and which soil are suitable, how to plant step by step, how to combine plants beautifully and how to care for them throughout the season.

## Determine the location

The orientation of the balcony determines the choice of plants.

| Alignment | Hours of sunshine | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| South | 6 to 10 hours, midday sun | Hot, dry, high water requirements |
| West | Afternoon and evening sun | Warm, often very hot in summer |
| East | Morning sun | Mild, ideal for many plants |
| North | Hardly any direct sun | Cool, shady |

Watch your balcony on a sunny day: How many hours does the sun really shine on the boxes? A balcony above you, a tree or neighboring buildings can greatly reduce the hours of sunshine. Wind also plays a role: it is often significantly windier on upper floors, which dries out plants and ruffles flowers.

## Plants by location

| Location | Flowering plants | Foliage and structural plants | Herbs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sunny (South, West) | Geraniums, petunias, magic bells, purslane, verbena, lantana, gazania | Ornamental grasses, silver leaf, sweet potato | Rosemary, thyme, lavender, sage, oregano |
| Partial shade (east) | Fuchsias, lobelias, men's faithfulness, fairy mirrors, begonias | Coleus, ivy, hostas | Parsley, chives, mint |
| Shady (North) | Busy Lizzies, tuberous begonias, fuchsias | Ferns, ivy, purple bells | Mint, woodruff, wild garlic |

## The right box

- **Size:** Better bigger. Boxes that are at least 18 to 20 cm high and deep hold more water and nutrients. Small boxes dry out within a few hours in summer.
- **Extractor holes:** Essential. Without holes, there is water in the box after every rain.
- **Water storage boxes:** Boxes with water reservoirs in the ground supply the plants for several days. Ideal for sunny balconies and people with little time. See [Balcony Irrigation](/ratgeber/balkon-bewaesserung/).
- **Material:** Plastic is light and retains moisture, terracotta is beautiful but heavy and dries out faster, wood insulates well but needs a foil or insert.
- **Fastening:** Boxes on the outside of the parapet must be securely fastened. A box is very heavy with wet soil. Check brackets regularly.

## The right earth

High-quality **balcony or pot plant soil is suitable for balcony boxes.** It is structurally stable, stores water and contains a starting supply of nutrients. Cheap soil collapses and becomes compacted over the course of the summer. For herbs such as rosemary and thyme, thin the soil with sand or pumice. Further information in [Potting soil and substrate](/ratgeber/blumenerde-und-substrat/).

Old soil from the previous year should not be reused; it is leached and can contain pathogens. You can put it in the compost or garden.

## When to plant?

Frost-sensitive summer flowers only come outside **after the Ice Saints, in mid-May**. There is a risk of night frosts beforehand. Spring plantings with pansies, horned violets, primroses and onion flowers are possible from March.

## Planting step by step

1. **Water plants:** Before planting, immerse the root balls of the plants in a bucket of water until no more air bubbles appear.
2. **Drainage:** Put a two to three centimeter thick layer of expanded clay on the bottom of the box, with a fleece on top so that the soil does not trickle into the drainage. This is not the case with water storage boxes.
3. **Fill in soil:** About halfway up.
4. **Arrange plants:** Test-position the plants in their pots until the arrangement works.
5. **Insert:** Unpot the plants, loosen the matted roots slightly and plant them so deep that the top edge of the ball is just below the edge of the box.
6. **Fill and press down:** Fill up the soil between the plants and press down lightly. Leave a pouring rim of about two centimeters at the top.
7. **Watering:** Thoroughly until water runs out of the drainage holes.

### Planting distances

| Box length | Number of plants |
|---|---|
| 60cm | 3 to 4 |
| 80cm | 4 to 5 |
| 100cm | 5 to 6 |

The plants look a bit sparse at first, but grow together quickly. Planting too densely leads to competition for water and nutrients and promotes fungal diseases.

## Design: leader plant, filler, hanging plant

Many gardeners use a simple scheme for beautiful boxes:

- **Leader Plant** (Thriller): An upright, showy plant in the middle or back, such as a standing geranium, ornamental grass or daisy.
- **Filler** (Filler): Bushy, medium-tall plants that fill the space, such as verbena, fairy mirror or begonia.
- **Hanging Plant** (Spiller): Plants that grow over the edge, such as hanging petunias, magic bells, lobelia, ivy or sweet potatoes.

### Example combinations

| style | Combination |
|---|---|
| Classically sunny | Red standing geranium, white verbena, blue hanging lobelia |
| Mediterranean | Lavender, rosemary, thyme, nasturtium |
| Insect friendly | Zinnias, cosmos, scented rocks, fan flowers |
| Partial shade romantic | Fuchsia, elf mirror, ivy |
| Shade modern | Tuberous begonia, purple bells, fern |

Unfilled flowers are better for bees and other insects than heavily double varieties. More about this in [Bee-friendly balcony](/ratgeber/bienenfreundlicher-balkon/).

## Care through the season

### Pouring

In mid-summer, sunny boxes often need water **daily, sometimes twice daily**. It is best to water early in the morning or in the evening, directly onto the soil and not over leaves and flowers. The finger test shows whether it is necessary.

### Fertilize

The nutrients in the soil only last a few weeks. Starting around four to six weeks after planting, fertilize **weekly with liquid fertilizer for balcony plants**, or incorporate a slow-release fertilizer when planting. Geraniums and petunias are particularly hungry.

### Clean out

Remove dead flowers regularly. The plant does not produce seeds, but rather new flowers. For petunias, geraniums and daisies, this extends flowering considerably.

### Pruning

When plants become long and untidy in mid-summer, cutting them back by a third can encourage new shoots and a second bloom.

### Pests

Aphids, spider mites and whiteflies are typical balcony pests. Check regularly. See [Fighting Aphids Naturally](/ratgeber/blattlaeuse-natuerlich-bekaempfen/).

## Autumn and winter

In autumn, summer flowers can be replaced by autumn plantings of heather, chrysanthemums, grasses, purple bells and ornamental cabbage. Perennial plants such as geraniums or fuchsias can be overwintered frost-free. See [Overwintering potted plants](/ratgeber/kuebelpflanzen-ueberwintern/).

## Practical example: The south-facing balcony on the third floor

Every July, on a south-facing balcony on the third floor, the petunias in the small 15 cm boxes dried up, even though they were watered daily. The following year the boxes were replaced with 20 cm deep water storage boxes. Geraniums were planted as leading plants, purslane and magic bells as well as hanging verbena. A slow-release fertilizer was mixed in and liquid fertilizer was given weekly starting in July. Watering was only necessary every two to three days, and the boxes bloomed until October.

## Plan costs realistically

A well-planted balcony box doesn't have to be expensive, but in some places it's worth not saving money. The box itself will last for many years if it is of good quality, as will the holders and coasters. When it comes to soil, high-quality goods pay off because they stay loose throughout the season and store water better. There are big differences between the plants: young plants from the nursery are more expensive than those from discount stores, but are often stronger and free of pests. If you want to save money, sow annual flowers such as nasturtiums, marigolds, cornflowers or zinnias yourself or overwinter geraniums and fuchsias from the previous year. Cuttings from friends and neighbors are also a good source. A small supply of liquid fertilizer is enough for a whole season and several boxes. This is how you can create a lush balcony with a manageable budget.

## Plant sustainably

There are now many good peat-free soils available for balcony boxes. They protect peatlands, which are important as habitat and carbon storage. Peat-free soil behaves slightly differently than peat-containing soil: the surface dries more quickly, even though there is still moisture underneath, and the wood fibers it contains initially bind some nitrogen. Therefore, check the moisture with your finger instead of by the appearance of the surface, and start fertilizing a little earlier.

It is also sustainable not to buy everything new every year. Perennial herbs such as thyme and chives, hardy perennials and spring bulbs such as crocuses and grape hyacinths can stay in the box for years if it is large enough and has some protection in winter. Your own cuttings of pelargoniums and other favorite plants also save money and transport costs.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many plants fit in an 80cm box?

About four to five, depending on the growth width.

### Can I plant vegetables in window boxes?

Yes, such as lettuce, radishes, herbs and strawberries. Containers are better for tomatoes and peppers. See [Tomatoes on the Balcony](/ratgeber/tomaten-auf-dem-balkon/).

### Does every box need drainage?

A drainage layer is helpful for boxes with drainage holes. The holes themselves are more important.

### Can I reuse soil from last year?

Partially. Old balcony soil has been leached out and its structure has usually collapsed. For sturdy plants, you can use them if you remove old roots and mix about half with fresh soil or mature compost. It is better to use fresh soil for seeds and sensitive plants. Soil from boxes with pests such as fungus gnats or black weevil larvae or with diseased plants belongs in the organic waste bin.

### How often do I have to fertilize balcony boxes?

Most potting soils are pre-fertilized for the first four to six weeks. After that, heavily flowering plants such as petunias, pelargoniums or fuchsias need regular replenishment, either weekly with liquid fertilizer in the specified dosage or with a slow-release fertilizer that you mix in when planting. You should stop fertilizing perennial plants from the end of August onwards so that their shoots mature before winter.

## Conclusion

A beautiful balcony box begins with the correct location analysis. Choose plants that suit sun or shade, use sufficiently large boxes with drainage holes and high-quality soil, do not plant too densely and combine upright, bushy and hanging plants. Regular watering, fertilizing and cleaning ensures a long flowering period until autumn.
