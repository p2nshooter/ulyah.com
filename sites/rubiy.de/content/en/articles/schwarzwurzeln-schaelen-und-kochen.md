Salsify doesn't look very inviting: long, thin, earthy stalks with dark, almost black bark. Anyone who peels them for the first time will have sticky, brown hands and wonder whether the effort is worth it. It's worth it. Under the rind there is white, tender flesh with a fine, slightly nutty taste that is reminiscent of asparagus and artichoke. It is not without reason that salsify is called “winter asparagus” or “poor man's asparagus”. From October to March it brings variety to the winter months, which are otherwise dominated by cabbage and turnips.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Salsify is in season from October to March. Good roots are long, straight, firm and undamaged.
> - When peeled, a sticky milky juice comes out, which turns the hands and board brown. Wear disposable gloves and peel under running water.
> - Immediately place peeled stalks in water with vinegar or lemon juice and a spoonful of flour, otherwise they will turn gray.
> - It's easier if you boil the washed, unpeeled roots for 15 minutes and then peel off the skin.
> - When cooked in pieces, salsify takes 15 to 20 minutes. They are traditionally served in a light cream sauce.

## Recognizing fresh salsify

Salsify is usually sold in bundles or loose by weight. Pay attention to these points:

- **Firm and Crisp:** A fresh root will break when bent instead of bending smoothly. Limp rods have dried out.
- **Undamaged:** Broken or cut roots lose milky juice and dry out more quickly. Small breaks are not a problem if you process the roots soon.
- **Straight and evenly thick:** Long, straight sticks are easiest to peel. Thin, branchy roots mean a lot of waste.
- **Soil is normal:** Salsify is sold unwashed, which protects it from drying out.

Allow about 250 to 300 grams of unpeeled roots per person as a side dish. Up to a third is lost during peeling.

## Store

Wrap unwashed salsify in damp newspaper or kitchen paper and place it in the vegetable compartment. They last like this for one to two weeks. You can store them in moist sand in a cool cellar like before, then they will stay fresh for several weeks.

## Freeze or jar

Fresh salsify freezes well. Peel them, cut them into pieces and blanch them in boiling water with a dash of lemon juice for two to three minutes. Then rinse in ice water, drain and freeze in portions. They last for several months at minus 18 degrees. Add them frozen directly to the boiling water or sauce. More about the technique can be found in [Freezing and blanching vegetables](/kueche/gemuese-einfrieren-und-blanchieren/).

If you want to save yourself the hassle of peeling, salsify can also be found pre-cooked in a jar. They are peeled, cut into pieces and preserved in a slightly acidic infusion. Rinse them briefly and just warm them in the sauce because they are already soft. In terms of taste, they don't quite come close to fresh roots, but they are a good shortcut for a quick gratin or soup.

## The problem with the latex

If you damage the bark of a black salsify, a white, sticky milky sap will come out. When exposed to air it oxidizes and turns brown. It is harmless on the skin, but stubborn: the stains are difficult to remove with soap and water and only fade after a day or two. Wooden boards, kitchen towels and clothing can also become permanently discolored.

These precautions help:

- Wear disposable nitrile or latex gloves.
- Place a plastic board or a layer of baking paper underneath.
- Wear an apron and not light-colored clothing.
- If your hands do get stains, rub them with lemon juice or a little cooking oil before washing them. This will loosen the sticky film.

## Two methods for peeling

### Peel raw

1. Brush the roots thoroughly under running water until there is no more soil attached.
2. Prepare a bowl of cold water in which you stir in a dash of vinegar or the juice of half a lemon and a tablespoon of flour. The acidic flour water keeps the peeled sticks light.
3. Peel the roots with a vegetable peeler, preferably under running water or in a bowl of water. The milky juice is washed away immediately.
4. Cut off the ends and place each peeled stick directly into the acidic water.
5. Just before cooking, cut them into three to five centimeter pieces.

### Cook unpeeled and then peel

This method is cleaner and faster, especially for larger quantities.

1. Brush the roots thoroughly and cut them to pot length if necessary.
2. Cook them unpeeled in salted water with a splash of vinegar until almost tender, about 15 minutes.
3. Rinse them briefly in cold water.
4. Peel off the skin with a small knife or your fingers. It now comes off easily and the milky juice is bound by cooking.

This method is particularly suitable if the salsify is then added to a sauce or soup. For baked salsify, raw peeling is better because the stalks remain firmer.

## Cooking

Place the peeled pieces in boiling salted water with a splash of milk or a squeeze of lemon juice. Both help to maintain the light color. Cook for 15 to 20 minutes, until soft but not broken down. The cooking time depends heavily on the thickness, so test the cooking with a sharp knife. When draining, save some of the cooking water for the sauce.

## Creamed black salsify with ham

This is the classic preparation, very similar to asparagus in a light sauce.

### Ingredients for four people

- 1 kg salsify
- Salt, 1 dash of milk for the cooking water
- 30g butter
- 30g flour
- 250 ml cooking water of the salsify
- 200 ml cream or milk
- Nutmeg, white pepper, 1 splash of lemon juice
- 2 tbsp chopped parsley
- 150 g cooked ham, cut into strips (optional)

### Preparation

1. Peel and cook the salsify as described above and keep them warm.
2. Melt the butter, stir in the flour and sweat it briefly without letting it take any color.
3. Pour in the cooking water, then the cream, stirring constantly. Let the sauce simmer gently for five minutes.
4. Season to taste with salt, nutmeg, pepper and lemon juice.
5. Add the salsify and the ham strips to the sauce and heat everything briefly. Sprinkle with parsley.

Serve with boiled potatoes or parsley potatoes, veal schnitzel, meatballs or a fried fish fillet. Without ham, the dish is vegetarian and particularly delicious with roasted hazelnuts.

## Baked salsify

Pre-cook raw peeled sticks in salted water for ten minutes so that they still have some bite. After draining and patting dry, coat them in flour, beaten egg and breadcrumbs and fry them in plenty of clarified butter or oil until golden brown. With lemon wedges and a tartar sauce or a herb dip, they are a delicious winter dish.

## Velvety salsify soup

Sauté a diced onion in butter, add 500 grams of peeled, diced salsify and a floury potato and pour in 800 milliliters of [vegetable broth] (/kueche/gemuesebruehe-selbst-kochen/). Let everything simmer for 20 minutes, puree the soup finely and refine it with cream, nutmeg and a splash of lemon juice. Roasted hazelnuts or crispy bacon cubes on top make them a holiday starter.

## Salsify gratin

A gratin is the easiest way to prepare salsify for guests. Pre-cook 1 kg of peeled salsify in pieces for about twelve minutes so that they still have some bite and layer them in a greased baking dish. Prepare a light sauce like for the creamed black salsify and finally stir in 50 g of grated mountain cheese. Pour the sauce over the vegetables, sprinkle another 50 g of cheese and a few breadcrumbs if desired and bake the gratin at 200 degrees top and bottom heat for 20 to 25 minutes until the surface is golden brown. A green salad or a piece of roast goes well with this.

## What goes well with salsify

The mild, nutty taste does not tolerate loud partners. The following have proven themselves:

| Category | Suitable companions |
|---|---|
| herbs and spices | Parsley, chervil, chives, nutmeg, white pepper |
| Acid | Lemon juice, a dash of white wine in the sauce |
| Nuts | Hazelnuts, walnuts, almonds, each roasted |
| meat and fish | Cooked ham, veal, poultry, salmon, pike perch |
| Supplements | Boiled potatoes, mashed potatoes, spaetzle, rice |

## Salsify in your own garden

Salsify comes from southern Europe and is a member of the daisy family. Its botanical name Scorzonera hispanica refers to Spain. In the garden you sow them directly into the bed from March to April. It needs deep, loose and stone-free soil because its taproots grow to 30 centimeters long and longer. Harvesting begins in October. Dig out the roots carefully with the digging fork without damaging them, otherwise milky sap will come out. Salsify is hardy and can stay in the bed until spring as long as the ground is not frozen.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you eat salsify raw?

Yes. Finely grated or thinly sliced, young salsify tastes nutty and crunchy when eaten raw in a salad. Place them immediately in lemon dressing to prevent them from browning.

### Why are they called winter asparagus?

Because the appearance of the peeled stalks and taste are reminiscent of white asparagus and they are in season in winter when asparagus is not available. The preparation is also similar. You can read how asparagus is cooked in our [asparagus article](/kueche/spargel-schaelen-kochen/).

### Are salsify healthy?

They contain a lot of inulin, a fiber that is used by bacteria in the intestine, as well as potassium and vitamin E. In sensitive people, large amounts of inulin can cause flatulence.

### Why are my salsify turning gray?

The peeled stalks were usually exposed to air or in water without acid for too long. Immediately after peeling, place them in vinegar or lemon water and also add a dash of milk or lemon juice to the cooking water. A slight discoloration is harmless and does not change the taste.

### Can I prepare salsify the day before?

Yes. Peeled sticks can be kept completely covered in sour flour water in the refrigerator until the next day. Store cooked salsify, covered, in the refrigerator for one to two days and then warm it in the sauce.
