The cherry season is short and intense. From the beginning of June to the end of July, the trees in the Altes Land near Hamburg, in Franconia, on the Kaiserstuhl and around Lake Constance are full of fruit, and there are stands with freshly picked cherries on the roadsides. If you want to take advantage of the season, you should know the differences between the varieties, know how to keep cherries fresh, and know a trick for pitting them, because that's the real work with cakes, compotes and jam. As a reward at the end there is a Kirschenmichel, a juicy casserole made from stale bread, milk and cherries that generations of children have loved.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Sweet cherries are available from June to the end of July, sour cherries mainly in July.
> - Sweet cherries are suitable for eating raw, and sour cherries such as morello cherries are particularly suitable for baking and preserving.
> - Wash the cherries just before eating, store them with the stems on and use them in the refrigerator in two to three days.
> - Without a corer, a sturdy drinking straw, a piping nozzle or a chopstick with a bottle will help.
> - Kirschenmichel uses old bread and is a complete sweet main course.

## Sweet cherries and sour cherries

**Sweet cherries** are divided into two groups. **Heart cherries** have soft, very juicy flesh and ripen early, often at the beginning of June. They taste best fresh from the tree and are less suitable for baking because they lose a lot of juice. **Cartilaginous cherries** have firm, crunchy flesh and ripen later. They last longer, transport better and are the types you usually find in stores. The color ranges from yellow with red cheeks to almost black.

**Sour cherries** are smaller, softer and noticeably more sour. The best-known variety is the **Shadow Morelle**, dark red and very aromatic. It is the classic cherry for Black Forest cake, cherry crumble, compote and jam because its acidity stands up well against sugar and cream when baking. Fresh from the tree, sour cherries are only for lovers.

| Cherry | season | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Heart cherry | Early to mid June | eat fresh, fruit salad |
| Cartilage cherry | Mid-June to the end of July | eat fresh, clafoutis, cherry michel |
| Morello cherries | July | Cake, compote, red fruit jelly, preserves, jam |

## Shopping and storage

Ripe cherries are plump, shiny and have green, fresh stems. Brown, dried stems show that the fruits are already a few days old. Cherries do not ripen after harvest. What is bought sour stays sour.

Store cherries unwashed and with the stems in the vegetable compartment of the refrigerator, preferably flat in a bowl so that the bottom fruit is not pressed. They last like this for two to three days. Washed cherries and those without stems spoil more quickly because juice comes out at the base. Take them out of the fridge half an hour before eating, then they will taste more aromatic.

Wash cherries briefly in cold water immediately before eating. Cherries that have burst open after a rain are edible as long as they smell fresh, but should be processed immediately.

## Pick yourself: this way the cherries stay fresh

Many fruit farms offer pick-your-own cherries in June and July. A few rules make the harvest more productive:

- **Pick early in the day** while the fruit is cool. Warm cherries soften more quickly.
- **Harvest with the stem:** Grasp the stem and carefully twist it from the branch. Cherries without stems lose juice at the base and only last for a day.
- **Protect the fruit wood:** Do not tear off any tufts including the rosette of leaves. Flowers will grow again next year on these short shoots.
- **Take flat boxes with you:** In tall buckets, the top cherries flatten the bottom ones.
- **Only pick ripe fruits:** They come off easily, have the color typical of the variety and taste sweet.

## Pitting cherries without special equipment

A metal cherry pitter is the quickest solution if you process kilos every year. For occasional quantities it also works like this:

1. **Drinking straw:** Remove the stem and press a sturdy metal or glass drinking straw through the cherry from above. The stone falls out at the bottom. Work over a bowl to catch the juice.
2. **Bottle and chopsticks:** Place the stemless cherry on the opening of a glass bottle and use a chopstick to push the stone down into the bottle. The bottle catches stones and splashes.
3. **Piping nozzle:** Place a metal nozzle with a smooth edge on the work surface and press the stem side of the cherry onto the tip.
4. **Halve:** For cake topping, you can also cut the cherries all around and remove the stone with the tip of a knife.

Wear an apron and dark clothing when pitting. Cherry juice leaves stubborn stains.

Like many fruit seeds, the seeds contain amygdalin. Whole cherry stones that are accidentally swallowed are harmless because they are excreted undigested. You shouldn't bite them. You can use washed, cooked and completely dried kernels for a heating pad, as is common in many households.

## Cherry Michel

The Kirschenmichel, also known locally as Kirschenplotzer or Kirschenmännle, comes from southwest Germany. It used to be eaten on days without meat and used rolls from the previous day. For a baking dish measuring approximately 20 × 30 centimeters:

### Ingredients

- 6 stale rolls or 300 g of white bread from the day before
- 500 ml milk
- 4 eggs
- 80 g soft butter, plus some for the mold
- 80g sugar
- 1 packet of vanilla sugar or the pulp of half a vanilla pod
- grated peel of an organic lemon
- 1 pinch of salt
- 50 g ground almonds
- 750 g cherries, pitted (sweet or sour cherries, sour cherries with 1 to 2 tablespoons more sugar)
- 2 tbsp breadcrumbs
- Powdered sugar for dusting

### Preparation

1. Cut the rolls into thin slices or small cubes and pour the lukewarm milk over them. Let them soak for 15 minutes.
2. Preheat the oven to 180 degrees top and bottom heat. Grease the mold and sprinkle it with breadcrumbs.
3. Separate the eggs. Mix the butter, sugar, vanilla, lemon zest and salt until fluffy and gradually add the egg yolks.
4. Mix the butter mixture and almonds into the soaked rolls. The mixture should be moist but not runny.
5. Beat the egg whites until stiff and fold them in carefully.
6. Fold in the cherries or alternate layers of bread mixture and cherries in the mold. The top layer is bread mixture.
7. Bake the Kirschenmichel for 45 to 50 minutes, until golden brown and set in the center.

Dust it with powdered sugar before serving. This goes well with a vanilla sauce like the one we describe in the recipe for [Red Fruit Jelly](/kueche/rote-gruetze-und-johannisbeeren/). Warm, the Kirschenmichel is a sweet lunch, cold, a cake with coffee. You can find more ideas for stale bread in [leftover kitchen with a plan](/kueche/resteverwertung-in-der-kueche/).

## Clafoutis: the French relative

While Kirschenmichel uses old bread, Limousin clafoutis only needs a simple egg batter. Traditionally, the cherries are not pitted because the pits give off a delicate almond aroma when baked. If you have children or guests at the table, stone them anyway and add some ground almonds to the dough.

To make a mold of around 24 cm, beat 3 eggs with 80 g sugar and a pinch of salt, stir in 60 g flour until smooth and gradually add 250 ml milk. Grease the mold with butter, spread 500 g of cherries in it and pour the dough over it. Bake the clafoutis at 180 degrees top and bottom heat for 35 to 40 minutes until it is golden brown and set. It will collapse a little as it cools down, this is normal. It tastes best lukewarm with powdered sugar.

## Preserve cherries

- **Freezing:** Pre-freeze pitted on a tray, then fill into bags. This means they don't stick together and can be removed in portions.
- **Preserving:** Boil the sour cherries in light syrup at 80 to 85 degrees for 30 minutes. Details can be found in [Preserving for beginners](/kueche/einkochen-grundlagen/).
- **Jam:** Cherries have little pectin. Take preserving sugar and some lemon juice.

## A pest you should know about: the cherry vinegar fly

The cherry vinegar fly, which comes from East Asia, has been spreading in Germany since around 2011. Unlike the native cherry fruit fly, it attacks not only cherries but also raspberries, blackberries, blueberries and grapes, and it lays its eggs in ripe, uninjured fruits. Infected cherries have tiny puncture marks, quickly become soft and smell slightly of vinegar. This is not dangerous for humans, but the fruits spoil in a short time. Therefore, process late cherries quickly and sort out soft specimens.

## What's in cherries

Sweet cherries provide around 60 kilocalories per 100 grams, sour cherries a little less. They owe their dark color to anthocyanins, plant pigments from the group of secondary plant substances. There is also potassium and some vitamin C, a little more in sour cherries than in sweet varieties. A handful of cherries is a light, naturally sweet snack for warm summer days.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you drink water after cherries?

The old warning is exaggerated. In very sensitive people, large amounts of cherries with a lot of water can cause flatulence, but this is not dangerous with clean drinking water.

### Why do some cherries have small white maggots?

These are larvae of the cherry fruit fly, especially in late varieties. They are not harmful to health, but they are unappetizing. Place the cherries in cold water for half an hour and the larvae will usually come out. Early ripening varieties are less likely to be affected.

### Can I use sweet cherries instead of sour cherries for baking?

Yes, but reduce the sugar and add a squeeze of lemon juice so that the cake doesn't taste flat. Cartilage cherries are better than soft heart cherries.

### How many cherries do I need to buy for a recipe?

Recipes usually mention the weight of pitted cherries. When removing stones, expect to lose around a tenth due to seeds and juice and buy a little more accordingly. So for 750 g of pitted cherries you need a good 800 to 850 g of whole fruit.

### How do I get cherry stains out of clothes?

The fresher, the better. Immediately rinse the back of the stain with cold water to prevent the juice from penetrating deeper into the fabric. Then rub with gall soap and wash the item of clothing as usual. Soak dried stains for a few hours before washing.
