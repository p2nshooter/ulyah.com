Freezing is the easiest way to stock up on seasonal supplies. It requires no special equipment other than a freezer, it preserves color, flavor, and nutrients better than most other methods, and it is safer than canning low-acid vegetables. Whether it's peas in June, beans in August or kale in December, what's in abundance can be saved for the lean months.

To ensure that frozen vegetables still taste good months later, it all comes down to a few simple steps. The most important one is blanching.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Most vegetables are briefly blanched before freezing, i.e. heated in boiling water and then quenched in ice-cold water.
> - Blanching stops enzymes that would otherwise break down color, flavor and vitamins in the freezer.
> - Depending on the vegetables, the blanching time is between one and four minutes.
> - Well packaged and as air-free as possible, most vegetables last eight to twelve months at minus 18 degrees.
> - Lettuce, cucumbers, radishes and raw potatoes are not suitable for freezing.

## Why blanch?

Vegetables contain enzymes that continue to work after they are harvested. They cause vegetables to wilt, change color, lose vitamins and develop a strawy or bitter taste. Cold slows down these enzymes, but does not stop them completely. Unblanched vegetables therefore become grey-green, tough and bland in the freezer over the months.

When blanching, the vegetables are briefly heated to such an extent that the enzymes are destroyed without them cooking properly. It is then immediately cooled in ice water so that it does not continue to cook. In addition, germs on the surface are reduced and the color remains bright. Many vegetables also collapse a little and can be packed to save space.

## Blanch step by step

1. **Prepare:** Wash and clean the vegetables and cut them into the size you want to use them later.
2. **Boil water:** Bring plenty of water to the boil in a large pot, about four to five liters for 500 grams of vegetables. Salt is not necessary.
3. **Prepare ice water:** Fill a large bowl with cold water and ice cubes.
4. **Blanch in batches:** Only add enough vegetables to the water so that they quickly boil again, preferably in a sieve or frying pan. The blanching time begins as soon as the water boils again.
5. **Queeing:** Remove the vegetables and immediately immerse them in the ice water. Leave it there for about the same amount of time as the blanching time, until it is thoroughly cold.
6. **Drying:** Drain the vegetables in a colander and pat dry on a kitchen towel. The drier it is, the fewer ice crystals form.

### Blanch in steam

Instead of blanching in water, you can also blanch vegetables in steam, for example in a pot with a sieve insert and lid. Fewer water-soluble vitamins and minerals are lost. Allow the blanching time to be about one and a half times as long as in water and only place the vegetables in one layer in the insert so that the steam reaches all the pieces evenly. It is not advisable to use the microwave: it heats unevenly and the enzymes are not reliably stopped in all pieces.

## Blanching times

The information applies to medium-sized pieces. Smaller pieces take a little less time, larger pieces take a little more time.

| vegetables | Preparation | Blanching time |
|---|---|---|
| Peas | spread out | 1-2 minutes |
| Spinach, chard | washed, rough stems removed | 1-2 minutes |
| Kale | plucked | 2 minutes |
| Green beans | Ends cut off, whole or in pieces | 3 minutes |
| Broccoli, cauliflower | in florets | 3 minutes |
| carrots | in slices or sticks | 3 minutes |
| Kohlrabi | in sticks or cubes | 3 minutes |
| Brussels sprouts | cleaned, small florets | 3-4 minutes |
| Savoy cabbage, white cabbage | in strips | 2 minutes |
| Sugar snap peas | completely | 1-2 minutes |

Raw green beans contain a toxic protein called phasin. Brief blanching is not enough to destroy it completely. You must therefore cook frozen beans thoroughly before eating, just like fresh beans. There is more about this in the article about [green beans](/kueche/gruene-bohnen-richtig-garen/).

## Which can be done without blanching

You can freeze some vegetables and all types of fruit raw:

- **Peppers, onions, leeks:** in strips or cubes, raw. They become soft after defrosting, but are good for cooking.
- **Herbs:** finely chopped, in ice cube trays with a little water or oil or loose in cans. Parsley, chives and dill remain more aromatic than dried.
- **Asparagus:** peeled raw, without blanching, as described in the article about [asparagus](/kueche/spargel-schaelen-kochen/).
- **Tomatoes:** whole or quartered, raw. After thawing, the skin can be easily removed. Only suitable for sauces and soups.
- **Fruit:** Berries, cherries, plums and rhubarb raw, washed and well dried.

## Freeze fruit

To prevent berries and pieces of fruit from freezing together in a lump, after washing and drying, spread them out on a tray or board with baking paper, leaving a little space between them, and freeze them for one to two hours. Then you fill them into bags or cans. This way you can later take exactly the amount you need.

Apples and pears quickly turn brown and mushy when frozen raw. It is better to freeze them as puree or compote. Cut stone fruits like plums in half and remove the stone before freezing.

## Pack correctly

Air is the enemy in the freezer. It removes moisture from the frozen food and leads to freezer burn: grayish-white, dried out areas that taste tough and strawy. Freezer burn is harmless to your health, but it is annoying in terms of taste.

- Use freezer-safe bags or cans. Thin breakfast bags are too permeable.
- Squeeze the air out of bags before sealing them. A drinking straw in the opening helps to suck out the last air. Vacuum sealers are even more thorough.
- Fill portions that you use at once.
- For liquids like soups or sauces, leave two to three centimeters of space because they expand when frozen.
- Label each package with contents and date.
- Freeze flat bags lying flat. They freeze faster, thaw faster and can be stacked later to save space.

## Use the freezer correctly

How quickly vegetables freeze determines their quality. Slow freezing causes large ice crystals to grow, destroying cell walls, and the vegetables become soft after thawing. That's why it's worth taking a look at the device's nameplate: it shows the freezing capacity in kilograms per 24 hours. You shouldn't freeze more fresh produce in one day. Many devices also have a quick-freeze function that you switch on a few hours before storing.

Check the temperature every now and then with a thermometer. The goal is minus 18 degrees or colder. Place fresh packages in the coldest compartment with some space between them and only stack them when they are frozen.

### And if the power goes out?

Keep the door closed. A full, well-stocked freezer keeps the cold much longer than a half-empty one. How many hours your device can survive without power is stated in the instructions for use as the “storage time in the event of a fault”. If vegetables or fruit have only thawed and still contain ice crystals, you can usually freeze them again, but the quality will suffer somewhat. You can cook completely thawed vegetables and use them quickly or freeze them as a finished dish.

## How long does frozen food last?

At a constant minus 18 degrees or colder, no microorganisms multiply. The shelf life information therefore primarily refers to the quality.

| Food | Recommended storage time at −18 °C |
|---|---|
| Blanched vegetables | 8-12 months |
| Herbs | 6-8 months |
| Berries and stone fruits | 8-12 months |
| Fruit puree and compote | 8-12 months |
| Cooked dishes, soups | 2-3 months |

Only freeze fresh, perfect goods. Freezing doesn't improve anything, it just fixes the situation. Wilted vegetables stay wilted.

## Thaw and use

It's best to add vegetables frozen directly to the pot or pan. It then cooks a little faster than fresh because it is already blanched. Defrosting would make it unnecessarily soft. You can add fruit to the dough frozen for cakes or let it thaw in the fridge, or directly into the hot liquid for grits and compote.

You should not refreeze raw vegetables that have been thawed. Cook it and freeze the finished dish.

## What is not suitable for freezing

- **Leaf lettuce, rocket, cress:** become mushy.
- **Cucumbers, radishes, radishes:** become translucent and soft.
- **Raw potatoes:** become sweet and spongy. Boiled potatoes in stews, as puree or dumplings work on the other hand.
- **Whole raw eggs in the shell:** burst.
- **Pure yogurt and quark:** become grainy, but can still be used for cooking and baking.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I freeze vegetables without blanching?

This is possible for many varieties for a short period of time, up to three months. Afterwards, unblanched vegetables noticeably lose their color and taste. Blanching is worthwhile for supplies over the winter.

### Why are my frozen vegetables mushy?

Most of the time it was blanched for too long, not cooled quickly enough, or frozen too slowly. Freeze small, flat portions and place them in the coldest compartment.

### Do I have to defrost the freezer and chest?

If a thick layer of ice forms, yes. It consumes energy and makes maintaining temperature difficult. It's best to use the cold season for this and store the contents in cool bags during this time.

### Can I still eat frozen food after the recommended storage period has expired?

As a rule, yes, if the temperature was constantly at minus 18 degrees the entire time. The information refers to quality, not safety. Older frozen food often tastes blander and can cause freezer burn. Cut away any dry spots generously and use it in soups, stews or sauces.

### How do I defrost berries for dessert?

Place them in a colander over a bowl and let them thaw slowly in the refrigerator. The juice that comes out collects in the bowl and, with a little sugar, makes a quick fruit sauce. Served half frozen, berries hold their shape best.
