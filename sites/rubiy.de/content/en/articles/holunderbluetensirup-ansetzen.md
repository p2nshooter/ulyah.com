At the end of May, when the days become long, the elder bushes bloom on the edges of forests, railway embankments and in old gardens. Their plate-sized, creamy-white umbels smell sweet, a little like nutmeg and honey, and in many families this is the start of a ritual: collecting flowers, adding sugar and lemon, waiting a few days and then bottling syrup that lasts from early summer to winter. With cold sparkling water it makes one of the best lemonades there is. And if you dip the cones in dough and bake them, you have a dish called elderberry cakes that is only available a few weeks a year.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Depending on the region, black elderberry blooms from late May to late June. Harvest fully bloomed, fragrant umbels on a dry, sunny morning.
> - Do not wash the flowers, just shake them out. The yellow pollen carries the aroma.
> - Elderberry is a shrub or tree with woody branches. White umbelliferous plants on the ground like poison hemlock are something completely different.
> - To make the syrup, let the cones steep in sugar water with lemon for two to three days, strain, bring to the boil and fill hot into sterile bottles.
> - Remove green stems if possible and never eat elderberries raw.

## Recognize black elderberry safely

The black elderberry is a shrub or small tree, usually two to six meters high. Its branches are woody, the bark is gray-brown with small cork warts, and there is white, spongy pith on the inside. The leaves are pinnate with five to seven serrated leaflets. The flowers grow in flat, umbrella-shaped umbels that are 10 to 20 centimeters wide. Each individual flower is small, creamy white, with five petals and yellow anthers.

There are two plants in particular that are likely to be confused:

| plant | growth | blossom | Fragrance | Edible? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Black elderberry | woody shrub or tree | flat, creamy white umbrellas, yellow anthers | sweet, pleasant | Flowers processed yes |
| Dwarf elderberry (Attic) | herbaceous, dies in winter, up to 1.5 m high | white with reddish-purple anthers, more erect | unpleasant, strict | no, poisonous |
| Umbelliferous plants such as hemlock | herbaceous, hollow, often with spotted stems | lots of little bells, put together | often unpleasant, mouse-like | no, sometimes very poisonous |

The rule of thumb is: True elderflowers grow on a woody bush, not on a plant that comes out of the ground. Only collect what you recognize for sure.

## Harvest correctly

- **Location:** Collect away from busy roads, sprayed fields and dog trails. If in doubt, ask the owner if you can harvest. Collecting small quantities in the wild for your own needs is permitted in Germany, except in nature reserves.
- **Time:** A sunny, dry morning is ideal. After rain the aroma is washed out and in the midday heat the flowers are limp.
- **Selection:** Take umbels whose flowers are almost all open and have a strong scent. Brown, wilting flowers taste musty.
- **Conserve quantity:** Leave enough flowers on each bush. In late summer they become the berries that birds live on and which you can use for elderberry juice.
- **Transportation:** Place the cones loosely in a basket, not a plastic bag. Process them the same day.

At home, shake each umbel vigorously to remove small insects. To do this, place them on a white cloth with the flowers facing down for half an hour, then most of the animals will crawl out. Do not wash the flowers or the pollen will be lost.

## Recipe: Elderflower syrup

### Ingredients for about 2 liters

- 20 to 25 large elderflower umbels
- 1.5 liters of water
- 1.5kg sugar
- 3 organic lemons
- 30 g citric acid (from a pharmacy, drugstore or supermarket) or the juice of two more lemons

The citric acid provides freshness and lowers the pH value, which makes the syrup more durable. If you don't use it, use more lemon juice and store the syrup in a cooler place.

### Preparation

1. **Boil sugar water:** Bring water and sugar to a boil and stir until sugar is completely dissolved. Allow the syrup to cool completely. Hot syrup would destroy the delicate floral aroma.
2. **Preparation:** Cut the lemons into slices. Cut off the thick green stems of the umbels, leaving only the flowers with the fine stems. Place the flowers, lemon slices and citric acid in a large bowl or earthenware pot and pour the cold sugar water over it. The flowers should be covered.
3. **Let it steep:** Cover the container with a clean cloth and let it sit in a cool place for two to three days. Stir once daily with a clean spoon. You shouldn't wait any longer, otherwise the mixture may start to ferment.
4. **Strain:** Pour the mixture through a sieve lined with cheesecloth or a fine tea towel. Squeeze the flowers lightly.
5. **Boil and bottle:** Bring the syrup briefly to the boil and immediately fill it to the brim into hot, rinsed or sterilized bottles. Seal it right away.

## Shelf life and storage

When filled hot and tightly sealed, elderflower syrup will keep for about a year in a cool, dark place. Once opened, the bottle belongs in the refrigerator and should be used within four to six weeks. If mold forms, the syrup foams or smells fermented, throw it away.

You can also freeze the syrup in portions in ice cube trays. This means you always have small quantities on hand for drinks or desserts.

## Usage

- **Lemonade:** One part syrup to eight to ten parts cold mineral water, with lemon slices and mint leaves. This is the easiest and most popular use.
- **Schorle:** With apple juice and sparkling water for children.
- **Desserts:** A dash of syrup over strawberries, in yogurt, quark or cream. Elderberry and strawberries go particularly well together. You can find out more about the strawberry season in our article about [German-grown strawberries](/kueche/erdbeerkuchen-und-erdbeeren/).
- **Jelly:** Cook syrup with preserving sugar and water to make elderflower jelly. The basic technique describes [cooking jam and preserves](/kueche/konfituere-und-marmelade-kochen/).
- **Rhubarb and elderberry:** A spoonful of syrup in rhubarb compote combines two spring flavors.

## Elderberry cakes: fried elderflowers

In Bavaria, Swabia and Austria, the cones are dipped in dough and fried in hot fat. The result is tender, crispy flower cakes that are dusted with powdered sugar.

1. Mix 150 g flour, 200 ml milk, 2 eggs, 1 tablespoon sugar and a pinch of salt into a smooth, thick dough. Let it soak for 15 minutes.
2. Heat oil or clarified butter in a pot about three centimeters high to around 170 degrees. Small bubbles should appear on the handle of a wooden spoon.
3. Grab an umbel by the stem, dip it into the batter, let any excess drip off, and bake, florets side down, until golden brown, about two minutes.
4. Drain the cakes on kitchen paper and cut off the thick stem. Dust with powdered sugar and cinnamon.

Vanilla ice cream, applesauce or rhubarb compote go well with this. You don't eat the stalk itself.

## Dry flowers and elderflower vinegar

Not every cone has to go into the syrup. To make **tea**, place the shaken out umbels on paper with the flowers facing down or hang them upside down in a shady, airy place. After a few days, the flowers rustle and can be rubbed off the stems with your fingers. Stored in a dark glass, they make a mild, honey-like tea in winter: two teaspoons of flowers per cup, poured over with boiling water and left to steep for ten minutes. In folk medicine, elderflower tea is considered a classic tea for cold days. How herbs are generally dried gently can be found in [Drying fruits, mushrooms and herbs](/kueche/obst-und-kraeuter-trocknen/).

To make **Elderflower Vinegar**, loosely fill a clean jar with flowers without coarse stems, pour mild white wine vinegar over it until everything is covered, and leave it bright, but not in direct sunlight, for two to three weeks. Then strain and fill into bottles. The vinegar smells floral and goes well with leaf salads with strawberries or asparagus.

## Elderberry in your own garden

The black elderberry is undemanding, grows quickly and thrives in sun and partial shade. It is a valuable tree for nature: the flowers attract insects, and the berries feed many species of birds in late summer. Planting takes place in autumn or early spring. Because elderberry flowers and fruit particularly richly on young shoots, cut out some of the oldest shoots close to the ground every few years in winter. This way the shrub remains vital and manageable.

## Holler in customs

Hardly any other native plant is so closely linked to folk beliefs. There used to be an elder bush on many farmhouses because it was supposed to protect the house and farm. Some associate the name with Mrs. Holle from the fairy tale, and the saying has been passed down that one should take one's hat off to the elderberry. Cutting down the bush was considered bad luck. Even if no one takes this literally anymore, it explains why so many old farms still have an elderberry on their house.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are elderflowers poisonous?

Green parts of the plant, unripe berries and raw elderberry berries contain substances that can cause nausea and vomiting. There is very little of it in the flowers. The flowers have not been a problem in syrup, jelly and cakes for generations. Nevertheless, remove the thick green stems and do not use leaves.

### Can I take less sugar?

Yes, but then the syrup has a shorter shelf life. With significantly less sugar, you can store it in the fridge and use it within a few weeks, or freeze it.

### What do I do with the berries in autumn?

Ripe black elderberries are only used cooked, for example as juice, jelly or soup. To do this, heat them thoroughly, at least until boiling, and remove the stems beforehand.

### Why has my syrup become cloudy?

Some cloudiness from pollen and fine particles is normal and harmless. If you want clear syrup, strain the mixture twice through a fresh cloth and let it stand for an hour before boiling so that suspended particles settle. However, if the syrup smells fermented or if it forms bubbles, it is spoiled.
