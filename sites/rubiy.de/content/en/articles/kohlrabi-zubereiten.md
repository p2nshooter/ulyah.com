Kohlrabi is so German that even English and Americans call it by its German name. The tuberous relative of cabbage and broccoli grows in our country from spring to autumn; the first tubers from the field arrive in May. This is precisely when kohlrabi is tender, juicy and mildly sweet, with a light cabbage aroma that children also like. It tastes good raw as a snack, grated in a salad or traditionally in a light cream sauce with meatballs and boiled potatoes. If you also use the leaves, which many people throw away carelessly, you get significantly more out of each tuber.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Buy medium-sized tubers with fresh, upright leaves. Very large kohlrabi often become woody.
> - Cut off the leaves immediately at home, otherwise they will draw water from the tuber. You can use the tender heart leaves like spinach.
> - Peel kohlrabi from top to bottom and particularly generously at the root end, where the hard fibers are located.
> - Raw, it tastes mild and crunchy. When cooked in sticks or slices, it only takes eight to ten minutes.
> - The tuber will keep in the vegetable compartment for about a week without leaves.

## White or blue?

You can find two colors in stores: light green, often called “white” kohlrabi, and blue-violet. The difference is only in the shell. Both are light inside and taste almost the same. Blue varieties are considered to be somewhat more robust and are more often harvested in autumn, while white varieties are more common in spring. The color doesn't matter for the kitchen. Peel off the blue peel; it will turn gray when cooked.

There are also giant kohlrabi such as the “Super Schmelz” variety, which can weigh several kilograms and still remain tender. Such varieties are the exception. The following applies to normal kohlrabi: A tuber with a diameter of around 8 to 10 centimeters is ideal.

### A name with history

The name comes from Italian, where the vegetable is called cavolo rapa, literally “turnip.” Botanically, kohlrabi is a form of vegetable cabbage in which it is not the leaves but rather the stem that thickens into a tuber just above the ground. So it grows above the ground and is not a root. In Switzerland they also say turnip cabbage.

## Recognize good kohlrabi

Pay attention to these features when shopping:

- **Leaves:** fresh, green and upright. Droopy, yellow leaves show that the kohlrabi has been lying for a long time.
- **Shell:** smooth, without cracks. Bursted tubers have grown after a dry season after heavy rain and are often woody.
- **Weight:** The tuber should be heavy in the hand. Light tubers are sometimes spongy inside.
- **Root end:** Cut as small and cleanly as possible.

## Store

Cut off the leaves immediately after shopping. They evaporate a lot of water and otherwise cause the tuber to quickly become soft. The tuber will keep in the vegetable compartment of the refrigerator in a slightly damp cloth or a perforated bag for about a week. Wrap the delicate inner leaves separately in a damp cloth and use them within one to two days. You can discard large, hard outer leaves with thick stems or use them for [vegetable stock](/kueche/gemuesebruehe-selbst-kochen/).

## Peel properly

1. Cut off the leaf base at the top and the root end flat at the bottom so that the tuber stands securely on the board.
2. Using a sharp knife, peel the peel into strips from top to bottom, following the round shape. A peeler also works for young tubers.
3. Check the root end: there are often woody fibers directly under the peel. Cut generously until the flesh is evenly light and juicy.
4. Depending on the recipe, cut the tuber into slices, sticks, cubes or grate it.

If a kohlrabi is still woody on the inside, the only thing that helps is to cut out the fibrous parts. Cooking does not soften wood fibers. Such tubers are most suitable for a soup, which is then pureed and strained through a sieve.

## Raw: crisp and mild

Young kohlrabi makes an excellent snack raw. Cut into sticks, it goes well with dips such as herb quark or hummus and is often the first raw vegetable that children eat voluntarily.

**Kohlrabi and apple salad:** Coarsely grate a kohlrabi and a sour apple. Mix 3 tablespoons of yogurt, 1 tablespoon of lemon juice, 1 teaspoon of honey, salt and pepper into a dressing and mix in the kohlrabi, apple and a handful of chopped walnuts or sunflower seeds. Sprinkle with chives. The salad tastes fresh and lasts until the next day.

**Kohlrabi carpaccio:** Slice a peeled tuber into wafer-thin slices and place them overlapping on a plate. Drizzle them with good oil and a little lemon juice, salt lightly and sprinkle cress, radish slices or roasted nuts over them. After ten minutes of steeping, the slices are tender and pliable.

## Classic: kohlrabi vegetables in a light sauce

This is the kohlrabi as many people know it from childhood: in a mild, slightly nutty cream sauce, with boiled potatoes and meatballs or a fried egg.

### Ingredients for four people

- 3 kohlrabi with leaves
- Salt
- 25g butter
- 25g flour
- 150 ml milk or cream
- freshly grated nutmeg, white pepper
- a few splashes of lemon juice
- 2 tbsp chopped parsley

### Preparation

1. Peel the kohlrabi and cut it into sticks or slices of about half a centimeter. Finely chop the tender heart leaves.
2. Cook the kohlrabi pieces in enough salted water to just cover them, eight to 10 minutes, until they are al dente. Drain them, reserving the cooking water. You need around 350 milliliters for the sauce.
3. Melt the butter in a saucepan, stir in the flour and let the roux cook for a minute while stirring, without letting it brown.
4. Gradually add the cooking water, stirring constantly so that no lumps form. Add the milk or cream and let the sauce simmer gently for five minutes.
5. Season with salt, nutmeg, pepper and a squeeze of lemon juice.
6. Add the kohlrabi pieces and chopped leaves to the sauce and heat everything for two minutes. Sprinkle with parsley.

The cooking water is the trick: it contains a lot of the kohlrabi flavor and makes the sauce more aromatic than pure broth.

## Kohlrabi schnitzel

Breaded and fried, kohlrabi becomes a vegetarian main dish that is crispy on the outside and juicy on the inside.

1. Cut two large kohlrabi into slices about one centimeter thick.
2. Cook the slices in salted water for five to six minutes so that they still have some bite. Drain them and pat them dry.
3. Dredge them one after the other in flour, beaten egg with a little salt and breadcrumbs. For more spice, mix grated mountain cheese or paprika powder into the breadcrumbs.
4. Fry the slices in plenty of oil or clarified butter over medium heat for three to four minutes on each side until golden brown.

Potato salad, a herb dip or cranberries go well with this. Our article about [types of potatoes and types of cooking](/kueche/kartoffelsorten-kochtypen/) explains which potato you use for the salad.

## Quick stir-fry vegetables with ginger and sesame

Kohlrabi goes well with Asian flavors. Cut a tuber and two carrots into fine sticks, two spring onions into rings and a thumb-sized piece of ginger into fine cubes. Heat two tablespoons of neutral oil in a large pan or wok, fry the kohlrabi and carrots over high heat for three to four minutes while stirring, add the ginger and spring onions and deglaze with two tablespoons of soy sauce and a dash of water. After another minute the vegetables will be hot but still crunchy. Drizzle with some sesame oil and sprinkle toasted sesame seeds on top. Rice or noodles go well with this.

## More ideas

- **From the oven:** Roast the kohlrabi cubes with oil, salt and thyme at 200 degrees for 25 to 30 minutes until the edges are brown. When roasted, kohlrabi tastes nutty and sweet.
- **Soup:** Cook kohlrabi, a potato and an onion in broth until soft, puree and season with cream and nutmeg. The leaves as a green insert.
- **Filled:** Hollow out the tubers, pre-cook them and bake them in the oven with a minced meat or lentil filling.
- **In vegetable puree:** Kohlrabi is often used for babies because it is mild and easy to digest.

## What's in kohlrabi

Kohlrabi is low in calories and provides plenty of vitamin C, as well as potassium, folate and fiber. The green leaves also contain more carotenoids and minerals than the light tuber. Another reason not to throw away the delicate heart leaves. Because vitamin C is water-soluble, it is best to cook kohlrabi in a little water and use the cooking water for the sauce, as in the recipe above.

## Kohlrabi in your own garden

Kohlrabi is one of the fastest vegetables in the bed. Depending on the season, it only takes eight to twelve weeks from planting to harvesting. You can plant early varieties under fleece or in a cold frame from March onwards, and later varieties until summer. This means you can plant a new row every few weeks and harvest from May to October. Uniform moisture is important: if dry and wet phases alternate, the tubers burst open or become woody. Harvest them when they are about the size of a tennis ball to a fist. If you wait too long, you will get large but fibrous tubers.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you eat kohlrabi leaves?

Yes. The young, tender leaves from the center are very aromatic and can be steamed like spinach, added to soups or finely chopped and stirred into the sauce. Large, tough leaves with thick ribs are less pleasant.

### Why does my kohlrabi taste spicy?

Like all types of cabbage, kohlrabi contains mustard oil compounds. In drought and heat, the plant produces more of it. Short cooking makes spicy tubers milder.

### Can I freeze kohlrabi?

Yes, blanch in sticks or cubes for two to three minutes, rinse in ice water, drain well and freeze in portions. After that, it is no longer suitable for raw vegetables, but it is suitable for soups and creamed vegetables.

### How much kohlrabi do I need per person?

As a side dish, you should expect around one small to medium-sized tuber per person, i.e. 200 to 250 grams unpeeled. When peeling, a quarter to a third is lost, depending on the size and root end.
