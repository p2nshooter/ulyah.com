Beetroot has long suffered from a dusty reputation. Many people only know them from the jar, pickled in sweet and sour, as an accompaniment to labskaus or herring. The tuber is one of the most versatile winter vegetables. It tastes earthy and sweet, its color is spectacular, and it can be boiled, roasted, grated raw, pickled and even baked into cakes.

Beetroot is harvested in Germany from around July. The early, small tubers are often sold with leaves. The main fall harvest is stored and is available throughout the winter and into spring. This makes beetroot one of the vegetables that bring fresh color to your plate in the lean season.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Beetroot is cooked or baked unpeeled so that it does not bleed. Depending on the size, it takes 30 to 60 minutes.
> - After cooking, you can rub the peel with your fingers. Disposable gloves protect against red hands.
> - Grated raw, it tastes crunchy and fresh, especially with apple, lemon and nuts.
> - Stains on hands and boards can be removed with lemon juice or a little salt.
> - The young leaves are edible and can be prepared like chard.

## Shopping and storage

Good beetroot is firm, heavy and has a smooth, undamaged skin. Small to medium-sized tubers with a diameter of five to eight centimeters are usually more tender and cook faster than very large ones. If there are still leaves on them, they should look fresh and not wilted.

At home, you twist the leaves about three centimeters above the tuber without damaging the tuber itself. Otherwise the leaves would deprive it of moisture. Store them separately and use them within a day or two.

The tubers will last for two to three weeks in the vegetable compartment of the refrigerator, or even for several months in a cool cellar in a box with slightly damp sand. It is important that the shell remains intact and that the root tip is attached.

## In addition to red, there are also yellow and stripes

In addition to the classic red variety, in markets you can also find yellow beets, white varieties and ringed beets, the cut of which shows red-white rings, often under the name Chioggia. The yellow and white varieties do not stain and taste a little milder. When cooked, the beetroot loses its pattern and turns pale pink. It looks best raw in wafer-thin slices.

## Cook beets

Boiling is the easiest and fastest method.

1. Wash the tubers thoroughly under running water without damaging the peel. Leave the root tip and leaf base on.
2. Place them in a pot, cover them with water and add a teaspoon of salt and a dash of vinegar. The vinegar helps preserve the color.
3. Bring the water to the boil and let the tubers cook, covered, over medium heat. Small tubers need about 30 minutes, medium-sized tubers 40 to 50, very large tubers up to 60 minutes or more.
4. Check with a sharp knife: the tuber is cooked if it slides in without resistance.
5. Pour off the water and briefly rinse the tubers in cold water. You can then rub the peel with your fingers or a kitchen towel and cut off the root and leaf base.

In the pressure cooker, the cooking time is reduced to around 15 to 20 minutes.

## Beetroot from the oven

Cooked in the oven, beetroot tastes more concentrated and sweeter because it is not in water.

1. Preheat the oven to 200 degrees top and bottom heat.
2. Wrap the washed, unpeeled tubers individually in baking paper and then in aluminum foil, or place them in a baking dish with a lid and add a centimeter of water to it.
3. Cook for 45 to 75 minutes, depending on size, until a knife slides in easily.
4. Let them cool a little, then peel them.

For roasted beetroot with roasted aromas, peel the raw tubers, cut them into wedges, mix them with oil, salt and thyme and roast them on a tray at 200 degrees for 35 to 45 minutes until the edges caramelize. Goat cheese, walnuts and a few drops of honey go well with it.

## Raw beetroot

Raw beetroot is crunchy and earthy-sweet. Peel them with a vegetable peeler and grate them coarsely or cut them into wafer-thin slices with a slicer. For a simple raw vegetable salad, mix two grated tubers with a grated apple, the juice of half a lemon, two tablespoons of oil, salt, a little honey and roasted sunflower seeds.

Beets, like many leafy vegetables and roots, contain nitrate and oxalic acid. In normal quantities this is harmless to healthy people. However, anyone who is prone to kidney stones should not eat large quantities and discuss this with their doctor. For babies under one year of age, beetroot is only recommended in small quantities due to its nitrate content.

## Classic beetroot salad with apple and horseradish

This salad goes well with potato pancakes, fish, jacket potatoes or simply with a hearty bread.

For 4 people as a side dish:

- 600 g beetroot, cooked and peeled
- 1 sour apple, for example Boskoop
- 1 small red onion
- 3 tbsp apple cider vinegar
- 3 tbsp rapeseed oil
- 1 to 2 tsp freshly grated horseradish or 1 tbsp from the jar
- 1 teaspoon honey or sugar
- ½ tsp salt, pepper
- ½ tsp caraway, if desired
- some chives or dill

1. Cut the beets into small cubes or thin slices.
2. Peel the apple, remove the core and cut it into cubes. Dice the onion very finely.
3. Mix vinegar, oil, horseradish, honey, salt, pepper and caraway into a dressing.
4. Mix everything and let the salad sit for at least 30 minutes, preferably two hours. Season again before serving. Beetroot absorbs a lot of acid and salt.
5. Sprinkle with chives or dill.

The salad will keep in a sealed container in the refrigerator for three to four days.

## Sweet and sour pickled beetroot

For about four glasses of 400 milliliters each you need 1.5 kg of cooked, peeled beetroot in slices, 500 ml of water, 300 ml of white wine or brandy vinegar (5%), 150 g of sugar, 1 teaspoon of salt, 1 onion in rings, 2 bay leaves, 6 cloves and 1 teaspoon of mustard seeds. Boil the stock with all the spices for five minutes, layer the beetroot and onion rings in clean jars, pour the boiling stock over them so that everything is covered and close the jars. Boiled at 90 degrees for 30 minutes, they will last for about a year if stored in a dark and cool place. Without boiling, store the jars in the refrigerator and eat them within three to four weeks.

## Borscht: the most famous beetroot soup

In Eastern Europe, beetroot is the basis of an entire chapter of cuisine. The best known is borscht, whose Ukrainian culinary culture has been recognized by UNESCO as an intangible cultural heritage. For a simple version for four people, sauté an onion, a carrot and a piece of celery in strips in a little oil. Add 400 g of raw beetroot in fine strips and a tablespoon of tomato paste, deglaze with a dash of vinegar and pour in 1.2 liters of broth. After 15 minutes, add two diced potatoes and 200 g of finely chopped white cabbage. Let everything simmer for another 20 minutes until the vegetables are soft and season to taste with salt, pepper, a pinch of sugar and a crushed garlic clove. The borscht is served with a dollop of sour cream and fresh dill. Like many soups, it tastes even better the next day.

## Beetroot in cake

What sounds unusual at first is a tried and tested trick: finely grated, cooked beetroot makes chocolate cake particularly juicy, similar to carrots in carrot cake. For a sponge cake in a loaf pan, calculate about 250 g of pureed or finely grated tuber, which you fold into the dough. The color disappears in the cocoa, the earthy taste takes a backseat to the chocolate, and hardly anyone guesses the ingredient.

## Avoid and remove stains

The red pigment in beetroot, betanin, has a strong color. A few tricks help:

- Wear disposable gloves when peeling and cutting.
- Use a plastic board, which is easier to clean than wood, or place baking paper on the wooden board.
- Rub your hands and boards with lemon juice and a little salt, then wash them with soap.
- Rinse fresh stains on textiles immediately with cold water. Warm water can fix the stain.

## Use the leaves

The leaves of young beetroot taste similar to chard, to which the plant is closely related. Cut the stems into pieces and the leaves into strips. First, sauté the stems in a little butter or olive oil for two to three minutes, then add the leaves and let them wilt. Season with salt, pepper, garlic and a squeeze of lemon. Like the tuber, the leaves also contain oxalic acid, so eat them occasionally and cooked.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is red urine dangerous after eating beetroot?

No. In some people, the dye is not completely broken down and causes urine or stool to have a reddish color. This is harmless and goes away after a day or two. If you are unsure whether the coloring really comes from beetroot, or if it occurs without beetroot, have it checked by a doctor.

### Can I freeze beets?

Cooked and peeled, in cubes or slices, yes. It becomes a little softer after defrosting, but is good for soups, stews, smoothies or cakes. However, when frozen raw it becomes mushy.

### Why does my beetroot taste earthy?

The earthy taste comes from a natural substance called geosmin, which beetroot produces itself. Acidity and sweetness, such as vinegar, lemon, apple or honey, soften it. Roasting in the oven also makes the taste more rounded.
