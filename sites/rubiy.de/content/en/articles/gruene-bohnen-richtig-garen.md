Green beans come from the field from July to September, and then they are cheap, tender and full of flavor. Hardly any other vegetable goes so well with German summer cuisine: as a lukewarm bean salad with meatballs, as bacon beans for a Sunday roast or in northern Germany as “pears, beans and bacon”. However, there is one special feature that distinguishes beans from almost all other vegetables: raw, they are not only indigestible, but poisonous. If you know this and choose the cooking time correctly, you will have an uncomplicated and very versatile summer vegetable.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Raw green beans contain the protein phasin, which can cause stomach pain, vomiting and diarrhea. Only thorough cooking makes it harmless.
> - Cook beans in boiling water or steam for at least ten minutes. Brief blanching for two or three minutes is not enough.
> - Never allow beans to be eaten raw, not even by children, and do not add them raw to salads.
> - Savory is the classic spice: It tastes great and belongs in many regional recipes.
> - Fresh beans break with an audible crack. Store them in the vegetable compartment for a maximum of two or three days.

## Why green beans are not eaten raw

Beans are legumes, and many legumes contain lectins when raw. In common beans, this substance is called phasin. It causes red blood cells to stick together in the intestines and can irritate the intestinal wall. Even a few raw beans can cause nausea, abdominal cramps, vomiting and diarrhea in sensitive people and especially children. The symptoms often begin within a few hours.

The good news: Phasin is sensitive to heat. Cooking for a long enough time destroys it and cooked beans are completely safe. What is crucial is that the heat lasts long enough. That's why consumer agencies in Germany recommend cooking green beans for at least ten to fifteen minutes. That's longer than many modern recipes suggest, which only blanch beans until they're al dente. If you like very young, thin princess beans, you can immediately plunge them into ice water after ten minutes of cooking. This way they stay green and still have some bite.

The same applies to freezing: the usual short blanching only serves to preserve the color and enzymes. It does not replace cooking later. We explain how to properly prepare beans and other vegetables for the freezer in the article [Freezing vegetables and fruit] (/kueche/gemuese-einfrieren-und-blanchieren/).

## Which beans are there?

| variety | Appearance | cooking time | Good for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Princess beans, Kenya beans | very thin, round, delicate | 10 minutes | Side dish, bacon beans, salad |
| French beans | round, medium strength | 10 to 12 minutes | Bean salad, vegetable side dish |
| Runner beans | longer, often a little stronger | 12 to 15 minutes | Stews, braised dishes |
| Broad beans, cut beans | flat and wide, are cut diagonally | 12 to 15 minutes | Stew, “snipped beans” |
| wax beans | yellow, mild, buttery | 10 to 12 minutes | Salad, side dish with butter |

You should not confuse green beans with **broad beans**, also known as broad beans or broad beans. With them you only eat the large kernels from the pod. They are also only eaten cooked and have their own season in early summer.

## Shopping, storing and cleaning

Fresh beans are smooth, plump and brightly colored. If you bend a bean, it will break with a crack. Beans that are rubbery, limp or have brown spots and clearly visible seeds are old. Fresh beans will keep in a bag in the vegetable compartment of the refrigerator for two to three days. After that they quickly become tough.

When cleaning, cut off the ends or snap them off with your fingers. Most of today's varieties no longer have threads. Older varieties or large runner beans sometimes have a tough thread at the seams. You take it off when you snap it off. Whole beans are cooked, runner beans and broad beans are cut diagonally into three to four centimeter long pieces beforehand.

## Basic recipe: Cook beans safely and aromatically

1. Bring plenty of water to the boil and salt it generously, about a teaspoon of salt per liter.
2. Add a sprig of savory if you have some.
3. Add the beans to the bubbling water. From the time you boil it again, the cooking time counts: at least ten minutes for thin beans, up to fifteen minutes for strong beans.
4. Drain the beans. For salad or later processing, quench them in ice water to preserve the green color.
5. For a warm side dish, toss them directly in a little butter, salt, pepper and freshly chopped parsley or roasted savory.

**Steaming** is a good alternative: when using steam, beans take around twelve to fifteen minutes. They lose less flavor to the water, taste more intense and stay nice and green.

## Savory: the spice with the apt name

Savory tastes spicy and slightly peppery, which is why it is also known locally as pepperwort. You will come across two types in the kitchen: the annual summer savory with delicate leaves and the perennial winter savory, also known as mountain savory, which is stronger and somewhat bitter. Both go well not only with green beans, but also with lentils, white beans and potatoes. In popular cuisine, savory is considered a means of making legumes more digestible. Add fresh twigs to the cooking water and finally sprinkle a few finely chopped leaves over them. When dried, it retains its aroma well, so you can stock up in the summer for the winter.

## Lukewarm bean salad with savory

This salad is a classic with meatballs, fried potatoes or from the grill. For four people you need:

- 600 g French beans or princess beans
- 1 red or white onion, finely diced
- 3 tablespoons white wine or apple cider vinegar
- 4 tablespoons rapeseed oil or a mild olive oil
- 1 tsp medium hot mustard
- 1 teaspoon sugar or honey
- Salt, pepper
- 2 sprigs of savory, finely chopped, or 1 teaspoon dried
- optional: 80 g diced bacon or roasted sunflower seeds

Here's how to proceed: Cook the beans as described above and rinse them briefly so that they are still warm. Mix vinegar, mustard, sugar, salt, pepper and oil into a marinade and mix in the diced onions and savory. Add the beans while they are still warm. Warm beans absorb the marinade much better than cold ones. Let the salad sit for at least 30 minutes and then season it again because beans absorb a lot of salt and acid. If you like, fry the bacon until crispy and add it with a little frying fat over the finished salad.

Bean salad lasts for two days in the refrigerator. On the second day it is often even better, but loses some of its green color.

## Bacon beans: small bunches for frying

Bacon beans look festive and are still quick to make. Cook 400 grams of princess beans for ten minutes, rinse them and drain them well. Put eight to ten beans together in a bundle and wrap a slice of bacon around them. Fry the bundles in a pan with a little fat on all sides over medium heat until the bacon is crispy, or bake them in the oven at 200 degrees for 12 to 15 minutes. Bacon beans go well with roasts, roulades and potato gratin. For a meatless version, wrap the bundles in leek strips, drizzle them with oil and roast them in the oven with a little paprika powder.

## Pears, beans and bacon

The North German summer dish combines sweetness, salt and acidity. A piece of streaky bacon is cooked in water for about 30 minutes. Then add runner beans in pieces and whole, small cooking pears, seasoned with savory, salt and pepper. Everything cooks together for another 20 minutes until the beans and pears are soft. There are also boiled potatoes. At the end, the broth is lightly thickened with a little flour butter. If you don't eat bacon, use smoked tofu and a strong [vegetable broth](/kueche/gemuesebruehe-selbst-kochen/).

## Sour beans: stock the Baden way

In Baden and Swabia, green beans are traditionally fermented with lactic acid like sauerkraut. The beans are cleaned, cut into pieces, briefly cooked, layered with salt in a fermentation pot and weighed down until they are covered in their own brine. After a few weeks at a cool room temperature, they become pleasantly sour. Sour beans are cooked before eating, for example with bacon and potatoes to make a hearty stew. Fermentation does not necessarily make the phasin harmless, so cooking it before consumption remains mandatory. Our article [Make your own sauerkraut] (/kueche/sauerkraut-selbst-machen/) explains how lactic acid fermentation basically works.

Many experts advise against boiling green beans in a water bath. Beans are low in acid, and spores of the botulism pathogen are not reliably killed at temperatures of around 100 degrees. Freezing or souring are the safer options for the household.

## Beans in your own garden

Beans require heat. The seeds are only sown after the Ice Saints in mid-May, when the ground has warmed up to at least ten to twelve degrees. The seeds rot in cold, wet soil. Bush beans grow compactly and do not need any support, runner beans climb two to three meters high on poles or cords and therefore last longer. Harvest the pods young, every two to three days, before the kernels are clearly visible. Then they remain tender and the plant continues to produce new flowers. As legumes, beans bind nitrogen from the air with the help of nodule bacteria and leave behind nutrient-rich soil for the next crop.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are frozen beans already cooked?

Most of the time they are just blanched. Therefore, cook them like fresh beans and follow the instructions on the package. You should only accept a cooking time of less than ten minutes for frozen beans if the package explicitly states that the beans are already cooked.

### Can I fry beans in a wok until crispy?

Only if you have cooked them beforehand. In the wok, the beans do not reach the necessary temperature inside for long enough. Boil or steam them first and then briefly fry them with garlic and sesame seeds for roasted flavors.

### Why are my beans turning gray?

Gray, olive-colored beans occur when they are simmered with a lid on for too long in little water or are cooked with acid. Lots of sparkling water without a lid, quenching in ice water and vinegar only after cooking keep them green.
