Kale is the winter vegetable of the north. In Bremen, Oldenburg, East Frisia and large parts of Lower Saxony it is as much a part of winter as snow, which is rare there. Clubs, colleagues and groups of friends go on a kale trip, hike through the cold in a handcart and then stop at an inn where they serve kale with pee, smoked pork and fried potatoes. At the end, a cabbage king is crowned, usually the one who has eaten the most.

Kale has also gained many friends outside of the north, especially raw in salads or as chips. Anyone who has ever eaten it braised in the classic way knows why it is so loved in the north: when cooked for a long time, it becomes soft, mild and slightly sweet.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Kale is in season from around October to February. After cold nights it tastes milder and sweeter.
> - The leaves are plucked from the hard stems and washed thoroughly because sand is hidden in the curly leaves.
> - For the classic North German kale, the cabbage is braised with lard, onions and broth for 1.5 to 2 hours and thickened with oats.
> - Braised kale often tastes even better warmed up the next day.
> - It freezes well when blanched.

## This is how a cabbage trip works

A cabbage drive usually begins in the early afternoon with a walk along field paths and dikes. Drinks and provisions are carried in the handcart and there are games along the way. In East Frisia and the Oldenburger Land, Boßeln is often part of it, a Frisian competition in which a ball is thrown as far as possible over streets and paths. After a few kilometers, the group reaches the inn, where the kale is brought to the table in large bowls. The crowned Cabbage King, often together with a Cabbage Queen, not only has the honor but also a duty: he usually organizes the next year's cabbage drive.

## Kale and the frost

For a long time, the rule was to only harvest kale after the first frost. The reason: When it gets cold, the plant changes its metabolism. It converts some of its starch into sugar, which acts like an antifreeze. At the same time, sharp and bitter flavors decrease. This makes the cabbage taste milder and rounder.

The decisive factor is not the frost itself, but a phase with low temperatures in which the plant lives and grows. Cool nights around freezing point are sufficient for this. Today's varieties are also less bitter than before, which is why kale is now available from October. You can save yourself the trick of putting freshly harvested kale in the freezer to imitate the frost: a cut plant no longer produces sugar.

## Shop and prepare

Kale is sold either as a whole plant, loosely in leaves or already cleaned and cut in bags. Fresh kale has firm, dark green to blue-green leaves with no yellow spots. It is very productive, but you lose a lot of weight when cleaning: from two kilos of perennials, around one kilo of leaves remains.

This is how you clean it:

1. Pull the leaves off the stem.
2. Hold each leaf by the stem end and use your other hand to pull the greenery upwards. The thick midribs remain behind and are discarded.
3. Wash the leaves in a large sink with plenty of cold water. Give it a good shake, let it stand for a moment to allow the sand to sink, and lift it out. Repeat this two to three times until there is no more sand in the pool.
4. Blanch the leaves in portions in boiling salted water for two minutes, rinse them with cold water and squeeze them out.
5. Chop them roughly with the knife.

Blanching is not necessary, but it makes the cabbage milder and reduces the quantity so that it fits better in the pot.

## North German style kale

For 4 people:

- 2 kg of kale as a perennial or 1 kg of plucked leaves
- 3 large onions
- 3 tbsp goose or pork fat
- 750 ml meat or vegetable stock
- 3 to 4 tablespoons of wholesome oat flakes or oat groats
- 1 tsp medium hot mustard
- Salt, pepper, a pinch of sugar, some freshly grated nutmeg
- 4 pink sausages and 4 cabbage sausages or Mettenden
- 4 slices of smoked pork, about 150 g each, or a piece of smoked cheese comb weighing 600 g

1. Clean, blanch and chop the kale as described above.
2. Dice the onions. Heat the lard in a large, heavy pot and sauté the onions until translucent.
3. Add the kale, pour in the broth and bring everything to the boil. Cover and let the cabbage simmer over low heat for an hour.
4. Place the smoked pork, cabbage sausage and pee on the cabbage and stew everything for another 45 to 60 minutes. The pee can burst if it gets too hot, so the cabbage should only simmer gently. If you want to keep it intact, prick the skin a few times with a needle beforehand.
5. In the last half hour, sprinkle the oatmeal over the cabbage and stir it in. They bind the liquid and make the cabbage creamy. If you like, add it in two portions and check how thick the cabbage should be.
6. Season to taste with mustard, salt, pepper, sugar and nutmeg.

In Bremen and Oldenburg there are boiled potatoes or small potatoes glazed in butter and sugar, and in East Frisia they often have fried potatoes. A cold beer and a claret are part of the tradition for many.

### What is pee?

Pinkel is a smoked sausage made from bacon, oat or barley groats, onions, beef suet or lard and spices. It is not fried, but rather warmed in the cabbage and gives it fat and seasoning. When serving, the skin is cut open and the crumbly filling is eaten with the cabbage. Pee is not available everywhere outside of northern Germany. Cabbage sausage, Mettenden or coarse smoked sausages are good alternatives.

## Vegetarian kale

Even without meat, kale can taste strong. You get the smoky note that sausage and smoked pork usually provide from other ingredients.

For 4 people:

- 1 kg shredded kale
- 3 onions
- 3 tablespoons clarified butter or rapeseed oil
- 750 ml strong vegetable broth
- 3 tablespoons wholesome oat flakes
- 1 teaspoon smoked paprika powder
- 1 tsp medium hot mustard
- 200 g smoked tofu
- Salt, pepper, nutmeg, a pinch of sugar

The cabbage is braised as above, about 1.5 hours total. Add the paprika powder to the pot along with the onions. Cut the smoked tofu into cubes and fry it in a separate pan until crispy. It is only added over the cabbage when serving so that it does not become soft. Roasted sunflower seeds or a few drops of pumpkin seed oil round off the plate.

## Kale pesto

A kale pesto is a nice change from the braised version. Blanch 150 g of picked leaves for a minute, rinse them and squeeze them well. Puree them with 50 g of roasted walnuts or sunflower seeds, 40 g of grated hard cheese, a small clove of garlic, the juice of half a lemon and about 100 ml of rapeseed oil or olive oil. Season with salt and pepper. The pesto goes well with pasta, on bread and with baked potatoes and will keep for a few days in the fridge, covered with a little oil.

## Store and freeze kale

Fresh unwashed kale will last about four to five days in a bag in the vegetable compartment. Braised kale keeps well refrigerated for two to three days and tastes even better warmed up because the flavors combine.

To freeze, blanch the plucked leaves for two minutes, rinse them in ice water, squeeze them well and freeze them in portions. Ready-braised kale without sausage can also be frozen. It lasts several months. You can find out more about this topic in our article about [freezing and blanching vegetables](/kueche/gemuese-einfrieren-und-blanchieren/).

## Kale raw and quick

Young, tender leaves also taste good raw. To make a salad, tear them into small pieces, add some salt and lemon juice and knead them with your hands for two minutes. This causes the leaves to become softer and darker. It goes well with apple cubes, roasted walnuts, cranberries and a dressing made from mustard, honey and oil.

To make kale chips, mix dry, torn leaves with a little oil and salt and bake them at 130 degrees for 15 to 20 minutes. Stay close to the oven, the chips will burn quickly.

## What's in kale

Kale is one of the most nutrient-rich vegetables of all. It provides a lot of vitamin C, beta-carotene, folate and, for a vegetable, a noticeable amount of calcium. There are also fiber and secondary plant substances such as mustard oil glycosides, which are responsible for its bitter taste. It also contains plenty of vitamin K. Anyone who takes anticoagulant medications of the vitamin K antagonist type should avoid large fluctuations in consumption and discuss this with their doctor.

## Kale in your own garden

Kale is one of the hardiest winter vegetables. It is sown in May or June and planted out in July at a distance of around 50 centimeters. The plants grow over the summer and are harvested from October onwards. Always pick the outer, larger leaves and leave the heart. This way the plant continues to grow upwards and provides fresh leaves throughout the winter until February. Kale can also tolerate severe frosts and is often still in the bed long after everything else has been cleared away.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my kale bitter?

Maybe it wasn't in the cold long enough or it was a particularly strong variety. Blanching, longer braising and a pinch of sugar help. Too little fat also makes the taste more edgy.

### How much kale do I need per person?

For the main course, count around 500 grams of perennials or 250 grams of plucked leaves per person. Bought in a bag, already cut, about 250 to 300 grams are enough.

### Can I cook kale in the pressure cooker?

Yes. In the pressure cooker, the cabbage will be soft after 20 to 30 minutes. Then place the sausages and smoked meat in the open pot and let them steep in the cabbage over a low heat for 20 minutes.
