When the first plums are on the market in August, the Datschi season begins in Bavaria and Swabia. That's what the plum cake on the tin is called there, and Augsburg even calls itself "Datschiburg" with a wink. The principle is very simple: a thin yeast dough, on top of which are halved plums, cinnamon and sugar after baking, and a dollop of whipped cream. Precisely because the recipe is so simple, it's all about the details: the right fruits, the way you place them, and a few tricks to avoid a soggy base.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Use real plums, not round, juicy plums. Plums are oval, firmer and separate easily from the stone.
> - Do not cut the plums all the way through, but open them up and make slight cuts at two ends. Then place it tightly and at an angle like roof tiles.
> - A thin yeast dough is classic. Ground nuts or breadcrumbs on the bottom will absorb juice.
> - Sprinkle sugar only after baking. Beforehand, it draws juice from the fruit and the ground becomes wet.
> - Bake at 200 degrees top and bottom heat for about 30 to 35 minutes until the edges are lightly browned.

## Damson or plum?

Botanically they both belong to the same species, but in the kitchen they behave very differently.

| | Plum | Plum |
|---|---|---|
| shape | elongated-oval, tapering to a point at both ends | round, often with a clear seam |
| pulp | firm, rather dry, yellow-green | soft, very juicy |
| Stone | flat, comes off easily | often sticks to the pulp |
| When baking | retains its shape, becomes sweet and sour | melts, releases a lot of juice |

Plums are therefore ideal for cakes. The old variety of house plum, which ripens in September, is considered by many to be the best because it becomes particularly aromatic when baked. Earlier varieties from the end of July are often milder. Mirabelles and Reneklodes can also be baked on the tray, but they are sweeter and require less sugar.

Buy plums that are ripe but firm. By the way, slightly wrinkled fruits at the base of the stem are a good sign: they are sweet and aromatic. Sort out soft, cracked or moldy fruits.

### The plum season at a glance

| ripening time | Typical varieties | Character |
|---|---|---|
| End of July to beginning of August | Katinka | mild, dissolves well from the stone |
| Mid to late August | Hanita, Cacak's beauty | juicy, balanced |
| September | House plum | spicy, sour, ideal for baking |
| End of September to October | Presenta | late, sweet, stores well |

Times vary by one to two weeks depending on the region and weather. For the classic Datschi, it's worth waiting for the house plum.

## Ingredients for a baking tray

**yeast dough**

- 400 g wheat flour type 405 or 550
- ½ cube of fresh yeast (21 g) or 1 packet of dry yeast
- 200 ml lukewarm milk
- 50g sugar
- 60 g soft butter
- 1 egg
- 1 pinch of salt

**Topping**

- 2 to 2.5 kg plums
- 3 tablespoons ground hazelnuts, almonds or breadcrumbs
- 4 tablespoons sugar mixed with 1 teaspoon cinnamon
- optional: 2 tablespoons butter in flakes

## The yeast dough

1. Crumble the yeast into the lukewarm milk, add a teaspoon of sugar and stir until dissolved. The milk must not be hotter than about 37 degrees, otherwise the yeast will die.
2. Put the flour, remaining sugar, salt, egg and butter in a bowl, pour in the yeast milk and knead everything for at least five minutes to form a smooth, supple dough. It should come away from the edge of the bowl and stick slightly.
3. Cover the bowl and let the dough rise in a warm place for 45 to 60 minutes, until it has visibly increased in size.

While the dough is rising, prepare the plums. So the timing is perfect.

## Place the plums correctly

This is where it is decided whether the datschi looks like it comes from a pastry shop.

1. Wash the plums and drain them well. Wet fruit makes the dough soggy.
2. Cut each plum lengthwise, but not all the way through, and remove the stone.
3. Cut the two halves a little further at the tip without separating them. This means they can be stood upright without bulging.
4. Roll out the dough thinly, about 5 millimeters, directly on a baking tray lined with baking paper. Press a small bead up on the edge to prevent any juice from running out.
5. Sprinkle the ground nuts or breadcrumbs evenly over the dough.
6. Place the plums with the cut side up, slightly slanted and very close in rows, like roof tiles. Start on one long side. The fruits are allowed to support each other and overlap. They shrink when baked and plums that are set too loosely leave gaps.
7. Let the topped cake rise for another 15 minutes while the oven preheats.

## Baking

Preheat the oven to 200 degrees top and bottom heat. Bake the cake on the middle rack for 30 to 35 minutes. It is ready when the edge of the dough is golden brown and the plums are slightly caramelized at the tips. If you use butter pats, spread them over the fruit before baking.

Immediately after baking, sprinkle the cake with the cinnamon-sugar mixture. The sugar melts on the warm fruit and combines with the juice that comes out to form a light syrup. If you sprinkled the sugar beforehand, it would extract the juice from the plums in the oven and the dough underneath would become wet.

Let the dachi cool on the tray and cut it into rectangles. This includes lightly sweetened whipped cream.

## The most common mistakes and how to avoid them

- **The base is wet and soggy.** Most of the time the plums were wet, too juicy (plums instead of plums) or the sugar was added before baking. Nuts or crumbs as a base, well-drained fruit and sugar only at the end solve the problem. It is also helpful to place the tray on the lowest rack for the last ten minutes so that the base gets more heat.
- **The dough is too thick and bready.** For a datschi, the yeast dough is rolled out thin. He should bear the fruit, not play the main role. If you like more dough, you should bake a plum cake in a springform pan.
- **The plums are dry and leathery.** The fruit was too loose or the cake was in the oven for too long. Place them close enough to support each other and remove the cake once the edge has color.
- **The dough didn't rise.** The milk was too hot, the yeast was too old, or the place was too cool. An oven with a light on is a good place to go.

## Variants

**With sprinkles:** Knead 150 g flour, 100 g sugar, 100 g cold butter and a pinch of cinnamon to make sprinkles and distribute them over the plums before baking. In some families this is mandatory, in others it is sacrilege.

**With shortcrust pastry:** Instead of yeast dough, a short pastry made from 300 g flour, 150 g butter, 100 g sugar and an egg. The base will be crispier and a little firmer. Pre-bake the shortcrust pastry for 10 minutes before putting the plums.

**With curd oil dough:** Quickly and without rising time: Knead 150 g low-fat quark, 6 tbsp milk, 6 tbsp oil, 75 g sugar, 300 g flour and 1 packet of baking powder and roll out.

## Plum dumplings

In Bavaria and Austria, plum dumplings are one of the favorite dishes of the season. For about twelve dumplings, cook 500 g of floury potatoes as jacket potatoes, peel them hot and press them through the potato press. After cooling, knead them with 150 g flour, an egg, 30 g soft butter and a pinch of salt to form a smooth dough. Stone twelve plums without cutting them in half and put a sugar cube in each one. Cover each fruit with a sheet of dough, form round dumplings and let them sit in gently simmering salted water for about twelve minutes. The water must not bubble, otherwise they will fall apart. In the meantime, roast 80 g of breadcrumbs in 80 g of butter until golden brown, mix in a tablespoon of sugar and a little cinnamon and roll the drained dumplings in it.

## Plum jam from the oven

When the harvest is large, the plums are made into plum jam, called Powidl in Austria. Halve and stone 2.5 kg of ripe plums, mix them in a roasting pan with 250 g sugar, a teaspoon of cinnamon and two cloves and let them steep overnight. Place the roaster without a lid in the oven at 160 degrees and let the puree simmer for two to three hours. Stir occasionally and, if there is a lot of liquid, jam a wooden spoon into the oven door to let steam escape. The puree is ready when it is dark, thick and spreadable and a spoonful can almost stand in it. Take out the cloves, puree it as desired and fill it hot into clean screw-top jars. Plum jam goes well on bread, in Buchteln and with Kaiserschmarrn.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I use frozen plums?

Yes, if you place them frozen on the dough and top them well with nuts or crumbs. Thawed plums lose a lot of juice. The baking time is extended by about ten minutes. Our article [Freezing vegetables and fruit] (/kueche/gemuese-einfrieren-und-blanchieren/) explains how to freeze plums for the winter.

### How long does plum datschi last?

It tastes best on the day of baking. Covered and kept at room temperature until the next day. The yeast dough then becomes dry. You can freeze pieces and bake them at 150 degrees for ten minutes.

### Where to put the remaining plums?

Cooked in a light syrup, as plum jam or dried, they last until winter. You can find instructions in [Preserving for beginners](/kueche/einkochen-grundlagen/) and [Drying fruits and herbs](/kueche/obst-und-kraeuter-trocknen/).

### Why did the edge of the dough become hard?

Most of the time the dough was rolled out too thin around the edge or the cake was in the oven for too long. Leave a small bead on the edge and brush it with a little melted butter after baking. If cooled under a clean tea towel, the edge will remain softer.
