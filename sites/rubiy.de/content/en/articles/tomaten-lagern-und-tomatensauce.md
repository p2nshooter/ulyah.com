A tomato that has ripened in the sun has little in common with the pale, hard fruit on the winter shelf. It is fragrant right from the base of the stem, is juicy, sweet and at the same time has a fresh acidity. Such tomatoes are available from German open-air cultivation from around July to October, and even earlier from heated or unheated greenhouses. The peak is in August and September, when gardens and market stalls are overflowing and tomatoes are often available for cheap by the crate.

This is the moment to stock up: as sauce, as sugo, as preserved passata. To ensure that the fruits retain their aroma until then, it is important to store them correctly.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Store ripe tomatoes at room temperature, not in the refrigerator. Below about twelve degrees they lose their aroma.
> - Tomatoes release ethylene and cause neighboring fruits and vegetables to ripen faster. Store them separately.
> - Green spots at the base of the stem and unripe green fruits contain tomatin, a bitter substance. Cut them out generously.
> - To peel the skin, score the skin crosswise, put the tomatoes in boiling water for 20 to 30 seconds and plunge them into ice water.
> - To make a good fresh tomato sauce you need patience: it simmers for 45 to 60 minutes until it is thick enough.

## Which tomato for what?

There are thousands of varieties of tomatoes. In retail and on the market you will mainly encounter these groups:

| Type | Features | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Round tomatoes (salad tomatoes) | medium-sized, juicy, lots of fruity fluid | Salad, bread, stuffing |
| Vine tomatoes | harvested on the panicle, often more aromatic | Salad, oven, quick sauce |
| Bottle or plum tomatoes (e.g. Roma, San Marzano) | elongated, firm flesh, few seeds and juice | Sauce, passata, drying |
| Beef tomatoes (ox heart etc.) | big, meaty, mild | Carpaccio, salad, stuffing |
| Cherry and cocktail tomatoes | small, very sweet, burst easily | Snack, salad, stewing in the oven |
| Old and colored varieties | yellow, orange, green striped, almost black | Lettuce to show off color and flavor |

Bottle tomatoes are best suited for sauce because they have a lot of pulp and little water. The sauce thickens more quickly and has a more concentrated flavor. It also works with round tomatoes, you just have to cook them for longer.

## Store tomatoes properly

Tomatoes come from warm regions and are sensitive to cold. In the refrigerator at four to seven degrees, aromatic substances are broken down and the pulp becomes mealy. The loss of taste cannot be reversed even by reheating it later. Therefore, store tomatoes at room temperature, in a shady place, with the stem base facing down. This means they dry out more slowly.

Tomatoes release the ripening gas ethylene. Cucumbers, lettuce, broccoli and herbs near them will wilt faster. So put them in their own bowl. You can use the opposite effect: Half-ripe tomatoes ripen faster next to an apple in a paper bag.

Only cut tomatoes or ready-made tomato salads belong in the refrigerator. Take them out half an hour before eating, then they will taste more rounded again.

### Green tomatoes

Unripe, green tomatoes and the green areas around the base of the stem contain the alkaloid tomatine, which is related to bitter substances such as solanine in potatoes. In larger quantities it can cause nausea and stomach upset. Cut out green areas generously. Green tomatoes from the garden that no longer ripen before the first frost can be wrapped in newspaper and left to ripen at room temperature. There are recipes for chutney or jam made from green tomatoes, but you should only enjoy them occasionally and in small quantities. Varieties that remain green even when ripe, such as green-striped varieties, are not affected by this. You can tell they are ripe when they give in to light pressure and smell.

## Peel and seed the tomatoes

Tomatoes are often skinned for a fine sauce or soup because the skin contracts into tough rolls when cooked.

1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil and prepare a bowl of ice water.
2. Cut out the stem base of each tomato in a wedge shape and score the skin on the underside in a cross pattern.
3. Add the tomatoes in portions to the bubbling water for 20 to 30 seconds. Very ripe tomatoes need less, firmer ones need a little more.
4. Lift them out with the slotted spoon and immediately place them in the ice water.
5. Peel off the skin at the scratched areas. It now almost resolves itself.

To remove the seeds, cut the tomatoes in half crosswise and squeeze out the seeds with your finger or a teaspoon. This is not necessary for a sauce, but it is often useful for salads, bruschetta or fillings so that too much juice does not escape.

If you process a lot of tomatoes, you save yourself the hassle of skinning them and strain the cooked sauce through a lotte or strainer at the end. This leaves skin and seeds behind.

## Tomato sauce made from fresh tomatoes

This sauce is intentionally simple. It thrives on the taste of ripe tomatoes and is suitable for pasta, as a base for pizza, for stews or for freezing.

For about 1 liter of sauce:

- 2 kg ripe bottle tomatoes (or 2.5 kg round tomatoes)
- 2 medium onions
- 3 cloves of garlic
- 4 tbsp olive oil
- 1 teaspoon salt, more to taste
- 1 pinch of sugar, only if the tomatoes are very sour
- 1 small bunch of basil or 2 sprigs of oregano

1. Skin the tomatoes as described above or leave the skin on when straining the sauce later. Cut them into rough pieces and remove the stems.
2. Finely dice the onions and cut the garlic into thin slices.
3. Heat the olive oil in a wide pot. The wider the pot, the faster the water will evaporate later. Sauté the onions over medium heat for eight to ten minutes until translucent and soft, without allowing them to brown. Add the garlic for the last minute.
4. Add the tomatoes and salt to the pot. Bring everything to the boil and then let the sauce simmer uncovered over a low heat for 45 to 60 minutes. Stir regularly, especially towards the end, to ensure nothing sticks.
5. The sauce is ready when a wooden spoon that you drag across the bottom of the pot leaves a trail that only slowly closes again.
6. Puree as desired or pass through the Lotte liquor. Season with salt and possibly a pinch of sugar. Pluck the basil at the very end and stir it in so that it stays fresh.

### Variant from the oven

If you have a lot of time but little desire to stir, halve the tomatoes, place them cut side up on a deep tray, distribute onion wedges and unpeeled garlic cloves between them, drizzle everything with olive oil and salt. Cook the tomatoes at 180 degrees for about an hour until they have collapsed and are lightly browned on the edges. Then squeeze the garlic out of the peel and puree everything. This sauce tastes toastier and sweeter.

## Make it durable

Once cooled, the sauce will keep in a sealed container in the refrigerator for about three to four days. Frozen in portion containers or freezer bags, it lasts at least six months, often significantly longer. Leave some room when freezing because liquid expands when it freezes.

Tomatoes are good for preserving because they contain a relatively high amount of acid. However, the acidity varies depending on the variety and degree of maturity, and onions, garlic and oil make the sauce less acidic. If you want to cook down tomato sauce, add about two tablespoons of lemon juice per liter so that it is acidic enough and cook it at 90 to 100 degrees for at least 30 minutes. For sauces with oil, lots of onions or vegetables, freezing is a safer choice. You can read all about the basics in our article about [boiling](/kueche/einkochen-grundlagen/).

## Quick ideas for ripe tomatoes

- **Tomato salad:** Sliced tomatoes, salt, freshly ground pepper, finely chopped onion or chives, a dash of mild vinegar and good oil. Let it steep for ten minutes.
- **Bread with tomato:** Grate toasted bread with a halved clove of garlic, grate a very ripe tomato on top, add salt and drizzle with olive oil, just like you eat in Spain.
- **Braised cherry tomatoes:** Braise in the oven with olive oil, salt and thyme for 20 minutes at 200 degrees. Goes well with pasta, polenta or goat cheese.
- **Cold tomato soup:** Puree a kilo of very ripe tomatoes with half a cucumber, a red pepper, a piece of white bread, a clove of garlic, vinegar, oil and salt and cool well.

## Tomatoes in your own garden

The most flavorful tomatoes are the ones you harvest yourself. Tomatoes are only brought outside in a sunny, warm place after the Ice Saints in mid-May. The biggest enemy is late blight, a fungal disease that spreads rapidly when leaves are wet. A rain shelter or a small tomato house protects the plants, and watering is always done directly at the roots, never over the leaves. With stick tomatoes, you regularly break out the side shoots in the leaf axils, the so-called pinching out. The plant puts its energy into fewer, larger fruits. Dark, sunken spots on the underside of the fruit are called blossom end rot. It occurs due to a lack of calcium in the fruit, usually caused by irregular watering. Even moisture prevents this.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do supermarket tomatoes taste so bland in winter?

They are often harvested unripe so that they can survive long transport and only ripen in storage. There are also varieties that are bred for durability and yield and refrigerated storage. In winter, it's better to use canned tomatoes or your frozen summer sauce for sauces.

### Do I have to remove the seeds so that the sauce doesn't become bitter?

No. The seeds are not bitter. They just make the sauce a little runnier and grainier. A sauce is more likely to become bitter if garlic has been roasted too dark or green spots have been cooked.

### Can I still use burst tomatoes?

If they are freshly burst, not moldy and smell good, yes. But process them into sauce or soup the same day. If there is mold, throw away the whole tomato because the fungal network in the soft, water-rich flesh can extend far beyond the visible area.
