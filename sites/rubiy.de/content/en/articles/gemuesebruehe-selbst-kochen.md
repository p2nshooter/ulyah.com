A good broth is the silent foundation of the kitchen. It adds flavor to soups, stews, risottos and sauces, it makes bean salads round and kohlrabi vegetables aromatic. Store-bought bouillon cubes are convenient, but often taste mainly of salt and yeast extract. Homemade broth, on the other hand, is almost free: it is made from peels, ends and leftovers that would otherwise end up in organic waste, and from a few hours of patience. Here you will find out how to make a clear vegetable broth, how to make a strong bone broth and how to always have broth in the fridge with a salt-vegetable paste.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Collect clean vegetable scraps such as carrot peels, leek greens, celery scraps, and parsley stalks in a bag in the freezer until you have enough for stock.
> - Cabbage, potato peels, beetroot and lots of onion peels make broth bitter, cloudy or very colored.
> - Only let the broth simmer gently, do not boil until bubbling, otherwise it will become cloudy.
> - Vegetable broth takes 45 to 60 minutes, bone broth takes three to six hours.
> - Do not boil the broth in a water bath. It is low in acid. Freeze them or keep them in the fridge for a few days.

## The basic ingredients

The classic basis for broth in Germany is **soup green**: a bunch of carrot, a piece of celeriac, leek and parsley, in some regions with parsley root. There are also onions and spices.

| ingredient | effect in the broth |
|---|---|
| carrots | Sweetness, color |
| Celeriac or celeriac | spicy depth, typical broth taste |
| Leeks, especially the green ones | mild onion aroma |
| Onions, halved and fried | Color and roasted aroma |
| Parsley stems, parsley root | fresh seasoning, more about this in [parsnips and parsley roots](/kueche/pastinaken-und-petersilienwurzeln/) |
| Tomato or leftover tomatoes | Umami and slight acidity |
| Leftover mushrooms, dried mushrooms | strong, fleshy note |
| Bay leaf, peppercorns, allspice, juniper | seasoning |
| Lovage | intense “Maggikraut” taste, use sparingly |

**It's better not to put it in the broth:** Leftover cabbage, broccoli stalks and Brussels sprout leaves make it sulphurous. Potato peels release starch and cloud the broth. Beetroot turns everything red. Vegetables that are already wilted, slimy or moldy belong in the organic waste, not in the pot. Broth concentrates flavor, even bad ones.

## Collect vegetable scraps

Place a large freezer bag or container in the freezer. Every time you clean vegetables, clean leftovers come in: carrot peels and ends, celery greens, leek greens, onion ends, parsley stalks, mushroom stalks, tomato seeds and fennel greens. Wash the vegetables before peeling so that no soil gets into the broth. Once the bag is full, about 500 to 800 grams, cook broth. This creates a supply at the same time, in the spirit of our [leftover kitchen with a plan](/kueche/resteverwertung-in-der-kueche/).

## Basic recipe: clear vegetable broth

### Ingredients for about 2 liters

- 800 g of vegetables and vegetable scraps, including at least one carrot, a piece of celery and some leek
- 2 onions
- 1 tbsp oil
- 2.5 liters of cold water
- 2 bay leaves, 1 teaspoon peppercorns, 3 allspice seeds
- a few parsley stalks
- 1 tsp salt

### Preparation

1. **Roast the onions:** Halve the onions with the peel on and place them cut side down in a dry pot. Roast them over medium heat until the cut sides are a deep dark brown. This gives the broth color and roasted aroma.
2. **Sauté vegetables:** Add the oil and roughly chopped vegetables and sauté for five minutes until fragrant.
3. **Pour in:** Pour in the cold water and add the spices. Cold water draws more flavor from the vegetables than hot water.
4. **Simmer gently:** Bring the broth slowly to a boil and then let it simmer, uncovered, over low heat for 45 to 60 minutes. Only a few bubbles should rise to the surface. Vigorous boiling emulsifies cloudy matter into the broth.
5. **Strain:** Pour the broth through a fine sieve. For a particularly clear broth, line the sieve with a cloth. Don't squeeze the vegetables.
6. **Taste:** Salt only at the end and only lightly. This means you can later season or reduce the broth to suit any dish without it becoming too salty.

Cooking vegetable broth for longer than an hour is of no use. The vegetables are leached and can become bitter.

## Mushroom broth for vegetarian cuisine

If you're looking for a vegetarian broth with depth, try dried mushrooms. Soak 20g of dried porcini mushrooms or shiitake in half a liter of warm water for 20 minutes. Roast two halved onions as in the basic recipe, add 300 g fresh sliced ​​mushrooms, a carrot and a piece of celery and sweat everything until the mushrooms take on color. Pour the soaked mushrooms with the soaking water through a coffee filter and another 1.5 liters of water. After 45 minutes of gentle simmering, strain and season with a dash of soy sauce. This broth supports risotto, lentil stews and gravies without meat.

## Strong beef broth made from roasted bones

Bone broth takes more time, but hardly more work.

1. Place 1.5 to 2 kg of beef bones, preferably with some meat, for example leg slices and marrow bones, on a baking tray and roast them at 200 degrees for about 40 minutes until they are browned.
2. Place the bones in a large pot and cover with about 3.5 liters of cold water. Loosen the roasting grounds from the tray with a little water and add it.
3. Bring everything slowly to the boil and skim off the gray foam that rises in the first half hour.
4. Let the broth steep quietly for three to four hours, or up to six hours for even more body. In the last hour, add greens, roasted onions and spices.
5. Strain the broth and let it cool. Place them in the refrigerator overnight. The fat solidifies on the surface and can be removed as a plate.

A good bone broth will gel in the refrigerator. This is a sign that collagen has been released from the bones and will later give full body to soups and sauces.

### What happens to the cooked meat?

The meat from leg slices or soup meat is tender after several hours and is by no means worthless. Tear it into bite-sized pieces and add it back to the soup, or make a beef salad out of it: Cut the meat into fine strips, dress with onion rings, vinegar, oil, a little broth, mustard and chives and let it sit for an hour. The cooked vegetables, on the other hand, hardly have any taste and go into the compost.

## Clarify the broth like in a restaurant

To make a crystal-clear broth, for example as a starter on a holiday, clarify the broth with protein. Whisk two egg whites with a little cold water and a handful of finely chopped soup vegetables. Stir the mixture into 1 liter of cold, defatted stock and heat slowly, stirring gently. As soon as the egg white sets and rises to the surface, stop stirring. The coagulating protein binds the cloudy substances and forms a lid. Let the broth simmer quietly underneath for 20 to 30 minutes and then carefully pour it off through a cloth. The result is clear as tea.

## Faster with the pressure cooker

The cooking time is significantly reduced in a pressure cooker. Vegetable broth is ready after about 15 to 20 minutes, bone broth after about one and a half to two hours. Fill the pot no more than to the maximum mark and release the pressure slowly after cooking so that the broth does not foam and the valve remains free. The broth will not be any clearer than in an open pot, but this is not a disadvantage for soups and stews.

## Reserve broth

- **Refrigerator:** Allow the broth to cool quickly, for example in a cold water bath, and store it covered in the refrigerator. It stays there for three to four days. Bring to the boil once before using.
- **Freezing:** Pour broth into freezer containers, bags or ice cube trays in portions. This will keep it for three to six months. Ice cubes from boiled broth are practical for seasoning sauces.
- **Preserving:** We advise against this. Broth is low in acid, and boiling it in a water bath does not reliably kill spores of the botulism pathogen. [Preserving for beginners](/kueche/einkochen-grundlagen/) explains why this is the case.

If you want to save space, boil the strained broth uncovered to a third and freeze the concentrate. To use, dilute it again with water.

## Soup seasoning in a jar: salt and vegetable paste

A paste made from fresh vegetables and lots of salt replaces bouillon cubes and keeps for a long time in the fridge.

1. Clean 500 g of soup vegetables, for example carrots, celery, leeks, an onion and a large bunch of parsley, and chop everything roughly.
2. Chop the vegetables very finely in a blender or speed chopper.
3. Mix in 100 g of salt. The ratio of about one part salt to five parts vegetables is important because it is this amount of salt that makes the paste last.
4. Pour the paste into clean screw-top jars, press them firmly so that no air bubbles remain and store them in the refrigerator.

One teaspoon of paste in 250 milliliters of hot water makes one cup of broth. The paste will keep well refrigerated for several months as long as you always remove it with a clean spoon. If it smells fermented or shows mold, throw the jar away.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my broth cloudy?

It was overcooked or there were starchy ingredients like potato peelings. This doesn't matter for soups and stews. For a clear broth, just let it simmer gently next time.

### Can I cook chicken broth the same way?

Yes. Take a soup chicken or chicken carcasses, put them cold and let them sit for two to three hours. The vegetables are added in the last hour.

### How much salt should you put in broth?

For a drinking broth, around 6 to 8 grams per liter. For a stock stock that you later cook down or use for sauces, add little or no salt.

### Can I cook onion peels?

In moderation, yes. A few washed peels give the broth a nice golden color. Too many, on the other hand, make them very dark and slightly bitter. One to two onions with skins per two liters of broth is a good guideline.
