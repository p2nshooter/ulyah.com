In Frankfurt am Main it is more than just a sauce: the “Grie Soß” is as much a part of the city as apple wine. As soon as the first herbs come from the nurseries in the Oberrad district in spring, there are packages wrapped in white paper with exactly seven herbs on the markets. Traditionally, the sauce is served for the first time on Maundy Thursday, with jacket potatoes and hard-boiled eggs. The sauce is cold, fresh, slightly sour and tastes as green as it looks. And it is a wonderful example of how good regional, seasonal cuisine can be without much effort.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - The seven herbs are borage, chervil, cress, parsley, pimpinelle, sorrel and chives.
> - Allow 250 to 300 grams of herbs for four people, i.e. a classic package.
> - The base of sour cream, yoghurt or quark, boiled egg yolk, oil, mustard and a little vinegar makes the sauce creamy.
> - Chop the herbs very finely, preferably with a chopping knife. Pulse briefly in the mixer.
> - Let the sauce sit for at least an hour and eat the same or next day.

## The seven herbs

The name “Frankfurter Grüne Sauce” has been registered as a protected geographical indication in the European Union since 2016. This also specifies which herbs belong in it and that they must come from the Frankfurt region. Each of the seven herbs brings something of its own:

| herb | taste | Roll in the sauce |
|---|---|---|
| Parsley | fresh, spicy | green base, lots of volume |
| Chives | mildly oniony | Spice, freshness |
| Chervil | slightly aniseed, sweet | fine, round aroma |
| Borage | cucumber-like | Freshness, some juiciness |
| Pimpinelle (Little Meadow Button) | nutty, slightly cucumbery | typical, unmistakable basic tone |
| sorrel | lemony-sour | Acidity, liveliness |
| Cress | spicy, mustardy | Whistle and spiciness |

The mixture is not equal parts. Parsley and chives usually make up the largest proportion, while sorrel and cress only make up a small proportion because they are dominant. If you are putting together the herbs yourself, a good rule of thumb is: a fifth each of parsley and chives, the rest evenly distributed among the other five herbs, with a little less sorrel and cress.

You will rarely find the finished packages outside of Hesse. Parsley, chives, chervil and cress are available almost everywhere. You can get borage, pimpinelle and sorrel at weekly markets, in garden centers or you can grow them in pots. If a herb is missing, the sauce will still be good, just not in the strict sense of “Frankfurter”.

## A little history of semolina sauce

Similar cold herb sauces are known from France as sauce verte and from Italy as salsa verde. It is not clear how the sauce came to Frankfurt. A widespread assumption attributes them to immigrants from southern Europe or to Huguenot religious refugees. It is also often said that Goethe already loved her, but this has not been proven.

What is certain is that the garden centers in the Oberrad district have been growing the herbs for generations and the sauce is part of Frankfurt's identity. In Oberrad there is a monument made up of seven small greenhouses, one for each herb, and every spring the town celebrates its own green sauce festival, where innkeepers compete against each other with their recipes.

## Ingredients for four people

- 250 to 300 g of seven types of herbs
- 4 eggs
- 200 g sour cream or sour cream
- 250 g yogurt (3.5% fat)
- 2 tablespoons rapeseed oil or a mild vegetable oil
- 1 tsp medium hot mustard
- 1 to 2 tablespoons mild white wine vinegar or lemon juice
- Salt, black pepper, a pinch of sugar
- plus: 1 kg waxy or predominantly waxy potatoes and 4 to 8 more eggs for serving

Instead of yogurt, some families use quark with a little milk or mineral water. This creates a thicker sauce. With just sour cream it becomes rich, with just yoghurt it becomes lighter and fresher.

## Preparation

1. **Cooking eggs:** For the sauce, hard-boil the four eggs for ten minutes, then rinse and peel them. Separate egg yolks and whites.
2. **Wash herbs:** Wash the herbs in plenty of cold water so that the sand and soil separate. Shake them dry well or spin them in the salad spinner. Wet herbs water down the sauce.
3. **Prepare herbs:** Pluck the leaves from thick stems, especially parsley, borage and pimpinelle. Fine stems of chervil and cress are allowed.
4. **Chopping:** Chop the herbs very finely. The aroma is best preserved with a chopping knife. In a blender or speed chopper, add the herbs with a little yoghurt and work with short pulses so that they don't turn into a warm paste.
5. **Stir the base:** Crush the egg yolks very finely with a fork or strain them through a sieve. Mix it with mustard, oil and vinegar, then with sour cream and yoghurt to form a smooth cream.
6. **Mix:** Stir in the herbs. Chop the egg white finely and add it.
7. **Taste:** Season with salt, pepper and a pinch of sugar. The sauce should be fresh and sour, but not spicy.
8. **Let it steep:** Cover and refrigerate the sauce for at least an hour. Taste again before serving as the herbs absorb salt.

## Plus: jacket potatoes and eggs

Boil the potatoes with their skin on in salted water for about 20 to 25 minutes until they are cooked through. Peel them warm or serve them with the peel, as is customary in Frankfurt apple wine taverns. There are also one to two halved hard-boiled eggs per person. We explain which potatoes are best suited for jacket potatoes in [Potato types and cooking types](/kueche/kartoffelsorten-kochtypen/).

In addition to the vegetarian version, the green sauce is also popular with cooked beef (Tafelspitz), schnitzel or fried fish.

## Typical mistakes

- **Wet herbs:** They water down the sauce and make it thin. After washing, dry the herbs thoroughly.
- **Too long in the blender:** The blades heat the herbs, they become bitter and the color becomes dull. It's better to chop by hand or just pulse briefly.
- **Too much sorrel or cress:** Both herbs quickly overwhelm the others. Stick to small portions.
- **Served too early:** Without resting time, the sauce tastes like individual herbs instead of a whole. An hour in the fridge combines the flavors.
- **Mixed too tightly:** With a lot of curd, the sauce turns into a paste. Dilute it with a little milk or mineral water until it runs creamy from the spoon.

## Use leftovers wisely

If there is any sauce left over, it is a good spread the next day on strong farmer's bread, with radish slices on top. It goes well as a dip with [asparagus](/kueche/spargel-schaelen-kochen/), fried potatoes and baked potatoes. Diluted with a little more vinegar and oil, it becomes a dressing for a potato salad. However, you shouldn't keep them for longer than a day because of the eggs.

## Variants from the region

In **Kassel** and Northern Hesse the sauce is also called green sauce, but is often made with other herbs, including dill, lemon balm or lovage, and often with mayonnaise instead of yogurt. In the **Mainzer** version, tarragon and lemon balm are occasionally added. These variations are not a mistake, but show how much the sauce runs in families.

In some areas, a **warm green sauce** is prepared as a cream sauce with roux and broth and refined with the herbs just before serving. It goes well with boiled potatoes and boiled meat.

## Pull the seven herbs yourself

If you make green sauce often, you can grow the hard-to-find herbs yourself. All seven grow easily in pots on the balcony or in a small bed.

- **Pimpinelle** is perennial, hardy and undemanding. One pot is enough for many sauces. Cut off the flower stems so that it produces lots of young leaves.
- **Sorrel** is also perennial and sprouts early every spring. Young leaves are more tender and less acidic than old ones.
- **Borage** is an annual, but likes to self-seed. It needs some space and has blue flowers, which attracts bees.
- **Chervil** grows quickly and blooms early when it is warm. Re-sow it every three to four weeks and place it in partial shade.
- **Cress** germinates on the windowsill in a few days and is ready to harvest after a week.
- **Parsley and chives** belong in every herb pot anyway.

Because sorrel contains oxalic acid, people prone to kidney stones should only eat it in small amounts. Its proportion in the sauce is small.

## Keep herbs fresh

It's best to buy herbs for green sauce the same day or the day before. Wrap them in a damp kitchen towel and place them in a bag in the vegetable compartment. This way they stay crisp for a day or two. Borage and chervil wilt particularly quickly. You can freeze remaining herbs finely chopped. Afterwards, they are only suitable for green sauce to a limited extent because they become soft when thawed. But they are good in soups, herb quark or herb butter.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does green sauce last?

Chill well for about a day. Because of the eggs and fresh herbs, you shouldn't keep them longer. On the second day it also loses color and freshness.

### Can I make the sauce without eggs?

Yes. Without the egg yolk it will be a little less creamy. A tablespoon more mustard and a little oil balance this out. With plant-based yogurt and oil it becomes vegan.

### Is the sauce available all year round?

In Frankfurt the herbal packets are available from March to October. The herbs are most aromatic in spring and early summer.

### Can I use dried herbs?

No. Dried herbs have neither the color nor the freshness that the sauce relies on. Frozen herb mixtures are at best an emergency solution in winter, as the sauce becomes softer and paler.

### Is Pimpinelle the same as Bibernelle?

Not quite. In the green sauce, Pimpinelle refers to the small meadow button, a rose plant with round, toothed pinnules and a light cucumber aroma. The Bibernelle is an umbelliferous plant that is also locally called Pimpinelle. When purchasing, pay attention to the name Kleiner Wiesen Knopf.
