In German households, a large amount of food ends up in the trash every year, a significant portion of which would still have been edible. Above all, fruit, vegetables, bread and cooked leftovers are thrown away. Leftover cooking used to be a given: stale bread was used to make dumplings and poor knights, potatoes from the previous day were made into fried potatoes, and vegetable trimmings were made into broth.

Cooking seasonally and using leftovers go hand in hand. Those who buy whatever is in abundance at the market sometimes end up with more than they can eat at once. With a few basic rules and ideas, what's left becomes the basis for the next meal.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Cool cooked leftovers quickly, preferably within one to two hours, and store them covered in the refrigerator.
> - Most cooked leftovers last two to three days in the refrigerator. When reheating, you heat them thoroughly once so that they are hot through and through.
> - Cool cooked rice particularly quickly and eat it the next day if possible.
> - Cook an aromatic broth from vegetable peels and trimmings collected in a freezer bag.
> - The best before date is not an expiry date. Check with your eyes, nose and tongue.

## Best before date and use by date

Two dates on packaging are often confused:

| Information | What it means | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Best before | Until this date, the manufacturer guarantees taste, color and consistency if stored correctly. | The food is often good for a long time afterwards. Look at it, smell it, taste it carefully. |
| To be consumed by | For perishable foods such as minced meat, raw poultry or fish. | Do not eat after the date. |

Yoghurt, cheese, pasta, rice, flour, canned goods and many other foods are usually still perfect after the best-before date. However, if there is mold, an unusual smell, domed lids or fermentation bubbles, the following rule always applies: throw it away.

## Basic rules for cooked leftovers

Bacteria multiply fastest between around 10 and 60 degrees. A large pot of soup left to cool on the stove overnight will stay in this area for many hours. Therefore:

1. **Cool quickly:** Divide large amounts into several shallow containers. Place a pot in a sink of cold water to cool, stirring occasionally.
2. **Into the refrigerator quickly:** As soon as the food is at room temperature, cover it and place it in the refrigerator. Lukewarm food is allowed as long as the refrigerator is not overloaded.
3. **Label:** A piece of masking tape with the contents and date prevents leftovers from being forgotten in the back corner.
4. **Consume within two to three days**, dishes with fish, minced meat or poultry sooner.
5. **Heat thoroughly:** When reheating, the food should be hot through and through, at least 70 degrees in the core. Stir soups and stews as they heat to avoid leaving any cold spots.
6. **Only reheat once:** Eat what you have reheated or throw away the rest.

### Special case rice and pasta

Rice may contain spores of the bacterium *Bacillus cereus* that survive cooking. If cooked rice is left warm for a long time, they germinate and form toxins that cannot be destroyed by reheating and can cause nausea and vomiting. Therefore, cool rice particularly quickly, store it in the refrigerator and eat it the next day if possible. The same applies in a weaker form to cooked pasta and other starchy foods.

## Stale bread

Bread dries out quickly, but is far from spoiled. As long as it doesn't get moldy, you can use it in many ways. If mold appears in one place, throw the whole bread away because the fungal growth can spread invisibly.

- **Breadcrumbs:** Break dry bread into pieces, let it dry completely in the oven at 120 degrees and chop in a blender or with a grater. They will last several weeks in a well-sealed jar.
- **Croûtons:** Roast the bread cubes in the pan with a little oil, salt and herbs until crispy. For soups and salads.
- **Bread dumplings:** You can find the classic recipe in the article about [chanterelles and porcini mushrooms](/kueche/pfifferlinge-und-steinpilze/).
- **Bread salad:** Mix roasted bread cubes with ripe tomatoes, cucumber, onion, basil, vinegar and olive oil and let sit for 15 minutes.

### Store bread properly

To prevent bread from becoming leftovers, it is worth storing it correctly. A clay or earthenware bread pot keeps the humidity even so that the crust does not become tough and the crumb does not become dry. The refrigerator, on the other hand, is the worst place: at temperatures just above zero degrees, bread goes stale particularly quickly because the starch sets there more quickly. If you can't make it in the next few days, freeze it in slices and toast it straight from the freezer when you need it.

### Poor knights

For 4 people:

- 8 slices of stale white bread, brioche or yeast plait
- 250 ml milk
- 2 eggs
- 1 tablespoon sugar, 1 packet vanilla sugar, ½ teaspoon cinnamon, 1 pinch of salt
- 3 tablespoons butter for frying

Whisk together milk, eggs, sugar, vanilla sugar, cinnamon and salt in a shallow bowl. Place the bread slices in one at a time and turn them until they are soaked without falling apart. Fry them in butter over medium heat for two to three minutes on each side until golden brown. Applesauce, compote, berries or cinnamon sugar go well with this.

## Vegetable cuttings and peels

When cleaning vegetables, many parts are left over that are too good to throw away: carrot peels and ends, onion peels, leek greens, celery greens, parsley stalks, mushroom stalks, fennel greens, tomato residues.

### Vegetable broth from leftovers

1. Collect clean, washed clippings in a freezer bag in the freezer over one to two weeks.
2. When the bag is full, about 500 to 700 grams, pour the contents into a large pot, cover it with 2 liters of cold water and add a bay leaf, a few peppercorns and, if desired, a fried onion half.
3. Bring everything to the boil and let the broth simmer gently for 45 to 60 minutes.
4. Pour them through a fine sieve and only now salt them to taste.

Too much cabbage, broccoli or Brussels sprouts makes the broth sulphurous, beetroot peels turn it red, potato peels make it cloudy. Therefore, use these sections sparingly. Wilted, slimy or moldy parts do not belong. The broth lasts three to four days in the refrigerator and can be frozen in portions.

### More ideas

- **Herb Stems:** Parsley and coriander stems are full of flavor. Finely chopped, they go well in pesto, salsa verde or soups.
- **Broccoli trunk:** Peel, cut into slices and cook. It is tender and sweet.
- **Kohlrabi and radish leaves:** Steam like spinach or use in pesto.
- **Cauliflower leaves:** Roast in the oven with oil and salt.

## A leftover becomes a meal

- **Roasted potatoes:** Peel boiled jacket potatoes from the day before, cut into slices and fry until crispy in clarified butter with onions and bacon. They work best with cold, waxy potatoes.
- **Frittata:** Spread cooked vegetables, potatoes or pasta in an ovenproof pan, pour over six beaten eggs with cheese, let it set on the stove and finish baking in the oven.
- **Soup:** Puree leftover oven-baked vegetables with broth and refine with a little cream or yoghurt.
- **Vegetable rice:** Fry cooked rice from the day before with cooked vegetables, egg and soy sauce in a hot pan.
- **Overripe bananas and soft apples:** For banana bread, pancakes, smoothies or applesauce.

## Dairy products and cheese: what else is possible

- **Yoghurt, quark and cream** are often good for days after the best-before date if the package was unopened. If something smells or tastes bitter or fermented, if the lid bulges or if there is mold, dispose of the product completely.
- You can save **hard cheese** with a small spot of mold by generously cutting away at least a centimeter around the area. Soft cheese, cream cheese and grated cheese with mold, on the other hand, belong in the trash.
- **Sour milk** made from pasteurized milk usually tastes bitter and should be thrown away. Raw milk, which becomes thick and sour, used to be popular as curdled milk, but you shouldn't rely on it for store-bought milk.

## A leftovers plan for the week

This is what a week can look like in which every big meal turns into a second one:

| day | Cooking takes place | This becomes the next day |
|---|---|---|
| Sunday | Roast with red cabbage and potatoes | Roast bread and potato salad |
| Monday | Jacket potatoes with quark | Fried potatoes with fried eggs |
| Tuesday | Oven vegetables from the tray | Vegetable soup, pureed |
| Wednesday | Rice with vegetable curry | fried rice with egg |
| Thursday | Pasta with tomato sauce | Pasta casserole with cheese |
| Friday | Fridge Empty Day: Frittata or pizza with whatever is left | |

If you plan like this, you will shop more specifically and still have a different meal on the table every day.

## pass on food

What you don't manage to do despite all your planning doesn't have to end up in the trash. In many cities there are public refrigerators and shelves, so-called fair dividers, in which you can put unspoiled food and from which you can help yourself. Neighbors are often happy to receive a box of apples or zucchini from the garden. And some bakeries and restaurants sell leftover goods in the evening via apps or at reduced prices, so that less is thrown away there too.

## Plan instead of throwing away

The most effective way to combat food waste is to prevent leftovers from forming in the first place. Check your fridge and pantry before shopping, plan meals that share ingredients, and consciously cook more when planning leftovers for a specific purpose. If you cook jacket potatoes on Thursdays, you can make fried potatoes on Fridays. How to store fruit and vegetables so that they stay fresh longer can be found in the article about [storing fruit and vegetables](/kueche/obst-und-gemuese-lagern/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I reheat spinach?

Yes, if you cool it quickly after cooking, store it in the fridge and reheat it thoroughly the next day. The concern stems from the fact that nitrate can convert to nitrite when kept warm. However, warmed spinach should not be used for infants.

### Can I freeze leftovers that have already been thawed?

You should not refreeze raw foods that have been thawed. Once you have cooked them, you can freeze the resulting dish.

### How long do frozen leftovers last?

Cooked dishes, soups and stews usually last two to three months in good quality at minus 18 degrees, often longer. Label each can with contents and date.
