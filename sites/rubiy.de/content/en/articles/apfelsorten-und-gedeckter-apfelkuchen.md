The apple is the Germans' favorite fruit, and not without reason. It grows almost everywhere in the country, is available from local harvests from August to spring and goes well with both sweet and savory dishes. There are still hundreds of varieties in old orchards, many of which are only known regionally. You can usually find around a dozen in the supermarket.

Not every apple is suitable for every purpose. An apple that is crisp and juicy when raw may turn to mush in the oven, and an apple that makes wonderful puree is often too sour when raw. Those who know the differences make more targeted choices.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Sour, aromatic varieties such as Boskoop, Topaz or Berlepsch are suitable for cakes and applesauce.
> - If you want the pieces in the cake to hold their shape, use firm varieties such as Braeburn or Elstar.
> - Apples are best stored in a cool place at 2 to 4 degrees, with high humidity and separately from other fruits and vegetables.
> - Apples release ethylene and cause nearby foods to ripen or wilt more quickly.
> - For a covered apple pie you need about 1.5 kilos of tart apples for a 26 springform pan.

## Apple varieties at a glance

| variety | taste | Consistency | Best for | Harvest time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gravensteiner | spicy, sweet and sour, fragrant | juicy, quickly becomes floury | eat fresh, applesauce | August to September |
| Elstar | sweet and sour, aromatic | crisp, juicy | raw, cake | September |
| Cox Orange | spicy, nutty | firm, later crumbly | raw, fruit salad | September |
| Boskoop | strong sour | solid, falls apart when baking | Apple pie, puree, baked apple | October |
| Topaz | sour-sweet, intense | firm, juicy | raw, cake | October |
| Braeburn | sweet and sour, fresh | very crisp, dimensionally stable | raw, tart, apple rings | October |
| Jonagold | sweet, mild | juicy, slightly softer | raw, compote | October |
| Berlepsch | sour, very aromatic | fixed | Cake, puree, raw | October |

The harvest times given are rough guidelines and vary depending on the region and year. Many autumn and winter varieties are not yet ready for consumption after harvest and only develop their full aroma after a few weeks of storage. A Boskoop is still hard and very sour in October, but balanced and crumbly in December.

### Apples for allergy sufferers

Some people react to raw apples with a tingling mouth or a furry feeling, often related to a birch pollen allergy. Some old varieties and certain newer varieties such as Santana are better tolerated by some people. But that varies greatly from person to person. Cooked or baked apples are not a problem for many pollen allergy sufferers because the triggering protein is changed by heat. If you have more serious allergies, please consult a doctor before experimenting.

## Ready to pick and enjoy

There are two types of ripeness for apples. An apple is **ready to pick** when it comes off the branch with a slight lifting and twisting motion without you having to pull. If you cut open a sample apple, the cores will be brown. It is **ready for consumption** when it has developed its full aroma. For summer apples like clear apples, both times almost coincide; they taste good straight from the tree and only last for a short time. Winter apples such as Boskoop or Topaz, on the other hand, are picked in autumn and ripen in storage until they reach their best taste in winter. If you harvest your own apples, don't pick them too early: apples picked unripe remain bland and shrivel in storage.

## Store apples properly

Apples are alive after harvest. They breathe, consume sugar and acid and release the ripening gas ethylene. The warmer it is, the faster they age. A cool basement or garage with 2 to 4 degrees, no frost, and high humidity is ideal.

- Only store apples that are in perfect condition without bruises or wormholes. You eat or process damaged fruit immediately.
- Place the apples one at a time next to each other on wooden racks, in flat boxes or on newspaper, with the stem upwards so that they do not touch each other if possible.
- Check supplies every week and sort out rotten apples.
- Don't store apples next to potatoes, cabbage, carrots, or bananas. Potatoes germinate more quickly due to the ethylene, carrots can become bitter.

In the apartment, apples will last for about one to two weeks in a fruit bowl, or for several weeks in the vegetable drawer of the refrigerator in a bag with a few holes. You can find out more about storage in the article about [storing fruit and vegetables](/kueche/obst-und-gemuese-lagern/).

## Covered apple pie

The covered apple cake is a classic of the German coffee table: a shortcrust pastry with a juicy apple filling, covered with a sheet of dough and topped with lemon icing. It tastes best the day after baking when the filling has set.

For a springform pan with a diameter of 26 cm:

**shortcrust pastry**

- 350 g wheat flour (Type 405)
- 125g sugar
- 1 packet of vanilla sugar
- 1 pinch of salt
- 1 egg (size M)
- 200 g cold butter, cut into cubes

**Filling**

- 1.5 kg tart apples, for example Boskoop
- Juice of half a lemon
- 60 to 80 g sugar, depending on the acidity of the apples
- 1 tsp ground cinnamon
- 50 g raisins, soaked in 2 tablespoons apple juice or rum, if desired
- 2 tbsp water
- 1 tbsp cornstarch

**Cast**

- 150g powdered sugar
- 2 to 3 tbsp lemon juice

### Preparation

1. For the dough, mix flour, sugar, vanilla sugar and salt. Add the egg and butter and quickly knead everything into a smooth dough using your hands or the dough hook. Don't knead for too long, otherwise the dough will become warm and tough later. Wrap the dough in cling film and chill for at least 30 minutes.
2. Peel the apples, quarter them, core them and cut them into slices about one centimeter thick. Mix immediately with the lemon juice.
3. Place apples with sugar, cinnamon and water in a wide pot and cover and simmer over medium heat for five minutes until they are slightly softer but still have a bite. Mix the cornstarch with a tablespoon of cold water until smooth, stir in and bring to the boil. Add raisins and let the filling cool completely. By pre-cooking, the filling does not collapse in the oven and there is no large cavity between the lid and the apples.
4. Preheat the oven to 180 degrees top and bottom heat. Line the bottom of the springform pan with baking paper and grease the edge.
5. Roll out about two thirds of the dough on a floured surface and line the mold with it, forming an edge about three centimeters high. Prick the base several times with a fork.
6. Distribute the cooled filling evenly.
7. Roll out the remaining dough between two layers of baking paper to a diameter of 26 centimeters, place it on the filling as a lid and press it lightly on the edge. Pierce the lid several times with a fork to allow steam to escape.
8. Bake the cake on the middle shelf for 50 to 60 minutes until golden brown.
9. Mix the powdered sugar with lemon juice to form a thick icing and spread over the still warm cake. Allow the cake to cool completely in the tin.

Lightly whipped cream or vanilla sauce goes well with this. The cake will keep covered at room temperature for two to three days.

## Baked apples for cold evenings

For baked apples, use tart, firm varieties like Boskoop, which soften in the oven without completely disintegrating. For four apples, cut out the core with an apple corer without piercing the apple all the way through, or cut off a top and hollow it out with a spoon. Mix 50 g chopped walnuts or almonds, 30 g raisins, a tablespoon of honey, a pinch of cinnamon and, if desired, 50 g marzipan in small pieces and fill the apples with them. Place a knob of butter on each opening, place the apples in a baking dish with a finger's breadth of apple juice and bake them at 180 degrees for 25 to 35 minutes until the skin bursts and the flesh is soft. Vanilla sauce goes well with it.

## Quick applesauce

To make about one kilo of puree, peel 1.2 kg of tart apples, remove the core and cut them into pieces. Cover and cook with 100 ml water or apple juice, a tablespoon of lemon juice and a cinnamon stick for about 15 minutes until the apples fall apart. Remove the cinnamon stick, mash or puree the apples with a potato masher and sweeten with sugar or honey to taste. If you cook the apples unpeeled and then pass the puree through the liquor, it becomes more aromatic and often has a rosy color.

Filled into clean jars, you can cook applesauce at 90 degrees for 30 minutes. Our article on the [basics of preserving] (/kueche/einkochen-grundlagen/) explains how this works.

## Orchards: diversity that you can taste

Tall fruit trees scattered across a meadow characterize the landscape in many regions. Orchards are among the most species-rich habitats in Central Europe: insects, birds such as little owls and green woodpeckers and many plants find a home here. At the same time, they preserve old apple varieties that are not found in supermarkets, such as Kaiser Wilhelm, Goldparmäne or Rheinischer Bohnapfel. The cultivation of orchards is included in the nationwide register of intangible cultural heritage.

If you want to support this diversity, buy juice from orchards, visit apple markets and variety exhibitions in autumn or sponsor a tree at a local association. Many communities and initiatives also offer areas where fruit can be harvested for a small fee or free of charge. Look out for markings such as a yellow band on the trunk, which in some regions indicates that picking is permitted.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do sliced apples turn brown?

When cutting, enzymes in the apple come into contact with oxygen and form brown pigments. Lemon juice slows this down. Varieties with a lot of vitamin C and few so-called polyphenols brown more slowly.

### Can I still use floury apples?

Yes, for applesauce, compote, apple pancakes and cakes in which the apples are allowed to fall apart. Raw, however, they usually taste disappointing.

### Do I have to pre-cook the apples for the cake?

No, but it helps. Raw apples release a lot of juice in the oven and shrink, causing the dough lid to sink or a cavity to form. Apples that have been briefly pre-cooked and bound with a little starch produce a juicy, compact filling.
