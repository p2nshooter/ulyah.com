Anyone who plants zucchini in the garden knows this phenomenon. In June you are happy about the first small fruit, in July you harvest every other day, and in August suddenly a specimen the size of your forearm is hidden under the large leaves. Neighbors, colleagues and relatives receive presents until no one can see zucchini anymore. Zucchini are also cheaper on the market in midsummer than almost any other vegetable.

Luckily, the zucchini is very versatile. It tastes good raw, fried, braised, stuffed, as a buffer or in cake. Before we get into recipes, though, an important safety note that anyone who grows or eats zucchini should know.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Before cooking, taste a small piece of the raw zucchini. If it tastes bitter, spit it out and throw the whole fruit away.
> - Bitter substances in zucchini, so-called cucurbitacins, can cause serious poisoning. Cooking doesn't destroy them.
> - Zucchini that are 15 to 20 centimeters long taste best. Large fruits are suitable for stuffing, soup and cakes.
> - To make fritters, grate the zucchini, salt them and squeeze them well so that the fritters become crispy.
> - Zucchini is best stored at around 10 to 12 degrees; it will only last a few days in the refrigerator.

## Warning: bitter zucchini

Like pumpkins and cucumbers, zucchini belong to the cucurbit family. Their wild ancestors produce cucurbitacins, very bitter substances that protect the plant from predators. These substances have largely been bred away in today's cultivated varieties. But they can appear again:

- if you sow seeds from your own fruits and the mother plant has crossed with ornamental pumpkins,
- less often due to stress on the plant, for example due to extreme heat and drought.

Cucurbitacins can cause nausea, vomiting, severe diarrhea and circulatory problems. In Germany there has even been a death in the past after eating a stew made from home-grown bitter zucchini. Heat does not destroy the substances, so cooking, frying or baking does not help.

The rule is therefore simple: before preparing it, cut off a piece of the raw end and try it. If it tastes noticeably bitter, spit it out, rinse your mouth and discard the whole fruit. If a dish is already ready and tastes bitter, the same applies. Buy new zucchini and squash seeds every year instead of using your own seeds.

## A pumpkin from Italy

Botanically, zucchini is a form of garden squash whose ancestors come from America. It acquired its current form in Italy, where gardeners in the 19th century selected pumpkins to be harvested young. The name is the diminutive of the Italian word for pumpkin, zucca, meaning “little pumpkin”. In Switzerland the vegetable is called zucchini. The fact that the zucchini belongs to the pumpkin family also explains why it can cross with ornamental pumpkins in the garden.

## Shopping and storage

The best zucchini are 15 to 20 centimeters long, have smooth, shiny skin and are firm to the touch. At this stage the seeds are still small and soft and the flesh is tender. Yellow and round varieties also taste similar and can be used in the same way.

Zucchini are sensitive to cold. In the refrigerator they quickly develop soft, glassy spots. A cool place at 10 to 12 degrees is ideal, such as a cool pantry. They stay there for a good week. You should use them in the vegetable compartment of the refrigerator within three to four days. Don't store them next to tomatoes or apples, whose ripening gas causes them to age faster.

## Zucchini fritters

For 4 people, about 12 buffers:

- 750g zucchini
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 small onion or 2 spring onions
- 2 eggs
- 70 g flour (type 405 or 550)
- 60 g grated mountain cheese or feta, crumbled
- 1 small bunch of dill or mint
- Pepper, some grated lemon peel
- Rapeseed oil for frying

1. Roughly grate the zucchini, mix it with the salt in a bowl and let it stand for 15 minutes. The salt draws water.
2. Place the zucchini in a clean kitchen towel, twist it together and squeeze out as much liquid as possible. This step is crucial. 750 grams of zucchini easily yields 200 milliliters of water.
3. Cut the onion very finely, chop the herbs. Mix zucchini, onion, eggs, flour, cheese, herbs, pepper and lemon zest. The dough should hold together but not be tight. If it is too wet, add another tablespoon of flour.
4. Heat plenty of oil in a large pan. Place a heaping tablespoon of dough into each buffer and flatten it.
5. Fry the pancakes over medium heat for three to four minutes on each side until golden brown. Only turn them when the bottom comes off easily.
6. Drain them on kitchen paper and keep them warm in the oven at 80 degrees until they are all done.

This goes well with a yogurt dip made from 250 g Greek yogurt, a grated garlic clove, salt, lemon juice and a little olive oil, and a tomato salad.

## Stuffed zucchini

If you happen to have a very large zucchini slip through, it is suitable for stuffing. For 4 people, take two medium-sized zucchini, about 400 grams each, or one large one weighing one kilo.

For the filling:

- 1 onion, 1 clove of garlic
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 300 g mixed minced meat or 150 g cooked lentils and 100 g bulgur (vegetarian)
- the hollowed out zucchini flesh, diced
- 2 tomatoes, diced
- 1 teaspoon dried oregano or thyme, salt, pepper
- 100 g grated cheese, such as Gouda or mountain cheese

1. Halve the zucchini lengthwise and scrape out the insides with a spoon, leaving an edge about a centimeter thick. If you have large zucchini, also remove the hard seeds.
2. Sauté the onion and garlic in the oil, fry the minced meat until crumbly. For the vegetarian filling, prepare the bulgur according to the instructions on the package.
3. Add the zucchini meat and tomatoes and let everything simmer for ten minutes until the liquid has almost evaporated. Season with oregano, salt and pepper. For the vegetarian version, fold in the lentils and bulgur now.
4. Place the zucchini halves in a greased baking dish, lightly salt the inside and spread the filling on top. Pour 100 ml of water or stock into the mold.
5. Sprinkle everything with cheese and bake the zucchini at 200 degrees top and bottom heat for 25 to 35 minutes until they are soft and the cheese is browned.

## More ideas against the flood

- **Raw as a salad:** Cut the young zucchini into thin ribbons with a vegetable peeler, marinate with lemon juice, olive oil, salt, mint and roasted almonds.
- **Fried:** Cut into slices, fry in hot olive oil until brown, then add salt. With garlic, chili and parsley for pasta.
- **Zucchini soup:** Sauté onion and potato, add a kilo of diced zucchini and a liter of broth, cook for 15 minutes, puree and refine with cream cheese.
- **Zucchini cake:** Finely grated zucchini makes pound cakes and chocolate cakes moist without being tasteful.
- **Flowers:** Zucchini flowers can be filled with ricotta and baked in dough.

## Ratatouille: the classic from Provence

In the south of France, the zucchini glut has always been made into ratatouille. For four people, cut an eggplant, two zucchini, two peppers and an onion into bite-sized cubes. Fry the eggplant, zucchini and peppers one at a time in olive oil, just enough to color the vegetables, and set aside. In the same pot, sauté the onion and two cloves of garlic, add 500 g of diced, ripe tomatoes and let them simmer for ten minutes. Return the vegetables, season with thyme, rosemary, salt and pepper and let everything simmer over a low heat for 20 minutes. Frying them separately takes a little longer, but ensures that each vegetable retains its bite and flavor. Ratatouille tastes warm with rice or baguette, lukewarm as a starter and even better the next day.

## In the garden: less flood, more joy

- **A few plants are enough:** One or two zucchini plants are enough for a family. Each one easily produces ten or more fruits over the summer.
- **Harvest young and regularly:** If you harvest every two to three days, you will get tender fruits and encourage the plant to continue to bloom.
- **Flowers without fruits:** If young fruits fall withered, the female flowers were not pollinated. When there are few insects flying around, it helps to transfer pollen from a male to a female flower with a brush. You can recognize female flowers by the small fruit base underneath the flower.
- **Mildew in late summer:** A white, mealy coating on the leaves is powdery mildew. Remove infected leaves early and water at the roots in the morning, not over the leaves.

## Make it durable

Raw zucchini becomes mushy after freezing. It is better to fry them beforehand or to make them into a soup or sauce and freeze them. You can freeze grated zucchini in portions for cakes or pancakes.

Sweet and sour pickled zucchini are a nice side dish for snacks and grilled dishes. You can read how pickling in vinegar works in the article about [spiced cucumbers](/kueche/gewuerzgurken-einlegen/), the method can be transferred to zucchini.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to peel zucchini?

No. The peel is thin and edible and provides color and support. Peeling can only be useful for very large zucchini with a hard shell.

### Why do my zucchini get mushy when frying?

The pan is usually too full or not hot enough, so the zucchini cooks in its own juice instead of frying. Fry them in several batches and only add salt at the end.

### Are yellow zucchini something different?

No, they are varieties with yellow skin. There is hardly any difference in taste and preparation. The same applies to the bitter sample as to the green one.

### Can I eat zucchini raw?

Yes, young zucchini taste mild and slightly nutty raw, for example as ribbons in a salad or as sticks for dip. Do the bitter test here too. Large, older fruits are often watery and bland when raw and cook better when cooked.
