Rhubarb is the first fruit of the year, although botanically it is a vegetable. When the red and green sticks come onto the market in April, there is no stopping many people: finally something fresh and sour after the long winter with apples and preserved compote. Whether as a cake, compote, groats or syrup, rhubarb brings a distinctive acidity to spring cuisine.

His season is short. Depending on the weather, it begins at the end of March or April and traditionally ends on St. John's Day, June 24th. Afterwards, the perennial needs rest to gather strength for the next year.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Rhubarb is in season from around April to June 24th.
> - You don't have to peel young, thin stalks. For thick, fibrous sticks, peel off the skin.
> - The leaves contain a lot of oxalic acid and are not suitable for eating.
> - You should eat rhubarb cooked and combine it with dairy products, which bind some of the oxalic acid.
> - For a sheet cake you need about a kilo of cleaned rhubarb.

## From medicine to cake topping

Rhubarb comes from the mountainous regions of Central Asia. For centuries, it was not the stick that was in demand, but the dried root, which was traded as a remedy all the way to Europe. The stems only became established as food in the 18th and 19th centuries, first in England and later in Germany. The name is usually derived from the Latin rha barbarum, which roughly means “foreign root”.

## Buying and storing rhubarb

Fresh stalks are firm, shiny and break with a snap. The cut ends should be juicy, not dried out. There are varieties with red flesh and those with a green interior and red skin. The red varieties, often referred to as raspberry or strawberry rhubarb, taste a little milder and require less sugar. The green varieties have a stronger, sour aroma.

At home, wrap the sticks in a damp cloth and put them in the vegetable compartment. They last like this for three to five days. You can freeze rhubarb raw when cleaned and cut into pieces. It becomes soft after thawing and is then suitable for compotes, cakes and groats.

## Peel or not?

That depends on the rhubarb. Young spears from early spring and the red varieties have thin skin that becomes soft when cooked. Here you just cut off the ends. In thick, older rods, the fibers in the skin are tough. Place a small knife at the end, grasp the skin between the blade and thumb and pull it upwards in strips.

## Oxalic acid: what you should pay attention to

Rhubarb contains oxalic acid, which gives it its typical sour taste and causes the feeling of dull teeth. Oxalic acid binds calcium in the body and, in large quantities, can contribute to the formation of kidney stones. For healthy people, rhubarb is safe in normal quantities if you keep a few things in mind:

- **Do not eat leaves:** The oxalic acid content is particularly high in the leaves. They belong in the compost.
- **Eat cooked:** Rhubarb is not recommended raw in large quantities. When cooking, some of the oxalic acid passes into the cooking water.
- **Combine with dairy products:** Cream, quark, yoghurt or vanilla pudding provide calcium, which binds some of the oxalic acid. Rhubarb with vanilla sauce is not just a classic taste.
- **Special caution:** People with kidney disease, a tendency to kidney stones or gout should only eat rhubarb in moderation and discuss this with their doctor.

Towards the end of the season, it is widely believed that the oxalic acid content in the stalks increases. This is also why it makes sense to stick to the old rule and not harvest rhubarb after the end of June.

## Rhubarb cake with crumbles from the tray

For a baking tray (approx. 30 × 40 cm), makes around 20 pieces:

**Sponge**

- 250 g soft butter
- 200g sugar
- 1 packet of vanilla sugar
- 1 pinch of salt
- 4 eggs (size M)
- 400 g wheat flour (Type 405)
- 1 packet of baking powder (15 g)
- 100 ml milk

**Topping**

- 1 kg rhubarb, weighed, cleaned
- 2 tbsp sugar

**Sprinkles**

- 300 g wheat flour
- 150g sugar
- 1 packet of vanilla sugar
- 1 pinch of salt
- ½ tsp ground cinnamon, if desired
- 200 g cold butter

### Preparation

1. Wash the rhubarb, trim the ends and peel if necessary. Cut it into pieces about two centimeters long, mix them with two tablespoons of sugar and let them steep for 20 minutes. Pour off the juice before topping, otherwise the cake will be soggy. You can drink the juice with mineral water as a spritzer.
2. For the streusel, mix flour, sugar, vanilla sugar, salt and cinnamon. Add the cold butter in pieces and work everything into coarse crumbles with your fingers or the dough hook. Refrigerate until ready to use.
3. Preheat the oven to 180 degrees top and bottom heat. Line the tray with baking paper.
4. Beat the soft butter with sugar, vanilla sugar and salt for three to four minutes until light and creamy. Add the eggs one at a time and stir each one in for about half a minute.
5. Mix the flour and baking powder and stir it briefly into the butter mixture, alternating with the milk, just until a smooth dough is formed.
6. Spread the dough evenly onto the tray. Spread the rhubarb pieces on top and press them lightly.
7. Sprinkle the streusel evenly over the rhubarb.
8. Bake the cake on the middle rack for 40 to 45 minutes, until the streusel is golden brown. Do the stick test: A wooden stick that you insert into the dough should come out without any moist crumbs.
9. Let the cake cool on the tray and dust it with powdered sugar if desired before serving.

Serve with lightly sweetened whipped cream or vanilla sauce. The cake will keep covered at room temperature for two days, after which it will become a little soft due to the moist rhubarb. It freezes well in pieces.

## Variant: Rhubarb cake with meringue topping

Instead of sprinkles, you can crown the cake with an airy meringue topping. First bake the dough and rhubarb for 30 minutes at 180 degrees. In the meantime, beat four egg whites with a pinch of salt until stiff and gradually add 200 g of sugar until the mixture is shiny and holds peaks. Spread the egg whites on the pre-baked cake, use a spoon to create small peaks and bake for another 10 to 15 minutes until the meringue is golden brown. The meringue should only be ready shortly before spreading, otherwise it will collapse. The sweet, crunchy top is the perfect counterpart to the acidity of the rhubarb.

## Rhubarb compote

Compote is the easiest and most versatile way to use rhubarb. It tastes good with semolina porridge, rice pudding, pancakes, yoghurt and ice cream.

For 4 people:

- 750 g rhubarb, cleaned
- 100 to 150 g of sugar, depending on the type and taste
- 100 ml water or apple juice
- 1 vanilla pod or 1 packet of vanilla sugar
- 250 g strawberries if desired

1. Cut the rhubarb into two centimeter long pieces.
2. Boil water, sugar and the slit vanilla pod.
3. Add the rhubarb and cook, covered, over a low heat for four to six minutes, until it is soft but the pieces still hold their shape. It breaks down very quickly, so it's better to remove it from the heat too early. It continues to cook in the hot liquid.
4. Halve the strawberries and fold them into the still warm compote.

If you want the compote to be thicker, mix a tablespoon of cornstarch with a little cold water until smooth, stir it in and bring everything to the boil.

## Rhubarb syrup

To make about 700 ml of syrup, boil a kilo of rhubarb in pieces with 500 ml of water for 15 minutes until it has broken down and drain the mixture through a sieve lined with a cloth. Boil the juice with 400 grams of sugar and the juice of one lemon for five minutes and fill it into clean bottles while it is boiling hot. Stored in a cool place, it lasts for several months. Diluted with mineral water, it makes a refreshing spritzer.

## Savory: rhubarb chutney

Rhubarb doesn't always have to be sweet. To make a chutney, sauté a finely diced red onion and a piece of grated ginger in a little oil, add 500 g rhubarb in pieces, 150 g brown sugar, 100 ml apple cider vinegar, a pinch of salt and, if desired, a handful of raisins and a pinch of chili. Let everything simmer uncovered for 20 to 30 minutes until a thick, jam-like mixture forms and pour it hot into clean jars. The chutney goes well with cheese, grilled meat, fried halloumi and cold roasts.

## Rhubarb in your own garden

Rhubarb is a perennial that will produce stalks for eight to ten years or longer in a sunny spot with nutrient-rich soil. It is best planted in autumn. In the first year you don't harvest at all so that the plant forms strong roots, in the second year you only harvest a little. The rods are not cut off, but pulled off at the base with a slight twist. Always leave at least a third of the leaves. You break out the inflorescences early because they take a lot of energy out of the plant. After the harvest and in autumn, the perennial is happy to receive a dose of compost.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does rhubarb taste so dull on your teeth?

The oxalic acid combines with the calcium in saliva to form fine crystals that settle on the teeth. The feeling is harmless and disappears after a short time. It rarely occurs when eaten with dairy products.

### Can I still eat rhubarb after June 24th?

You can eat the purchased rhubarb that is offered afterwards. The rule primarily applies to harvesting in your own garden so that the plant can recover.

### Why is my rhubarb pie soggy at the bottom?

Rhubarb consists mostly of water. If you let it steep with sugar beforehand and pour off the juice, the base will remain firmer. A thin layer of ground almonds or breadcrumbs on the dough also catches juice.
