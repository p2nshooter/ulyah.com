A potato salad that turns into mush or dumplings that don't hold together when cooked: Most of the time it's not the recipe, but the wrong potato. More than 200 types of potatoes are permitted in Germany, and they differ significantly in how they behave when cooked. Luckily, you don't have to memorize variety names. The type of cook is written on every package and it tells you almost everything you need to know in the kitchen.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - **Washy:** stays in shape, firm, moist. Ideal for potato salad, gratin, fried potatoes made from raw potatoes.
> - **Predominantly waxy:** the all-rounder for boiled potatoes, jacket potatoes, fried potatoes, hash browns and stews.
> - **Floury boiling:** becomes loose and dry, falls apart easily. Ideal for puree, dumplings, potato soup and buffers.
> - Store potatoes in a cool, dark and airy place, ideally at 4 to 8 degrees, but not in the refrigerator and not next to apples.
> - Cut away green spots and germs generously. Stop eating strong green potatoes.

## What the cooking type means

The difference lies in the strength. Potatoes with less starch and firmer cell walls retain their shape when cooked. Varieties with more starch absorb a lot of water during cooking, the cells burst and the potato becomes loose and dry. In Germany there are three types of cooking, which are often marked with colors on bags and nets, usually green for waxy, red for predominantly waxy and blue for floury. But rely on the font, not the color, because the color codes are not uniformly prescribed.

| Cooking type | Properties | Example varieties | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| boiling solid | cut-resistant, fine-grained, moist, hardly bursts | Linda, Sieglinde, Annabelle, Belana | Potato salad, gratin, fried potatoes, boiled potatoes with a bite |
| predominantly waxy | a little looser, slightly floury, versatile | Gala, Laura, Marabel | Jacket potatoes, boiled potatoes, hash browns, stew, baked potatoes |
| floury boiling | loose, dry, coarse-grained, falls apart easily | Adretta, Afra | Puree, dumplings, soup, potato pancakes, gnocchi |

The variety names are examples. You will find others depending on the region, season and retailer. For the kitchen, the type of cook is enough.

## Which potato for which dish?

### potato salad

For potato salad you need waxy potatoes. They slice thinly without crumbling and absorb a warm broth-vinegar marinade without becoming mushy. Cook them as jacket potatoes, peel them while they are still warm and marinate them immediately. For southern German potato salad with broth, a slightly floury note is actually desirable because it binds the marinade. Then you can mix in a portion of mainly waxy potatoes.

### Fried potatoes

It's best to fry waxy varieties of raw potatoes because they don't fall apart in the pan. For the classic version of boiled potatoes from the day before, waxy ones are ideal: they become crispy on the outside and soft on the inside. It is important that the potatoes are well cooled and dry. You can find out more about the safe use of cooked leftovers in our article [Leftover kitchen with a plan](/kueche/resteverwertung-in-der-kueche/).

### Puree and dumplings

Mashed potatoes become light and airy with floury potatoes. Waxy varieties produce a tough, sticky puree, especially when processed with a hand blender. Floury varieties are mandatory for dumplings and dumplings because their starch binds the dough.

### Baked potatoes and fries

Large, predominantly waxy or floury potatoes become beautifully flaky on the inside in the oven. For wedges with a crispy crust, waxy potatoes are a good compromise.

### Stew and soup

For a stew in which you want the diced potatoes to remain recognizable, use mainly waxy ones. If you want the soup to be thick, add some floury potatoes. They break down and thicken the broth without flour or cream.

## New potatoes and storage potatoes

From the end of May, the first **early potatoes** from Germany will go on sale, previously often imported from the Mediterranean region. New potatoes have a wafer-thin skin that can be rubbed off by hand and a delicate, nutty taste. They only last for one to two weeks and are best cooked with the skin on, with butter, salt and herbs.

The **storage potatoes** will be harvested from September. They have a firm shell, are ripe and, if stored properly, will last until spring. Over the winter they become a little softer and sweeter because some of the starch is converted into sugar.

## Buying potatoes: how to recognize good products

Good table potatoes are plump and firm, the skin is smooth and undamaged. Leave tubers with green spots, soft bruises, cracks or visible sprouts. A musty smell from the bag is also a warning signal, as a single rotting tuber quickly infects the neighbors. The variety, cooking type and origin are usually listed on the package or price tag.

Only buy as much as you can store in a cool, dark place for the next few weeks. If you don't have a cellar, it's better to use small quantities than the cheap ten-kilo bag that starts to germinate in the warm kitchen. Loose goods have the advantage that you choose each potato yourself. When it comes to nets and bags, it's worth taking a look around. At home, the potatoes should be immediately taken out of the plastic bag and into a box, basket or paper sack.

## Store potatoes properly

- **Dark:** Light causes potatoes to turn green.
- **Cool:** 4 to 8 degrees is ideal, for example in a cool basement, an unheated pantry or a box on the balcony that is protected from frost.
- **Not in the fridge:** Below about 4 degrees, potatoes convert starch into sugar. They then taste sweet and more acrylamide is formed when frying and deep-frying.
- **Airy:** Store in boxes, baskets or paper bags, not in closed plastic bags, otherwise moisture and rot will form.
- **Separate from apples:** Apples release the ripening gas ethylene, which allows potatoes to germinate faster.

You can find further rules for the refrigerator, cellar and kitchen table in [Store fruit and vegetables correctly](/kueche/obst-und-gemuese-lagern/).

## Green spots and germs

Green spots, germs and the so-called eyes contain increased amounts of solanine and related glycoalkaloids. In large quantities, these substances can cause nausea, abdominal pain and diarrhea. They are hardly destroyed by cooking. The rules are simple:

- Cut out small green spots and germs generously.
- Stop eating potatoes with large green areas or long sprouts.
- Children should eat potatoes peeled because the substances accumulate in and just under the peel.
- Do not reuse potato cooking water.

## Cooking times at a glance

Always soak whole potatoes in cold, salted water. This way they cook evenly and the outer layers don't fall apart before the center is soft.

| Preparation | Cooking time from boiling |
|---|---|
| Jacket potatoes, medium size | 20 to 25 minutes |
| Boiled potatoes, peeled and quartered | 15 to 20 minutes |
| New potatoes, small | 15 to 20 minutes |
| Potatoes for puree, in pieces | 15 to 20 minutes |
| Baked potatoes, whole, at 200°C | 50 to 70 minutes |

Test the doneness with a sharp knife: it should slide in without resistance. Then drain the potatoes and let them steam briefly in the open pot. Dry potatoes taste better and absorb butter and sauce more evenly.

## Cook with or without peel?

When cooked as jacket potatoes, potatoes lose fewer vitamins and minerals into the cooking water because the peel acts like a protective coating. This is especially true for vitamin C and potassium, both of which are water-soluble. Steaming in the sieve insert is even gentler: the potatoes are not in the water and the taste remains stronger.

Whether you eat the peel is a matter of taste. It is common for new potatoes with their thin skin, but it is better to peel storage potatoes with a thick skin. In any case: brush thoroughly under running water and remove all green spots.

Interesting for everyone who pays attention to their digestion: When cooked potatoes cool down, some of the starch rearranges itself and becomes what is known as resistant starch. It is hardly digested in the small intestine and acts similarly to fiber in the large intestine. A potato salad from the day before is not only more aromatic, but also fills you up a little longer.

## A classic to practice: jacket potatoes with herb quark

For four people you need 1 kg of waxy or predominantly waxy potatoes, 500 g of quark, about 100 ml of milk, a bunch of chives, a few sprigs of parsley, a small onion, salt and pepper. In Lusatia, a dash of linseed oil is included, which gives the dish its nutty, slightly bitter taste.

1. Wash the potatoes, put them in cold salted water and cook for 20 to 25 minutes.
2. Mix the quark with the milk until smooth.
3. Dice the onion very finely, chop the herbs finely and stir in. Season with salt and pepper.
4. Drain the potatoes, let them evaporate briefly and serve hot, peeled or with the skin on.
5. Drizzle linseed oil over the quark if desired.

The dish shows how good a simple potato tastes when cooked correctly and not overcooked.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my puree getting sticky?

Either you used waxy potatoes or overprocessed the puree. Take floury potatoes, mash them with a masher or press them through a potato press and just briefly stir in the warm milk and butter. A hand blender turns each potato into paste.

### Can I save cooked potatoes?

Yes. Cool cooked potatoes quickly and store them covered in the refrigerator. Used within two to three days, they are perfect for fried potatoes, salad or hash browns.

### Are red or blue potatoes different?

The color of the shell and meat says nothing about the type of cook. Red-skinned varieties like Laura are predominantly waxy, blue varieties are available in all types of cooking. Here too, pay attention to the information on the packaging.

### Can I freeze potatoes?

Raw potatoes are not suitable: after defrosting, they are watery, glassy and taste sweet. However, ready-made potato dishes such as puree, soup, dumplings or gratin can be frozen well. Whole cooked potatoes become soft after defrosting and are then only suitable for soup or puree.

### Why do boiled potatoes sometimes turn gray?

This is the so-called cooking darkening. After cooking, chlorogenic acid and iron from the tuber combine to form a complex that darkens when exposed to air. It's harmless, but it looks ugly. A dash of vinegar or lemon juice in the cooking water significantly weakens the effect.
