Homemade pickles are crunchier, spicier and less sweet than most jars from the supermarket. The season for this is short: small pickling cucumbers are available from July to September, most of them in August. If you buy a few kilos and put them in one afternoon, you will have supplies for snacks, potato salad and roulades until next summer.

Pickled cucumbers are among the safest preserving products of all because the vinegar inhibits the growth of dangerous germs. For this to work, the brew has to be acidic enough. And so that the cucumbers still crack after weeks in the jar, there are a few simple steps that many recipes leave out.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Take small, firm pickling cucumbers measuring 6 to 12 centimeters, if possible on the day of harvest.
> - Cut off the flower heads and place the cucumbers in cold salt water overnight: This keeps them crunchy.
> - The brew needs enough acid: vinegar with 5 percent acid and water in a ratio of at least 1 to 2, for long storage it is better to use a ratio of 1 to 1.
> - In a water bath, 80 to 85 degrees is enough for 20 to 25 minutes. Higher temperatures make cucumbers soft.
> - After boiling, let them sit for four to six weeks, then they are at their best.

## Which cucumbers are suitable?

For pickles you need **pickling cucumbers**, not the long cucumbers. Pickling cucumbers are short, have a warty, thin skin, few seeds and a firm flesh. The smaller they are, the crisper they stay. Cucumbers between 6 and 12 centimeters fit well in jars and are evenly acidified. You can quarter larger ones lengthwise or use them for mustard cucumbers, for which you have to peel and de-seed large cucumbers anyway.

Pay attention to these points when shopping:

- The cucumbers are plump and firm, with no soft spots or yellowish skin.
- The ends have not shrunk. Wrinkled ends show that the cucumber has already lost water.
- As fresh as possible: Cucumbers that have been in the store for several days soften more quickly in the jar. At the weekly market or directly from the producer you can often get goods from the same morning.

For a 1 liter jar, calculate around 500 to 600 grams of cucumbers, depending on the size.

## The trick for crunchy cucumbers

Soft, mushy pickles are the most common disappointment. The causes are known, and almost all of them can be avoided:

1. **Cut off the flower base.** There are enzymes at the flower end of the cucumber that break down pectin and soften the pulp. Cut a thin slice of about two millimeters from this end. If you don't know which end is which, just trim both ends close.
2. **Soak overnight.** Place the washed cucumbers in cold water with about a tablespoon of salt per liter for 8 to 12 hours. The cucumbers get full and become fuller.
3. **Tannins in the glass.** A piece of horseradish root, a grape leaf, a cherry or oak leaf in the glass provide tannins that hold the pulp firmly. This is an old home remedy that actually helps.
4. **Do not cook too hot.** Cucumbers cannot tolerate 100 degrees. At 80 to 85 degrees they remain firm, but when boiling water they become soft.
5. **Do not pour boiling over the stock** if you are cooking it afterwards. A hot but no longer bubbling brew is enough.

## The vinegar broth: it has to be so sour

The acid makes pickles last longer. Pathogens that can be dangerous in airtight jars do not grow in a sufficiently acidic environment. Therefore, the ratio of vinegar to water is not a question of taste that should be changed at will.

Use vinegar with 5 percent acid, such as brandy vinegar, white wine vinegar or mild apple cider vinegar. You have to dilute vinegar essence heavily and calculate precisely, which is why ready-made 5 percent vinegar is easier for beginners. You should not use home-made vinegar with unknown acidity for supplies.

| Brew for about 4 glasses of 1 liter each | quantity |
|---|---|
| Vinegar, 5% acid | 750ml |
| water | 750ml |
| sugar | 60 to 120 g, depending on taste |
| Salt (without iodine and anti-caking aid) | 30g, about 2 level tablespoons |

With this ratio of 1 to 1 you are on the safe side and the cucumbers taste very sour. If you prefer it milder, you can go as low as one part vinegar and two parts water. You shouldn't use less vinegar for supplies that have been sitting in the basement for months. You can adjust sugar and salt to taste; they have little impact on safety.

Iodized salt does not cloud the brew dangerously, but it can change the color of the cucumbers slightly. Pure boiled or rock salt without additives is therefore the better choice.

## Spices: the classic mix

This mixture has proven itself per liter jar:

- 1 to 2 dill flowers or a small bunch of dill with stems
- 1 teaspoon mustard seeds, yellow or brown
- ½ teaspoon black peppercorns
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 small onion in rings or 4 pearl onions
- 1 to 2 cloves of garlic, to taste
- 1 piece of horseradish, about the size of your thumb, peeled and sliced

Allspice, juniper berries, coriander seeds or a few strips of pepper are suitable for variations. Chili provides spiciness. The only important thing is that everything is fresh and clean: dried spices hardly carry any germs, you should wash fresh herbs and onions well.

## Insert step by step

1. **Prepare glasses:** Rinse twist-off or iron-on glasses with hot water and boil them or sterilize them in the oven at 120 degrees. Only scald the lid and rubber rings while they are hot.
2. **Prepare the cucumbers:** The day before, brush them thoroughly under running water, cut off the flower heads and place them in cold salt water overnight.
3. **Boil the stock:** Bring the vinegar, water, sugar and salt to the boil until everything is dissolved. Remove from heat and let cool slightly.
4. **Layers:** Put some of the spices on the bottom of the glass, place the cucumbers vertically and tightly, distribute the remaining spices in between and on top. The closer the cucumbers are, the less likely they will float.
5. **Pour:** Pour the hot broth over the cucumbers until they are completely covered. Leave about two centimeters of space at the top. Cucumbers that stick out will change color and become soft.
6. **Clean and seal the edge.**
7. **Preserving:** Place the jars in the preserving pot, pour in water until just below the lid and heat to 80 to 85 degrees. Once the temperature is reached, hold for 20 to 25 minutes.
8. **Cool:** Remove the jars, place them on a cloth and let them cool slowly. The next day check that all lids are tight.

### Without boiling?

Some recipes omit the water bath step and just pour boiling stock. Such cucumbers are ready more quickly and remain very crunchy, but they do not last as long and should be stored in the refrigerator after opening and, ideally, beforehand. For a stock that will overwinter in the cellar, short boiling is the more reliable method.

## Allow to steep and store

Freshly pickled cucumbers still taste like raw vinegar. Only after four to six weeks have the acid, salt and spices combined. Store the jars in a cool, dark place. If left unopened, they will last for about a year if you work carefully. Once opened, they go into the fridge and should be eaten within two to three weeks. Always remove the cucumbers with a clean fork, not your fingers.

Discard a jar if the lid is bulging, the brew foams, becomes slimy, or the cucumbers smell unpleasant. However, a slight cloudiness due to spices or a few sunken mustard seeds are normal.

## Variants: Salted cucumbers and mustard cucumbers

**Salted cucumbers** do not become sour with vinegar, but rather through lactic acid fermentation, similar to [sauerkraut](/kueche/sauerkraut-selbst-machen/). The cucumbers lie in a brine with dill, garlic and horseradish with around 40 to 60 grams of salt per liter of water and ferment at room temperature for one to two weeks. They taste fresher and milder, but only last a few weeks in the refrigerator.

**Mustard cucumbers** are made from large braised cucumbers: peel them, halve them lengthwise, remove the seeds, cut them into pieces, salt them overnight and pickle them with a sweet and sour broth containing lots of mustard seeds. What else you can cook with fresh braised cucumbers can be found in our article about [cucumber salad and braised cucumbers](/kueche/gurkensalat-und-schmorgurken/).

## What pickles are used for in the kitchen

Gherkins are more than just a side dish for a snack. They can be found in many classics in German cuisine:

- **Beef roulades:** A strip of gherkin belongs in the filling alongside mustard, bacon and onions and gives the sauce a delicate acidity.
- **Potato salad:** Diced cucumbers and a dash of cucumber stock make North German potato salad with mayonnaise fresh and spicy.
- **Remoulade:** Finely chopped gherkins, capers and herbs transform mayonnaise into the classic sauce for fish and fried potatoes.
- **Labskaus and herring salad:** In the north, jarred cucumbers are indispensable here.
- **For a snack:** With farmer's bread, cheese and radishes, a good pickle is simply a treat in itself.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why have my cucumbers become hollow?

Hollow cucumbers usually appear in the field, for example in dry conditions or if they have hung on the bush for too long. They are harmless in the glass, but taste less crunchy. Small, fresh cucumbers are less likely to be hollow.

### My brew has become milky. Is that bad?

With pickles, the stock should remain clear. A milky cloudiness can be due to iodized salt, hard water quality or starch from onions, but also to yeast or bacteria. If the jar smells strange, the lid is no longer tight or bubbles appear, throw it away. In salted cucumbers, however, cloudiness is a normal sign of fermentation.

### Can I reuse the brew?

Not for a new stock because it is diluted by the cucumbers and less acidic. But it is suitable as a seasoning for potato salad, herring salad or a dressing.

### Can I also pickle cucumbers?

For a quick cucumber salad in a jar that lasts a few days in the fridge, yes. They are less suitable for stock because their water-rich pulp quickly becomes soft in the brew. Real pickling cucumbers with a firm, warty skin remain much crunchier.
