For many people, the first strawberries from the region are the real sign that summer is coming. When the strawberry-shaped sales houses are set up on the roadsides in May and the fields open for self-picking, a short, intensive season begins. Outdoor strawberries from Germany are usually available from the end of May to July. The first fruits come from polytunnels and greenhouses in April, and so-called remontant or everbearing varieties deliver smaller quantities until autumn.

Anyone who has ever eaten a sun-warmed, fully ripe strawberry straight from the field knows how much difference ripeness and freshness make. This post will help you recognize good strawberries, treat them correctly and bake them into a classic strawberry cake.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Outdoor strawberries from Germany are in season from around the end of May to July.
> - Strawberries do not ripen after harvest. Only buy completely red, fragrant fruits.
> - Wash them carefully and only just before eating, before removing the green cup.
> - Unwashed and laid out flat, they will last one to two days in the refrigerator.
> - For a strawberry cake with sponge cake base and vanilla cream you need around 750 grams of strawberries.

## Recognize good strawberries

Strawberries are one of the fruits that no longer ripen after harvest. They become a little darker and softer, but not sweeter. That's why it's important to pay attention to maturity when buying:

- **Color:** The fruit should be red all the way down to the calyx. White or green shoulders at the base of the stem mean it was picked too early.
- **Fragrance:** Ripe strawberries smell intense, even from the shell.
- **Cup:** It should be fresh and green, not dried out.
- **Shine and firmness:** The surface is shiny, the fruit is plump but not hard. Mud, bruises and juice on the bottom of the bowl are signs of older goods.
- **Size:** It says little about the taste. Smaller fruits are often more aromatic.

### Well-known varieties

In the trade, strawberries rarely have a variety name. It's worth asking at the market and when picking yourself. Senga Sengana is an old, dark red variety with soft flesh that is particularly suitable for jam. Mieze Schindler is considered one of the most aromatic old varieties, but is very sensitive and rarely commercially available. Many modern varieties are bred for firmness and portability, which doesn't always benefit the taste.

### A berry that isn't one

Botanically, the strawberry is not a berry at all, but a collective nut fruit. The red, juicy pulp arises from the thickened flower base. The actual fruits are the small yellowish nuts on the surface that many people think are seeds. A strawberry usually produces a few hundred of them. This is a nice side note for the kitchen, and an amazing discovery for children on their next strawberry cake.

## Pick yourself

You can get particularly fresh fruit from self-picking fields, often cheaper than in the store. A few tips:

- Go early in the day when it is still cool. Strawberries picked in the midday heat quickly become soft.
- Pick the fruit with the calyx and a short piece of stem. This way they last longer.
- Bring shallow baskets or bowls, not deep buckets. The lower fruits are crushed in deep vessels.
- Immediately cool the harvest at home.

## Wash and store

Strawberries are delicate and absorb water easily. Water dilutes the taste and causes them to spoil more quickly. Therefore:

1. Wash the strawberries just before eating or processing.
2. Place them in a large bowl of cold water, swirl gently and lift out. A hard jet of water from the tap damages the fruit.
3. Only remove the cup after washing. Otherwise water will penetrate the fruit.
4. Drain them on a kitchen towel.

To store, spread the unwashed strawberries flat on a plate or tray lined with kitchen paper, sort out any damaged or moldy fruits and place them in the refrigerator. You should have used them up after a day or two. Take them out half an hour before eating so they taste more aromatic.

To freeze, wash and clean the strawberries, pre-freeze them individually on a tray and then fill them into bags. After defrosting, they are soft and suitable for sauces, smoothies, cakes and jams, but no longer for a fruit cake with fresh fruit.

## Strawberry cake with sponge cake base

For a fruit base mold or springform pan with a diameter of 26 to 28 cm:

**Sponge cake**

- 3 eggs (size M), room temperature
- 3 tbsp hot water
- 100g sugar
- 1 packet of vanilla sugar
- 1 pinch of salt
- 75 g wheat flour (Type 405)
- 25 g cornstarch
- 1 level teaspoon of baking powder
- Butter and breadcrumbs for the mold

**Vanilla cream**

- 1 packet of vanilla pudding powder (for 500 ml milk)
- 400 ml milk
- 40g sugar

**Topping and casting**

- 750g strawberries
- 1 packet of red cake icing
- 250 ml water or apple juice
- 2 tbsp sugar

### Bake sponge cake

1. Preheat the oven to 180 degrees top and bottom heat. Grease the mold thoroughly and sprinkle it with breadcrumbs. This is particularly important with a fruit base shape so that the base will loosen later.
2. Using the beaters of a hand mixer, beat the eggs and hot water for one minute until frothy. Slowly pour in the sugar, vanilla sugar and salt while beating and beat the mixture for another five to seven minutes until it is light, creamy and has significantly increased in volume.
3. Mix flour, cornstarch and baking powder and sift them into the egg mixture. Fold them in carefully with a spatula, just until there is no more flour to be seen. Do not stir or the dough will collapse.
4. Pour the dough into the tin, smooth it out and bake it on the middle shelf for 15 to 20 minutes, until it is golden and springs back when lightly pressed.
5. Let the base rest in the tin for five minutes, then turn it out onto a wire rack and let it cool completely.

### Cream and topping

1. Mix the pudding powder with the sugar and six tablespoons of the milk until smooth. Bring the remaining milk to the boil, remove the pot from the heat and stir in the mixed powder. Let everything cook for a minute while stirring. With less milk than stated on the package, the pudding becomes firmer and spreads easily.
2. Let the pudding cool down a little, stir it occasionally and spread it on the base while it is still lukewarm. Let it cool completely.
3. Wash the strawberries, remove the calyxes and halve larger fruits. Place them close together on the cream, with the cut side down or upright in a cone shape.
4. Prepare the cake icing according to the package instructions with water or apple juice and sugar and use a tablespoon to spread it evenly over the strawberries from the center.
5. Chill the cake for at least an hour until the icing is firm.

Strawberry cake tastes best with freshly whipped cream the day you make it. It will keep in the fridge until the next day, but the base will then become softer.

## Quick layered dessert without baking

If you're in a hurry, layer strawberries with a curd cream in glasses. Mix 250 g low-fat quark with 200 g yoghurt, two tablespoons of sugar, a packet of vanilla sugar and some grated lemon peel. Cut 300 g strawberries into small pieces and mix them with a tablespoon of sugar. Roughly crumble six ladyfingers or a few shortbread cookies. Alternately layer crumbs, cream and strawberries in four glasses and let the dessert sit in the fridge for at least half an hour. The crumbs absorb some of the strawberry juice and become soft like in a tiramisu.

## Strawberries in the garden and on the balcony

Strawberries grow in beds, in raised beds, in balcony boxes and even in hanging baskets. The best planting time for the classic, once-bearing varieties is late summer, around the end of July to the end of August. The plants then form strong roots by winter and bear abundantly in the next early summer. A layer of straw under the ripening fruits keeps them clean and dry and prevents gray mold. After three to four years the yield decreases. Then you grow young plants from the runners and plant them in a new spot. Everbearing varieties that produce individual fruits from June to autumn are suitable for balconies.

## What's in strawberries

Strawberries are low in calories and at the same time rich in vitamin C. Just around 200 grams cover the daily requirement of an adult. They also provide folate, potassium and fiber. Their red color comes from anthocyanins. Because vitamin C is sensitive to heat, fresh strawberries are more valuable than cooked jam in this regard.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why are my strawberries white inside?

Some varieties have a naturally light interior. If the fruit is not completely red on the outside, it was harvested too early. It won't get any sweeter then.

### Why is the sponge cake base soggy?

Moisture from fruit and cream soaks into the soil. A thin layer of melted chocolate or some jam spread before the cream acts as a barrier. Also, only cover the cake when the base and cream are cold.

### Can I ripen strawberries from the supermarket?

No. Strawberries do not ripen after harvest. Unripe strawberries are most suitable for compotes, jams or smoothies, where you can add a little sugar.
