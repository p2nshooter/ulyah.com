Those who shop seasonally often buy in larger quantities: a box of apples from the farm, a sack of potatoes, the vegetable box with cabbage, carrots and leeks. To ensure that none of it spoils, proper storage is crucial. There is no uniform rule. Some varieties love the cold, others get brown spots from it. Some give off ripening gases to which others are sensitive.

A little knowledge of the needs of each variety saves money and hassle and ensures that vegetables taste the same at the end of the week as they did on market day.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Tomatoes, cucumbers, zucchini, eggplant, peppers, bananas and citrus fruits are particularly sensitive to cold. They are better stored at 10 to 15 degrees.
> - Leafy greens, cabbage, roots, berries and most herbs belong in the vegetable compartment of the refrigerator.
> - Apples, tomatoes, bananas, pears and apricots release ethylene. Store them separately from sensitive varieties.
> - Potatoes should be stored dark and cool, but not in the refrigerator; onions should be stored dry and airy, not next to potatoes.
> - Immediately remove the greens from carrots, radishes and kohlrabi so that the tubers do not wilt.

## Why fruits and vegetables spoil

Fruits and vegetables live on after they are harvested. They breathe, use up their reserves and lose water. The warmer it is, the faster this process occurs. This is why most vegetables last longer when refrigerated. At the same time, many vegetables and fruits from warm regions are not adapted to cold. Below a certain temperature, so-called cold damage occurs: the cells are damaged, the vegetables become glassy, ​​spotty, soft or lose their aroma.

There is also the ripening gas ethylene. It is released in larger quantities by some fruits and accelerates the ripening and aging of nearby fruits and vegetables. A single apple can turn an entire bowl of broccoli yellow faster.

## The zones of the refrigerator

In most refrigerators, it is not the same cold everywhere.

| zone | Temperature (approx.) | Suitable for |
|---|---|---|
| Bottom glass plate | 2-3°C | raw meat, fish |
| Medium compartments | 4-5°C | Dairy products, cheese, leftovers |
| Upper compartments | 6-8°C | Jams, cooked foods, drinks |
| vegetable compartment | 8–10 °C, higher humidity | Vegetables, lettuce, berries, herbs |
| door | 8-10°C | Butter, eggs, mustard, drinks |

The vegetable compartment is separated from the rest by the glass plate and has a higher humidity. Vegetables are best here. Don't put it too tightly or in airtight plastic bags, otherwise condensation will form and it will rot more quickly. Cloth bags, paper bags, cans with some kitchen paper or bags with a few air holes have proven successful.

## What belongs in the fridge and what doesn't

| Storage | Fruits and vegetables |
|---|---|
| Refrigerator, vegetable compartment | Leaf lettuce, spinach, chard, broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage, leek, carrots, celery, radishes, kohlrabi, beetroot, asparagus, peas, mushrooms, berries, cherries, grapes, fresh herbs (except basil) |
| Cool, 10-15°C, e.g. B. Pantry | Tomatoes, cucumbers, zucchini, eggplant, peppers, pumpkin, beans (short), citrus fruits, bananas, pineapple, mangoes |
| Dark and cool, 4-10°C, not in the fridge | Potatoes |
| Dry, airy, cool | Onions, garlic, shallots |
| Cool, 2–4 °C, in the cellar or refrigerator | Apples, pears (ripe) |
| Room temperature for ripening | unripe pears, peaches, apricots, avocados, kiwis, then cool |

Green beans are a special case: They like around 8 degrees and become spotty if too cold. They will keep in the vegetable compartment for two to three days. Basil comes from warm regions and turns black in the refrigerator. Place it on the counter like a bouquet of flowers in a glass of water, out of the direct sun.

## Ethylene: who releases it, who is sensitive

**Strong ethylene formers:** Apples, apricots, avocados, bananas, pears, peaches, plums, tomatoes, mangoes, passion fruits.

**Ethylene sensitive:** Broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage, Brussels sprouts, cucumbers, lettuce, herbs, carrots, potatoes, kiwis, eggplant, spinach.

Store the two groups separately, such as the apples in the pantry and the broccoli in the refrigerator. But you can also use ethylene: A hard avocado or unripe kiwi will ripen in a paper bag with an apple within one or two days.

## The big storage vegetables

### Potatoes

Potatoes like it dark, cool and airy; 4 to 8 degrees is ideal. Light turns them green. They produce solanine, which in large quantities can lead to gastrointestinal problems. Cut out green areas and sprouts generously and throw away any tubers that have turned green.

Potatoes don't belong in the fridge. At temperatures below around 4 degrees they convert starch into sugar. They then taste sweet and more acrylamide is produced when frying, baking and deep-frying. Store potatoes in a paper bag, burlap sack or wooden box, not plastic, and not next to apples or onions. You can read more about the varieties in the article about [potato types and cooking types] (/kueche/kartoffelsorten-kochtypen/).

### Onions and garlic

Onions need it dry and airy. Moisture causes them to rot or sprout. A basket, net or clay pot with holes in a cool place is ideal. They should not be placed next to potatoes because both encourage each other to sprout. Store cut onions cut side down on a small plate or in a can in the refrigerator and use them in two to three days.

### Carrots, beetroot and other roots

Turn off the greens immediately after purchase; they remove water from the roots. Carrots will last two to three weeks in the vegetable compartment, wrapped in a damp cloth or in a can. Carrots that have already become soft will become crunchy if you place them in cold water for a few hours. If you have a cellar, you can put root vegetables in boxes with slightly damp sand as before. This way they last until spring.

### Cabbage

White cabbage, red cabbage and savoy cabbage last for several weeks in the refrigerator, whole heads last even longer in the cool cellar. Only remove wilted outer leaves shortly before cooking; they protect the head. Wrap cut cabbage in foil or beeswax cloth.

## Keep lettuce and herbs fresher for longer

It is best to wash leaf salads just before eating. Unwashed, wrapped in a damp kitchen towel or in a container with kitchen paper, lettuce and romaine last two to four days, lamb's lettuce and rocket last one to two days. Limp lettuce recovers in a bowl of cold water.

Parsley, dill, coriander and chives will keep for about a week in a damp kitchen paper towel in a tin in the vegetable compartment. You can also place parsley and coriander like flowers in a glass of water and loosely place a bag over it.

## Berries and stone fruits

Berries are particularly sensitive. After shopping, sort them out, sort out moldy or mushy fruits and store them unwashed, laid out flat, in the refrigerator. Wash them just before eating. Strawberries, raspberries and blackberries last one to two days, blueberries and currants last significantly longer. Stone fruits like cherries also belong in the fridge. You let peaches, nectarines and apricots ripen at room temperature and only cool them when they are soft.

## In what order consume?

If you sort the vegetable box from the market according to its shelf life, you hardly throw anything away. Plan the week's meals so that the most delicate ingredients go first.

| Consume within | Fruits and vegetables |
|---|---|
| 1 to 2 days | Berries, lamb's lettuce, rocket, spinach, mushrooms, asparagus, corn on the cob, herbs such as basil |
| 3 to 5 days | Broccoli, cauliflower, green beans, zucchini, lettuce, peaches, cherries |
| 1 to 2 weeks | Peppers, cucumbers, kohlrabi, leeks, Brussels sprouts, citrus fruits |
| several weeks | Carrots, beets, cabbages, apples, pumpkins, potatoes, onions |

A look in the fridge and storage basket before the next purchase shows what needs to go first. Anything that won't be eaten for the foreseeable future will go into soup, casserole or freezer in good time. ## Storage without a basement

Very few city apartments have a cool basement. A few alternatives still work:

- **Unheated room:** A storage room, a guest room with the heating turned off or a hallway on the outside wall often stays below 12 degrees in winter. That's enough for apples, pumpkins, onions and potatoes.
- **Balcony:** Apples, carrots and potatoes are stored in a Styrofoam box or an old cool box, lined with newspaper, protected from frost over the winter. If there is severe frost, bring the box into the stairwell for a few days.
- **Soil rent in the garden:** In the past, root vegetables and cabbage were stored in pits lined with straw and covered with soil. A buried, perforated bucket with a lid serves the same purpose for small quantities.

For all variants, it is important to have a thermometer so that you can keep an eye on the temperature, and a weekly check to sort out any rotten pieces.

## Why good storage is worth it

According to calculations by the Thünen Institute, around 78 kilograms of food per capita ends up in waste in German private households every year. Fruit and vegetables make up a large proportion of this, often simply because they were stored incorrectly or were forgotten. If you know your supplies and store them correctly, you not only save money, but also the work and resources that go into every head of lettuce and every kilo of apples.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I still eat fruit with a moldy spot?

Not with soft, water-rich fruits such as berries, peaches, tomatoes or cucumbers because the mold can spread invisibly throughout the flesh. For very firm vegetables such as carrots or cabbage, you can generously cut out small areas if the rest is perfect.

### Why do bananas turn brown in the fridge?

Bananas are sensitive to cold. The skin turns brown to black in cold weather, but the flesh often remains good for a while. It is therefore best to store bananas at room temperature, separate from other fruit.

### How do I store cut vegetables?

Cut side down on a plate or in a container in the fridge. Use it within two to three days. Cut avocados will last longer if you leave the pit in and brush the cut surface with lemon juice.
