Hardly any other vegetable is as celebrated in Germany as asparagus. When the first spears come out of the dams in April, there are asparagus stalls on the side of the road in many cities, inns print their own asparagus cards, and queues form in front of the asparagus farmers' stands at the weekly market. The season is short: traditionally it ends on June 24th, St. John's Day. The plant then needs its strength to form new shoots and create reserves for the next year.

Especially because time is so short, it is worth handling asparagus carefully. A few steps when shopping, peeling and cooking decide whether the stalks arrive on the plate tender and juicy or fibrous and watery.

> **The most important things in brief**
>
> - Fresh asparagus squeaks when you rub the stalks together and has juicy, not dried out cut ends.
> - You peel white asparagus from the head down and leave the head alone; You only peel green asparagus in the lower third at most.
> - Depending on the thickness, white asparagus takes around 10 to 15 minutes, green usually only 5 to 8 minutes.
> - Wrapped in a damp cloth, asparagus will keep in the vegetable compartment for two to three days.
> - Peeled raw, it can be frozen and added directly to boiling water when frozen.

## White, green or purple?

White, green and purple asparagus are not different plants, but differ primarily in how they grow. White asparagus grows under earthen dams, which are often additionally covered with foil. It does not receive any light, therefore does not form chlorophyll and remains light. As soon as the tip hits the surface and receives light, it turns purple. Green asparagus, on the other hand, grows above ground and turns green like any other plant in the sun.

| variety | taste | Peel | Cooking time (medium thickness) |
|---|---|---|---|
| White asparagus | mild, slightly nutty, slightly tart | whole, from about 2 cm below the head | Cook for 10-15 minutes |
| Purple asparagus | a little stronger, slightly sweet | like white asparagus | 10-12 minutes |
| Green asparagus | spicy, grassy, reminiscent of peas | only the lower third, often not at all | Cook for 5-8 minutes or fry for 4-6 minutes |

In Germany, white asparagus is usually used for classic asparagus dishes with potatoes and sauce. Green asparagus is better for roasting, grilling and salads because it is firmer and retains its flavor even over high heat.

## Recognize fresh asparagus

Asparagus quickly loses moisture and aroma after being pierced. The shorter the time between field and pot, the better. When shopping, look out for these signs:

- **Cut ends:** They should be juicy and light. If you press it with your fingernail, some juice will come out. Dry, woody or brownish ends indicate older goods.
- **Sound:** If you rub two rods together, fresh rods will squeak audibly.
- **Heads:** They should be closed and tight. With white asparagus, a slight purple color is not a defect, just a question of taste.
- **Surface:** smooth and shiny, without wrinkles.
- **Plexibility:** Fresh sticks are difficult to bend. Soft, limp rods are too old.

Asparagus is sold in commercial grades based on thickness, straightness and appearance. This is less important for taste than you think. Crooked or broken spears, often offered cheaper as soup asparagus, taste just as good and are ideal for soups, salads and ragouts.

### How much do you need?

For the main dish with potatoes, count around 500 grams of white asparagus per person, weighed before peeling. You lose around a quarter of the weight through peels and ends. As a side dish, 250 to 300 grams per person is sufficient.

## Store asparagus

It's best to cook asparagus on the day you buy it. If that's not possible, wrap the unpeeled stalks in a damp kitchen towel and place them in the vegetable compartment of the refrigerator. This way they stay fresh for two, at most three days. You can also place green asparagus upright in a glass with a little water, like a bouquet of flowers, and loosely place a bag over it.

To freeze, peel the asparagus, cut off the ends and freeze the raw spears flat on a board without blanching. Then they go into a freezer bag. To cook, add them frozen directly to the bubbling water. You shouldn't defrost them because otherwise they will become soft and fibrous.

## Peel white asparagus

To peel, you need a sharp peeler. Special asparagus peelers with a movable blade and guide make the work easier, but an ordinary peeler also works.

1. Place the stick flat on your hand or over your forearm so it doesn't break when peeling.
2. Place the peeler about two centimeters below the head and peel downwards in long strokes.
3. Rotate the rod and peel all around until there is no peel left. Towards the end you can peel a little more forcefully because the peel is thicker there.
4. Finally, cut off the ends, about one to two centimeters, or more if the ends are woody.

The most common mistake: peeling too thinly. If leftover peel remains, the asparagus will become fibrous, no matter how long you cook it. You'll know whether you've been thorough enough when you run your finger over the bar: it should feel evenly smooth.

### Do not throw away peels

You cook an aromatic stock from the peels and ends. Put them in a pot with about 1.5 liters of water, a pinch of salt and a pinch of sugar, let everything simmer gently for 20 minutes and pour the broth through a sieve. You can then cook the asparagus in it, or you can use the stock as a basis for a cream of asparagus soup. It can also be frozen when cooled.

## Cook white asparagus

For four people as a main course you need:

- 2 kg white asparagus
- 1 teaspoon salt per liter of water
- 1 tsp sugar
- 1 tbsp butter
- 1 slice of lemon if desired

Bring enough water to the boil in a wide pot to cover the stalks well. Add salt, sugar, butter and lemon. The sugar softens the fine bitter substances, the lemon keeps the asparagus light. Place the spears in the bubbling water, reduce the heat and let them cook over a mild heat for 10 to 15 minutes, depending on the thickness. Very thin sticks are cooked after just eight minutes, very thick ones take up to 18.

The asparagus is ready when it bends slightly in the middle when you lift it with a fork and the knife slides in without resistance. It should still have a slight bite. Remove it with a slotted spoon and let it drain briefly.

### Asparagus from the oven

If you don't want to leach the asparagus in water, cook it in a package. Place the peeled sticks on a large piece of baking paper, add some butter, salt and a pinch of sugar, fold the paper into a tight package and cook it at 180 degrees top and bottom heat for about 35 to 40 minutes. The asparagus cooks in its own juice and tastes particularly intense.

## Prepare green asparagus

You usually don't have to peel green asparagus at all. Cut off the ends and test whether the bottom third is woody. If so, you only peel there. Green asparagus cooks in five to eight minutes.

It tastes particularly good when fried: Cut the spears diagonally into pieces or leave them whole, heat some olive oil in a large pan and fry the asparagus over medium to high heat for four to six minutes until it is browned in places. Only add salt at the end and serve with a little lemon peel and freshly grated hard cheese.

## Hollandaise sauce

The hollandaise is considered difficult, but can be done with a little patience. It is crucial that the egg yolk does not get too hot, otherwise it will set and the sauce will curdle.

For four people:

- 3 very fresh egg yolks
- 3 tbsp white wine or water
- 200g butter
- 1 to 2 teaspoons lemon juice
- Salt, a pinch of cayenne pepper

1. Melt the butter in a small saucepan and let it cool slightly. If you like, skim off the white foam.
2. Bring some water to simmer in a pot. It shouldn't bubble.
3. Place the egg yolks and wine in a metal bowl, place on top of the pot and beat with a whisk until light, creamy and thick, about three to five minutes. The whisk then leaves clear marks.
4. Remove the bowl from the water bath and let the warm, not hot butter pour in, first drop by drop and then in a thin stream. Stir constantly.
5. Season with lemon juice, salt and cayenne pepper and serve immediately.

If the mixture gets too hot and starts to flocculate, immediately remove the bowl from the heat and stir in a tablespoon of cold water. If the sauce has already curdled, beat a fresh egg yolk with a tablespoon of water in a new bowl and slowly stir in the curdled sauce.

Since the egg yolk is not completely heated through with hollandaise, pregnant women, small children, the elderly and people with weakened immune systems should use another sauce, such as melted butter with toasted breadcrumbs, the so-called Polish butter, or a vinaigrette.

## Classic side dishes

In Germany, white asparagus includes new potatoes, ideally the first early potatoes of the season. Cooked or raw ham is often served with this, and in northern Germany also a schnitzel or pancakes. If you prefer something lighter, eat asparagus with a vinaigrette made from white wine vinegar, mustard, oil, finely chopped shallots and chives.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does urine smell after eating asparagus?

When the aspartic acid contained in asparagus breaks down, sulfur-containing compounds are formed, which are excreted shortly after eating. This is harmless. Interestingly, not all people can detect the smell.

### Can I reheat asparagus?

Yes. Cooked asparagus will keep covered in the refrigerator for a day. To warm it up, place it briefly in hot asparagus stock. However, it becomes a little softer. Leftovers are good for salad or soup.

### Why shouldn't you cut asparagus after June 24th?

The plant needs around 100 days after harvest to store enough nutrients in the rootstock with its leaves. If you prick for too long, the harvest will be weaker the following year. St. John's Day is the old rule of thumb for this.

### Is bitter asparagus spoiled?

No. A slight bitterness occurs especially at the ends and on sticks that have received light. It can be alleviated by peeling it thoroughly and adding a pinch of sugar to the cooking water. However, if asparagus smells musty or is slimy, it belongs in the trash.
