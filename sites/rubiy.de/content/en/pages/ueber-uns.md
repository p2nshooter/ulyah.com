RUBIY stands for **turnip, **pear and **season, although we ended up making it a Y because it looks nicer on a label. We write for everyone who wants to cook with what is currently growing: for the household that receives a box of vegetables every week and doesn't know what to do with the fourth head of savoy cabbage. For the hobby gardener whose zucchini grow faster in August than she can harvest them. For everyone who stands in front of a quince at the weekly market and wonders what to actually do with it.

## Why RUBIY exists

Cooking seasonally sounds easy and actually it is. Nevertheless, the knowledge that was previously passed on as a matter of course is often missing in everyday life: When does the asparagus season begin and when does it end? Why shouldn't you harvest rhubarb after the end of June? How do you distinguish wild garlic from the poisonous lily of the valley? How long does homemade jam last, and why is preserving green beans trickier than preserving cherries?

You can find many contradictory answers to such questions on the Internet. We want to be a calm, reliable kitchen library: with recipes that work and with background knowledge that explains why they work.

## What you will find with us

RUBIY is divided into six shelves like a well-stocked pantry:

1. **Spring:** Asparagus, wild garlic, rhubarb, strawberries, young potatoes and the first herbs
2. **Summer:** Tomatoes, zucchini, berries, cucumbers, plums and light dishes for hot days
3. **Autumn:** Pumpkin, apples, mushrooms, red cabbage, chestnuts, quinces and onion cake
4. **Winter:** Kale, beetroot, turnip, lamb's lettuce, lentils, sauerkraut and root vegetables
5. **Storage & Preservation:** Canning, jam, fermentation, freezing, drying and herbs
6. **Kitchen knowledge:** Storage, shopping, cooking leftovers, knife knowledge and everything that doesn't fit into any season

There is also our [seasonal calendar](/saisonkalender/), which shows month after month what is fresh from the field or from the warehouse in Germany.

## How our recipes are created

Each contribution is created specifically for RUBIY and is not compiled from other websites. We also use AI tools when researching and writing. The recipes follow the regional cooking tradition and what generations of cooks have tried over decades; Amounts and times are guidelines that you adapt to your stove and your ingredients. When it comes to food safety issues, such as when preserving, fermenting or collecting wild plants, we rely on the recommendations of authorities and specialist bodies for food safety and consumer protection. How we work exactly is stated in our [Editorial Principles](/redaktionsgrundsaetze/).

## What we don't do

We don't name brands, sell anything, and don't get any money from food or kitchen appliance manufacturers. RUBIY is financed through advertisements, which are recognizable as such and have no influence on our content.

## The name and the picture

At King Solomon's court, the Bible tells us, large quantities of fine flour, semolina and all kinds of provisions were consumed every day. A palace lives from its pantry. Our pantry is smaller: cedar shelves, a copper pot, and rows of jars that fill with the colors of the seasons as the year progresses. When you hover over the menu, the lid rotates and the jar fills.
