Cooking seasonally means cooking with what is currently growing and harvested nearby. It tastes better because ripe fruits and vegetables have more flavor. It is often cheaper because the selection is large. And it brings variety to the kitchen because each month brings its own ingredients.

This calendar shows when fruit and vegetables are typically harvested in Germany. Depending on the region, weather and year, the season can be delayed by a few weeks: in the Rhine Valley and the Palatinate it starts earlier than in the north or at higher altitudes.

> **How to read the calendar**
>
> - **Outdoor:** freshly harvested from the field, the heart of the season.
> - **Protected cultivation:** from the unheated polytunnel or greenhouse, often a few weeks earlier than outdoors.
> - **Storage:** harvested in autumn and stored in a cool place, for example potatoes, carrots, cabbage, apples and onions. Stocked goods are the regional basis in winter.

## Why cook seasonally?

- **Taste:** Fruit and vegetables that are harvested ripe and sold quickly have more flavor than goods that are harvested unripe and transported for a long time.
- **Price:** In high season the offer is large and the price is often low.
- **Environment:** Seasonal goods from the region generally do not require heated greenhouses or long transport. Heated off-season cultivation can use a lot of energy.
- **Variety:** If you cook with the seasons, you eat a wide variety throughout the year.

Seasonal doesn't mean strict: Citrus fruits in winter, rice, spices or coffee are of course part of many kitchens. The seasonal calendar should be a guide, not a ban.
