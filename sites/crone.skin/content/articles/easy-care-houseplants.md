There is something deeply comforting about a home filled with green, growing things. A trailing plant on a bookshelf, a tall leafy specimen in a bright corner, a pot of herbs on the kitchen windowsill: plants soften a room, bring life to quiet spaces and give us something to tend and watch. For women who have downsized from a house with a garden, or who are spending more time at home, houseplants can be a real source of pleasure. Yet many people are put off by memories of plants that wilted, yellowed or simply gave up despite their best efforts.

The secret is choosing the right plants for your home and caring for them simply. This guide explains how to read the light in your rooms, which plants are forgiving for beginners, how to water without overdoing it, choosing pots and soil, dealing with common pests, keeping plants safe around pets and grandchildren, and making the most of small spaces.

## Start with the light

Light is the single most important factor for houseplants. Before buying, look at where you would like to put a plant and notice how much light it receives.

- **Bright direct light:** sun shines straight onto the spot for several hours, often near south-facing windows in the northern hemisphere or north-facing windows in the southern hemisphere.
- **Bright indirect light:** a well-lit spot near a window but out of direct sun, or behind a sheer curtain.
- **Medium light:** a few metres from a window, or near a window that receives little sun.
- **Low light:** far from windows, in hallways or dim corners. Very few plants truly thrive here, but some tolerate it.

Most popular houseplants prefer **bright indirect light**. Matching the plant to the light is far more important than any fertiliser or gadget.

## Forgiving plants for beginners

These plants are widely regarded as tolerant of occasional neglect:

| Plant | Light | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Snake plant | low to bright indirect | upright leaves; needs little water; very tolerant |
| ZZ plant | low to bright indirect | glossy leaves; stores water; dislikes overwatering |
| Pothos | low to bright indirect | trailing; shows clearly when it needs water |
| Spider plant | medium to bright indirect | produces baby plants you can pass on |
| Cast iron plant | low to medium | lives up to its name; slow-growing |
| Peace lily | medium to bright indirect | droops dramatically when thirsty, then recovers |
| Philodendron (heartleaf) | medium to bright indirect | trailing or climbing; easy to propagate |
| Christmas cactus | bright indirect | flowers in winter; less spiky than desert cacti |
| Herbs such as mint and parsley | bright, ideally some sun | useful and fragrant on a kitchen windowsill |

Start with two or three plants. Once you feel confident, add more.

## Watering: less is often more

More houseplants die from overwatering than from underwatering. Roots need air as well as water, and soil that stays soggy can cause them to rot.

- **Check before you water.** Push a finger into the soil up to the first knuckle. For most plants, water when the top layer feels dry.
- **Water thoroughly**, until water runs out of the drainage holes, then let it drain. Empty saucers so the plant is not sitting in water.
- **Water less in winter**, when most plants grow more slowly.
- **Use room-temperature water**, and let tap water stand if your plants seem sensitive to it.
- **Learn each plant's signals:** drooping can mean thirst in some plants and overwatering in others; yellow lower leaves often point to too much water.

Some people find it helpful to keep a simple watering note on the calendar, but checking the soil is more reliable than a fixed schedule.

## Pots and soil

- **Drainage holes are essential.** If you love a decorative pot without holes, use it as an outer pot and keep the plant in a plastic pot inside it.
- **Choose a pot slightly larger** than the root ball when repotting; a pot that is far too big holds excess water.
- **Use the right compost**: a general houseplant compost suits most plants, while cacti and succulents need a free-draining mix.
- **Repot** when roots fill the pot or grow out of the bottom, usually in spring.
- **Lighter pots**, such as plastic or fibreglass, are easier to move than heavy ceramic or stone.

## Feeding and cleaning

- **Feed sparingly** during the growing season with a houseplant fertiliser, following the instructions; most plants need little or no feed in winter.
- **Dust the leaves** with a soft, damp cloth so they can absorb light.
- **Remove dead leaves** to keep plants tidy and healthy.
- **Turn plants** occasionally so they grow evenly rather than leaning towards the window.

## Common pests and problems

Even healthy plants sometimes attract pests:

- **fungus gnats**: small flies around the soil, often a sign of overwatering; let the soil dry out more between waterings;
- **mealybugs**: white, cottony spots on leaves and stems; wipe them away with a cloth or cotton bud;
- **spider mites**: fine webbing and speckled leaves, often in dry air; rinse the plant and increase humidity;
- **scale insects**: small brown bumps on stems and leaves; scrape them off gently.

Isolate affected plants, check others nearby and remove badly affected leaves. If you use a treatment, choose one designed for houseplants and follow the label carefully.

## Pets and grandchildren

Some common houseplants can be harmful if eaten by cats, dogs or small children.

- **Check each plant** before you bring it home, using reliable sources such as veterinary or poison information services in your country.
- **Place plants out of reach** of curious pets and toddlers, or choose pet-safe varieties.
- **Keep fertilisers and treatments** locked away.

If you think a pet or child has eaten part of a plant and is unwell, contact a vet, doctor or poison information service promptly.

## Plants for small spaces

If space is tight:

- **trailing plants** on high shelves and in hanging pots use vertical space;
- **windowsill herbs** combine beauty with flavour; our guide to [balcony and container gardening](/journal/balcony-and-container-gardening/) has more ideas for small outdoor spaces;
- **compact plants** such as small succulents or peperomias suit desks and bedside tables;
- **plant stands** with several tiers create a green corner in little floor space.

## Making plant care easy on the body

- keep a **lightweight watering can** with a long spout for reaching plants without stretching;
- **group plants** together so watering is quicker and humidity higher;
- place large pots on **plant caddies with wheels**, so you can move them without lifting;
- **avoid placing plants** where you would have to climb or stretch to reach them.

## Buying healthy plants

A healthy plant from the start is far more likely to thrive. When choosing one in a shop or garden centre:

- look for **firm, evenly coloured leaves** without yellow patches, brown edges or spots;
- check the **undersides of leaves** and the joints of stems for pests;
- avoid plants whose **soil is soggy or smells sour**, or whose roots are bursting out of the pot;
- choose plants with **new growth**, a sign that they are actively growing;
- buy from places where plants are **kept in good light**, not stacked in dark corners for weeks.

Once you bring a new plant home, keep it apart from your other plants for a week or two, so any hidden pests do not spread. Then place it in the spot you chose for its light, and resist the urge to repot it immediately; most plants settle better if they are given a few weeks to adjust first.

## Sharing and swapping

Many plants are easy to propagate from cuttings or offsets: pothos, philodendron, spider plants and many succulents root easily in water or compost. Sharing cuttings is a lovely way to connect with friends, neighbours and grandchildren, and plant swaps are popular in many communities. ## Frequently asked questions

### Why are the leaves on my plant turning yellow?

The most common reason is overwatering, but it can also be natural ageing of old leaves, too little light or a need for feeding. Check the soil moisture first.

### Can I grow plants in a room without windows?

Very few plants survive long without natural light. If you want greenery there, consider a plant grow light, or rotate plants between a bright room and the darker one.

### How do I care for my plants when I go away?

Water thoroughly before leaving, group plants together away from direct sun, and ask a neighbour to check on them for longer trips. Self-watering pots can help.

## The bottom line

Houseplants thrive when they are matched to the light in your home and watered with restraint. Notice the light in each spot, start with forgiving plants such as snake plants, ZZ plants, pothos, spider plants and peace lilies, and water only when the top of the soil feels dry. Use pots with drainage holes, a suitable compost and light feeding in the growing season. Watch for common pests, check whether plants are safe for pets and children, use vertical space in small homes and make care easy on your body. And when your plants grow, share the cuttings.
