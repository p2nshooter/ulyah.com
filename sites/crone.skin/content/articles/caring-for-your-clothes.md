After fifty, many women find that they buy fewer clothes but better ones. A cashmere jumper that feels like a hug, a perfectly cut pair of trousers, a silk blouse that flatters every time. These are pieces we want to keep for years. Yet clothes rarely wear out from wearing; more often they are worn out by washing too hot, drying too fast, storing badly or ignoring a small problem until it becomes a big one. Good care keeps clothes looking new, saves money and is kinder to the planet.

This guide covers how to read care labels, wash more gently, dry without shrinking or stretching, deal with pilling and stains, protect knitwear from moths, store seasonal clothes, and make small repairs that extend the life of favourite pieces.

## Read the care label

The care label tells you how the manufacturer expects the garment to be cleaned. The symbols vary slightly between regions, but usually show:

- **washing:** a tub symbol, with a temperature or dots, and a hand for hand washing;
- **bleaching:** a triangle;
- **drying:** a square, with a circle inside for tumble drying;
- **ironing:** an iron, with dots for temperature;
- **professional cleaning:** a circle.

A cross through any symbol means "do not". If the label has been cut out, treat the garment gently: cool wash, mild detergent, air dry.

## Wash less, and more gently

### Wash only when needed

Many clothes, especially knitwear, jeans, jackets and trousers, do not need washing after every wear. Air them on a hanger by an open window, brush off dust and treat small marks straight away. Washing less often keeps colours brighter and fibres stronger.

### Sort carefully

- by **colour**: whites, lights and darks;
- by **fabric**: delicates separately from heavy cottons and towels;
- by **dirt level**: very dirty items separately.

### Lower temperatures

Most everyday clothes clean well at lower temperatures with modern detergents. Cooler washes reduce shrinking, fading and wear, and save energy. Keep hotter washes for items that need them, such as towels, bedding and underwear, following care labels.

### Protect delicate items

- **turn clothes inside out**, especially dark colours, prints and knitwear;
- **close zips and fasten hooks** so they do not snag other items;
- **use mesh laundry bags** for lingerie, tights and delicate tops;
- **do not overload the machine**, so clothes can move freely.

### Choose the right detergent

- use a **gentle detergent** for wool and silk, designed for those fibres;
- use **less detergent** than you might think; too much leaves residue and stiffness;
- consider **colour-protecting detergent** for darks.

### Hand washing

For cashmere, fine wool, silk and delicate lace, hand washing is often safest:

1. use lukewarm water and a gentle detergent;
2. soak briefly and press gently, without rubbing or wringing;
3. rinse in water of the same temperature;
4. press out water by rolling the garment in a towel.

## Drying without damage

- **Air dry** where possible. It is gentler and costs nothing.
- **Dry knitwear flat**, on a towel or a mesh drying rack, reshaping it while damp. Hanging wet knitwear stretches it.
- **Hang shirts and dresses** on hangers to dry, which reduces creases.
- **Keep clothes out of direct strong sunlight** for long periods if you want to avoid fading, especially darks and bright colours.
- **Use the tumble dryer selectively**, at low heat, and avoid it for wool, silk and anything with elastic or delicate trims.

Line drying has a pleasant side effect: it costs nothing and saves the energy a dryer would use.

## Bras, lingerie and hosiery

Underwear works hard and is often treated roughly, yet a well-fitting bra is one of the most important foundations of any outfit.

- **Hand wash bras** in lukewarm water with a gentle detergent, or wash them in a mesh bag on a delicate cycle with the hooks fastened.
- **Never tumble dry bras**; heat damages elastic and underwires. Reshape the cups and dry them flat or hanging by the centre.
- **Rotate bras** so that each one has a day or two of rest between wears, which helps the elastic recover.
- **Store moulded bras** with the cups nested together rather than folded inside out.
- **Wash tights and stockings** in a mesh bag, or by hand, and dry them away from heat.
- **Have your bra size checked** from time to time, because sizes often change over the years. A good fit improves comfort and the way clothes hang.

## Wardrobe care routines

A few minutes a week keeps a wardrobe in good order:

- hang or fold clothes as soon as you take them off;
- check pockets and fastenings before washing;
- keep a small kit of needle, thread, spare buttons and a lint roller handy;
- set aside anything that needs attention in one basket, and deal with it once a week.

## Ironing and steaming

- **Iron at the right temperature** for the fabric, starting with the coolest.
- **Iron dark fabrics inside out** to avoid shine.
- **Use a pressing cloth** on wool and delicate fabrics.
- **A handheld steamer** refreshes dresses, blouses and jackets gently, without flattening the fabric.

## Pilling, bobbles and fuzz

Pilling happens when short fibres rub together and form little balls. It is common on knitwear, especially under the arms and on the sides.

- **Remove pills gently** with a fabric comb, a sweater stone or an electric fabric shaver, working lightly.
- **Wash knitwear inside out**, on a gentle cycle or by hand.
- **Avoid rubbing**, for example from shoulder bags or seatbelts, where possible.

## Stains: act quickly

- **Blot, don't rub**, with a clean cloth.
- **Rinse from the back** of the fabric with cold water.
- **Test any stain remover** on a hidden area first.
- **Do not put heat on a stain** until it is gone; hot water and dryers can set some stains.
- **Take delicate or valuable items** to a professional cleaner, and tell them what the stain is.

## Protecting knitwear from moths

Clothes moths are attracted to natural fibres such as wool, cashmere and silk, especially when they are stored dirty.

- **Clean before storing**: moths are drawn to traces of food and body oils.
- **Store in sealed bags or boxes**, especially out of season.
- **Use cedar blocks or lavender sachets**, which some people find helpful as deterrents, refreshed regularly.
- **Check stored knitwear** every few weeks, and vacuum the corners of wardrobes and drawers.
- **If you find moths**, wash or professionally clean affected items, clean the storage area thoroughly and consider freezing small items in sealed bags, a method some people use to kill eggs.

## Storing clothes well

### Hangers

- **Use shaped wooden or padded hangers** for jackets and coats, so the shoulders keep their shape.
- **Use velvet or padded hangers** for slippery fabrics.
- **Avoid thin wire hangers**, which distort shoulders.

### Fold, don't hang, knitwear

Heavy jumpers stretch on hangers. Fold them and store them on shelves or in drawers.

### Seasonal rotation

Storing out-of-season clothes makes the wardrobe easier to use and protects the stored items:

1. clean everything before storing;
2. repair anything that needs it;
3. store in breathable cotton bags or sealed boxes in a cool, dry, dark place;
4. label the boxes.

At the change of season, it is a good moment to review what you have. Our guides to [editing your wardrobe](/journal/editing-your-wardrobe/) and [building a capsule wardrobe](/journal/capsule-wardrobe-after-fifty/) can help.

### Shoes and bags

- **Use shoe trees** or stuff shoes with paper to keep their shape.
- **Clean and condition leather** regularly.
- **Store bags stuffed** with paper, in dust bags, away from heat.

## Small repairs that make a big difference

Many garments are discarded for a missing button, a loose hem or a small hole. Learning a few basic repairs, or finding a good local tailor, extends their life considerably:

- **sew on buttons** as soon as they become loose;
- **repair hems** before they drop further;
- **darn small holes** in knitwear, or have them invisibly mended;
- **replace worn elastic** in waistbands;
- **re-heel and re-sole** good shoes.

## Caring for special fabrics

| Fabric | Key care points |
|---|---|
| Cashmere and fine wool | hand wash or gentle wool cycle, dry flat, store folded and protected from moths |
| Silk | hand wash or professional cleaning, no wringing, iron on low heat inside out |
| Linen | wash gently, iron while slightly damp, accept a few creases as part of its charm |
| Leather and suede | professional cleaning, protect from rain, condition leather regularly |
| Lace and beading | hand wash or professional cleaning; store flat or carefully hung |

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I really wash cashmere at home?

Many cashmere pieces can be hand washed carefully, following the care label. Use a wool detergent, lukewarm water and dry flat.

### Why do my clothes fade so quickly?

Hot washes, too much detergent, overloading and strong sunlight while drying all speed up fading. Wash darks inside out at lower temperatures and dry them away from direct sun.

### How can I make my jumpers last longer?

Wash them less often, wash gently, dry flat, fold rather than hang, remove pills lightly and protect them from moths.

## The bottom line

Clothes last longest when they are washed less often and more gently, dried carefully and stored well. Read the care label, sort laundry, use lower temperatures and gentle detergents, turn clothes inside out and hand wash delicate pieces. Air dry and dry knitwear flat, remove pilling lightly, tackle stains quickly without heat, and protect natural fibres from moths by storing them clean and sealed. Use good hangers, fold knitwear, rotate seasonal clothes and fix small problems early. Your favourite pieces will thank you by looking good for years.
