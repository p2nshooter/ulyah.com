For many women, the years after fifty bring a new accessory that sits right in the middle of the face: glasses. Some have worn them all their lives; others start with a pair of reading glasses and gradually find themselves wearing them most of the day. Glasses change how the face looks more than almost any other item. The right frames can lift the face, bring out the eyes and become part of a signature style. The wrong ones can drag features down, hide the brows or make eye makeup disappear.

This guide covers how to choose frames that flatter your face shape, colouring and brows, how lenses affect the way your eyes look, how to adapt eye makeup, concealer and lipstick to work with glasses, and some practical tips for reading glasses and varifocals. For your eyesight itself, regular eye examinations with an optometrist or eye doctor are the most important step of all.

## Start with a proper eye test

Before thinking about style, make sure your prescription is up to date. Eyes change with age, and many eye conditions develop without obvious symptoms in their early stages. Regular eye examinations check not only your prescription but also the health of your eyes. Ask your optometrist how often you should have them, and seek advice promptly if you notice sudden changes in vision, flashes, new floaters or eye pain.

## Choosing frames that flatter

### Follow the line of your brows

Your brows and frames work as a pair. As a general guide:

- the **top of the frame** should follow the natural curve of your brows;
- ideally, the brows should sit **just above the frame** or be partly visible through it, rather than hidden behind it;
- frames that sit **too low** can make the eyes look tired; frames that sit too high can look surprised.

If your brows have thinned, choose frames with a little more weight along the top edge, which can give back some of the definition. Our guide to [eyebrows and lashes after fifty](/journal/eyebrows-and-lashes-after-fifty/) explains how to fill and shape brows.

### Consider your face shape

| Face shape | Often flattered by |
|---|---|
| Round | angular or rectangular frames that add structure |
| Square | rounder or oval frames that soften the jaw |
| Oval | most shapes; keep the proportion in balance with your features |
| Heart-shaped | frames that are slightly wider at the bottom, or lighter on top |
| Long | deeper frames, or frames with detail at the sides, that shorten the face |

These are starting points, not rules. Personality and comfort matter just as much.

### Lift the face

A few frame features tend to give a lifted, bright look:

- **frames that angle slightly upwards** at the outer corners, such as soft cat-eye shapes;
- **detail or colour at the top corners**;
- **a frame width** that matches the widest part of your face, so the frames do not look too narrow or too wide.

### Colour

Frame colour can flatter or fight with your colouring.

- With **silver or white hair**, soft tortoiseshell, burgundy, navy, deep green, slate grey or even bright colours can look striking. Very harsh black can sometimes look heavy.
- With **warm colouring**, honey, amber, warm tortoiseshell and gold tend to flatter.
- With **cool colouring**, silver, grey, blue, plum and cool tortoiseshell often suit.

Our guide to [finding your best colours](/journal/finding-your-best-colours/) explains how to identify your undertone.

### Comfort matters

- the frames should **sit evenly** on your nose and ears without pinching;
- they should **not slide down** when you look down;
- **lightweight materials** make a big difference if you wear glasses all day;
- **adjustable nose pads** can help with comfort and positioning.

A good optician can adjust frames so they sit correctly.

## How lenses change your eyes

- **Lenses for long-sightedness**, including many reading glasses, tend to **magnify** the eyes. This makes eye makeup more visible, so precision matters.
- **Lenses for short-sightedness** tend to make the eyes look **smaller**. A little more definition can help them stand out.
- **Anti-reflective coatings** reduce reflections, so others can see your eyes, and your makeup, more clearly. They also make glasses more comfortable in many situations.

## Adapting your eye makeup

### If your lenses magnify

- **keep eyeshadow neat and well blended**, because every edge will show;
- **choose matt or satin textures** rather than heavy shimmer, which can look emphasised;
- **use a fine line** of eyeliner close to the lashes rather than a thick one;
- **separate lashes** carefully, as clumps will be magnified.

### If your lenses make eyes look smaller

- **define the lash line** with eyeliner or a dark eyeshadow;
- **use a slightly deeper shade** in the crease to add dimension;
- **curl the lashes** and apply lengthening mascara;
- **brighten the inner corners** with a soft, light eyeshadow.

### For everyone

- **Keep it light around the lower lashes**, where frames can catch smudges.
- **Choose smudge-resistant mascara**, as lashes can brush against the lenses.
- **Wear lighter colours on the lid** if your frames are bold, so the frames and makeup do not compete.

## Concealer and the frame shadow

Frames can cast a slight shadow under the eyes, and their weight can leave marks on the nose.

- **Use a light, hydrating concealer** under the eyes, applied in a thin layer.
- **Set lightly** with a fine powder only where needed, so it does not settle into lines.
- **Check the bridge of the nose and the sides**, where frames sit, and apply a little concealer if there are red marks.

## Lipstick and balance

Glasses draw attention to the upper part of the face. A defined lip colour can balance them beautifully:

- with **bold or dark frames**, a lipstick with some colour, such as a rose, berry or warm red, balances the face;
- with **delicate or rimless frames**, a softer lip shade keeps the look harmonious.

## Reading glasses and varifocals

### Reading glasses

Many women keep several pairs of reading glasses around the house. If you wear them often:

- **choose at least one pair you love**, rather than only the cheapest ones;
- **keep a stylish case** in your bag, so you are not hunting for them;
- **consider a chain or cord** if you constantly take them on and off;
- **ask your optometrist** whether ready-made readers are suitable for you, or whether prescription lenses would be better, especially if your eyes differ from each other.

### Varifocals and multifocals

Varifocal lenses allow you to see at different distances through one pair of glasses. They take some getting used to:

- **frames need to be deep enough** to fit the different zones of the lens;
- **ask the optician** to take precise measurements;
- **give yourself time to adapt**, especially on stairs and kerbs, and ask for advice if you still struggle after a few weeks.

## Sunglasses

Sunglasses protect the eyes and the delicate skin around them. Look for lenses that offer **UV protection** according to recognised standards in your country, and consider prescription sunglasses or clip-ons if you need them. Large frames also protect more of the skin around the eyes. Our guide to [everyday sun protection](/journal/everyday-sun-protection/) explains why this matters.

## Caring for your glasses

Clean, well-adjusted glasses look better and are kinder to your makeup:

- **clean lenses** with a microfibre cloth and a lens spray or a drop of mild washing-up liquid under lukewarm water, then dry gently; avoid paper tissues and clothing, which can scratch coatings;
- **keep them in a hard case** when you are not wearing them;
- **take them off with both hands**, which keeps the frame straight;
- **ask your optician to adjust them** if they slip, pinch or sit unevenly, which many opticians do at little or no cost;
- **wipe the nose pads and the inside of the frame** regularly, where foundation and skincare build up.

## Building a small glasses wardrobe

If your budget allows, two or three pairs can change your look as easily as a scarf or a lipstick:

- an **everyday pair** in a flattering, versatile colour;
- a **bolder pair** for evenings or when you want a statement;
- **sunglasses**, with your prescription if needed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should my glasses match my hair colour?

Not necessarily. They should complement your skin tone, eye colour and hair. Contrast can be very flattering, especially with silver hair.

### Can I wear eyeliner with reading glasses?

Yes. Keep it fine and close to the lash line, because reading lenses often magnify the eyes.

### How often should I replace my frames?

When they no longer fit well, are damaged, or no longer suit your face or prescription. Your optometrist will tell you when your prescription needs updating.

## The bottom line

Glasses and makeup work best when they are chosen together. Start with an up-to-date eye examination, then choose frames that follow your brows, suit your face shape and colouring, and feel comfortable all day. Adjust your eye makeup to your lenses: neater and finer if they magnify, more defined if they make eyes look smaller. Use a light concealer for frame shadows, balance bold frames with a little lip colour, give yourself time to adapt to varifocals, and protect your eyes with good sunglasses.
