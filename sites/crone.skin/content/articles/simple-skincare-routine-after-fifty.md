Walk into any beauty hall and you might believe that skin after fifty needs a twelve-step routine, a drawer full of serums and a small fortune. The truth is far simpler and far kinder. Healthy, comfortable, glowing skin in later life depends on a handful of basics done consistently: cleansing gently, keeping skin moisturised, protecting it from the sun, and adding one or two well-chosen extras if you enjoy them.

This guide sets out a straightforward routine, explains why each step matters, and helps you decide where to spend and where to save. It is general information, not medical advice. If you have a skin condition, persistent irritation or any changes that concern you, ask a pharmacist, doctor or dermatologist.

## How skin changes after fifty

Understanding what is happening to your skin makes it easier to choose the right products.

- **Less oil and more dryness.** Oil production generally decreases with age, and around and after menopause many women notice their skin becomes drier, tighter and more easily irritated.
- **Thinner skin.** The skin's supporting proteins, such as collagen, decline over time, so skin becomes thinner and less firm.
- **Slower renewal.** Skin cells turn over more slowly, which can make the complexion look duller.
- **Sun damage shows.** Years of sun exposure can appear as pigmentation, uneven tone, fine lines and rough patches.
- **More sensitivity.** Mature skin can react more readily to fragrance, harsh cleansers and strong active ingredients.

The aim of a good routine is not to fight these changes but to support the skin: keep its protective barrier healthy, prevent further sun damage and keep it comfortable.

## The five steps

### 1. Cleanse gently

Cleansing removes dirt, sunscreen, makeup and pollution, but harsh cleansing strips the skin of its natural oils and can leave it dry and irritated.

- **Choose a gentle, non-foaming or low-foaming cleanser**, such as a cream, milk, balm or gentle gel cleanser.
- **Use lukewarm water**, not hot, which can dry skin.
- **Cleanse thoroughly in the evening** to remove the day's makeup and sunscreen. A cleansing balm or oil followed by a gentle second cleanse works well if you wear makeup.
- **In the morning, a splash of water or a quick, gentle cleanse** is often enough, especially for dry skin.
- **Pat dry with a soft towel**; avoid rubbing.

Avoid harsh scrubs with rough particles, which can irritate and damage delicate skin.

### 2. Treat (optional)

This is the step where serums and treatments come in. It is entirely optional, but a few ingredients have good evidence behind them and can make a difference:

- **Retinoids** (vitamin A derivatives, including retinol and stronger prescription forms) can help improve the appearance of fine lines, texture and uneven tone over time. They can cause dryness, peeling and irritation at first, so start slowly — perhaps two or three evenings a week — with a low strength, and always use sunscreen during the day, as they can increase sun sensitivity. Prescription-strength retinoids require advice from a doctor or pharmacist. Do not use retinoids if your doctor has advised against them.
- **Vitamin C** serums, used in the morning, can help brighten the complexion and offer some antioxidant protection. Some formulations can sting sensitive skin.
- **Niacinamide** is generally well tolerated and may help with redness, uneven tone and the skin barrier.
- **Hyaluronic acid** attracts and holds water, helping skin feel plumper and more hydrated. Apply it to damp skin and follow with moisturiser.
- **Gentle exfoliating acids** such as lactic acid or low-strength glycolic acid can help brighten dull skin, but use them sparingly — once or twice a week at most — and never at the same time as a retinoid unless advised.

You do not need all of these. One or two, chosen for your main concerns, is plenty. Introduce one new product at a time, so you can tell what suits your skin.

### 3. Moisturise

Moisturiser is essential for most mature skin. It helps prevent water loss, protects the skin barrier and makes skin feel soft and comfortable.

- **Look for ingredients that support the barrier**, such as ceramides, glycerin, shea butter, squalane and fatty acids.
- **Choose a richer cream** if your skin is dry, and a lighter lotion if it is combination or oily.
- **Use a richer night cream** if you like, as skin may feel drier overnight.
- **Do not forget your neck and the backs of your hands**, which show sun damage and dryness too.
- **Fragrance-free products** are often a good choice for sensitive skin.

Expensive moisturisers are not necessarily better. Many affordable pharmacy brands contain excellent barrier-supporting ingredients.

### 4. Protect with sunscreen every day

If you do only one thing for your skin, make it daily sun protection. Ultraviolet (UV) radiation is a major cause of visible skin ageing — pigmentation, fine lines, loss of firmness — and of skin cancer. UV reaches us on cloudy days and through windows, so daily protection matters all year round, not just on holiday.

- **Choose a broad-spectrum sunscreen**, which protects against both UVA and UVB rays.
- **Use an adequate SPF**; many health organisations recommend at least SPF 30 for everyday use.
- **Apply generously** to the face, neck, ears and the backs of the hands. Most people apply far less than the amount used in testing.
- **Reapply** if you are outdoors for long periods, sweating or swimming.
- **Combine with shade, hats and sunglasses** in strong sunshine.

If you dislike the feel of sunscreen, try different textures: fluids, gels and tinted sunscreens can feel lighter than traditional creams. Some moisturisers contain SPF, but check that you are applying enough to reach the stated protection.

### 5. Look after your lips and eyes

- **Lips**: use a nourishing balm daily and one with SPF when outdoors. Lips have little natural protection and dry easily.
- **Eyes**: the skin around the eyes is thin. An eye cream is optional — a gentle moisturiser often works just as well — but apply it with a light, patting touch. Sunglasses help protect the delicate skin around the eyes and reduce squinting.

## Putting it together

### Morning routine

1. Splash with water or gently cleanse.
2. Optional: vitamin C or another serum.
3. Moisturiser.
4. Broad-spectrum sunscreen.
5. Lip balm with SPF.

### Evening routine

1. Remove makeup and sunscreen with a gentle cleanser.
2. Optional: retinoid (a few nights a week) or hydrating serum.
3. Moisturiser or night cream.
4. Lip balm.

That is all most skin needs.

## Weekly extras

- **A gentle exfoliation** once a week, using a mild acid or a soft cloth, can brighten skin. Skip it if you use retinoids or have sensitive skin.
- **A hydrating mask** can be a pleasant, soothing treat.
- **A facial massage** while applying moisturiser can feel relaxing and help you check your skin for changes.

## Beyond products: lifestyle and skin

Skin reflects how you live as well as what you apply:

- **Do not smoke.** Smoking significantly accelerates skin ageing and harms overall health. If you smoke, stopping at any age helps.
- **Sleep well.** Poor sleep shows on the skin.
- **Eat a varied diet** with plenty of fruit, vegetables, healthy fats and protein.
- **Drink enough fluids.**
- **Keep active.** Exercise boosts circulation and wellbeing.
- **Manage stress.** Stress can worsen some skin conditions.
- **Use a humidifier** in dry, heated rooms if your skin feels tight in winter.

## Adjusting with the seasons

Skin's needs change through the year. In winter, cold air outside and central heating inside can leave skin drier and more sensitive, so a richer moisturiser, a gentler cleanser and a nourishing night balm often help. In summer, a lighter moisturiser may be enough, but sun protection becomes even more important, and you may need to reapply sunscreen more often. Adjust your routine gradually and listen to how your skin feels.

## Common mistakes

- **Using too many products at once**, which can cause irritation and makes it hard to know what works.
- **Over-exfoliating**, especially combining scrubs, acids and retinoids.
- **Skipping sunscreen** on cloudy days or in winter.
- **Washing with hot water.**
- **Expecting instant results.** Most treatments take weeks or months to show benefits.
- **Stopping a product too early** — or continuing one that clearly irritates your skin.

## Spending wisely

Spend where it makes a difference — on a sunscreen you love wearing daily and, if you wish, a proven treatment such as a retinoid. Save on cleansers and moisturisers, where affordable products perform very well. Ignore claims that a cream can "erase" wrinkles or match the effect of medical procedures. Read ingredient lists rather than marketing slogans.

## When to see a professional

See a pharmacist, doctor or dermatologist if you notice:

- a mole or spot that changes in size, shape or colour, bleeds, itches or does not heal;
- persistent redness, flushing or acne-like breakouts;
- severe dryness, itching or rashes;
- skin reactions to products.

Regularly checking your skin, including areas you cannot easily see, is a good habit. Ask a partner or friend to help, or use a mirror.

## The bottom line

Skincare after fifty does not need to be complicated. Cleanse gently, moisturise well, protect your skin from the sun every single day, and add one or two proven treatments if you want them. Look after your overall health, be patient, and enjoy the ritual. Comfortable, healthy, well-cared-for skin is beautiful at every age.
