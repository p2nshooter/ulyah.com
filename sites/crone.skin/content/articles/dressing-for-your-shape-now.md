Most women's bodies change over the years. The waist may thicken, weight may settle around the tummy, the bust may change shape, arms may feel softer, and posture may shift slightly. Clothes that once fitted perfectly start to pull, gape or cling in new places. It is entirely normal — and yet it can leave us feeling that fashion has stopped catering for us.

This guide is not about hiding your body or following strict rules about what women of a certain age "should" wear. It is about understanding fit and proportion so that clothes work with the body you have now. When clothes fit well, they feel comfortable, look polished and let your personality shine.

## How bodies commonly change

Changes vary greatly from person to person, but common ones include:

- **A thicker waist** and weight gain around the middle, often linked to hormonal changes around menopause.
- **Changes in bust size and shape.**
- **A softer upper arm.**
- **Flatter or wider hips.**
- **Changes in posture**, such as rounded shoulders.
- **A change in overall size**, either up or down.

It helps to look at your body with fresh, kind eyes, rather than comparing it with a younger version of yourself.

## Start with fit

Fit is the foundation of good style. Even the most beautiful garment looks wrong if it fits poorly, and simple clothes look elegant when they fit well.

### Know your current size

Sizes vary between brands and change over time. Take your measurements — bust, waist, hips and inside leg — and compare them with size charts. Do not be attached to a number on a label; buy what fits.

### Signs of a good fit

- **Shoulders**: seams sit at the edge of the shoulder.
- **Bust**: no pulling across buttons, no gaping.
- **Waist**: comfortable, without digging in or bunching.
- **Hips and seat**: fabric skims without straining.
- **Length**: sleeves and hems are in proportion.
- **Movement**: you can sit, bend and raise your arms comfortably.

### Use alterations

Few women fit perfectly into off-the-peg clothes. Taking up hems, adjusting waistbands, shortening sleeves or taking in a jacket can transform how a garment looks. Our guide to tailoring and alterations explains what is possible.

## Understanding proportion

Proportion is the relationship between different parts of an outfit. A few principles help create balance:

- **Balance volume.** Pair a loose, flowing top with slimmer trousers, or a fitted top with wide-leg trousers or a full skirt.
- **Create a defined point.** Defining the narrowest part of your torso — whether the natural waist, under the bust, or the hips — creates shape.
- **Use vertical lines.** Long cardigans, open jackets, V-necks, long necklaces and column dressing (wearing one colour from top to toe) create a lengthening line.
- **Think about where hems fall.** Hems that end at the narrowest part of the leg (just below the knee, at the calf or at the ankle) are often flattering.

## Styling ideas by area

These ideas are suggestions, not rules. Choose what makes you feel comfortable and confident.

### A thicker waist or tummy

- **Choose fabrics that skim** rather than cling, such as soft jersey, crepe, viscose and drapey knits.
- **Try wrap tops and wrap dresses**, which create a V-neck and a gentle waist definition.
- **Look for ruching or gathering** across the middle, which disguises soft areas.
- **Choose mid-rise or high-rise trousers** with a flat front or a comfortable, wide waistband.
- **Untucked longer tops**, tunics and shirts that fall below the hip can be flattering, particularly over slim trousers.
- **Try the "half tuck"**: tucking just the front of a top into trousers creates shape without emphasising the tummy.
- **Wear open layers**: a jacket or cardigan worn open creates vertical lines.
- **Avoid tight belts at the narrowest point** if they dig in; a soft belt worn slightly lower, or a belted jacket, may be more comfortable.

### Arms

- **Three-quarter, elbow-length and flutter sleeves** are flattering and comfortable.
- **Choose sleeves that are not tight** around the upper arm.
- **Use light layers**, such as a sheer kimono, a fine cardigan or a shawl, if you prefer to cover your arms in summer.
- **Do not feel obliged to cover up.** Many women love sleeveless tops in hot weather, and that is entirely their choice.

### Bust

- **Get a professional bra fitting.** A well-fitting bra changes the way every top looks. Bust size and shape often change after menopause, and many women wear the wrong size.
- **For a larger bust**, V-necks, scoop necks and wrap styles are often flattering. Avoid high, tight necklines and boxy tops that can make the upper body look larger. Shirts with hidden button plackets or stretch help prevent gaping.
- **For a smaller bust**, details such as ruffles, pockets, pleats and textured fabrics add interest. Higher necklines often work well.

### Hips and thighs

- **A-line skirts** and **wide-leg or straight-leg trousers** skim hips and thighs.
- **Darker colours on the lower half** and brighter colours or details on top draw the eye upwards.
- **Jackets that end at or just below the hip bone** often balance proportions.

### Legs

- **Cropped or ankle-length trousers** show the narrowest part of the leg.
- **Midi skirts and dresses** that end below the knee are versatile and comfortable.
- **Tonal tights and shoes** in the same colour lengthen the leg.
- **Wide-leg trousers with a slight heel** or platform create a long line.

### Shoulders and posture

- **Structured jackets with soft shoulder shaping** can square up rounded shoulders.
- **Good posture** helps every outfit. Gentle exercises to strengthen the back and open the chest can help; see our guides to strength and flexibility.

## Fabrics that flatter

- **Matte fabrics** are often more forgiving than shiny ones, which reflect light and highlight curves.
- **Fabrics with drape**, such as crepe, viscose, silk and fine knits, move with the body.
- **Structured fabrics**, such as ponte, wool and cotton twill, hold their shape and smooth lines.
- **Avoid very thin, clingy jerseys** that show every line.
- **Look for a little stretch** in trousers and jeans for comfort.

## Underwear: the foundation

Good underwear makes clothes look better:

- **Get measured for a bra** every year or two.
- **Choose smooth, seamless styles** under fitted clothes.
- **Consider gentle shaping underwear** if you like the smoother line it creates, but choose comfortable styles; underwear that is too tight can be uncomfortable and affect circulation.
- **Wear skin-toned underwear** under light-coloured clothes.

## Necklines

Necklines frame the face and affect the overall silhouette:

- **V-necks** lengthen the neck and flatter most shapes.
- **Scoop necks** soften the look.
- **Cowl necks** add softness and volume.
- **Boat necks** widen the shoulders and suit narrower frames.
- **Collared shirts** look crisp and polished.

## Jeans and trousers that work

Finding trousers that fit is one of the most common frustrations. A few tips make the search easier:

- **Try several cuts**: straight-leg, wide-leg, bootcut and slim styles all suit different shapes, and the "right" one may not be what you wore before.
- **Look at the rise**: mid- and high-rise trousers sit more comfortably on many midlife bodies than low-rise styles and avoid the waistband cutting in when you sit.
- **Check the back view**: pockets placed well, at a moderate size, flatter the seat; a gaping waist at the back can be fixed by a tailor.
- **Choose darker washes** of denim for a polished look; they also work for more occasions.
- **Look for comfort waistbands**: wide, flat elasticated or partly elasticated waistbands now come in smart styles that look tailored from the front.
- **Hem for your shoes**: have trousers shortened to suit the shoes you wear most with them.

## Dresses for every day

Dresses are wonderfully easy to wear: one piece, and the outfit is done. Wrap dresses, shirt dresses, softly fitted jersey dresses and A-line styles suit many shapes. Add a cardigan, jacket or belt to change the silhouette, and pair with trainers by day or smarter shoes in the evening. If you prefer not to show your legs, wear opaque tights, leggings or wide-leg trousers underneath a longer dress or tunic.

## Building confidence

Dressing for your shape is as much about mindset as it is about technique:

- **Focus on what you like** about your body and highlight it — beautiful collarbones, elegant hands, great legs, lovely eyes.
- **Wear clothes that make you feel good**, regardless of rules.
- **Keep comfort central.** You will not feel confident in clothes that pinch or restrict.
- **Experiment.** Try styles you have not worn before; you might be surprised.
- **Be kind to yourself.** Your body has carried you through decades of life.

## A quick checklist when trying on clothes

1. Does it fit at the shoulders, bust, waist and hips?
2. Can I move, sit and bend comfortably?
3. Is the fabric comfortable and flattering?
4. Is the length right for my proportions?
5. Does it work with at least three other items in my wardrobe?
6. Does the colour suit me?
7. Do I feel like myself in it?

## The bottom line

Dressing well after fifty is not about hiding but about harmony. Prioritise fit, use proportion to create balance, choose fabrics that skim and drape, invest in good underwear, and highlight the features you love. Most of all, wear what makes you feel comfortable and confident — because a woman who feels good in her clothes always looks her best.
