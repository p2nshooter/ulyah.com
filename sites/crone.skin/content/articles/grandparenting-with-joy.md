Becoming a grandparent is, for many women, one of the great joys of later life. There is the wonder of holding a new baby, the delight of a toddler's first words, the fun of shared adventures, and the quiet pride of watching a child grow into a young adult. Many grandmothers describe the relationship as uniquely special: all the love of parenthood, with a little more time, patience and perspective.

But grandparenting today can also bring challenges. Families may live far apart, parenting styles have changed, expectations about childcare vary, and family relationships can be complicated by divorce, blended families or different cultural traditions. This guide offers ideas for building close, joyful bonds with grandchildren while supporting their parents and looking after yourself.

## The grandparent role today

Grandparents play many roles: loving companion, storyteller, keeper of family history, childcare provider, emergency support, mentor, playmate and safe harbour. Some grandmothers provide regular childcare; others see grandchildren occasionally because of distance or circumstance. There is no single "right" way to be a grandparent. What matters most is love, consistency and respect.

## Supporting the parents

The relationship with your grandchildren is closely tied to your relationship with their parents — your son or daughter and their partner. Supporting the parents well is one of the best things you can do for your grandchildren.

### Respect their decisions

Parenting advice has changed a great deal over the decades, from sleep safety to feeding, screen time and discipline. Even when you disagree, the parents are in charge. Follow their guidelines about routines, food, bedtimes, screen use and rules. If you are unsure, ask.

### Offer help, not instructions

- **Ask what would help**, rather than assuming.
- **Offer practical support**: cooking meals, doing laundry, taking older children out so the parents can rest, especially in the early weeks with a new baby.
- **Give advice only when asked**, and gently.
- **Praise the parents.** New parents often feel uncertain; your encouragement means a lot.

### Keep communication open

- **Talk about expectations**: how often you will visit, whether you will provide childcare, how holidays will be shared.
- **Be honest about your limits**, as well as your wishes.
- **Address misunderstandings calmly** and early.

### Be mindful with in-laws and other grandparents

Grandchildren often have several sets of grandparents. Avoid competing for time or affection. Cooperation and goodwill make family gatherings and holidays much easier for everyone.

## Building bonds at every age

### Babies and toddlers

- **Spend unhurried time** together: cuddling, singing, reading and playing.
- **Learn current safety guidelines** for sleeping, car seats and feeding — they may differ from when you were a parent.
- **Establish familiar routines** when you look after them.
- **Talk and sing** constantly; babies love voices.

### Young children

- **Play together**: building, imaginative play, games, crafts.
- **Read stories** and share favourite books from your own childhood.
- **Explore outdoors**: parks, nature walks, puddle jumping, gardening (see our guide to a low-maintenance garden and container gardening for child-friendly growing ideas).
- **Bake and cook** simple recipes together.
- **Create traditions**: a special outing, a sleepover, a weekly phone call or a favourite treat.

### Older children

- **Share hobbies**: teach them to sew, knit, fish, garden, play cards or chess, or let them teach you their interests.
- **Take them on outings**: museums, theatres, sports events, days out.
- **Listen**: older children often value an adult who listens without judging.
- **Help with homework or reading**, if the parents welcome it.

### Teenagers

- **Respect their growing independence.**
- **Stay in touch** through their preferred channels — messaging, video calls or social media, within the parents' guidelines.
- **Be a trusted confidant**, while respecting the parents' role.
- **Share experiences**: a meal out, a shopping trip, a concert or a walk.
- **Show interest** in their passions, friends and plans.

### Adult grandchildren

Relationships can deepen into friendship as grandchildren become adults. Share family stories, offer wisdom when asked, and enjoy their company as individuals.

## Staying close across distance

Many grandparents live far from their grandchildren, sometimes in different countries. Distance does not have to weaken bonds.

- **Video calls**: regular calls let children see your face and share their news. With young children, short, frequent calls work better than long ones. Read a story, show them your garden, or play a simple game over video.
- **Letters and postcards**: children love receiving post. Send small parcels, drawings or pressed flowers.
- **Shared activities at a distance**: read the same book, watch the same film, cook the same recipe and compare results.
- **Recordings**: record yourself reading bedtime stories for them to play at home.
- **Visits**: plan regular visits, perhaps for birthdays or holidays, and consider having grandchildren to stay as they grow older.
- **Photo books**: create albums of time spent together.

## Providing childcare

Many grandparents provide regular childcare, which can be rewarding and a great help to families. It can also be tiring and, at times, complicated.

### Before agreeing

- **Be realistic** about your energy, health and other commitments.
- **Agree on days and hours**, and how flexible the arrangement will be.
- **Discuss rules and routines**: meals, naps, screen time, discipline.
- **Discuss practical matters**: transport, equipment, costs and who pays for outings and food.
- **Plan for holidays and illness**, both yours and the children's.

### During childcare

- **Follow the parents' routines** as closely as possible.
- **Keep the home safe**: check for hazards such as medicines, cleaning products, small objects, hot drinks, blind cords and unsecured furniture. Safety guidance may have changed since you raised your own children.
- **Make sure you know what to do in an emergency**, including the children's medical information and parents' contact details. A paediatric first aid course is worthwhile.
- **Communicate**: share news of the day with the parents.

### Look after yourself

Childcare can be physically demanding. Take breaks, accept help, and be honest if the arrangement becomes too much. Your own health and wellbeing matter too.

## Keeping family history alive

Grandparents are often the keepers of family stories, traditions and history:

- **Tell stories** about your childhood, the children's parents when they were young, and earlier generations.
- **Share photographs** and family heirlooms, explaining their significance.
- **Cook family recipes** together.
- **Create a family tree** together.
- **Write or record your memories** (see our guide to writing your life story).

Children love hearing stories about their parents' childhood mischief.

## Navigating difficult situations

Family life is not always easy.

- **Divorce or separation**: stay neutral, keep loving the grandchildren, and maintain respectful contact with both parents where possible.
- **Blended families**: welcome step-grandchildren warmly and treat children fairly.
- **Disagreements with parents**: try to resolve conflicts calmly, focusing on the children's wellbeing. Family mediation may help in serious cases.
- **Estrangement**: being cut off from grandchildren is deeply painful. Support groups and counselling can help. In some places, legal routes exist for grandparents to seek contact, but they are often complex; seek specialist advice.
- **Caring for grandchildren full-time**: some grandparents become full-time carers for grandchildren. Support, advice and sometimes financial help may be available; contact specialist organisations for guidance.

## Gifts and treats

Grandparents love to give, but more is not always better.

- **Ask the parents** before buying large gifts.
- **Consider experiences**: a day out, a class, a trip to the theatre.
- **Give your time**: an afternoon together is often more memorable than a toy.
- **Contribute to savings** for education or future goals, if you wish and can afford it.

A small, thoughtful present chosen together — a book, a seed packet, a craft kit — often means more than an expensive toy.

## Grandparenting in the digital age

Technology can be a wonderful bridge between generations, and grandchildren are often delighted to teach grandparents how to use it. Shared photo albums let you see everyday moments as they happen; family group chats keep everyone in touch; and video calls make it possible to read a bedtime story from hundreds of miles away. At the same time, follow the parents' wishes about photographs of the children online: many families prefer not to have their children's pictures shared publicly on social media, and it is worth asking before you post anything. Respect agreed rules on screen time during visits too, and offer screen-free alternatives — cards, board games, baking and outdoor play — that children often remember most fondly.

## When you become a grandmother for the first time

The arrival of a first grandchild can bring a surprising mix of emotions: joy and pride, of course, but sometimes also a sense of your own life moving into a new stage, memories of your own early parenting, or worry about how the new parents are coping. It helps to talk about these feelings with friends who have been through the same transition. In the early weeks, the most useful gifts are often practical: a cooked meal, a tidy kitchen, a walk with the pram so the parents can sleep. Let the new parents set the pace for visits, and remember that holding back from advice is itself a gift of trust.

## Looking after yourself

Being an active grandparent is easier when you are healthy, rested and fulfilled in your own life:

- **Stay active** to keep up with lively grandchildren (see our guides to walking and strength training).
- **Maintain your own friendships and interests.**
- **Set boundaries** around time and energy.
- **Accept that you cannot do everything.**

## The bottom line

Grandparenting can be one of life's greatest joys. Support the parents with respect and generosity, build bonds through time, play, stories and listening, stay close across distance with creativity, and set realistic boundaries for childcare. Keep family history alive, navigate difficulties with patience, and look after your own wellbeing. The love you share with your grandchildren will be part of their lives — and yours — forever.
