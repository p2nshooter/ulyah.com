Imagine spending three weeks in a cottage in the countryside, a flat overlooking a harbour or a house with a garden in a city you have always wanted to know, with a kitchen to cook in, a sofa to read on and neighbours to greet, without paying for a hotel. For a growing number of women after fifty, house swapping and house sitting make this possible. Both approaches turn travel into something slower and more local: instead of passing through, you live somewhere for a while. They suit people with flexible time, a love of homes and gardens, and a sense of adventure.

This guide explains how home exchanges and house sitting work, their advantages and drawbacks, how to find reputable platforms and build trust, how to prepare your own home or care for someone else's, the agreements and insurance to consider, and how to stay safe. It is general guidance; always read the terms of any platform carefully and check insurance and legal questions with the relevant providers.

## Two different ideas

### Home exchange

In a home exchange, you **swap homes** with another household. They stay in your home while you stay in theirs, either at the same time or on different dates. Some platforms use a points system, so you can stay in someone's home and they can stay in someone else's later.

### House sitting

In house sitting, you **stay in someone's home while they are away**, usually caring for their pets, garden or property in return for free accommodation. You do not need to offer your own home in return, which makes it an option for people who rent, have downsized or simply prefer not to have guests in their home.

## Advantages

- **Lower accommodation costs**, which can make longer trips affordable.
- **Space and comfort**: a whole home, often with a kitchen, garden and washing machine.
- **Living like a local**: shopping at markets, finding a favourite café, getting to know neighbours.
- **Longer stays**: weeks or even months rather than a few nights.
- **Animal company**, for those who love pets but no longer have their own.
- **Peace of mind** for your own home, in an exchange, when someone is living there.

## Drawbacks to consider

- **Responsibility**: you are caring for someone's home, and possibly their pets and plants.
- **Less flexibility**: dates and plans depend on the other household.
- **Variable standards**: homes may not match hotel standards of cleanliness or comfort.
- **Pets' needs**: caring for animals means you cannot always go out all day or travel spontaneously.
- **Preparation**: getting your own home ready for guests in an exchange takes time.
- **Trust**: you need to be comfortable with strangers in your home, or with staying in theirs.

Being honest about these drawbacks helps you decide which approach, if any, suits you.

## Finding a reputable platform

Most home exchanges and house sits are arranged through online platforms, which usually charge a membership fee. When choosing one:

- **read independent reviews** and ask friends who have used it;
- **check how members are verified**, such as identity checks and reviews from past exchanges;
- **look at the protections offered**, such as guarantees or insurance in case of damage or cancellation, and read exactly what they cover;
- **review the cancellation policies**;
- **see how many listings there are** in the places you want to visit.

## Creating a good profile

Whether you are offering your home or applying for house sits, a clear, honest profile builds trust:

- **write about yourself**: your interests, how you like to travel, why you enjoy home exchange or house sitting;
- **describe your home honestly**, with good photographs, if you are exchanging;
- **mention relevant experience**, such as caring for pets or gardens;
- **gather reviews** after each stay, and leave reviews for others;
- **complete any verification steps** the platform offers.

## Building trust with the other household

Before agreeing to an exchange or sit:

- **exchange messages** and ask questions about the home, the area, the pets and their expectations;
- **have a video call**, which helps both sides feel comfortable;
- **read their reviews** carefully;
- **agree everything in writing**, ideally using the platform's messaging or agreement tools.

Questions worth asking include:

- What exactly is expected: pets, plants, garden, mail, security?
- Are there any rules about guests, smoking or use of the car?
- Who should be contacted in an emergency, such as a neighbour or a vet?
- What happens if plans change?

## Preparing your home for an exchange

If guests will stay in your home:

- **declutter and clean** thoroughly;
- **make space** in wardrobes and drawers;
- **lock away valuables**, private papers and anything you would not want used;
- **write a home guide**: how appliances work, where things are, rubbish collection days, Wi-Fi details, local shops, transport and emergency contacts;
- **tell your neighbours** that guests will be staying;
- **check with your insurer** about whether guests are covered, and whether you need to inform them;
- **check any tenancy or property rules**, if you rent or live in a building with regulations about guests.

## Caring for someone else's home

As a house sitter or exchange guest, treat the home as you would want yours to be treated.

- **Follow the instructions** you have been given, especially for pets, plants and security.
- **Keep the home clean and tidy**, and leave it as you found it, or better.
- **Report any problems promptly**, such as a broken appliance or a sick pet.
- **Respect privacy**: do not look through personal belongings.
- **Keep in touch** as agreed, perhaps with occasional photos of happy pets.

### Pet care

Many house sits involve pets. Before agreeing, make sure:

- you are **comfortable with the animals** involved and their needs;
- you know their **routines, food, medication and vet details**;
- you understand **how much time they need**, as some animals cannot be left alone for long.

## Agreements and insurance

- **Put the agreement in writing**: dates, responsibilities, contacts and any special conditions.
- **Check home insurance** for both homes, and what happens if something is damaged.
- **Check travel insurance**, which you will still need for health and travel problems.
- **Check car insurance** if a car is included in the exchange; many policies have rules about who can drive.
- **Understand the platform's protections**, and their limits.

## Staying safe

- **Use reputable platforms** and keep communication on them where possible.
- **Never send money outside the platform**, and be wary of anyone who asks you to.
- **Verify the address** and the identity of the other household.
- **Share your plans** with family or friends: where you will be and how to contact you.
- **Have a backup plan** in case something goes wrong, such as the name of a nearby hotel.
- **Trust your instincts**: if something feels wrong, it is fine to decline.

## Your first exchange or sit

For a first experience, many people start close to home and for a short time, perhaps a long weekend in a nearby region or a week of house sitting in a town they already know. A short first stay lets you learn how the platform works, what kind of homes and hosts you feel comfortable with, and how you like to prepare. It also helps you collect your first reviews, which make future exchanges and sits much easier to arrange. After one or two short stays, you will have a much clearer idea of whether you enjoy this way of travelling, and you can plan longer and more distant trips with confidence.

It also helps to be flexible about dates and destinations at first. Popular places at popular times attract many requests, while quieter seasons and less famous towns often have more opportunities and very welcoming hosts.

## Making the most of your stay

- **Live slowly**: find a favourite café, walk the same streets, shop at the market.
- **Explore the surroundings** with day trips.
- **Meet neighbours** and local people, who often share the best tips.
- **Keep a journal** of your stay.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is house swapping safe?

Many people exchange homes successfully, especially through reputable platforms with verification and reviews. Clear agreements, good communication and appropriate insurance reduce the risks.

### Do I need to own my home to exchange?

Not always, but if you rent you need to check your tenancy agreement and your landlord's rules. House sitting does not require offering your own home.

### What if something is broken during my stay?

Tell the owner straight away, follow the agreement and any platform guidelines, and offer to help put it right.

## The bottom line

House swapping and house sitting turn travel into living somewhere for a while, often for much less than a hotel. In a home exchange you swap homes; in house sitting you look after a home, and often pets, in return for accommodation. Choose a reputable platform, build an honest profile, communicate clearly, agree everything in writing and check insurance for homes, cars and travel. Prepare your own home carefully if you are hosting, treat other people's homes with respect, keep money on the platform, share your plans with family and enjoy the slower pace of life as a temporary local.
