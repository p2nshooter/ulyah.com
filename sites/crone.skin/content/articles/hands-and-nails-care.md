Our hands work hard for us every day. They cook, garden, type, carry shopping, hold grandchildren and gesture as we talk. They are also exposed to the elements more than almost any other part of the body: sun, wind, cold, hot water and cleaning products. It is no surprise that hands often show the years more than the face, with dryness, thinner skin, prominent veins, sun spots and nails that split or ridge.

The good news is that hands respond beautifully to a little regular care. Moisture, protection and a simple, gentle manicure routine can make them look and feel softer, smoother and well cared for. This guide covers a complete approach for hands and nails after fifty. It is general information; if you notice unusual changes to your skin or nails, ask a pharmacist or doctor.

## How hands and nails change with age

- **Thinner, drier skin.** The skin on the backs of the hands is naturally thin and has fewer oil glands. With age, it loses some of its supporting collagen and fat, making veins and tendons more visible.
- **Sun damage.** Years of sun exposure can lead to age spots (also known as sun spots or solar lentigines), uneven tone and rough patches.
- **Slower nail growth.** Nails typically grow more slowly with age.
- **Ridges.** Vertical ridges running from the cuticle to the tip are very common and usually harmless.
- **Brittleness.** Nails can become drier and more prone to splitting, peeling and breaking.
- **Changes in thickness and colour.** Some nails become thicker or slightly yellowish, particularly toenails.

Most of these changes are normal, but some nail changes can signal health issues or infections (see the section on when to seek advice).

## Daily hand care

### Moisturise often

The single most important habit is moisturising regularly.

- **Keep hand cream by every sink**, in your bag, by the bed and on your desk, so you remember to use it.
- **Apply after every hand wash**, when skin is most likely to dry out.
- **Choose a rich cream** with ingredients such as glycerin, shea butter, urea, ceramides, lanolin or petroleum jelly for very dry hands. Lighter lotions absorb faster during the day.
- **Massage it in thoroughly**, including the backs of the hands, between the fingers and around the nails.

### Protect from the sun

The backs of the hands receive a great deal of sun exposure, and sun damage is a major cause of age spots and thinning skin.

- **Apply broad-spectrum sunscreen** to the backs of your hands every day, just as you would to your face.
- **Reapply after washing** your hands, if you are outdoors.
- **Wear gloves** for long periods outdoors in strong sun, such as gardening or driving long distances.

### Wear gloves for chores

- **Rubber or nitrile gloves** for washing up and cleaning protect against hot water and harsh chemicals. Cotton-lined gloves are more comfortable for longer jobs, and nitrile is an alternative for those sensitive to latex.
- **Gardening gloves** protect against soil, thorns and dryness.
- **Warm gloves in winter** prevent chapping from cold and wind.

### Wash gently

- **Use lukewarm water** rather than hot.
- **Choose a mild, moisturising hand wash.**
- **Pat dry thoroughly**, including between the fingers.

### An overnight treatment

For very dry hands, apply a thick layer of rich hand cream or balm at bedtime and wear soft cotton gloves overnight. By morning, hands feel noticeably softer.

## Caring for your nails

### Keep nails a manageable length

Shorter nails are less likely to snag, split and break, and they look neat and modern. A soft, rounded square or oval shape is flattering and strong.

### File, do not cut, where possible

- **Use a fine-grit glass or crystal file**, which is gentler than coarse emery boards.
- **File in one direction** rather than sawing back and forth, which can cause splitting.
- **If you need to cut nails**, use sharp nail scissors or clippers, then smooth the edges with a file. Nails are softer and less likely to split after a bath or shower.

### Look after cuticles

Cuticles protect the nail bed from infection.

- **Do not cut cuticles**, as this can lead to infection.
- **Soften them** with cuticle oil or cream, then gently push them back with a soft cloth or a wooden stick after bathing.
- **Massage cuticle oil** into the nail and surrounding skin daily, or several times a week. Oils such as jojoba, almond or simple petroleum jelly all help nails stay flexible.

### Strengthen brittle nails

- **Moisturise nails** along with your hands.
- **Limit water exposure** by wearing gloves for chores; repeated wetting and drying makes nails brittle.
- **Use acetone-free nail polish remover**, which is less drying, and limit how often you remove polish.
- **Consider a strengthening base coat**, but avoid formulas that make nails too hard and brittle.
- **Do not use nails as tools** for opening cans or scraping labels.

## The at-home manicure

A weekly or fortnightly manicure keeps hands looking polished and gives you a relaxing ritual.

1. **Remove old polish** with an acetone-free remover.
2. **Shape the nails** with a fine file.
3. **Soak fingertips** in warm water for a few minutes to soften the cuticles.
4. **Gently push back cuticles** with a soft cloth or orange stick.
5. **Exfoliate the hands** with a gentle scrub or a mix of sugar and oil, then rinse.
6. **Massage in hand cream and cuticle oil.**
7. **Wipe the nails** with remover or a little alcohol to remove oils before painting.
8. **Apply a base coat**, two thin coats of colour and a top coat.
9. **Allow to dry fully** — longer than you think.

### Buffing

Gently buffing nails with a soft buffer can smooth ridges and give a natural shine without polish. Do it lightly and only occasionally, as over-buffing thins the nail.

## Choosing flattering polish colours

Nail polish is a quick way to look polished and to express personality.

- **Soft neutrals and sheer pinks** look elegant and make hands appear smooth, and chips are less noticeable.
- **Classic reds and berries** are timeless and glamorous.
- **Muted mauves, taupes and rosy browns** look sophisticated.
- **Bright corals and fuchsias** add joy in summer.
- **Very dark colours** can look striking on short, neat nails but show chips quickly.
- **Very pale, chalky shades** can sometimes emphasise unevenness in skin tone; a sheer, slightly warm nude is often more flattering.

A ridge-filling base coat smooths the nail surface so polish looks glossy and even.

### Gel and acrylic nails

Gel manicures last longer but involve curing under a lamp and removal with acetone, which can dry and thin nails, especially with repeated use. Acrylic extensions can damage the natural nail. If you enjoy these treatments, give your nails breaks between applications, use a reputable salon, and ensure removal is done gently. Applying sunscreen to the hands or wearing fingerless gloves during lamp curing is often recommended.

## Hand massage and exercises

A short hand massage while applying cream feels wonderful and helps you notice changes. Gentle hand exercises — making a fist and slowly opening it, spreading the fingers wide, touching each fingertip to the thumb — can help keep fingers flexible. If you have arthritis or pain in your hands, ask your doctor or a physiotherapist or occupational therapist for exercises suitable for you.

## Age spots

Age spots on the backs of the hands are very common and are usually harmless signs of sun exposure. To reduce their appearance and prevent new ones:

- **Use sunscreen daily** on the backs of your hands.
- **Consider a cream with gentle exfoliating or brightening ingredients**, used as directed.
- **Ask a dermatologist** about treatments if they bother you.

Always have any spot that changes in size, shape or colour, bleeds or itches checked by a doctor, because some skin cancers can look like age spots.

## Rings and jewellery

Rings and bracelets draw the eye to the hands, so a well-chosen piece can be a lovely finishing touch. If your knuckles have become larger, rings may be hard to remove; a jeweller can resize them or fit a hinged shank. Remove rings before applying hand cream, washing up or gardening to keep both the jewellery and your skin in good condition.

## Toenails and feet

Feet deserve care too, especially as it may become harder to reach them.

- **Cut toenails straight across** to help prevent ingrown nails.
- **Moisturise the feet**, avoiding between the toes.
- **Wear comfortable, well-fitting shoes.**
- **See a podiatrist or chiropodist** if toenails become thick, difficult to cut or painful, or if you have diabetes or poor circulation, which require extra foot care.

## When to seek advice

Most nail changes are harmless, but see a pharmacist or doctor if you notice:

- a dark streak or spot under the nail that is new or changing;
- nails that become very thick, discoloured or crumbly, which may be a fungal infection;
- redness, swelling or pain around the nail;
- nails lifting away from the nail bed;
- sudden changes in nail shape, such as clubbing or spooning;
- persistent splitting or brittleness despite good care.

## The bottom line

Hands and nails reflect years of hard work, and they deserve gentle, regular care. Moisturise often, protect your hands from sun, water and cold, keep nails short and smooth, care for your cuticles with oil, and enjoy a weekly manicure ritual with colours that make you smile. With a little attention, your hands can look soft, graceful and beautifully cared for at every age.
