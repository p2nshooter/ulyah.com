Many of us find it easy to stay active in spring and early autumn, when the weather is mild and the days are bright. Then winter arrives with dark evenings, ice and cold winds, or summer brings heat that makes even a short walk feel exhausting, and our good habits quietly fade. By the time the weather improves, it can feel as though we are starting again from scratch. Yet regular movement through the whole year is one of the best things we can do for energy, mood, strength and balance.

This guide offers practical ways to keep moving in every season: how to walk safely in winter, how to stay cool and safe in summer heat, what to do on wet and windy days, how to dress and what to wear on your feet, and how to build a routine flexible enough to survive the change of seasons. If you have heart or breathing conditions, take medication that affects how your body handles heat or cold, or have balance problems, ask your doctor for advice on exercising in extreme weather.

## Why year-round movement matters

Moving regularly helps maintain muscle strength, joint flexibility, balance, heart health and mood. Long breaks can lead to lost strength and stamina, which can be harder to rebuild than to maintain. A few minutes of movement on difficult days keeps the habit alive until better weather returns.

## Winter: cold, dark and slippery

### Walking safely in the cold

- **Dress in layers**: a base layer that wicks moisture, a warm middle layer and a windproof or waterproof outer layer. You can remove a layer as you warm up.
- **Cover your extremities**: a hat, gloves and a scarf or neck warmer make a big difference.
- **Warm up indoors** for a few minutes before going out, with gentle marching and arm swings.
- **Walk during daylight** where possible, and choose well-lit routes.

### Ice and snow

Falls are a real risk in icy weather.

- **Wear shoes or boots with good grip**, and consider slip-on ice grips for very icy days.
- **Take shorter, flatter steps**, keeping your weight over your feet.
- **Use walking poles** for extra stability if you are comfortable with them.
- **Stay on gritted or cleared paths** and avoid shiny surfaces.
- **Keep your hands free**, using a backpack rather than carrying bags.
- **Stay indoors** on the worst days; there is no shame in an indoor session.

Our guide to [balance exercises](/journal/balance-exercises-to-stay-steady/) helps build the stability that protects against falls.

### Darkness

- **Wear bright and reflective clothing**, and carry a small torch or use a head torch.
- **Tell someone your route** if you walk alone.
- **Consider walking groups**, which make winter walks safer and more sociable.

## Summer: heat and sun

### Staying cool

- **Move early or late**, avoiding the hottest part of the day.
- **Choose shady routes**, such as tree-lined paths or parks.
- **Wear loose, light-coloured, breathable clothing** and a wide-brimmed hat.
- **Slow down.** Your usual pace may feel much harder in the heat.

### Hydration

- **Drink water regularly** before, during and after activity.
- **Carry water** on walks, even short ones.
- **Watch for signs of overheating**, such as dizziness, headache, nausea, confusion, very heavy sweating or, worryingly, no sweating at all. Stop, move to shade, drink water and seek medical help if symptoms do not improve quickly.

Some medicines affect how the body handles heat or fluids. If you take regular medication, ask your doctor or pharmacist whether you need to take extra care.

### Sun protection

Protect your skin and eyes with sunscreen, a hat and sunglasses. Our guide to [everyday sun protection](/journal/everyday-sun-protection/) explains the essentials.

### Water-based activities

Swimming and water exercise are wonderful in summer, cooling the body while you move. Our guide to [swimming and water exercise](/journal/swimming-and-water-exercise/) explains how to get started.

## Rainy and windy days

Rain does not have to stop you, but it helps to be prepared:

- **A good waterproof jacket**, ideally breathable and with a hood.
- **Water-resistant shoes** with grip.
- **Shorter routes** on wet days, with the option to cut them shorter still.
- **Care on wet leaves and smooth surfaces**, which can be as slippery as ice.

On very wet or stormy days, switch to an indoor plan.

## Indoor alternatives

Having a few indoor options means bad weather never becomes an excuse:

- **Strength exercises** with body weight, resistance bands or light weights; see our guide to [strength training for women over fifty](/journal/strength-training-for-women-over-fifty/).
- **Yoga and Pilates**, at home or in a class; see our guide to [yoga and Pilates for beginners](/journal/yoga-and-pilates-for-beginners/).
- **Dancing** to music in the living room; see our guide to [dancing for fitness and joy](/journal/dancing-for-fitness-and-joy/).
- **Walking indoors**: some shopping centres, museums and leisure centres allow walking, and some communities run indoor walking groups.
- **Stairs**, carefully and with a handrail, if you are steady on your feet.
- **Stretching routines**; see our guide to [stretching and flexibility](/journal/stretching-and-flexibility/).
- **Online classes**, many of them free, at different levels.

A simple plan might be: walk outdoors when the weather allows, and on other days do twenty minutes of indoor strength and stretching.

## Spring and autumn: the transition months

The in-between seasons are often the most pleasant for being active, but they bring their own challenges. Temperatures can change sharply between morning and afternoon, so layers that are easy to remove and carry are especially useful. Wet leaves in autumn and muddy paths in spring can be surprisingly slippery, so good grip matters just as much as in winter. And as the clocks change in many countries, a walk that was in daylight one week may be in darkness the next.

These months are also a good time to review your routine. In spring, gradually lengthen walks and add outdoor activities you avoided in winter. In autumn, set up your indoor plan before the dark evenings arrive, so the switch feels natural rather than like a loss.

## Everyday movement counts

Not every bit of activity has to be a formal workout. Gardening, carrying shopping, cleaning windows, playing with grandchildren and taking the stairs all keep the body moving, and in bad weather they can fill gaps in your routine. Gardening in particular combines bending, lifting, reaching and walking; our guide to [creating a low-maintenance garden](/journal/low-maintenance-garden/) shows how to keep it enjoyable rather than exhausting. Simple habits help too: standing up and moving for a few minutes every hour, walking while on the phone, or parking a little further from the shop when it is safe to do so.

## Footwear through the year

| Season | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Winter | waterproof boots with deep grip; warm socks; ice grips for icy days |
| Summer | breathable, supportive shoes; socks that wick moisture |
| Wet seasons | water-resistant shoes with good grip |
| Indoors | supportive trainers or bare feet for some yoga styles, on a non-slip surface |

## Building a routine that survives the seasons

1. **Set a minimum**: decide on the least you will do on any day, for example ten minutes of movement. On good days you will do more.
2. **Have a plan A and a plan B**: an outdoor activity and an indoor alternative.
3. **Schedule movement** at the same times each week.
4. **Find company**: a walking friend, a class or a group makes it harder to skip.
5. **Track what you do**, in a notebook or on a calendar, and celebrate consistency.
6. **Adjust with the seasons**: earlier walks in summer, midday walks in winter.

## Mood and light

Many people feel lower during the darker months. Getting outdoors in daylight, even on cloudy days, moving regularly and spending time with others can help. If low mood persists or affects daily life, talk to your doctor.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it safe to exercise in the cold?

For most people, yes, with warm layers, a proper warm-up and care on icy surfaces. People with heart or breathing conditions should ask their doctor for advice.

### How do I know if it is too hot to walk?

If the heat makes you feel unwell, or if official heat warnings are in place, choose a cooler time, a shady route or an indoor activity instead.

### What if I lose my routine during a bad spell of weather?

Start again gently, with shorter and easier sessions, and build back up over a few weeks.

## The bottom line

Staying active through the year is about adapting, not stopping. In winter, dress in layers, wear shoes with grip, walk in daylight and stay indoors on icy days. In summer, move early or late, seek shade, drink water and watch for signs of overheating. Prepare for rain with waterproofs and good shoes, and keep a set of indoor activities ready for the worst days. Set a minimum, keep a plan A and plan B, find company and ask your doctor for advice if you have health conditions that affect how you handle heat or cold.
