For a large part of the year, in many climates, a coat is the first thing people see. It goes over everything else, it is worn almost every day, and it can make a simple outfit look polished or turn a carefully chosen look into a shapeless bundle. A good coat is also one of the most valuable investments in a wardrobe: well chosen, it can serve beautifully for many winters.

This guide explains the main styles of coat and who they suit, the fabrics and fillings that keep you warm, how to check fit and construction in the shop, which colours and details make a coat versatile, how to judge value for money and how to care for a coat so it lasts.

## Start with how you live

Before falling in love with a coat in a shop window, think about your daily life:

- **Climate:** mild and wet, cold and dry, very cold, or changeable.
- **How you travel:** walking, public transport, driving or cycling.
- **What you wear underneath:** tailored clothes, knitwear, dresses, casual layers.
- **How much you carry:** a coat that has to accommodate a shoulder bag or a backpack needs room in the shoulders.

A beautiful coat that is too cold, too heavy or too formal for your everyday life will hang in the wardrobe while you wear an old one.

## The main coat styles

| Style | Description | Often suits |
|---|---|---|
| Classic wool overcoat | tailored, usually knee length or longer | most women; works with tailored and casual clothes |
| Wrap coat | belted, no buttons or few, soft shape | women who like waist definition |
| Trench coat | belted, often water-resistant, with storm flaps | changeable, wet climates; adds polish to casual outfits |
| Cocoon or oversized coat | rounded, roomy shape | those who want comfort and room for layers |
| Pea coat or short coat | hip length, often double-breasted | petite figures, drivers, casual style |
| Puffer or quilted coat | insulated with down or synthetic filling | cold climates, walking, outdoor life |
| Parka | longer, often hooded, insulated | very cold or wet weather |
| Cape or poncho coat | draped over the shoulders | a dramatic, elegant look; less practical for carrying bags |

Many women find that **one smart coat and one practical coat** cover most needs.

## Length

- **Hip length** suits petite figures, drivers and casual style.
- **Knee length** is the most versatile and works with trousers, skirts and dresses.
- **Midi and long coats** add elegance and warmth, and work well with longer skirts and dresses, but can be less practical on stairs and public transport.

A useful guide: your coat should be **longer than any skirt or dress** you plan to wear with it, or clearly shorter, rather than ending just above the hem.

## Fabrics and fillings

### Wool and wool blends

Wool is warm, breathable and naturally resistant to creasing. In blends:

- **a higher wool content** usually means more warmth and better ageing;
- **some synthetic content** can add strength and reduce cost;
- **cashmere or alpaca in the blend** adds softness, but very soft fibres may pill more.

Check the label for the percentage of each fibre.

### Down and synthetic insulation

- **Down** is very warm for its weight and packs small, but loses warmth when wet unless treated. Look for information about responsible sourcing and the fill power, which indicates how well the down lofts.
- **Synthetic insulation** stays warmer when damp and is often easier to wash.

### Waterproof and water-resistant fabrics

For wet climates, look for:

- **waterproof** fabrics with **taped seams**, which keep out heavy rain;
- **water-resistant** finishes, which handle showers;
- **breathability**, so you do not become clammy when walking.

### Linings

A good lining helps a coat slide on and off easily over knitwear, adds warmth and protects the outer fabric.

## Checking fit

Wear or bring the kind of clothes you will wear underneath, such as a thick jumper, when you try on coats.

1. **Shoulders:** the shoulder seams should sit at or just beyond your shoulder bone, unless the design is intentionally dropped.
2. **Movement:** reach forward, cross your arms and lift them. The coat should not pull across the back or rise too much.
3. **Buttons:** fastened, the coat should close without pulling, with room for layers.
4. **Sleeves:** they should reach the base of your thumb with your arms relaxed, covering your cuffs.
5. **Length:** check it in a mirror with the shoes you usually wear.
6. **Collar:** it should sit close to the neck and, if you like, turn up comfortably against the wind.

Small adjustments, such as shortening sleeves, are often possible. Our guide to [tailoring and alterations](/journal/tailoring-and-alterations/) explains which changes are worthwhile.

## Checking quality

You do not need to be an expert to spot signs of good construction:

- **seams** are straight and even, with no loose threads;
- **patterns**, such as checks, match at the seams;
- **buttons** are sewn on firmly, ideally with a spare provided;
- **buttonholes** are neat and do not fray;
- **pockets** are deep enough to be useful and reinforced at the corners;
- **lining** is smooth and sewn in without puckering;
- **zips** run smoothly, and storm flaps cover them on outdoor coats.

## Colours that work with everything

A coat is seen with almost everything you own, so colour matters.

- **Camel, navy, charcoal, grey, black and deep olive** are versatile and easy to combine.
- **Rich colours**, such as burgundy, forest green, cobalt or rust, can be just as versatile if they suit your colouring and wardrobe, and lift grey days.
- **Soft light colours**, such as cream or pale grey, look elegant but need more cleaning.

Choose a colour that flatters your face, since a coat is worn close to it. Our guide to [finding your best colours](/journal/finding-your-best-colours/) explains how, and our guide to [the art of accessories](/journal/scarves-and-jewellery-styling/) shows how a scarf can bring colour close to the face whatever the coat.

## Details that make a difference

- **A hood**, removable or built in, for rain and wind.
- **Inside pockets** for a phone and keys.
- **Cuffs** that keep out draughts.
- **A two-way zip**, which makes long coats easier to sit and walk in.
- **Reflective details**, for walking in the dark.

## Value for money

A good coat can be expensive, but it is worn very often. Think about the cost per wear: a coat worn most days for several winters can be excellent value even at a higher price.

- **Shop at the end of the season** for reductions.
- **Look second-hand and vintage**, where well-made wool coats are often available. Our guide to [second-hand and vintage style](/journal/second-hand-and-vintage-style/) explains what to check.
- **Buy for your life**, not for an imagined one.

## Caring for your coat

- **Hang it on a sturdy, shaped hanger** to keep the shoulders in shape.
- **Brush wool coats** regularly with a clothes brush to remove dust and lint.
- **Let a wet coat dry naturally**, away from direct heat.
- **Clean as directed** on the care label, but not too often; spot clean small marks.
- **Store it clean** at the end of the season, in a breathable cover, protected from moths. Our guide to [caring for your clothes](/journal/caring-for-your-clothes/) covers moth prevention.
- **Re-proof waterproof coats** occasionally, following the manufacturer's advice.
- **Replace buttons or repair the lining** as soon as they need it.

## Restore or replace?

A coat that has served you well for years does not always need replacing. Before buying a new one, consider whether a little work would bring the old one back to life. New buttons can transform a coat completely, especially if the originals were plain or have been lost and replaced with mismatched ones. A worn or torn lining can be replaced by a tailor, which often costs much less than a new coat of the same quality. Professional cleaning and pressing can restore the shape of a tired wool coat, and a clothes shaver can remove pilling from the sleeves and sides.

It may be time to replace a coat when the outer fabric is thin or shiny in many places, when it no longer keeps you warm or dry, or when it no longer fits comfortably over the clothes you wear now. If so, consider passing the old one on to a charity shop, where it may keep someone else warm.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should my coat be one size larger than my clothes?

Not always. Many coats are designed to fit over layers in your usual size. Try it on over a jumper to check.

### Is a black coat the most versatile?

It is versatile, but softer dark colours such as navy, charcoal or camel can be more flattering for many women after fifty, especially near the face.

### How many coats do I need?

Many women manage well with one smart coat and one practical, weatherproof coat, plus a light jacket for warmer days.

## The bottom line

A good coat is chosen for the life you lead: your climate, how you travel, what you wear underneath and how much you carry. Choose a style and length that suit your figure and wardrobe, a fabric and filling that keep you warm and dry, and check the fit over a jumper, especially in the shoulders and sleeves. Look for signs of good construction, choose a colour that flatters your face and works with everything, and think in cost per wear. Care for it with a good hanger, a clothes brush and clean storage, and it will serve you for many winters.
