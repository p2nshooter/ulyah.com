At some point in your forties or fifties, the makeup that served you faithfully for years begins to behave differently. Foundation settles into lines it never used to find. Powder looks dusty by lunchtime. Eyeliner that once looked crisp suddenly seems harsh, and the lipstick that was your signature starts to feather at the edges. None of this means you should stop wearing makeup — only that the rules have changed, and most of the changes are in your favour. Mature skin generally looks its best with less product, softer textures and a little more thought about where light falls.

This guide walks through a complete, realistic routine for skin that has lived a little: preparation, base, concealer, brows, eyes, cheeks and lips. It is not about looking younger. It is about looking rested, bright and like yourself on a good day.

## Why makeup behaves differently now

Several natural changes affect how makeup sits on the skin:

- **Drier skin.** Skin often produces less oil with age, and around menopause many women notice it feels tighter and drier. Dry skin makes powders and matte formulas look flat and emphasises texture.
- **Thinner skin and finer lines.** Product collects in lines around the eyes and mouth, especially if it is heavy or applied thickly.
- **Changes in colour.** Skin tone can become uneven, with areas of redness, sun spots or sallowness. Lips and cheeks often lose some natural colour.
- **Softer contours.** Eyelids may be hoodier, brows sparser and the jawline softer, which changes where shadow and highlight work best.
- **Eyesight.** Many of us now apply makeup without the sharp close-up vision we once had. A magnifying mirror and good light make an enormous difference.

Knowing these changes makes the strategy clear: hydrate first, use thinner layers, choose creams over powders where possible, and put colour and light back where they have faded.

## Start with good light and the right mirror

Before buying a single new product, look at where you apply makeup. A dim bathroom with an overhead bulb casts shadows downwards, making under-eye circles look deeper and encouraging you to apply too much concealer. Natural daylight from a window in front of you is ideal. If that is not possible, a mirror with even, daylight-balanced lighting is worth the investment. A magnifying mirror helps with eyeliner and brows, but always check the overall effect in a normal mirror at arm's length, because that is how other people see you.

## Skin preparation: the step that changes everything

Makeup can only be as good as the surface beneath it. A few minutes of preparation makes base products glide on and stay put.

1. **Cleanse gently.** Use a mild, non-stripping cleanser. Harsh foaming cleansers can leave mature skin tight and flaky.
2. **Hydrate.** Apply a moisturiser suited to your skin and give it a few minutes to absorb. If your skin is very dry, a hydrating serum underneath helps.
3. **Protect.** In the daytime, a broad-spectrum sunscreen is the single most useful product for the long-term health and appearance of your skin. Let it settle before the next step.
4. **Prime if helpful.** A primer is optional. A hydrating or illuminating primer can help foundation last on dry skin; silicone-based "blurring" primers can soften the look of pores but may emphasise flakiness if your skin is dry.
5. **Lip balm on early.** Apply balm before you begin your face so lips are soft and smooth by the time you reach lipstick.

## Foundation: less is more

Heavy, full-coverage foundation is the most common reason makeup looks ageing. It sits on the surface, settles into lines and looks mask-like in daylight.

### Choose the right texture

- **Tinted moisturisers, skin tints and serum foundations** give a sheer, natural finish that lets real skin show through.
- **Light-reflecting or "radiant" finishes** bounce light and make skin look fresh, but avoid anything with large glitter particles.
- **Avoid very matte, long-wear formulas** unless your skin is genuinely oily; they tend to look dry and emphasise texture.

### Match the shade carefully

Your foundation shade may have changed over the years, and many women still buy the colour they wore at thirty. Test shades along the jawline in daylight; the right one disappears into your skin. It is better to choose a shade very slightly warmer than too pale, which can look grey.

### Apply only where needed

Start in the centre of the face — around the nose, the inner cheeks and the chin, where redness is usually most noticeable — and blend outwards with fingers, a damp sponge or a soft brush. Many women find they do not need foundation over the whole face at all. Fingers warm the product and press it into the skin for a natural finish.

## Concealer: precise, not plentiful

Concealer is useful for under-eye shadows, redness around the nose and age spots, but a thick layer under the eyes will crease within an hour.

- **Use a creamy, hydrating formula** rather than a thick, dry one.
- **Apply only to the darkest part of the shadow**, usually the inner corner of the eye, rather than painting a large triangle beneath the eye.
- **Tap it in gently** with a fingertip.
- **For spots and redness**, use a slightly firmer concealer in an exact shade match and pat it on with a small brush.
- **Colour correction** can help with strong discolouration: a peach or apricot tone can neutralise blue-grey under-eye shadows on many skin tones. Use sparingly.

## Powder: a whisper, not a layer

Many women over fifty stop using powder altogether, and their skin looks better for it. If you need to control shine or set concealer:

- **Use a finely milled, translucent powder.**
- **Apply with a small brush** only to the areas that need it, typically the sides of the nose, the centre of the forehead and the chin.
- **Avoid powdering under the eyes**, where it settles into fine lines.
- **Blotting papers** remove shine during the day without adding layers.

## Brows: the frame that lifts the face

Eyebrows often become sparser, lighter or greyer with age, and the outer ends can disappear. Because brows frame the face, gently restoring them is one of the most effective changes you can make.

- **Choose a shade close to your natural hair colour**, or one shade lighter if your hair is grey or silver. Very dark brows against light hair can look severe.
- **Use a fine pencil to draw hair-like strokes** in sparse areas, following the direction of growth, rather than drawing a solid line.
- **Set with a tinted or clear brow gel** to hold hairs in place and add a little fullness.
- **Find the natural shape**: the brow usually starts above the inner corner of the eye, arches roughly above the outer edge of the iris, and ends where a line from the side of the nostril through the outer corner of the eye meets the brow bone. Extending a faded tail slightly can lift the whole eye area.
- **Professional help** — a brow shaping, tint or lamination appointment — can be a worthwhile occasional treat if you find brows difficult.

## Eyes: definition without heaviness

### Eyeshadow

- **Matte or satin finishes** are generally more flattering on crepey lids than heavy shimmer, which highlights texture. A soft sheen on the centre of the lid can still look lovely.
- **Cream shadows** in stick or pot form are easy to blend with a fingertip and do not look dusty — but use a primer or a long-wear formula if they crease on oily lids.
- **Neutral tones** such as taupe, soft brown, warm grey, mushroom and muted plum suit most people and define the eye gently.

### Working with hooded lids

If your upper lid has become hooded, eyeshadow applied only on the mobile lid may disappear when your eyes are open. Look straight ahead into a mirror with your eyes open and apply a soft, medium shade just above the crease — where it will be visible — blending upwards and outwards.

### Eyeliner

- **Swap liquid liner for a soft pencil or powder**, which looks less harsh.
- **Keep the line close to the lashes** and slightly thicker at the outer corner.
- **Try "tightlining"**, pressing pencil into the roots of the upper lashes, for definition that does not look like makeup at all.
- **Choose softer colours** such as brown, charcoal, navy or deep plum rather than jet black, unless black is part of your signature look.
- **Avoid lining the whole lower lash line**, which can make eyes look smaller and draw attention to shadows.

### Mascara

Curling lashes before mascara opens the eye noticeably. Choose a lengthening rather than very thick, clumping formula, and concentrate on the upper lashes. If your lashes are sparse or light, a brown-black shade looks softer than jet black. Remove mascara gently at night to protect fragile lashes.

## Cheeks: bring back the glow

Blusher is perhaps the most underrated product for mature skin. A touch of colour instantly makes the face look healthier and more awake.

- **Choose cream or liquid blusher**, which blends into the skin and gives a natural flush. Powder blusher can look patchy on dry skin.
- **Pick soft, warm tones** — rose, peach, apricot, berry or terracotta, depending on your colouring.
- **Place it slightly higher** than you might have done when younger: on the upper part of the cheek, blended up towards the temple. This lifts the face rather than dragging it down.
- **Start small and build.** You can always add more.

### Highlighter

A subtle cream or liquid highlighter with a fine sheen on the tops of the cheekbones, the brow bone and the centre of the upper lip catches the light beautifully. Avoid chunky shimmer and keep it away from areas with visible texture.

### Bronzer and contour

Strong contouring rarely suits mature faces. If you like a sun-kissed look, a soft, warm bronzer in a natural shade swept lightly where the sun naturally hits — forehead, cheekbones, bridge of the nose — is more flattering than sharp lines.

## Lips: colour that stays put

Lips often become thinner and paler, and fine lines around the mouth can cause lipstick to bleed.

- **Prepare with balm** and blot off the excess before applying colour.
- **Use a lip liner** in a shade matching your lips or lipstick to define the shape and prevent feathering. Lining the whole lip and filling in gives colour a base to cling to.
- **Choose creamy or satin finishes** over very matte formulas, which can be drying, and over very glossy ones, which can travel into lines.
- **Medium-depth shades often flatter most**: rosy pinks, berries, warm corals and soft reds. Very pale or nude lipsticks can wash out the face, while extremely dark shades can make lips look thinner.
- **A dab of colour in the centre** of the lower lip, pressed together, gives a fuller look without overdrawing.

## A five-minute everyday face

1. Moisturiser and sunscreen.
2. Skin tint or light foundation in the centre of the face only.
3. Concealer on the inner corner of the eyes and any redness.
4. Brow pencil and gel.
5. Cream blusher on the upper cheeks.
6. One coat of mascara.
7. Tinted lip balm or a sheer lipstick.

## Tools and hygiene

- **Fingers are excellent tools** for creams and skin tints.
- **A damp makeup sponge** gives a sheer, natural finish.
- **A few good brushes** — a fluffy blending brush, a small concealer brush and an angled brow brush — cover most needs.
- **Clean brushes and sponges regularly**, as build-up can irritate skin and spoil application.
- **Replace mascara every few months** and discard products that change smell or texture.

## Skin changes worth checking

Makeup is for enjoyment, not for hiding health concerns. If you notice a mole that changes in size, shape or colour, a sore that does not heal, a new patch of skin that bleeds or itches, or sudden changes in your skin or hair, see a doctor promptly. Sensitive or reactive skin may also benefit from a pharmacist's or dermatologist's advice about suitable products.

## The bottom line

Makeup after fifty is about refinement rather than coverage. Prepare the skin well, use thinner layers and creamier textures, restore definition to brows and eyes, bring warmth back to the cheeks and lips, and let your real skin show through. The result is a face that looks fresh, confident and entirely your own — which is far more attractive than any attempt to turn back the clock.
