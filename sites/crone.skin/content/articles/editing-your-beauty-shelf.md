Most of us have a bathroom cabinet, a dressing table drawer or a bag stuffed with beauty products: half-used foundations in shades that never quite matched, lipsticks bought for a wedding a decade ago, serums that promised miracles, free samples, gift sets and hair products for a style you no longer wear. It is easy to accumulate far more than we use, and the clutter makes getting ready slower and more confusing.

Editing your beauty shelf is a satisfying project. It clears space, helps you rediscover products you love, keeps you safe from out-of-date items, and makes your daily routine simpler and more enjoyable. It also helps you shop more wisely in future. This guide walks through the process step by step.

## Why edit your beauty products?

- **Safety.** Old makeup and skincare can harbour bacteria, and some ingredients become less effective or irritating as they age.
- **Simplicity.** A smaller selection of products you genuinely love makes getting ready quicker and calmer.
- **Your face has changed.** Colours and textures that suited you at thirty may not suit you now. Our guide to makeup for mature skin explains how needs change.
- **Money.** When you know what you have, you stop buying duplicates.
- **Space.** A tidy shelf or drawer is a pleasure to use.

## Step one: gather everything

Collect every beauty product from every location: bathroom cabinets, dressing tables, handbags, travel bags, gym bags, drawers and the backs of cupboards. Put everything on a table or on a towel on the floor. Seeing it all together is often surprising.

## Step two: sort into categories

Group products by type:

- **Skincare**: cleansers, moisturisers, serums, sunscreens, masks.
- **Base makeup**: foundation, concealer, powder, primer.
- **Cheeks**: blusher, bronzer, highlighter.
- **Eyes**: eyeshadow, eyeliner, mascara, brow products.
- **Lips**: lipsticks, glosses, liners, balms.
- **Hair care and styling.**
- **Body care**: lotions, scrubs, deodorants.
- **Fragrance.**
- **Nail care.**
- **Tools**: brushes, sponges, tweezers, curlers.

## Step three: check for expired products

Many beauty products have a limited life once opened. Look for the **period-after-opening symbol** — a small open jar with a number and the letter "M", such as "12M", meaning the product should be used within twelve months of opening. Some products also have a printed expiry date.

General guidelines that many experts suggest (always follow the specific product's guidance):

- **Mascara and liquid eyeliner**: around three months, because they are used close to the eyes and can harbour bacteria.
- **Liquid foundation and concealer**: often six to twelve months.
- **Cream products** such as cream blushers and cream eyeshadows: often six to twelve months.
- **Powder products**: often longer, sometimes up to two years, if kept clean and dry.
- **Lipsticks and lip glosses**: often around one to two years.
- **Skincare**: often six to twelve months once opened, sometimes less for products with active ingredients such as vitamin C.
- **Sunscreen**: check the expiry date; expired sunscreen may not protect properly.
- **Fragrance**: can last years if stored away from heat and light, but discard if the smell has changed.

### Signs a product has gone off

Regardless of dates, discard anything that has:

- **changed smell**, often becoming sour, musty or rancid;
- **changed texture** — separating, becoming lumpy, grainy, dried out or watery;
- **changed colour**;
- **caused irritation**, redness or breakouts;
- **been used during an eye infection** (replace eye products afterwards).

## Step four: decide what to keep

For each product that is still in date and in good condition, ask:

- **Do I use it?** If you have not used it in the past six months, you probably will not.
- **Does it suit me now?** Is the shade right? Does the texture work on my skin?
- **Do I love it?** Does it make me feel good?
- **Do I have a better version?** Keep the best and let go of duplicates.
- **Does it irritate my skin?** If so, let it go.

Be honest. It is tempting to keep things because they were expensive or were gifts, but unused products simply take up space.

## Step five: dispose of products responsibly

- **Empty containers** where possible and check local recycling guidance; some cosmetic packaging is recyclable, and some retailers and brands run take-back schemes for empty containers.
- **Do not pour large amounts of products down the drain**; wipe out with a tissue and put in household waste if appropriate.
- **Pass on unused, unopened products** to friends, family or organisations that accept toiletry donations, such as some charities and shelters. Never pass on opened or used makeup.
- **Fragrance and aerosols** may need special disposal; check local rules.

## Step six: build your core kit

After editing, you will probably have a much smaller collection. A practical core kit for most women over fifty might include:

### Skincare

- A gentle cleanser.
- A moisturiser (and perhaps a richer night cream).
- A daily broad-spectrum sunscreen.
- One or two treatments you genuinely use, such as a retinoid or vitamin C serum.
- A lip balm.

See our guide to a simple skincare routine after fifty.

### Makeup

- A skin tint or light foundation in the right shade.
- A creamy concealer.
- A brow pencil and gel.
- A neutral eyeshadow palette or one or two cream shadows.
- A soft eyeliner pencil.
- A mascara.
- A cream blusher.
- Two or three lipsticks in flattering shades, plus a lip liner.

### Tools

- A few good brushes.
- A makeup sponge.
- Tweezers.
- An eyelash curler.
- A magnifying mirror.

## Step seven: organise what remains

- **Store products by category** in drawers, boxes or clear containers.
- **Keep everyday items within easy reach**, and special-occasion products separately.
- **Store away from heat, humidity and sunlight**, which can spoil products. A bedroom drawer is often better than a steamy bathroom.
- **Label the opening date** on products with a small sticker or marker, so you know when to replace them.
- **Create a small travel bag** with minis or samples, so you are not constantly moving products between bags.

## Step eight: clean your tools

Dirty brushes and sponges can harbour bacteria, cause breakouts and make makeup look patchy.

- **Wash brushes** every week or two with mild soap or brush cleanser and lukewarm water.
- **Reshape and dry flat** or bristles downwards, so water does not loosen the glue.
- **Wash or replace sponges** regularly.
- **Clean tweezers and curlers** with alcohol wipes.
- **Sharpen pencils** before use to expose a fresh surface.

## Rediscovering forgotten favourites

Editing is not only about throwing things away. Many women find treasures at the back of the drawer: a lipstick shade they loved and forgot, a perfume they had stopped wearing, a cream blusher that suddenly looks perfect again. Before you decide something has to go, try it on with fresh eyes. Some products need a new way of using them — an eyeshadow pressed onto the cheeks as blusher, a lipstick tapped on as a soft lip stain, or a highlighter mixed into moisturiser for a gentle glow.

## Keeping it simple day to day

Once your shelf is edited, a few habits stop clutter returning:

- **Keep a "one in, one out" rule** for each category.
- **Do a quick check every season**, discarding anything past its best.
- **Keep samples in one small box** and use them up, rather than letting them accumulate.
- **Review your routine** when your skin, hair or lifestyle changes.

## Shopping smarter in future

Editing your shelf often reveals buying patterns — the many similar nude lipsticks, the three unopened eye creams. Use what you learn:

- **Make a list** of what you need before shopping.
- **Use up before you buy more.** Finish a product before opening a new one in the same category.
- **Ask for samples** and test new products before buying full sizes.
- **Test shades in daylight**, on your skin, before buying.
- **Be wary of trends and miracle claims.** A product that "erases" lines overnight will not.
- **Choose quality over quantity** for items you use daily.
- **Avoid gift sets** unless you will use every item.
- **Keep a note** of products and shades that work for you, so you can repurchase easily.

## Editing hair and body products

The same approach works for the shower shelf and hair-styling drawer. Keep one shampoo and conditioner that suit your hair as it is now (finer, greyer or drier hair may need different products from those you used years ago), one or two styling products you reach for every week, and the heat tools you actually use. Old aerosols, half-used gels and products for styles you no longer wear can usually go. Check that sunscreen for the body is in date before summer.

## A quick editing checklist

1. Gather every product from every location.
2. Sort into categories.
3. Discard expired, changed or irritating products.
4. Keep only what you use, love and suits you now.
5. Donate unopened items and recycle packaging where possible.
6. Build a small core kit.
7. Store products neatly, away from heat and humidity.
8. Clean your brushes and tools.
9. Label opening dates.
10. Shop with a list in future.

## The bottom line

Editing your beauty shelf is a small project with big rewards. It keeps you safe from old and contaminated products, simplifies your routine, saves money and helps you rediscover the products that truly suit you now. Keep the essentials you love, clear the rest, and enjoy a calm, organised space where getting ready feels like a pleasure.
