There is a special kind of contentment that comes with a train journey. You settle into your seat with a book, a flask of tea and a window full of moving landscape. Fields, rivers, villages and mountains slip by. You can stand up, stretch, wander to the buffet car or chat to the person opposite. You arrive in the heart of a town rather than at an airport miles away. The journey itself becomes part of the holiday.

Rail travel is increasingly popular with women over fifty, and with good reason. It is comfortable, relaxing and often kinder to the environment than flying. It allows you to see a country as you travel through it. And there are journeys to suit every taste: scenic mountain railways, classic overnight sleepers, high-speed routes linking great cities, and slow local lines through quiet countryside.

This guide will help you plan a rail journey that is enjoyable from the moment you board.

## Why travel by train?

- **Comfort.** More legroom than most planes, the freedom to move around, and the option to relax or work.
- **Scenery.** Many routes pass through stunning landscapes that you would never see from the air.
- **Simplicity.** No long security queues, fewer luggage restrictions, and stations in city centres.
- **Sociability.** Trains are easy places to strike up conversations, especially in dining cars and observation carriages.
- **Sustainability.** Rail is generally a lower-carbon way to travel than flying or driving.
- **The journey as experience.** A train trip can be a holiday in itself.

## Types of rail journey

### Scenic day journeys

Many countries have famous scenic lines through mountains, along coasts or across wild landscapes. Some offer panoramic carriages with large windows, commentary and refreshments. These make wonderful day trips or highlights of a longer holiday.

### High-speed intercity travel

Fast trains link major cities quickly and comfortably, often faster than flying when you include travel to and from airports. They are ideal for multi-city trips.

### Overnight sleeper trains

Sleeper trains let you travel long distances while you sleep, saving a night's accommodation and arriving refreshed in a new place. Cabins range from shared couchettes to private compartments with beds, washbasins and sometimes en-suite facilities.

### Luxury rail journeys

Some trains offer a luxurious, all-inclusive experience, with elegant carriages, fine dining and guided excursions. These are a special treat for a milestone birthday or anniversary.

### Slow local lines

Branch lines and local trains offer a gentle way to explore rural areas, stopping at small stations and villages. Combine them with walking or cycling for a relaxed holiday.

## Planning your route

Begin by thinking about what you want from the trip:

- **A single scenic journey** as part of a wider holiday?
- **A multi-city tour** linking several destinations?
- **A grand journey** across a country or continent?
- **A relaxed base** with day trips by local trains?

Use journey planners and rail operator websites to check routes, journey times and connections. Specialist rail travel websites and guidebooks offer excellent advice on routes, tickets and the best seats for views.

When planning connections, allow generous time — especially when changing stations, crossing borders or travelling with luggage. A missed connection is much less stressful if you have built in a buffer, or even planned an overnight stop.

## Tickets and passes

### Point-to-point tickets

For a simple journey, buy a ticket between two stations. Booking in advance often brings substantial savings, especially on long-distance and high-speed routes. Many operators release tickets a few months ahead.

### Rail passes

For extensive travel within a country or region, a rail pass can offer good value and flexibility. Passes allow travel on a set number of days within a period. Some trains still require seat reservations in addition to a pass, so check the rules.

### Discounts for older travellers

Many countries and operators offer discounts for older travellers, sometimes through railcards or senior passes. Check eligibility and terms on the relevant operator's website.

### First or standard class?

First class usually offers more space, quieter carriages, larger seats and sometimes complimentary refreshments. On long journeys, especially scenic ones, it can be worth the extra cost. Advance-purchase first-class fares are sometimes surprisingly affordable.

## Choosing your seat

Seat choice can transform your journey:

- **Choose a window seat** on scenic routes. Research which side of the train has the best views.
- **Facing the direction of travel** suits many people better.
- **Table seats** are good for spreading out, eating and playing cards.
- **Quiet carriages** are ideal if you want peace.
- **Seats near luggage racks** let you keep an eye on your bags.

Many booking systems let you choose your seat, sometimes with a seat map.

## Sleeper trains

Overnight trains can be a highlight of a rail holiday. Tips:

- **Book early** for private cabins, which are popular.
- **Choose your berth**: lower berths are easier to get into; upper berths offer more privacy.
- **Pack a small overnight bag** with nightwear, toiletries, medications, earplugs, an eye mask and a change of clothes, so you do not need to open your main case.
- **Bring snacks and water**, though many sleepers have dining cars or refreshment service.
- **Check facilities**: washbasins, showers and toilets vary considerably.
- **Expect some movement and noise.** Earplugs and an eye mask help.

## Luggage

Trains are generally more relaxed about luggage than airlines, but space is still limited, and you need to lift bags onto racks yourself.

- **Pack light**, with a bag you can lift comfortably.
- **Choose a wheeled case** that fits in overhead racks or the luggage area.
- **Keep valuables with you**, not in the luggage rack.
- **Use a small daypack** for essentials during the journey.
- **Consider luggage-forwarding services**, available in some countries, which send your bags ahead.

Some stations have stairs and long platforms. If you need help, many rail operators offer free assistance with boarding and alighting; book it in advance where required.

## Food and drink

Eating on trains can be one of the great pleasures of rail travel. Options include:

- **Dining cars**, where you can enjoy a meal with a view — sometimes a highlight in itself.
- **Buffet or café cars** with snacks and drinks.
- **Trolley services** on some routes.
- **Your own picnic**: pack local bread, cheese, fruit and treats from a market before you board.

Bring a refillable water bottle, as some trains have limited supplies.

## Making the journey comfortable

- **Wear comfortable, layered clothing**, as carriage temperatures vary.
- **Bring a light scarf or shawl** for warmth or as a pillow.
- **Pack entertainment**: books, a journal, puzzles, downloaded films or podcasts and headphones.
- **Carry a charger and power bank**, though many trains have sockets.
- **Get up and walk** regularly on long journeys to stretch your legs.
- **Keep medications and essentials** with you.

## Crossing borders by train

International rail journeys can be wonderfully seamless, but check:

- **Passport and visa requirements** for each country.
- **Border checks**, which may happen on board or at stations.
- **Currency** for each country.
- **Different operators**, with different booking systems and rules.

## When things go wrong

Delays and cancellations happen. To reduce stress:

- **Build buffers** into connections.
- **Check the operator's policy** on delays and missed connections — many offer rebooking or compensation.
- **Keep your tickets and receipts.**
- **Have a backup plan**, such as a list of hotels near key stations.
- **Stay calm and ask staff for help.** Rail staff are usually very helpful.

## A sample ten-day rail holiday

To show how a rail trip can come together, here is an outline that many first-time rail travellers enjoy. Adapt the cities and distances to your own country or region.

- **Days one and two:** begin in a capital city you already know a little. Stay near the main station, so your first departure is a short walk away, and spend a gentle day at a museum or park.
- **Day three:** take a morning high-speed train to a second city, arriving in time for a late lunch. Keep the afternoon free for a walk through the old town.
- **Day four:** a day trip on a local line to a nearby lake, castle or coastal town, returning in the early evening.
- **Day five:** a long scenic journey — the centrepiece of the trip — in a window seat on the best side for the views, with a picnic bought at the market that morning.
- **Days six and seven:** rest in a smaller town at the end of the scenic line. Walk, read, and enjoy not travelling for a while.
- **Day eight:** an overnight sleeper back towards your starting point, with dinner in the dining car.
- **Days nine and ten:** a final city stay, unhurried, before heading home.

Notice the rhythm: travel days are followed by quieter days, and the longest journey is treated as an experience rather than a transfer. That balance is what keeps a rail holiday relaxing rather than tiring.

## Keeping a record of your journey

A train window is a wonderful place to write. Many rail travellers keep a small journal of each journey: the time of departure, the landscapes, the people they spoke to, what they ate in the dining car. Others sketch, collect tickets and postcards, or take one photograph from the window every hour. These records become treasured reminders of the trip — and are often the first step to planning the next one.

## Ideas for memorable train journeys

Inspiration for your planning:

- **A mountain railway** with panoramic carriages and alpine views.
- **A coastal line** hugging cliffs and beaches.
- **A night train** from one great city to another.
- **A multi-city tour** linking historic capitals.
- **A rural branch line** combined with walking or cycling.
- **A luxury rail experience** for a special celebration.

Research specific routes through rail operators, specialist travel companies and travel guides.

## Enjoy the ride

Train travel invites you to slow down, look out of the window and enjoy the passage of time and landscape. With a little planning — the right tickets, a good seat, light luggage and a well-stocked picnic — your journey can be as memorable as your destination.

Next time you travel, consider leaving the car at home and skipping the airport. Take the train, settle in, and let the world roll by.
