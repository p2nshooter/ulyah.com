Of all the changes a face goes through after fifty, few make as much difference to the overall impression as the eyebrows. Brows frame the eyes, give the face its expression and balance the features. When they become thinner, paler, sparser at the tail or a little unruly, the whole face can look tired, even when we feel anything but. Lashes, too, often become shorter and finer. The good news is that a few minutes of care and the right products can restore that frame beautifully, without a heavy, drawn-on look.

This guide explains why brows and lashes change, how to find a shape that suits your face now, the pros and cons of pencils, powders, gels and tints, how to choose a mascara that flatters rather than flakes, and when it is worth speaking to a doctor about hair loss.

## Why brows and lashes change

Hair everywhere on the body tends to change with age. Many women find that their brows:

- **thin out**, especially towards the outer ends;
- **turn grey or white**, so they almost disappear against the skin;
- **become coarser or wirier** in places, with a few long hairs that grow in unexpected directions;
- **show the effects of years of plucking**, because hair that was removed repeatedly in the past sometimes does not grow back.

Lashes may become shorter, sparser and lighter in colour. Hormonal changes around menopause, genetics and general health all play a part.

Gradual change is normal. Sudden or patchy loss of brow or lash hair, or loss accompanied by other symptoms, is a different matter and is worth discussing with a doctor, because it can sometimes be linked to medical conditions that can be treated.

## Finding your best brow shape now

The shape that suited you at twenty-five may not be the most flattering today. Softer, slightly fuller brows tend to lift and brighten a mature face, while very thin, high or sharply angled brows can look severe.

A classic way to map your brows uses a long, straight object such as a pencil:

1. **The start:** hold the pencil vertically against the side of your nose. Where it meets the brow is a natural starting point.
2. **The arch:** angle the pencil from the side of your nose through the outer edge of your iris while looking straight ahead. The arch usually looks best around that point.
3. **The end:** angle the pencil from the side of your nose to the outer corner of your eye. The brow can end around there.

Make a tiny dot with a brow pencil at each point. Treat them as a guide rather than a rule: your natural bone structure matters more than any formula.

### Shape tips for a mature face

- **Keep the tail from drooping** too far down, which can make the eyes look downturned.
- **Avoid a very high arch**, which can look surprised.
- **Do not over-pluck.** Remove only obvious stray hairs below the brow line and between the brows.
- **Trim, don't pluck, long hairs.** Brush the brows upwards and trim any hairs that extend far beyond the shape with small scissors.

If you are unsure, a session with a skilled brow technician can be a good investment. Ask them to shape conservatively and to explain how to maintain the shape at home.

## Filling in: pencils, powders, gels and pens

The aim is to recreate the look of natural hair, not to draw a solid block of colour.

### Choosing a colour

- If your brows are **grey or white**, choose a soft taupe, ash brown or grey that is a shade or two lighter than your hair once was, or one that complements your silver hair.
- If your hair is **coloured**, choose a brow shade one or two tones lighter than your hair for a soft look, or a touch deeper if you prefer more definition.
- **Avoid very dark, warm or reddish shades** unless they match your natural colouring; they can look harsh against mature skin.

Our guide to [finding your best colours](/journal/finding-your-best-colours/) explains how undertones affect the shades that flatter you.

### Products compared

| Product | Best for | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Fine pencil | drawing hair-like strokes in sparse areas | keep it sharp and use light, feathery strokes |
| Powder | soft, diffused fullness | apply with a small angled brush |
| Tinted gel | adding colour and holding unruly hairs | comb through, then brush into place |
| Clear gel | taming without adding colour | ideal over pencil or powder to set the look |
| Brow pen | longer-lasting hair strokes | practise on the back of your hand first |

Many women find that combining two products works best: powder for overall softness and a fine pencil to fill specific gaps, finished with a clear or tinted gel.

### A simple routine

1. **Brush** the brows upwards and outwards with a spoolie brush.
2. **Fill sparse areas** with light strokes, following the direction of hair growth.
3. **Soften the start** of the brow, near the nose, so it is lighter than the arch and tail.
4. **Brush through again** to blend.
5. **Set with gel.**

Good lighting makes a big difference, especially when filling in pale brows. Our guide to [makeup for mature skin](/journal/makeup-for-mature-skin/) covers the rest of a light, flattering routine.

## Tinting

Brow tinting adds colour to pale or grey hairs, making them visible again without daily makeup. It is usually done by a beauty therapist, and the colour fades over several weeks.

- **Always have a patch test** before tinting, as recommended by the product manufacturer and your therapist, even if you have used tints before. Sensitivities can develop over time.
- **Choose a soft shade**, especially for the first time.
- **Ask about products for sensitive skin** if you react easily.

Lash tinting follows the same principles, but because it is done so close to the eyes, it should only be carried out by trained professionals using products intended for that purpose. Home use of hair dyes on brows or lashes is not recommended.

## Semi-permanent options

Some women consider semi-permanent brow treatments, such as tattooing techniques that create the look of hair strokes. These can be life-changing for people with very little brow hair, but they last a long time and can fade unevenly. If you are considering one:

- research the practitioner's training, hygiene standards and licensing in your area;
- look at photographs of healed results, not just fresh ones;
- ask about how the colour will fade over time;
- discuss any health conditions or medications with the practitioner, and with your doctor if needed.

## Lashes: mascara and care

### Choosing a mascara

- **Lengthening formulas** with a fine brush suit short, sparse lashes better than heavy volumising ones, which can clump.
- **Soft brown or brown-black shades** often look more natural than jet black on fair or silver-haired women.
- **Tubing mascaras**, which wrap lashes in small tubes that slide off with warm water, are less likely to smudge or flake onto the skin under the eyes.
- **Water-resistant formulas** hold up well but can be harder to remove; gentle removal is important for fragile lashes.

### Applying it well

1. **Curl the lashes gently**, if you like, before applying mascara.
2. **Wiggle the wand at the base** of the lashes, then sweep upwards.
3. **Use a light coat on the lower lashes**, or skip them, which can soften the look around the eyes.
4. **Replace mascara regularly**, following the manufacturer's guidance, to reduce the risk of eye irritation.

### Caring for lashes

- **Remove makeup gently** every evening with a suitable eye makeup remover and a soft pad, without rubbing.
- **Avoid sleeping in mascara.**
- **Be cautious with lash extensions and strong adhesives**, which can stress fragile lashes. If you have extensions, choose a trained technician.
- **Lash serums:** some products claim to lengthen lashes. Some contain active ingredients that can have side effects around the eyes, so read the label carefully and ask a pharmacist or doctor before using them, especially if you have eye conditions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will my brows grow back if I stop plucking?

Often some hair returns, but after years of plucking, some follicles may not regrow. Give it a few months before deciding how to fill the gaps.

### Is it normal for my brows to turn white?

Yes. Many women find that their brows grey later or earlier than the hair on their head. A soft tint or a brow product can bring back definition.

### When should I see a doctor?

If you notice sudden, patchy or rapid loss of brow or lash hair, or loss accompanied by other symptoms such as fatigue, skin changes or itching, speak to your doctor.

## The bottom line

Brows and lashes often thin, lighten and change texture after fifty, but they can still frame the eyes beautifully. Find a soft shape based on your natural bone structure, avoid over-plucking and trim long hairs rather than removing them. Fill sparse areas with light, hair-like strokes in a soft shade, set them with gel, and consider professional tinting after a patch test. Choose a lengthening or tubing mascara in a gentle shade, remove makeup carefully every night, and speak to a doctor if hair loss is sudden or patchy.
