A trip with grandchildren can create memories that last a lifetime, for them and for you. Many adults remember a holiday with a grandmother as one of the happiest parts of childhood: the freedom, the treats, the stories, the feeling of being special. For grandparents, travelling with grandchildren is a chance to share passions, see the world through young eyes and build a relationship that belongs only to you. It also brings responsibilities and practical challenges, from documents and safety to energy levels and the inevitable tantrum at the airport.

This guide explains how to plan a trip with grandchildren that works for everyone: agreeing the details with parents, choosing destinations and activities for different ages, sorting out documents and consent, planning the journey, pacing the days, keeping everyone safe and healthy, setting a budget, and looking after your own energy along the way.

## Start with the parents

Before anything else, talk to the children's parents. Agree on:

- **the dates, destination and length** of the trip;
- **the rules**: bedtimes, screen time, food, money and treats;
- **health information**: allergies, medications, medical conditions, vaccination requirements for the destination;
- **emergency plans**: who to call, what to do if a child is ill or injured;
- **how often to keep in touch**, and how.

Respect their parenting choices, even if they differ from how you raised your own children. A trip that leaves parents anxious or undermined will not feel like a success.

## Choosing a destination by age

Match the trip to the children's ages and interests, and to your own energy.

| Age | Ideas that often work | Things to consider |
|---|---|---|
| Toddlers and pre-school | a cottage near a beach or farm, a short journey, simple routines | naps, nappies, buggies, early nights |
| Primary school | nature parks, zoos, beaches, science museums, gentle walks, short city breaks | interests, energy, a mix of active and quiet times |
| Pre-teens | adventure activities, historic sites with stories, cooking classes, cycling | giving them some choice and responsibility |
| Teenagers | cities, cultural trips, activity holidays, a trip built around a shared passion | independence, privacy, phone use, their own interests |

Shorter trips closer to home are a good start, especially if it is your first trip together without parents.

## Involve the grandchildren

Children enjoy trips more when they help plan them:

- **let them choose** one activity or place;
- **look at maps and photos** together before you go;
- **give them a small job**: carrying the snacks, keeping the tickets, taking photos;
- **create a travel journal** or scrapbook for them to fill.

## Documents and consent

Travelling with children who are not your own may require extra paperwork, especially across borders.

- **Passports or identity cards:** check that each child's document is valid for the whole trip and meets the destination's entry rules.
- **Consent letters:** many countries and airlines recommend or require a letter from the parents authorising you to travel with the children, sometimes signed in a particular way. Check the rules of the destination and the countries you pass through, as well as your airline.
- **Birth certificates** or other proof of relationship may be requested in some cases.
- **Medical consent:** a signed letter from the parents allowing you to arrange medical treatment if needed.
- **Travel insurance** that covers everyone, including any pre-existing conditions, which you should declare.
- **Copies of all documents**, stored separately from the originals and digitally.

Official government travel advice websites provide information about entry requirements and documents.

## Planning the journey

The journey is often the most challenging part of a trip with children.

- **Choose shorter, direct journeys** where possible.
- **Travel at times that suit their routines**, such as after a nap for toddlers.
- **Pack a "journey bag"** with snacks, water, wipes, a change of clothes, small toys, books, colouring and headphones.
- **Build in breaks** on long car journeys, every couple of hours.
- **Arrive early** at airports and stations to avoid rushing.
- **Use car seats** that are correct for each child's age and size, and fitted properly, following local laws.

## Pacing the days

Children and grandparents both need a balance of activity and rest.

- **Plan one main activity a day**, with free time around it.
- **Keep some routines**, such as mealtimes and bedtimes, which help children feel secure.
- **Include quiet time** after lunch: a rest, reading or a film.
- **Mix active and calm activities**: a morning at the beach, an afternoon of drawing.
- **Have rainy-day options** ready.
- **Allow for boredom**; it often leads to imaginative play.

## Safety and health

- **Keep children close in crowded places** and agree on what to do if anyone gets separated: a meeting point, a card in their pocket with your phone number and accommodation address.
- **Check accommodation for hazards**: balconies, pools, stairs, windows, medicines and cleaning products.
- **Supervise closely near water**, at all times.
- **Protect from the sun** with hats, sunscreen, shade and water. Our guide to [everyday sun protection](/journal/everyday-sun-protection/) explains the basics.
- **Carry a small first-aid kit** and any medications, with dosage instructions from the parents.
- **Know how to contact local emergency services** and the nearest medical centre.
- **Keep food safe**, especially in hot climates, and offer plenty of water.

## Money and budgets

- **Agree with parents** who pays for what, including travel, accommodation and spending money.
- **Set a daily budget** for treats and souvenirs, and explain it to the children.
- **Look for family discounts** at attractions and on transport.
- **Choose self-catering accommodation** for some meals, which saves money and suits children's tastes.
- **Give older children a small amount of spending money**, which teaches budgeting and avoids constant requests.

## Looking after yourself

Caring for grandchildren full-time on holiday can be tiring.

- **Be honest about your energy** when planning.
- **Travel with another adult** if possible, such as a partner, friend or older grandchild.
- **Build in rest for yourself**, such as an early night or a quiet coffee while children have screen time.
- **Keep up your own medication and routines.**
- **Lower your expectations of perfection**; the memories that last are often the unplanned ones.

## Handling tears, tantrums and tired evenings

Even the happiest trips have difficult moments. Children who are tired, hungry, overstimulated or missing their parents may become tearful or cross, often at the least convenient time. A few approaches help:

- **look for the cause first**: many meltdowns disappear with a snack, a drink, a rest or a quieter place;
- **stay calm and kind**, keeping your voice low and your words simple;
- **follow the parents' approach** to behaviour, so children receive consistent messages;
- **offer choices** rather than commands, for example "Would you like to walk or ride in the buggy?";
- **plan the end of the day gently**, with a bath, a story and an early night after busy days;
- **forgive yourself and them**: one bad afternoon does not spoil a holiday.

Older children and teenagers can feel bored or frustrated too. Giving them some independence within agreed limits, such as choosing an activity or exploring a safe area with a sibling, often eases tension.

## Keeping parents in the loop

- **Send a quick message or photo** each day, as agreed.
- **Arrange calls** at times that do not disrupt routines or make homesick children sadder.
- **Share any concerns** promptly.

## After the trip

- **Make a photo book or scrapbook** together.
- **Share stories** with the rest of the family.
- **Talk about what everyone enjoyed most**, and what you might do differently next time.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a consent letter to travel with my grandchildren?

Often it is strongly recommended, and sometimes required, especially when crossing borders. Check the rules of the destination, any transit countries and your airline.

### What is a good first trip with grandchildren?

A short trip close to home, with simple activities and comfortable accommodation, is often ideal before attempting longer journeys.

### How do I handle homesickness?

Keep routines familiar, bring a comfort item, keep calls with parents short and upbeat, and keep the child busy with enjoyable activities.

You will find more ideas for journeys that suit your pace in the [Travel & Discovery](/chapters/travel-and-discovery/) chapter.

## The bottom line

Travelling with grandchildren can create memories that last a lifetime when it is planned with care. Agree everything with the parents, from rules to health information and emergency plans; choose destinations and activities that suit each child's age; and involve the grandchildren in the planning. Check passports, consent letters, medical consent and travel insurance well in advance, plan the journey around children's routines and pace the days with one main activity, rest and flexibility. Keep everyone safe, agree budgets, keep parents in the loop, and look after your own energy so that you enjoy the trip as much as they do.
