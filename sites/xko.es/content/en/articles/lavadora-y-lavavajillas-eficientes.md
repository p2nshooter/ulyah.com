Washing machines and dishwashers are two of the most-used appliances in a family home. Together they can use 350 to 550 kWh a year, as well as water and detergent. Most of that energy is used not by the motor but by the **element that heats the water.** That is why the temperature and programme you choose weigh far more on consumption than the appliance itself.

This guide explains how much they use, why the eco programme is longer but more efficient, which temperatures are enough, how to load them properly, when to schedule them to pay less and what to look for when buying a new one.

## Where the energy goes

| Stage | Share of consumption in a typical wash |
|---|---|
| Heating the water | 70 to 90 % |
| Motor and pump | 10 to 20 % |
| Spinning (washing machine) or drying (dishwasher) | Varies |

A washing machine washing at 60 °C can use more than twice as much as at 30 °C. A dishwasher on an intensive programme at 70 °C uses considerably more than on eco.

## The eco programme: longer, but uses less

Many people avoid the eco programme because it takes three hours or more. But that is exactly why it uses less: it makes up with **more soaking and mechanical action** for what it saves in temperature. The water is heated less and enzyme-based detergents have longer to work.

European rules require the energy-label figures for washing machines and dishwashers to be measured on the eco programme, and that programme to be the default or easy to select. See [the energy label](/guias/etiqueta-energetica/).

| Dishwasher programme | Indicative consumption per cycle |
|---|---|
| Eco | 0.7 to 0.9 kWh |
| Normal or automatic | 1.0 to 1.3 kWh |
| Intensive | 1.4 to 1.8 kWh |
| Quick, 30 to 60 min | 0.8 to 1.2 kWh (with variable results) |

## Washing machine: temperature and programmes

### Which temperature to use

- **Cold or 20 °C:** lightly soiled clothes, delicate colours.
- **30 °C:** most everyday washes with today's detergents.
- **40 °C:** dirtier clothes, towels in normal use.
- **60 °C:** bedding and towels when you want more hygiene, clothes of people who are ill, tea towels.

Going down from 40 to 30 °C noticeably cuts the consumption of each wash. Modern detergents are formulated to work well at low temperatures.

### Tips for the washing machine

- **Full loads,** without overloading: fill the drum, leaving a hand's width at the top.
- **Use the eco 40-60 programme** for mixed loads: it washes normal cotton clothes at a lower effective temperature with good results.
- **Spin at high speed** if you will use a dryer afterwards: the clothes come out drier and the dryer uses less.
- **Dose the detergent** to the water hardness and the dirt; extra does not wash better.
- **Clean the filter** and run a hot maintenance wash now and then to prevent smells.

## Dishwasher: loading and use

### Dishwasher or by hand?

A modern dishwasher on eco usually uses less water and less energy than washing up by hand with the hot tap running. The key is to run it **with a full load** and **without rinsing the plates under hot water** before loading them.

### Tips for the dishwasher

- **Scrape off food scraps** with a spatula, without pre-rinsing in water.
- **Load it fully** following the manufacturer's layout: plates facing the centre, glasses at the top, without blocking the spray arms.
- **Use the eco programme** routinely and the intensive one only for very dirty loads.
- **Open the door at the end** so that the dishes dry with the residual heat, if your model does not do it automatically.
- **Clean the filter** every one or two weeks.
- **Use salt and rinse aid** in hard-water areas to avoid limescale on the element, which increases consumption.

## When to run them: use the cheap hours

Washing machines and dishwashers are the easiest loads to shift:

- **Time-of-use tariff or PVPC:** schedule them for the off-peak period, in the early hours or at the weekend. See [time-of-use periods](/guias/tramos-horarios-punta-llano-valle/).
- **Solar panels:** run them at midday to use your own energy. See [solar self-consumption](/guias/autoconsumo-solar-guia-completa/).
- **Precautions:** avoid night-time noise in flats with thin walls and do not leave wet clothes in the drum for hours; time the end of the cycle for when you get up.

## Hot-water fill

Some washing machines and dishwashers can be connected to the home's **hot-water** supply. It makes sense if the hot water comes from an efficient or free source, such as solar thermal or a heat pump. If it comes from an electric water heater, the advantage disappears. Check the manufacturer's instructions.

## The tumble dryer: the big user

If you use a dryer, it can use more than the washing machine:

- **A heat pump dryer:** uses less than half as much as a resistive one.
- **Spin well before drying.**
- **Clean the lint filter** every time.
- **Hang clothes out to dry** whenever you can.

## Choosing a new model

- **The right size:** a 9 kg washing machine run with half loads is less efficient than a full 7 kg one.
- **Consumption in kWh per 100 cycles** on the label, not just the letter.
- **Water per cycle.**
- **Noise,** if it is near living areas.
- **Useful features:** delayed start, automatic detergent dosing, load sensing, and auto-open drying on dishwashers.
- **Length of the eco programme:** to see whether it fits your routine.

## How much you can save

| Measure | Indicative annual saving |
|---|---|
| Washing at 30 °C instead of 40 °C (4 washes a week) | 40 to 60 kWh |
| Using the dishwasher's eco programme instead of normal | 60 to 100 kWh |
| Not pre-rinsing plates in hot water | Water and energy from the water heater or boiler |
| Scheduling for off-peak or sunny hours | 15 to 40 % of these appliances' cost |
| A heat pump dryer instead of a resistive one | 150 to 300 kWh |

## Case study: a family of five

A family of five in Seville did 7 washes a week at 40 °C, always on the cotton programme, and ran the dishwasher every day on normal after rinsing the plates with hot water from the water heater.

The changes they made:

1. Washes at 30 °C or on eco 40-60, with 60 °C only for towels and bedding every two weeks.
2. The dishwasher on eco, without rinsing in hot water, programmed to finish at 7:00.
3. The washing machine scheduled for weekends and the early hours.

Estimated result after a year: about 180 kWh less between the two appliances, about 2,500 litres less hot water from the water heater, and most cycles moved to off-peak hours. The total saving was around €80 to €100 a year without buying anything.

## Checklist

- Use the eco programme by default.
- Wash everyday clothes at 30 °C.
- Load both appliances fully.
- Do not rinse plates in hot water.
- Clean the filters regularly.
- Schedule cycles for cheap or sunny hours.

## Dishwasher or washing up by hand?

It is one of the most common questions. A modern dishwasher, full and on eco, usually uses less water than washing the same dishes by hand with the tap running, and its electricity consumption is moderate if intensive programmes are not overused. The advantage is lost if plates are rinsed in hot water before loading or if it is run half full. It is enough to remove food scraps with a spatula or a napkin: today's detergents are designed for normal dirt.

If you wash up by hand, use two sinks or bowls, one with soapy water and one for rinsing, instead of leaving the hot tap running throughout.

## Repair before you replace

When a washing machine or dishwasher breaks down, it does not always need replacing. Many common faults, such as a blocked drain pump, a worn door seal, a clogged filter or a scaled-up element, can be fixed at a reasonable cost. To decide:

- **Consider the appliance's age** and efficiency: a very old, inefficient one may not justify an expensive repair.
- **Ask for a written quote** before authorising the work.
- **Remember the legal guarantee:** if the appliance is less than three years old and was bought from 2022 onwards, the fault may be covered.
- **Ask about parts:** European ecodesign rules require spare parts to be kept available for years for these appliances.

Repairing extends the appliance's life and avoids the energy and materials needed to make a new one.

## Common mistakes

- **Always washing at 60 °C** out of habit.
- **Running the dishwasher half full** or rinsing first in hot water.
- **Overusing quick programmes,** which tend to use more energy.
- **Using too much detergent.**
- **Using the dryer** when you could hang clothes out.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does washing cold disinfect?

For everyday clothes, modern detergents clean well at low temperatures. Where special hygiene is needed, use 60 °C.

### Why does the quick programme not save?

To clean in less time, it usually heats the water more or uses more water. For lightly soiled loads it can be reasonable, but it does not always save compared with eco.

### Is it bad to leave the washing machine programmed overnight?

No, as long as the installation is in good condition and the water inlet does not leak. Check the hoses from time to time.

### How often should the filters be cleaned?

The washing machine's every few months; the dishwasher's every one or two weeks; the dryer's lint filter every time you use it.

## In short

Most of what washing machines and dishwashers use goes on heating water. Use the eco programme even though it takes longer, wash clothes at 30 °C unless you need more, load the appliances fully without pre-rinsing in hot water, keep the filters clean and schedule them for cheap or sunny hours. If you use a dryer, a heat pump model halves its consumption.
