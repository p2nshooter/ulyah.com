The electricity bill is one of the most important documents in household finances and, at the same time, one of the least understood. Many people look only at the total and the payment date, but those two or three pages hold all the information you need to spend less: how much power you have contracted, how much energy you use and at what times, what price you pay for each kilowatt-hour, and which part of the amount is taxes that do not depend on you.

In this guide we go through the bill from top to bottom, using the layout every retailer in Spain uses for domestic contracts of up to 15 kW, the so-called 2.0TD access tariff.

## The two big parts: power and energy

The whole cost of electricity is split into two blocks that are worth telling apart from the start:

| Item | What you pay for | Does it depend on what you use? |
|---|---|---|
| Power term | The capacity you have available, in kilowatts (kW), for each day of the period | No: you pay it even if you switch nothing on |
| Energy term | The electricity you actually use, in kilowatt-hours (kWh) | Yes: the more you use, the more you pay |

Think of power as the width of the pipe and energy as the water that flows through it. You pay for the pipe every month even with the tap closed; the water only when you use it.

This distinction is the key to saving. There are two different routes: adjusting your contracted power, which cuts the fixed part, and reducing or shifting your consumption, which cuts the variable part. We explain this in detail in our guide to [the power term and the energy term](/guias/termino-de-potencia-y-termino-de-energia/).

## The contract details

On the first page, or in a box of contract details, you will find information worth keeping to hand:

- **Holder and supply address.** The person who signed the contract and the home it relates to.
- **CUPS.** The Universal Supply Point Code (*Código Universal del Punto de Suministro*), a string of about twenty letters and numbers beginning with ES. It identifies your installation uniquely and does not change when you change company. You will need it to switch retailer or to ask the distribution company for information.
- **Retailer.** The company that sells you the energy and sends you the bill.
- **Distribution company.** The company that owns the cables and the meter in your area. You do not choose it: it depends on where you live. It is the one to call if there is a power cut.
- **Contracted power.** Expressed in kW, usually with two values: one for the peak and mid-peak period (P1) and one for the off-peak period (P2).
- **Type of contract.** Whether you are on the regulated market (PVPC) or the free market, and whether the price is fixed, time-banded or indexed.
- **Contract end date and tie-in period,** if there is one.

## The billing period and the readings

The bill shows the period it covers, usually a month, and the number of days. It also shows the meter readings at the start and end of the period, broken down by time-of-use period.

If the readings are **actual**, the smart meter has sent them to the distribution company. If they are shown as **estimated**, the company has calculated your consumption from your history. Estimates are corrected later with the next actual reading, but keep an eye on them: an estimate that is too high brings payments forward, and one that is too low can lead to a large catch-up bill later.

## The time-of-use periods

Since June 2021, energy on domestic contracts has been measured in three periods according to the time of day:

| Period | Hours on working days (mainland) |
|---|---|
| Peak (P1) | 10:00 to 14:00 and 18:00 to 22:00 |
| Mid-peak (P2) | 08:00 to 10:00, 14:00 to 18:00 and 22:00 to 24:00 |
| Off-peak (P3) | 00:00 to 08:00 |

On Saturdays, Sundays and national holidays the whole day is off-peak. The bill shows how many kWh you used in each period. If your contract has different prices per period, that split has a big influence on the amount. We explain it in the guide to [the peak, mid-peak and off-peak periods](/guias/tramos-horarios-punta-llano-valle/).

## The power term in detail

The calculation is simple: kilowatts contracted × number of days × price per kW per day. It is done separately for P1 and P2.

**Example:** with 4.6 kW in P1 and in P2, in a 30-day month:

- P1: 4.6 kW × 30 days × P1 price.
- P2: 4.6 kW × 30 days × P2 price.

The power price in P1 is a good deal higher than in P2, because it includes most of the system's network tolls and charges. As a guide, each contracted kilowatt costs in the order of €30 to €45 a year before taxes, depending on the retailer. That is why having more power contracted than you need is money lost every month.

## The energy term in detail

Here each kWh used is multiplied by its price. It can appear in two ways:

- **A single price** for every hour, typical of many fixed free-market offers.
- **Three prices,** one per period, in time-of-use offers and in the PVPC, whose price also changes every hour.

On the PVPC you will often see the cost of energy split into network tolls and charges on one side and the cost of energy on the other. Do not be alarmed: the sum is what counts.

## Other items that appear

- **The special electricity tax.** A percentage applied to the sum of power and energy.
- **Rental of the metering equipment.** What you pay the distribution company for the meter. For a domestic digital meter it is usually less than a euro a month. If you own the meter, you do not pay it.
- **VAT.** It is applied to everything above.
- **The social discount or other discounts,** if you are entitled to them.
- **Add-on services.** Maintenance insurance, technical assistance or repair packages. Check them: many people pay for them without knowing they have them, and they are not compulsory.

We explain the taxes in more detail in [the taxes on the electricity bill](/guias/impuestos-de-la-factura-de-la-luz/).

## The consumption chart

Most bills include a chart of your consumption over recent months. It is a valuable tool:

- It lets you spot months with unusual consumption.
- It shows seasonality: if you use much more in winter or in summer, heating or cooling is your big item.
- It lets you compare with the average consumption of similar households, which some companies also show.

## Where to look for savings

With the bill in front of you, check these five points:

1. **Power:** does it match what you really need? If the switch never trips, you may have too much.
2. **Split by period:** what percentage do you use at peak times? Moving the washing machine, the dishwasher or charging the car to off-peak can save a lot with time-of-use prices.
3. **Energy price:** compare it with other offers on the CNMC comparison tool.
4. **Add-on services:** cancel any you do not use.
5. **Total consumption:** compare it with similar households and find out which appliances weigh most.

## A five-minute checklist

Every time a bill arrives, check these points in this order:

1. **Period and days billed:** compare bills covering the same number of days.
2. **Actual or estimated reading:** if it is estimated, note your meter reading.
3. **Total consumption and consumption by period:** is it similar to the same month last year?
4. **Contracted power in P1 and P2:** is it what you need?
5. **Power and energy prices:** do they match your contract? Has a discount ended?
6. **Add-on services:** are you paying for something you do not use?
7. **Total with taxes and payment method.**

If something does not add up, see [how to complain about an electricity bill](/guias/como-reclamar-una-factura-de-la-luz/).

## Keep a simple record

A spreadsheet with four columns — month, kWh used, contracted power and total amount — lets you see at a glance how your consumption develops, spot price rises and measure the effect of every change you make at home. After a year you will have your own reference consumption, far more useful than any national average.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do I pay even when I have not been at home?

Because the power term, the meter rental and the related taxes are charged by the day, whatever your consumption.

### Where can I see my consumption by hour?

In your distribution company's customer area, which has the hourly data from your smart meter. You can download it free of charge with your CUPS.

### Is the gas bill read the same way?

It follows similar logic, with a fixed term and a variable term, but consumption is measured in cubic metres that are converted into kWh. The taxes and tolls are different.

## In short

The electricity bill has two big parts: power, which you pay even if you use nothing, and energy, which depends on what you use and when you use it. On top come the electricity tax, the meter rental and VAT. Identify your CUPS, your power, your split by period and your price per kWh, and you will have everything you need to compare offers and start saving.
