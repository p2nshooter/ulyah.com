Switching electricity company in Spain is free, needs no building work or new meter and does not interrupt your supply. Even so, many households stay with the same retailer and the same offer for years, often paying more than they need to because a discount ended or the price rose on renewal. Others switch after a sales call without comparing and end up with a worse contract.

This guide explains how to switch retailer step by step, which details you need, how to compare sensibly, what to check before signing and how to protect yourself from misleading sales practices.

## What does not change when you switch

Before you start, it helps to be clear about what stays the same:

- **The distribution company:** the company that owns the networks in your area stays the same. It is responsible for the physical supply, the meter and faults.
- **The meter:** it is not touched.
- **The quality of supply:** the electricity that reaches your home is exactly the same.
- **The CUPS:** the code that identifies your supply point does not change.

The only thing that changes is the company that sells you the energy and sends you the bill, and with it the prices and conditions.

## What you need

- **The CUPS:** a code of about 20 to 22 characters beginning with "ES", shown on your bill.
- **The account holder's details:** name, DNI or NIE, supply address.
- **A bank account** for direct debits.
- **The contracted power** you want to keep or change.
- **A recent bill,** so that you have your current consumption and prices to hand.

If the contract is not in your name — in a rented home, for example — you can ask for a change of holder at the same time, with whatever documents the retailer requests.

## Step 1: know your real consumption

Do not choose an offer on the price per kWh alone. You need:

- **Annual consumption in kWh,** ideally split by period (peak, mid-peak, off-peak).
- **Contracted power in P1 and P2.**
- **Your hourly pattern:** whether you use more at night, at midday or in the evening.

You can download your hourly consumption from your distribution company's website with your CUPS. See [estimated readings and smart meters](/guias/lecturas-estimadas-y-contador-inteligente/).

## Step 2: compare offers with your consumption

### Tools

- **The CNMC offer comparison tool** (the National Markets and Competition Commission): you enter your consumption and power and see the estimated annual cost of the registered offers.
- **Private comparison sites:** useful, but check whether they show every offer or only those of companies they work with.
- **Your own spreadsheet:** multiply your consumption in each period by each offer's prices, add the power charge and add taxes.

### What to compare

| Item | What to look at |
|---|---|
| Energy price | Per kWh, by period if there is time-of-use pricing |
| Power price | Per kW per day or per kW per year; some charge more than the regulated rate |
| How long the price lasts | Fixed for 12 months? Only for the first few months? |
| Discounts | Permanent or temporary? Conditional? |
| Add-on services | Maintenance, insurance: compulsory or optional? |
| Tie-in period | Is there a penalty for leaving early? |
| Surplus compensation | If you have self-consumption |

To understand the differences between the regulated price and the free market, see [PVPC or the free market](/guias/pvpc-o-mercado-libre/).

### Your comparison in a spreadsheet, step by step

1. **Note your figures:** annual kWh in peak, mid-peak and off-peak, and kW contracted in P1 and P2.
2. **Open a column for each offer** with its prices. If power is given in €/kW per year, divide by 365 to get €/kW per day.
3. **Energy:** multiply each period's kWh by its price and add them up. If the offer has a single price, use total consumption.
4. **Power:** kW in P1 × P1 price × 365, plus the same for P2.
5. **Services you cannot remove:** add their annual cost.
6. **If the price changes mid-year,** split consumption between the months of each price.
7. **Taxes:** apply the current electricity tax and VAT to every offer. See [taxes on the electricity bill](/guias/impuestos-de-la-factura-de-la-luz/).

Meter rental is a regulated amount that does not depend on the retailer, so it does not change the result of the comparison.

## Step 3: check the contract before signing

- **Specific conditions:** exact prices, power, duration and start date.
- **General conditions:** renewal, price changes, penalties.
- **Additional services:** untick any you do not want.
- **Billing:** monthly or every two months, electronic or paper.
- **Correct details:** CUPS, holder, address and account.

Keep a copy of the contract and the offer.

## Step 4: sign up

You can sign up online, by phone or at an office of the new retailer. The new company:

1. Requests the switch from the distribution company.
2. The distribution company makes the change in its systems, usually with a meter reading on a particular date.
3. The previous retailer sends a **final bill** up to the switch date.
4. The new retailer starts billing you from that date.

**You do not have to cancel the old contract:** the new retailer handles it. Cancelling it yourself could cut off your supply.

### How long it takes

The switch is usually completed within a few days or, at most, a few weeks. With remotely read meters it can be very quick.

### Right of withdrawal

If you sign up at a distance (by phone or online) or away from business premises, you have **14 calendar days** to withdraw without penalty.

## Step 5: check the first bills

- **The old company's final bill:** the period should end on the switch date.
- **The new company's first bill:** it should start on that date, with no overlap.
- **Prices and power:** they should match the contract.
- **Services:** no services you did not sign up for should appear.

## How to protect yourself from misleading sales

Aggressive sales practices in the electricity sector have been the subject of many complaints. Some warning signs:

- **Calls or visits** claiming to come "on behalf of your company" or "from the electricity company" for a supposed inspection, a compulsory price cut or a meter change.
- **Requests for your CUPS or a bill** without a clear explanation of which company it is and what it is offering.
- **Pressure to sign** on the spot.
- **Promises of savings** without showing the prices in writing.

### What to do

- **Do not give your CUPS, your DNI or your bank account** to anyone who does not identify themselves clearly.
- **Ask for the offer in writing** and compare it calmly.
- **Check who is calling:** hang up and ring your company's official number yourself.
- If you are switched without your consent, **complain** and ask for it to be reversed, to the company and to the consumer authorities. See [how to complain about an electricity bill](/guias/como-reclamar-una-factura-de-la-luz/).
- You can register on the **Lista Robinson** opt-out list to reduce sales calls, and the rules limit unsolicited commercial calls.

## Special cases

### The social discount

To get the social discount you must be on the PVPC with a reference retailer. If you move to the free market, you lose it. See [the electricity social discount](/guias/bono-social-electrico/).

### Self-consumption

If you have solar panels, check how each retailer handles surplus compensation and what happens to your virtual-battery balance when you switch. See [surplus compensation](/guias/compensacion-de-excedentes/).

### Electricity and gas together

Some combined offers include discounts, but check that each supply is still competitive on its own.

### Moving house and homes without an active contract

- **If the home has power** in the previous occupant's name, what you need is a change of holder, and you can use it to choose your retailer. Note the meter reading on the day you get the keys.
- **If the supply has been cancelled,** a new connection is needed, with regulated charges collected by the distribution company. If the installation is old or has been out of service for a while, an electrical installation certificate from an authorised installer may be required.
- **When you leave a home,** ask for the supply to be cancelled or the holder changed; otherwise you will remain liable for whatever the next occupant uses. In a rental, agree it with the landlord.

## When to review your contract again

- **When the fixed price or the discount ends:** put the date in your calendar on the day you sign.
- **When you are told of a change in conditions:** the retailer must notify contract changes in advance, and that notice lets you end the contract without penalty. Compare before the new price takes effect.
- **When the way you use energy changes:** an electric car, working from home, solar panels or a heat pump change which offer suits you. See [tariffs for electric cars](/guias/tarifa-para-coche-electrico/).
- **Once a year,** even if nothing changes: repeat the comparison with the last twelve months' consumption.

## Case study: comparing three offers with real data

A household uses 3,400 kWh a year: 30 % in peak, 30 % in mid-peak and 40 % in off-peak, with 4.6 kW contracted. It compares three offers, with indicative prices before taxes:

| Item | Offer A (single price) | Offer B (three periods) | Offer C (single price, 6-month discount) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Energy | €0.13/kWh | Peak 0.18 / Mid 0.12 / Off-peak 0.08 €/kWh | €0.11/kWh for the first 6 months, then 0.15 |
| P1 power | €0.09/kW per day | €0.08/kW per day | €0.11/kW per day |
| Annual energy cost | €442 | €415 | €442 |
| Annual P1 power cost | €151 | €134 | €185 |
| **Total before taxes** | **€593** | **€549** | **€627** |

Offer C looked the cheapest from the advert, but after the six months and with dearer power it turns out to be the worst. Offer B wins because 40 % of this household's consumption is off-peak. With a different pattern — say, a lot of consumption at peak times — the result would change.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will I be left without power during the switch?

No. The physical supply is not interrupted.

### How many times can I switch?

As many as you like, as long as you respect any tie-in periods in your contracts.

### Am I charged for switching?

Switching retailer is free. There can only be penalties if your contract has a tie-in period and you break it.

### Do I have to change my power too?

It is not compulsory, but it is a good moment to review it. See [how much power to contract](/guias/que-potencia-contratar/).

## In short

Switching electricity company is free, quick and does not affect your supply. Know your real consumption, compare offers with your own figures and with taxes, check the duration, tie-in period and add-on services, and sign up with the new company, which handles leaving the old one. Check the first bills, and do not give your details to anyone who does not identify themselves clearly.
