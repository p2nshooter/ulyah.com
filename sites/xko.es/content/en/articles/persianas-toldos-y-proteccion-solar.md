In much of Spain, summer has become the season that weighs most on many households' electricity bills. Heatwaves are more frequent and last longer, and more and more homes rely on air conditioning for weeks on end. Yet a large part of the heat that gets into a home comes in through the windows as direct solar radiation, and it can be stopped before it gets inside with simple, cheap solutions.

This guide explains why external sun protection is so effective, which solutions suit each façade's orientation, how to use them through the day and how to combine them with night-time ventilation to cut your use of air conditioning.

## Why sun on the windows heats so much

When solar radiation passes through glass, it warms the floors, furniture and walls inside, which in turn give off heat that can no longer easily escape through the glass. This is the so-called greenhouse effect. A single large west-facing window can let in as much heat on a summer afternoon as an electric radiator left on for hours.

### Outside versus inside

The golden rule is simple: **it is far more effective to stop the sun before the glass than behind it.**

| Protection | Approximate share of solar heat it keeps out |
|---|---|
| External awning, shutter or window shutter | 70 to 90 % |
| Solar control film on the glass | 30 to 60 % |
| Light-coloured interior curtain or blind | 20 to 40 % |
| Dark interior curtain | Less than 20 % |

An interior curtain stops the light, but the heat is already in the room. An external shutter that is down stops it outside.

## External sun protection solutions

### Roller shutters

These are the most common in Spain. Used well, they are very effective.

- **Adjustable-slat shutters or insulated aluminium shutters:** they let you control the light and insulate well.
- **The key trick:** lower the shutter leaving the gaps between the slats slightly open, so that a little light gets in and air circulates between the shutter and the glass. That cuts the heat without leaving the room in darkness.
- **Motorised shutters with sensors:** they can be scheduled by the hour or lower automatically with sun sensors.

### Awnings

- **Folding-arm awnings:** for terraces and large windows, especially facing south.
- **Vertical drop awnings:** very effective on east and west façades, where the sun comes in low.
- **Drop-arm awnings:** for balconies and windows.

Light-coloured fabrics reflect more heat, and micro-perforated or screen fabrics let you see out and let in some light while blocking much of the radiation.

### Window shutters and lattices

Traditional in many Mediterranean regions, they are extremely effective. Lattices let you ventilate with the sun blocked.

### Eaves, overhangs and pergolas

An eave or a pergola over a south-facing window blocks the high summer sun and lets in the low winter sun. Pergolas with deciduous climbers, such as a vine, work in a similar way: shade in summer and sun in winter.

### Plants

Deciduous trees in front of south and west façades, climbers on walls and pergolas, or planters on balconies lower the temperature of the surroundings and of surfaces.

## What to do for each orientation

| Orientation | When the sun reaches it | Best protection |
|---|---|---|
| South | Midday, high sun in summer | Eaves, folding-arm awnings, shutters |
| East | Morning, low sun | Shutters, vertical awnings, window shutters |
| West | Afternoon, low and very hot sun | Vertical awnings, shutters, window shutters, plants |
| North | Little direct sun | Usually needs no protection |

The **west** façade is the most problematic in summer: it gets low sun in the hottest hours of the day, when the house is already warm. Protect it first.

## Solar control film

Adhesive films applied to the glass reduce the solar heat coming in. They are a good option when external protection cannot be fitted, for example in buildings with strict façade rules.

- **External films:** more effective, but they degrade sooner.
- **Internal films:** more durable; some also reduce heat loss in winter (low-emissivity).
- **Caution:** on some double glazing, certain dark films can overheat the glass. Check the manufacturer's recommendations.
- **Drawback:** they also reduce the benefit of the sun in winter.

## A summer routine for the home

Combining sun protection and ventilation is the most effective way to keep the house cool:

1. **At night and first thing in the morning:** open windows on opposite façades to create a through-draught and cool walls and furniture with the cooler air. This is **night-time ventilation.**
2. **When the sun reaches each façade:** close the windows and lower the shutters or roll out the awnings on that side. East in the morning, south at midday, west in the afternoon.
3. **In the middle of the day:** keep windows shut if it is hotter outside than inside.
4. **At dusk:** when the outside temperature drops below the inside one, open up again.

This routine can keep the home several degrees cooler than leaving the shutters up and the windows open at midday.

## Fans: the cheapest companion

A fan does not cool the air in the room, but air moving over your skin makes you feel cooler. That is why, combined with good sun protection, it lets you get through many days without air conditioning, and when you do use it, lets you raise the setting by a couple of degrees without losing comfort.

- **They use very little** compared with an air-conditioning unit.
- **They cool people, not rooms:** switch them off when you leave the room.
- **Ceiling fans:** in summer they should push air downwards; many models have a switch to change the direction of rotation with the season.
- **At night,** a fan next to an open window helps draw in cool outside air and speeds up night-time ventilation.
- **Clean the blades** at the start of the season: accumulated dust reduces their performance and worsens the air.

During heatwaves, keeping at least one room in the house well shaded from the sun and ventilated at night is also a simple measure that helps the most vulnerable — babies, older people or anyone who is ill — to rest better.

## How much it saves on air conditioning

By reducing the heat coming in, the air conditioning needs to run for fewer hours and at lower power. In homes with large unprotected windows facing west or south, external sun protection can cut cooling consumption very significantly, in many cases by 20 to 40 %, and on other occasions make it unnecessary to switch the unit on at all on moderate days.

A house protected from the sun also stays more bearable if the air conditioning is not available or if there is a power cut, which matters more and more in heatwaves.

| Drop-arm awning for a window | €150 to €400 |
| Vertical awning | €150 to €500 |

In blocks of flats, external protection may need approval to keep the look of the façade. Check your building's statutes and rules.

## Common mistakes

- **Lowering the shutter fully,** leaving the room dark and turning the lights on, when leaving the slats slightly open is enough.
- **Opening the windows at midday** "to let the air through", which lets in air hotter than the air inside.
- **Relying only on interior curtains.**
- **Forgetting the west façade.**
- **Not retracting awnings in strong wind,** which can damage them.

## Case study: a west-facing top-floor flat

A top-floor flat in Madrid with a large west-facing window reached 31 °C on July afternoons with the air conditioning running non-stop. The family fitted a vertical screen-fabric awning and started ventilating in the early hours.

Results the following summer:

- The indoor temperature at 19:00 fell by about 3 to 4 °C on the hottest days.
- The air conditioning went from running almost all afternoon to a few hours, set to 26 °C.
- Cooling consumption in July and August fell by around a third.

The awning, about €400 installed, paid for itself within a few summers on energy alone, not counting the improvement in comfort.

## Summer checklist

- Find the windows that get direct sun at each time of day.
- Put external protection first on the west and south façades.
- Lower the shutters with the slats slightly open during the sunny hours.
- Ventilate at night and first thing in the morning.
- Close the windows when it is hotter outside than inside.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a light or a dark shutter better?

Light-coloured shutters and awnings reflect more radiation and heat up less.

### Are interior blinds any use?

A little, especially those with reflective fabric on the side facing the glass, but they are far less effective than external protection.

### When should I ventilate in summer?

When the outside temperature is lower than the inside one, usually at night and first thing in the morning.

### Is sun protection any use in winter?

In winter you want the opposite: let the sun in on south façades during the day and lower the shutters at night to cut heat loss.

## In short

Stopping the sun before it passes through the glass is the cheapest and most effective way to keep the house cool. Use shutters with the slats slightly open, vertical awnings on east and west façades, eaves or awnings on the south side and plants wherever possible. Combine that with night-time ventilation and closed windows in the hot hours. You will use the air conditioning less and gain comfort, even in heatwaves.
