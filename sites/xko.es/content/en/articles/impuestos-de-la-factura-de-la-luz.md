When you look at the total on an electricity bill, it is easy to forget that a large part of it is neither power nor energy used, but taxes and regulated items. In a typical household these add up to more than a quarter of the final amount. Understanding how they are calculated helps you read the bill, compare offers honestly and avoid being taken in by adverts showing prices "before taxes" as if they were the real cost.

This guide explains each item that appears below power and energy: the special electricity tax, the meter rental, VAT and other less common charges. It also works through a complete example and makes clear which part you can reduce and which you cannot.

## The order in which a bill is calculated

Spanish electricity bills always follow the same sequence, although each retailer presents it in its own design:

1. **Power term:** the kilowatts contracted, times the days in the period, times the price of each kW.
2. **Energy term:** the kilowatt-hours used times their price, by period if your tariff distinguishes them.
3. **Discounts or surcharges** specific to the offer, if any.
4. **The special electricity tax:** a percentage of the sum of power and energy.
5. **Rental of the metering equipment:** the meter.
6. **Other services** you have signed up for, such as maintenance or insurance.
7. **VAT:** it is applied to everything above, including the electricity tax.

The order matters because VAT is also charged on the electricity tax and the meter rental. So every euro of power or energy carries a little over one euro and thirty cents into the total.

## The special electricity tax

The **special electricity tax** (*impuesto especial sobre la electricidad*, often shortened to IEE or simply "the electricity tax") is a national tax on the supply of electrical energy. It is calculated as a percentage of the sum of the power term and the energy term.

- **Standard rate:** 5.11269632 %. This is the percentage shown on most domestic bills when no temporary reductions are in force.
- **Minimum amount:** there is a minimum per kWh used, designed so that the tax does not disappear with very low consumption. In households with normal consumption it almost never applies, but it can show up in second homes.

During the 2021 to 2023 energy crisis this tax was temporarily reduced and then restored in stages. If you compare old bills with current ones, you may see different percentages for that reason. What does not change is how it is calculated: always on power plus energy.

### Why the electricity tax depends on you

Because it is a percentage, anything that reduces power or energy also reduces this tax. Lowering a kilowatt of power you do not need, or moving consumption to cheap hours, also saves the corresponding share of tax. To find out how much power you really need, see [how much power to contract](/guias/que-potencia-contratar/).

## The meter rental

The electricity meter normally belongs to the **distribution company** in your area, not to you or to the retailer. You pay a regulated rental for using it, which appears as "rental of metering equipment" or similar.

- For a domestic single-phase digital meter, the rental is around **€0.80 a month,** that is, about €10 a year before VAT.
- Three-phase meters or ones with special features have a somewhat higher rental.
- The amount is the same with every retailer, because it is set by regulation.

### Can you buy the meter?

The rules allow consumers to own their meter, as long as it meets the technical requirements and has been verified. In practice almost nobody does: the saving is small and you become responsible for maintenance and verification. For most households the regulated rental is the sensible option.

### Check that you are not paying twice

Some older bills carried charges for meters that no longer existed or for duplicate equipment. If you see a rental much higher than usual, compare with your previous bills and ask the distribution company. You can see how to complain in [how to complain about an electricity bill](/guias/como-reclamar-una-factura-de-la-luz/).

## VAT on electricity

**VAT** is applied at the end, on the sum of all the items before it: power, energy, electricity tax, meter rental and other services.

- **Standard rate:** 21 %.
- During the years of high prices, the government applied temporary reduced rates for lower-power domestic supplies. Those reductions had end dates and have been phased out.
- In the **Canary Islands** VAT does not apply; instead there is IGIC, with its own rates for domestic electricity, and in **Ceuta and Melilla** IPSI applies.

Because VAT is the last step, it multiplies the effect of every other item. That is why offers must always be compared including taxes.

## Other items that may appear

### Add-on services

Many retailers offer electrical maintenance services, emergency call-outs or appliance insurance. They are voluntary. Check whether you have any, how much they cost a year and whether you have ever used them. Cancelling them is usually one of the quickest ways to bring the bill down.

### The social discount and its financing

If you receive the social discount, you will see a discount on power and energy. If you do not, you may see a small item for financing the social discount, which is the contribution the rules assign to retailers and which some pass on explicitly on the bill. More in [the electricity social discount](/guias/bono-social-electrico/).

### Excess power and reactive energy

On domestic supplies with a smart meter, the meter itself cuts the supply if you exceed your power, so no excess charges are made. Reactive energy is only billed on higher-power supplies, usually businesses. If you see these items on a home's bill, it is worth asking.

### Temporary mechanisms

Between 2022 and 2024 there was an item on the regulated market linked to the so-called "gas price cap". If you look at bills from that time you will see amounts that no longer appear. It is not a permanent item on the bill.

## A complete example

Imagine a home with 4.6 kW contracted, using 280 kWh in a 30-day month, on a free-market offer. The figures are indicative:

| Item | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| P1 power | 4.6 kW × 30 days × €0.09/kW per day | €12.42 |
| P2 power | 4.6 kW × 30 days × €0.02/kW per day | €2.76 |
| Energy | 280 kWh × €0.13/kWh | €36.40 |
| Subtotal power + energy | | €51.58 |
| Electricity tax | €51.58 × 5.11269632 % | €2.64 |
| Meter rental | 30 days × €0.0266 | €0.80 |
| Taxable base | | €55.02 |
| VAT 21 % | €55.02 × 21 % | €11.55 |
| **Total** | | **€66.57** |

In this example, taxes and the meter come to about €15, almost a quarter of the total. If the power were lowered to 3.45 kW with nothing else changed, the annual saving would be several tens of euros, taxes included.

## Which part you can reduce

| Item | Does it depend on you? | How to reduce it |
|---|---|---|
| Power term | Yes | Match your power to what you really need |
| Energy term | Yes | Use less and move consumption to off-peak hours |
| Electricity tax | Indirectly | It falls when power and energy fall |
| Meter rental | No | It is regulated and the same for everyone |
| Add-on services | Yes | Cancel them if you do not use them |
| VAT | Indirectly | It falls when everything above falls |

The practical conclusion is simple: taxes cannot be negotiated, but they fall automatically when you lower power and energy. Saving measures pay a little more than they seem to at first sight.

## How to compare offers without traps

- **Always ask for the price including taxes,** or work out the total yourself with the electricity tax and VAT.
- **Compare with your real consumption,** not with the typical consumption in the advert. Your distribution company gives you access to your hourly data.
- **Look at power and energy separately.** An offer with cheap energy but expensive power can work out worse if you use little.
- **Be wary of temporary discounts** that disappear after a few months.
- **Check for add-on services** included by default.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the electricity tax the same as VAT?

No. The electricity tax is a special tax that only applies to electricity and is calculated on power and energy. VAT is the general consumption tax and is applied at the end to everything, including the electricity tax.

### Why do I pay tax on other taxes?

Because VAT rules include special taxes in the taxable base. The same happens with motor fuels.

### Do second homes pay more tax?

They pay the same rates, but with low consumption the power charge weighs much more in the total, and the minimum electricity tax may apply. Reviewing the power is the measure that saves most in these cases.

### Does changing retailer change the taxes?

No. The rates are the same with every retailer. What changes are the power and energy prices, and with them the base on which taxes are calculated.

## In short

Below power and energy, the bill includes the special electricity tax, the regulated meter rental and VAT, which is applied to everything before it. Together they make up around a quarter of the amount. They cannot be negotiated, but they fall by themselves when you reduce power and energy, which is why every saving measure pays a little more than it seems. Always compare offers including taxes and with your real consumption.
