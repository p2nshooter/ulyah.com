"Do I use a lot of electricity, or is this normal?" It is one of the most common questions when a high bill arrives. To answer it you need a reference: how many kilowatt-hours a year a household like yours uses. This guide brings together indicative figures for electricity consumption in Spain by household size, type of home and heating system, explains where a home's electricity goes, and helps you tell whether your consumption is reasonable and where to start cutting it.

The figures are indicative and come from consumption studies and practical experience. Every household is different: climate, appliances, routines and habits change the result a great deal.

## The average consumption of a Spanish household

An average household in Spain uses around **3,000 to 3,500 kWh of electricity a year,** although the figure varies widely. Households with electric heating or an electric car can double it, while a small flat with gas for cooking and hot water can stay below 2,000 kWh.

### By number of people

| People in the household | Indicative annual consumption (without electric heating) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1,500 to 2,200 kWh |
| 2 | 2,200 to 3,000 kWh |
| 3 | 2,800 to 3,600 kWh |
| 4 | 3,300 to 4,200 kWh |
| 5 or more | 4,000 to 5,000 kWh |

### By type of home and equipment

| Home | Indicative annual consumption |
|---|---|
| Flat with gas for hot water and cooking | 1,800 to 2,800 kWh |
| All-electric flat with water heater and electric cooker | 3,000 to 4,500 kWh |
| Detached house with air conditioning | 4,000 to 6,000 kWh |
| Home with electric radiators as main heating | 6,000 to 10,000 kWh or more |
| Home with a heat pump for heating and hot water | 4,500 to 8,000 kWh |
| Electric car (10,000 to 15,000 km a year) | Adds 1,500 to 2,500 kWh |

## Where the electricity goes

In an average household without electric heating, consumption is roughly shared out like this:

| Use | Indicative share |
|---|---|
| Fridge and freezer | 15 to 25 % |
| Hot water from an electric water heater (if any) | 20 to 30 % |
| Hob, oven and microwave | 10 to 15 % |
| Washing machine, dishwasher and dryer | 10 to 15 % |
| Cooling (air conditioning) | 5 to 20 % depending on the area |
| Lighting | 5 to 10 % |
| Television, computers, router and electronics | 10 to 15 % |
| Standby consumption | 5 to 10 % |

Several of these loads run 24 hours a day: the fridge, the router and appliances on standby. That is why even an empty home uses something every hour. See [standby consumption](/guias/consumo-en-standby/).

## How to tell whether your consumption is high

1. **Look at your annual consumption:** add up the kWh on a year's bills or download it from your distribution company's website. See [estimated readings and smart meters](/guias/lecturas-estimadas-y-contador-inteligente/).
2. **Compare it with the tables** for similar households.
3. **Look at the seasonal pattern:** if consumption shoots up in winter, electric heating or the water heater are the main candidates; if it rises in summer, the air conditioning.
4. **Look at the base load:** consumption in the early hours, when everyone is asleep, shows your constant load. An efficient home is usually at 50 to 150 W. Above 300 W, some appliance is using more than it should.

### Signs of excessive consumption

- An electric water heater keeping water at 70 °C around the clock.
- A very old fridge or freezer, or one whose door does not close properly.
- Low-efficiency portable air conditioners.
- Pool or water pumps running longer than necessary.
- Aquariums, terrariums or computer equipment left on permanently.
- Electric radiators with no thermostat or schedule.

## What each kWh costs

To turn consumption into euros, bear in mind that the total price per kWh, including the share of the power charge, the electricity tax and VAT, is usually between €0.18 and €0.30 depending on the tariff and consumption. A household using 3,000 kWh a year can pay, with taxes, somewhere between about €550 and €900 a year.

Remember that part of the bill is fixed: the power term and the meter rental. See [the power term and the energy term](/guias/termino-de-potencia-y-termino-de-energia/).

## Where to start cutting

Depending on your profile, the measures with the biggest impact are:

| If your high consumption comes from… | Start with… |
|---|---|
| Electric heating | Using a heat pump (the air conditioner) instead of radiators, insulating, scheduling |
| An electric water heater | Lowering the temperature, scheduling it for cheap hours, efficient shower heads |
| Air conditioning | Sun protection, 25 to 27 °C, maintenance |
| An old fridge | Considering replacing it with a high-class one |
| A high base load | Tracking down appliances left on and standby |
| High contracted power | Adjusting your contracted power |

See [how much power to contract](/guias/que-potencia-contratar/), [air conditioning: consumption and efficient use](/guias/aire-acondicionado-consumo-y-uso-eficiente/) and [hot water: electric water heaters and the alternatives](/guias/agua-caliente-termo-electrico-y-alternativas/).

## How to measure each appliance's consumption

- **A plug-in energy meter:** it goes between the socket and the appliance and shows the power and accumulated kWh. It is cheap and very useful for fridges, plug-in water heaters, computers and appliances on standby.
- **A monitor in the fuse board:** it shows total consumption in real time on your phone.
- **The energy label:** it shows the estimated annual consumption of appliances. See [the energy label](/guias/etiqueta-energetica/).

## Differences by climate zone

The climate changes electricity consumption a lot, especially in homes with electric heating or cooling:

| Area | Main feature | Effect on electricity consumption |
|---|---|---|
| The Cantabrian coast | Mild summers, damp winters | Little air conditioning; more dryer and dehumidifier use |
| The northern and central interior | Cold winters, hot summers | Peaks in winter if heating is electric and in summer from air conditioning |
| The Guadalquivir valley and the south-east | Very hot summers | Air conditioning weighs heavily from June to September |
| The Mediterranean coast | Mild winters, humid summers | Air conditioning and dehumidifying in summer |
| The Canary Islands | Mild temperatures all year | Steady consumption, little heating or cooling |

That is why comparing your consumption with a friend's in another region can lead you astray. Always compare with households in your area and with your own history.

## An example analysis

A couple in Madrid use 4,100 kWh a year in a flat with gas heating. According to the tables, a two-person household without electric heating is usually at 2,200 to 3,000 kWh. Looking at the distribution company's hourly data, they find:

- A constant 280 W in the early hours.
- Daily consumption peaks at 19:00 all winter.

Investigating, they discover an old electric water heater set to 70 °C that is never switched off and an electric fan heater in the home office. After lowering the water heater to 55 °C, scheduling it for off-peak hours and using the heat-pump air conditioner in the office, their consumption falls to about 3,100 kWh a year.

## How consumption changes over the life of a household

A home's consumption is not fixed: it changes with the lives of the people who live there, and every big change is a good moment to review your power and tariff.

| Change in the household | Usual effect on electricity |
|---|---|
| Working from home | More hours of computer, lighting and heating or cooling during the day |
| A new baby | More washes, tumble drying and often more heating or cooling |
| An electric car | One of the biggest possible increases, concentrated in charging |
| A heat pump instead of a gas boiler | Electricity consumption rises even though total energy spending falls |
| Solar panels | Less electricity bought from the grid, especially during the day |
| Children leaving home | Fewer washes, showers and meals, but the fixed appliances keep running |

When one of these changes happens, compare consumption in the following months with the previous year. If it has risen a lot, check whether your contracted power is still enough or more than you need, and whether your tariff still fits your routine. We explain this in [how much power to contract](/guias/que-potencia-contratar/) and [PVPC or the free market](/guias/pvpc-o-mercado-libre/).

## Common mistakes

- **Comparing yourself with the average** without considering whether your heating or hot water is electric.
- **Drawing conclusions from a single bill,** when consumption varies a lot between seasons.
- **Confusing kW with kWh:** power is not consumption.
- **Ignoring the base load** in the early hours, which gives away appliances left on for no reason.
- **Not reviewing the tariff** after a big change in the household.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it normal to use 300 kWh a month?

For a household of three or four without electric heating, it is a typical figure. For one person living alone, it would be high.

### Why do I use more in winter if I have no electric heating?

More hours of artificial light, more use of the oven and dryer, colder water for the water heater and, often, small heaters.

### Does a house use more than a flat?

Usually yes, because of the larger floor area, the exposure of walls and roof, and equipment such as pools or gardens.

### How much does an empty home use?

Between the fridge, the router and appliances on standby, an empty home can use 1 to 3 kWh a day.

## In short

An average household in Spain uses around 3,000 to 3,500 kWh of electricity a year, with big differences depending on the number of people, the type of home and, above all, whether heating, hot water or the car are electric. Compare your annual consumption with similar households, check your base load in the early hours and your seasonal pattern, measure the suspect appliances and start with the measures that tackle your biggest loads.
