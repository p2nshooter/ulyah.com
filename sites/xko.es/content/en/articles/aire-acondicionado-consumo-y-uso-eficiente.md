Air conditioning has gone from a luxury to a necessity in much of Spain. Heatwaves last longer, tropical nights are more frequent, and in many inland and southern cities it is hard to sleep in summer without cooling. At the same time, air conditioning can send the electricity bill soaring if it is used badly or if the unit is old and inefficient.

This guide explains how much an air conditioner really uses, how to read its efficiency, which temperature to set, which habits cut the cost, what maintenance it needs and what to look for when buying a new unit.

## How much an air conditioner uses

Consumption depends on the unit's output, its efficiency, the difference between indoor and outdoor temperature, the home's insulation and how long it runs.

A useful reference: a modern, efficient split unit of 3,000 frigories (about 3.5 kW of cooling capacity) uses 0.6 to 1 kWh for each hour at full power, but in real use, with the compressor modulating thanks to inverter technology, average consumption is usually a good deal lower.

### Example of consumption over a summer

| Situation | Hours of use | Estimated consumption | Cost at €0.16/kWh |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bedroom, at night only, efficient unit | 6 h/day × 75 days | 225 kWh | €36 |
| Living room, summer afternoons, efficient unit | 6 h/day × 75 days | 300 to 400 kWh | €48 to €64 |
| Living room with an old non-inverter unit | 6 h/day × 75 days | 600 to 800 kWh | €96 to €128 |

An efficient unit used well does not have to ruin your bill. The problems come with old units, very low settings, windows left exposed to the sun and running it continuously in an empty house.

## Frigories, kilowatts and the right size

- **Frigories per hour:** the traditional unit of cooling capacity used in Spain. 1 kW is roughly 860 frigories per hour.
- **Rule of thumb:** 100 to 150 frigories per square metre, depending on orientation, insulation, ceiling height and climate.

| Floor area | Indicative capacity |
|---|---|
| Up to 15 m² | 2,000 to 2,250 frigories (2.5 kW) |
| 15 to 25 m² | 2,500 to 3,000 frigories (3.5 kW) |
| 25 to 35 m² | 3,000 to 4,500 frigories (5 kW) |

A unit that is too small will always run flat out without cooling properly. One that is too big will cool quickly, dehumidify badly and cycle on and off. A technician should fit the calculation to each room.

## Efficiency: SEER and SCOP

The energy label for air conditioners still uses the A+++ to D scale. The figures that really matter are:

- **SEER:** seasonal cooling efficiency. A SEER of 7 means it delivers on average 7 kWh of cooling for every kWh of electricity.
- **SCOP:** seasonal heating efficiency, if the unit has a heat pump.

| Cooling class | SEER |
|---|---|
| A+++ | 8.5 or more |
| A++ | 6.1 to 8.5 |
| A+ | 5.6 to 6.1 |
| A | 5.1 to 5.6 |

A unit from 15 years ago may have a SEER of 3 or less. Replacing it with one with a SEER of 7 to 8 cuts consumption by more than half for the same use.

### How to estimate your own unit's consumption

1. **Find the design cooling load and the SEER on the label.**
2. **Divide one by the other:** a 3.5 kW unit with a SEER of 7 uses on average about 0.5 kWh for each hour equivalent at full load.
3. **Adjust for your use:** with an inverter, the compressor spends much of the time at half power, so six hours switched on are not six hours at full load.
4. **Check it against your data:** download your hourly consumption from your distribution company and compare a hot day with the air conditioning on with a mild day without it. The difference is what your unit uses with your habits.

The annual consumption shown on the label is calculated with the same reference hours of use for every model: it is for comparing units, not for predicting your bill.

## Which temperature to set

- **General recommendation in summer: 25 to 27 °C.** This is the reference temperature for summer comfort in light clothing.
- **Each degree lower** increases consumption noticeably, by around 7 to 10 % depending on conditions.
- **Do not set 18 °C to cool down faster:** the unit does not cool any faster because you set a lower temperature; it will just keep running longer and use more.
- **At night,** use sleep mode, which raises the temperature gradually.

## The tips that save the most

### Cut the heat that gets in

- **Lower the shutters or roll out the awnings** on sunny façades. See [shutters, awnings and sun protection](/guias/persianas-toldos-y-proteccion-solar/).
- **Close the windows** while the air conditioning is on.
- **Ventilate at night** when it is cool outside, and switch the air conditioning on only when you need it.
- **Switch off unnecessary appliances and lights** that give off heat.

### Use the unit well

- **Combine it with fans:** moving air increases the feeling of coolness and lets you raise the setting by one or two degrees.
- **Use dehumidify mode (DRY)** on humid days that are not excessively hot: it lowers the humidity, improves comfort and usually uses less.
- **Close the doors** of the room being cooled.
- **Schedule it to come on** a little before you get home, instead of leaving it on all day or switching it on at full power when you arrive.
- **Point the louvres upwards** in cooling mode: cold air sinks by itself.
- **Do not block the indoor unit** with curtains or furniture.

### Make the most of the sun and your tariff

- If you have **solar panels,** run the air conditioning in the hours of peak production and pre-cool the house.
- If you have a time-of-use tariff, bear in mind that many summer afternoons fall in the peak period. Pre-cooling at midday, in the mid-price period, can work out a little cheaper. See [time-of-use periods](/guias/tramos-horarios-punta-llano-valle/).

## Maintenance

- **Clean the filters** of the indoor unit every two to four weeks during the season. Dirty filters lower efficiency and make the air worse.
- **Check the outdoor unit:** it should not be blocked by leaves, boxes or plants, and it needs good ventilation.
- **A professional service** every one or two years: checking the refrigerant charge, cleaning the coils and the drain.
- **Check the condensate drain** to avoid drips.

### Common problems and what they point to

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A bad smell when it starts | Damp and dirt in the filters and the indoor coil | Clean the filters; after switching off, leave fan mode on for a few minutes to dry the coil if the unit does not do it by itself |
| Water drips from the indoor unit | The drain is blocked or badly sloped | Check the condensate pipe; if you cannot see the blockage, call the technician |
| It cools poorly even though it runs | Dirty filters, a blocked outdoor unit or a lack of refrigerant | Clean and clear it; if nothing changes, have it serviced |
| Frost on the pipes or on the indoor unit | A lack of refrigerant or a blocked airflow | Switch it off and call the technician |

An air conditioner does not use up refrigerant: if it is short, there is a leak that must be found and repaired; topping it up is not enough. Any work on the refrigerant circuit must be done by a professional certified to handle fluorinated gases.

## Choosing a new unit

- **Inverter technology:** essential. It modulates the compressor's output and uses far less than on-off units.
- **High SEER and SCOP.**
- **A heat pump:** so that it can also be used as efficient heating in winter.
- **The noise level** of the indoor unit, especially in bedrooms (look for low dB values at minimum speed).
- **The refrigerant:** current units use gases with a lower climate impact, such as R32.
- **Professional installation:** an excellent unit installed badly performs poorly.

### Split, multi-split or ducted

| System | Advantages | Drawbacks |
|---|---|---|
| Single split | Cheap, efficient, independent in each room | One outdoor unit per indoor unit |
| Multi-split | One outdoor unit for several indoor units | If the outdoor unit fails, they all fail |
| Ducted | Discreet, cools the whole house | Needs a false ceiling, costs more, less control per room unless zoned |
| Portable | No building work | Very inefficient, noisy, less effective |

Portable units with an exhaust hose out of the window are far less efficient than splits and usually let hot air in through the half-open window. They are a last resort.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is it better to leave it on all day or switch it off when I go out?

For absences of several hours, it is better to switch it off or raise the setting and schedule it to start before you return. For short absences, with an inverter unit it may be better to keep it at a moderate temperature.

### Can air conditioning be used for heating?

If it has a heat pump, yes, and it is one of the most efficient forms of electric heating.

### How much does it cost to install a split?

An installed 3,000-frigorie split can cost between €600 and €1,500, depending on the brand, the efficiency and how complex the installation is.

### Do I need to raise my contracted power?

A domestic split uses less than 1.5 kW. You do not usually need more power unless it runs at the same time as other high-power appliances.

### Does a fan use less?

Much less: a standing or ceiling fan uses tens of watts. But it does not lower the temperature; it only makes you feel cooler by moving air over your skin. It works well on moderate days or alongside the air conditioning; leaving it on in an empty room does nothing.

## In short

A modern inverter air conditioner with a good SEER, used at 25 to 27 °C, with the windows shaded from the sun and helped by fans, uses far less than people tend to think. The keys are to cut the heat that gets into the home, avoid settings that are too low, schedule it rather than leaving it on for nothing, clean the filters and, if the unit is old, consider replacing it with an efficient one with a heat pump.
