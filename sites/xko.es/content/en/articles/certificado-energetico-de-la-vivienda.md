Every advert to sell or let a home in Spain must show its **energy rating**, a letter from A to G that sums up how much energy the home needs and how much CO₂ it emits. For many owners it is just another formality, but the energy certificate holds useful information about how the home behaves and which improvements would be most effective. It is also the key to many grants and tax deductions for energy renovation.

This guide explains what the energy certificate is, when it is compulsory, who issues it, what it costs, how to read it and which measures improve the rating.

## What the energy performance certificate is

It is an official document that describes the energy characteristics of a property and gives it a **rating** on a seven-letter scale, from A (most efficient) to G (least efficient). It is currently governed by Royal Decree 390/2021.

The certificate includes:

- **A rating for CO₂ emissions** (kg CO₂/m² per year).
- **A rating for non-renewable primary energy consumption** (kWh/m² per year).
- **Heating and cooling demand,** which shows how much energy the home needs to stay comfortable, whatever system provides it.
- **A description of the envelope and the installations:** walls, roof, windows, heating, hot water, cooling.
- **Recommended improvements,** with the rating each would achieve.

The energy label, the graphic summary with the coloured letters, is what appears in adverts.

## When it is compulsory

| Situation | Compulsory? |
|---|---|
| Selling a home | Yes, it must be handed to the buyer |
| Letting a home (including seasonal lets, with some exceptions) | Yes, it must be made available to the tenant |
| Sale or rental adverts | Yes, the rating must appear in the advert |
| New buildings | Yes |
| Public buildings above a certain size | Yes, and displayed somewhere visible |
| A main home that is neither sold nor let | No |

There are some exceptions, such as officially protected buildings where improvements would affect their character, temporary structures or very small buildings. Fines for not having a certificate when it is compulsory can be high.

## Who issues it and what it costs

The certificate is issued by a **qualified professional**: architects, building engineers, engineers, technical engineers and other professionals authorised under the rules.

The usual process is:

1. **A visit to the home:** the assessor takes measurements and checks the windows, the walls, the orientation and the installations.
2. **A calculation with software recognised** by the Ministry.
3. **Issue and signature** of the certificate.
4. **Registration** with the relevant regional register, which is required for it to be valid. Some autonomous communities charge a fee.

**Indicative cost:** for a flat, €60 to €200 plus the regional fee if there is one. Be wary of very low prices without a visit: the assessor must inspect the home.

### How to prepare for the visit

When the assessor cannot verify a piece of data, the software applies default values based on the period of construction. Those values are conservative, so an improvement you cannot prove may not show in the letter. Have ready:

- **Invoices or data sheets** for windows, insulation or earlier renovations.
- **Make, model and year** of the boiler, heat pump, water heater and air conditioning. A photo of the rating plate will do.
- **The cadastral reference, plans or title deed,** to confirm the floor area and year of construction.
- **Details of any solar installation:** its output and legalisation documents.
- **Access** to the roof terrace, the courtyard or the roof space, if the insulation needs checking.

## Validity

The certificate is valid for a maximum of **ten years**. For homes rated G, Royal Decree 390/2021 cut the validity to five years. If you carry out works that change the efficiency, it is worth updating it.

## How to tell whether the certificate was done properly

A cheap, quick certificate can turn out expensive if the rating does not reflect the home or if it is later no good for applying for a grant. Look out for these signs:

- **There was no visit,** or it lasted a few minutes with no measurements or photos.
- **The description does not match reality:** windows of a different type, a heating system that no longer exists or a floor area very different from the cadastral one without explanation.
- **The recommended improvements are generic** and do not state the rating each would achieve.
- **There is no record of registration** with the regional body.

If you find mistakes, ask the assessor to correct them and register the certificate again. If they will not, you can commission a new one from another professional.

## How to read the rating

| Letter | General meaning |
|---|---|
| A | Very efficient; high-quality new homes or deep renovations |
| B | Very good; recent new homes |
| C | Good; some new or renovated homes |
| D | Average |
| E | Common in homes from the 1980s and 1990s |
| F and G | Old homes without insulation and with inefficient systems |

Most of Spain's housing stock falls in letters E, F and G.

### Demand versus consumption

Look at the **heating and cooling demand.** High demand means the envelope insulates poorly: the home loses a lot of heat in winter or gains a lot in summer. High consumption with moderate demand means the problem lies with the systems.

### A word of warning

The rating is calculated under standard conditions of use, not with your real consumption. A home rated E can have low bills if the heating is used little, and a B can have high ones with bad habits. It is a tool for comparing properties and guiding improvements, not an exact prediction of your bill.

## Which improvements raise the rating

The certificate's recommendations show which measures would have the most effect in your home. The most common are:

### Improvements to the envelope

- **Wall insulation** (external, cavity fill or internal lining) and **roof insulation**. See [wall and roof insulation](/guias/aislamiento-de-fachada-y-cubierta/).
- **Replacing windows** with better-insulated ones. See [efficient windows](/guias/ventanas-eficientes/).
- **Correcting thermal bridges.**

### Improvements to the installations

- **Replacing an old boiler** with a heat pump or, where appropriate, a condensing boiler. See [air-source heat pumps](/guias/aerotermia/).
- **Replacing electric radiators** with a heat pump.
- **Hot water from a heat pump** or solar thermal.
- **A solar self-consumption installation,** which reduces non-renewable energy consumption.

### Indicative effect of some measures

| Measure | Typical effect on the rating |
|---|---|
| Replacing an oil boiler with a heat pump | Can raise it two or three letters |
| External insulation on an uninsulated building | Can raise it one or two letters |
| New windows | Usually half a letter or one letter |
| Solar self-consumption panels | Can raise consumption and emissions by one letter |
| LED lighting | Small effect in homes |

The effects do not add up in a straight line: combining envelope and systems usually gives the best results.

## The certificate and grants

Many energy-renovation grants and the income-tax (IRPF) deductions for efficiency works require the improvement to be proven with **one certificate before and another after** the work. In some cases a minimum reduction in demand or consumption is required, or reaching certain letters. Ask for the initial certificate before starting any work if you want to qualify for these grants.

## The certificate when buying, selling and renting

- **For buyers and tenants:** compare the letters and, above all, the heating and cooling demand. A home with a good rating will have lower bills and be more comfortable.
- **For sellers and landlords:** a good rating can be a selling point. Improving it before selling can add value to the home.

## Flats in a block: certificate for the home or the building

In a block of flats, each owner certifies their flat when they sell or let it. The owners' association can also commission a **certificate for the whole building,** very useful when a joint renovation of the façade, roof or central heating is being considered, because it shows the effect of each measure on the whole property, and many grants aimed at buildings require it.

When comparing flats in the same block, bear their position in mind. A top-floor flat under an uninsulated roof, or a first-floor flat above an unheated entrance or shop, usually needs more energy than a mid-floor flat, because it has more surface in contact with the outside or with cold spaces. Two flats with identical floor plans can have different letters for this reason.

## Case study: up two letters before selling

A 1982 flat in Valladolid was rated E for emissions and consumption, with individual oil-fired heating and aluminium windows without a thermal break. Before putting it on the market, the owner asked the assessor to simulate several improvements with the certification software:

| Measure | Resulting rating | Indicative cost |
|---|---|---|
| New windows only | E (improvement within the letter) | €6,000 |
| Replacing the oil boiler with a heat pump only | C | €11,000 |
| Heat pump plus windows | B | €17,000 |

They chose the heat pump, which also qualified for grants and a tax deduction proven with the before and after certificates. The home went from E to C, and the advert could highlight efficient, renewable heating, an increasingly valued selling point.

## Checklist

- Check whether your home already has a registered certificate, and its date.
- Ask the assessor to explain the heating and cooling demand.
- Look at the recommended improvements and their effect on the rating.
- If you are going to renovate, ask for the certificate before the work starts.
- Keep the proof of regional registration.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I do my own home's certificate?

Only if you are an authorised professional. Otherwise, it must be done by one.

### What happens if I have no certificate when I let?

The owner can be fined. In addition, the tenant is entitled to know the rating.

### Does the certificate say how much I will pay for electricity?

Not exactly. It shows estimated consumption under standard conditions. Your real bill depends on your habits, your tariff and each year's weather.

### How often does it need renewing?

At most every ten years, or every five if the rating is G. It is also worth updating it after major renovations.

## In short

The energy certificate rates a home from A to G according to its emissions and its non-renewable energy consumption, and it is compulsory for selling or letting. It is issued by an authorised professional after visiting the property, must be registered and lasts up to ten years. Beyond the formality, its demand figures and recommendations show which improvements would be most effective, and it is essential for many energy-renovation grants.
