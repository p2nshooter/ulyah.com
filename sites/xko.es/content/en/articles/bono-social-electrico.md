The electricity social discount — the *bono social eléctrico* — is a reduction on the electricity bill for low-income households or households in especially vulnerable situations. It can save hundreds of euros a year and, in the most serious cases, protects against disconnection. Even so, many families who could benefit do not apply, either because they do not know they qualify or because the process looks complicated.

This guide explains what the social discount is, who can apply, how the discount is calculated, which documents are needed and how to apply without mistakes. Because the percentages and some thresholds have changed several times in recent years, always check the current figures on the Spanish government's official social discount website before doing your sums.

## What the social discount is

The social discount is a **reduction on the power term and the energy term** of the electricity bill, applied up to a maximum annual consumption that depends on the type of household. It is not a cash payment or a subsidy you have to account for: once approved, it is applied directly to every bill.

Basic requirements for the supply:

- You must be a **private individual** and the holder of the electricity contract.
- The supply must be your **main home**.
- The **contracted power must be 10 kW or less**.
- You must be on the **PVPC**, the regulated tariff, with a reference retailer. If you have a free-market offer, you will have to switch to the PVPC. More in [PVPC or the free market](/guias/pvpc-o-mercado-libre/).

## Consumer categories

The discount distinguishes several categories with different discounts:

| Category | General situation | Protection |
|---|---|---|
| Vulnerable | Income below certain thresholds, a large family or a pensioner on the minimum pension | A discount on the bill |
| Severely vulnerable | Even lower income or special circumstances | A larger discount |
| At risk of social exclusion | Severely vulnerable and supported by social services, which pay part of the bill | The supply cannot be cut off for non-payment |

The discount percentages have been adjusted several times. During the energy crisis they were raised temporarily and they have been revised since. Check the current percentage on the official website before calculating your saving.

## Income requirements

The main criterion is the **annual income of the whole household**, compared with the **IPREM** (the Public Income Indicator of Multiple Effects, Spain's reference index for benefits) over 14 payments. The threshold rises with the number of household members and in particular with the number of children.

As a guide, the thresholds work like this:

- A household without children: one multiple of the IPREM.
- With one child: a somewhat higher multiple.
- With two children: a higher multiple still.
- Increases are added for special circumstances, such as a disability of 33 % or more, being a victim of gender violence or terrorism, a situation of dependency or a single-parent family.

For the severely vulnerable category the thresholds are lower, usually half those for vulnerable consumers.

### Situations that give direct access

Regardless of the general thresholds, these can obtain the discount:

- **Large families** (*familias numerosas*) with a valid large-family certificate.
- **Social Security pensioners** receiving a retirement or permanent incapacity pension at the **minimum level** and with no other significant income.
- **Recipients of the Minimum Living Income** (*Ingreso Mínimo Vital*, IMV), who have simplified access.

## How much you can save

The discount applies to power and energy up to an **annual consumption limit**, which varies with the make-up of the household: it is higher for families with children and for large families. Consumption above that limit is paid at the normal price.

An indicative example: a household of four using 3,000 kWh a year, within the limit for its category, could save between a third and a little over half of the power and energy charges, depending on the current percentage. On an annual bill of €800, the saving can be more than €250.

## Documents you will need

Reference retailers usually ask for:

- The **social discount application form**, available on their website and on the official website.
- The **DNI or NIE** (identity document) of the holder and of every member of the household aged over 14.
- A **certificate of registration** (*empadronamiento*) for the whole household.
- The **family book** (*libro de familia*) or an equivalent document if there are children or a spouse.
- The **large-family certificate,** where applicable.
- **Certificates** proving special circumstances: disability, gender violence, dependency.
- **Authorisation** for the administration to check income data with the Tax Agency.

In many cases the retailer checks income directly through a Ministry platform, so you do not need to provide your tax return.

## How to apply, step by step

1. **Check your retailer.** If you are on the free market, sign up to the PVPC with a reference retailer. You can do this at the same time as applying for the discount.
2. **Download the form** from the reference retailer's website or from the official social discount website.
3. **Gather the documents** for every member of the household.
4. **Send the application** by whatever channel the retailer offers: website, email, phone, post or in person.
5. **Wait for the decision.** The retailer must reply within a few weeks. If a document is missing, it will ask for it.
6. **Check the first bill** with the discount applied.

### Renewal

The social discount is granted for a limited period, usually two years, except for large families, whose entitlement is tied to the validity of their certificate. Before it expires, the retailer must let you know. **Renew in time** so as not to lose the discount.

## Common mistakes

- **Applying while on the free market.** This is the most common reason for refusal. You must be on the PVPC first.
- **The contract holder is not the applicant.** The contract must be in the name of one of the household members applying.
- **Not including every member** on the register in the application, or including people who do not live in the home.
- **An expired large-family certificate.**
- **Not renewing in time,** which makes the discount disappear from the bill.
- **Confusing the electricity social discount with the thermal social discount.** The thermal one (*bono social térmico*) is an annual payment for heating and hot water that is granted automatically to recipients of the electricity discount; you do not apply for it separately.

## Other linked protections

- **The thermal social discount:** an annual payment towards heating, hot water or cooking, granted automatically to those who had the electricity discount on 31 December of the previous year.
- **A longer period before disconnection for non-payment** for vulnerable consumers.
- **Essential supply:** in certain situations involving children, dependent people or people with disabilities, the supply cannot be cut off.

## Saving measures that add to the discount

The social discount only applies up to a consumption limit. Reducing consumption means a larger share of your bill falls within the discount. The most effective measures are:

- Adjusting your contracted power. See [how much power to contract](/guias/que-potencia-contratar/).
- Moving the washing machine, dishwasher and water heater to the cheap hours. See [time-of-use periods](/guias/tramos-horarios-punta-llano-valle/).
- Checking how much your appliances use on standby.

## Case study: a large family

A family with three children and a valid large-family certificate had a fixed-price free-market offer. When they learned about the social discount, they took these steps:

1. They signed up to the PVPC with a reference retailer and, in the same process, applied for the social discount.
2. They provided the form, the adults' identity documents, the family book and the large-family certificate.
3. A few weeks later the retailer confirmed the discount, in the vulnerable consumer category.

On their first discounted bill, the power term and the energy term appeared reduced by the current percentage. Their annual consumption was below the limit for their category, so the discount applied to almost all their energy. Because their entitlement is tied to the large-family certificate, they put the certificate's expiry date in the calendar so as to renew both in time.

## Checklist

- Check that the contract is in the name of a household member.
- Make sure your contracted power is 10 kW or less.
- Gather the documents for every member.
- Sign up to the PVPC with a reference retailer.
- Note the discount's renewal date.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I claim the social discount if I rent?

Yes, as long as the electricity contract is in your name or that of a member of your household and the home is your main residence. If the contract is in the landlord's name, you can ask for the holder to be changed.

### Does the social discount cost anything?

No. Applying is free and involves no tie-in period.

### What if my situation changes?

You must tell the retailer. If you no longer meet the requirements, the discount is withdrawn; if your situation gets worse, you may move to a category with a larger discount.

### Can I have the social discount with solar panels?

The requirement is to be on the PVPC with a reference retailer and to meet the conditions. Ask your retailer how it combines with surplus compensation.

## In short

The electricity social discount offers a significant reduction to low-income households, large families and pensioners on the minimum pension, and protects the most vulnerable against disconnection. It requires being on the PVPC, with contracted power of up to 10 kW, in your main home. Gather the documents for the whole household, apply to a reference retailer, check the current percentages on the official website and do not forget to renew before it expires.
