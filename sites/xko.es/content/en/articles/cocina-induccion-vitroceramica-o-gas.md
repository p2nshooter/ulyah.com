The kitchen is one of the places where the technological change of recent years shows most. Gas hobs, which dominated for decades, now sit alongside radiant ceramic hobs and, more and more, induction hobs. When you refit a kitchen or move house, the question is unavoidable: which uses the least, which is cheapest to run and which should you choose?

This guide compares the three technologies on efficiency, running cost, the electrical power needed, safety, indoor air quality and price, and ends with tips for spending less when cooking with any of them.

## How each technology works

### Induction

The hob creates a magnetic field that heats **the pan directly**; the pan must be made of a ferromagnetic material. The glass surface only gets hot through contact with the pan.

### Radiant ceramic

An element under the glass glows red hot and heats the surface, which in turn passes the heat to the pan.

### Gas

A flame heats the pan. Part of the heat is lost around the pan and into the room.

## Efficiency compared

| Technology | Share of the heat that reaches the food (indicative) |
|---|---|
| Induction | 80 to 90 % |
| Radiant ceramic | 65 to 75 % |
| Gas | 40 to 55 % |

Induction is the most efficient: almost all the energy goes into the pan. It is also the fastest: it boils water in less time than the other two.

## Running cost

The cost depends on efficiency and on the price of energy. With indicative prices of €0.18/kWh for electricity (energy with taxes) and €0.09/kWh for gas (energy with taxes):

| Technology | Energy to boil 2 litres of water | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Induction | 0.21 kWh | 3.8 cents |
| Ceramic | 0.26 kWh | 4.7 cents |
| Gas | 0.38 kWh | 3.4 cents |

The cost per use of induction and gas is similar, because gas is cheaper per kWh but less efficient. The big difference lies in the **fixed costs**:

- If gas is only used for cooking, the **fixed gas charge** and the meter rental can come to more than €100 a year, far more than the gas actually used for cooking. See [the gas bill explained](/guias/factura-del-gas-explicada/).
- If the home already has gas for heating and hot water, the fixed charge is paid anyway, and cooking with gas adds no fixed cost.

## Electrical power needed

Induction hobs have maximum outputs of 3.5 to 7.4 kW. If several zones are used at full power at the same time, they can trip the power. Solutions:

- **The hob's power limiter:** most induction hobs let you limit the total power from the menu, for example to 2.8 or 3.7 kW. In daily use you hardly notice.
- **Adjusting your contracted power** if necessary. See [how much power to contract](/guias/que-potencia-contratar/).
- **Avoiding overlaps** with the oven, the washing machine or the dishwasher heating water.

## Pans

- **Induction:** it needs ferromagnetic pans. Test with a magnet: if it sticks to the base, the pan will work. The base must be flat and the size of the zone.
- **Ceramic:** any pan with a flat, smooth base.
- **Gas:** any pan; thick-based ones spread the heat better.

Moving to induction may mean buying a few new pots and frying pans.

## If you are coming from gas: getting used to induction

Induction responds instantly, but differently from a flame. In the first few days, bear in mind:

- **Prepare your ingredients beforehand.** Oil and water heat much faster; what you used to chop while the gas warmed up will now burn.
- **Do not heat an empty frying pan at maximum power** or with the boost function. The base overheats in seconds and non-stick coatings are ruined.
- **Fry gently on the middle settings.** The top settings are meant for boiling or searing.
- **Lift pans rather than sliding them,** so as not to scratch the glass.
- **A traditional round-bottomed wok does not work well.** You need a flat-bottomed one made for induction.
- **Use each zone's timer,** which switches off when cooking is done and prevents forgotten pans.

## Safety

- **Induction:** the surface only gets hot through contact with the pan and cools quickly. It detects when there is no pan. It is the safest option for households with children or older people.
- **Ceramic:** the surface stays very hot for a while; residual-heat indicators warn you.
- **Gas:** an open flame, and a risk of leaks and of carbon monoxide if combustion is poor or ventilation is inadequate. The installation needs inspections.

## Indoor air quality

Burning gas produces nitrogen dioxide and other substances which, without proper ventilation, can worsen indoor air quality. So:

- Always use the **cooker hood** when cooking with gas, preferably one that vents outside.
- **Ventilate** the kitchen.
- **Do not cover** the compulsory ventilation grilles.

Electric hobs produce no combustion gases, although cooking itself gives off particles and vapours that are also best extracted.

## Care and warning signs

- **Induction and ceramic:** clean the glass with a scraper and purpose-made products, never with metal scourers. On ceramic hobs, wipe up sugar or syrup spills at once, because they can damage the glass as they harden on the hot surface. If the glass cracks, stop using the hob and disconnect it until a technician has checked it.
- **Gas:** the flame should be blue and steady. A yellow flame, one that flickers or one that leaves soot on the pans points to poor combustion; clean the burners and, if it does not improve, call an authorised technician.
- **If you smell gas:** do not switch on lights or electrical appliances, turn off the main tap, open the windows and, once outside the home, call your gas supplier's emergency number or 112.

## Purchase and installation price

| Technology | Indicative price of the hob |
|---|---|
| Radiant ceramic | €150 to €400 |
| Induction | €250 to €1,000 or more |
| Gas hob | €150 to €500 |

Moving from gas to induction may need a dedicated circuit from the fuse board if the current installation is not ready for it.

### Before the refit: what the installer should check

- **The kitchen circuit:** the cable size and protection must be what the manufacturer specifies for the hob's power.
- **The cut-out in the worktop:** the cut-out dimensions vary between models, and many induction hobs need ventilation underneath, especially above an oven.
- **The gas point:** if you remove the gas hob, the gas point must be capped by an authorised installer. Closing the tap is not enough.
- **The cooker hood:** with induction it is still needed for steam and grease. It is a good moment to consider one that vents outside.

## Which to choose?

| Situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| A home without gas, or one that only uses gas for cooking | Induction, and consider cancelling the gas |
| A kitchen refit | Induction, for efficiency, speed and safety |
| A home with gas heating and hot water and a gas hob in good condition | Keep the gas until it needs replacing, with good ventilation |
| A very limited budget | Ceramic, although it uses more than induction |
| A household with children or older people | Induction |

## Tips for spending less when you cook

- **Put lids on pans:** it greatly cuts the time and energy needed to boil.
- **Match the pan to the size of the zone** or the flame.
- **Use only as much water as you need.**
- **Make use of the residual heat** on ceramic hobs: switch off a few minutes early.
- **A pressure cooker:** cuts the cooking time of pulses and stews by 50 to 70 %.
- **An electric kettle** for heating water: usually more efficient than the hob for small amounts.
- **A microwave or an air fryer** for small portions instead of the oven.
- **The oven:** avoid opening it all the time and cook several things at once. See [what each appliance uses](/guias/consumo-de-los-electrodomesticos/).
- **With solar panels,** cook at midday to use your own energy.

## Case study: cancelling the gas

A flat in Valencia is heated with a heat pump, has hot water from an electric water heater and uses natural gas only for a four-burner hob. Its annual gas bill:

| Item | Indicative annual amount |
|---|---|
| Fixed charge | €60 to €70 |
| Meter rental | €7 to €10 |
| Gas used (about 600 kWh) | €45 to €55 |
| Taxes | €25 to €30 |
| **Total** | **about €140 to €165** |

With an induction hob, the kitchen's electricity consumption would be about 350 to 400 kWh a year, around €70 with taxes. Cancelling the gas would save about €70 to €90 a year, and do away with the periodic inspection of the gas installation. With a mid-range induction hob and a new frying pan or two, the investment would be recovered within a few years, and the kitchen would gain in safety and air quality.

Before cancelling the gas, check that no other appliance uses it and ask the distribution company about the procedure and the cost of a possible reconnection in future.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is induction bad for your health?

The magnetic fields of induction hobs comply with the established exposure limits. People with pacemakers or other implanted medical devices should check the manufacturer's and their doctor's advice on a safe distance.

### Can I use my aluminium pans on induction?

Only if they have a ferromagnetic base. Test with a magnet.

### Is induction noisy?

At high power it can hum slightly, especially with certain pans. That is normal.

### How much do I save by cancelling the gas?

The fixed charge and the meter rental, which can exceed €100 a year, minus the increase in the kitchen's electricity consumption, which is usually small.

## In short

Induction is the most efficient, fastest and safest technology for cooking. Its cost per use is similar to gas, but if gas is only used for cooking, its fixed costs make induction cheaper overall. Limit the hob's power if you need to, test your pans with a magnet and, whatever you cook on, put lids on pans, match the size, and make use of the pressure cooker and residual heat.
