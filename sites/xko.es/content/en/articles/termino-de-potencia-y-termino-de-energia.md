When someone wants to cut their electricity bill, the first thing they usually do is switch off lights and unplug appliances. That is a good idea, but it only works on one of the bill's two parts. The other, contracted power, is paid even when the house is empty, and it often has the most room for saving without changing any habit.

This guide explains the two parts, how they are calculated, how much each weighs in a typical household and which measures reduce each one.

## Power: the capacity you have available

**Power** is measured in kilowatts (kW) and represents the maximum amount of electricity you can use at once. If you switch on the oven (2 kW), the washing machine while it heats water (2 kW) and the microwave (1 kW) at the same time, you are asking for about 5 kW. If your contracted power is 4.6 kW, the meter will cut the supply — what people in Spain call the "fuse" or "the ICP" tripping.

On domestic bills, power is contracted in two periods:

- **P1 (peak and mid-peak):** 8:00 to 24:00 on working days.
- **P2 (off-peak):** 0:00 to 8:00, and all day at weekends and on national holidays.

You can contract different values in each period. This is useful, for example, for someone who charges an electric car at night and needs more power off-peak.

### How it is calculated

**Power term = kW contracted × days × price per kW per day**

It is calculated separately for P1 and P2 and the two are added. The P1 price is much higher than P2's, because it carries most of the electricity system's network tolls and charges.

| Power contracted in P1 | Indicative annual cost of P1 before taxes |
|---|---|
| 3.45 kW | €105 to €155 |
| 4.6 kW | €140 to €205 |
| 5.75 kW | €175 to €260 |
| 6.9 kW | €210 to €310 |

The ranges depend on the retailer and on whether you are on the regulated or the free market. What matters is the proportion: every spare kilowatt costs several tens of euros a year.

## Energy: what you actually use

**Energy** is measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh). One kWh is the energy used by a 1 kW appliance running for one hour. A 10 W LED bulb takes 100 hours to use one kWh; a 2 kW oven, only half an hour.

### How it is calculated

**Energy term = kWh used × price per kWh**

If your contract has a single price, you just multiply the total. If it has prices by period, the consumption in each period is multiplied by its price and the results are added.

## How much each part weighs

In a flat for two or three people without electric heating, using about 3,000 kWh a year with 4.6 kW contracted, the typical split before taxes is roughly:

| Part | Indicative share |
|---|---|
| Power term | 25 to 35 % |
| Energy term | 60 to 70 % |
| Meter rental and other items | 1 to 3 % |

In homes with very little consumption, such as second homes, power can be more than half the bill. In homes with high consumption, such as those with electric heating or an electric car, energy weighs more.

## How to reduce the power term

1. **Match your power to your real use.** If the switch has not tripped in years, you can probably go down a step. Your distribution company lets you see the maximum power you demanded each month, the so-called maximeter, in its customer area.
2. **Stagger the use of big appliances.** Do not run the oven, washing machine, dishwasher and dryer together. Programming them for different times lets you live with less power.
3. **Contract different power in P1 and P2 if it suits you.** For example, more power off-peak if you charge the car at night.

We explain this in more detail in [how much power to contract](/guias/que-potencia-contratar/) and [changing your contracted power](/guias/cambiar-la-potencia-contratada/).

## How to reduce the energy term

1. **Use less,** with efficient appliances, good insulation and sensible habits.
2. **Use energy in the cheapest hours,** if your tariff has different prices by period.
3. **Pay less for each kWh,** by comparing offers and choosing the one that best fits your profile.
4. **Generate your own energy** with solar self-consumption, which reduces the kWh you buy from the grid.

## A complete example

Imagine a household with 5.75 kW contracted that uses 250 kWh a month. Checking the maximeter, it finds that its monthly peak has never gone above 3.8 kW. It lowers its power to 4.6 kW.

- Saving on power: 1.15 kW × about €35 per kW per year = **about €40 a year,** without changing anything in daily life.
- If it also moves 20 % of its consumption from peak to off-peak, with a price difference of 10 cents per kWh, it saves another 3,000 kWh × 20 % × €0.10 = **about €60 a year,** and more with higher consumption.

## What is inside each term: tolls, charges and margin

Both the power price and the energy price are the sum of several pieces, and understanding them helps you see what can be negotiated and what cannot:

| Component | Who sets it | Can you choose it? |
|---|---|---|
| Transmission and distribution tolls | The National Markets and Competition Commission | No: they are the same with every retailer |
| System charges | The government | No: they are common too |
| The cost of energy | The wholesale market or the price agreed with the retailer | Yes: it depends on the tariff |
| Commercial margin | Each retailer | Yes: it is what companies compete on |

That is why two offers with the same power term can differ a lot on energy, and vice versa: each retailer spreads its margin between the two terms as it likes. When comparing, always look at the total with your real consumption, not at a single price.

Outside the two terms, the bill includes other items that do not depend on your tariff: the special electricity tax, the meter rental when you do not own the meter, the financing of the social discount and VAT, which is applied at the end to everything else. Their rates are set by the rules in force at any time. Because taxes are calculated largely on the power and energy amounts, the way to pay less of them is to reduce those amounts.

## Common mistakes

- **Contracting power "just in case".** It is the most common cause of invisible overspending.
- **Lowering your power without checking the maximeter.** If you go too low, the switch will trip and you will have to raise it again, with a processing cost.
- **Thinking that switching off standby makes up for oversized power.** They are different parts of the bill.

## Case study: two households with the same bill and different problems

Marta lives alone in a 55-square-metre flat in Zaragoza. She works away from home all day, cooks with gas and has 5.75 kW contracted because that was the contract when she moved in. She uses about 1,600 kWh a year. Her annual bill is around €520 with taxes, and almost half of it is the power term.

Luis and Ana live with their two children in a 90-square-metre flat in Málaga. They have an electric water heater, an induction hob and air conditioning, with 4.6 kW contracted. They use 4,300 kWh a year. Their annual bill is around €1,050, of which power is less than a quarter.

The recipes for each household are different:

| | Marta | Luis and Ana |
|---|---|---|
| Main problem | Oversized power | High energy consumption |
| First measure | Lower to 3.45 kW after checking her maximeter | Schedule the water heater and washing machine for off-peak |
| Second measure | Compare offers with a low power price | Review the air conditioning and sun protection |
| Indicative annual saving | €70 to €100 | €100 to €180 |

This example shows why it is always worth looking at the two parts separately before deciding what to do.

## Checklist

- Find the kW contracted in P1 and P2 on your bill.
- Look up your maximum power over the past year on your distribution company's website.
- Work out what percentage of your bill is power and what percentage is energy.
- If power is more than 40 %, check whether you can lower it.
- If energy is more than 70 %, look for the big loads and the hours when they happen.
- Compare offers looking at the price of each part, not just the price per kWh.

## Frequently asked questions

### What happens if I go over my contracted power?

With smart meters, the meter itself cuts the supply. Just switch something off and wait a few seconds or reset the switch.

### Am I charged more if I use little of my contracted power?

No. You pay for the power contracted, whether you use it or not.

### Are kWh and kW the same thing?

No. kW measure power, that is, how much electricity you are using at a given instant. kWh measure energy, that is, how much electricity you have used over a period of time.

## In short

The fixed part of the bill depends on the kilowatts contracted and is reduced by matching your power to your real use. The variable part depends on the kilowatt-hours used and their price, and is reduced by using less, at better times or at a better price. Reviewing both is the basis of any saving plan.
