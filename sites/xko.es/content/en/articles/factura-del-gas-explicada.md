In many Spanish homes, natural gas heats the water, runs the heating and sometimes the hob. Its bill is like the electricity bill in some respects, such as having a fixed part and a variable part, but it has its own peculiarities: consumption is measured in cubic metres and billed in kilowatt-hours, there is a regulated tariff called the TUR with several bands, and inspections of the installation have their own rules.

This guide explains how to read the gas bill, how each item is calculated, when the regulated tariff suits you better than the free market and which measures genuinely cut the cost.

## The parts of the gas bill

### Fixed term

It is paid every day, whatever your consumption. It includes the tolls for access to the distribution network and other fixed costs. The amount depends on the access tariff, which in turn depends on the home's annual consumption.

### Variable or energy term

It is consumption in kWh multiplied by the price of energy.

### Meter rental

As with electricity, the meter usually belongs to the distribution company and you pay a regulated monthly rental.

### The special tax on hydrocarbons

It is applied to gas consumption at a rate per kWh.

### VAT

It is applied to the sum of all the items. The standard rate is 21 %, although temporary reductions were applied during the energy crisis.

### Other items

- **Maintenance services** for the boiler or the installation, if you have signed up for them.
- **The compulsory periodic inspection,** which the distribution company charges on the bill when it is carried out, every five years.

## From cubic metres to kilowatt-hours

The gas meter measures **volume** in cubic metres (m³), but the bill is calculated in **energy** (kWh). The conversion uses:

- **The volume conversion factor:** it corrects the volume for the supply pressure and temperature.
- **The gross calorific value (PCS):** the energy contained in each cubic metre of gas, which varies slightly depending on where the gas comes from.

**kWh = m³ × conversion factor × PCS**

As a guide, each cubic metre of natural gas is about 11 to 12 kWh. Both factors appear on the bill.

## The Last Resort Tariff (TUR)

The TUR (*Tarifa de Último Recurso*) is the regulated price of natural gas, set by the government and usually reviewed every quarter. Only last-resort retailers can offer it.

| Tariff | Annual consumption | For whom |
|---|---|---|
| TUR 1 | Up to 5,000 kWh | Homes with gas for hot water and cooking, or very little heating |
| TUR 2 | 5,000 to 15,000 kWh | Homes with gas heating |
| TUR 3 | 15,000 to 50,000 kWh | Large homes or buildings with central heating (with conditions) |

### TUR or the free market

- **TUR:** no tie-in period or compulsory services. Its price follows the market with quarterly reviews. In many recent periods it has been among the cheapest options.
- **Free market:** prices set by each retailer, often stable for a year, sometimes combined with electricity or with services.

As with electricity, compare using your real annual consumption and including taxes. The CNMC offers a comparison tool for gas offers.

## Why the bill is so different in winter and summer

Gas for heating is concentrated in the cold months. In summer, if you only use gas for hot water and cooking, consumption drops sharply, but the **fixed term** is still paid. In homes with very little consumption, the fixed term can be most of the bill.

### Is it worth cancelling the gas?

If you only use gas for cooking, the fixed term may not be worth it. Switching to an induction hob and cancelling the supply can save money, although reconnecting in future has costs. See [induction, ceramic or gas hob](/guias/cocina-induccion-vitroceramica-o-gas/).

## How to cut your gas spending

### Heating

- **Set the thermostat** to 19 to 21 °C by day and lower at night. See [the thermostat and the ideal temperature](/guias/termostato-y-temperatura-ideal/).
- **Schedule the times** to match when the home is occupied.
- **Lower the boiler's water temperature** so that it condenses. See [condensing boilers](/guias/caldera-de-condensacion/).
- **Bleed the radiators** at the start of each season.
- **Fit thermostatic valves.**
- **Improve the insulation** and get rid of draughts. See [draught strips and air leaks](/guias/burletes-y-corrientes-de-aire/).

### Hot water

- **Set the temperature** to 45 or 50 °C.
- **Efficient shower heads.**
- **Shorter showers.**

### Tariff and contract

- **Check your TUR band or your free-market offer** against your annual consumption.
- **Drop maintenance services** that duplicate the compulsory inspection or that you do not use.
- **Check the readings:** as with electricity, estimated readings can lead to catch-up bills. Submit your reading if necessary.

## Gas meter readings

Unlike electricity meters, many gas meters are still not read remotely. The distribution company takes periodic readings and, when it cannot get access, estimates consumption. To avoid estimates:

- **Submit your reading** on the website or app of the distribution company or retailer.
- **Give access** to the meter if it is inside the home.

## Safety

- The compulsory **periodic inspection** is carried out by the distribution company every five years, with prior notice. Be wary of unannounced visits demanding cash payments.
- **Boiler maintenance** must be done by a registered company in line with the rules.
- **If you smell gas:** do not switch anything on or off, open the windows, turn off the main tap and call the distribution company's emergency number from outside the home.
- Fit a **carbon monoxide detector** if you have combustion appliances in the home.

## An annotated example of a gas bill

A home in Burgos with gas heating and hot water, on TUR 2, January bill for 31 days:

| Item | Indicative calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed term | 31 days × €0.22/day | €6.82 |
| Consumption | 120 m³ × 1.00 × 11.6 kWh/m³ = 1,392 kWh × €0.065/kWh | €90.48 |
| Hydrocarbons tax | 1,392 kWh × €0.00234/kWh | €3.26 |
| Meter rental | 31 days × €0.04/day | €1.24 |
| Taxable base | | €101.80 |
| VAT 21 % | | €21.38 |
| **Total** | | **€123.18** |

In summer, the same home may use only 15 m³ a month for hot water: about 175 kWh and a bill of about €20, almost half of which is fixed items. The prices in the example are illustrative; the current ones appear on your bill and in the quarterly TUR publication.

## Checklist for your gas bill

- Check whether the reading is actual or estimated.
- Verify the conversion factor and calorific value applied.
- Check your TUR band or the price of your free-market offer.
- Look for maintenance services you may not need.
- Compare consumption with the same month last year.

## Switching retailer without interruptions or surprises

Switching gas retailer is free and involves no interruption of supply or changes to the installation: the gas comes through the same network and the distribution company stays the same. The new company handles the switch with your details, the CUPS code on the bill and the meter reading. Before you sign:

- **Check whether your current contract has a tie-in period** and whether leaving early carries a penalty.
- **Check the add-on services:** maintenance, insurance or assistance plans that push up the bill and are sometimes signed up for without realising.
- **Ask for the price in writing,** with the fixed term, the variable term and how long they last.
- **If you want to go back to the TUR,** remember that only last-resort retailers offer it.

Be wary of unsolicited calls and visits from supposed technicians or sales staff claiming to come "from the gas" to check your bill or installation. Never give your bank details or a photo of your bill to anyone you did not contact yourself, and always check the company's identity. The compulsory inspections of the installation are announced in advance by the distribution company.

## Common mistakes

- **Looking only at the price per kWh** and forgetting the fixed term.
- **Paying for maintenance services** that are not used or that duplicate the compulsory inspection.
- **Accepting estimated readings** for months without submitting the actual one.
- **Not checking your TUR band** when your consumption changes.
- **Giving details to sales staff** who call or visit without being asked.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do I pay for gas if I do not use it in summer?

Because of the fixed term and the meter rental, which are paid every day whatever your consumption.

### Which TUR band applies to me?

The one that matches your annual consumption. If your habits change — for example when you install a heat pump — your band may change.

### Can I have electricity with one company and gas with another?

Yes. They are separate contracts.

### Is gas cheaper than electricity for heating?

Per kWh, gas is usually cheaper than electricity, but an electric heat pump delivers 3 to 5 kWh of heat for every kWh of electricity, which makes it cheaper to run. See [air-source heat pumps](/guias/aerotermia/).

## In short

The gas bill includes a daily fixed term, consumption in kWh calculated from cubic metres, the meter rental, the hydrocarbons tax and VAT. The TUR is a regulated tariff with no tie-in period that is worth comparing with free-market offers against your annual consumption. To pay less, set the thermostat, make the boiler condense, cut hot-water use and check whether the fixed term is worth paying when use is very low.
