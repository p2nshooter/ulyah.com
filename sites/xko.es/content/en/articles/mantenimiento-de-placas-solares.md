One of the advantages of solar panels is that they need very little maintenance: they have no moving parts, use no fuel and can run for decades. But "little maintenance" does not mean "none". Dirt, new shading, a loose connection or an inverter that switches off can cut output for months without anyone noticing, and every kilowatt-hour lost is a saving that never reaches the bill.

This guide explains which check-ups a home installation needs, when and how to clean the panels, how to spot a drop in output in time, which faults are most common and what the guarantees cover.

## Why maintenance matters

A self-consumption installation has a useful life of 25 to 30 years or more. Over that time:

- The panels lose a small percentage of performance each year, usually around 0.3 to 0.5 %.
- The inverter is the component most likely to fail; its life is usually 10 to 15 years.
- Dust, pollen, bird droppings and pollution build up on the surface.
- New shading can appear: a tree that grows, an aerial, a nearby building.

Spotting any of these problems in time avoids losing output and, in some cases, greater damage.

## The main tool: monitoring

Almost every current inverter comes with an app or website that shows output in real time and the history. It is the simplest and most effective form of maintenance.

### What to check in the app

- **Daily output on sunny days:** compare similar clear days. A clear fall with no weather explanation points to a problem.
- **Monthly output against the same month last year:** differences of more than 10 to 15 % deserve a closer look.
- **Output against the initial estimate:** the installer should have given you a monthly estimate.
- **The inverter's alarms and error codes.**
- **Disconnections:** days or periods with no output.

Turn on the app's **notifications** so that you are alerted if the inverter stops reporting or detects a fault.

## Cleaning the panels

### Do they need cleaning?

In much of Spain, rain cleans the panels well enough several times a year, especially if they are tilted at 15 degrees or more. Cleaning is more necessary in:

- Dry areas with little rain and a lot of dust, such as the south-east of the peninsula or the interior in summer.
- Episodes of **calima,** dust blown from the Sahara, especially in the Canary Islands and the south.
- Places near unpaved roads, farmland, building sites or industry.
- Panels with a shallow tilt, where water does not wash the dirt away.
- Areas with a lot of birds.

### How much dirt costs

The loss from dirt is usually a small percentage in normal conditions, but it can exceed 10 % after months without rain or after heavy calima. Bird droppings are especially harmful because they create localised shading.

### How to clean them

- **When:** early in the morning or at dusk, with the panels cool. Never cold water on hot panels at midday, because of thermal shock.
- **Water:** ideally demineralised or soft water, so as not to leave limescale marks.
- **Tools:** a soft brush or a sponge on a long handle. No scourers, abrasive products or harsh detergents.
- **Pressure:** avoid high-pressure washers, which can damage the seals.
- **Safety:** do not climb onto the roof without proper safety measures. If the installation cannot be reached from the ground with a pole, hire a professional.

## Regular visual check

Once or twice a year, from the ground or from a window if possible, check:

- That there are no **broken or cracked panels** or strange marks.
- That the **frame** is firm and free of rust.
- That there are no **loose cables** or cables chewed by animals.
- That no **new shading** has appeared.
- That there are no **nests** under the panels. Pigeons can nest underneath and damage the wiring; protective mesh is available.

## Professional check-up

A technical check-up every one to three years is advisable, especially after the first few years. A technician can:

- Check that the **connections are tight** and the connectors are in good condition.
- Measure the **insulation** and the voltages of the panel strings.
- Check the electrical **protection devices.**
- Carry out **thermal imaging** to find cells or connections that run too hot.
- Update the inverter's **firmware.**

Some installers offer maintenance contracts. Consider whether the annual cost is worth it compared with occasional check-ups.

## The most common faults

| Symptom | Possible cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Zero output | Inverter off, a tripped protection device, grid failure | Check the fuse board and the inverter's codes; contact the installer |
| Low output on sunny days | Dirt, shade, a damaged panel, a faulty connection | Check visually and compare with previous days |
| Inverter with an insulation alarm | Damp in connectors, a damaged cable | Call a technician; do not touch the direct-current side |
| Inverter disconnecting at midday | High grid voltage | Tell the installer and, if it continues, the distribution company |
| The app shows no data | A wifi problem or a fault in the communication module | Restart the router and check the inverter's connection |

### Care with direct current

Panels produce direct current at voltages that can be dangerous whenever there is light. **Do not open junction boxes or disconnect direct-current cables** unless you are a professional.

## Guarantees

A home installation usually has several guarantees:

- **The panels' product guarantee:** it covers manufacturing defects, usually for 12 to 25 years depending on the manufacturer.
- **The panels' performance guarantee:** it ensures a minimum percentage of power after 25 or 30 years, usually around 80 to 90 %.
- **The inverter guarantee:** 5 to 12 years, sometimes extendable.
- **The battery guarantee,** if there is one: years, cycles or residual capacity.
- **The installation guarantee:** the one the installer gives on its own work.

Keep the invoices, serial numbers and documents. Register the equipment on the manufacturer's website if that is required to activate the guarantee.

## Insurance

Check whether your home insurance covers the solar installation against storm damage, hail, theft or fire. Many insurers include it if it is declared; others require an extension.

## An indicative calendar

| How often | Task |
|---|---|
| Weekly or monthly | Check output in the app |
| After calima or long dry spells | Consider cleaning |
| Every 6 to 12 months | Visual check of panels, frame and cables |
| Every 1 to 3 years | Professional technical check-up |
| Around 10 to 15 years | Plan for a possible inverter replacement |

## Case study: a drop in output nobody saw

A home in Albacete with 3.2 kWp produced an average of 14 kWh on clear days in May. Looking at the app in July, the owner saw that the daily maximum was not getting past 10 kWh, even though the sky was clear. Comparing with the same month the previous year confirmed a fall of almost 30 %.

The technical check found a badly crimped direct-current connector on one of the two panel strings, which had partly come apart. The repair cost less than €100 and was covered by the installation guarantee. Without monitoring, the problem would have gone unnoticed for months, with a loss of several hundred kilowatt-hours.

## Monthly checklist

- Compare output on sunny days with previous months.
- Check the inverter's alarms.
- Make sure the app is still receiving data.
- Look out for new shading.
- After calima or long dry spells, consider cleaning.

## Pigeons, nests and vegetation

One of the most common problems on roofs with panels is not technical but animal. The gap between the panels and the roof gives pigeons and other birds a sheltered place to nest. The consequences can be serious: droppings that soil the panels, twigs and debris that block gutters, cables that are pecked or damaged, and noise. Concentrated patches of droppings are also worse than spread-out dust, because they can cause localised heating in the cells.

- **Perimeter mesh:** it closes the gap under the panels without damaging them and is the most common solution. It is best fitted by a professional, with fixings that neither drill into nor scratch the modules.
- **Removing nests:** if there are already nests, remove them outside the breeding season or call in specialist companies, which know the rules that apply to wildlife.
- **Vegetation:** trees grow, and a branch that cast no shade in the first year may do so by the third. Check the shading in winter, when the sun is lower, and prune if you can.

## Common mistakes

- **Not looking at the monitoring** for months and missing faults.
- **Cleaning with a pressure washer,** harsh products or stiff brushes.
- **Climbing onto the roof without fall protection.**
- **Touching the direct-current side** without being a professional.
- **Not registering the equipment** to activate its guarantees.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can hail break the panels?

Certified panels withstand hail up to a certain size. Very severe hailstorms can damage them. Check the installation after a strong storm.

### Do they produce less in the heat?

Yes. Panels lose a little performance at high temperatures, which is why in high summer they may produce less than expected for the radiation. That is normal.

### Do I need to clean the panels if it rains often?

Usually not. Rain is usually enough, except for bird droppings or baked-on dirt.

### What do I do if the inverter is replaced?

Check that the new unit is compatible, that the installation's documents are updated and that monitoring works again.

## In short

Solar panels need little maintenance, but it is worth keeping an eye on output with the inverter's app, cleaning the panels when the dirt justifies it, doing regular visual checks and having a technical check-up every few years. The inverter is the most sensitive component. Keep the guarantee documents and check that your insurance covers the installation, to protect an investment that should last for decades.
