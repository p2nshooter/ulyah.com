Practically every home in Spain has had a **remotely managed digital meter** for years, popularly known as a smart meter. It measures consumption hour by hour and sends the data to the distribution company remotely, which should have put an end to estimated readings and surprise catch-up bills. In most cases it has, but there are still situations in which the bill is based on estimates, and many consumers do not know that they can look up their detailed consumption for free.

This guide explains how the meter works, how to read its screens, why estimated readings appear, how to access your consumption data and what to do if you suspect the meter is measuring wrongly.

## How the digital meter works

The digital meter:

- **Measures the energy you use** and, if you have self-consumption, the energy you export to the grid.
- **Records consumption by the hour** and the maximum power demanded.
- **Sends the data** to the distribution company over the power line itself (PLC) or other means.
- **Controls power:** it cuts the supply if you exceed your contracted power and allows the power to be changed remotely.
- **Allows connections, disconnections and changes** without a visit in many cases.

The meter normally belongs to the distribution company, which charges a regulated rental on the bill. See [taxes on the electricity bill](/guias/impuestos-de-la-factura-de-la-luz/).

## How to read the meter's screens

Meters show several screens that change automatically or that you step through with a button. The codes vary by manufacturer, but the most common are:

| Screen or code | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Date and time | The meter's internal clock |
| Total active energy (for example 1.8.0) | The cumulative total reading of energy used, in kWh |
| Readings by period (1.8.1, 1.8.2, 1.8.3) | Energy used in peak, mid-peak and off-peak |
| Exported energy (2.8.0) | Energy fed into the grid, if you have self-consumption |
| Instantaneous power | What is being used at that moment |
| Maximum power (maximeter) | The peak recorded in the period |
| Power-control switch status | Whether the power-control switch is connected |

### To check a reading

1. Note the total reading in kWh and the date.
2. Compare it with the reading on the bill for the same or a nearby day.
3. Take a dated photo: it is useful evidence if you need to complain.

## Why there are estimated readings

Even though the meter is remotely managed, the bill may use an estimated reading when:

- **There are communication problems** between the meter and the distribution company, for example interference on the network, meters in rural areas or a faulty data concentrator.
- **The meter is not integrated** into the remote-management system.
- **You switch retailer** and the reading for the day of the switch is not available.
- **The meter is faulty.**

The bill must state whether the reading is **actual** or **estimated.** Estimates are usually based on historical consumption for the same period.

### What happens next

When the distribution company gets an actual reading, it makes a **catch-up adjustment:** it calculates the real consumption for the period and compares it with what was billed. If too much was estimated, the difference is refunded; if too little, it is charged.

Several estimates in a row can end in a large catch-up bill. If you get one, check:

- That the total consumption matches the difference between the previous actual reading and the new actual reading.
- That the prices applied correspond to each period.
- That you can ask to pay in instalments.

If there is a mistake, see [how to complain about an electricity bill](/guias/como-reclamar-una-factura-de-la-luz/).

### How to avoid estimates

- If you receive several estimated bills in a row, **submit the reading** to your retailer or the distribution company with a photo of the meter. Many allow this on their website or app.
- **Ask the distribution company** to check the meter's communications.

## Get your hourly consumption

Distribution companies give consumers free access to their consumption data. To register you need:

- The **CUPS** of your supply (it is on the bill).
- The holder's DNI.
- Sometimes a contract number or a recent bill number.

On the distribution company's website or app you can see and download:

- Consumption **by the hour** for recent months or years.
- The **maximum power** demanded.
- **Exported energy** if you have self-consumption.
- Load curves.

### What the data is good for

- **Choosing the right power.** See [how much power to contract](/guias/que-potencia-contratar/).
- **Comparing tariffs** with your real consumption. See [PVPC or the free market](/guias/pvpc-o-mercado-libre/).
- **Spotting unusual consumption:** high night-time consumption may point to an appliance left on or a badly scheduled water heater.
- **Sizing solar panels.** See [how many solar panels do I need](/guias/cuantas-placas-solares-necesito/).
- **Checking bills.**

## Finding hidden loads with the meter

You can use the meter's instantaneous power reading to find out how much your home uses "doing nothing":

1. At night, with everything off except the essentials (fridge, router), look at the instantaneous power.
2. A value of 50 to 150 W is normal. If you see 300 W or more, something is drawing power all the time.
3. Unplug appliances or switch off circuits at the fuse board one by one to find the load.

More in [standby consumption](/guias/consumo-en-standby/).

## If you suspect the meter is measuring wrongly

Meters are subject to metrological control and rarely measure wrongly, but it can happen. If you suspect a mistake:

1. **Compare your consumption** with your habits and with earlier periods.
2. **Do a test:** switch off the main switch and check that the meter records no consumption.
3. **Ask the distribution company for a verification.** If the meter measures within the legal margins, you may have to pay for the verification; if it measures wrongly, the distribution company must correct it and re-bill.

## Security and privacy

Hourly data reveals household habits: when someone is at home, when people sleep. Data-protection rules limit how it can be used. Do not share your distribution-company login, and be wary of calls asking for your CUPS for supposed meter "checks": they are not needed and may be an attempt to switch your company without your consent.

## Case study: a €380 catch-up bill

A household received estimated bills for five months, all showing about 180 kWh a month, less than the real figure because that winter they used their electric radiators more. When the distribution company read the meter again, a catch-up bill arrived for almost 1,900 kWh and €380.

Looking at the hourly data on the distribution company's website, they saw that the total consumption was correct, so there was no mistake, but they were able to:

1. Ask the retailer to spread the payment over several months.
2. Check that the prices applied to each month were those in their contract.
3. Tell the distribution company to check the meter's communications, which had a problem with the building's data concentrator.

Since then they look at their consumption on the distribution company's website every month and, if the bill is estimated again, they send a photo of the reading.

## Checklist

- Register your CUPS on the distribution company's website.
- Check whether each bill shows an actual or an estimated reading.
- Photograph the meter reading when in doubt.
- Look at your hourly consumption to spot unusual loads.
- Submit readings if you get several estimates in a row.

## How to find your meter in a block of flats

In blocks of flats, the meters are usually grouped in a meter room: a room or cupboard on the ground floor, in the basement or by the entrance, with dozens of identical units. To find yours:

- **Look for the meter number** on the bill or in the distribution company's customer area and compare it with the one on the front of each unit.
- **Look at the labels:** many meters are marked with the floor and door, although they are not always up to date.
- **Check with a simple test:** if you switch off your home's main switch, the instantaneous power on your meter should drop almost to zero.

The keys to the meter room are usually held by the president of the owners' association, the property manager or the caretaker. In detached houses, the meter is usually in a cabinet on the façade or at the boundary of the plot, accessible from the street to make reading easier.

With smart meters the reading is taken remotely, but knowing where yours is remains useful for checking readings, resetting after an over-power trip or finding hidden loads.

## Common mistakes

- **Ignoring estimated readings** until a large catch-up bill arrives.
- **Mistaking a neighbour's meter** for your own in the meter room.
- **Never downloading your hourly consumption,** which is free and very useful.
- **Giving your CUPS** to someone who calls without being asked.
- **Requesting a meter verification** without first ruling out hidden loads at home.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can they change my meter without warning?

The distribution company can replace the meter as part of its maintenance, normally with prior notice if it needs access to your home.

### Why does the meter cut the power?

Because you have exceeded your contracted power. Switch something off and reset. If it happens often, review your habits or your power.

### Can I see consumption in real time?

On the meter's display, yes. On the distribution company's website the data usually appears a day or two later. To see real-time consumption on your phone, you can fit an energy monitor in the fuse board.

### Does the meter measure my exported energy if I have panels?

Yes, if it is bidirectional and the distribution company has configured it for self-consumption.

## In short

The digital meter measures your consumption hour by hour and sends it to the distribution company, which reduces estimated readings, although they can still appear because of communication failures. Learn to read its display, take photos of the readings, register your CUPS on the distribution company's website to see your hourly consumption, and use it to choose your power, compare tariffs, find hidden loads and check your bills.
