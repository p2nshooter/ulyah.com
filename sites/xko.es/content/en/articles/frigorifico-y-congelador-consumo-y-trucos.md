The fridge is the only major appliance that runs 24 hours a day, 365 days a year. Although its power is low, running continuously makes it, in many homes without electric heating, one of the biggest users of electricity, especially if it is old or used badly. An extra freezer in the garage or storeroom can add even more.

This guide explains how much fridges and freezers use, which temperature to set, how location and habits affect them, what maintenance they need and when it pays to replace an old appliance.

## How much they use

| Appliance | Indicative annual consumption |
|---|---|
| Modern high-class fridge-freezer | 110 to 180 kWh |
| Modern mid-class fridge-freezer | 180 to 280 kWh |
| Fridge more than 15 years old | 400 to 700 kWh |
| American-style (side-by-side) fridge | 300 to 500 kWh |
| Modern chest or upright freezer | 150 to 300 kWh |
| Old freezer | 400 to 800 kWh |

At €0.20/kWh, an old fridge can cost more than €100 a year, while a modern, efficient one may stay at €25 to €40.

## The right temperature

- **Fridge:** **4 to 5 °C.** Below 3 °C it uses more without benefiting the food and may freeze fresh produce.
- **Freezer:** **−18 °C.** Each degree lower increases consumption without noticeably improving preservation.

Many fridges have a numbered dial instead of degrees. Use a fridge thermometer to check the real temperature on the middle shelf.

## Location: the forgotten factor

The fridge expels the heat it removes from inside, usually at the back or underneath. If it cannot get rid of it, it works harder.

- **Away from heat sources:** oven, hob, dishwasher, radiators or direct sun through a window.
- **Leave room for ventilation:** keep the gap to the wall and units that the manufacturer specifies, usually a few centimetres behind and above.
- **Freezers in garages or storerooms:** check that the appliance is designed for the room temperature of that space (its **climate class**). A very hot garage in summer sends consumption soaring; a very cold one in winter can affect how some models work.

## Habits that save

- **Open the door as little as possible** and close it quickly. Think about what you are going to take out before you open it.
- **Do not put hot food in:** let it cool at room temperature for a reasonable time, without going beyond food-safety recommendations.
- **Defrost in the fridge:** frozen food helps cool the compartment.
- **Do not overfill it** or leave it nearly empty: air must circulate, but a reasonably full fridge holds its temperature better.
- **Cover containers:** it reduces humidity and frost.
- **Organise the food** so you can find what you want quickly.

## Maintenance

### Door seals

Worn seals let the cold escape. Try the paper test: close the door on a sheet of paper; if it slides out without resistance, the seal does not close properly at that point. Clean the seals with soap and water and replace them if they are torn or misshapen.

### Frost and defrosting

In freezers without a frost-free system, a layer of frost more than a few millimetres thick acts as insulation and increases consumption. Defrost when the ice exceeds 3 to 5 mm.

### Grille or condenser

Clean the dust off the rear or lower grille once or twice a year, with the appliance unplugged. Accumulated dust makes it harder to get rid of heat.

### Drain

Check that the small drain hole inside the fridge is not blocked.

## When it pays to replace it

A fridge more than 15 years old can use three times as much as a modern high-class one. To see whether replacing it pays:

1. **Measure the real consumption** with a plug-in meter for a week and multiply by 52.
2. **Compare** it with the annual consumption on the label of the new model you are interested in. See [the energy label](/guias/etiqueta-energetica/).
3. **Work out the annual saving** and divide the price of the new appliance by it.

### Example

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Measured consumption of the old fridge | 550 kWh a year |
| Consumption of the new model | 160 kWh a year |
| Annual saving | 390 kWh × €0.20 = €78 |
| Price of the new model | €650 |
| Payback | About 8 years |

If the old fridge also breaks down, has worn seals or does not hold its temperature well, the decision is clearer.

### And the second freezer or fridge?

Many households have a second, old fridge in the garage for drinks or a nearly empty freezer. If it is only used occasionally, unplugging it can save €50 to €150 a year.

## Choosing a new fridge

- **The right size:** a fridge that is too big uses more. As a guide, 250 to 350 litres for two or three people.
- **Annual consumption in kWh,** not just the letter.
- **A climate class** suited to where it will stand.
- **Frost-free:** it prevents frost, although it may use slightly more than a well-maintained manual-defrost model.
- **Noise,** if the kitchen is open to the living room.
- **Collection of the old appliance:** the seller must offer to take it away. Fridges contain refrigerant gases and insulating foams that must be treated at authorised facilities.

## What to do in a power cut

- **Do not open the doors:** a closed fridge keeps cold for a few hours, and a full freezer for a day or two.
- After a long cut, check perishable foods and, if in doubt, throw them away.

## How to measure your fridge's consumption

The label gives a laboratory figure, but your kitchen's may differ. To measure it:

1. **Plug a meter in** between the socket and the fridge. They cost €10 to €25.
2. **Leave it for at least seven days** with normal use. The fridge runs in cycles, so a few hours' measurement is not representative.
3. **Multiply the weekly consumption by 52** to get the annual figure.
4. **Repeat in summer and winter** if you can: consumption changes with the kitchen temperature.

If your fridge-freezer uses more than 1.2 kWh a day, it is a clear candidate for a check-up or replacement.

## Organising the inside wisely

Each zone of the fridge has a slightly different temperature. Making use of that improves preservation and cuts down on long openings:

| Zone | Relative temperature | What to keep there |
|---|---|---|
| Bottom shelf, above the drawers | The coldest | Raw meat and fish, well wrapped |
| Middle shelves | Intermediate | Dairy, leftovers, cured meats |
| Top shelf | Slightly warmer | Cooked food, mature cheeses |
| Drawers | More humid | Fruit and vegetables |
| Door | The warmest | Drinks, sauces, eggs depending on the model |

Labelling leftovers with the date and putting what needs eating first at the front saves searching with the door open and cuts food waste.

## Freezing sensibly: saving money and food safety

The freezer uses less and preserves better when used methodically:

- **Reference temperature:** −18 °C. Above it, food quality suffers sooner.
- **Flat portions:** thin, flattened bags freeze and thaw faster than thick blocks, and stack better.
- **Label and date:** write what it is and when you froze it. Whatever cannot be identified ends up forgotten for months, taking up cold.
- **Fast-freeze in moderation:** many appliances have a fast-freeze function for when you put in a large amount of food. Switch it on a few hours beforehand and off afterwards; if it is left on, the compressor runs non-stop and consumption rises.
- **A full freezer holds the cold better:** if it is half empty, a few bottles of frozen water fill the gap and help during power cuts.

When defrosting, the safety rule is simple: do it in the fridge, which also makes use of the cold, or in the microwave if you are going to cook the food straight away. Avoid defrosting meat or fish at room temperature on the worktop. Raw food defrosted in the fridge can be refrozen, although it loses quality; if it was defrosted any other way, cook it before freezing it again.

## Holidays and second homes

If you are going away for a few days, holiday mode or a slightly higher temperature in an empty fridge cuts consumption without switching off the freezer. If you will be away for several weeks, or it is a second home that stays shut for months, the most efficient thing is to empty the fridge, unplug it, clean it and leave the door ajar so that smells and mould do not appear. Keeping an empty appliance running for months is one of the most common hidden costs in holiday homes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a full fridge use more than an empty one?

A reasonably full fridge holds its temperature better, because cold food acts as a store of cold. But do not overload it or block the air vents.

### Is it normal for the sides of the fridge to get warm?

Many models give off heat through the sides. It is normal, as long as there is room for ventilation.

### Can I put the fridge next to the oven?

It is best avoided. If there is no alternative, leave a gap or put insulation between them.

### How much does holiday mode use?

It keeps the fridge at a higher temperature with the door closed, using less during long absences. Remove perishable food first.

## In short

The fridge runs all year, which is why its efficiency and how you use it weigh so much on the bill. Set it to 4 or 5 °C and the freezer to −18 °C, put it away from heat with room to ventilate, check the seals and the frost, clean the grille and open the doors only as much as you need. If your appliance is more than 15 years old, measure its consumption: replacing it can pay for itself within a few years, and unplugging an under-used second fridge saves from day one.
