Since June 2021, domestic electricity bills in Spain have been governed by the 2.0TD access tariff, which divides the day into periods with different prices. Even if you have a fixed-price offer, the network tolls your retailer pays follow that structure, and on the regulated market or on time-of-use offers the price difference between hours shows clearly on the bill.

This guide explains which hours are peak, mid-peak and off-peak, how they apply to energy and to power, which loads are worth moving and which are not, with real examples from an average household.

## The three energy periods

For the energy you use, the 2.0TD tariff distinguishes three periods on the mainland, the Balearic Islands and the Canary Islands:

| Period | Name | Hours, Monday to Friday working days |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Peak | 10:00 to 14:00 and 18:00 to 22:00 |
| P2 | Mid-peak | 8:00 to 10:00, 14:00 to 18:00 and 22:00 to 24:00 |
| P3 | Off-peak | 0:00 to 8:00 |

**Saturdays, Sundays and national holidays** are off-peak for all 24 hours. Regional or local holidays do not count as off-peak unless they coincide with a national holiday. In Ceuta and Melilla the hours are shifted because of the particular features of their electricity systems.

## The two power periods

Contracted power has only two periods:

- **P1 (peak and mid-peak):** 8:00 to 24:00 on working days.
- **P2 (off-peak):** 0:00 to 8:00 on working days and all day at weekends and on national holidays.

You can contract different power in each. Off-peak power is much cheaper, which is useful for charging an electric car at night. To choose the right value, see [how much power to contract](/guias/que-potencia-contratar/).

## How much the price changes between periods

The difference depends on the type of contract:

- **Regulated market (PVPC):** the price changes every hour with the wholesale market and the tolls. Off-peak hours are usually considerably cheaper than peak ones, although on days with a lot of solar output the middle of the day can be surprisingly cheap.
- **Free market with time-of-use pricing:** the retailer sets three prices, one per period. The difference between peak and off-peak can be double or more.
- **Free market with a single price:** you pay the same at any hour. The periods do not change your price, but it is worth knowing them if you are thinking of changing offer.

To decide between them, see [PVPC or the free market](/guias/pvpc-o-mercado-libre/).

## Which loads can be shifted

Not all of a home's consumption is flexible. It helps to distinguish three groups:

### Fixed loads that do not move

The fridge, the freezer, the router and appliances on standby run 24 hours a day. They are a significant part of consumption, but there is no point trying to shift them. The only way to reduce them is to use more efficient equipment. See [standby consumption](/guias/consumo-en-standby/).

### Loads tied to daily life

Cooking, showering with an electric water heater, lighting and television depend on the family's timetable. They can be adjusted a little, but forcing yourself to have dinner at midnight is not reasonable.

### Programmable loads

This is where the real saving lies:

- **Washing machine and dishwasher:** almost all have a delayed start. Programming them to run in the early hours or at the weekend is easy.
- **Tumble dryer:** the same as the washing machine.
- **Electric water heater:** with a timer or built-in scheduling, it can heat the water at night and keep it hot.
- **Charging an electric car:** the biggest load and the easiest to shift.
- **A heat pump with a cylinder or underfloor heating:** in some cases the home or the water can be pre-heated in cheap hours.

## An example with numbers

Imagine a household that uses 250 kWh a month on a time-of-use offer with these indicative prices: peak €0.20/kWh, mid-peak €0.13/kWh and off-peak €0.08/kWh.

| Scenario | Peak | Mid-peak | Off-peak | Energy cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No change in habits | 35 % | 30 % | 35 % | €34.25 |
| Washing machine, dishwasher and water heater off-peak | 25 % | 27 % | 48 % | €31.05 |

The saving of about €3 a month, about €38 a year before taxes, comes just from programming three appliances. With an electric car charged off-peak, the saving compared with charging at peak times can easily exceed €200 a year.

## Weekends count as off-peak

It is a detail many households forget: all of Saturday and Sunday is charged at the off-peak price on time-of-use offers. Doing the ironing, big washes, baking or batch-cooking for the week at the weekend is an easy way of using it without staying up late.

## When time-of-use pricing does not pay

- **If almost all your consumption is at peak times,** for example because you work from home with air conditioning and cook at midday, a single-price offer may work out better.
- **If the offer's price difference is small,** the effort of shifting consumption is not worth it.
- **If you use very little,** the absolute saving is minimal and the power term weighs more.

The best way to find out is to download your hourly consumption from the distribution company's website and work out what you would pay with each offer. Many comparison tools accept that file directly.

## Noise and safety when running appliances at night

Programming appliances for the early hours calls for a few precautions:

- **Noise:** in flats with thin walls, a night-time spin cycle can disturb the neighbours. Choose quiet programmes or use them early at the weekend.
- **Safety:** do not leave dryers running unattended if the lint filter is not clean, and avoid plugging high-power appliances into power strips.
- **Damp clothes:** if the washing machine finishes at four in the morning, the clothes will sit wet for hours. Time the end of the cycle for when you get up.

## Checklist for making the most of the periods

- Download your hourly consumption from the distribution company and work out the split between peak, mid-peak and off-peak.
- Identify the programmable appliances in your home: washing machine, dishwasher, dryer, water heater, car.
- Set each one's delayed start so that it finishes when you wake up or runs at the weekend.
- If you have an electric water heater without its own scheduling, add a timer or a programmable plug.
- Check on the bill each month how the split by period changes.
- At least once a year, compare your offer with other time-of-use offers and with the PVPC.

## A trial month

If you are torn between a single-price offer and a time-of-use one, try a simple test: for a month, schedule every appliance you can for off-peak and note the resulting consumption split on the bill. With that real split you can work out what you would pay on each type of offer. Many households find they can move 40 to 50 % of their consumption to off-peak without effort, which usually tips the balance towards time-of-use pricing.

## The periods when you have solar panels

With solar self-consumption, the logic of the periods changes. Panels produce mostly in the middle of the day, which largely coincides with the morning peak from 10:00 to 14:00 and with the afternoon mid-peak. So in a home with panels:

- **The cheapest time to use energy is when the sun is out:** the energy you generate and use at once is not bought from the grid, whatever the period.
- **Programmable appliances move to midday,** not the early hours: the washing machine, dishwasher, water heater or car charging work best with the sun high.
- **Off-peak is still useful** for what the sun does not cover: overnight car charging or cloudy days.
- **The evening peak, from 18:00 to 22:00,** is the hardest to cover, because solar output falls just when the home uses most. That is where a home battery can help a little, although its cost means doing the sums carefully.

If you have panels, also check whether a time-of-use tariff or a single-price one suits you better: because you buy less energy in the sunny hours, the pattern of your grid consumption changes. ## Common mistakes

- **Thinking regional holidays** count as off-peak.
- **Running noisy appliances in the early hours** in flats with thin walls.
- **Changing habits without checking** whether the offer really has different prices by period.
- **Forgetting the weekend,** which is off-peak all day.
- **With panels, moving loads to the night** instead of to midday.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are my autonomous community's holidays off-peak?

Only fixed-date national holidays count as off-peak. Regional and local holidays are billed as a normal working day.

### Do the hours change in summer?

No. Since the 2.0TD tariff, the periods are the same all year, with no difference between winter and summer.

### If I have a fixed price, do the periods affect me?

Your price does not change with the time of day, but power is still split into P1 and P2. And knowing your pattern lets you judge whether a time-of-use offer would be cheaper.

### How do I know how much I use in each period?

The bill breaks down energy by period. For more detail, your distribution company's website shows consumption hour by hour.

## In short

The domestic tariff divides energy into peak, mid-peak and off-peak, and power into two periods. Off-peak includes every night from 0:00 to 8:00 and the whole of weekends and national holidays. The saving lies in programmable loads: the washing machine, dishwasher, dryer, water heater and, above all, an electric car. Before choosing a time-of-use offer, check with your real hourly data whether it really pays.
