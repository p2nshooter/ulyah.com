Feeling a cold draught by the window, hearing the front door whistle on windy days or watching the curtains move with everything shut are all signs of a very common problem: **air leaks**. Through those gaps warm air escapes in winter and heat comes in during summer, and the heating or air conditioning has to work harder to make up for it.

The good news is that sealing air leaks is one of the cheapest and most profitable efficiency improvements there is. With draught strips, sealants and a little patience, you can get rid of many draughts in a weekend and for very little money. This guide explains how to find the leaks, which materials to use in each case and which precautions to take so as not to harm ventilation.

## Why air leaks matter

In an older home, air leaks can account for a considerable share of heat losses in winter. Besides the energy, they affect comfort:

- Cold draughts make you feel cold even when the thermostat shows a reasonable temperature, which leads you to turn it up.
- Dust, noise, insects and smells come in.
- In summer, hot outdoor air reduces the effectiveness of the air conditioning.

Cutting air leaks lets you stay just as comfortable with the thermostat one or two degrees lower in winter, and each degree less means a noticeable saving on heating. See [the thermostat and the ideal temperature](/guias/termostato-y-temperatura-ideal/).

## How to find the leaks

### The candle or incense method

On a windy day, with windows and doors shut, hold a lit candle or a stick of incense near the edges of windows, doors, shutter boxes and sockets on outside walls. If the flame or smoke moves, there is a leak. Be very careful with curtains and anything flammable.

### The wet-hand method

Run a damp hand along the edges: you will easily feel the cold air in winter.

### The sheet-of-paper method

Close the window or door on a sheet of paper. If you can pull it out easily, the seal does not close properly at that point.

### Thermal camera

Some energy-efficiency firms and assessors offer thermal imaging that shows the cold spots. There are also affordable thermal cameras that plug into a mobile phone.

## The most common leak points

| Point | Why it leaks | Typical fix |
|---|---|---|
| Seals between sash and frame on windows | Worn or missing seals on old windows | Adhesive draught strip or a new seal |
| Shutter box | A hollow, uninsulated box with a slot for the strap | Insulate the inside of the box and fit a brush in the slot |
| Front door | Gap at the bottom and the sides | A perimeter strip and a door-bottom seal |
| Doors to terraces | Like windows, with a bigger surface | Draught strips and adjusting the hardware |
| Joint between frame and wall | Gaps left unsealed during building | Silicone or acrylic sealant |
| Sockets and switches on outside walls | Unsealed boxes | Insulating gaskets under the cover |
| Pipe and cable openings | Gaps around them | Foam or sealant |
| Unused chimneys | Open flue | A chimney plug or balloon |
| Hatches and lofts | No seal | Draught strip and insulation |

## Before you buy anything: adjust the hardware and hinges

Many windows and doors do not close properly because the sash has dropped or the locking points press too lightly, not because a draught strip is missing.

- **Casement and tilt-and-turn windows:** on many models, the locking points on the edge of the sash are eccentric cams that turn with an Allen key to press the sash against the seal. Turn them all by the same amount and check that the handle still moves without forcing.
- **Dropped sashes:** if the sash rubs at the bottom or leaves a bigger gap at the top than at the bottom, the hinges usually have height and side adjustment screws.
- **Doors:** tightening the hinge screws or moving the strike plate can close side gaps without adding anything.
- **Original seals:** on PVC windows or thermally broken aluminium windows, the seal usually sits in a groove and is sold by the metre. Replacing it with an equivalent one seals better than sticking a strip on top.

## Types of draught strip

### Adhesive foam strips

Cheap and easy to fit. Useful for small, even gaps. They do not last long, usually one or two seasons.

### Rubber strips (P, E and D profiles)

More durable and effective. The letter shows the shape of the profile:

- **E profile:** for gaps of 1 to 3 mm.
- **P profile:** for gaps of 3 to 5 mm.
- **D profile:** for gaps of 3 to 7 mm.

Measure the gap before buying: a strip that is too thick stops the window closing properly, one that is too thin does not seal.

### Silicone strips

Very durable and flexible, suitable for windows that are opened often.

### Door-bottom seals

- **Adhesive with a brush or rubber blade:** stuck to the bottom of the door.
- **Automatic seals:** hidden in the door and dropping down when it closes. They are the most effective and discreet.
- **Tube or "snake" draught excluders:** laid on the floor, useful for interior doors.

### Brushes

For sliding windows and for the shutter-strap slot, where a rigid strip does not work.

## How to fit an adhesive draught strip

1. **Clean and dry** the surface thoroughly, with no dust or grease left. A little alcohol helps.
2. **Measure the gap** with the window closed, for example with modelling clay.
3. **Choose the right profile.**
4. **Fit the strip on the frame,** not on the moving sash, unless the manufacturer says otherwise.
5. **Press it down firmly** along its whole length and avoid stretching it.
6. **Check that it closes:** the window should shut without excessive effort.

The best time to fit them is in autumn, when temperatures are mild, because adhesives stick less well in cold and damp weather.

## Insulating the shutter box

This is one of the most effective improvements in older flats:

1. Open the box cover (it is usually screwed or clipped on).
2. Clean the inside.
3. Fit thin **insulating boards** (extruded polystyrene, foil-faced mineral wool or purpose-made panels) on the walls and the cover, without getting in the way of the shutter as it rolls up.
4. Seal the joints of the cover with a draught strip.
5. Fit a **brush** in the slot the strap comes through.

There are complete kits for insulating shutter boxes at a very affordable price.

## Sealing the joint between frame and wall

If air comes in between the window frame and the wall:

- **Neutral-cure silicone or paintable acrylic sealant** for fine joints indoors.
- **Polyurethane foam** for large gaps, protected afterwards with sealant or trim, because it breaks down in light.
- Outdoors, use weather-resistant products.

## Ventilation is still necessary

Sealing does not mean leaving the house without air. A home needs fresh air to remove moisture, CO₂ and indoor pollutants.

- **Ventilate briefly and thoroughly:** 5 to 10 minutes a day with opposite windows open is enough in winter.
- **Respect the compulsory ventilation grilles** in kitchens with gas and in bathrooms. **Never cover them:** they are a safety matter.
- **Keep an eye on humidity:** if condensation or mould appears after sealing, ventilate more.

## Common sealing mistakes

- **Sticking the strip over an old or dirty seal:** it comes off within weeks and leaves channels for air to keep coming in. Remove the damaged seal and old adhesive first.
- **Using acetic-cure silicone on marble, natural stone or metal:** it can stain or attack some surfaces; neutral-cure silicone is the safe choice.
- **Leaving polyurethane foam exposed:** trim it once dry and cover it, because it breaks down in light.
- **Covering trickle vents:** in recent homes, the windows or shutter boxes may have trickle vents that are part of the ventilation system. They are not a leak but a planned air inlet.
- **Sealing the shutter box shut:** the cover must still open so that the strap can be changed or the axle repaired.
- **Forgetting interior doors:** a seal under the door of a cold room you do not heat, such as a box room or an enclosed balcony, stops its cold air reaching the area you do heat.

## How much it saves

The saving depends on how leaky the home is. In older flats with a lot of air leaks, sealing windows, doors and shutter boxes can cut heating consumption by around 5 to 15 %, for an outlay that often does not exceed €50 to €150. It is one of the measures with the quickest payback, often within a single season.

| Improvement | Indicative cost | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| Draught strips on 5 windows | €20 to €50 | Low |
| Automatic door-bottom seal | €20 to €60 | Medium |
| Insulating 4 shutter boxes | €40 to €100 | Medium |
| Socket gaskets | €5 to €15 | Low |
| Sealing frames | €10 to €30 | Low to medium |

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should draught strips be replaced?

Foam ones every one or two seasons. Good-quality rubber or silicone ones can last several years. Replace them when they are flattened, cracked or coming unstuck.

### Can I seal the kitchen grilles?

Not if you have gas appliances. They are compulsory for safety, to prevent carbon monoxide building up.

### Do draught strips work on sliding windows?

Yes, but with brushes or profiles made for sliding windows, because compression strips make the window hard to slide.

### How do I remove the remains of an old draught strip?

Warm the adhesive with a hairdryer to soften it, peel it off with a plastic scraper and clean what is left with isopropyl alcohol or an adhesive remover. Avoid metal blades on PVC or painted aluminium, because they scratch.

### Do draught strips also cut noise?

In part. Noise comes in easily through gaps, so sealing them makes a difference to voices and nearby traffic. If the noise is loud, the glass and the window itself set the limit. See [efficient windows](/guias/ventanas-eficientes/).

### Is it worth it if I am going to replace the windows soon?

If the replacement is months away, perhaps not. If it is years away, yes: the cost is so low that it pays for itself quickly.

## In short

Draughts are a source of energy loss and discomfort that is easy and cheap to fix. Find the leaks with a candle, your hand or a sheet of paper; choose the right strip for each gap; insulate the shutter boxes; seal the frames and the sockets on outside walls; and keep up a short daily airing. For very little money you will gain comfort and be able to turn the thermostat down by one or two degrees without noticing.
