An electricity bill that is higher than usual is not always a mistake: it may be down to a cold winter, a catch-up bill after months of estimated readings or a price rise. But mistakes do happen, and knowing how to complain methodically makes the difference between getting your money back within a few weeks and losing months on calls that go nowhere.

This guide explains how to tell whether there is a real mistake, which details to check, how to make a written complaint, how long companies have to reply and which bodies to turn to if they do not resolve it.

## Before you complain: check whether it is a mistake

Go through these points with the bill and your previous bills in front of you. See [how to read your electricity bill](/guias/como-leer-la-factura-de-la-luz/) if you need help understanding each item.

1. **Billing period:** how many days does it cover? A 45-day bill cannot be compared with a 30-day one.
2. **Type of reading:** is it actual or estimated? Estimated readings can lead to later catch-up bills.
3. **Consumption:** compare it with the same period last year.
4. **Contracted power:** is it the power you contracted?
5. **Prices:** do they match your contract or offer? Has a temporary discount ended?
6. **Add-on services:** are there insurance or maintenance charges you did not sign up for?
7. **Holder and CUPS:** are they yours?
8. **Taxes:** are VAT and the electricity tax applied correctly? See [taxes on the electricity bill](/guias/impuestos-de-la-factura-de-la-luz/).

### Check your consumption on the distribution company's website

With your CUPS you can register on your distribution company's website and download the hourly consumption measured by your meter. If the consumption billed does not match what was recorded, you have solid evidence.

## The most common mistakes

| Mistake | Sign |
|---|---|
| A very high estimated reading | Consumption far above normal with an "estimated" reading |
| A badly calculated catch-up | A large amount after several estimated months, with no clear breakdown |
| A price different from the contract | The price per kWh or per kW does not match the offer |
| An end of discount you were not told about | A price rise with no prior notice |
| Services you did not order | Unfamiliar insurance or maintenance charges |
| Double billing | Two bills for the same period, common after changing retailer |
| Wrong power | Power different from what you contracted |
| Social discount not applied | A discount that does not appear after approval |

## How to recalculate the amount yourself

With a catch-up bill after estimated readings, you can work out yourself how much to claim:

1. **Take two actual readings:** the last actual one before the estimates and the new one. The difference is the true consumption for the whole period.
2. **Add up the kWh already charged** on the estimated bills in between.
3. **Subtract:** the result is the kWh still to be billed. If the catch-up bill charges more, there is a mistake.
4. **Check the price** applied to those kWh. If you have different prices by time-of-use period, do the sum for each period.

For example, between an actual reading of 10,250 kWh and another of 11,090 kWh there are 840 kWh. If the estimated bills have already charged 510 kWh, the catch-up should bill 330 kWh. If it bills 450, you claim 120 kWh multiplied by your price, plus the corresponding taxes.

## Whom to complain to

- **The retailer:** about prices, contract terms, add-on services, wrongful charges and any aspect of the bill.
- **The distribution company:** about meter readings, faults, quality of supply or technical problems. You can usually lodge the complaint through the retailer, which must pass it on, but also directly with the distribution company.

If you do not know which your distribution company is, it appears on the bill, next to the fault-reporting number.

## How to make the complaint

### Do it in writing

Phone calls can help clear up doubts, but the complaint itself should be **in writing,** with a date and a reference number. The usual routes are:

- The complaints form on the retailer's website or app.
- An email to customer services.
- A recorded-delivery letter or burofax for serious cases.
- An official complaint form (*hoja de reclamaciones*) at a high-street office.

### What to include

- The holder's name, DNI and contact details.
- The **CUPS** and the contract number.
- The number of the bill you are disputing and its period.
- **A clear description of the problem:** what is wrong and why.
- **Evidence:** a dated photo of the meter reading, consumption data from the distribution company, a copy of the contract or offer, previous bills.
- **What you are asking for:** correction of the bill, a refund, cancellation of a service.

### A short template

> Subject: Complaint about bill no. 123456 – CUPS ES00XXXXXXXXXXXX
>
> I request the correction of the bill above, for the period from 1 to 31 March, because it bills 650 kWh on an estimated reading, while the consumption recorded by the meter according to the distribution company's website is 290 kWh. I attach a photo of the meter and the consumption report. I request a corrected bill and a refund of the difference charged.

Always keep the **complaint number** you are given.

## Response deadlines

Retailers must reply to complaints within a reasonable period set by consumer-protection rules. If you get no reply within a month, or the reply does not satisfy you, you can escalate the complaint.

### Do I have to pay the disputed bill?

In general, it is wise to pay the undisputed part to avoid non-payment problems. If the amount is very high and clearly wrong, say so in the complaint and ask for collection to be held while it is resolved. Disconnection for non-payment has deadlines and notice requirements; vulnerable consumers have additional protections.

## If they do not resolve it

### Consumer arbitration

If the company belongs to the **Consumer Arbitration System**, you can lodge a free arbitration request with the Arbitration Board of your autonomous community. The award is binding on both parties.

### The municipal consumer office (OMIC) and regional bodies

You can lodge a complaint with your town hall's OMIC (*Oficina Municipal de Información al Consumidor*) or with your autonomous community's consumer body. They can mediate with the company and, where appropriate, fine it.

### The regional energy authority

Complaints about supply, readings and technical matters can be lodged with your autonomous community's energy or industry department.

### Consumer associations

They can advise you and, in some cases, represent you.

### The courts

For large amounts or complex cases, the courts are always an option. For small claims, the verbal procedure does not require a lawyer or court representative below certain amounts.

## Mistakes that weaken a complaint

- **Not saying exactly what you want.** "Please review my bill" invites a generic reply; "issue a corrected bill deducting 120 kWh" obliges them to answer on that point.
- **Mixing several problems** without separating the amounts. Give each mistake its own section, with its evidence.
- **Returning the direct debit without warning.** The company may treat it as non-payment. It is better to pay the undisputed part and explain in writing in the complaint.

## Special cases

### Bills after changing retailer

It is common to receive a final bill from the previous company and the first from the new one. Check that the periods do not overlap.

### Catch-up bills after estimated readings

If consumption was estimated for months and then an actual reading arrives, the catch-up can be large. Check that the total consumption matches the difference between actual readings. You can ask to pay in instalments. See [estimated readings and smart meters](/guias/lecturas-estimadas-y-contador-inteligente/).

### Contracts or services you did not ask for

If you have been switched to another company or had services added without your consent, ask for them to be cancelled and refunded. Misleading sales practices can be reported to the consumer authorities.

### Appliances damaged by power surges or cuts

If a voltage surge or a network outage damages appliances, the complaint goes to the **distribution company,** which is responsible for the quality of supply. For it to succeed:

- Note the date and time of the incident and whether your neighbours were affected too.
- Keep the damaged appliances and get a **technical report** stating the likely cause of the fault.
- Gather the repair invoices or the receipt for the replacement appliance.
- Check your home insurance, because many policies cover electrical damage.

The rules also set limits on the number and length of supply interruptions, and exceeding them entitles you to a discount on the bill. If you suffer frequent cuts, ask your distribution company for information in writing.

## Case study: a maintenance service nobody asked for

Going through her bill, a resident of Murcia found an "electrical assistance service" charge of €7.99 a month that she did not remember signing up for. She had been paying it for fourteen months.

She took these steps:

1. She asked the retailer in writing for a copy of the contract or of the recording showing her consent.
2. The retailer could provide no proof of the sign-up.
3. She asked in writing for the service to be cancelled at once and the €112 charged to be refunded.
4. When the first reply offered only the cancellation, she lodged a complaint with her town hall's consumer office.
5. A few weeks later the retailer issued a corrected bill with a full refund.

Checking every line of the bill once a year, even when the amount does not look high, helps you catch charges like this.

## Checklist

- Gather the bill, the contract and previous bills.
- Download your consumption from the distribution company's website.
- Complain in writing and keep the reference number.
- Set a date to escalate if there is no reply.
- Keep every communication.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do I have to complain?

As soon as possible. For amounts wrongly charged, the limitation periods for claims are long, but complaining early makes the evidence easier.

### Can I complain by phone?

You can start by phone, but always ask for a complaint number and confirm it in writing.

### What if they cut off my power over a disputed bill?

Notice requirements must be met before any disconnection. If you are cut off wrongly, complain at once to the retailer and to the regional authority.

### Do they pay interest?

Corrected bills refund the amount overcharged. If you have suffered further losses, you can claim them through arbitration or the courts.

## In short

Before complaining, check the bill's period, type of reading, prices and services, and download your real consumption from the distribution company's website. Complain in writing to the retailer with evidence and keep the reference number. If there is no solution within a reasonable time, go to consumer arbitration, the OMIC or the regional authority. With method and documents, most mistakes get corrected.
