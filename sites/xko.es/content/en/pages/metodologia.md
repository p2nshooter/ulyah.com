At XKO we would rather teach you to calculate than hand you a magic number. This page explains how we arrive at the figures in our guides, so that you can repeat them with your own data.

## The sources we use

- **Legislation:** the Official State Gazette, BOE (network tolls, system charges, self-consumption, the social discount, taxes).
- **Regulator:** the National Markets and Competition Commission (CNMC), including its offer comparison tool.
- **Electricity system:** Red Eléctrica (PVPC prices, demand, generation).
- **Efficiency:** the Institute for Energy Diversification and Saving (IDAE) and the Technical Building Code.
- **Equipment:** manufacturers' data sheets and energy labels, with data measured under European standards.

## The basic formula: energy times price

Almost all our calculations start from the same idea:

**Consumption (kWh) = power of the appliance (kW) × hours of use**

**Cost = consumption (kWh) × energy price (€/kWh)**

For example, a 1,500 W (1.5 kW) electric radiator switched on for 4 hours uses 6 kWh. If you pay €0.15/kWh for energy, it costs €0.90 that day. Swap in the price on your bill and you have your own figure.

## Reference prices

Because prices change, our examples use **round, cautious prices**, stated in each guide, rather than today's figure, which would soon be out of date. When the result depends heavily on the price, we show a table with several scenarios. We recommend replacing them with the real price on your bill, which appears in the breakdown of the energy you used.

## Consumption assumptions

- **Typical household:** unless we say otherwise, we use a reference annual consumption of about 3,000 to 3,500 kWh, usual for a flat with two or three people and no electric heating.
- **Climate:** we distinguish between warm, mild and cold areas when it affects heating, cooling or solar panels.
- **Efficiency:** for heat pumps we use moderate seasonal efficiencies, not the maximum catalogue values.
- **Solar output:** we use ranges of output per kilowatt-peak by area, with losses included.

## Savings and payback

We calculate the **simple payback period**: the cost of the investment divided by the estimated annual saving. We include neither financing nor inflation unless we say so. We round savings down and costs up, so that the figure is a cautious one.

## What we do not take into account

We cannot foresee future rises or falls in prices, tax changes or new grants. Nor do we know your home. That is why every guide lists the figures you should check for your own case.

## Corrections

If you spot a mistake in a calculation, write to us from the [contact](/contacto/) page. We correct the guide and update its review date.
